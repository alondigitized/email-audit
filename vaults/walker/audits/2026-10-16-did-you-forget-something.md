# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- Third cart nag this week, and I still haven't put anything in a cart. The big gold "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner with a black "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is a lot for something I never started. The shoe underneath is a black and green marbled D'Lites "Spring Bloom". It's a sneaker with no obvious tie to a cart of mine, and the grid below is mostly women's and boys' shoes.
- The one thing I'd care about is the dark band reading "MEMBERS TAKE 20% OFF SELECT FULL-PRICED STYLES". It sits well down the page, after the products. The men's pick is a John Deere waterproof Slip-in boot-shoe, a hands-free style I might actually look at, but it's one of six small tiles. I wouldn't click. I might scroll to see the 20%, and I wouldn't forward it. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The 20% off members band is easy to read, with big white numbers on a dark background.
- The men's John Deere waterproof Slip-in shows up in the grid. It's hands-free and my kind of shoe.
- The Men / Women / Kids / Sale list is big, plain text with tap targets I could hit on my phone.
- The footer states the offer dates (valid through 10/21) clearly enough that I know the deadline.
- The Skechers logo at the top matches the brand I subscribed to.

## What's weak

- I never started an order, so "Ready to check out?" and "Complete your order" feel false. This is the same pink-flower-sneaker style of nag as "Your cart expires soon!" earlier today.
- The hero shoe is a flashy marbled D'Lites. It isn't a cushioned, arch-supported walker, and it doesn't say whether it's a men's or women's shoe.
- Four of the six grid tiles are women's or boys' styles. Only one is for me, so this isn't aimed at a 62-year-old man.
- The hero and the grid both use "Complete your order" buttons, so I'm told twice to finish something I never began.
- The best offer, 20% off, sits below the fold. It's buried under all those shoes, and the fine print says it's for Skechers Plus members only.
- There's nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support, or how Skechers compares with Hoka or Brooks. The shoe tiles have names and no prices.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 20% members offer and put a men's slip-in right under it. Drop the fake "your order" language unless there's really a cart behind it.
- 2. Fill the grid with shoes that match what I've browsed, such as men's Slip-ins, walking and arch-support styles. Show prices and the member price.
- 3. Add a few words on comfort, like "cushioned, hands-free, all-day wear," so I know why the shoe fits me.
- 4. Cut the duplicate button and use one clear "Shop men's Slip-ins" button.
- 5. Don't send this the same week as other cart reminders. Space them out, or send the 20% offer by itself.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker, your comfort picks are still here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hands-free fit, cushioned soles, and 20% off for members. Ends 10/21.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's Slip-ins you looked at, plus member pricing through 10/21.`

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has two confirmed technical defects: a failing unsubscribe link (HTTP 400) and a missing plain-text part (0 chars). It also loads most images over insecure `http://` and has several images with no alt text.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **Unsubscribe link returns 400.** The link with text "Unsubscribe" points to `https://skechers.attn.tv/p/dvm?token=ChQwME5HVjkzZ1h1MmZpSFY0ajRYThIDMVN5GN6-9wUgAg&attn_macro=dW5zdWJzY3JpYmVMaW5r&...`. It is the only hard link failure in the QA run.
  - The 400 may be caused by the probe, for example a token tied to the recipient or a missing session. It could also be a real defect. It needs a manual check from a real inbox.
- **All tracked links go through Attentive redirects.** Examples are `https://skechers.e.attentivemail.com/e/c/...` for the "web version" link, the logo, "Ready to check out? Complete your order" and the hero image. This is normal for the ESP. The final destinations can't be verified here.
- **Two links returned 429.** These are the "Ready to check out? Complete your order" CTA and the `de642685-...jpg` image link. A 429 is rate limiting, not a confirmed break, so I'm not counting them as defects. The abandoned-cart CTA is the primary conversion link and its destination is unconfirmed.
- **Probe coverage was partial.** 12 links were probed and 25 were skipped, so the remaining footer and social links are unverified.
