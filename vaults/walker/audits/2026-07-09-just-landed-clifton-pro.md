# Just Landed: Clifton PRO
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- "Just Landed: Clifton PRO." I know the Clifton name — it's one of HOKA's most respected road shoes — so I tapped it. But the second I'm in the body, it's clear this email wasn't built with me in mind. Big red headline says "Run it again." The body copy says "Built for runners who chase progress every day." The lifestyle shots are all fast-cadence action frames of someone in their 30s mid-stride. I'm 62, I walk three miles every morning, and my feet demand cushion and support — not PROGUIDE™ rebound geometry.
- This is a product launch email, not an offer email. No price. No discount. No sale end date. Just a new shoe with a new tech name I've never heard of. And after yesterday's "look fast, feel fast, go fast" misfire, I was hoping HOKA would swing back toward the walking-and-comfort lane they hit on July 3 and July 7. They didn't.
- I'll mark it read and move on. Wouldn't click. Wouldn't forward to a friend. If I were actually in the market for the Clifton line, I'd go to the site directly and look up fit and width details — because this email gives me nothing to work with on that front.

## What's working

- **HOKA brand recognition** — I know and trust this brand after months of emails; I'd open on name alone.
- **"Just Landed" subject** — 24 characters, clean, no spam caps. Tells me exactly what this is without making me hunt.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight** — CLIFTON PRO in giant red text, then headline, then product shots, then features. Nothing competes.
- **"Shop Clifton PRO" CTA** — verb + specific noun, unambiguous, repeated sensibly. I know exactly what clicking does.
- **No broken images, no garbled layout** — renders clean top to bottom, which isn't always a given on mobile.

## What's weak

- **"Built for runners" is not me** — I'm a walker. Every line of copy is built around running cadence, mile splits, and progress tracking. Zero mention of the words I care about: support, comfort, arch, or wide.
- **No price anywhere** — I can't even do the value math. If this is a $160 shoe, I need to know that before I consider clicking.
- **PROGUIDE™ means nothing to me** — Brand tech names land flat without a plain-English translation. "Responsive rebound geometry" is not the same as "your feet feel good after two hours on concrete."
- **Models are the wrong demographic** — Every action shot features a younger, lean, competitive-looking runner. That's not the mirror I see, and it tells me this shoe was designed for someone else.
- **Third running-focused send in a row** — July 7 walked me right up to the door, July 8 slammed it in my face, and today is more of the same. The cadence fatigue is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with comfort, not performance** — The Clifton has a reputation for plush cushioning even among non-runners. Say that in the hero. "The cushioned ride walkers keep discovering" is a completely different email.
- 2. **Show the price** — A product launch email with no price signals either a luxury tier or a fear of sticker shock. Either way, I'm not clicking blind.
- 3. **Swap the lifestyle shot** — One image of someone my age on a trail or sidewalk (not a track) reframes the whole email without touching a word of copy.
- 4. **Translate the tech** — "PROGUIDE™" needs a one-liner: "keeps your stride smooth mile after mile." That's it. That's what I need to hear.
- 5. **Reintegrate the comfort brand thread** — The July 3 "Get the right fit" email and July 7 "Walk Stars" send were HOKA at their best for me. This launch could have leaned on the Clifton's wide-toe-box reputation or its plush midsole — instead it chased the performance runner.
