- Buried at the bottom is "up to 40% off select styles from Nike" with a real logo and a real number — that's the only line in the whole email I'd even glance at twice, and I have to scroll past a heels banner, two women's outfit shots, and a "shop by size" grid that stops at women's sizing before I even get there.

## What's working

- "Up to 40% off select styles from Nike" is a real brand and a real number, same as the last few sends this week — at least it's consistent.
- The sneaker product shots (the chunky white one and the blue ASICS-looking trainer) are clean, no render bugs, and could've been men's crossover styles if the copy around them wasn't so campus/co-ed focused.
- "Shop by Size" and "Shop Wide Width" buttons are a fit-focused touch I'd actually want — just wish the sizes shown weren't only women's 5–11.

## What's weak

- Hero is "The Queens of Campus" with a woman doing a yoga-pose jump in a yellow dress — nothing here says "62-year-old guy needs walking shoes."
- Heels from $29.99 as the top banner offer — I don't wear heels, and it's the same heels banner DSW's run three days running now (per the 8/11 "Heels from $29.99" send).
- Every model, every outfit shot, every "start your year on the right foot" lifestyle photo is young and female — zero demographic match.
- "Give Shoes, Get $10" VIP Rewards teaser is buried mid-email in tiny text where I'd never notice it, and there's no mention of comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease anywhere.
- This is the fourth near-identical DSW promo I've opened this week and it's the same layout, same bait-and-switch structure — Nike name at top or bottom, everything else is women's product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment men's subscribers out of "back to school/campus" sends entirely — send me a men's comfort-walking or Nike men's sale instead, not a rebadged women's heels email.
- 2. Lead with the actual concrete offer (40% off Nike) instead of burying it under three scrolls of unrelated product — that's the only thing in here with real pull.
- 3. If DSW wants my click, show a cushioned, slip-on, arch-support shoe above the fold — that's what gets me to tap "Shop Now," not a yellow-dress campus lifestyle shot.
- 4. Cut the send frequency or vary the creative — four lookalike promos in a week trains me to stop opening.
