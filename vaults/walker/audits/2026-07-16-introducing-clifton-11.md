# Introducing Clifton 11
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go — another HOKA in my inbox, and this time it's not the Clifton PRO again, so I'll give them that much. "Introducing Clifton 11" caught my eye because I actually know the Clifton name from when I've been kicking tires on cushioned road shoes. The PRO version they've been hammering all week — July 9th launch, July 11th color grid, July 14th "Run it again" nudge — wasn't built for a guy like me. This one at least opens with "Look what smooth can do" and backs it into "Signature comfort. Legendary ride." That's closer to my language.
- That said, the email still skews young and athletic. The hero shows a runner going full stride — not someone taking a morning walk around the neighborhood. The models throughout are trim and twenty-something, which is fine, but it doesn't make me feel like HOKA built this shoe with my feet in mind. The free shipping offer buried at the bottom is a nice pull, but I had to scroll a while to find anything that told me what this shoe would do for a 62-year-old who needs cushion and arch support for daily miles, not race performance.
- I'd mark this as read-and-return-to-later. I'm not clicking today, but I'd probably come back to it if my current pair starts giving me trouble. Wouldn't forward it — nothing in here makes a 62-year-old guy look like the target.

## What's working

- "Look what smooth can do" is real comfort copy — not speed language, not hype. That's the right register for me.
- "Signature comfort. Legendary ride." lands well. Two words I actually care about, right in the headline hierarchy.
- "MADE FOR DAILY MILES" — that's mine. I do daily miles. That framing made me slow down.
- Two product shots side-by-side with men's and women's variants — clean and easy to scan on a phone.
- Free Expedited Shipping visible at the bottom — a quiet win even if it's asterisked.

## What's weak

- Every model in the email is young, lean, and mid-sprint. Not one person who looks like they're 60 and just wants their feet to stop aching.
- After a week of Clifton PRO emails, this one blurs together — HOKA needs to signal more clearly that the 11 and the PRO are different products for different people.
- The asterisk on "Free Expedited Shipping*" immediately made me squint — what's the catch? Nothing visible to explain it, which erodes trust.
- No price anywhere. I cross-shop Skechers, New Balance, and Brooks regularly — if I don't know what the Clifton 11 costs, the CTA asks me to click blind.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price in the email.** Even a "Starting at $145" line next to the product shots would keep me from clicking away the second I land on a PDP I wasn't mentally prepped for.
- 2. **Swap at least one lifestyle image** for someone older — mid-50s or 60s, casual clothes, walking posture. Not everyone in a HOKA is chasing a PR.
- 3. **Differentiate Clifton 11 from Clifton PRO visually and in copy** — one line explaining "the everyday Clifton, not the race-day one" would reduce the blur from last week's sends.
- 4. **Resolve the asterisk** — either include the free-shipping condition inline or drop the asterisk entirely if it's broadly available.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HIGH — Broken mobile CSS (`.nav_padding`):**
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
```
Missing numeric values. These declarations are invalid and will be silently ignored, collapsing nav spacing on `max-width: 480px` viewports.

**HIGH — Broken mobile CSS (`.nav_border`):**
```css
.nav_border { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Missing both width value and color value. Invalid declaration.

**MEDIUM — Duplicate conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` tags:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
The second tag adds `user-scalable=yes`, which contradicts `maximum-scale=1` in the first. Behavior is browser/client-dependent; one tag should be removed.

**MEDIUM — Malformed charset meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without it, some legacy clients may not recognize the charset declaration.

**LOW — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Displays as a blank tab label in webmail clients (Gmail browser, Outlook.com). Should be set to subject line or brand name.

**LOW — Aggressive CSS reset (`* { border:none; }`):**
Strips borders from all elements globally. If any layout component (e.g., a divider `<td>` using `border-top`) relies on borders, it will silently break.

**INFO — Custom web fonts blocked in major clients:**
`TheFutureHOKA` is loaded via `@font-face` from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/...`. Gmail, Outlook (Windows), and Yahoo Mail block external font loading. Fallback stack rendering should be QA'd in those clients specifically.

**INFO — `font-display: swap` in `@font-face`:**
Not supported in any email client. Harmless but dead code.
