# David Beckham Precision Remade — available now
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, and this is the seventh in a week. This one is soccer cleats, and they're a pair of David Beckham's. I'm 62 and I walk for comfort, so I don't play soccer. The hero shows a gnarly gray hand clutching a silver cleat, which looks more like a horror poster than a shoe ad. It's a nice story about a free kick from 2002, but nothing in it helps me.
- The bottom half is a gift card pitch with a red background and snowflakes. At least that one is clear. There's no price, no percent off and no cushioning talk. The fine print says the cleats are only at four stores, in New York and Las Vegas, and "first-come, first-served." So the headline product may not be something I can even buy. I'd skim it and delete it. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The text is big and the headlines are plain, so I can read it on my phone without zooming.
- The black "SHOP NOW" and "FIND STORE NOW" buttons are large and easy to tap.
- The Beckham story is short and easy to follow. A soccer fan would enjoy it.
- The gift card section is a clear idea and fits the season, so I could see using it for a grandkid.
- It's clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Cleats mean nothing to a 62-year-old walker, and it's another email with no comfort shoe in it. Compare the Oct 19 "Low-profile shoes" email, which at least had everyday sneakers.
- The hand-holding-a-cleat photo is dark and creepy, and the shoe is tough to make out.
- There's no price and no deal. The black banner at the top, "Explore the latest adidas best sellers," is generic.
- The fine print says it's only at four stores and "purchase is not guaranteed." That feels like a tease, and nowhere near me.
- Two buttons in a row, "Shop Now" and "Find Store Now," don't say which one to press or what I'd be shopping for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Send me something I'd wear. Lead with a cushioned walking or slip-on shoe and say the benefit in plain words, like "all-day comfort." Keep the Beckham cleats for the soccer list.
- 2. Put a real offer up top, with a number or free shipping, and make the button name the product.
- 3. If a product is only in four stores, don't make it the hero of a national email. Say up front that it's limited, or send it only to people near those stores.
- 4. Give the gift card section a dollar amount or a date, such as "order by Dec 15 for delivery."

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has one confirmed deliverability defect: it has no plain-text part. It also has minor image and accessibility problems, including HTTP image sources and missing alt text. The HTML is truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe link and final link destinations could not be checked.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 22 links go through the click-redirect domain `click.comms.adidas.com`. The QA probe skipped them, so destinations, redirect health and UTM params are unverified.
- The open-tracking pixel loads over plain HTTP: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a1574d72d5-.../tsADdTrGJp-1yMdN-DW0MNp65SHtys8-pU6Zwu0hszY=452`. Clients that block or rewrite non-HTTPS images will drop it, which undercounts opens. It also has no alt attribute, which is harmless for a pixel but is flagged.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` is set. It is only relevant if the email is viewed in a browser.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- The `arrow` image (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) loads over HTTP and appears 6 times: `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-...jpg`. It may be blocked or show mixed-content warnings. It does have alt text.
- `https://dv.adidas.com/o/e70f2c7b-4418-4892-af81-006e5b503782?...` has no alt text. If it is a content image, it is invisible to screen readers and to image-blocked clients.
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it is not a valid charset declaration. Clients that sniff the encoding could garble non-ASCII characters. This depends on the HTTP and MIME `Content-Type` header, which was not provided.
- The `@font-face` rules for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` only use `src: local('Arial')…`. They effectively fall back to system fonts, which is not a defect.
