# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-06-05
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, I know Skechers — I've bought from them before — but right this second they're yelling "GAME ON!" at me with three pairs of colorful retro court shoes on a grass background, and I'm standing here wondering if I walked into the wrong store. Skechers is a big brand with a lot going on, and the loyalty banner up top tells me they actually know I'm a member. But the big hero moment right now has nothing to do with why I usually come here.

## What's working

- **"BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart"** — That top blue banner is doing heavy lifting. I see the deal before I even see the logo. No hunting, no code to type. If I was already going to buy shoes, this moves me.
- **Member Month / 2X Points strip** — "Earn 2X Points now thru 6/13 Login | details" tells me the brand remembers I'm in the program. That matters. I'm not a points chaser, but I notice when a brand treats me like a regular.
- **"Slip-ins" and "Arch Fit" in the nav bar** — Those two words are exactly why I come to Skechers. Having them one tap away, above the fold, without burying them under a hamburger menu — that's smart. On a phone, that nav strip is the first real thing I'd tap.
- **Clean render on mobile** — No layout jank, text is legible, the banner stacks in a way that makes sense. For a guy my age who gets annoyed when websites look broken, this passes.

## What's weak

- **"GAME ON!" hero is targeting someone else's closet** — Three pairs of colorful retro sneakers (green, navy, red) on a grassy court background and a brand anniversary badge. This is a sneakerhead drop, not a comfort-shoe pitch. Nothing in that hero says "feels great on your feet all day."
- **No CTA button above the fold** — I see category nav links but not a single button. On a phone, there's a difference between a nav tab and a big "Shop Men's Slip-ins" button. The nav is easy to miss for someone who isn't already primed to scroll down.
- **No one like me visible** — No model, no lifestyle shot, no person over 50 walking a dog or standing at a counter. Just shoes on grass. Hard to imagine myself in this scene.
- **No free-shipping mention anywhere in this viewport** — That's a trust signal that moves Boomers. I shouldn't have to scroll or guess.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the hero for something comfort-relevant**: A lifestyle shot of a real-looking 60-year-old in Slip-ins walking somewhere recognizable — a park path, a kitchen, a hardware store — with copy like "All Day Comfort. Zero Laces." Retro court shoes can have their own page; the homepage hero should earn the broadest audience.
- **Add a prominent Men's / Shop Comfort button**: Put a clear tappable button right under the nav strip — "Shop Men's Slip-ins" or "Explore Arch Fit" — so someone like me can get there in one intentional tap, not a nav hunt.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold in the top promo strip** — Replace or stack it next to the BOGO banner. "BOGO 50% OFF + Free Shipping on Orders $50+" in one banner does more than two separate items I might not both see.
- **Put a star-rating teaser on the hero** — Even just "4.7 stars · 200K+ reviews" under the GAME ON headline gives first-timers and returning shoppers a reason to trust. Right now this viewport has zero social proof.
