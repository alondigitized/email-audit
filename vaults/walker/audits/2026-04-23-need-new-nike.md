# Need New Nike?
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a Nike-focused sale email from Shoe Carnival — a brand I barely think about because I'm a Skechers guy. The moment I see "Need New Nike?" in my inbox, I'm already half-checked out. When I open it, I get athletic sneakers, Air Max shoes, basketball gear, and a Jordan brand sweepstakes. None of that is me. I wear slip-ins. I walk the neighborhood, not the basketball court. This email was built for someone 30 years younger, and it doesn't even try to hide it.
- Wrong brand, wrong product category, wrong audience — for me. The email itself is structurally competent, but Shoe Carnival is fishing in Skechers waters and using the wrong bait entirely.

## What's working

- **The top offer is clear and immediate.** "Up to 30% Off Sneakers & Slides" with a "Sale Ends Today" urgency bar — I can read that fast, understand it fast. That part is fine.
- **The $10 off coupon at the bottom** is the one thing that actually speaks my language. Ten bucks off a $74.99 purchase, with an online code — that's the kind of practical deal I'd clip if this were a store I shopped.
- **Clean, readable layout.** Even on a phone, the text isn't tiny, the sections are stacked vertically. Easy enough to scroll through.

## What's weak

- **Not one slip-in or comfort shoe in the entire email.** I scrolled the whole thing. It's Nike Air Max, Jordan brand, athletic sneakers. I don't run. I don't play basketball. I need something I can slide on without bending over.
- **The sweepstakes feels like clutter.** "Play. Shoot. Win. Enter to win up to $100!" with a big Jordan logo — I genuinely don't know what I'm supposed to do with that. It looks like a lottery ticket and I don't trust it.
- **"Athletic Sale — Adults Starting at $59.98" doesn't move me.** Athletic sale means sports shoes. That's not my aisle.
- **The Shoe Carnival membership section (2-3X points)** is buried mid-email and I almost missed it. If they want me to feel like a known customer, lead with the loyalty benefit.
- **The preview text is a broken tracking URL.** An absolute mess in my inbox — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7In..." — that's just embarrassing. Looks like spam right off the bat.
- **No men's comfort content whatsoever.** No wide widths, no memory foam, no walking shoes, no "made for all-day wear" messaging. I don't know why they think this email belongs in my inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of athletic/basketball campaigns.** If they have my purchase history or browsing behavior, they should know I'm not a Nike runner. Send me the comfort/casual/walking shoe emails.
- 2. **Lead with the loyalty points** if I'm a member — that's the hook that would actually land for me as a loyal-type shopper.
- 3. **Fix the preheader immediately.** A raw tracking URL as preview text screams spam. Even a blank preheader would be better.
- 4. **Move the $10 coupon higher.** That's the most universally appealing offer in the email, and it's buried at the bottom. Put it in the first scroll.
- 5. **If Nike is the focus, show walking/casual Nike** — there are Nike comfort walkers. That's a bridge category that could still reach me.

Product and hero images without `alt` text fail in image-blocked environments (notably Outlook on corporate networks) — recipients see blank space with no fallback context. Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen reader noise; omitting the attribute entirely is technically noncompliant with WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1.

**Legacy doctype:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` is the declared doctype. Modern ESP best practice is HTML5 (`<!DOCTYPE html>`). Not an active rendering bug in major clients given the extensive fallback CSS, but inconsistent with the `xmlns` XHTML attribute also present on `<html>`.

**`color-scheme: light` forced** via meta tag — dark mode is explicitly suppressed. This is a deliberate choice but means dark-mode Gmail/Apple Mail users will see a white background email. No defect if intentional.
