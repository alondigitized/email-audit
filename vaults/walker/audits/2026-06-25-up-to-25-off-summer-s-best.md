# Up to 25% off summer’s best
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- My thumb opened this on muscle memory — DSW, 25% off, summer clearance, same as the last six or seven sends going back to June 15. No hesitation on my part. But then the email loaded and the first thing I read was "fun florals, natural styles, and stillies." I had to Google "stillies" when this brand used that word in a prior send, and I'm still not sure what it means for a 62-year-old guy who just wants something comfortable to slip on. The hero might as well have been addressed to someone else.
- What pulled me back was the brands section. New Balance and Crocs showing up in the same block with a 25% off label — that's the part I actually care about. Both of those are comfort brands I've been rotating for a couple years now. The "UP TO 50% OFF CLEARANCE" row below that also registered, because clearance at DSW is where I've found some of my best buys. But that's a lot of scrolling past women's sandals to get there.
- Mark as read, maybe a slow scroll back to the brands block if I'm bored. No forward — nothing in here that screams "hey, send this to your buddy who walks a lot."

## What's working

- **Brand logos are the real hero.** New Balance and Crocs in the same "25% off" row are exactly the brands I'd stop scrolling for — that block earned its place.
- **The clearance callout is honest and clear.** "Up to 50% OFF Clearance" in big bold text, right there — I know what I'm clicking into.
- **No render problems.** Email loaded clean, no broken images, text is readable at normal zoom on my phone.
- **DSW's name recognition.** I'm conditioned to open these. That's brand equity working.
- **Loyalty nudge at the bottom.** I spotted the rewards/perks mention — I'm a VIP member, and any reminder I'm getting extra off lands a little warmer.

## What's weak

- **"Fun florals, natural styles, and stillies" — that's not my aisle.** The hero copy might as well say "not for you, Walker." Three phrases and none of them landed.
- **The Shop by Size grid shows women's sizes.** I'm a 10.5 to 11 wide. Seeing a row that starts at 6 and tops out around 9 or 10 doesn't invite me to tap.
- **Another 25% off email — this is the third or fourth in two weeks.** The June 15 "It's still on: up to 25% off" was the freshest version of this. By today this offer feels like wallpaper.
- **"PSST... TRY THESE, TOO" row is labeled Women's Shoes first.** If that's the warm editorial recommendation block, leading with women's product in a non-segmented send is a miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment or at least split the hero.** Lead men with comfort-brand copy — "New Balance, Crocs, + more — 25% off this week" over an image of a walking shoe. Keep the florals for the women's version. This is table stakes segmentation.
- 2. **Lead the brands block higher.** Bring the Nike / New Balance / Crocs block up to second position, right under the hero. That's where I actually stopped scrolling.
- 3. **Put a deadline on the offer.** "Ends Sunday" or "Through June 29" would make the 25% off feel like action, not ambient noise. Every recent DSW send has the same % off with no expiration — it trains me to ignore it.
- 4. **Men's size grid instead of (or alongside) women's.** If the size-shop row starts at 9 instead of 6, I actually use it.
- 5. **Rotate creative.** The June 15 "It's still on: up to 25% off" sent almost identical copy. By the fourth week of 25% off emails, I'm tuning out.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed defects:**

**A. `@font-face` uses `Src:` (capital S) — invalid CSS**
All 10 `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` instead of the valid lowercase `src:`:
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');  /* ← invalid */
}
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec but `Src:` is treated as an unknown property by strict CSS parsers (Outlook WebKit rendering, Apple Mail on older OS versions). These clients will silently fall back to system fonts. The Futura PT and HelveticaNow brand fonts will not load in those environments.

**B. Two meta tags missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, these are no-op declarations. The charset declaration is especially important — without it, Outlook 2016/2019 may mis-detect encoding on non-ASCII characters (accented letters, special punctuation, price symbols).

**C. Font format coverage gaps**
- Futura PT: `.woff` only — no `.woff2`, no `.ttf`. Gmail app on Android does not support `.woff`; fonts will silently degrade.
- HelveticaNow: `.ttf` only — `.woff2` would load faster and is supported in all clients that support `.ttf`.

**D. Near-black color substitution (intentional, flagged for awareness)**
Several color values use near-black rather than true black:
- `border-top-black`: `#000001` instead of `#000000`
- `.dm-font-black`: `#000001`
- `.dm-bg-black`: `#00000F`

This is a known workaround to prevent Gmail's dark mode from treating `#000000` as transparent. Intentional — no change needed, but document it so QA doesn't flag it as an error.

**Cannot audit from truncated source:** image `alt` attributes, link text for screen readers, color contrast ratios on text elements.
