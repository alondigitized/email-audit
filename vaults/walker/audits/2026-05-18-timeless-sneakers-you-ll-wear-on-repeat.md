# Timeless sneakers you'll wear on repeat
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Seventh adidas email in my inbox in roughly a week, and this one's putting the Samba, Gazelle, and Spezial front and center — three shoes I associate with guys half my age who tuck their jeans into their socks. The big red shoe in the hero shot is a genuinely good product image; I'll give them that much. But then I read "timeless soccer culture from the stands to the streets" and I knew exactly what this was: another heritage/fashion send with nothing in it for me.
- No price. No discount. No "free shipping today only." Just a "SHOP NOW" button in a top banner and a three-sentence brand manifesto underneath. After yesterday's Formula 1 jersey sweepstakes email, I was already running thin on patience with adidas in my inbox. This one didn't win any of it back.
- Mark as read and move on. I'm not clicking, and there's nothing here I'd forward.

## What's working

- The red Spezial hero shot is crisp and well-lit — if I were shopping for someone else, I'd notice it.
- Clean, uncluttered layout with no broken images; renders fine on my phone without squinting.
- "SHOP NOW →" in the top banner is unambiguous — I know exactly where that button goes.
- Preview text ("Retro soccer culture, reimagined for everyday wear") is real copy and doesn't just repeat the subject — at least that's something.

## What's weak

- Zero offer — no discount, no free shipping, no member pricing. I opened the email and got nothing actionable.
- Spezial, Samba, Gazelle are fashion sneakers aimed at a younger crowd; not a single word about cushioning, arch support, or all-day comfort.
- "Iconic style starts with the classics" is brand-speak filler — it gives me no reason to scroll, let alone click.
- There's no CTA below the product section, just the footer. Once I scroll past the hero I'm just done — no second hook.
- Seven adidas emails in about a week and not one of them has been aimed at what I actually shop for. The cadence has worn me out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price on something.** Even a "From $80" badge or a single sale callout would give me a reason to click. No price, no decision.
- 2. **Swap the hero product or add a comfort angle.** If you're going to show me a Samba, at least mention the cushioned footbed or the gum sole's flex. Give my feet a reason to care.
- 3. **Rewrite the body copy to be actionable.** "Iconic style starts with the classics" tells me nothing I can use. Try "Everyday wear that holds up — Spezial, Gazelle, Samba" instead.
- 4. **Add a second CTA below the product grid** — not just in the top banner. By the time I scroll down, that button is gone.
- 5. **Pull back the send frequency.** Seven emails in a week from one brand is too many, especially when none of them land for me.

### 1. Technical Summary

One CTA resolves to a broken relative path, blocking that purchase flow entirely. The email also ships with zero plain-text content, a compliance and deliverability risk that could increase spam scoring.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken CTA — relative URL in redirect chain**

The QA probe surfaced an unresolvable URL type:

```
unknown url type: '/g/d4c4da33-91be-4dfc-bc4b-8621bdea9a65?txn=b404df00-5277-11f1-bc24-6e9b95041fa3'
```

The outer redirect URL is:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/d4c4da33-91be-4dfc-bc4b-8621bdea9a65?cp_tp=v3...
```

`dv.adidas.com` is the personalized content redirect domain. The `/g/` path prefix in the resolved destination indicates the redirect resolves to a root-relative path rather than an absolute URL. Any subscriber whose click-tracking lookup fails or whose personalization token expires will land on a broken destination. This is the highest-priority fix.

**[WARN] 32 tracking links not verified**

All 32 click-redirect links pass through `click.comms.adidas.com` and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination integrity is unconfirmed for the bulk of CTAs.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Six HTTP (non-HTTPS) image references — same asset, repeated**

The arrow icon is referenced six times over HTTP:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail (2019+) and Outlook.com block mixed-content images. All six instances will render as broken images in those clients. Fix: switch the image host reference to `https://`.

**[WARN] Open-tracking pixel served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e397b9a9b-426312cb-9da6-453e-98d0-483e026d5b38-000000/hZ6HTV5Xnl2tY0vI9JihFxn-H...
```

An HTTP tracking pixel will be blocked by image-proxy clients (Apple MPP, Gmail proxy), inflating open-rate nulls and reducing tracking accuracy further. The ESP should serve this pixel over HTTPS.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `d4c4da33-91be-4dfc-bc4b-8621bdea9a65` (dynamic content image, `dv.adidas.com`)
- `hZ6HTV5Xnl2tY0vI9JihFxn-H...452` (tracking pixel — `alt=""` is acceptable here, but currently absent)

The dynamic content image missing `alt` means screen readers and image-off clients get no fallback label.

**Incomplete `<meta charset>` declaration**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Without it, this tag has no effect; charset is declared in the MIME headers only. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` or use the HTML5 shorthand `<meta charset="UTF-8">`.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `local()` fallback chains. No web font URL is loaded — the declarations are system-font aliases only. The duplication is harmless but adds ~1 KB of dead CSS.

CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its complete absence increases spam filter scoring across SpamAssassin-based systems (missing `text/plain` MIME part) and violates best-practice guidance from most ESPs. It also means subscribers on text-only clients (some corporate environments, accessibility tools) receive a blank email.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** — This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header for any resources loaded by the email client. This is intentional for privacy but will also strip referrer data from any analytics calls triggered on link-open in webmail clients.
