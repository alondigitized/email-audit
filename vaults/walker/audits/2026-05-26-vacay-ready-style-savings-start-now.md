# Vacay-Ready Style Savings Start Now!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Well, I'll be — Crocs actually showed me a pair of plain white classic clogs in the hero image. No studs, no platforms, no florals, no women's wedges. Just the shoe. That alone puts this email above the last week's worth of sends from them.
- But then I scroll down and the offer is "Buy 2, Take 30% Off." So if I want the discount, I've got to buy two pairs of clogs. I don't need two pairs of clogs. I need one pair that fits my men's 10 wide and supports my arch. The vacation angle in the subject line never shows up in the body either — no sandals, no travel shoes, just clogs and a Jibbitz charms callout I don't care about.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? No. Would I mark as read and move on? Yes — and honestly, after eight or nine of these in two weeks, I'm getting close to unsubscribing.

## What's working

- The hero image is finally just classic clogs in plain white — clean, recognizable, no costume jewelry stuck to them.
- The offer text is big and readable — "Buy 2, Take 30% Off!" doesn't make me squint.
- No layout bugs, no broken images, hierarchy is clean.
- Logo top-left, app badges and social icons at the bottom — looks like a real brand email, not a phishing attempt.

## What's weak

- "Buy 2" to get the discount is a non-starter for me — I'm one pair of shoes, one foot at a time.
- The subject promises "Vacay-Ready Style" but the body shows clogs, not sandals or travel shoes — feels like a bait-and-switch.
- "Jibbitz Deals: $3 Single Charms, $7 Packs" is meaningless to me. I'm not decorating my shoes.
- Nothing about fit, width, arch support, or men's sizing — the things that actually decide my purchase.
- The preview text in the inbox was a string of invisible spacer characters — looked like junk next to the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the "Buy 2" gate. A flat 20% off one pair would convert me. Asking a 62-year-old to double up on clogs to save 30% just leaves the discount on the table.
- 2. Match subject to body. If the subject says "Vacay-Ready," show me a travel-ready slip-on or a sandal — not the same classic clog you've shown six times this month.
- 3. Add a men's comfort callout — "Roomy fit, men's 7–15, arch-supportive footbed." One line of fit information beats another charm promo.
- 4. Kill the Jibbitz line for an audience over 50. Replace it with a review snippet or a "trusted by walkers and nurses" trust cue.
- 5. Fix the preheader — right now it's invisible spacer junk in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Classic Clogs — 20% off your size today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort that travels: $15 off the Classic Clog`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Roomy fit, cushioned footbed, ends Wednesday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One pair, one discount — no bundle required.`
