# Major savings are happening NOW
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- DSW again — my thumb was already moving on autopilot before I finished reading the sender name. After six straight days of emails from these folks, I've got the reflex trained. "Major savings are happening NOW" at least broke the sandal-deal-of-the-day pattern, so I opened it.
- Then I actually looked at it. Animal print. Leopard slides. Zebra straps. "It's a Zoo Over Here." The whole email is a fashion spread aimed at someone who is decidedly not a 62-year-old man looking for something with decent arch support and a heel that won't blow out his knee. Every shoe in this email belongs in a completely different closet than mine.
- I'm marking it as read. Not clicking. Maybe forwarding to my daughter — she'd get a kick out of the "Sasso cute" caption — but this one is zero help to me.

## What's working

- DSW is a name I trust, so it got the open on brand equity alone
- "Extra 10% Off Clearance" is a clean, concrete offer — not buried in the footer, visible right near the top
- The email rendered perfectly on my phone — images loaded, nothing broken, easy to scroll
- "It's a Zoo Over Here" is clever copy — I'd just rather it was selling me something I'd actually wear
- The "Shop by Size" section is a smart feature in theory — gets me to the shelf faster

## What's weak

- Every single shoe in this email is a women's strappy heel or animal-print slide — I'm the wrong person to be looking at this
- Animal print is the opposite of what I look for; I want cushioned, I want plain, I want slip-on
- "Major savings are happening NOW" tells me nothing about what category, what price, or why I should care
- I'm riding a week-long wave of DSW emails and the fatigue is real — this one landed in that tired zone
- Not one word about comfort, cushioning, arch support, or any feature that actually matters to me when buying shoes

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender, or at least include a men's panel** — one row of clearance walking shoes for me to scan would have earned a click. Showing me heels is like mailing me a knitting catalog.
- 2. **Put the actual offer in the subject** — "Major savings" is filler. The email has a real story (Extra 10% off clearance), lead with it.
- 3. **Make "NOW" mean something** — if there's a deadline, show it. Give me a date. "Now" by itself reads like urgency theater.
- 4. **Dial back the cadence** — seven emails inside a week from a single brand, even one I like, starts feeling like noise.
- 5. **Let the animal print campaign live alongside a practical panel** — one gender-neutral or men's column keeps it relevant for more of the list.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your clearance size is on sale: extra 10% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's, women's, kids — big brands, marked-down prices`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalization in all `@font-face` blocks**
All 10 `@font-face` declarations use `Src:` (capital S) instead of `src:`. CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but some email client CSS parsers (notably older Outlook WebView) treat capitalized property names as unknown declarations and silently drop the rule, causing font load failure.

**`Margin: 0` capitalization**
The heading reset block (`h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li`) uses `Margin: 0` alongside the standard `margin: 0 !important`. Same risk as above; also results in a redundant declaration.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust` value conflict**
In the `*` reset block, `-ms-text-size-adjust` is set to `100%` first, then immediately overridden to `none` in the same ruleset:
```css
-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* line 1 */
-ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* later in same * block */
```
`none` wins (cascade order), which disables automatic text scaling on older Windows Phone / IE-based clients. Intended value is almost certainly `100%`.

**`border-top` non-standard property order**
```css
border-top: 1px #000001 solid !important;
```
Correct shorthand order is `width style color`. Some strict parsers will reject this declaration. Should be:
```css
border-top: 1px solid #000001 !important;
```

**Font cache-busting inconsistency**
HelveticaNow font URLs include cache-busting timestamps (`?1755182939`), Futura PT URLs do not. If Futura PT assets are ever replaced at the same path, clients holding a cached copy won't pick up the update.

**Web font availability**
Gmail (all variants) and Outlook (Windows) block `@font-face` entirely — the email will silently fall back to system fonts. The fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source but must be verified.
