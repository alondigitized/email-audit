# 4(!) trends to try in May
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- Not my kind of email. DSW sent me a trends roundup — four May trends I'm supposed to try — and by the time I scrolled the first screen, I already knew this wasn't aimed at me. The models I can see appear to be women in sandals and floral outfits. The featured brands are Keds and Michael Kors. The 25% off banner is the only thing worth stopping for, and even that's got an asterisk I don't trust. The one genuinely useful piece — "Shop Wide Width / Shop Wide Calf" — is buried so low I almost missed it. If I opened this at all, it was because the subject line was short and DSW is a name I recognize. But I wouldn't click.

## What's working

- The discount banner earns attention. "UP TO 25% OFF BIG-DEAL BRANDS" sits right at the top and it's the most legible element on the page — big enough to read without zooming on my phone. I appreciate that.
- The "Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf" buttons near the bottom are actually useful to me personally. Wide fits matter at my age, and DSW carrying that is a real reason I go there. Problem is, it's buried at the bottom and clearly not the point of this email.
- The email loaded without broken images, and the layout doesn't fall apart on a phone screen. Basic, but I appreciate when things just work.

## What's weak

- This email is aimed at someone's wife or daughter, not me. The hero shot is a woman in a yellow floral dress. The featured brands — Keds, Michael Kors — are not what I'm shopping. "May's Top 4 Trends" as the whole premise assumes I care about trends, which I don't. I care about whether my feet hurt by the third hour of a Costco run.
- The Mother's Day gift card push in the preheader is a pivot I didn't expect and it makes the email feel unfocused. Is this a trends email or a gifting email? Pick one.
- "Up to 25% off big-deal brands*" — that asterisk always means something. It probably means the brands I'd actually want are excluded. I've been burned by that before.
- The four trend sections also mean there's no single, clear reason to click. Four ideas is three too many for me to act on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me differently.** I should be getting a men's comfort/casual shoes email, not a trends roundup. If DSW has my purchase history, they know I'm not buying Keds.
- 2. **Lead with the wide-fit section.** "Shop Wide Width" should be a hero module or near-top callout, not a footer footnote. That's a real differentiator for me.
- 3. **Drop the asterisk or explain it.** "Up to 25% off" with fine print implies I'll get to the site and find everything I want is excluded. Either be specific about what's included or I'm not clicking.
- 4. **Cut the trends framing entirely for my segment.** Replace "4 trends to try" with something like "Comfortable fits, spring prices" and I'm paying attention.
- 5. **One CTA, not five.** Gift cards, trends, size shop, wide width — I don't know where to go. One clear action per email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — accessibility violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks user zoom. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200%. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Invalid CSS property case in all `@font-face` blocks**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
The property `Src:` uses a capital S — invalid CSS. Most email clients are case-insensitive here, but it is non-conformant and will fail strict parsers. Should be lowercase `src:`.

**No `woff2` format declared for Futura PT fonts**
Only `.woff` is loaded. `woff2` is ~30% smaller and supported everywhere `woff` is. Bandwidth impact is minor but avoidable. Helvetica Now loads `.ttf` only — even less efficient.

**Image alt text** — cannot verify from truncated source. Must be confirmed: every `<img>` tag should have a meaningful `alt=""` or `alt="[description]"` attribute for screen readers and image-blocked rendering.

**`<title>DSW</title>`** — Some clients surface this as tab/preview text. Not an error, but a missed opportunity to match the subject line for consistency.
