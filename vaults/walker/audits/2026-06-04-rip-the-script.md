# Rip the Script
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- I've been on Nike's list for about a month now, and I still can't figure out why they think I'm a soccer fan. "Rip the Script" — I had no idea what I was opening. Turns out it's a full soccer campaign: cleats called Mercurio, national team jerseys, something about LEGO, a prompt to download the Nike app so I can "go behind the scenes with players." I'm 62. My idea of going behind the scenes is checking if the shoe comes in wide width.
- Nike sent me a 20% off email twice in the last two weeks — that at least got my attention because it was a real offer. This one's a lifestyle campaign built entirely around a sport I don't follow. There's no discount, no comfort footwear, no reason for me to be here. The email looks sharp and produced, I'll give them that, but looking sharp for the wrong audience is still the wrong audience.
- I'll mark this as read and move on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it, and if this cadence keeps up — soccer kits, women's gifting, now this — I'm going to start hitting unsubscribe.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** The dark hero image draws the eye immediately, and the Shop / Watch buttons sit right underneath it — no hunting required even on a phone.
- **No clutter or render bugs.** Text is legible, images loaded, no overlapping elements. The layout is straightforward even on a small screen.
- **Brand is recognizable.** I know it's Nike before I read a single word — the Swoosh, the black-and-white palette, the tone. That's something.
- **"Shop Soccer Now" button is unambiguous.** It's a clear, tappable call to action with real copy — not "Discover More" nonsense.

## What's weak

- **Zero relevance to me.** Cleats, soccer jerseys, LEGO tie-ins — none of this connects to why I'd ever open a Nike email. I want walking shoes with cushioning. I got a World Cup warm-up.
- **"Rip the Script" tells me nothing.** Subject line is pure brand poetry. No offer, no product hint, no reason to open. I almost deleted it.
- **No offer or price signal anywhere.** The May emails had 20% off front and center. This one has no discount, no loyalty pricing, no hook for someone who's price-aware. Just "Shop."
- **Models and content skew young and athletic.** Nothing in this email signals it's for a 62-year-old guy browsing on his phone who wants something comfortable for a walk around the neighborhood.
- **The App download ask felt like extra homework.** I'm not downloading an app to watch soccer highlights. That section is dead space for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment before sending.** Anyone who's opened the 20% off emails but never clicked a soccer product should not be on this list. Send me the comfort and running shoe campaigns; give soccer to the soccer subscribers.
- 2. **Swap the hero for something I'd actually buy.** If Nike insists on sending to the broad list, a comfort/casual walking shoe in the hero with the soccer campaign as a secondary module would at least give me a reason to scroll.
- 3. **Lead with a price signal.** Even "Starting at $X" in the hero would give me a reason to tap Shop. Right now there's nothing to grab onto.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `New Comfort Styles — Yours to Explore`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `Built for the Long Walk, Not the Pitch`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned. Supportive. Sized for real life — shop men's comfort now.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `No jersey required. New styles that go the distance.`

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Re-submit full HTML source** for complete link inventory, UTM audit, merge token scan, and footer compliance check — the truncation blocks ~70% of what matters technically.
