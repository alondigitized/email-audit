# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 1, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The homepage hooks me right — BOGO deal front and center, Hands Free Slip-ins splashed big below the fold. Navigation into Men's and finding Slip-ins is clean and comfortable. The product page looks solid. Then it all falls apart: the cart is a blank white screen. Again. And from the moment I "logged in," nothing about this site knew who I was. No name, no Plus member welcome, no Men's preference remembered. Search dumped Women's shoes on me first. Two steps forward, two steps back.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 5/10

**+1 from yesterday.** Homepage and Men's navigation improved my confidence. But a blank cart still means zero revenue from my visit. That's the bottom line.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** That blue banner saying "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" is right at the top on every screen. As a price-conscious guy, that grabs my attention immediately and keeps me shopping.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins is hero content.** The homepage scrolls right into big bold "HANDS FREE Slip-ins" branding with a real-looking guy in the photo. That's me. That's my shoe. I felt seen.
- **Men's menu is genuinely clean.** The slide-out nav has large text, full-width tap targets, and "Hands Free Slip-ins" listed right there as a direct shortcut — no hunting. "Comfort Technologies" is also there, which is exactly the kind of thing I care about.
- **Slip-ins category page delivers.** 273 results, width filter chips (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) right up top, clean product thumbnails. That's a shopper's dream landing page.
- **Product page is well-structured.** Name, price ($110), star rating (4.5 stars, 394 reviews), BOGO deal reminder, and the Slip-ins badge all visible above the fold. I know exactly what I'm looking at.
- **ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, large, bright blue, sticky at the bottom. My arthritic thumbs appreciate it. Hard to miss.
- **Popup friction was minimal.** Step 2 showed a simple banner swap ("Get Mom a Gift") — no intrusive modal covering the screen, no aggressive overlay to fight through. That's respectful.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart is a blank white screen. Still.** Step 8 is completely empty. Nothing. No items, no total, no checkout button. This is the same catastrophic failure as yesterday. I picked a shoe, went to buy it, and got nothing. That's a lost sale. Every time.
- **Login means absolutely nothing.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 are visually identical. After "logging in," there's no "Hi Walker," no Plus member badge, no points balance, no "Welcome back" — nothing. The account icon in the header looks the same logged out as logged in. I have no idea if I'm even signed in.
- **Search ignores that I'm a man.** I searched "slip in" and the first two results shown are both labeled "Women's." I'm a 62-year-old guy who just navigated through the Men's section. The site knows nothing about me. 1,030 results with no Men's pre-filter applied is overwhelming.
- **Size selector didn't auto-remember.** Step 7 shows a "Please select a size" error — no saved size preference from my account, even though I should be logged in. A personalized experience would pre-select my usual size or at least hint at it.
- **Cart count stuck at 0 through the add-to-cart flow.** The cart badge shows 0 in Step 7 even while I'm on the add-to-cart screen. No visual feedback that anything is in progress. Confusing for someone who second-guesses themselves on the phone.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page. Full stop.** This is Priority One. Nothing else matters if the checkout experience returns a blank screen. Investigate whether this is a JavaScript render failure or a session/auth issue tied to the logged-in state.
2. **Show me I'm logged in, immediately.** After login, display "Hi Walker" or at minimum a filled-in account icon. Show my Plus points somewhere on the homepage or header. I earned those points — show them to me.
3. **Apply Men's filter to search results by default when I'm logged in as a male customer.** Or at minimum, surface a "Men's" quick-filter chip at the top of search results. Don't show me Women's shoes first.
4. **Save size preference in my account.** If I've bought from Skechers before as a Plus member, pre-select my size or surface it prominently on the product page.
5. **Add "Men's Slip-ins" as a persistent homepage card** for logged-in male users — not just a scrolled-to section, but a dedicated card in the hero area.

Today was better than yesterday getting *to* the shoe. The homepage, nav, and product page held up. But I still couldn't buy anything because the cart is broken. And not once did this site make me feel like a known customer. I'm a loyal Plus member who came specifically for Slip-ins — the site should have rolled out the red carpet. ## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey audited: Homepage → Login → Men category → Men > Shoes → PDP → Add to Cart → View Cart → Search. Primary concerns are Kasada bot-protection rate limiting surfacing to real sessions, a broken View Cart page (missing foundational HTML attributes), a Stylitics CORS failure, and an extremely heavy third-party script payload with duplicate loads.

---

## Accessibility

**16 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (9 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — ARIA role used without required ancestor. Likely a carousel or listbox widget rendered outside its expected container.
- `link-name` [serious] — Icon-only links with no `aria-label` or visible text. Screen readers will announce the URL or be silent.
- `heading-order` [moderate] — Heading hierarchy skipped (e.g., `h1` → `h3`). Common with hero components injected by Monetate/personalization.
- `landmark-*` violations (4) — Duplicate `<main>`, nested `<aside>` inside `<main>`, and uncontained content. Suggests template-level structure is not audited against landmark rules.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — `role` value applied to element type that prohibits it.

**View Cart (4 violations — highest severity):**
- `document-title` [serious] and `html-has-lang` [serious] — The cart page is missing both `<title>` and `lang="en"`. This indicates the cart renders in a separate document context (likely an iframe or SPA shell) where the base HTML template is not applied. This is a regression.
- `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<main>` or `<h1>`. Confirms the cart document is structurally incomplete.

**Search (3 violations):** Landmark nesting and containment issues, same class as Homepage.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`** — Googlebot will index this as an untitled document if it crawls it. Noindex is not confirmed in the data, so this is a risk.
- **View Cart missing `lang` attribute** — Hreflang signals and language detection break for this URL.
- **Heading order violations** on Homepage signal that content injected by Monetate/personalization is not coordinated with the base template's heading structure. This degrades semantic signal for crawlers.
- TTFB values (24–33ms) are excellent across non-cart pages — CDN edge caching is working correctly.

**View Cart TTFB spike (359ms):** 10–14× higher than all other pages. The simultaneous 429 on `/cart/` in the network log is the direct cause — the server rate-limited the session before serving the document. Whether this was a bot-protection false positive or a genuine capacity issue is unclear, but it produced a degraded user session.

**Third-party script bloat (Homepage head — partial list):**
TrustArc, TikTok pixel (×2 files), Pinterest, Google Tag Manager (×3 destination tags + 1 gtag.js), Bing, Yahoo UTC (loaded **twice** — duplicate), Reddit, Quantum Metric, Impact, CreativeCDN, Bloomreach tracker, Monetate (×2 entry points), Snapchat, Facebook Events, NextDoor, Taboola, Stapecdn, Pulse Insights, go2sdk/Tune, Stylitics, WebFontLoader from cdnjs.

All are `async`, which is correct, but the sheer count creates main-thread contention post-load. Yahoo (`s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js`) and CreativeCDN (`tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js`) are each **loaded twice** — identical `<script>` tags duplicated in the document.

**`--vh: 6.640000000000001px`** — JavaScript is computing viewport height and writing it as a CSS custom property. The floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` instead of `6.64`) is a minor artifact but indicates the value is not being rounded before assignment.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` custom property pattern is a known workaround for mobile browser address bar height variation. Its presence is expected, but floating-point precision should be clamped (`toFixed(2)` or similar).
- No LCP data means it is unknown whether the above-the-fold image (likely a hero banner served by Monetate) is properly prioritized with `fetchpriority="high"` and `loading="eager"`. This is the single highest-impact unknown in the dataset.
- `vice-managed-src` on several scripts (Facebook, Pinterest, Yahoo, NextDoor, Taboola, Impact) is the consent management system (likely OneTrust/TrustArc) deferring load until consent. This is correct behavior, but means these scripts have no `src` on initial parse — they cannot be preloaded.

---

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada 429s (5 network, 6 console):**
The endpoint `/{uuid}/{uuid}/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.386` is Kasada's client-side fingerprinting for bot detection. This endpoint is rate-limited — the session generated 5+ requests to it and Kasada's server-side policy rejected them. In a real user session this would mean bot-protection challenges could appear or cart functionality could degrade (the `/cart/` 429 is likely downstream of Kasada blocking the session token).

**Root cause:** The fingerprint poller is firing at a rate Kasada's own rate limit rejects. This is a Kasada configuration issue — either the polling interval is too aggressive, or the session token is not being cached client-side between page navigations, causing redundant re-fingerprinting on each page load.

**Stylitics CORS failure:**
```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=0a2c7cd600'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics is an outfit/shoppable-look widget vendor. Their CDN is not returning the CORS header for this stylesheet. This is a **vendor-side misconfiguration** but it breaks Skechers' product widget. The `ERR_FAILED` in the console is the consequence. The affected feature (likely "Complete the Look" on PDP) is silently broken.
