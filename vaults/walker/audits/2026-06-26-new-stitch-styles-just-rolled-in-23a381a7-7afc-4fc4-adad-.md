# New Stitch styles just rolled in 🌊
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Well, I know Crocs — I've been getting their emails all month, mostly that buy-two-save-20% deal they keep recycling. So I opened this one because at least the subject was different. Then I saw what it was: Disney's Stitch. Blue cartoon dog clogs with little character charms. Look, I'm 62, not 12. I wear Crocs because they slip on easy and my feet don't ache by noon — not because I want a stuffed alien riding my shoe.
- The email itself is clean and loads fast. Big hero photo, simple layout, readable buttons. The "Surf, Sun, Stitch" headline is catchy if you're the right audience. I'm not. There's no price, no discount, no comfort story — just "here's a Disney product, go shop." After six straight emails hammering me with tiered discount math, I'll give them credit for trying something different. But "different" and "for me" aren't the same thing.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Won't click, won't forward — unless I have a grandkid who's obsessed with Stitch, which I don't.

## What's working

- **Clean, fast layout.** One hero image, a tagline, four clear buttons — nothing fighting for my attention or loading slowly on my phone.
- **"Shop Sale" button is visible.** Even buried among three other CTAs, it's right there. That's the button I'd tap if anything dragged me in.
- **Subject line breaks the pattern.** After weeks of discount emails, a new product launch feels fresher. I noticed it in my inbox for that reason alone.
- **Seasonal tie-in lands.** "Surf, Sun" fits late June. Doesn't feel stale or out of season.

## What's weak

- **This is a kids' / Disney-fan product pushed to a 62-year-old man.** No comfort angle, no arch support mention, no cushioning story — just cartoon charms. Wrong audience entirely.
- **No offer.** Every Crocs email this month had a discount. This one has nothing. "Shop Sale" is a ghost — where does it go? What's on sale? I have no idea without clicking.
- **Four CTAs with no clear primary.** Shop Clogs, Shop Jibbitz Charms, Shop Sale, Shop Sandals — they all look identical. My eye doesn't know where to go first.
- **The preview text is broken.** All I saw in my inbox next to the subject was garbled invisible characters. That's a bad first impression before I even opened it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send.** Disney collab emails should go to buyers who've purchased novelty styles before — not the whole list. I've never bought a Jibbitz charm. I shouldn't be getting this.
- 2. **Lead with a comfort hook if you must send broadly.** "Classic Clog cushioning, now in a Stitch print" still sells the character fan AND reminds me why I buy Crocs in the first place.
- 3. **Consolidate to one CTA.** Pick the highest-intent destination — probably "Shop New Arrivals" — and make it the hero button. Save the secondary links for a scrollable shelf below.
- 4. **Fix the preview text.** Blank/invisible preheader is a wasted line. Use it.
- 5. **Add a price anchor.** Even "From $59.99" does work. Right now the email has zero price signal.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (7 affected images):**

All footer images are served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:

| YouTube | `1187ef58-07fe-4985-8349-93e338d86694.png` |

Gmail (since 2017), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook.com all block or proxy HTTP image sources. These images will show as broken in a large share of the audience.

**Missing alt on tracking pixel:**

```html
<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1"
  src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">
```

No `alt=""` attribute. Technically harmless since it's hidden, but trips accessibility linters and some corporate mail scanners.

**Malformed meta charset tag:**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, this tag is ignored — the charset declaration is a no-op. Should be:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

UTF-8 content without a declared charset can corrupt special characters (emoji in subject line, `™` in "Jibbitz™") in older or strict-mode clients.

**`<style>` block inside `<body>`:**

```html
</head>
<body class="email-body">
<style> div.preheader { display: none !important; } </style>
```

The preheader suppression style is injected at body-open rather than in `<head>`. Gmail and some Outlook versions strip `<style>` tags inside `<body>`, which could expose the preheader text as visible body content.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` missing `content` attribute:**

```html
<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting" />
```

Technically accepted by Apple Mail without `content=""`, but the spec expects `content=""`. Low risk but worth correcting for forward compatibility.
