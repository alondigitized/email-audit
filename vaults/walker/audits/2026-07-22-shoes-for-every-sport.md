# Shoes for every sport 👟🏆
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Another Famous Footwear in the inbox — and at this point I recognize their sender the way I recognize my neighbor's pickup truck. The big pink BOGO graphic hits me right away and I know the deal is still running, same one they kicked off on July 16. So far so good. Then I get into the body and the whole thing is organized like a sports catalog: Basketball. Running. Training. Recovery. I'm 62 years old. I don't play basketball. I'm not training for anything. The only section in this email that speaks to my feet is the Recovery slides, and they're buried about two-thirds down behind a parade of high-tops and track spikes.
- Before any of that — the preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL. A long chain of random characters and numbers. That's the very first thing I read after the subject line, and it looks broken. If I weren't already curious whether the BOGO was still alive, that preview would've been the end of it.
- I'll mark it read. Won't click through, won't forward to anyone. If I want to browse comfort shoes, I'll go find the filter myself.

## What's working

- **BOGO graphic is impossible to miss.** Big, high-contrast, and sitting right at the top — I don't even need my glasses to catch it.
- **SHOP MEN button is right underneath the hero.** One tap to the right section, no hunting around for where the men's shoes actually live.
- **Recovery section is in there.** Slides and cushioned recovery shoes are exactly my category — I appreciate that they included it, even buried.
- **3X points on Birkenstock callout.** That's real value for a loyalty member choosing between two brands at a similar price point.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL.** A string of encoded junk showed up in my inbox before I even opened it. That's a technical mistake and it looks sloppy — like the email fell out of bed before it got dressed.
- **"Every sport" isn't my sport.** Basketball leads. Then running. Then training. Then, finally, Recovery. My category gets fourth billing in an email theoretically addressed to a shoe shopper like me.
- **Same BOGO since July 16.** They ran this exact offer six days ago and it scored high then because it was fresh. Now it's starting to feel like a store that never takes the clearance banner down. The offer's real, but the urgency is deflating.
- **Sport models don't look like me.** Every hero section has athletic builds in performance gear. I'm not seeing anyone my age, my pace, or my wardrobe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text before the next deploy.** Swap in a readable line — anything human. Even "BOGO 1/2 off all weekend — see what's new" is miles better than a URL dump.
- 2. **Move Recovery to the top or add a "Comfort & Casual" hero section.** If 30% of your subscriber list is comfort shoppers over 55, they shouldn't have to scroll past basketball to feel seen.
- 3. **Put an expiry date on the BOGO.** Floating "BOGO 1/2 off" with no deadline is an offer without a clock. "Ends Sunday" gets me off the couch.
- 4. **Name one specific product I already know.** A Birkenstock Arizona, a Hoka Bondi, a New Balance 990 — anchor me to something I recognize and I'm far more likely to click than if you just say "Shop Men."

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Shoes for every sport" (2026-07-22)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a functional legacy HTML 4.01 template with no catastrophic failures, but carries a high-severity deliverability risk from an oversaturated plain-text part and 25 images with missing alt text across the full email body.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL saturation (HIGH)**
QA reports 10,752 of 13,654 plain-text characters are URLs — **79% URL density**. This is well above the ~30% threshold that spam filters treat as suspicious. Plain-text parts generated from click-wrapped HTML tend to produce this pattern when the ESP doesn't strip redirect URLs in the text version. Risk: deliverability degradation, especially on corporate mail servers.

**34 click-tracked links — all skipped by probe**
Every link routes through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. QA could not validate any destination URL, so broken-link detection provides zero coverage for this send. This is an operational gap, not a bug in the email itself.

**Open pixel present**
Pixel at `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?VU4UBB7FMKJUTD75JPIJTI3QWQ.70253` is correctly sized (1×1), blank alt, and positioned before the main table — standard.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta (HIGH)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disabling pinch-zoom violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is disallowed by Apple's accessibility guidelines. Users with low vision cannot zoom the email.

**25 images missing alt text (MEDIUM)**
QA flagged every content image — product shots, the animated GIF (`721856e1`), brand logos, and the spacer/divider (`4ccff4ad`, referenced 4 times). The HTML source shows `alt=""` on all of them. For purely decorative spacers that is acceptable, but product and CTA images require descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked rendering.

Notable instances:
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — `alt=""` on a linked brand logo
- Animated hero: `721856e1-1af4-4bc2-bac9-f51f882b5e40.gif` — `alt=""` on primary content
- 10+ product images: `557b4ca5`, `a998f7e2`, `278f55d4`, etc. — all `alt=""`

**Malformed charset declaration (LOW)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without it, the charset hint is ignored by parsers. The `<head>` has no fallback `<meta charset="UTF-8">`. Actual rendering relies on the server's Content-Type header and the email client's default — risk of character misinterpretation in non-Latin locales.

**CSS typo — invalid property (LOW)**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (should be `line-height`). The intended line spacing is silently dropped on that `<td>`.

**Legacy DOCTYPE (INFO)**
`HTML 4.01 Transitional` is intentional for broad email client compatibility — no action needed.
