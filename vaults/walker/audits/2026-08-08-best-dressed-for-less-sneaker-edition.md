# Best Dressed for Less: Sneaker Edition
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction scrolling this one was déjà vu — didn't I already see a Nike "Family Sale" banner from these folks yesterday? Feels like the same red-and-black smudge graphic just got recycled into a new subject line. The "Back to School Sneaker Superlatives" bit lost me immediately too — I'm retired, my grandkids are the ones buying backpacks, not me.
- That said, buried in the middle there's a Brooks section that actually made me stop scrolling — "Price Break," starting at $89.98, with the Defyance Max and a plain black pair that looks like it'd be easy on my feet. Brooks is a brand I actually cross-shop, so that's the one panel in this whole email that felt aimed at somebody like me instead of at a teenager.
- Would I click? Only on the Brooks button, and only because I already trust that brand. The rest — New Balance modeled by a young gal in leopard-print energy, a Nike collage, a school-themed headline — I'd scroll past and probably just check my Shoe Perks balance out of habit. Not forwarding this one to anybody.

## What's working

- The Brooks "Price Break" panel shows real shoes at a real starting price ($89.98) — that's the kind of straight talk I want.
- Shoe Perks members get a free $10 reward through 8/31 — clear, dated, and I'm already in that program so it registered.
- The $20 off $99.98+ / $10 off $74.98+ coupon codes are laid out plainly at the bottom with an actual code (SAVEMORE) instead of making me hunt for it.
- Layout's clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, easy to read on my phone without zooming.

## What's weak

- "Back to School Sneaker Superlatives" as the lead theme tells me on sight this email isn't built for a 62-year-old — I'm long past school shopping.
- The hero model for New Balance is a young woman in leopard print sitting on a crate — striking photo, wrong customer.
- This is now two days running with a Nike "Family Sale" push (yesterday's email had the same red banner energy) — feels like the same promo just reshuffled.
- Nothing at the top of the email — before I even scroll — tells me there's a real discount waiting; I have to scroll past the whole yearbook bit first.
- No men's casual/comfort language anywhere — no mention of cushioning, arch support, slip-on ease, just "turn heads" and "fly off shelves" copy aimed at a younger shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Brooks comfort panel, not the back-to-school theme — that's the one product actually in my wheelhouse, so put it first and add a line about cushioning or all-day comfort.
- 2. Drop "Back to School" entirely for a subject/hero that doesn't automatically exclude half your list who don't have kids in school.
- 3. Fix the preview text — right now it's just spilling out a raw tracking link instead of real copy, which looks broken in the inbox.
- 4. Stop running the same Nike Family Sale creative two sends in a row — rotate it or fold it into one send instead of two near-identical ones.
- 5. Put the actual dollar-off coupon (the $20/$10 codes) higher up, above the fold, instead of making me scroll through three brand panels to find it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Shoe Perks $10 Reward + Up to 30% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save on Brooks, New Balance & Nike — plus your $10 member reward`
