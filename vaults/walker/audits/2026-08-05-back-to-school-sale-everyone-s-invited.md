# Back to School Sale, Everyone's Invited
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction scrolling this one: cute little retro computer-window graphics, but "Back to School Sale, Everyone's Invited" — everyone except me, seems like. I don't have kids in the house buying backpacks and school shoes anymore, so a wall of globes, notebooks, paint palettes, and beakers doesn't pull me in one bit. I kept scrolling waiting for something for me, and buried down in that markdown grid there actually was one — the Men's Skechers Slip-ins Relaxed Fit Altman, $55.99 marked down from $75. That's exactly my kind of shoe, hands-free and comfortable, but it's the third item in a six-pack grid, no bigger than the kids' sneakers around it.
- Skechers has been in my inbox constantly this week — this is at least the fourth or fifth email since Sunday, and I flagged the cadence problem back on 2026-07-24 already. Nothing here changes my mind on that. I'd scroll past this one, not click, and definitely not forward it — there's nothing here worth sharing with a buddy. Mark as read and move on.
- I will say the "up to 40% off select styles" banner near the bottom at least widens the net past kids' stuff, and that's the part that could've earned a click if it'd been up top instead of buried under three back-to-school banners first.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 40% OFF SELECT STYLES" banner is a real, clear discount that isn't limited to kids' gear.
- The markdown grid at the bottom actually has a men's slip-in in it — Altman, $55.99 — which is squarely my kind of shoe.
- Clean layout, nothing broken or overlapping, big legible buttons like "SHOP NOW" that are easy to tap on my phone.
- The retro "computer window" visual gimmick is kind of fun and different from their usual look — gave it a beat of attention before I realized it wasn't for me.

## What's weak

- The whole top two-thirds — hero, "Kids' Styles 25% off," Girls banner, Boys banner — is 100% not aimed at a 62-year-old guy shopping for himself.
- Subject line "Everyone's Invited" oversells it — I'm clearly not the invite list here.
- The preview text in my inbox was a garbled mess of code (some kind of "schema.org/Organization" junk), not a real sentence — looked like a glitch before I even opened it.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or slip-in comfort anywhere in the hero copy — the men's shoe that fits my life is an afterthought in a small grid.
- Fourth or fifth Skechers email this week — I'm getting numb to opening these.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send: back-to-school kids' promo goes to parents; comfort-shopping guys like me get our own email leading with the "up to 40% off" adult styles and the Slip-ins line — don't make me hunt for it in a grid.
- 2. Fix that preview text — right now it's showing raw code/schema markup instead of real copy, which looks broken before the email's even open.
- 3. Move the Men's Slip-ins Altman markdown up near the top if you're going to send this version to me at all — lead with the comfort shoe, not bury it as item three of six.
- 4. Ease off the frequency — this is close to daily at this point and it's making me tune out even the sends that would matter to me.
- 5. Add a line about arch support or all-day comfort somewhere in the copy — that's the hook that gets my thumb moving, not "Geography, English, Art, Science."

# Technical Audit: "Back to School Sale, Everyone's Invited" (Skechers)

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) transactional template (`click.emails.skechers.com`, `image.emails.skechers.com`) with multiple third-party tracking pixels; automated QA found 0 hard failures but 13 warnings, concentrated in insecure (HTTP) image sources and missing alt attributes.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **35 tracking/click-redirect links skipped from HTTP probe** — link destinations behind `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects were not resolved, so final landing pages are unverified (QA WARN).
- **Malformed pixel URL — missing URI scheme:** `<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" .../>` has no `http://`, `https://`, or `//` prefix. In an email client this resolves as a relative path against no valid base, so the Return Path/Validity pixel will not fire in most clients. This was not caught by the automated warnings and should be fixed to `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/...`.
- Open-tracking beacon present and correctly formed: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?Q4QVXP3RXNEENIJJ6B6QHBM53A.100224&d=100224&bmt=0`.
- Krux/Kantar beacons (`beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif` ×2, `1x1_ad_impression.gif`) are HTTPS and well-formed.
- Ink1000 engagement pixel (`http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/...`) uses HTTP — flagged separately in §3.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **9 images served over HTTP instead of HTTPS**, including the primary Skechers logo (`dde00662...png`), a content image, app-store badges, payment/store icons, and social icons, plus the `ink1000.com` tracking pixel. Mixed content on an HTTPS-rendered email client (Gmail/Outlook web) can cause these to be blocked or proxy-rewritten, risking a broken logo/hero image. (QA WARN, 9 instances)
- **2 images missing `alt` text:** the `ink1000.com` open-tracking gif (low impact, decorative) and `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png`, which appears to be a content image, not decorative — this one should get descriptive alt text for screen-reader users. (QA WARN)
- Template uses dual `.mobile-content`/`.desktop-content` blocks toggled via `@media (min-width: 768px)` display rules — standard responsive pattern, no defects observed in the provided markup.
- Legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype and extensive Outlook/`mso` conditional-style resets are present, consistent with standard ESP boilerplate — no issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No AMPscript (`%%...%%`), Handlebars, or merge-tag tokens are present in the provided source. No issues found in the visible markup (note: source is truncated, so body content beyond the header/tracking block was not reviewed).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)

This should be confirmed against the full message source before sign-off, as CAN-SPAM requires a visible unsubscribe mechanism and postal address in every commercial email.
