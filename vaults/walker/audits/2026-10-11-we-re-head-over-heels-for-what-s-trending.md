## Executive summary

- Here we go again. This is the seventh or so Shoe Carnival email this week, and the orange "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" coupon block is back on top, same as 10/05 and 10/10. The subject is "Head Over Heels," so I expected something about shoes for my feet. What I got was a young woman in tall brown boots, a suede mule clog, a lady on a yellow stool in patchwork boots, and women's "Mountain Blain" clogs. There's not one man and not one word about cushioning or arch support.
- I'm 62 and I want a cushioned slip-on. A fashion trend piece doesn't speak to me. The $10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98 deal is fine, but it's the same code (SAVEMORE) I've seen all week. The mule and clog pictures were the closest thing to my kind of shoe, and they're women's styles.
- Would I click? Probably not. Would I forward it? No, nobody in my circle wants "Prints Charming" boots. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The coupon block up top is big and easy to read: $10 off $74.98 or $25 off $149.98, with the code SAVEMORE. I get the deal in two seconds.
- The type is large, and the orange and yellow banners are easy on my eyes on a phone. I didn't need to zoom on the headlines.
- The wallet coupons with barcodes and the "Shoe Perks members get a $10 reward" box at the bottom are handy. They'd save me digging for a code at the register.
- The email matches the Shoe Carnival look I know, so I trust it's the real thing.
- The seasonal boots, mules and clogs fit the fall weather.

## What's weak

- Every model and every shoe is women's. The only things aimed at a man like me are a "MENS" link in the bottom nav and a tiny "Kids' Boots" block that isn't for me either.
- I see nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on convenience. It's all style talk like "Prints Charming" and "Patchwork Please."
- The "Shop Leopard," "Shop Mules" and "Shop Patchwork" links are small underlined text under the pictures. Those are hard targets to tap with my thumb.
- The coupon is the same one I've seen all week, so it feels stale. The fine print is also a huge wall of tiny text with exclusions, and I can't read it without zooming.
- It's long, with a BOGO block, kids' boots, Afterpay, an SMS signup and a Word game. I lost track of what the one main message was.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender and category. A 62-year-old man should get a men's comfort or walking-shoe hero, not women's boots. Even a single "Men's Comfort Slip-Ons" panel with Skechers, Hoka or Brooks would have grabbed me.
- 2. Turn the "Shop ___" text links into real buttons with a verb and a noun, like "Shop Men's Slip-Ons." Make them big enough for a thumb.
- 3. Change the offer every few days, or add a credible deadline, so the coupon block doesn't feel like a repeat. I'd also add a short line of plain-spoken proof, like a star rating or "cushioned all-day comfort."
- 4. Fix the preheader. It shows a tracking link instead of real copy.
- 5. Cut the lower half down to one clear message plus the coupons. It's too much for a phone.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The QA pass reported 100% with 0 issues, but the INFO items hide a few real defects: a tracking-pixel URL with an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder, a zero-width character inside another pixel URL, and malformed `<meta>` tags. The HTML was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and body links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved placeholder:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` has a literal `[UNIQUE]` in the query string. This looks like an unsubstituted ESP token. The open or unique-ID pixel probably isn't recording per-recipient data.
- **Zero-width character in URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261011_bogof_clogs_AM​` ends with an invisible U+200B after `_AM`. This can corrupt the campaign value or break the request, depending on how the redirector parses it.
- **Malformed `t=` parameter:** `t=utm_campaign=SCA_…` nests a second `=` inside the value, with no `&`/`%3D` separation. Whether the tracker parses it is unverified.
- **Unprobed links:** 32 tracking links were skipped as click-redirect domains, so their destinations and status codes are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** The two `emltrk.com` images (`ieezzy5m`, `0di86k1v`) have no alt. They are tracking pixels, so use `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`) so screen readers skip them.
- **Malformed meta tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and a malformed content value.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, so it does nothing.
- **Doctype mismatch:** The doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional while the markup uses `xmlns` and XHTML-style self-closing tags. This is harmless in most clients but inconsistent.
- **Links lose their styling:** The global rule `a, a:link, a:visited … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important }` strips underline and color from every link. Any inline text link is visually indistinguishable unless it sits in a button or styled wrapper. I couldn't check the body markup because of the truncation.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text { font-size:12px !important }` at max-width 600px is below the usual 14px minimum for mobile body text.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` is the only unresolved token found. No other tokens were visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
No issues found in the data provided. The footer and unsubscribe link were cut off, and no SPF/DKIM/DMARC or `List-Unsubscribe` headers were supplied, so I can't confirm compliance either way. The From domain is `email.shoecarnival.com`.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM seen is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261011_bogof_clogs_AM`, and it sits inside the pixel's `t=` parameter. I couldn't confirm that destination links carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium` or `utm_campaign`.
- The campaign name (`bogof_clogs`) doesn't obviously match the subject ("What's Trending"). I can't tell from the source whether this is a naming error or a segment or variant tag.
- Landing pages weren't probed, because the tracking links were skipped.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the ESP template so `[UNIQUE]` resolves to a real value in `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=…`. Send a test and confirm the rendered pixel URL.
2. Remove the U+200B from the `utm_campaign=SCA_20261011_bogof_clogs_AM` value and URL-encode the nested `=` in `t=`.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Replace the two meta tags with `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
5. Restore underline or color on inline text links, and raise the mobile `.responsive-text` size to at least 14px.
6. Re-run the audit on the full HTML. Resolve a sample of the 32 tracking links to verify destination UTMs, and check the footer, unsubscribe link and auth headers.
