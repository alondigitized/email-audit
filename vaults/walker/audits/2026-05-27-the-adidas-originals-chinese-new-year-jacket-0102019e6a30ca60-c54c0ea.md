# The adidas Originals Chinese New Year Jacket
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- I've lost count of how many adidas emails I've opened this spring — easily north of a dozen — and this one stopped me cold for the wrong reason. Chinese New Year jacket. In late May. Chinese New Year was back in January. That alone tells me somebody at adidas scheduled this send and forgot to pull it, or just doesn't care. Either way, it doesn't make me feel like a valued customer — it makes me feel like a name on a blast list.
- The email has two personalities and neither one is mine. The top half is a heritage fashion jacket worn by two young people who look like they're heading to a fashion week after-party. The bottom half pivots hard into a gift card deal — buy a $100 card for $80 — which is actually not a bad offer if you're an adidas loyalist stocking up. But those two halves don't belong together, and by the time I scrolled to the gift card section, I'd already mentally checked out.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Definitely not. I'd file this under "adidas is at it again" and move on to whatever's actually on sale at DSW.

## What's working

- **The gift card offer is concrete and visible.** "$100 gift card for $80" — that's real math I can do in my head without squinting, and the bold "SPEND LESS / GET MORE" headline is hard to miss.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loaded clean. Images showed up, text didn't overlap, layout was stable on screen.
- **Subject line was short and specific.** Under 45 characters, plain language — I knew exactly what I was clicking into even if I didn't want it.

## What's weak

- **Chinese New Year content landing in late May is embarrassing.** The cultural event it's built around was four months ago. This feels like a scheduling error that nobody caught.
- **Young models, wrong demographic for me.** Those two folks look like they're in their mid-twenties at most. Nothing in the visual says this jacket is for a 62-year-old who wants something easy to pull on and wear all day.
- **The email can't decide what it's selling.** Jacket up top, gift card in the middle, dense fine print at the bottom. The jacket never gets a real CTA, and the gift card offer feels bolted on like an afterthought from a separate campaign.
- **No footwear, no comfort angle, nothing about fit or feel.** I cross-shop adidas occasionally for walking shoes. There's zero in this email for that.
- **The adidas volume is getting exhausting.** I just audited an email from them two days ago. At this cadence, they're training me to ignore their sender name.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull this send entirely or reschedule for January 2027.** A Chinese New Year jacket arriving in May is a credibility hit. If the gift card deal is the real offer, lead with that and drop the jacket hook.
- 2. **If keeping the gift card offer, make it the hero.** Ditch the jacket entirely and build an email around the gift card with a clear CTA — "Stock up before summer styles sell out." Ties the deal to a reason to act now.
- 3. **Show me who the jacket is actually for.** If adidas wants to keep the heritage story, use models who look like the people actually wearing adidas Originals — a broader age range, a lifestyle shot rather than fashion editorial.
- 4. **Trim the fine print block.** There's a wall of small text at the bottom that's unreadable on mobile. Link to terms instead.
