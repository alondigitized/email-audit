# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-05**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old guy who wants a cushioned, slip-on shoe, and Skechers should be the easy answer for that. The front door and the Men's Shoes page did a decent job. The "Slip-ins / Wide Fit / Arch Fit / Max Cushioning" chips across the top, the "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile and the product page all speak my language. The trip fell apart when I tried to buy. My add-to-cart attempt got "Please select a size" and nothing was added. The cart page then came up as a **completely blank white screen**. I never saw a login take effect either, and the first "comfort shoes" results were women's styles. I like the shoe, but I couldn't finish the purchase.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10** (yesterday: 3/10)

This is a small bump, and I'd call it a tie. The browsing half of the site is solid. The checkout half is where a sale gets lost. I only have today's screenshots, so I can't point to anything that got worse since yesterday.

## 3. What's Working

- **My needs are right at the top of the homepage.** The chips for Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit and Max Cushioning are exactly what I shop for. That's the strongest thing on the site.
- **The Men menu is easy to use.** It slides in with big rows, plain labels and arrows. The "Comfort Technologies" row means I don't have to guess. It's easy to tap with a thumb.
- **The Men's Shoes page puts "Hands Free Slip-Ins" first**, with Walking Shoes next to it. The Filter and Sort buttons are easy to find.
- **The product page is clear.** It shows a big price ($88.00), 66 reviews at about 4.5 stars, the Slip-ins badge, and a big shoe photo.
- **The size picker is easy to read.** Sold-out sizes are crossed out, so I can see at a glance that 7.5, 8.0, 8.5 and 9.0 are available.
- **The Add to Cart button is large and bold.** It's the easiest thing to tap on the whole site.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" returned relevant products.** Both results I could see were comfort-oriented (Contour Foam, Glide-Step Comfort). 1,590 results is a lot, but they're on topic.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart screen was blank white.** I got no items, no total and no "your cart is empty" message. If this is a real load failure, shoppers are leaving here.
- **Add to Cart didn't add anything.** The red "Please select a size" error was showing and the cart badge still read 0. The error is fair, since I hadn't picked a size, but the page didn't scroll me to it or highlight it. I'd also like a size pre-selected from my past orders.
- **I couldn't tell whether I was logged in.** The Log In step looks the same as the homepage, with no greeting, no name and no "Welcome back." Nothing felt personalized to me.
- **The pop-up step changed almost nothing.** The one visible change was the banner switching to "The Fall Guide: SHOP NOW."
- **The text is small.** The promo bars ("Limited Time Only! 25% off Apparel & Bags…") and the review count are tiny. I'd have to pinch-zoom. The "Shop | Details" links are small and close together.
- **The header icons are small and tightly packed.** Search, account and cart sit close together, and the cart badge is cramped.
- **The "notify me" alarm icons on sold-out sizes are very small.**
- **"BOGO 50% – Applied at Cart" makes me do the math.** The $88 price is shown alone. I want to see what two pairs cost, because price-versus-quality is how I decide.
- **No comparison with Hoka, Brooks or New Balance anywhere**, and no arch-support or cushioning explanation on the pages I saw.
- **The search results led with women's shoes** ("Women's" is printed on both), even though I'd been browsing Men's. The page wasn't tuned to me.
- **The Men's Shoes page shows 749 results** with nothing to help me narrow them down.
- **The hero image is two models, with no headline and no comfort message.** It looks nice but tells me nothing.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It must always show either items or a clear empty-cart message. Check whether this blank screen is a real bug or a timing problem in the test.
2. **Make a missing size obvious.** Scroll to the size row, highlight it, and keep the Add to Cart button active. After a successful add, show a confirmation with a "View Cart" button, and update the badge.
3. **Show the BOGO math on the product page**, for example "2 pairs: $132 ($66 each)."
4. **Make the login visible.** Show "Hi, [name]" in the header, and use past sizes and widths to pre-select my size.
5. **Raise the small text to at least 14–16px** and give the header icons and promo links bigger tap areas. Keep the whole row tappable.
6. **Keep my gender context in search.** If I came from Men's, show men's results first and offer a Men/Women toggle.
7. **Put a one-line comfort message on the hero**, such as "Hands-free. Cushioned. Arch-supported," and add an arch-support/cushioning comparison to the product page.
8. **Add a "Compare cushioning" or "Why Skechers vs. Hoka/Brooks" panel.** I cross-shop all of them.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right shoe and the right words for me. The site gets me to a $88 slip-on I'd consider, and then I hit an error and a blank cart. A comfort shopper at my age won't debug a checkout. I'd close the tab and look at Hoka or Brooks. I'm giving it a point more than yesterday for the browsing and search, but I wouldn't call that a win until the cart works.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audit is partly compromised by bot-mitigation rate limiting (HTTP 429 from Kasada, `x-kpsdk`). The automated browser was challenged repeatedly, and the final cart request returned 429.

- **Cart results are unreliable.** The 4 View Cart axe violations (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) match a bare error response, not a rendered cart page. Treat them as an artifact of the 429 until re-tested.
- **Navigation may not have happened.** The HTML samples for Log In and Men category both report `https://www.skechers.com/`, the same URL as the homepage. Those steps likely stayed on the homepage, so their results duplicate the homepage.
- **LCP and CLS are missing for every step.** Core Web Vitals are not assessable from this run.
- **Homepage defects.** The homepage has two critical (`aria-required-parent`, `button-name`) and one serious (`link-name`) accessibility violation. It also has a landmark structure problem (duplicate and nested `<main>`).

## Accessibility

18 violations total (2 critical, 3 serious, 11 moderate, 2 minor). The distinct problems are:

- **Homepage, critical:**
  - `button-name`: buttons have no accessible name.
  - `aria-required-parent`: ARIA child roles sit outside their required parent, such as `listitem` outside `list`, or `tab` and `menuitem` outside their containers.
- **Homepage, serious:** `link-name`: links have no discernible text, probably icon-only or image-only links.
- **Landmark structure (Homepage and Search):**
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level`: there is more than one `<main>`, and at least one is nested. This is consistent with a server-rendered shell wrapping a second injected `<main>`.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`: complementary landmarks are nested, landmarks lack unique labels, and content sits outside any landmark. These repeat on the Search page, so the cause is a shared template.
- **Homepage, moderate:** `heading-order`: heading levels are skipped.
- **Homepage and Search, minor:** `aria-allowed-role`: a role is set on an element that doesn't allow it.
- **View Cart:** the four violations above are probably caused by the 429 response. Re-test.
- **Not covered:** Log In, Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart show no violations. That is either because they were clean or, for the first two, because they were never actually reached.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`.
- `<title>`, meta description, canonical tag, structured data and heading hierarchy are not visible in the truncated source, so they can't be assessed.
- `heading-order` and the multiple `<main>` elements on the homepage can make the document outline ambiguous for crawlers.
- The 429 on `/cart/` suggests crawlers and automation can be rate-limited. A shopper's cart page isn't indexed anyway, so the impact is low.

## Performance

- **TTFB (server response):**
  - Homepage, Log In and Men category: 406–412 ms. That is acceptable, though above the ~200 ms target for CDN-cached HTML.
  - View Cart: 128 ms.
  - The other steps: 25–30 ms. That is cache-level or SPA-style navigation, not full document loads.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured for any step. The collector should be fixed, and CLS and LCP should be measured with `PerformanceObserver` after a settle period.
- **Third-party script load:**
  - The `<head>` injects about 30 async tag scripts. They include GTM, Google Ads and Floodlight, Bing, TikTok, Pinterest, Reddit, Snapchat, Meta, Taboola, Nextdoor, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House, Monetate, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach and Tune.
  - Men category adds still more ad-pixel tags, so third-party weight grows through the journey.
  - Main-thread contention is likely, which would hurt INP and TBT.
- **Fonts:** the font load depends on `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs. The `wf-*-active` classes confirm this JS-driven approach, which risks FOUT and layout shift and adds a third-party dependency on the critical path. Self-hosted `@font-face` with `font-display` and `preload` would remove it.
- **Inline `--vh` style:** the page sets `--vh` through JS, which suggests a viewport-height hack.
- **Good practice:** the Pulse Insights script uses SRI (`integrity=` plus `crossorigin`). The other third-party scripts lack it, but that is expected for tag-manager-injected scripts.

## Mobile Optimization

- The viewport meta tag isn't visible in the truncated source, so it can't be verified.
- The `--vh` value of about 6.64 px implies a viewport height of about 664 px, so the mobile emulation appears to have applied.
- The JS `--vh` workaround could be replaced with `dvh` and `svh` units.
- The heavy tag load listed under Performance has a larger relative cost on mobile CPUs.

## Console & Network Errors

24 console errors were reported and 10 were listed. 6 network errors were reported.

- **429 on `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825` (5 times):**
  - This is the Kasada bot-defense fingerprint endpoint.
  - It is rate-limiting this client, probably because of the automated headless user agent.
  - If real users hit it, it would break the bot-defense handshake and downstream calls, so check the rate against real-user traffic.
- **429 on `/cart/`:** the cart document itself was blocked. Real users could hit this if the fingerprinting fails.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (6 times):**
  - A script, likely third-party, registers an `unload` listener that the page's Permissions-Policy blocks.
  - Unload handlers also prevent bfcache and are being deprecated. Find the offending tag and switch it to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (2 times):**
  - This is Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer API called with unsupported languages.
  - It most likely comes from the test browser or an injected script, not the site's own code. Confirm which script calls it.
- **Remaining 14 console errors:** the list was truncated, so they could not be reviewed.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the anti-bot challenge handled.** Use a trusted, allow-listed test IP or UA, slower pacing, or a staging bypass. Confirm Log In, Men and View Cart actually navigate, and record the real URL per step.
2. **Fix the collector.** Capture LCP and CLS (`PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`), and record INP or TBT.
3. **Fix the critical and serious accessibility defects.** Give the buttons and links accessible names (`aria-label`, visible text or `alt`). Correct the parent-child ARIA role structure.
4. **Restructure landmarks.** Use exactly one top-level `<main>`, give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put content inside landmarks. Fix `heading-order`. Re-test the cart page once it renders.
5. **Audit the third-party tags.** Remove duplicates and unused pixels, and load the rest after interaction or consent. Check for `unload` listeners and move them to `pagehide`.
6. **Self-host the fonts** with `font-display: swap` and `preload`, and drop the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency.
7. **Investigate the real-user 429 rate** on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint and on `/cart/`.
8. **Lower HTML TTFB from about 410 ms.** Check edge-cache hit rate on document responses.
