# For every stride
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- New Balance caught me twice in eight days with the same bait-and-switch. Back on May 30 they sent "Kids styles worth checking out" and I noted then that I was the wrong guy. Today they're back with "For every stride" — vague enough that I opened it — and the first thing I see is a kid's sneaker under a hero headline that reads "For every activity." Not my activity, not my feet. Fool me once.
- I'll give the email this much: it's clean. The layout holds together on my phone, the shoe grid is easy to read, and the "Never too young for style" clothing section at the bottom is organized. But none of it is aimed at a 62-year-old man looking for a cushioned walking shoe in a wide width. This is a full kids catalog, top to bottom — shoes, t-shirts, leggings, the works. I scrolled the whole thing hoping for one adult shoe sneaking in at the bottom. Nothing.
- I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding this. I'd maybe send it to my daughter if she has the grandkids to buy for this summer, but that's the most I can do with it.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — shoes are big, legible, and easy to tap on a phone screen without accidentally hitting the wrong one.
- "For every stride" is short enough to read in a half-second glance in the inbox — not a wall of text.
- Free shipping threshold ($75+) is pinned right at the top where you can't miss it.
- The shoe grid shows six distinct styles without feeling chaotic — good visual organization.
- No broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious render bugs. It just works.

## What's weak

- Wrong audience — entirely kids content on a subscriber who has never bought kids shoes from New Balance. I was the wrong guy for their May 30 send too; nothing has changed in my inbox profile since then.
- Subject line "For every stride" promises something that might apply to me, then the preview text immediately says "Reliable styles for active kids" — so the bait-and-switch is telegraphed before I even open.
- No adult crossover — not one men's shoe, not a "while you're here, shop your own styles" nudge at the bottom. A segmentation miss this clean is a waste of a send.
- No discount, no urgency. Just free shipping at $75 — not much to act on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this to adults who have never bought kids shoes.** Segment it to households with recent kids purchases or to subscribers who've browsed the kids category. I'm a walking-shoe guy; this email has zero business landing in my inbox.
- 2. **Add a footer "for you" module.** Even one row of two adult men's comfort shoes with a "still shopping for yourself?" link would recover some goodwill and give me something to click.
- 3. **Subject line is dangerously vague.** "For every stride" sounds like it could be about my walking shoes right up until the preview text kills it. If you're going to target-segment this correctly, just lead with the kids angle so the right parent opens it eagerly rather than tricking a 62-year-old into clicking.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

No anchor tags or tracking pixels are present in the truncated source — full link audit not possible. One flagged item from CSS:

- `.bgwhite` sets `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')` — the domain is masked/placeholder (`XXXXX`). If this reflects actual production HTML, the image will 404. The `http://` scheme (non-TLS) will also trigger mixed-content warnings in HTTPS webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com).

---

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**`.bg1` class defined twice with conflicting values:**
```css
/* declaration 1 */
.bg1 {background-color: #ffffff;}
/* declaration 2 — later in same <style> block */
.bg1 {background:#58546d;}
```
The second rule wins via cascade; any element relying on the white background will render as dark purple-gray instead. One definition must be removed or renamed.

**Malformed `X-UA-Compatible` meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Without this attribute the tag is ignored by Outlook/IE rendering engine. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**`@font-face` declarations have no `src:` property:**
All nine `@font-face` blocks (proxima-nova, linotype-didot, etc.) declare a `font-family` and weight but omit the `src:` URL. These rules are non-functional — they register no actual font file. The custom fonts will only render if loaded via a `<link>` or `@import` tag elsewhere in the document (not visible in truncated source). If no external load exists, all text falls back to Arial/Times New Roman silently.
