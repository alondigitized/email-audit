# Extra 20% off + New adidas for every ‘fit 🩰 👟

## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear, again. And as soon as I saw the preview text in my inbox — raw tracking URL, same as always — I nearly reflexed my way to the trash. I flagged this broken preheader on the June 21 Samba email, and the June 22 Spend & Save send, and at least two before that. At this point it doesn't read as a mistake, it reads as "Famous Footwear doesn't care how I see them in my inbox." That stings a little for a brand I've actually bought from.
- I tapped anyway, because "Extra 20% off" is a number, and numbers get through my irritation. Inside: it's another adidas fashion showcase. Young woman, big blue jacket, low Samba-style sneakers, and five sections of streetwear copy — "ON POINT," "GO LOW," "LET'S KICK IT." I kept waiting for something aimed at a 62-year-old man who wants cushioning and a shoe that slips on easy. The closest I got was the BOGO 50% off clearance at the bottom. That part I actually read twice.

- The 20% off badge is the first visual I see — prominent circle, can't miss it on a small phone screen; offer is immediately legible
- BOGO 50% off clearance at the bottom is the sleeper hero of this email; that's where the New Balance and Skechers walkers end up, and that's where my money goes
- "Shop Men's" is labeled and visible alongside "Shop Women's" — at least they didn't pretend I don't exist
- Famous Footwear is a brand I recognize and have actually bought from, so I'll give them the open even through the preheader mess

## What's weak

- The raw tracking URL is back in my preview text — again. I've counted five or six straight sends from Famous Footwear with this exact problem. This is now a trust issue, not a tech glitch.
- The hero model is a young woman in a fashion pose. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for walking shoes. The picture is not talking to me.
- "For every 'fit" — I had to read that subject line twice to parse it. Streetwear shorthand for "outfit" lands flat when you're in my demographic.
- All five section headers ("ON POINT," "GO LOW," "LACE UP," "LET'S KICK IT," "SCORE") are pure hype copy; not one mention of comfort, arch support, cushioning, or fit — the things that actually move me
- No urgency anywhere — the 20% off has no end date I can see, so "maybe later" wins over clicking now

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** Nothing else matters until this is resolved. Six emails. This is now a brand perception problem.
- 2. **Lead men's comfort into the hero, not alongside it.** Walker shoes, Skechers GOwalk, New Balance — one man in comfortable shoes at the top of the email would've changed the whole feel for me.
- 3. **Put the clearance BOGO higher.** That's the real hook for someone like me; burying it below five scrollable sections is leaving money on the table.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** "Extra 20% off through Sunday" creates a reason to act now. Without one, I save it and forget it.
- 5. **Ditch the hype headers.** "Comfort you can walk in all day" or "Built for every step" would pull me in more than "LET'S KICK IT."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Missing alt text on 38 images.** Every content image (product shots, banners, dividers) has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute at all. With images off — the default in many corporate email clients — the email renders as a blank page. The logo image (`3b6a7dc1-…ee.png`) also has `alt=""`, making the brand invisible in that state.

**High — Viewport zoom disabled.** The viewport meta tag reads:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` prevent users from zooming, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). This is a real accessibility failure, not a theoretical one.

**Medium — CSS typo in inline style.** Found in the header content cell:
```html
style="...font-size:14px;lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property. This rule silently drops — the intended spacing (likely `line-height` or `letter-spacing`) is not applied.

**Low — HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype.** The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration is outdated. Gmail and modern clients typically ignore it, but some legacy parsers may trigger quirks-mode rendering. No confirmed breakage, but worth noting.

**Low — `<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`.** The charset meta is:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Some older clients won't recognize charset without it. The correct form is `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Authentication)

**Cannot confirm from truncated source:** Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are required by CAN-SPAM but fall below the HTML truncation point. The automated QA shows 0 compliance issues flagged, which suggests these are present — but they should be verified in the full source.

**Deliverability risk — plain-text URL ratio.** The QA reports the plain-text part is **80% URLs** (9,800 of 12,200 chars). Bayesian spam filters and several inbox providers (notably Outlook/Hotmail) weight the plain-text version heavily. A text body that is mostly raw tracking URLs is a known spam signal.
