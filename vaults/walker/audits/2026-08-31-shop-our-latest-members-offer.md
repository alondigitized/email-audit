# Shop our latest members’ offer
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Same shoe boxes stacked in a pile — I've now seen this exact photo twice this month, once for the 30% off code on the 19th and now here. If I'm noticing the recycled art, I'm not the target audience they're hoping for. The whole pitch this time isn't even a sale, it's "join our membership program," which is a harder ask than just showing me a discount and letting me decide.
- Once I scroll past the boxes, it's fine — clean headline, two clear buttons, a price-tier sort (under $50, $50-100, over $100), and then a grid of six shoes. Problem is the grid's got trail runners, a suede lifestyle shoe, a "Numeric" skate shoe, and one that might work — the 1080v15 looks like a real cushioned walking shoe, but there's zero copy telling me that. I have to guess from a thumbnail.
- Would I click? Maybe on the 1080v15 if I looked close, but nothing here says "join now for arch support and cushioning," it just says "join for 30% off." I'm not filling out a membership form for a maybe-discount on shoes I have to identify myself. Not forwarding this one, not really an open-and-click for me, more of a skim-and-close.

## What's working

- The "up to 30% off select styles" line is a real, specific number, not vague marketing fluff.
- Two clean, high-contrast buttons (Shop now / Join) — I can tap those fine on my phone without squinting.
- The $50/$100 price sorting is genuinely useful — I shop by price-vs-quality math, so tiers like that save me time.
- Free shipping on $75+ banner up top is a real, honest perk I'd actually use.

## What's weak

- Recycled hero image — same stack of New Balance boxes as the August 19th 30% off email. Feels lazy, not fresh.
- The whole email leads with "become a member" instead of leading with the shoe or the deal — that's a bigger ask than I want from a cold email.
- Product grid is mostly running/trail/skate shoes (Fresh Foam Hierro trail runner, T500, NB Numeric 480) — nothing calls out which of these are actually comfort, slip-on, or arch-support styles for a guy like me.
- No age or lifestyle signal anywhere — no comfort walking model, no casual context shot, just product-on-white and a shoe box tower.
- Fine print buried at the bottom is a wall of text about exclusions and log-in requirements — makes the "30% off" feel more complicated than it should.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual shoe, not a membership pitch — put the 1080v15 or something comfort-focused front and center with copy like "Cushioned. Supportive. All-day comfort." and mention the membership discount as the way to unlock it, not the headline itself.
- 2. Cut the recycled box photo — I've now seen it in two separate emails this month, use a real shoe or lifestyle shot instead.
- 3. Label the product grid by category (walking/comfort vs. running vs. lifestyle) so I'm not guessing which of the six shoes might fit me.
- 4. Move the exclusions fine print into a smaller footnote instead of a dense paragraph right under the price tiers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members get up to 30% off — starting today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your comfort favorites, up to 30% off (members only)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $75+, plus early access through Sept 2`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Join now — 30% off select styles, exclusive to members`
