# Up to 50% off their school look
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Third time Nike's hit me with a back-to-school push in the last month. The July 19 send had the same big yellow graphic and "Back to School sale" energy — I thought that was already pushing it. This one doubles down. I've got zero school-age kids or grandkids in the picture, so "their school look" means absolutely nothing to me. That "their" in the subject actually tripped me up before I even opened it — whose look, exactly?
- I opened it anyway because 50% off is 50% off, and Nike does carry some decent men's walking shoes if you dig. But the body told me everything fast: women's Dunks, women's Air Max 90s, women's leggings. Not a men's comfort shoe in sight. No cushioning callout, no arch support, no wide width — nothing that makes me put my coffee down and start a checkout.
- Closing it and moving on. Won't click, won't forward. My grandson might want it, but he doesn't check my inbox.

## What's working

- The "Up to 50% off" hero is big and immediate — the number hits before anything else, which is right for a deal email.
- "Extra 25% off with code DAYONE" is a stackable, specific offer — I know exactly what to type at checkout, no hunting.
- Layout is clean and loads fast on a phone; no broken images, no garbled text, easy to scroll.
- "Members Free Shipping on Orders $80+" in the top bar is a decent member callout — at least they remembered I'm on their list.
- The 25% Off Apparel section lower in the email is visually distinct and clean — good visual separation even if the content isn't for me.

## What's weak

- Every shoe shown is women's — Nike Dunk Low Suede Women's, Nike Air Max 90 Women's. I can read the product names right in the email. This section was built for someone else.
- "Their school look" in the subject puts me at arm's length from the start — I'm not outfitting anyone for school.
- The sale date reads "7.17.25 - 8.13.26" — if that's July 2025 through August 2026, that's a strange year-spanning range that made me stop and stare. Looks like a data entry error on the start year.
- Neon yellow on lime green design screams Gen Z TikTok hallway, not my living room — the creative has no visual cue that a 60-something man is welcome here.
- This is the same back-to-school campaign from three days ago dressed up slightly differently; at this cadence, the next one risks getting swiped without a second look.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one men's comfort or walking module** — even a single row of men's styles gives me a reason to scroll past the women's section. Right now there's no natural landing spot for my wallet.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to own the customer** — drop "their," put me in the offer. "Up to 50% off" is the hook; "their school look" is the escape hatch.
- 3. **Fix the date string** — "7.17.25 - 8.13.26" looks like a production error. If the sale ends August 13, just write "ends August 13."
- 4. **Broaden the creative frame** — not everyone on Nike's list has school-age dependents. "Summer Sale" or "End of Season" covers the same promotional window without alienating a significant chunk of the list.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off men's summer styles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off — plus extra 25% with code DAYONE`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $80+ — sale ends August 13`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's shoes and apparel up to half off through 8/13`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has three confirmed infrastructure defects: a missing plain-text alternative body, a tracking pixel served over HTTP, and that same pixel lacking alt text. The 20 "doubled-domain" QA failures across 10 links appear to be **heuristic false positives** — the pattern match conflates `click.em.nike.com` (ESP click tracker) with `www.nike.com` (destination domain) appearing in the same URL string, which is standard click-wrapping behavior. End-to-end link resolution should be spot-checked to confirm before suppressing the alert class.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Doubled-domain flags (10 links, 20 FAIL entries) — likely false positives, verify before suppressing**

The QA tool fires two separate checks on the same 10 URLs. Decoding the URLs:

- Direct links: `https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...` — ESP tracker wraps destination. `nike.com` appears in both subdomain and destination; this is standard single-wrap.
- Product deep links: `https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fnike.sng.link%2FAstn5%2Fesj97%3F..._fallback_redirect=https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...` — Branch.io/Singular deep link with `www.nike.com` legitimately appearing three times as `_fallback_redirect`, `_ios_redirect`, and `_android_redirect` params.

Neither pattern represents a double-encoding bug (that would look like `click.em.nike.com/CL0/click.em.nike.com/CL0/...`). **Action required**: spot-check 2–3 links end-to-end to confirm resolution, then document the false-positive class in QA config.

**28 tracking links unprobed**

HTTP resolution skipped for all `click.em.nike.com/CL0/` links. No dead-link confirmation is possible from the QA run alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP tracking pixel — will be blocked**
```
src: http://click.em.nike.com/CI0/0100019f89d54f46-5de6ac73-b60f-4fca-b267-6a4508fe84a0-000000/q19ZDzYl5YouKvsHJz4S7GhlL88JLY
```
Non-HTTPS image sources are blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook under mixed-content rules. Open tracking will under-report on all clients that enforce HTTPS.

**Same image missing `alt` attribute**

No `alt` on the open-pixel image. For tracking pixels `alt=""` is the correct value; omitting it triggers accessibility linting failures and can affect spam scoring on some filters.

**Custom web font (`@font-face "Nike Futura"`) not email-safe**

Loaded from `https://www.nike.com/assets/ncss/2.2/dotcom/fonts/nike-futura-extra-bold-v200.woff2`. Web fonts are ignored by Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Samsung Mail. The fallback chain (`Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif`) visible in footer styles will apply for most recipients — no rendering break, but branded typography will not display for the majority of the list.
