# Top Mother’s Day Gifts
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-27**
## Executive summary

- Wrong brand, mostly wrong content — but the wrong-brand part is actually the bigger issue. I'm a Skechers guy. I don't have a HOKA account, I'm not a HOKA loyalist, and nothing in this email tells me why I should be. That said, the Mother's Day angle at least gives me a reason to be here — I could be buying something for my wife or daughter. But when I scrolled through, every product I saw was women's performance athletic gear: bright running shoes, running shorts, a hydration vest. That's not a gift I'd buy unless I knew exactly what she wanted. The email treated me like a stranger, not even acknowledging that I might be a male buying for someone else. I deleted it before I got to the bottom.

## What's working

- **The headline is clear.** "Top Gifts for Mom" — I got it in one second. No confusion about what this email is trying to do.
- **The CTA button is visible.** "Shop Top Gifts" sits right under the hero and is a reasonable tap size on mobile.
- **Free Expedited Shipping** is a meaningful hook. If I were gift-shopping on a deadline, that would matter. Good that it's called out lower in the email.

## What's weak

- **Wrong brand for me entirely.** I'm not a HOKA customer. I wear Skechers Slip-ins. This email landed in my inbox cold and there's no relationship to build on.
- **Nothing for a male buyer.** The whole email assumes the shopper *is* the mom, not buying *for* her. No guidance like "not sure what size? gift it with a gift card" or "she loves running — here's where to start." I'm lost.
- **Product images are tiny.** On a phone, the grid of shoes and shorts is so compressed I can barely tell what I'm looking at. I'm 62 — I need bigger visuals or I move on.
- **No prices anywhere.** I'm price-conscious. I want to know if I'm looking at a $90 shoe or a $180 shoe before I click. Hiding that makes me suspicious.
- **Performance gear, not comfort.** HOKA makes running shoes, not casual slip-ins. The products I see — bright athletic trainers, running shorts, a vest — are not what I'd buy for a gift unless I already knew the recipient was a serious runner. No soft entry point for a casual gift-buyer like me.
- **SMS sign-up mid-scroll is a distraction.** The "Be the First to Know" module interrupted my browsing right when I was starting to look at products. Bad placement.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a gift-buyer frame.** One short line above the grid — "Buying for Mom? Here's where to start" — changes the entire dynamic for male shoppers who don't know HOKA.
- 2. **Show prices in the product grid.** Even a range ($100–$160) helps me decide if I'm in the right store.
- 3. **Make the product images larger.** On mobile, the grid is too compressed. Either reduce items shown or go to a single-column layout.
- 4. **Move SMS capture to the footer.** It's a list-growth tactic, not a conversion driver — it shouldn't compete with products mid-email.
- 5. **Add a gift card option.** For buyers like me who aren't sure about size or style, a gift card CTA is a low-friction conversion that this email completely misses.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting viewport meta tags** — Two `<meta name="viewport">` declarations appear with contradictory directives:
```html
<!-- tag 1 -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<!-- tag 2 -->
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` and `user-scalable=yes` directly contradict each other. The second tag wins on most parsers, but behavior is client-defined.

**Malformed charset meta** — Missing required `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<!-- present -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<!-- correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag is ignored by strict parsers; charset falls back to client default.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` renders as a blank string in Apple Mail preview pane and some Android notifications. Should be set to the subject line or a short descriptor.

**Overly aggressive text-size reset** — Global selector `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }` disables iOS auto-sizing across all elements, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) for users who rely on browser/OS zoom.

**Web fonts will not render in most clients** — Typekit fonts are loaded via `@import url("https://p.typekit.net/...")`. This is blocked in Gmail (all variants), Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and Samsung Email. No fallback `font-family` stack is visible in the truncated source; if none exists downstream, clients will render system-default serif/sans rather than the intended Futura PT. Typekit CSS comment shows `last_published: 2022-03-23` — verify the kit is still active.

**Near-black link color** — `a { color: #000001; }` is one bit off pure black. This is likely intentional to suppress default blue underlines, but it means auto-detected links (phone numbers, addresses) rendered by Apple Mail's data detectors will still show as blue, creating inconsistent link styling.
