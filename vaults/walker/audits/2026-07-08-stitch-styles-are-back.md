# Stitch styles are back 💙
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Crocs emails for what feels like two straight weeks — clearance sale after clearance sale, and then that neutrals collection aimed at women half my age — and now I open this one and it's... Disney Stitch? I had to double-check I didn't accidentally open my grandkid's inbox. Blue cartoon alien, the word "Drip" as a headline, a matching tote bag. I like Crocs, I've worn their slides, but there is nothing in this email that makes me think Crocs was thinking about me when they sent it.
- The layout itself is fine. Clean, easy to see on the phone, no junk cluttering it up. And I noticed the "Shop Sale" button tucked in there, which got a half-second of my attention. But the whole premise — character-themed novelty clogs with matching Jibbitz charms — that's not where my head is. I'm shopping for something that keeps my feet from aching by dinnertime, not something that makes me look like I'm headed to a theme park.
- Mark as read, move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. If a neighbor asked me about Crocs sandals I might mention there's a sale section, but that's about it.

## What's working

- **Clean layout** — single column, easy to scan on a phone without zooming or pinching.
- **Four distinct CTAs** — "Shop Clogs," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals" — at least one of those (Sale, Sandals) could be relevant to a comfort shopper if they bothered looking.
- **No visual clutter or broken images** — the Stitch product hero loaded fine, color is vivid and attention-grabbing, whatever I think of the theme.
- **Brand mark is clear** — the Crocs logo up top, I know immediately who's talking to me.

## What's weak

- **"New Drip" as a headline** — I genuinely did not know that was a sale announcement for about two seconds. That's slang aimed at 25-year-olds, not me.
- **Zero comfort language** — not a single word about cushioning, arch support, fit, or why a grown man my age should buy these. The whole email could've been for a middle schooler.
- **Disney Stitch theme** — I have no emotional connection to this character. This feels like it landed in my inbox by accident.
- **No offer with a number on it** — "Shop Sale" is vague. What's the deal? 20% off? $10 off? After a week of "Extra 30% Off Clearance" emails, this feels like a step backward in value.
- **Preheader is invisible** — the preview next to the subject in my inbox was completely blank. Wasted real estate when they could've put "Now in men's sizes" or literally anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add comfort/fit language in the body copy** — Even one sentence: "Lightweight foam, roomy fit — comfortable all day." Right now there's zero reason for someone like me to click.
- 2. **Replace "New Drip" with something a human being would say** — "New Stitch Styles, Now in Men's" or even just "The Stitch Collection Is Back." Simple beats clever when you're targeting a broad age range.
- 3. **Fix the preheader** — Blank preheaders are a free miss. Put the actual value there.
- 4. **Surface the sale offer with a number** — If there's any discount attached to the Stitch launch, put it in the body. "From $34.99" or "20% off through Sunday" — something concrete.
- 5. **Consider whether this list needs to be segmented** — After a week of clearance emails plus this character-licensed drop, I'm starting to feel like Crocs just dumps their full list on every campaign. If I'd clicked men's walking or comfort styles before, I shouldn't be getting a Stitch tote pitch.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken merge tokens visible in the truncated source. The primary actionable issues are 7 footer images served over plain HTTP and a malformed charset meta tag that could cause rendering fallback in strict clients.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**32 click-tracking links** — all wrapped in ESP redirects; destinations and UTM parameters could not be probed by the automated checker. No broken raw URLs are visible in the source.

**Tracking pixel** — `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...` (Adobe AAM/Demdex). Served over HTTPS. The `d_dpuid` value (`9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d3b39ef90f188...`) appears to be a resolved hashed identifier, not an unreplaced token. `campaignID=us_20260708_collection_liloandstitch_sustain` is correctly populated.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (7 images)** — confirmed by QA scanner. All 7 footer/social icons are served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/...`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail on iOS can block or silently fail to load mixed-content HTTP images, leaving blank icon slots in the footer:

- `9c36c0ef` — App Store badge
- `13246762` — Google Play badge
- `c4df71ec` — Find Your Store
- `e18949ff` — Instagram
- `80f68cd8` — Facebook
- `51d8144e` — TikTok
- `1187ef58` — YouTube

**Malformed charset meta tag** — the `<meta>` in `<head>` is missing `http-equiv`:

```html
<!-- actual -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, the charset declaration is non-functional per the HTML4/XHTML spec that most email clients follow. Clients may fall back to their own default encoding, potentially mangling special characters (e.g., the `™` in "Jibbitz™").

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel** — `demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event` pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. Low severity (it's hidden), but will generate accessibility linter warnings. Correct: `alt=""`.

**`font-display: swap`** — present on `@font-face` declarations for `TT Crocs`. This CSS property has no effect in any email client; it's harmless but dead code.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unreplaced merge tokens visible in the available HTML. The `d_dpuid`, `campaignID`, and preheader text all appear fully resolved. Full verification requires the complete (non-truncated) source.

### 5. Compliance

- CAN-SPAM requires a physical postal address and one-click unsubscribe mechanism — footer is not in the provided excerpt.
- Sending domain `crocs-email.com` is an ESP subdomain separate from `crocs.com`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML; must be verified at the mail server/header level.

**Preheader implementation** — two preheader constructs are present: a `div.preheader` suppressed via a body `<style>` block, plus a unicode-padded `div` with inline `display:none`. The body `<style>` approach is correct for Gmail; if CSS is stripped, the first preheader div could become visible. No immediate bug, but the dual-div pattern is redundant.
