# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 21, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I came to skechers.com looking for a comfortable slip-on with arch support — right in Skechers' wheelhouse. The navigation menu is genuinely good, the slip-in sizing screen is clean, and the fit filters are helpful. But everything after "tap Add to Cart" fell apart: the cart page loaded completely blank, and when I searched "comfort shoes" the first results shown were Women's shoes. If I can't check out and the site doesn't even know I'm a man, what am I doing here?

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The cart is still broken (blank white page), and search personalization still ignores my gender. The navigation improvements don't offset a checkout pipeline that doesn't function.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** — Best screen in the whole journey. Clean white drawer, large readable text, well-organized rows. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are right there as quick links below the main categories. That's exactly what I need — no hunting. Touch targets are generous, easy to hit without squinting or mis-tapping.

**Hands Free Slip-ins listing page (Step 5)** — The fit filter buttons (Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit) are full-width blue pills that are hard to miss and easy to tap. "313 results" tells me inventory is deep. The two-column grid with color swatches works on a phone screen.

**Size selection screen (Step 7)** — The size grid is big, well-spaced, and the "Please select a size" validation message in red is clear. The sticky full-width "ADD TO CART" button pinned to the bottom of the screen is the right call — I don't have to scroll to find it.

**Ship it / Check in-store availability** — I liked seeing both options on the product page. Checking if my local store has it before driving over matters at my age.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart is a blank white page (Step 8) — Critical failure.** I went through the whole process — found a shoe, picked my size — and the cart page rendered nothing. Completely white. No items, no total, no checkout button, no error message. I'd assume the site was broken and leave. This is the same failure as yesterday. If this is persistent, it's costing Skechers real sales every day.

**Hero image doesn't speak to me (Steps 1–2).** The homepage pushes "Skechers Running" with a young athlete and a basketball. I'm 62 and I want something that won't kill my feet at the grocery store. The Slip-ins and Arch Fit nav pills are there, but the hero visual sends the wrong signal. Hoka and New Balance are both showing older adults in comfort contexts right now. Skechers is leaving that positioning on the table.

**Login step showed no login (Step 3).** The screenshot looks identical to the homepage — no login form appeared, no confirmation that I was signed in. If I'm supposedly logged in, there's no "Welcome back, Walker" moment, no loyalty points visible, nothing personalized in the header. The cart still shows 0. It feels like the login either didn't happen or had no effect.

**"No Reviews" on the product page (Step 6).** The Arch Fit 3.0 Kaliver is priced at $115 and has zero reviews. That's a deal-killer for me. I cross-shop. If I can't read what other people's feet said about this shoe, I'm going to DSW where I can at least try it on. Skechers should not be surfacing zero-review products as a destination result.

**"Excluded from promotions" warning (Step 6).** I got an email saying 40% off. I land on a $115 shoe and the first thing I see below the price is a warning that it's excluded. That's a trust problem. I feel like I was misled.

**Search returns Women's results first (Step 9).** I searched "comfort shoes" and the first two results are explicitly labeled Women's. I'm a logged-in male. 1,457 results and Skechers is showing me women's mules. This is a basic personalization failure. If the site knows who I am, use it.

**Popup dismissal was invisible (Step 2).** The banner just rotated to a different promotion (Care Bears → Britto). No actual popup was shown or dismissed. Either the test didn't surface a popup today, or the step was a no-op.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately.** A blank cart page is a broken checkout. This is priority zero. No UX improvement matters if the transaction doesn't complete.

2. **Filter search results by logged-in gender.** If I'm signed in as a man, women's products should not appear before men's in a "comfort shoes" search. Apply a gender preference filter by default, with a clear option to "See all results."

3. **Surface the loyalty/account state after login.** Show a "Hi Walker" greeting or a points balance in the header. A logged-in user should feel recognized, not like a ghost.

4. **Don't surface zero-review products as primary results.** Either seed those pages with editorial content ("Why we picked this shoe") or filter them out of top results until reviews accumulate.

5. **Align the promotion warning with the email.** If a shoe is excluded from a 40%-off promotion, don't show it as a landing page after a 40%-off email blast. That's a conversion killer and a trust eroder.

6. **Update the hero for the comfort shopper.** A rotating hero that sometimes shows an older adult walking comfortably would resonate far more with my demographic than a basketball court.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers' navigation is genuinely built for someone like me — the slip-in and fit-width emphasis in the menu is smart, and the size picker works well on a phone. But the cart is broken, search doesn't know I'm a man, and the logged-in experience feels identical to being a stranger. I'd cross-shop to Hoka or check DSW before trying again here. **Score: 4/10 — no change from yesterday.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform is Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware), confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` path. The mobile journey has three distinct problem clusters: PerimeterX bot-detection self-rate-limiting on critical endpoints (including `/cart/`), a View Cart page missing foundational HTML shell elements, and a 20+ third-party script payload with confirmed duplicates.

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages. 4 require immediate remediation.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA roles missing required ancestor container |
| Critical | `button-name` | Homepage | One or more `<button>` elements have no accessible label |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` element is empty or absent |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | `<html>` element missing `lang` attribute |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level` | Homepage | `<main>` is nested inside another element |
| Moderate | `landmark-no-duplicate-main` | Homepage | Multiple `<main>` landmarks present |

| Moderate | `landmark-one-main` | View Cart | No `<main>` landmark at all |

The View Cart violations (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`) indicate the cart is rendered through a different template path — likely an iframe or a modal overlay that bypasses the standard SFCC page shell. This is a template-layer bug, not a content issue.

The duplicate `<main>` + missing `<main>` pattern across Homepage vs. View Cart suggests inconsistent slot/region configuration in the SFCC template hierarchy.

These two pages share a TTFB value exactly, suggesting a shared middleware path with a hard timeout or cache miss behavior.

LCP and CLS are not collected — the audit tooling needs `PerformanceObserver` instrumentation or Lighthouse traces to populate these. Without LCP, it is not possible to assess rendering completeness.

**Third-party script load (from HTML):**

Scripts loaded on every page (partial list): GTM, Google Analytics (2 IDs: `G-BH2DL4JXJL`, `G-XZTV9LQ9DQ`), TikTok Pixel, Bing Ads, Yahoo YTC, Monetate (2 endpoints: `se.monetate.net/js/2` + `se.monetate.net/js/3`), Quantum Metric, Pinterest, Impact, Bloomreach, CreativeCDN, PulseInsights, Go2SDK/Tune, Snapchat, Reddit, Stape CDN, Facebook, NextDoor, Taboola.

**Confirmed duplicate script loads:**
- `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` — injected twice on Men category
- `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` — injected twice on Homepage and Log In

Duplicates indicate the GTM container and the `vice`-managed consent layer are both injecting the same tags without deduplication. Each duplicate fires the underlying pixel twice, corrupting analytics data and wasting bandwidth.

**WebFont loading:** Mulish loaded via WebFontLoader.js (6 weights: n3–n8) as async JS. This is the FOUT-prone pattern — content renders in fallback font until JS executes and fonts load. Modern alternative is `<link rel="preload">` + CSS `@font-face` with `font-display: swap`.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` CSS custom property is a JavaScript-computed viewport height hack for mobile browsers (100vh !== window.innerHeight on iOS Safari). The floating-point imprecision in `6.640000000000001` is harmless but indicates the JS is not rounding the value — `parseFloat((window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(4))` would be cleaner.
- No `viewport` meta tag visible in the HTML truncation, though it is likely present further into `<head>`. Should be confirmed: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">`.
- No evidence of adaptive image serving or `srcset` in the HTML samples.

## Console & Network Errors

**PerimeterX / KapSolver self-rate-limiting (6× 429):**

```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```

The UUID path (`149e9513.../2d206a39...`) and `x-kpsdk-v` parameter identify this as PerimeterX's client-side fingerprinting SDK (KP SDK v1.2.522). The endpoint is returning 429 to the browser's own fingerprint polling — meaning PerimeterX's rate-limit config is throttling legitimate user sessions, not just bots. This breaks the bot-scoring pipeline and can cause downstream friction (CAPTCHAs, blocked cart actions).

**Cart returning 429:**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```

The cart page itself is rate-limited in this session. A 429 on `/cart/` during a real user flow is a conversion-blocking bug. If the PerimeterX fingerprint polling above failed to establish a valid score before the cart request, PerimeterX's Enforcer may be blocking the page request. The fingerprint 429s and the cart 429 are likely causally linked.

**Stylitics CORS block:**

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

Stylitics (outfit inspiration widget) is not serving the correct CORS headers for `https://www.skechers.com`. This breaks the outfit widget styling. Resolution requires Stylitics adding `https://www.skechers.com` to their allowed origins, or Skechers proxying the asset through their own CDN. The `ERR_FAILED` immediately following is the stylesheet load failing as a consequence.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **Cart 429**: Audit PerimeterX Enforcer thresholds. The fingerprint endpoint is rate-limiting itself, causing the Enforcer to treat valid sessions as suspicious. Coordinate with PerimeterX support to raise the `/fp` polling rate limit or switch to a single fingerprint-on-load model instead of continuous polling.

2. **View Cart template**: Add `<title>`, `<html lang>`, `<main>`, and `<h1>` to the cart page template. These are missing entirely — the cart is likely rendered through a non-standard SFCC content slot or SPA overlay that bypasses the global page template.

**P1 — High priority:**

3. **Deduplicate GTM tags**: Audit the GTM container and the `vice` consent management tags — `creativecdn` and `ytc.js` are being injected twice. Add deduplication guards in GTM or remove the redundant injection path.

4. **Fix button accessibility**: Identify the unlabeled `<button>` elements on Homepage (likely icon-only buttons: hamburger menu, search, close). Add `aria-label` or visually-hidden text.

5. **Fix `aria-required-parent`**: Audit any `role="option"`, `role="menuitem"`, `role="tab"`, or similar ARIA roles on Homepage that are not inside the expected parent container.

**P2 — Performance:**

6. **Log In / Men category TTFB**: Profile the server-side request path for these two pages specifically. Add server-timing headers to identify which middleware layer is adding ~410ms. Likely candidates: session hydration, Monetate experiment fetch, or A/B testing synchronous call.

7. **WebFont strategy**: Replace WebFontLoader.js with `<link rel="preload" as="font">` + `font-display: swap` in CSS. This eliminates the JS-dependency on font rendering.

8. **Instrument LCP and CLS**: The audit is missing LCP and CLS for all 8 steps. Add `PerformanceObserver` with `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` entry types to the test harness.
