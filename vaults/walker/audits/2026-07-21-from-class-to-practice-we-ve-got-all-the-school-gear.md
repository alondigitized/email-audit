# From class to practice, we've got all the school gear
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- I looked at this thing and felt the exact same thing I felt on July 19th when they sent me the "Score fall looks ahead of rest" email — back to school, young female athlete, nothing in here for me. This one at least doesn't have soccer jerseys, I'll give them that. The email is clean and focused on one thing: a Harper Murray collection pitch and an app-exclusive 25% off deal. Two problems. First, Harper Murray means nothing to me. Second, "app exclusive" is my least favorite phrase in retail. I'm not downloading an app to unlock a discount I'm already being promised in the subject line.
- The offer is real — 25% off plus 6x adiClub points for in-app purchases — and the layout is clear enough. But I'm 62, I shop on my phone browser, and this email is squarely aimed at someone sending their kid back to school. The hero model looks like she's maybe 20 years old. The "TRUE TO YOU" headline is the vaguest thing I've read this week. I'd scroll past this without a second look.
- I'm marking it read and moving on. Wouldn't forward it; wouldn't click. This is the sixth or seventh adidas email I've gotten in the past week and I'm starting to wonder if they know who I am at all.

## What's working

- The 25% off is concrete and clearly stated — no guesswork about what the discount actually is.
- The layout has a clean three-section flow: hero, app sale teaser, offer details. Easy to skim on a phone.
- No render bugs, no broken images, text doesn't overlap — this was a technically solid send.
- The adiClub 6x points mention is a good reminder that I'm a member, even if buried under the app requirement.

## What's weak

- App-exclusive deal is a wall for me. I don't shop in apps and I'm not starting now just to save 25%.
- Harper Murray means nothing to me — they never told me who this person is or why I should care.
- "TRUE TO YOU" copy is aspirational mush — no shoe, no feature, no fit detail, nothing I can evaluate.
- Demographics are completely off: young woman, school athlete, back-to-class framing. I graduated high school in 1982.
- They're in my inbox constantly. Six emails in the last eight days. The cadence has become noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the app gate or at least offer a browser fallback.** If the offer requires the app, say it big and early — and link to a web alternative. Right now the headline teases 25% off and the fine print takes it away.
- 2. **Lead with a product I can actually buy.** Show me a men's walking shoe — cushioned, slip-on, something with arch support — and attach the 25% off to that item specifically. That's a click.
- 3. **Identify Harper Murray.** One line — "Harper Murray, 2024 national volleyball champion" — gives me context. Without it, it's just a photo of a young woman I don't recognize.
- 4. **Reduce cadence.** After six emails in eight days, every send is fighting for attention it hasn't earned.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's walking styles: 25% off in the adidas app`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the sale in-app — cushioned, casual, and on sale through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited time: 6x points + 25% off your next pair`

## Technical Audit — adidas "School Gear" Email

**From:** adidas <adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com>
**Subject:** From class to practice, we've got all the school gear

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken link due to a malformed relative URL in a click-tracking redirect, and a missing plain-text MIME alternative. Six HTTP image sources risk blocking in secure email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect — relative URL in redirect target**

The adidas click-tracking service (`dv.adidas.com/o/`) is resolving the destination URL as a relative path, producing a `unknown url type` error:

```
Redirect target (broken): /g/dfecbfb9-d968-49b6-a045-5f7089279a60?txn=a5be8380-84b1-11f1-b606-7685877ed2a2
Outer tracking URL:        https://dv.adidas.com/o/dfecbfb9-d968-49b6-a045-5f7089279a60?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The `cp_tp` payload decodes to a destination path beginning with `/g/` rather than `https://www.adidas.com/g/`. This is a payload encoding bug — the absolute origin was stripped before encoding. Clicking this link results in a dead/error page for the recipient.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `click.comms.adidas.com` and `dv.adidas.com`; not probed for reachability. No additional broken links confirmed, but the broken-redirect pattern above may affect more than one destination if the same encoding pipeline was used.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Six HTTP image sources — content may be blocked**

The same arrow icon is referenced via HTTP six times across the email:

```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Apple Mail (macOS/iOS), Gmail, and Outlook with "load images securely" policies will silently block HTTP image loads. Since this is a repeated UI element (navigation arrow), its absence degrades visual layout in a predictable, widespread way.

**[WARN] One pixel/beacon image missing `alt` text**

```
src: https://dv.adidas.com/o/dfecbfb9-d968-49b6-a045-5f7089279a60?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

This tracking pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers will announce the raw URL fragment. Should be `alt=""` to suppress.

**[WARN] One click.comms.adidas.com image missing `alt` text and served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f82a50b9c-e1fee22e-...
```

Dual issue: no `alt` attribute and HTTP source. Likely a tracking pixel; both should be corrected.

**`@font-face` declared twice** — `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are defined in two separate `<style>` blocks with slightly different `src` fallback lists (second block omits `Calibri` and `Sans Serif`). This is redundant and may cause inconsistent font resolution depending on CSS parse order in certain email clients.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME alternative absent**

```
Text version length: 0 chars
```

CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its absence degrades deliverability scoring with spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule adds score). Many B2C ESPs auto-generate a plain-text fallback; this suggests the generation step was skipped or the output was empty-string. A non-empty plain-text part with the unsubscribe URL and physical address is strongly recommended.

**Physical address / unsubscribe** — not verified in the truncated source, but the sending domain (`us-news.comms.adidas.com`) and infrastructure suggest a commercial ESP. If the footer containing the postal address and unsubscribe link was in the truncated portion, no issue; if absent, that is a CAN-SPAM §5 violation.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** — present and correct; suppresses referrer leakage on link clicks before the tracking redirect fires.

**`<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />`** — present; prevents cached/forwarded copies from being indexed.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

For the 29 functioning tracking links, UTM propagation was not probed (redirects skipped), but the `cp_tp` JWT payload in the `dv.adidas.com` links is the standard adidas click-tracking mechanism and typically appends UTMs at redirect resolution time — no structural issue observed in the encoding pattern itself (only the missing `https://www.adidas.com` origin prefix on the broken link).
