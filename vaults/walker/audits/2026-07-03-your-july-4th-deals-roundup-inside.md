# Your July 4th Deals Roundup Inside🍉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Third email in three days from Shoe Carnival, and they went with a watermelon emoji for a July 4th sale — I'm not sure what the fruit has to do with fireworks, but I shrugged and opened it anyway out of habit. The moment I'm inside, I see "$10 Off $74.99+" up top and think, okay, useful. Then it unspools into a full buffet of deals and I lose the thread. Women's sandals, kids' blowout, family sale — half this email isn't talking to me at all. The Skechers Slip-Ins and Brooks sections buried toward the bottom? That's exactly my aisle. But I had to scroll past a lot of pink and kids' shoes to get there.
- Also, same broken preview text as June 24th. That URL garbage is back. Shoe Carnival clearly still hasn't fixed that, which tells me nobody at the company actually looks at their own emails in an inbox view. That's a trust ding, not a dealbreaker, but it's embarrassing at this point.
- Would I click? Maybe one tap — probably the Skechers Slip-Ins button or Brooks — but the roundup format makes me work for it. I wouldn't forward this. If a buddy asked me about July 4th shoe deals, I'd just text them "Shoe Carnival has something going on" rather than forward this wall of content.

## What's working

- **$10 off $74.99+ is front and center** — a coupon code I can grab immediately, no digging required.
- **Skechers Slip-Ins highlighted with their own section** — that's my kind of shoe (hands-free, slip-on, casual) and it's actually called out by name, not just lumped into a generic "sandals" bucket.
- **Brooks Price Break with a specific starting price ($89.98)** — I cross-shop Brooks, so seeing an actual number rather than "great deals!" made me stop and look.
- **July 4th timing feels credible** — there's a real deadline baked into this, not a fake "limited time" vague warning.
- **2.50X Points carry-over from the July 2nd email** — at least that's consistent messaging across sends; feels like a real campaign, not a one-off.

## What's weak

- **Women's & Kids' sections lead the body** — the first real deal block is "Women's & Kids' Sandals 40%–60% off." I'm a 62-year-old man. That's not my section, and it's the first thing eating my attention.
- **Broken preheader URL is back** — this is at least the second time I've seen this exact problem (June 24th was the last time). It looked sloppy then, it looks worse now because they had time to fix it.
- **Roundup format creates decision fatigue** — six or seven separate sale modules competing for my eyes means none of them really win. I'd rather one focused offer than a scroll-a-thon.
- **Watermelon emoji in the subject** — I honestly don't know what a watermelon has to do with July 4th shoe deals. It's a head-scratcher, not a hook.
- **25% off Skechers Slip-Ins** — that's not a very deep discount for a loyalty member who's been getting 50% off emails all month. Feels thin compared to what they've been offering.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the broken preheader — immediately, before the next send.** This is now a documented repeat offense. Use real copy: "Slip-Ins, Brooks, and $10 off — all before the 4th."
- 2. **Lead the body with the men's comfort angle, not women's sandals.** If I'm a segmented subscriber (and Shoe Carnival should know I buy men's comfort shoes), the first section should be Brooks or Skechers, not 60% off women's sandals.
- 3. **Drop two or three modules** — Skechers Slip-Ins, Brooks Price Break, and the $10 coupon are enough for one email. The Family Sale and Women's Blowout can be separate sends.
- 4. **Give the Skechers Slip-Ins a better discount number.** 25% off after months of 30–50% off emails feels like a downgrade. If it's the best you can offer, say why (new styles, limited stock).
- 5. **Lose the watermelon emoji.** Use a flag or a sparkler if you need July 4th visual shorthand.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 off + Skechers Slip-Ins on sale — July 4th only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Brooks from $89, Slip-Ins 25% off — 4th of July deals inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Slip-Ins, Brooks, and $10 off your next pair — ends July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your July 4th savings: $10 coupon + Skechers Slip-Ins + Brooks deals.`

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "July 4th Deals Roundup" (2026-07-03)

### 1. Technical Summary

All five tracking beacon URLs contain an embedded zero-width space (U+200B) that breaks link validation and corrupts click/open metric collection. Three images are also missing alt text, including a visible content image.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in tracking URLs**

Every tracking variant (`rd`, `rl`, `p`, `f`, and the pixel `i`) has a U+200B appended immediately after the UTM value:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260703_friday_deal_roundup_AM​
                                                                                                        ↑ U+200B here (position 82)
```

This character is non-ASCII and causes the QA validator to fail with `'ascii' codec can't encode character '\u200b'`. In production, HTTP clients that do not strip the character before following the redirect will receive a malformed URL. ESP redirect infrastructure may silently drop or misroute these requests, resulting in undercounted opens and clicks.

**Source**: Confirmed in all 5 `FAIL` entries from automated QA. The character is likely introduced by a template editor paste (e.g., copying from a rich-text field) or a variable concatenation bug in the ESP.

**25 tracking links skipped** — The redirect-only domains were not probed for destination health; these should be validated in a separate pass against final landing page URLs.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (3 images)**

| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| Content image (`465aca37…`) | `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/465aca37-a584-4d52-a5a1-61002d176bb3.png` |

| Delivery pixel (`0di86k1v`) | `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` |

The content image missing alt text is the accessibility issue; tracking pixel `alt=""` is standard, so those two should use empty-string alt, not omit the attribute.

**Invalid `<meta>` tag**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

Missing required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. The tag is silently ignored by all clients but is malformed HTML.

**Duplicate CSS property**

In the global anchor rule, `color: inherit` is declared twice in the same block — once with `!important` and once without. The `!important` instance wins, but the redundant declaration adds dead weight and signals a copy-paste origin.

```css
color:inherit !important;
color:inherit;   /* redundant */
```

**Overlapping media query breakpoints**

Three overlapping breakpoints — `max-width: 600px`, `max-width: 660px`, and `max-width: 450px` — define conflicting rules for `.pad20`, `.photo`, `.image`, and `.show`. On viewports between 451–600px, both the 660px and 600px blocks apply. This is non-fatal if cascade order is intentional, but it is worth confirming `.responsive-text` font-size behavior in that range is as designed.

**Dark mode**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```

Dark mode is explicitly opted out. This is a deliberate choice, not a bug, but means Apple Mail / Outlook dark-mode auto-inversion will apply to images if backgrounds are not declared.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTMs / Landing Page)

The UTM campaign value embedded in tracking URLs is:

```

```

Because the U+200B is appended to this value, any tracking redirect that passes the `t=` parameter downstream to the landing page URL will inject the invisible character into `utm_campaign` as received by GA4/analytics. This would cause session attribution under `SCA_20260703_friday_deal_roundup_AM​` (with zero-width space) rather than the canonical `SCA_20260703_friday_deal_roundup_AM`, splitting campaign data across two keys.

`utm_source` and `utm_medium` are not visible in the truncated tracking URL structure — verify they are present in the final resolved destination URLs.
