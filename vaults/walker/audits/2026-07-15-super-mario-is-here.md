# Super Mario™ is here! 🍄
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Crocs emails all summer — clearance blowouts, the Stitch collab, that retro sneaker story last week that I actually kind of liked — and now I open this one and it's Super Mario. The mushroom emoji in the subject line already told me this wasn't for me, but I opened anyway. The blue Classic Clog against those green pipes looks sharp, I'll give them that. The photography is fun. But I'm 62 years old. I'm not hunting for a Bowser Jibbitz charm to clip onto my shoe. I want my feet to stop hurting by 3 PM.
- There's zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere I can see. No price, no discount — just "shop clogs" and "shop sandals" buttons. The "Pipe Dreams" headline is clever if you get the Mario reference, but it tells me nothing about why I'd want these shoes. "Flutter Into Fun" for the second section means nothing to me at all. I marked it read and moved on. I'm not forwarding this to anyone — my buddies would look at me sideways.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Definitely not.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely eye-catching — bright blue clog on a green pipe backdrop. Even as someone who doesn't care about Mario, I registered it immediately.
- Four CTA buttons at the bottom are clearly labeled and easy to tap on a phone — "Shop Clogs," "Shop Sandals," "Shop Sale" are all words I understand.
- The layout is clean, no broken images, text doesn't overlap. Rendered fine on my screen.
- "Shop Sale" in the button row at least gives me a way out — if I was going to click anything, it'd be that, just to see if there's something useful in the clearance pile.

## What's weak

- Nothing about this email is aimed at a 62-year-old man with tired feet. No comfort language, no arch support callout, no mention of how the shoe actually feels on a long walk.
- No price or offer anywhere — not even a "starting at $X." I need a reason to click, and "it exists" isn't one.
- "Flutter Into Fun" means nothing to me. I stared at that headline for three seconds and still don't know what I'm looking at or why I'd want it.
- The preview text in my inbox showed nothing useful — just "Crocs" and then blank. If I hadn't already known the mushroom emoji in the subject meant Mario, I'd have no idea what this email was even about before opening.
- After all those clearance emails earlier in the month, a full-price character collab launch feels like a step sideways. I was hoping they'd found something more relevant to me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one comfort anchor in the hero copy.** Even a single line — "still the Classic Clog fit you trust" — would let me know this shoe is for walking, not just dressing up. Right now this email could be for a 10-year-old.
- 2. **Show a price or lead with "Shop Sale."** I have no idea if these are $40 or $90. Give me a number or lead with the sale angle for subscribers who aren't here for the collab.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** Whatever is happening with the invisible characters, it needs to be real copy. Something that gives me a reason to open beyond the subject line alone.
- 4. **Segment this one away from me.** If Crocs knows I've only clicked comfort/walking content, this email shouldn't reach me. Send it to the folks who opened the Stitch collab.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Mario Clogs — plus sale styles from $35`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Game On: Mario Clogs just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New collab styles for adults, kids & toddlers — plus sale picks still up`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Classic Clog comfort meets Super Mario — shop the drop`

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute.** The demdex open-tracking `<img>` has no `alt=""`. Should carry an empty alt for accessibility compliance: `alt=""`.

Custom font (`TT Crocs`) is loaded via HTTPS from `media.crocs.com` in woff2/woff with correct `font-display: swap` — no issues. Responsive breakpoint at 639px and all visibility utility classes (`mobileHide`, `mobileView`) are present and well-formed.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[FAIL — Broken Experience] Empty first-name merge token.** QA detected a greeting rendered as `"Hi ,"` — the first name field resolved to an empty string. This is likely a missing fallback default in the ESP template logic.

**Fix:** Add a fallback value to the merge tag, e.g.:
- Salesforce MC: `%%=v(@FirstName)=%%` → wrap with `IIF(EMPTY(@FirstName), 'there', @FirstName)`
- Klaviyo: `{{ first_name | default: 'there' }}`
- Generic: ensure the subscriber profile has `first_name` populated before send, or suppress records where it is null.
