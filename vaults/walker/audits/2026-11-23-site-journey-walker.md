# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-23**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got as far as picking a shoe, and then the trip fell apart. The homepage is loud with Black Friday banners, but the "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" tabs along the top are what I came for. The Men's menu and the Men's Shoes page are decent. Then the sizes were all unselected, "Add to Cart" gave me a red "Please select a size" warning, and the cart icon still said 0. The cart page was a completely blank white screen. Search for "comfort shoes" then showed me women's loafers and wedges first. I never got to a checkout, and nothing told me I was logged in.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 4/10)

This is a small regression. I don't have yesterday's screenshots, so I can't compare step by step. The blank cart page is the likely cause. The cart itself failed to show anything, and a failed cart outweighs the nice category pages.

## 3. What's Working

- **The comfort tabs are right up top.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cus…" (probably Max Cushioning) sit under the logo. That is how I shop.
- **The Men's Shoes page is clear.** The heading is big and the filter chips are easy to read. The picture tiles for "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" put my kind of shoe up front.
- **The Men's menu is simple.** It has big text, one list, and "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," and "Shop by Activity" as the first entries. The arrows are easy to tap.
- **The product page text is readable.** The price is bold ($155.00), there are 100 reviews, and the Klarna/Afterpay monthly payments help me figure out the cost.
- **The size buttons are large.** They're roomy, and sold-out sizes (12.5, 14.0) are crossed out with a bell icon. That is much better than finding out later.
- **"Add to Cart" is a big navy button** with a clear glow around it.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page was blank.** It was just white, with no items, no message, and no way forward. I would have left.
- **The cart didn't update.** I pressed "Add to Cart" with no size picked. The page showed "Please select a size," and the cart badge stayed at 0. A size should be selected by default, or the page should scroll to the problem. The warning is also easy to miss.
- **I never saw that I was logged in.** Steps 2 and 3 look exactly like the homepage. There was no "Hi, Walker," no points balance, and no sign-in form. Nothing felt like it was made for me.
- **Search sent a man to women's shoes.** "Comfort shoes" returned 1,658 results. The first two were Women's loafers and wedges, and I was in the Men's section earlier. Nothing about the results said comfort, such as arch support or cushioning.
- **The page is cluttered with discounts.** There's a blue bar, a gray bar, a black banner, and a gray banner, and they carry two different offers: "30-50% off" and "Extra 15% off with code BF2026." I'd have to do math in my head. Search results also show "30% OFF! Applied at Cart" next to a members-only code, and I couldn't tell what I'd actually pay.
- **No comparison against other brands.** Nothing helps me weigh Skechers against Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance. I also see no "worth the price" explanation for a $155 shoe.
- **The tab row gets cut off.** "Max Cus…" runs off the edge of the screen, so it isn't obvious that I can scroll sideways. The tap targets for the top icons (search, account, cart) are fairly small.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the empty cart page.** Show my items, or at least show "Your cart is empty" with buttons to keep shopping. A blank page is the worst result.
2. **Preselect a size, or make the "Please select a size" warning hard to miss.** Scroll up to it and highlight the size buttons. Never leave a shopper wondering why the cart stayed at 0.
3. **Make the logged-in state obvious.** Show my name, my saved sizes, and "Recently viewed" at the top.
4. **Tune search to the shopper.** Put men's results first when I came from Men's, and add comfort labels such as "Arch Fit," "Slip-ins," and "Cushioned" on each result.
5. **Show one offer at a time.** Pick either the percentage or the code, and show the final price on the shoe.
6. **Add a short "Compare comfort" section** that is honest about the other brands, with cushioning and arch-support notes and a price-per-year-of-wear idea.
7. **Add an arrow or fade to the tab row** so people know it scrolls, and make the top icons larger.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the Men's Shoes page and the comfort tabs. They tell me Skechers understands what I want. But the shopping trip stopped working at the cart. Add to Cart didn't add anything, the cart page was blank, and search showed me the wrong shoes. I'd go to Brooks or New Balance instead, which would take me five minutes and works every time. The shoe has to fit, and so does the checkout.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Axe found 18 violations across 3 of the 8 steps (Homepage 10, View Cart 4, Search 4). Two are critical (`aria-required-parent`, `button-name`), both on the Homepage.
- The `429` on `/cart/` is the only network error. The View Cart violations (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare error document, so they are probably artifacts of that 429 and not the real cart template. This needs a re-run to confirm.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals verdict.
- The Log In and Men category HTML samples both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Those steps were probably overlays or menus, not page loads. Their metrics therefore don't describe distinct documents.
- Only 10 of the 21 console errors were provided.

## Accessibility

**Homepage (10 violations):**
- **Critical, `button-name`:** one or more buttons have no accessible name, probably icon-only buttons such as menu, search, or close.
- **Critical, `aria-required-parent`:** elements with child roles (such as `listitem` or `tab`) sit outside the required parent role.
- **Serious, `link-name`:** links have no discernible text, probably image or icon links.
- **Moderate, landmarks:**
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level` indicate a nested or duplicated `<main>`.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` means an `<aside>` is nested inside another landmark.
  - `landmark-unique` means landmarks share the same role and label.
  - `region` means content sits outside any landmark.
- **Moderate, `page-has-heading-one`:** the page has no `<h1>`.
- **Minor, `aria-allowed-role`:** a `role` is used on an element that doesn't allow it.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4 violations):** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, and `region`. These are the same template-level problems as the Homepage and probably come from shared header and footer markup.

**View Cart (4 violations):**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` are serious.
- `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` are moderate.
- The Homepage HTML does have `lang="en"`, so these four are most likely the 429 response body and not the cart template.

**Not covered:** Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart have no axe results listed. I can't tell whether they were clean or never scanned.

## SEO

- The Homepage has no `<h1>`, which weakens the primary heading signal.
- The View Cart document has no `<title>` or `lang`. If this is the 429 page, the response could be indexed or cached as an error. Cart URLs should also be `noindex`.
- I can't verify canonicals, meta description, structured data, or per-page `<title>` values, because the HTML samples are truncated before those elements.
- The Log In and Men category samples are identical to the Homepage and share its URL. I can't verify the category pages' own SEO signals (title, canonical, h1) from this run.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** Homepage is 477ms and Log In/Men category are 438ms, all under 800ms. The later steps (23–28ms, 109ms) are too low for real document fetches. They are probably soft navigations, cached responses, or reused navigation-timing entries. Treat only the Homepage TTFB as a real server-response measurement.
- **LCP and CLS:** not collected for any step. The LCP observer or the metrics hook needs fixing before this audit can say anything about them.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage `<head>` injects about 25 third-party scripts. They come from GTM, TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Yahoo, Taboola, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach, Impact, Criteo/RTB House and Tune/go2sdk. Most are `async`, but the main-thread and network cost on mobile is likely significant. I couldn't measure it here.
- **Web fonts:** `webfontloader` is pulled from the cdnjs CDN, and the Mulish weights (300–800) load via JS. The font request chain starts late and may add layout shift or FOIT/FOUT. I couldn't measure the effect without CLS data.

## Mobile Optimization

- The viewport appears to be about 664px tall, from the `--vh: 6.64px` custom property. The `--vh` property is set by JS, a common `100vh` workaround for mobile browser chrome, and it works.
- I can't verify the viewport meta tag or tap-target sizing. The samples are truncated before `<head>` content, and axe's `target-size` result isn't reported.
- The unnamed buttons and links in the Accessibility section also affect screen-reader navigation on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** this is rate limiting or bot protection and is likely triggered by the automated journey. If real users can hit it, the cart is unreachable for them. It could also explain the malformed View Cart document.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed" (6 of 10 listed):** a script registers an `unload` handler where the policy disallows it. This is probably a third-party tag. Unload handlers also block back/forward cache eligibility.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (4 of 10 listed):** this comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called without supported languages. I couldn't determine whether the caller is first-party, a third-party script, or the browser or automation environment. It is low severity and non-blocking.
- **Not shown:** 11 of the 21 reported console errors were not provided.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the cart step** with throttling or a warmed session. Confirm whether the `/cart/` 429 reproduces and whether the four View Cart axe violations disappear. If real users can trigger the 429, raise its priority.
2. **Fix the Homepage critical issues:**
   - Add `aria-label` or visible text to icon-only buttons and links.
   - Correct the parent/child ARIA role structure.
3. **Fix the landmark structure:** one top-level `<main>`, `<aside>` outside other landmarks, unique labels for repeated landmarks, and page content inside landmarks. Add an `<h1>` on the Homepage.
4. **Fix the metrics harness:**
   - Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` using `buffered: true`.
   - Record navigation timing only for real document loads.
   - Log distinct URLs for the Log In and Men category steps.
5. **Find the source of the `unload` handlers** and move them to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`. Test which third-party tag triggers the warning.
6. **Audit third-party tags** by main-thread time and defer non-essential tags until after interaction or consent. Preload or `font-display: swap` the critical Mulish weights and trim unused ones.
7. **Re-run with full capture:** all 21 console errors, untruncated HTML including `<head>`, and axe on every step. That will allow checking canonical, meta, structured data and viewport tags.
