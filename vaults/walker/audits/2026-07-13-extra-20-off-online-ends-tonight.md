# Extra 20% off online ends TONIGHT
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. Famous Footwear has now officially sent me a promotional email every single day this week — by my count this makes ten sends in seven days from these folks. I recognized the sender before I even read the subject line, and not in a good way. My first thought when I saw the preview column in my inbox was "didn't I just delete one of these yesterday?" Because I did.
- Once I opened it, the hero is a big FLASH 30% OFF banner, which is fine — big, readable, the sale is front and center. But the subject said "Extra 20% off," and now the body is saying "30% off." Those two numbers do not match, and at 62 years old I've been on enough car lots and flipped through enough Sunday circulars to notice when a number changes between the window sticker and the showroom floor. That sets off a small alarm.
- The content below the hero is almost entirely women's sandals — 60% off women's sandals, kids' sandals, Birkenstock drops. There's a slim Brooks section near the bottom with a shoe at $109.99 that's actually in my wheelhouse, but by the time I scroll that far I've already mentally checked out. I'd mark this one read, probably wouldn't click, and I would not be forwarding it to anyone.

## What's working

- **Brooks section made it in.** A real men's walking/running shoe at a specific price ($109.99) — that's the only moment in this email where I felt like I was the intended customer.
- **Hero is big and legible.** Large text, high contrast, no squinting required on a phone screen. The 30% off reads instantly.
- **Free shipping for Rewards members** is called out clearly near the top — I'm a Rewards member and I noticed it.
- **Birkenstock callout.** I know the brand, I know it means comfort sandals. "Limited time price drops" is plain language I understand.

## What's weak

- **Raw tracking URL in the preheader — again.** Second Famous Footwear email in a row where the preview text in my inbox was a raw `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/...` link instead of real copy. This is a repeat mistake and it still looks like a phishing attempt before I even open it.
- **20% off in the subject, 30% off in the hero.** That mismatch erodes trust immediately. I shouldn't have to do math to figure out if these are the same sale or different ones.
- **Mostly women's and kids' content.** The 60% off women's sandals section and the kids' sandals grid are not remotely targeted at me. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for comfort walking shoes.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Ten emails in seven days. I like Famous Footwear, but this is starting to feel like the brand doesn't know when to stop talking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — immediately, this send.** Put actual copy there: something like "Flash sale on Birkenstock, Brooks + more — today only" would have been infinitely better than a tracking URL. This is a straightforward production error that's happened twice in a row now.
- 2. **Reconcile the 20% / 30% messaging.** If the flash sale is 30% off and the "extra 20%" is a stacking offer on clearance, say that plainly. If they're different pools, segment the email. Confusion is a click-killer.
- 3. **Promote the Brooks / men's comfort section higher.** If my account history shows I'm buying men's walking shoes, lead with the Brooks shoe, not a 60% off women's sandals header.
- 4. **One fewer email this week.** Seriously. Pull back to three sends a week maximum. Scarcity makes urgency credible; when everything ends "TONIGHT," nothing does.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks, Birkenstock + more — 30% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Flash sale: comfort brands marked down, ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Birkenstock price drops, Brooks deals, free shipping for Rewards members — today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your flash sale — comfort styles marked down. Free shipping for Rewards members.`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Extra 20% Off" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with table-based layout and Responsys/ExactTarget-era ESP tooling. No structural rendering failures detected, but deliverability is at risk from an abnormally high URL-to-text ratio in the plain-text part, and accessibility is broadly degraded by missing alt text across all content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **31 click-tracking links** all route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` — standard Responsys redirect. Destination URLs could not be probed (expected; tracking domains block HEAD requests).
- **Open pixel** present: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?6QJMIHVEOBGELM3T6M42THG3K4.70251` — single 1×1 `<img>` with empty `alt=""`. Functional; will be blocked by Apple MPP and Gmail image proxy (expected).
- No broken or malformed `href` values detected in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — missing alt text (28 images):** Every content image in the email has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. This affects the logo, hero banner, product GIFs, divider bars, and footer assets. Confirmed images include:

- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`
- Hero/dividers: `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` (appears 4×)
- Product GIFs: `fd389a3a...gif`, `34edefc5...gif`
- 20+ product/CTA images

When images are blocked (Outlook default, corporate mail), the email renders as a blank table grid — no fallback text communicates the offer or CTA.

**Additional rendering notes:**
- `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag prevents pinch-zoom on mobile — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).
- `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the `http-equiv` attribute: correct form is `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Most clients tolerate this but it is malformed.
- `lineapacing` typo on a `<td>` style attribute (`lineapacing:20px`) — not a valid CSS property; likely meant `line-height`.
- HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype will trigger quirks mode in some clients; a basic XHTML 1.0 Transitional or no-doctype approach is more predictable for table-based email.
