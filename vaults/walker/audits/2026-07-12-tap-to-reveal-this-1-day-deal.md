# Tap to reveal this 1-DAY DEAL
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again — another DSW email, and this time they decided to play a game with me. "Tap to reveal" — I've been around long enough to know that when a store hides the price tag, it's usually not because the number is embarrassing for them. After getting hammered with six or seven nearly identical $10-off pitches over the past few days, I'll give DSW credit for trying something different. At least it's not another "LAST DAY" banner.
- But let me be honest: the entire body of this email is just a giant "we're not going to tell you." There's a young woman in sunglasses staring off into the distance, some vague "SUMMER SAVINGS" language, and an orange button. No product. No price. No men's shoes whatsoever. I'm 62, I want to know if my feet are going to feel better in whatever they're selling before I bother clicking anything.
- Mark as read. I'm not playing scratch-off with my Saturday morning. If the deal were worth talking about, they'd talk about it.

## What's working

- **Subject line changed the pattern.** After six "$10 off" emails in three days, "Tap to reveal this 1-DAY DEAL" at least felt like a different pitch — my thumb almost hesitated before I scrolled on.
- **Clean layout, no broken images.** Everything rendered fine on my phone; the hierarchy is clear and nothing was fighting for attention.
- **DSW VIP perks block is a solid reminder.** Free shipping and 2% back in rewards — that's real value, and it's the kind of thing I actually use.
- **"1-DAY" urgency reads as credible** — more believable than the three consecutive "LAST DAY" emails they sent earlier this week.

## What's weak

- **I have no idea what the offer is.** That's the whole point of the email and they buried it behind a click. For someone who wants to know if something's worth their time before committing, this is off-putting.
- **Zero men's shoes anywhere.** Young woman model, no comfort product, no walking shoe, no arch support — nothing in the visual that speaks to me at all.
- **"Your wallet will thank you" is filler.** That's the entire preview text. It tells me nothing and doesn't push me any closer to tapping.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Seven emails in three days. Even with a new gimmick, DSW has spent my goodwill down to near zero this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the deal.** Mystery mechanics work on impulse buyers. I'm a deliberate shopper — if there's a real offer, show it in the hero. "Save 20% on men's comfort styles — today only" would have had me scrolling down immediately.
- 2. **Put one men's comfort product in frame.** A cushioned walking shoe or a slip-on with arch support next to that orange button would do more than any amount of "SUMMER SAVINGS" copy.
- 3. **Dial back the send frequency.** Seven emails in three days has trained me to deprioritize DSW in my inbox. Space these out or segment better — one well-targeted send beats seven blasts.
- 4. **Preheader needs to do real work.** "Your wallet will thank you" wastes the second line of real estate. Tell me the category or the floor on the discount.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on two meta tags:**

```html
<!-- As-sent -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Required form -->
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, neither tag does anything. The charset declaration is especially risky — some older Outlook/webmail renderers rely on the explicit `http-equiv="Content-Type"` form.

**`Src:` (capitalized) in all `@font-face` blocks:**

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
}
```

CSS property names are technically case-insensitive per spec, but several email client CSS parsers (notably older Gmail Android) treat this as an unknown property and silently skip the font load. All 10 `@font-face` declarations share this defect.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Users with low vision cannot pinch-to-zoom on mobile.

**Near-black colors are non-standard but likely intentional:**

- `border-top-black` uses `#000001` (not `#000000`)
- Dark mode `dm-bg-black` uses `#00000F`

These are a known workaround to prevent Outlook from auto-converting pure `#000000` to transparent. Not a bug, but worth documenting.
