# The Secret's in the Sole
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I'll be straight with you — this email almost didn't get opened. When I glanced at my inbox, the preview text wasn't a hook, a deal, or even a sentence. It was raw code gibberish — JSON brackets and URLs spilling across the preview field. That's the kind of thing that makes a 62-year-old think the email got corrupted in transit. I only opened it because I know the Skechers sender address by heart at this point; seven emails in seven days will do that.
- Once I got inside, though, the story changed. The BOGO banner is right there at the top, same deal I saw in the May 14th send and the Memorial Day push on the 16th — buy one, get one 50% off, or 50% off a single pair. I know that offer. And then below it I see "ARCH FIT," "HANDS FREE SLIP-INS," "MAX CUSHIONING" — those are my words, my priorities, right on the page. The hero image even shows a guy about my age out for a walk. This email knows who it's talking to.
- So: would I click? Yes, probably the Glide-Step or Hands Free section — both show up strong and I've been meaning to replace my walking pair. Forward to a friend? No. This is a personal shoe hunt, not a group shopping trip.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% banner leads the email** — same deal I've been seeing from Skechers this week, and it still grabs me. Price clarity above the fold is exactly right.
- **Comfort features are front and center** — Arch Fit, Hands Free Slip-Ins, and Max Cushioning are my three priorities named explicitly, not buried in fine print.
- **Hero image matches my demographic** — the guy in the main photo looks like he's in his late 50s or early 60s, dressed in casual, practical clothes. That's rare in shoe emails.
- **Glide-Step has star ratings visible** — I trust reviews. Seeing four-plus stars on a specific shoe pushes me toward clicking that section.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — everything renders, the buttons are large, I didn't have to pinch or zoom to read the offer.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is too cute and too vague** — "The Secret's in the Sole" tells me nothing about an offer, a product, or why I should open it right now. After the much more direct "Hands-Free Hassle-Free" subject two weeks ago, this feels like a step backward.
- **The preview text is a technical disaster** — raw JSON schema markup leaked into the preheader. That is not copy. That is not a hook. In the inbox it looked like a corrupted message.
- **"Explore the Tech" CTA doesn't match me** — I don't open Skechers emails because I care about technology. I open them because my feet hurt less. That button copy is for a different customer.
- **25% off apparel section is noise** — I came here for shoes. The clothing block at the bottom dilutes the message and adds scroll distance between me and the CTAs I actually want.
- **Eighth email in eight days** — the cadence is starting to blur. I couldn't immediately tell you what made this one different from the Memorial Day email two days ago.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — the JSON schema leak is a production bug. Whatever structured data block is generating that markup needs to be moved out of the preheader slot. The cost is one confused open out of every ten.
- 2. **Lead the subject with the specific comfort hook, not a riddle** — something like "Your Arch Fit + BOGO 50% Is Here" does more work than a pun. Walker has limited patience for cleverness when he's scanning fifty emails on his phone.
- 3. **Swap "Explore the Tech" for comfort-first copy** — "Shop Comfort Collections" or "Find Your Fit" would land better for the Boomer male who opened this email based on brand recognition, not tech curiosity.
- 4. **Pull one signature product into the hero** — the May 14th email with BOGO front-and-center on a specific shoe did this better. Five competing product grids (Arch Fit, Hands Free, Max Cushioning, Glide-Step, Cozy Fit) make it hard to decide where to click first.
- 5. **Either cut the apparel section or target it separately** — if the data shows Walker buys apparel, he hasn't seen it. If he doesn't, that block is just dead weight below the fold.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — SKECHERS "The Secret's in the Sole" (2026-05-17)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with standard ESP scaffolding, but ships with a confirmed merge-token failure (empty greeting), a third-party pixel served over plain HTTP, and 12+ content images served over HTTP from `image.emails.skechers.com` — all at risk of silent blocking in security-conscious clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken destination links confirmed** — all 27 click-redirect URLs pass through `click.emails.skechers.com` and were correctly skipped by the HTTP probe (expected behavior for tracked links).

**Pixel integrity — minor concern:** The open-tracking pixel at `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` is referenced without a protocol prefix (`src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/..."` — no `http://` or `https://`). Protocol-relative or bare-host URLs will resolve to `http://` in most clients, risking a mixed-content block.

**Third-party match pixels:** Two `beacon.krxd.net` pixels and one `ink1000.com` pixel fire on open. This is normal DMP/audience-sync behavior but is flagged for disclosure compliance review (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — high risk of blocking (12 instances):**
All content images are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com enforce HTTPS-only image loading; these images will silently fail to render for a significant portion of recipients.

Affected assets (sample):
- `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-…png` (Skechers logo)
- `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-…png` (missing alt)
- App Store, Google Play, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons — all HTTP

**Missing alt text (2 images confirmed):**
- `o.gif` tracking pixel at `ink1000.com` — no `alt` attribute
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — no `alt` attribute; content image with no fallback text

**CSS display toggling conflict:** `.mobile-content { display: block }` / `.desktop-content { display: none }` are set in a non-media-query block, meaning the desktop default is hidden before any `@media` rule fires. The `@media screen and (min-width: 768px)` block corrects this for compliant clients, but clients that ignore media queries (Outlook desktop, some older Android Gmail) will show mobile layout regardless of viewport.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** applied globally (`* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }`). This suppresses iOS auto-scaling on all text, including small legal copy, which can make legally required disclosure text unreadable on small screens.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**CRITICAL — Empty greeting slot (confirmed FAIL):**
QA reports `"Hi ,"` — the name merge token rendered empty. This indicates the subscriber record has a null/blank first-name field and no fallback value was defined in the ESP template logic.

Fix: add a conditional fallback in the template, e.g.:
```
%%[IF @firstName != "" THEN]%%Hi %%=v(@firstName)=%%,%%[ELSE]%%Hi there,%%[ENDIF]%%
```
(AMPscript syntax for Salesforce Marketing Cloud, which the ESP fingerprint suggests.)

No other unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical address and unsubscribe mechanism not visible in the truncated HTML — these must be present; assume present in the footer below the truncation point.

**Third-party data sync on open:**
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif` — fires twice with `partner_uid` and SHA-256 hashed email (`9153f33f…ced00`). This is cross-site audience matching. GDPR/CCPA consent capture should be verified upstream before this pixel fires for EU/CA subscribers.
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif` — fires an ad impression event (`confid=so3yexmpm`) on email open, attributing to campaign `MKG_US_NONPUROPEN_U_COMF_NONPURCH_EN_05172026`. Same consent concern applies.

**Authentication headers** not inspectable from HTML source alone; SPF/DKIM/DMARC status for `emails.skechers.com` should be verified via mail headers in the delivered message.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `MKG_US_NONPUROPEN_U_COMF_NONPURCH_EN_05172026` is present in both the Krux ad impression pixel and the `ink1000.com` pixel, indicating consistent internal campaign tagging.

UTM parameter coverage on destination links cannot be confirmed without probing the redirected URLs behind `click.emails.skechers.com`. The QA tool skipped all 27 tracking links. UTM presence on landing pages should be spot-checked manually or via a redirect-following audit tool.
