# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again — that same pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker, front and center under "READY TO CHECK OUT?" like I've got a cart waiting on it. I don't. This makes six times this week I've been shown a women's floral sneaker like I'm about to buy it for myself, and it's starting to feel like Skechers doesn't actually know who's on the other end of this email.
- The one bright spot buried down the page is the 20% off Skechers Plus sitewide code — that's a real, honest offer with a code and a date, and if I'm already a member that's worth a look. But it's stuck below a wall of cart-nagging and a grid of shoes that's mostly women's and kids' styles, with maybe one pair — the men's Delson slip-ons — that's actually in my lane.
- I'm not clicking this one. I'll scroll past it same as the last five, and if this keeps up I'm going to start ignoring Skechers emails altogether, which is a shame because the "Classics Made for Every Step" send from a couple days back actually got it right.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus code (SXXPLUS) is a clear, specific, dated offer — that part reads honest.
- Men's Delson 3.0 slip-ons showed up in the product grid — slip-on, cushioned-looking, actually my category.
- Layout is clean and easy to scroll on my phone, big buttons, no clutter.
- The category nav (Women/Men/Kids/Clothing) at least lets me jump straight to men's if I want to bother.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker is back again — sixth straight day, still not my cart, still not my size, still not my shoe.
- "READY TO CHECK OUT?" implies I abandoned something, but I never put anything in a cart — feels like a guilt trip, not a reminder.
- Product grid is overwhelmingly women's and boys' styles; a 62-year-old guy has to hunt for the one relevant pair.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or comfort fit anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to buy.
- This is the fifth or sixth near-identical "your cart" email this week — the cadence alone is annoying at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop the fake-cart emails entirely for people who never added anything — or at minimum, rotate the hero product based on what I've actually browsed, not a blanket floral sneaker.
- 2. Lead with the 20% off Plus code up top instead of burying it two-thirds down — that's the one thing in here worth opening for.
- 3. If you're going to show me a product grid, weight it toward men's comfort styles for a recipient like me instead of defaulting to the same mixed catalog every send.
- 4. Bring back the comfort language — arch support, cushioned sole, slip-on ease — that the "Classics Made for Every Step" email used well.
