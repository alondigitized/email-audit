# Save an extra 30% right now
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Alright, here's adidas again — and I've lost count, but this has to be the tenth or eleventh email from them in the last couple of weeks. The subject promised "an extra 30% right now," which actually got my finger moving because a real discount is the one thing that might get me to consider adidas for a pair of walkers. But when I opened it, the whole thing is "End of May Savings" repeated twice, the same headline, the same paragraph — like they couldn't decide which block to delete and just left both in. No products. No shoes. No men's casuals. Just a faded blue background and a code: SCORE.
- The only image with anything specific is a FIFA World Cup ticket giveaway up top — soccer again, which is the umpteenth time they've leaned on the World Cup with me. There's no hero product, no "here's a slip-on for $59," nothing my eye can actually land on and want.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward this? Absolutely not — there's nothing to forward. I'd mark it read and move on, and honestly I'm getting close to unsubscribing.

## What's working

- The 30% off number is right there in big letters — I can see the deal without squinting.
- "SCORE" as a promo code is easy to remember, I'll give them that.
- Sender name "adidas" is clear and I recognize it.
- Text is large enough that I didn't need to zoom in on my phone.

## What's weak

- The same "End of May Savings / Save 30% on Summer Staples" block is repeated twice back to back — looks like a layout mistake or lazy template work.
- Not a single shoe pictured. For a footwear brand emailing a guy who buys shoes, that's a swing and a miss.
- "Exclusions apply" with no hint of what's excluded — last time I tried a code like this the comfort styles I wanted were locked out.
- Top banner is another World Cup tie-in. I'm 62, I'm not entering a soccer ticket sweepstakes, and they keep hitting that beat — same as the May 18 and May 23 sends.
- No CTA button I can see. Where do I tap? It's all text — I have to figure out the link myself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show a product. Anywhere. A men's Cloudfoam, an Ultraboost walker, a slip-on — give my eye something to land on that says "this is for you."
- 2. Delete the duplicate "End of May Savings" block. One headline, one paragraph, move on.
- 3. Add a real button — "Shop Men's Comfort" or "Shop Walking Shoes" — not a buried text link.
- 4. Spell out what's excluded right under the offer, or say "sitewide, no exclusions on these comfort styles."
- 5. Drop the FIFA ticket banner for the comfort-shopper segment. Eleven World Cup mentions in two weeks is too many.

# Technical Audit — adidas "Save an extra 30% right now"

## 1. Technical Summary
Email contains a malformed click-tracking URL that breaks the primary CTA destination, and ships without a plain-text MIME alternative. Multiple `http://` image references and missing `alt` attributes degrade accessibility and deliverability.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken primary link**: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/084c9b64-0580-46cc-a159-c1e691fbb74b?cp_tp=v3.eJz...` returned `unknown url type: '/g/084c9b64-0580-46cc-a159-c1e691fbb74b?txn=f6868580-5921-11f1-a25d-6e9b95041fa3'` — the redirect target appears to be a relative path missing its scheme/host, indicating a misconfigured redirect rule on `dv.adidas.com`.
- 36 click-redirect tracking links were not HTTP-probed by the QA harness; status of the remaining CTAs is unverified.
- Click tracker `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` uses plain HTTP — many MUAs upgrade or block insecure tracking endpoints.
