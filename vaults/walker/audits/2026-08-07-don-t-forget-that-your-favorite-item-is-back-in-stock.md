# Don't forget that your favorite item is back in stock!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Now this one's actually got a shoe I'd look twice at — a white and navy slip-in with what looks like a good chunky sole, none of that hot pink or Care Bears nonsense that's been cluttering my inbox from these folks all week. That said, my gut reaction is still "another Skechers email, already?" — this makes what feels like the seventh or eighth one from them this week, and by now I'm numb to the drumbeat. There's also a big empty gap between the headline and the shoe photo that looks like something didn't load right, which made me squint at my phone for a second wondering if I was missing content.
- I'd actually give this one a longer look than the cart-reminder emails from earlier this week, mostly because the shoe itself fits what I'd wear and it's not stuffed with stock photos of women's colorways up top. But there's no price anywhere on this thing — not the original, not a sale price, nothing — so I've got no idea if "back in stock" means "back in stock at full price" or if there's a deal here. That's a dealbreaker for actually clicking through for me.
- Bottom line: I'd glance at it, maybe tap through out of curiosity about the price, but I wouldn't forward this to anybody, and if the cadence from this brand doesn't slow down I'm going to start ignoring the sender outright.

## What's working

- The hero shoe — white/navy Skechers Slip-ins Contour Foam — actually looks like something a guy my age would wear, unlike the pink high-tops and kids' Care Bears shoes from the last two "forgot something" emails.
- "Slip-ins" is hands-free, which is exactly the kind of feature that gets my attention — I just wish they'd said so in words instead of making me infer it from the product name.
- The "BUY IT NOW" button is big, black, and impossible to miss — no hunting around for the call to action.
- Layout is clean and uncluttered — one hero product, then a simple related-items grid, nothing screaming at me.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere. Not the original price, not a discount, nothing. I shop on the price-versus-quality math, and this email gives me zero to work with.
- There's a big blank patch of white space above the shoe photo that looks like a banner or image that didn't render — makes the email feel unfinished before I even get to the good part.
- The size row is a jumble of numbers separated by squiggly dashes (1.0~1.5~2.0~2.5...) — hard to read at a glance and not how I'd expect sizing info to be laid out.
- The "You Might Also Be Interested In" grid swings right back to pink, cream, and a Martha Stewart collab shoe — feels like it was built for a totally different shopper than the one who'd click the hero item.
- Sixth-plus email this week from this brand (cart reminders Monday and today, best-sellers yesterday, now a restock alert) — I'm starting to feel spammed rather than informed.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price on the hero product — even just "$89.99" would do more for my click decision than anything else on this page.
- 2. Fix or remove that blank gap above the shoe photo — it reads as broken on a phone screen.
- 3. Clean up the size display into a normal list or dropdown instead of the tilde-separated string.
- 4. Match the "you might also like" grid to the hero item's audience — more men's/unisex colorways, fewer pink and cream picks right under a navy slip-in.
- 5. Slow the send cadence, or at least vary the subject lines more — I'm getting cart reminders and restock alerts that all blur together by Thursday.
