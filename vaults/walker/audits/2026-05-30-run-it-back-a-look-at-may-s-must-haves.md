# Run it Back: A Look at May's Must-Haves
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Skechers emails almost every day for the better part of two weeks, and this one feels like the brand hit a deadline, grabbed whatever was left in the folder, and hit send. "Run it Back" is basically an admission that they're recycling — and sure enough, that "May's Most Wanted" hero image looks like it walked straight out of the May 28th send. Same BOGO 50% offer I've seen since at least the 24th. On May 30th, with one day left in the month, this feels like cleanup, not a real promotion.
- The product categories are where it really falls apart for me. Low Profile Sneakers. Boat Shoes. Colorful Looks. I'm not a guy shopping for boat shoes — I'm a guy who wants something cushioned I can slip on for a long walk or a day on my feet. Not one section in this email mentions comfort, arch support, or cushioning. This reads like they grabbed the "men's style" template instead of the "men's comfort" one.
- Mark as read. I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is still front and center** — I see it in the banner immediately, no hunting required.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — banner, hero, product rows, footer. My eyes knew where to go.
- **"SHOP CASUAL SNEAKERS" and "SHOP BOAT SHOES" buttons are clear** — at least I know exactly where each CTA takes me.
- **No broken images or layout mess in the body** — the email itself renders fine.

## What's weak

- **The preheader is JSON code garbage. Again.** I've been staring at `[{ "@context": "http://schema.org/"...` in my inbox since at least May 17th. Nobody fixed it. At this point it's embarrassing.
- **Low Profile Sneakers and Boat Shoes aren't my world** — I want cushioning, arch support, GOwalk or Arch Fit. Style-first categories feel like a different mailing list.
- **"Run it Back" is filler language** — a month-end recap email signals the brand ran out of ideas, not that there's something worth coming back for.
- **25% off Apparel at the bottom** — I came here for shoes. Apparel is a total miss for me.
- **Same BOGO offer, same hero creative as two days ago** — I remember the May 28th email. This feels like a copy-paste.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the broken JSON preheader — this week, not next sprint.** It's been leaking for two weeks across every send. One bad preview line is enough to lose the open before the email even loads.
- 2. **Swap the product sections for comfort-first categories** — if I'm on a men's comfort/walking list, show me GOwalk, Arch Fit, or slip-ons with cushioning specs visible. Not Low Profile and Boat Shoes.
- 3. **Give the BOGO offer a visible expiration** — "Ends tonight" or "Last day in May" creates real urgency. Right now it's the same deal I've seen for eight days with no deadline.
- 4. **Don't resend the same hero creative two days later** — if the May 28 send already used "May's Most Wanted" imagery, this one needs something fresher to feel like a distinct send.
- 5. **Cut the apparel block entirely** — or move it to a dedicated apparel segment. It dilutes the shoe offer for a shopper like me.

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken merge token produces a visible personalization defect for all affected recipients. Eleven image assets — including the Skechers logo — are served over HTTP, risking silent blocking in Gmail, Apple Mail, and any client with mixed-content enforcement enabled.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path pixel missing protocol prefix — will silently fail**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
The `src` value has no `https://` prefix. Email clients will treat this as a relative URL, resolving it against nothing; the pixel never fires. Sender reputation measurement via Return Path is broken for this send.

**Third-party DMP pixels present**
Three `beacon.krxd.net` pixels fire on open: two user-match calls and one impression pixel tagged with campaign ID `MKG_US_NONPURCLICK_U_MULTI_NONPURCH_EN_05302026`. These are standard Salesforce/Krux DMP syncs; no anomaly, but worth noting for privacy disclosure purposes.

**26 click-redirect links unprobed**
All outbound links route through `click.emails.skechers.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 26. Final destinations and UTM coverage are unverified (see §6).

Gmail has enforced HTTPS image proxying since 2013; non-HTTPS sources are either blocked or rewritten through Google's proxy, which can break campaign-level impression tracking on the ink1000 pixel.

**Missing alt text**
`49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` has no `alt` attribute. If this is a content-bearing image (product shot, CTA graphic), it renders as a blank for image-blocked clients.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applying this globally disables iOS font scaling for all elements, including body copy. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) expects text to scale to 200% without loss. This should be scoped to headings or removed.

**Duplicate media query blocks**
The `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` blocks contain identical rule sets. A third block at `480px` duplicates `.show` and `.mobile-hidden`. No rendering defect, but it adds ~2 KB of redundant CSS that counts against Gmail's 102 KB clip threshold.

The merge tag for first name is either unmapped, blank in the subscriber record for this segment, or referencing the wrong field key. This affects every recipient who hits this send without a populated first-name value and is the single highest-priority defect in this email.
