# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-08**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through most of this trip fine. Then it broke where it counts. I picked a men's slip-in, and the site wouldn't let me buy it. I tapped Add to Cart without choosing a size, and it showed "Please select a size" in red. The cart badge stayed at 0. The cart page (step 8) came up as a **completely blank white screen**, with no cart, no message and no way back. A man who wants to give them his money gets nothing.

I never got logged in, and nothing in the screenshots felt personal. The search for "comfort shoes" did return results, but the first two I saw were women's styles.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday, so I see no improvement. Browsing is decent, but I can't complete the one thing the site exists for. I'm giving no credit for the pricing tools or the nav, because the cart page is blank.

## 3. What's Working

- **The Men's menu (step 4) is easy to use.** The rows are big and well spaced, with arrows and a clear "Back" and "X". The text is large enough that I didn't need to zoom. My thumb can hit every row.
- **The "Men's Shoes" page (step 5) has the right shortcuts.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two tiles. That's what I shop for.
- **The top nav has "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning."** Arch Fit and Wide Fit are what I look for. They're right under the logo.
- **The product page shows price and reviews up front.** It's $90.00, four stars and 17 reviews, with a Klarna/Afterpay breakdown ("$16/month"). The Hands Free Slip-ins badge is clear.
- **The Add to Cart button is big and bright blue.** It's easy to tap.
- **The search box found 1,647 results.** The Contour Foam and Relaxed Fit shoes it showed are the right kind of product for me.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page is blank (step 8).** This is the worst problem. I can't tell whether anything was added, and I can't check out.
- **Add to Cart didn't work.** The page said "Please select a size" and the cart still showed 0. The page jumped me down to the size grid, but nothing told me what to do next.
- **Most sizes are crossed out.** Sizes 6.5 through 10.0 and 11.5 are struck through with little bell icons. Only 10.5, 11, 12, 13 and 14 are open. A 62-year-old doesn't know what the bell means. That's a lot of crossed-out sizes on a shoe I'm trying to buy.
- **"Excluded from promotions."** The top banner says "20% OFF Regular Priced Items" and "30% OFF Select Styles." The product page then says my shoe is excluded. I understand it, but it feels like a bait-and-switch.
- **The homepage doesn't speak to me.** The hero is a loud Britto art collab with a woman in leggings. I see nothing about comfort, arch support or hands-free. The Slip-ins and Arch Fit links are in a small row that cuts off ("Max Cus…").
- **The login step looks identical to the homepage (step 3).** I saw no sign I was logged in: no name, no "Welcome back" and no member pricing. The search page even says "Members Get 20% OFF! Applied at Cart", which suggests I'm not recognized as a member.
- **The popup step showed nothing to dismiss.** The only change was that the banner text swapped to "25% OFF Work & Safety." Rotating banners that move while I'm reading them are annoying.
- **The first search results aren't for me.** They're women's wedge loafers and women's sneakers. I didn't see a men's filter or a "Men's" label on the results page.
- **I saw no price-vs-value or brand comparison anywhere.** There's nothing that helps me decide against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page.** It must never render blank. Show the item, the price and a Checkout button. If the cart can't load, show an error message and a link back to shopping.
2. **Make the size error easier to understand.** Add a "Select a size" prompt above the button, or pre-select a size that's in stock. Then say what happened, e.g. "Added to cart" with a clear View Cart button.
3. **Explain the crossed-out sizes.** Label the bell as "Notify me when back in stock." Also show how many sizes are in stock, or recommend a similar shoe.
4. **Be upfront about promotions on the product page.** Say "Excluded from promotions" near the price, and make the banner offers say which items they don't apply to.
5. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Lead with Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Wide Fit in a bigger tile row. Don't hide them behind a sideways scroll.
6. **Make personalization visible when I'm logged in.** Show "Hi, Walker," my member pricing and my saved size, and add a "Men's" tag or filter on search results.
7. **Add a plain comparison and value block.** For example, "Skechers vs. Hoka and Brooks: cushioning and price per month of wear."

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the menus and the slip-in and arch-support shortcuts. But I can't buy the shoe. The size selection tripped me up, then the cart went blank. A shopper my age doesn't retry; he goes to Brooks, Hoka or the DSW sale aisle. I'm scoring this **3/10, no better than yesterday**. Fix the cart first and everything else gets a lot easier.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Only TTFB was captured for each step. LCP and CLS came back as `?` for all 8 steps, so there are no Core Web Vitals to assess.
- The captured HTML for "Log In" and "Men category" is identical to the homepage, and all three report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Those steps most likely did not navigate, or they captured the wrong document. Findings for those steps are unreliable.
- `/cart/` returned **429**. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare error or rate-limit page, not a real cart. The cart step was probably not audited.
- 17 axe violations: 2 critical, 2 serious on the homepage, 2 serious on View Cart, and the rest moderate or minor.
- 21 console errors from two repeating sources, both outside Skechers' own code.

## Accessibility

**Homepage**
- `aria-required-parent` (critical): ARIA roles such as `listitem` or `tab` sit outside their required parent containers.
- `button-name` (critical): buttons have no accessible text, probably icon-only controls without `aria-label`.
- `link-name` (serious): links have no discernible text, probably image or icon links.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` (moderate): there are multiple or nested `<main>` landmarks and non-top-level `<aside>` elements. This suggests a template that nests full page shells.
- `region` (moderate): content sits outside landmarks.
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor): a role is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- The same `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` violations repeat. This points to a shared template, so one fix would clear both pages.

**View Cart**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (serious), `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` (moderate). These probably come from the 429 response body. Re-test against a real cart.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`, so that part is fine.
- Page-level `<title>`, meta description, canonical, `<h1>` and structured data can't be checked because the HTML samples are truncated before `<body>`.
- The View Cart page has no `<title>` and no `<h1>`. If that is the real cart markup, it should also carry `noindex`, but I can't confirm that from the sample.
- Per-page canonicals can't be verified for Log In or Men category, since the captured URLs are all the homepage.

## Performance

- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not collected, so the instrumentation (likely `PerformanceObserver` timing or SPA navigation handling) needs fixing.
- **TTFB:** the homepage is 441ms, and Log In and Men are 350ms, all under the 800ms "good" threshold. The 21–25ms values for Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search are consistent with cache hits or same-document (SPA) transitions, not fresh document loads.
- **Third-party load:** the `<head>` already injects 25+ third-party scripts. These include GTM, Monetate (two scripts), TikTok (three), Pinterest, Yahoo (`ytc.js`, twice), Bing, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Quantum Metric, Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Bloomreach, Pulse Insights and Tune. Main-thread contention is likely, and it is the most probable cause of any LCP and INP problems.
- **GTM preview parameters:** `gtm.js?...&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=...&gtm_cookies_win=x` is loading a non-default GTM environment. That is normal for staging and unusual for production. If it is production, it may serve unpublished tag changes.
- **Webfont loader:** fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, which adds a third-party DNS and TLS hop plus a JS dependency on the critical path. Self-hosted `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` and preload would avoid that.
- **Duplicate loads:** `ytc.js` appears twice and `tags.creativecdn.com` appears twice on the Men page, so tag deduplication is worth checking.
- **bfcache:** `unload` handlers (see below) block back/forward cache eligibility.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.64px` is set inline by JavaScript, which implies a viewport of about 664px high. That is the legacy `--vh` workaround. CSS `dvh`/`svh` units remove the JS dependency and the resize reflows it causes.
- Viewport meta, tap-target sizing and responsive images can't be checked from the truncated samples. The unlabeled buttons and links flagged above also affect screen readers on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** the cart was rate-limited or blocked. This is likely bot mitigation reacting to automated traffic (add-to-cart followed by an immediate cart request). It could also be a real cart-endpoint throttling problem for shoppers behind shared IPs.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (×4):** this comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with unsupported language codes. The caller is probably a third-party script or browser feature, not first-party code. It is not user-visible, but it adds console noise.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document" (×7):** a script registers an `unload` listener, and the page's Permissions-Policy blocks it. The source is probably an analytics or tag-manager vendor. Besides the console noise, the listener doesn't work and unload-based tracking silently fails.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the audit harness.** Confirm that Log In and Men navigate and record their real URLs. Add retry or backoff for the 429, then re-run the cart step. Capture LCP and CLS, using `buffered: true` observers or the `web-vitals` library.
2. **Fix critical a11y issues.** Add accessible names to the unlabeled buttons (`button-name`) and links (`link-name`). Repair the parent-role structures behind `aria-required-parent`.
3. **Fix the landmark structure.** Use exactly one top-level `<main>`, make `<aside>` elements top-level, and give duplicate landmarks distinct `aria-label`s. One template fix should clear both the homepage and search violations.
4. **Investigate the 429 on `/cart/`.** Check the rate-limit or WAF rules for this path, and make sure real shoppers can't hit them.
5. **Audit the tag load.** Defer or consolidate third-party pixels through a single tag manager, and remove duplicate loads. Verify that the production GTM container isn't pointing at `env-1` preview.
6. **Replace `unload` with `pagehide` / `visibilitychange`** in whichever script registers the handler. This also restores bfcache eligibility.
7. **Self-host fonts** and drop the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency.
8. **Replace the JS `--vh` workaround** with `dvh`/`svh`.
