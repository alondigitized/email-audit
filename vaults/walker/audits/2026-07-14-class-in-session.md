# Class in session.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Another one from New Balance. By my count — and I've been keeping count — this is somewhere around eleven or twelve emails in the past two weeks, and this one lands with a back-to-school theme dressed up in fashion-sneaker clothing. "Class in session." The hero is a moody, editorial shot that feels like it belongs in a magazine for someone half my age. "Back to basics" is the headline, which I'll admit is at least sensible, but the whole scroll is lifestyle product after lifestyle product — grid after grid of low-profile retro silhouettes on what looks like a school-locker backdrop. Nothing here is saying "Walker, here's a shoe for your plantar fasciitis."
- The one thing that actually catches my eye is buried at the very bottom: "Take an extra 20% off select markdowns." That's real. That's something I'd act on from New Balance if the right shoe is in that sale. But it's the last thing I see, not the first, and by the time I get there I've already scrolled past half a dozen fashion shots that weren't built for a 62-year-old man's feet. I'll mark this read, maybe check the markdowns page out of habit, but I'm not forwarding this to anybody.
- Would I click? Probably one tap on the markdown offer — but only because I've been trained by their recent sends that the sale section sometimes has a 990 or a 1080 in there. The email itself didn't earn that click.

## What's working

- **Men's CTA is right there in the hero** — "Shop Men's" is the first button I see, and that's the right call. No having to hunt for my category.
- **"Back to basics" headline is honest** — doesn't overpromise, and at this point in the season I'm okay with a lifestyle reset message.
- **20% off markdowns at the bottom is a real offer** — plain dollar-and-cents language, no percentage of a percentage chicanery visible here.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — renders fine on screen, text is readable, nothing is chopped off or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Back-to-school framing is a complete mismatch for me** — I'm 62, not putting my kid on a bus. "Class in session" earns zero emotional response from this inbox.
- **The offer is below the fold** — the 20% markdown deal is the only thing with real pull, and they hid it at the bottom under two full product grids and a trending section.
- **Every product I can see is a lifestyle/fashion silhouette** — I'm looking for cushioning, arch support, maybe a walking shoe. All I see are retro low-tops staged on a chair like a photo shoot.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere visible** — not a single mention of cushioning, wide width, support, or anything that addresses why I actually buy New Balance.
- **Cadence is burning me out** — they sent a "Take an extra 20% off" email on July 10th. This send four days later undercuts that email's urgency and makes me trust their sale cycles less, not more.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the markdown offer, not the lifestyle editorial.** Flip the email. Put "Extra 20% off select markdowns — today through Thursday" in the hero, not a moody chair photo. That's the only thing that has buying-intent pull for me.
- 2. **Filter the product grid to men's comfort and walking styles.** If they know I've been opening their emails for two weeks, they know I'm not clicking the fashion sneaker. Surface the 990, the 1080, the Fresh Foam — shoes with actual support language attached.
- 3. **Add one fit/comfort detail line.** Even one bullet — "Fresh Foam cushioning, extra-wide available" — would make me feel like the email was written for my feet, not just my age bracket.
- 4. **Cut email frequency or vary the creative harder.** I got a 20% off email four days ago. Sending the same mechanic again this week makes me feel like their pricing is arbitrary.

## Technical Audit — New Balance "Class in session."

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses an HTML 4.01 Transitional base (standard for email) with dark mode support layered on top. Several template/build artifacts are present in the delivered source, and all custom `@font-face` declarations are structurally broken.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. Issues visible in the `<head>`:

- **HTTP background image with unfilled placeholder token**: `.bgwhite { background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png') }` — two separate problems here:
  1. `http://` (not `https://`) will trigger mixed-content blocking in Gmail, Outlook web, and Apple Mail when the outer page is HTTPS.
  2. `XXXXX` is an unreplaced template variable. The image will 404 in production.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@font-face` declarations are all missing `src:`**

Every `@font-face` block declares `font-family`, `font-weight`, and `font-style` but omits the required `src:` property. Without a source URL, no custom font loads in any client — `proxima-nova` and `linotype-didot` will silently fall back to `Arial` / `Times New Roman` for all subscribers. This is not a graceful degradation; it's a broken declaration that will never load the intended typeface.

```css
/* Example — all @font-face blocks follow this broken pattern */
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova, sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: normal;
  /* src: missing */
}
```

**Duplicate property in bold-italic `@font-face`**

The `proxima-nova` bold-italic rule has a copy-paste error — `font-family` is declared twice, with the second declaration overwriting the first mid-rule:

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova, sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: italic; font-family: proxima-nova, sans-serif;  /* ← duplicate */
}
```

**Malformed `<meta>` for IE compatibility**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

The `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute is missing. This tag is silently ignored by all parsers and does nothing.

**Limited responsive breakpoint coverage**

The only mobile media query targets a single `.image1` class:

```css
@media only screen and (max-width:414px) {
  .image1 { width:auto !important; height:auto !important; max-width:100% !important; }
}
```

If there are additional images or layout tables in the body (likely), they have no responsive rules. Cannot confirm severity without the full HTML.

**Dark mode**: Partial implementation is visible (`color-scheme: light dark` meta, `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` with body and link overrides). Truncation prevents full audit of dark-mode color coverage for body content.
