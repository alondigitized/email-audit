# Ends TODAY: $14.98 Sandal Blowout
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen emails in fourteen days, and today I open to a sandal blowout at $14.98 — that number stopped my thumb for a second. Price-first subject lines work on me, and "Ends TODAY" does add a little push. But then I open the email and it's a teenager with a backpack under a "Final Bell Deals" banner, and half the content is Skechers Kids and something called "The Dude." I'm sixty-two years old. My grandkids are grown. I'm not shopping for a fifth grader.
- The sandal blowout hero is right up top and visible, and the price anchors the whole thing. But then my eye slides down and it's family athletics, kids' brands, and a wall of barcode coupons that look like a grocery store circular. The $20 off code buried near the bottom is actually more useful to me than the $14.98 starting price — which almost certainly means foam flip-flops, not anything with arch support. The starting-at language always worries me a little.
- I'd mark this read and move on. No click, no forward. If they'd sent me the same SAVEMORE code over a men's comfort sandal or walking shoe hero, they might have caught me. But this one's aimed at a mom getting kids ready for school, not me.

## What's working

- **$14.98 hero price is front and center** — the number is large and lands immediately, even on a small screen.
- **"Ends TODAY" urgency is credible** — matches the subject line and doesn't feel manufactured.
- **SAVEMORE coupon code is visible** — stackable discount codes are exactly the kind of thing I notice and use.
- **In-store barcode coupons at the bottom** — I actually go into physical Shoe Carnivals; having a printable/showable coupon has value.
- **No render bugs** — email loaded cleanly, no broken images, text didn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **The entire hero is Back-to-School** — a teenage girl with a school backpack has nothing to do with my shopping. The BTS framing undercuts the sandal offer the second my eye moves past the price.
- **Skechers Kids and "The Dude" sections are dead weight for me** — I'm here for adult men's comfort footwear; this email is 60% family/kids.
- **"Starting at $14.98" almost certainly means bottom-tier foam sandals** — no mention of cushioning, arch support, or brand name. That starting price raises more questions than it answers.
- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — whoever set up this deployment broke the preheader. I'd see a garbled link in my inbox before I even open, which looks like spam.
- **Day 14 of daily emails** — the fatigue is real. I'm not annoyed enough to unsubscribe yet, but the goodwill is wearing thin.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero to an adult men's comfort sandal with a named brand** — if there's a Skechers Foamies or Crocs deal in that blowout, put that front and center. A recognizable comfort brand at $14.98 would make me click. A faceless "starting at" with a BTS backdrop makes me scroll past.
- 2. **Drop the Kids and Family sections entirely, or push them below the fold** — Walker-type customers don't need to navigate around kids' content to find what they want.
- 3. **Fix the preheader immediately** — a tracking URL in the preview slot looks like phishing. That's a broken deployment, not a style choice.
- 4. **Move the SAVEMORE code higher** — it's the best offer in the email and it's buried. Put it right under the hero.
- 5. **Call out specific product details** — even one line like "Memory foam footbed, wide widths available" next to the sandal hero would change my calculus entirely.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — malformed IE-compatibility meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. As written, this is a no-op; it will not force IE/Outlook edge rendering. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Confirmed issue — charset meta placement:**
The `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` appears *after* two `<style>` blocks. The charset declaration must be the first element inside `<head>` (or within the first 1024 bytes of the document) to prevent encoding sniffing issues in some clients. Current order: `<style>` → `<style>` → `<meta charset>`.

**Confirmed issue — no dark mode support:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Email explicitly opts out of dark mode. Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS, and Gmail Android will force-invert or apply their own dark overrides on `light`-only emails, which commonly breaks white backgrounds used as spacers/borders, reverting them to black. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override block is present to control this.

**Minor — redundant `color: inherit` declarations:**
```css
color:inherit !important; color:inherit;
```
Duplicate property in the global anchor override rule. Harmless but indicates unclean CSS generation.

**Minor — `.ExternalClass` Hotmail hacks are legacy dead code.** Hotmail/Windows Live migrated to Outlook.com in 2013; these selectors have no effect in any current client. Not a breakage issue.
