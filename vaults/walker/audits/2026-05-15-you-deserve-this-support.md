# You deserve this support 🧡
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Crocs is still in my inbox — this is something like the seventh or eighth email in two weeks from them. I opened this one because "You deserve this support" had me wondering if they were finally talking about arch support or cushioning. Maybe a proper walking shoe for guys like me. Nope. This is an entirely different product — work clogs for kitchen and restaurant workers. Slip-resistant soles, enclosed toe, easy to clean with soap and water. Every inch of it is designed for someone standing on a greasy kitchen floor for eight hours, not a retired guy walking the neighborhood in the morning.
- The email itself is clean and renders fine on my phone, so I'll give them that. The three-section layout — Long Days, Busy Days, Ready When Your Shift Is — flows logically and the feature callouts on the product are actually readable. But none of it is me. I'm not on a shift. I'm not worried about spills on my shoes. I don't need an enclosed toe. I need something that slips on without bending down, cushions my heel, and holds up on pavement.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. And honestly, if I keep getting emails that aren't about anything I'd actually wear, I'm going to start hitting unsubscribe pretty soon.

## What's working

- **Email renders cleanly on mobile** — no broken images, no overlapping text, readable font size throughout.
- **Feature callouts on the product image** — "Slip-resistant tread," "Enclosed toe," "Easy to clean" are labeled right on the shoe. Clear if you're in their target market.
- **CTA buttons are legible** — "Shop Clogs," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals" are big enough for my thumbs and easy to tap.
- **No spam noise in the subject** — short, no all-caps, only one emoji. At least it doesn't feel like a shouting infomercial.

## What's weak

- **Wrong audience entirely** — this email is built for food service workers. "Ready When Your Shift Is" and the Bistro Work Clog have nothing to do with how I shop or what I need.
- **Preview text is completely broken** — pure garbage characters in the inbox. I see nothing useful before I open. This is the second or third Crocs email in a row that's done this.
- **No offer or discount anywhere** — there's a "Shop Sale" button buried at the bottom alongside three other CTAs, but no number, no percentage, no dollar amount. The welcome email two days ago gave me 15% off. Now I get nothing?
- **Four CTAs with no hierarchy** — "Shop Clogs," "Shop Jibbitz Charms," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals" are all the same size at the bottom. I don't know what they want me to do.
- **Cadence is getting heavy** — this is the third Crocs email in three days. I'm starting to tune them out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list** — anyone who opened or clicked on the welcome 15%-off email is a potential casual buyer; send them casual clogs, not kitchen gear. "Ready When Your Shift Is" is for an entirely different customer.
- 2. **Fix the preview text** — every send I've seen from Crocs in the last week has Unicode junk in the preheader. That's a real inbox first-impression problem. Put something useful there.
- 3. **Lead with an offer** — even a soft one like "Free shipping on orders over $50" would give me a reason to keep reading.
- 4. **Reduce to one primary CTA** — pick the most relevant one and make it the clear call to action.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Cushioned clogs built for all-day comfort`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrival: clogs that feel good from first step`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Arch support, lightweight build, easy on and off — see what's new`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on your next pair. Shop clogs built for comfort.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 7 HTTP image sources** (will be blocked or proxied-to-broken in Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail on iOS 15+):

| Alt text | HTTP URL |
|---|---|
| Download on the App Store | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/9c36c0ef-…png` |
| Get It On Google Play | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/13246762-…png` |
| Find Your Store | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/c4df71ec-…png` |
| Instagram | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/e18949ff-…png` |
| Facebook | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/80f68cd8-…png` |
| TikTok | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/51d8144e-…png` |
| YouTube | `http://image.crocs-email.com/…/1187ef58-…png` |

All seven share the same CDN host (`image.crocs-email.com`) and almost certainly support HTTPS — this is a template misconfiguration, not a CDN limitation.

**Missing alt on tracking pixel:**
```html
<img src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?…" height="1" width="1">
```
No `alt=""` present. Screen readers will attempt to announce the URL. Should be `alt=""` with `role="presentation"` or at minimum an empty alt.

**Malformed charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, Outlook (MIME-aware) and some webmail renderers ignore this declaration and fall back to their default encoding.

**Custom font (`TT Crocs`):** Loaded via `@font-face` with `woff2`/`woff` sources. Will silently fail in Outlook (all versions) and Gmail Android. Verify system font fallback stack is defined in all text elements — not verifiable from truncated source.

**Positive:** `a[x-apple-data-detectors]`, `#new-gmail-hack`, `mso-hide`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and `format-detection` are all correctly implemented.
