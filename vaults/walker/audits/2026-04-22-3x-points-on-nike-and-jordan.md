# 3X points on Nike AND Jordan ⛹️
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-22**
## Executive summary

- **From:** Famous Footwear | **Subject:** 3X points on Nike AND Jordan ⛹️
- This email showed up in my inbox from Famous Footwear — a brand I don't really use. Right off the bat, the subject line got my attention because of the points angle, but once I opened it, I realized this whole thing is aimed at someone about 30 years younger than me. It's Jordan sneakers, Nike Air stuff, basketball gear, red caps — nothing I'd ever buy. I wear Skechers Slip-ins for comfort, not Jordans for the court. I felt like I accidentally walked into a teenager's section at the mall. The 25% off clearance callout near the bottom was the only thing that briefly caught my eye, but even that didn't have anything I'd actually want.
- Wrong brand, wrong category, wrong generation. This email has near-zero purchase intent for me personally.

## What's working

- **The 3X points headline is readable** — even on a small phone screen, the "EARN 3X POINTS" text is big and clear, and I understood the offer immediately.
- **The 25% off clearance box** stands out visually with its outlined red border. That kind of deal framing gets my attention even if the products aren't right.
- **The 2X points section** near the bottom adds another layered value signal — I appreciate seeing the rewards pitch more than once.

## What's weak

- **Completely wrong audience for me.** Jordan sneakers, basketball shoes, a red cap — nothing here speaks to a 62-year-old guy looking for casual comfort. I don't care about Nike Air or Jordan retros.
- **The preview text is a broken URL** — raw tracking link junk, not a real message. That alone almost made me skip the open entirely.
- **It's an app-gated deal.** The top banner says this is an "exclusive app deal" — get the app to earn these points. I don't want to download another app just to save some money at a store I barely use.
- **Too much product, too small.** The "More from Jordan" grid shows a bunch of small thumbnails that are nearly impossible to see clearly on a phone. I'd have to pinch and zoom just to know what I'm looking at.
- **Famous Footwear isn't my brand.** I'm a Skechers guy, loyal to their Plus program. This email from a competitor doesn't give me a reason to switch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this to me at all** — the segmentation is off. If Famous Footwear has any purchase or browse history on me, Jordans and Nike performance gear shouldn't be in the mix.
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately.** A raw tracking URL as preview text is sloppy and unprofessional. At minimum, write something like "Earn triple points this week — exclusive app offer."
- 3. **Lead with the clearance deal earlier.** The 25% off section is buried below the fold. If the goal is to pull in a broader audience, that's the hook.
- 4. **Make the app download pitch optional**, not a requirement. I'm not downloading an app to access a deal. Show me the offer, then suggest the app as an enhancement.
- 5. **Bigger thumbnails or fewer products.** On mobile, I can barely see the shoes in the Jordan grid. Either show fewer items bigger, or cut the module entirely.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear: "3X points on Nike AND Jordan ⛹️"

**From:** famousfootwear@email.famousfootwear.com

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is functionally deliverable but carries systemic accessibility deficits (20+ images without alt text) and an unverified authentication chain through the AgentMail relay. One mixed-content HTTP image load is a hard rendering failure risk in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — HIGH**
QA flagged 6,271 of 8,701 plain-text characters (72%) as URLs. This ratio is a known spam-filter signal, particularly for Outlook/Gmail filtering heuristics. The plain-text part is likely auto-generated from the HTML with raw tracking URLs unexpanded.

**26 click-redirect links unverified**
All 26 tracked links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). HTTP probing was skipped, so broken-link status is unconfirmed. No direct destination URLs are visible in the truncated source to spot-check landing page alignment.

**Open pixel — present and correct**
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?7E2REV4JKKJE5B5KWR6DMCNKVM.70246
```
1×1 pixel present, rendered with `alt=""` — structurally correct.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed content — HTTP image load (FAIL)**
One image loads over plain HTTP:
```
src="http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NTR9..."
```
Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail) will block or strip this image. This appears to be a LiveContent/dynamic-content block — likely the loyalty points or personalized offer. Its failure silently removes functional content for a large share of recipients.

**20 images missing alt text (FAIL)**
Every product and promotional image in the email has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Affected assets include all hero/product images (`04978a9a`, `dd42b8ea`, `37dc6084`, etc.) plus a GIF (`1433c3b2`). When images are blocked — default behavior in many clients — recipients see a blank email with no fallback text. Screen reader users receive no information content.

The logo image (`3b6a7dc1`) also has `alt=""`. Acceptable for purely decorative marks, but if this is the only brand identifier in images-off mode, it should carry `alt="Famous Footwear"`.

**Duplicate image references**
`4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` (a 2px-tall spacer/divider image) appears 4 times in QA output. Spacer images should use `alt=""` and `role="presentation"` — confirm these are purely decorative, not content-bearing dividers.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disabling user scaling is a WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Visually impaired users who rely on pinch-zoom on mobile cannot resize content. This is a widespread ESP template default but should be flagged.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**
```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
Industry-standard for legacy email clients (Outlook). No functional issue, but confirms no HTML5 semantic elements should be expected.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` — missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. While most clients tolerate this, it is technically malformed and could cause charset mis-detection in edge cases. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No visible unrendered merge tokens (`%%`, `{{`, `[[`) in the truncated HTML. The "Join FAMOUSLY YOU REWARDS" block is hardcoded rather than personalized — expected behavior for a broadcast loyalty upsell. No issues detected from available source.

### 5. Compliance

**SPF/DKIM authentication — UNVERIFIED (WARN)**
QA found no `Authentication-Results` header through the AgentMail relay. Without confirmed SPF pass + DKIM signature from `email.famousfootwear.com`, deliverability to major providers is at risk. The sending domain is a dedicated ESP subdomain (Salesforce MC pattern) which should be authenticated — this needs to be verified against the raw received headers of an actual inbox copy.

**CAN-SPAM — not fully assessable**
The truncated HTML does not include the footer. Cannot confirm presence of:
- Physical mailing address
- Unsubscribe mechanism / opt-out link

These are required by CAN-SPAM §5. The email uses a dedicated `email.famousfootwear.com` subdomain (compliant sender identification), and the `From:` display name matches the sending domain. Footer must be verified in the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 outbound links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (SFMC click tracking). UTM parameter passthrough cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects, which were skipped by the HTTP probe. Key question: do resolved URLs include campaign/medium/source parameters so GA4 attributes sessions correctly rather than falling into direct/none?

Without resolved destination URLs, landing page alignment (e.g., does the Nike/Jordan link go to a filtered category page vs. homepage?) cannot be verified from this source.
