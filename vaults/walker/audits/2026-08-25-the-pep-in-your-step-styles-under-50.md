## Executive summary

- My first reaction scrolling through this one: I don't see myself anywhere in it. The hero shot is a teenage cheerleader mid-jump — "Put Some Pep In Your Step" — which tells me this is a back-to-school pep rally email, not a shoe email for a guy my age. I kept scrolling waiting for something I'd actually wear and found two options buried in the "Under $100" row — the Nike Air Max Invigor and the New Balance 515 — the rest is kids' Nike Court Borough, Converse Chuck Taylors, and women's Mary Janes and Havana Rush flats.
- To be fair, the coupon codes are real and I can read them without squinting — $20 off $99.98, $10 off $74.98, code SAVEMORE — and the Shoe Perks points bump is clearly laid out. So the mechanics of the email are solid, it's just aimed at somebody's teenager, not me.
- I'm not clicking this one. Nothing in the hero or the grid speaks to comfort, cushioning, or arch support, and I'm not digging through a "Kids/Women's Under $50" sneaker wall to find the one Air Max Invigor buried at the bottom. Straight to read, not forwarded — unless my granddaughter needs cleats, this isn't going anywhere.

## What's working

- The coupon codes are front and center and easy to read — $20 off $99.98+, $10 off $74.98+ — no fine print hunting required.
- "Lowest Price Guarantee" badge on the hero is the kind of trust cue that actually lands with me — I like knowing I'm not getting fleeced.
- Layout is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, the price-tier banners (Under $50/$75/$100) make it easy to scan even if the products in them aren't for me.
- Shoe Perks points multiplier (2-3x) is called out clearly if you're already a member.

## What's weak

- The hero is a cheerleader — I'm a 62-year-old guy, this doesn't speak to me at all, and it's the same school-season beat Shoe Carnival's been running all week (the backpack quiz on 8/24, the school supplies points push on 8/22).
- Almost the entire product grid is kids' and women's sneakers — Nike Court Borough, Adidas VL Court, Chuck Taylors, Havana Rush flats. I had to scroll past three price tiers to find two men's shoes worth a second look.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to click isn't in this email at all.
- Preview text in my inbox is just a raw tracking link, not real copy — looks broken before I even open it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this list — send me a "Men's Comfort Under $100" version instead of a shared grid where I'm hunting for two shoes out of a dozen kids' and women's styles.
- 2. Lead the hero with the actual offer ($20 off $99.98+) instead of a cheerleader jump shot — I don't need a mascot, I need a number.
- 3. Fix the preview text — right now it's a raw URL dumping into my inbox, which looks like spam before I even open the email.
- 4. Put a men's comfort shoe (something like the Air Max Invigor or a walking shoe with visible cushioning) in the hero slot if you're going to target guys like me at all.
