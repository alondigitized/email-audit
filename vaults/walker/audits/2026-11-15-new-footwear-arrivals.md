# New footwear arrivals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- Well, finally a New Balance email that opens with shoes, after a week of shopping bags and ballplayers. A group of fellows sitting on stools, a big "New Arrivals" headline, and then six sneakers in a grid. That's the right order. The trouble is that these are fashion sneakers, not walkers. A chunky 983, a cream 750 and a brown 574 are what a young guy wears with a hoodie, not what I wear on a three-mile walk.
- The good news is that the "Walking" link is down in the footer. The bad news is that it's buried below the fold, and nothing up top tells me which of these has cushioning or arch support. The 25% off deal doesn't show up until the very bottom, so I almost missed it. Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity, but I'd probably go straight to the Walking link. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The hero is a clean "New Arrivals" headline with two plain black buttons. I can find them without squinting.
- The six-shoe grid has each style's name underneath. Simple, and easy to scroll with my thumb.
- "Free shipping on all orders" sits right at the top. That's an honest, easy-to-understand perk.
- The 25% off deal states the dates and the $125 minimum plainly. I know where I stand.
- A "Walking" link is in the footer, so the category exists.

## What's weak

- Every shoe is a lifestyle sneaker. There's no hint of cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease, which is what I shop for.
- The models are young and trendy, so I don't see myself in it. The shoe names are just numbers, with no price or description.
- The 25% offer is buried at the bottom after a long scroll, past the gifts and the ornaments.
- The "Gifts for everyone" and ornament sections feel tacked on. The email can't decide whether it's about new shoes or Christmas, and it's the fourth holiday-flavored send this week.
- The "Shop now" links under the gift and offer sections are tiny, thin text. They're hard for me to tap on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 25% off offer up into the hero, or add a banner right under the free-shipping bar. The best deal shouldn't be the last thing I see.
- 2. Add a row of walking and comfort styles, such as the 990 or Fresh Foam, with a short note like "all-day cushioning". Put the "Walking" link up top, not in the footer.
- 3. Show prices under the shoe names, and add a one-line benefit for each shoe.
- 4. Make the small text links into real buttons, like the "Shop all" one.
- 5. Pick one theme per send, either new arrivals or gifts.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: new shoes for every step`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save 25% when you spend $125+ through Nov 18, and shipping is free.`

# Technical Audit: New Balance, "New footwear arrivals"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based ESP template (sender `newbalance@email.newbalance.com`, tracking domain `link.email.newbalance.com`) with dark-mode support. The QA run found no failures, 2 warnings and a 75% pass rate. The source and QA data show leftover template artifacts, 3 images with no alt attribute and an unusually link-heavy plain-text part. The HTML provided is truncated, so the body, footer and link inventory could not be fully audited.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text part is URL-dominated.** 11,748 of 13,591 characters (about 86%) are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. This is typical of long ESP-wrapped tracking URLs. It can hurt spam scoring and makes the text version hard to read.
- **Placeholder asset URL in CSS.** `.bgwhite { background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png'); }` was never replaced. It is also plain `http://`, so it is a mixed-content and broken-asset risk if `.bgwhite` is applied anywhere.
- **Link coverage is partial.** Only 12 links were probed and 28 skipped, ranked by click likelihood. Redirect and landing-page status for the skipped links is unverified. The QA output doesn't show per-link results, so I can't confirm any broken links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 3 images (QA-confirmed):**
  - `…/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `…/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12652402_5041411_23920459.gif`, which looks like the open-tracking pixel.

  The pixel should carry `alt=""` explicitly. The two shipping images are content and need descriptive alt text, or `alt=""` if decorative.
- **Template title not updated.** `<title>New Balance Template</title>`.
- **Malformed meta tag.** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing.
- **Mixed doctype and namespace.** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is combined with an `xmlns` attribute and self-closing XHTML-style tags. This is harmless in most clients but inconsistent.
- **Duplicate class definition.** `.bg1` is defined as `#ffffff`, then redefined as `#58546d`. The later rule overrides the earlier one, which is likely unintended.
- **`@font-face` rules have no `src`.** The rules for `proxima-nova`, `linotype-didot` and others declare no source, so they load nothing. Clients will fall back to `Arial` or `Times New Roman`.
- **Dark mode relies on `!important` overrides.** `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` sets `a {color:#cf0a2c !important}` globally. Contrast on dark backgrounds can't be verified from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the truncated HTML. The `XXXXX` in the CSS URL is a placeholder, covered in section 2. I can't verify the body for tokens because it was cut off.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication unverified.** The `Authentication-Results` header was not found. The QA note attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is a monitoring blind spot, not evidence of a failure.
- **Unsubscribe and postal address not confirmed.** The footer is beyond the truncation point. QA says an unsubscribe link is always included in probing, but the output doesn't report its result. I can't verify a physical address, `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers.
- The sending domain (`email.newbalance.com`) and the tracking domain (`link.email.newbalance.com`) share a parent domain, which is consistent for alignment.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Asset path points to a different campaign.** Both shipping images live under `/custloads/818512772/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/`, a welcome-series path, yet this is a new-arrivals email. This is likely reused modules. It may mean stale shipping messaging, but the visible content doesn't show that.
- **UTM parameters can't be assessed.** Links are wrapped in the ESP tracker, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the provided source or QA output.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`. Set `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
2. Remove or replace the `http://image.XXXXX.png` placeholder in `.bgwhite`. If it is kept, switch it to `https`.
3. Update `<title>` from "New Balance Template" to a real title, and fix or remove the `<meta content="IE=edge" />` tag.
4. Resolve the duplicate `.bg1` rule, and either give the `@font-face` blocks a `src` or remove them.
5. Check that the shipping modules in the `myNB_Welcome` asset path are current for this campaign.
6. Shorten the plain-text part by trimming tracking-URL length or reducing link count, so the text-to-URL ratio drops.
7. Get SPF, DKIM and DMARC results from the original message headers, since the relay drops `Authentication-Results`. Also check `List-Unsubscribe` and the footer address from the full HTML.
8. Re-run the audit with untruncated HTML and a wider link-probe budget so the footer, UTM and skipped-link checks can be completed.
