# Your Mondays, Unboxed: Gifts That Keep Giving
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again. That's the fifth or sixth one this week, and it's the same $10 off / $25 off HOLIDAY coupon I saw on Friday the 13th. The subject says "Mondays, Unboxed," but the email doesn't look like anything unboxed. It opens on a Black Friday Preview banner and the coupon. After that comes a long catalog of kids' clogs, women's boots and fuzzy slippers.
- The coupon is easy to read, even without my glasses. After that I'm scrolling past women's and girls' boots and Crocs, and there's almost nothing for a 62-year-old man who wants cushioning and arch support. The only men's shoe I saw is the Nike Air Max Fire. I'd skim it, maybe tap the coupon, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The "$10 OFF $74.98+ or $25 OFF $149.98+" coupon with code HOLIDAY is big and readable on my phone.
- The coupon barcodes near the bottom let me scan at the store without hunting for a code.
- The "Add to Mobile Wallet" option is handy.
- The Shoe Carnival logo and the Black Friday banner match what I've seen from them all week, so I trust it's real.
- The bottom nav (Womens, Mens, Kids, Accessories) is big and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "Gifts That Keep Giving" and a Monday theme, but the body is the same coupon and sale as last Friday. It's repetitive.
- Nearly all of the shoes are women's or kids' (Heydude Wendy, juicy winter boots, Makalu boots, Steve Madden). Very little is for me.
- Nothing mentions comfort, arch support, cushioning or slip-on, and no Skechers, Hoka or Brooks shows up. Crocs is the only comfort-style brand.
- The "Comfy Mistle-toes" section is fuzzy fashion boots, not comfort shoes in my sense.
- The product captions are small. I'd have to pinch and zoom to read them.
- The fine print is a block of tiny gray text, and the exclusions are buried in it.
- The email is very long, and several Play, SMS and Afterpay boxes sit between me and the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the content match the subject. Lead with one real gift idea, with men's comfort picks near the top: slip-ons, walking shoes, cushioned sneakers.
- 2. Cut the repeated coupon banners and reduce the length. One offer block is enough.
- 3. Add a "Men's comfort picks" row with brand names and price. Larger captions would help too.
- 4. Put the exclusions right under the coupon in readable type.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Shoe Carnival, "Your Mondays, Unboxed: Gifts That Keep Giving"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has no hard failures, but the QA data and the visible source show a likely unresolved merge placeholder (`?d=[UNIQUE]`) in a tracking pixel, a malformed UTM pixel URL, and several accessibility gaps. SPF/DKIM/DMARC status and link destinations couldn't be verified (see sections 2 and 5).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved placeholder in a tracking pixel.** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains a literal `[UNIQUE]`. This is probably a template token that wasn't substituted at send time. If so, that open pixel can't attribute opens to a recipient. It could also be a vendor convention, so confirm with the ESP.
- **Malformed pixel URL.** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261116_mwk_start_your_week_AM​` has two problems:
  - The value of `t=` is itself `utm_campaign=...`, a nested key=value inside one parameter. Parsers that split on `=` may read it incorrectly.
  - The URL ends with an invisible character, a zero-width space (U+200B) in the QA output. This can break strict URL parsing or matching.
- **59 tracking links were not probed.** The click-redirect domains were skipped, so no destination, status code, or redirect chain was verified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Link styling is stripped globally.** The `<style>` block sets `a, a:link, a:visited, a:hover, a:active { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }`. Inline text links therefore have no underline or colour distinction unless styled another way, which hurts link discoverability (WCAG 1.4.1).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Content images missing alt text:**
  - `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`

  If these are linked or informational, screen-reader users and image-blocked clients get nothing. Use descriptive alt text, or `alt=""` for purely decorative images.
- **Tracking pixels missing alt text.** Both `emltrk.com` pixels have no `alt`. This is low severity, but `alt=""` is cleaner.
- **Small mobile text.** `.responsive-text { font-size: 12px !important; }` at `max-width: 600px` sets 12px on mobile, below the common 14px minimum.
- **Invalid meta tags.** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` also lacks `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute still works, so this is cosmetic.
- **Mixed markup.** The HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is combined with `xmlns` and XHTML-style self-closing tags. This is harmless in practice and not a functional issue.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- `?d=[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` pixel appears unresolved (see section 2).
- The visible truncated source shows no other unresolved tokens such as `{{first_name}}` or `%%var%%`. The remainder was truncated, so it couldn't be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 11,550 of 14,722 characters (~78%) are URLs. Long tracking URLs in the text/plain alternative can raise spam-filter scores and make the text version hard to read.
- **Authentication results unavailable.** No `Authentication-Results` header was found. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment is unknown. This is a measurement gap, not evidence of failure.
- **Not verifiable.** The footer (physical address, unsubscribe link) and the `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers were not in the provided data. No finding is made.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM evidence is the pixel's `utm_campaign=SCA_20261116_mwk_start_your_week_AM`. The date matches the send date (2026-11-16, a Monday), and the name is consistent with the "Mondays" subject.
- `utm_source` and `utm_medium` were not observed, and click URLs were not resolved. Landing-page alignment and UTM propagation on the 59 skipped links could not be checked.
- The campaign UTM sits in the pixel's `t=` parameter. Confirm that the same convention is applied to the actual click URLs.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Confirm `?d=[UNIQUE]` is substituted per recipient. If it's a leaked template token, fix it in the ESP template.
2. Rebuild the `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` pixel URL. Remove the trailing U+200B and use a proper `&utm_campaign=...` parameter instead of `t=utm_campaign=...`.
3. Add descriptive `alt` to the two content images, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixels.
4. Restore underlines on inline text links, or limit the `text-decoration:none !important` rule to button and nav links.
5. Raise the mobile `.responsive-text` size from 12px to at least 14px.
6. Reduce URL density in the text/plain part by shortening or removing tracking parameters.
7. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml` (full headers and full HTML) to verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`, the footer address, and the tracked-link destinations and UTMs.
