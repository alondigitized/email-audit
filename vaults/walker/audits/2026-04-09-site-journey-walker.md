# Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Persona: Walker Miles | 62-year-old Male | Skechers Plus Member**
**Date: April 9, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Mixed bag today, and one catastrophic failure that overshadows any progress. The Men's category navigation is clean and organized — I can find "Hands Free Slip-ins" quickly from the menu, and the product page is readable with the deal clearly shown. But the homepage still treats me like I don't exist (full Mother's Day pink takeover), the cart page is a blank white screen (complete failure), and a search for "slip in" shows me Women's products first — even though I'm logged in as a male customer. Yesterday was rough at 4/10. Today doesn't improve much, and the blank cart is actually a step backward.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The cart failure alone is a conversion killer. I selected a shoe, tried to add it to cart, then hit a blank page. That's an abandoned purchase, full stop. No loyalty member should experience that.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO 50% OFF deal is persistent** — that banner stays at the top of every screen. As a price-conscious guy, I appreciated seeing it constantly. Didn't have to hunt for the deal.
- **Men's category navigation is solid** — clean list layout with "Hands Free Slip-ins" as a quick-tap shortcut right in the menu. No hunting required. Text is a good size, items are clearly separated.
- **Product page is readable** — big bold product name, price front and center at $135, deal reminder ("Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart"), and the Slip-ins badge. Felt like a real product page, not a cluttered mess.
- **261 Slip-in results** — good inventory depth, and width filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) are right at the top. That matters for a guy my age.
- **ADD TO CART button** — large, navy blue, full-width. Easy to tap. No complaints there.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Homepage: completely wrong audience** — I landed on a Mother's Day shop with a woman and little girl in pink flowers. I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for my own shoes. Not one piece of men's content above the fold. No "Welcome back, Walker." Nothing. I'm invisible.
- **Login step didn't register visually** — the screenshot after "Log In" looks identical to the homepage. No confirmation, no name in the header, no "Hi Walker" or Skechers Plus indicator. I don't know if I'm even logged in.
- **Cart page is a blank white screen** — this is the worst thing I saw today. I went through the whole process — found the shoe, looked at size options — and the cart rendered nothing. Zero. This is a broken purchase flow. A deal-breaker.
- **Size grid is cramped** — 14 sizes in a grid on a phone screen. The boxes aren't terrible, but for my fingers they're borderline. I'd accidentally tap 10.0 when I meant 10.5.
- **Search shows Women's first** — I searched "slip in" and got 1,024 results, but the first two visible products are both labeled "Women's." I'm logged in. Why am I seeing women's shoes? That's disrespectful to my time.
- **No personalization anywhere** — not on homepage, not after login, not in search. A Skechers Plus member with purchase history should see something tailored. I got nothing.
- **Popup dismiss step was invisible** — Step 2 looks identical to Step 1. Either there was no popup (fine) or it was dismissed so fast I couldn't see it. No issue either way, but the promo ticker text was cut off and unclear.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately** — blank cart page is a P0 bug. Revenue is walking out the door on every session this happens.
2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in members** — if I'm a Male Skechers Plus member who buys Slip-ins, show me men's content first. The Mother's Day banner can stay, but below a personalized hero.
3. **Show a logged-in confirmation** — add "Hi Walker" or a Plus member badge to the nav after login. I need to know my session is active.
4. **Fix search gender bias** — a logged-in male customer searching "slip in" should see Men's results first, not Women's. Simple personalization signal.
5. **Increase size selector tap targets** — slightly larger boxes or more spacing would reduce mis-taps for users my age.
6. **Surface Skechers Plus benefits earlier** — I'm a member. Remind me what I'm getting. Points balance, member pricing — none of that was visible.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones of a decent experience are there in the Men's navigation and product page. But the cart failing completely means I walked away without buying a $135 shoe I was ready to purchase. That's the whole ballgame. Until the cart works reliably and the homepage stops pretending I don't exist, this site is leaving loyal customers like me frustrated. **Score: 4/10. No improvement from yesterday.**

## Accessibility

**Critical (4):**
- `aria-required-children` — View Product: ARIA role used on parent without required child roles (e.g., `role="listbox"` missing `option` children)
- `aria-valid-attr-value` — View Product: malformed ARIA attribute values (likely `aria-controls` or `aria-labelledby` pointing to non-existent IDs)
- `button-name` — View Product: buttons rendered without accessible text (icon-only buttons with no `aria-label`)
- `image-alt` — View Product: `<img>` elements missing `alt` attribute, likely product images or swatches

**Serious (5):**

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh` custom property calculated in JS indicates awareness of the iOS Safari `100vh` bug. Correct approach, but floating-point precision artifact (`6.640000000000001px`) is benign unless used in pixel-snapped layouts.
- No viewport `<meta>` visible in truncated source — could not verify `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` is set. Absence would be a critical mobile rendering failure; this must be confirmed.
- `vice-managed-src` consent-deferred scripts load only after consent interaction — correct pattern for GDPR/CCPA compliance on mobile, but introduces timing variance in pixel fires.

## Console & Network Errors

**HTTP 429 — Kasada fingerprint endpoint (5 hits):**
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
```
Kasada's client-side challenge script is rate-limiting its own fingerprint collection calls. This indicates either: (a) the challenge is being re-fired too aggressively by the Kasada JS, (b) the session token is expiring/invalid, or (c) the test environment IP was flagged. In production this can interrupt bot-protection telemetry and potentially degrade protected endpoint behavior.

**HTTP 429 — Cart page:**
```
GET https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart endpoint is rate-limited. This is a direct conversion blocker — users hitting this during normal browsing cannot load their cart. Whether this is infrastructure capacity, Kasada misclassifying legitimate users, or a CDN misconfiguration needs investigation.

**CORS block — Stylitics (2 errors):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy
Access to XMLHttpRequest at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked
```
The Stylitics outfit/look widget stylesheet is being fetched both as a `<link>` and via XHR (likely a JS fallback), and both are blocked because `web-assets.stylitics.com` does not return `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. The widget will render unstyled or not at all. Fix requires Stylitics to add the CORS header on their CDN — Skechers should file a ticket with their Stylitics account team.

## Recommendations

1. **[P0] Investigate cart 429s** — Determine if rate limiting is hitting real users or only automated test agents. If production-affecting, adjust Kasada thresholds or CDN rate limit rules for `/cart/`.

2. **[P0] Fix Stylitics CORS** — Coordinate with Stylitics to enable `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `skechers.com` on `web-assets.stylitics.com`. The XHR fallback pattern for a CSS file is also architecturally incorrect — stylesheets should not be fetched via XHR.

3. **[P1] Fix 4 critical axe violations on View Product** — `image-alt`, `button-name`, `aria-required-children`, `aria-valid-attr-value` are all WCAG 2.1 Level A failures. Product pages are highest-value; these also create legal exposure.

4. **[P1] Instrument LCP and CLS** — All 8 steps are missing these metrics. Integrate `web-vitals` JS library or expand RUM (Quantum Metric may already have this — confirm dashboards). Without CLS data, the `--vh` JS and font loading impact is unknown.

5. **[P1] Deduplicate third-party tags** — Remove duplicate `ytc.js` (Yahoo) and `tags.creativecdn.com` loads. Audit GTM container for double-firing triggers. Each duplicate fires inflated conversion event counts.

6. **[P2] Replace WebFontLoader** — Migrate to `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` in CSS. `webfontloader.js` is unmaintained and adds a network round-trip before fonts render.

7. **[P2] Resolve landmark structure violations** — Nested `<main>`, duplicate `<main>`, and misplaced `<aside>`/`<header>` indicate template-level structural issues, likely in the Demandware page decorator layer. Fix in the base template affects all pages.

8. **[P3] Kasada 429s on fingerprint endpoint** — Review Kasada integration configuration. The challenge JS should not be triggering enough fingerprint requests to self-rate-limit within a single page session.
