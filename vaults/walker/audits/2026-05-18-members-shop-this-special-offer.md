# Members: shop this special offer
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- I open this and the first thing I see is a stack of empty shoe boxes next to a shopping bag. A shopping bag. Now I understand what they're selling — I just don't know why I'm looking at packaging instead of shoes. Still, the headline "An offer just for members" gets my attention fast enough. After getting the generic "Up to 25% off shoes" push on May 11 and the "Don't forget" nudge on May 16, seeing "members" in the subject actually made me feel like they knew I was already on the list and were giving me a first look at something. That's the right instinct from them.
- The offer itself is the clearest it's been in this whole stretch of New Balance emails — 25% off select shoes, 30% off clothing, deadline right there in the preview. The insoles section buried near the bottom surprised me in a good way. Insoles are something I actually think about; that's the arch support angle I never see addressed. But then I scroll to "More to explore" and I'm looking at two women's W990NV5s and a kids' shoe. One out of four products is aimed at me.
- I'd click — but almost in spite of the email's choices rather than because of them. Straight to the Men's sale tile and I'm gone from the shoe box photo. Wouldn't forward it; there's nothing in here a buddy would thank me for.

## What's working

- "An offer just for members" with a visible deadline lands as exclusive and time-sensitive — that combination works on me.
- Preview text "Offer ends May 25th" does exactly what preview text should do: tells me what the subject left out, without repeating it.
- The "Shop the Sale — Men / Women / Kids" tile row is clean and finger-friendly; I can tap straight to the men's section without hunting.
- The Fresh Foam Arishi v4 appeared in "More to explore" — Fresh Foam says cushioning to me, so I at least see one shoe for my feet.
- 25% off insoles is a genuine comfort-shopper hook; I use insoles for arch support and no previous New Balance email in my stack has touched that category.

## What's weak

- Hero image is a pile of empty shoe boxes and a shopping bag — I came to look at shoes, not packaging. Wasted visual real estate above the fold.
- Three of the four "More to explore" product photos are women's or kids' shoes; I'm a 62-year-old man, and the email knows that.
- The CTA button under the hero appears to say just "Shop" — a verb with no noun is not a direction, it's a shrug.
- This is the third 25% off shoes push in eight days (May 11, May 16, now May 18). The member-exclusive framing saves it, but the fatigue is real.
- Two different offer windows visible in the same email — main sale through May 25, insoles May 19–24 — requires me to read fine print to understand which clock applies to what.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the shoe-boxes hero for a men's comfort shoe in motion** — Fresh Foam, 990, anything on a foot. One hero product shot would triple the relevance signal instantly.
- 2. **Add "men's" or "comfort" language to the primary CTA button** — "Shop Men's Sale" beats "Shop" every time for a segmented audience.
- 3. **Filter "More to explore" by gender** — if you know I'm a male member, serve me four men's shoes, not a mixed rack.
- 4. **Surface the insoles deal higher** — put it right under the hero for comfort shoppers; don't bury the one comfort-specific offer at the bottom.
- 5. **Consolidate the offer windows or explain them clearly** — two different end dates in one email creates confusion I shouldn't have to work to resolve.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- If `XXXXX` is a placeholder and not redaction, this image URL is broken and the asset will 404.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS class collision — `.bg1` defined twice:**
```css
/* First definition (line ~6) */
.bg1 { background-color: #ffffff; }

/* Second definition (later in stylesheet) */
.bg1 { background:#58546d; }
```
The second rule wins via cascade. Any element using `.bg1` that was intended to render white will render as a dark purple (`#58546d`). This is a clear copy-paste bug.

**Malformed IE compatibility meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no effect in Outlook/IE rendering engines.

**`@font-face` declarations have no `src:` property:**
```css
@font-face { font-family: linotype-didot,serif; font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; }
@font-face { font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif; font-weight: 700; font-style: normal; }
/* etc. */
```
None of the nine `@font-face` blocks contain a `src:` URL. These declarations are no-ops — they declare names but load nothing. If these fonts are loaded via an Adobe Fonts `<link>` tag elsewhere in `<head>` (truncated), this is benign redundancy. If not, custom fonts will silently fall back to `Arial` / `Times New Roman` across all clients with no error.

**One malformed `@font-face` italic declaration:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: italic;
  font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif; /* duplicate property */
}
```
`font-family` is declared twice in the same rule — likely a copy-paste artifact. Harmless but indicates template hygiene issues.

**Dark mode implementation:** Correctly uses both `<meta name="color-scheme">` and `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` with `!important` overrides. No issues found here.

**HTML/XHTML mixing:** Document uses `HTML 4.01 Transitional` doctype with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`. This is intentional and standard in email for broad client compatibility. No issue.
