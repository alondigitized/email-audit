## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 6, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I came in looking for a comfortable slip-on I could actually slip on — and Skechers mostly pointed me in the right direction getting there. The Men's nav is clean, the Hands Free Slip-ins category is easy to find, and the BOGO deal auto-applying is a nice touch. But then it fell apart at the finish line. My cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen — I would have thought the site broke and closed the tab. And when I searched "comfort shoes," every result it showed me was Women's. I'm a logged-in man. That's not a minor slip; that's the site not knowing who I am. Same score as yesterday. Nothing got better where it counts.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **4 / 10**

*No change from yesterday. Navigation is passable, but the blank cart and gender-wrong search results are conversion killers.*

---

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO auto-applies.** The blue "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" banner is persistent across every page. I don't have to hunt for a code. That's respect for my time.

**Men's nav drawer is clean.** Big text, generous tap targets, clear hierarchy. "Shoes → Hands Free Slip-ins" took two taps. Even a "Comfort Technologies" subcategory right in the menu — that's exactly the kind of language that earns my attention.

**Fit filters are front and center.** Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit as pill buttons at the top of the 319-result Slip-ins page — I didn't have to dig. For someone with particular fit needs, that matters.

**Add to Cart button is excellent.** Full-width, bold blue, anchored to the bottom of the screen. Can't miss it, can't accidentally miss-tap it.

**Slip-ins in the top nav shortcut bar.** Right on the homepage — Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs — that's the four things someone like me actually wants. No hunting.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 was a white screen. Nothing. No items, no totals, no buttons. If this happened to me on my phone I'd assume the site crashed and go check DSW instead. This is the single most damaging issue on the site. It was dead yesterday and it's dead today.

**Search "comfort shoes" returned Women's products first.** 1,430 results and the first two I see — "Contour Foam - Cozy Fit Pure Comfort" and "Relaxed Fit: Glide-Step Comfort" — are both labeled **Women's**. I'm logged in as a man. The search engine is either ignoring my gender or the default sort surfaces Women's product first. Either way, I feel unseen.

**No evidence login actually worked.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 show identical screens — same hero, same nav, same everything. After I tapped the account icon, nothing on screen changed to confirm I was logged in. No "Welcome back, Walker." No personalized greeting. The cart still shows "0." I don't know if the site registered me at all.

**Hero speaks to a 35-year-old soccer fan, not me.** "No Early Checkout" with a soccer player holding a performance shoe. There's a small "Stay in Comfort" badge in the corner, but the visual leads with sport energy. I'm a 62-year-old looking for something comfortable for daily errands. This homepage isn't for me.

**Only 6 reviews on a $92 shoe.** That's thin. At that price point I need social proof. Hoka and New Balance product pages I've browsed have dozens of reviews. Six doesn't build confidence.

**Product page cart count stayed at 0 after "Add."** The cart icon stayed at "0" on the size selection screen. Either the add didn't fire, or the counter doesn't update — which feeds into why the cart was blank.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately.** A blank cart page is a hard stop on every single purchase. This is P0, not a nice-to-have.

2. **Lock search to logged-in gender.** If I'm logged in as a male account, "comfort shoes" search should default to Men's results or at minimum offer a gender toggle at the top. Showing Women's shoes first to a logged-in man is a personalization failure that erodes trust.

3. **Confirm login visually.** Show my name or a "Hi, Walker" somewhere in the header after I log in. Right now there's zero feedback that the login worked.

4. **Lead the homepage with comfort, not soccer.** Swap the hero — or at least rotate it — to feature a comfort/walking use case. Something like a man walking a trail or a dog would speak to me. The soccer campaign is fine for a different audience, but it shouldn't be the only thing I see when I land.

5. **Surface more reviews or a "be the first to review" prompt.** 6 reviews on a $92 shoe makes me nervous. Consider showing ratings from similar products or prompting more reviews at post-purchase.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers is a comfort-shoe brand that does comfort navigation reasonably well. The Men's menu, the fit filters, and the BOGO auto-apply all show they understand who I am as a shopper. But the blank cart and the gender-wrong search results mean the back half of the journey — the part where I actually buy something — is broken. Yesterday was 4/10. Today is still **4/10**. Until the cart works and search respects my gender, I'm browsing Skechers but buying somewhere else.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The site runs on **Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware)**, evidenced by `on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site` in script paths. The primary technical concerns are: (1) an active bot-detection rate-limiting failure affecting the cart page in production, (2) critical accessibility regressions on the View Cart page consistent with a degraded render, (3) a broken third-party CORS dependency (Stylitics), and (4) significant third-party script redundancy inflating page weight. LCP and CLS data were not captured, leaving two of three Core Web Vitals unmeasured.

---

## Accessibility

**16 violations across 3 steps.**

**View Cart — 2 serious violations (highest severity in the set):**
- `document-title`: `<title>` element is missing or empty. This is a WCAG 2.4.2 Level A failure, not a minor gap.
- `html-has-lang`: `<html>` element has no `lang` attribute — WCAG 3.1.1 Level A. These two together, combined with the 429 on `/cart/`, strongly suggest the cart page is serving a degraded/error response shell rather than the real page template.
- `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` follow from the same degraded render hypothesis.

**Homepage — 1 critical, 6 moderate, 1 minor:**
- `aria-required-parent` (critical): ARIA role present on an element outside its required parent container. Likely caused by a JS-rendered carousel or listbox widget where the parent container isn't in the DOM at parse time.
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-main-is-top-level`: Two `<main>` landmarks detected, one nested. Classic SFCC storefront issue where a third-party widget (Monetate, Quantum Metric injection) appends a second page shell.
- `heading-order`: Non-sequential heading levels — common when promotional banners use `<h2>` before `<h1>` for visual sizing.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`: Consistent with injected sidebar/aside widgets not placed in the primary DOM tree.

**Search — 3 moderate/minor:** Same `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, and `region` pattern as homepage, indicating the violations are in shared layout components, not page-specific code.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Even if the cart is `noindex`, a missing title degrades internal link signals and can cause Googlebot to misreport crawl anomalies.
- **Homepage heading order**: Crawlers use heading hierarchy to understand content structure; non-sequential h-tags on the homepage reduce topical signal clarity.
- **LCP not captured**: LCP is a Google ranking signal (Core Web Vitals). No data means the audit cannot confirm or deny a ranking impact. This needs a follow-up measurement via CrUX or lab tools.
- **TTFB on login/category (397ms)**: Both "Log In" and "Men category" steps show the same 397ms TTFB and the same base URL (`https://www.skechers.com/`) in HTML samples — the login is a modal overlay, not a separate page, and the category TTFB spike likely reflects server-side personalization or A/B test rendering delay (Monetate), not a separate route.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` CSS custom property is set inline on `<html>` — this is a standard mobile viewport-height fix for the iOS Safari 100vh bug, applied via JavaScript. Correct pattern, though the floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) indicates it's being set without rounding, which is harmless but sloppy.
- No `<meta name="viewport">` is visible in the truncated HTML samples — cannot confirm presence without a full source view. Should be verified.
- WebFontLoader (`cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/webfont/1.6.28/webfontloader.js`) is loaded `async` but triggers 6 Mulish font-weight loads (`wf-mulish-n3` through `n8`). On slow mobile connections this causes FOUT across 6 weight variants simultaneously.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — primary issue:**

The URL `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.543` is the **KapSolve (Akamai Bot Manager) client-side fingerprinting endpoint**. Five 429 responses on this endpoint indicate the bot protection layer is rate-limiting its own fingerprinting calls — this happens when: (a) the SDK fires too frequently on page transitions, or (b) the Akamai configuration has a misconfigured rate limit that fires on legitimate session activity. This will degrade real-user sessions that navigate quickly across multiple pages.

**`https://www.skechers.com/cart/` returning 429** is the critical failure: the cart page itself is rate-limited. This means the bot detection layer is blocking the cart render for automated test sessions and potentially for fast-navigating real users. A conversion-path page returning 429 is a direct revenue impact.

**CORS block on Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' from origin 'https://www.skechers.com' has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The `net::ERR_FAILED` immediately following is the stylesheet load failure. Stylitics's CDN (`web-assets.stylitics.com`) is not returning CORS headers for `skechers.com`. This breaks the "Shop the Look" / outfit widget on any page that loads it. This is a Stylitics-side configuration issue requiring a vendor ticket.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**
1. **Cart 429**: Investigate Akamai Bot Manager rules for `/cart/`. The cart route should be excluded from aggressive bot scoring or have a higher threshold to prevent legitimate users from being blocked.
2. **View Cart missing `<title>` and `lang`**: If these are rendering correctly in non-test conditions, the 429 is causing the error page shell to be evaluated. If they reproduce in a logged-in non-bot session, the cart page template has a regression in its base HTML.

**P1 — Fix within sprint:**
3. **KapSolve fingerprinting 429 storm**: Work with Akamai to tune the SDK fire rate or increase the per-session rate limit for the fingerprinting endpoint — 5 failures in a single user journey indicates misconfiguration.
4. **Stylitics CORS**: File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. The feature is silently broken.
5. **Duplicate script loads**: Remove the duplicate Yahoo pixel, duplicate CreativeCDN load on Men category, and resolve the Monetate v2/v3 dual-load. Each duplicate is an unnecessary network request and parse cycle on mobile.

**P2 — Technical debt:**
6. **Capture LCP and CLS**: Re-run the audit with Core Web Vitals instrumentation. TTFB alone is insufficient for a performance assessment.
7. **`aria-required-parent` (critical)**: Trace the widget that places an ARIA-role element outside its required parent — likely a carousel component that renders before its wrapper. Fix the DOM order or defer ARIA role assignment until after hydration.
8. **Homepage duplicate `<main>`**: Audit Monetate/third-party injection scripts for DOM writes that insert a second `<main>` element.
9. **WebFontLoader Mulish weight consolidation**: Evaluate whether all 6 Mulish weights (`n3`–`n8`) are actually used. Each unused weight load is a mobile bandwidth penalty.
