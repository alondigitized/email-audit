# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-16**
## Full review
Now I have everything I need. Here's my full review.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers.com delivered a mixed bag today. The navigation structure actually speaks my language — "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Arch Fit," "Comfort Technologies" right there in the menu. The product I landed on (Arch Fit D'Lites 330) hits everything I care about. But two things nearly ruined the trip: the cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen, and when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results shown were Women's shoes. I'm logged in. They know I'm a man. That's not good enough.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The blank cart is still catastrophic — a shopper who got that far and hit a white screen would close the tab and go to DSW. The personalization miss on search is a separate leak. The good bones in the navigation don't rescue a broken checkout funnel.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **The quick-tab strip on the homepage** — Slip-ins, Arch Fit right up front. I didn't have to go hunting.
- **The Men's menu drawer** — Clean, large text, generous tap targets. "Comfort Technologies" as its own category tells me Skechers gets it. "Hands Free Slip-ins" listed as a shortcut below the fold was easy to spot.
- **The Hands Free Slip-ins category page** — Fit filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) are front and center as big tappable buttons. Smart. Older guys with wider feet don't want to dig for that.
- **The product page layout** — Title is bold and readable. Price is immediate. The "Hands-free Slip-ins" badge and "Arch Fit" in the name told me everything I needed at a glance. No zooming required.
- **The Add to Cart size grid** — Big boxes, easy to tap. The "Please select a size" warning in red was clear. The full-width blue ADD TO CART button at the bottom is one of the best tap targets on the whole site.
- **No popup friction** — Steps 1 and 2 were identical. Whatever popup was expected, it either didn't fire or was handled silently. That's a win — I hate being ambushed with a coupon modal the second I land.
- **Promo visibility** — "Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER" was shown on both the product page and search results. I noticed it. That's the SUMMER code from the top banner actually making sense at the point of purchase.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is a blank white screen.** Complete failure. Nothing rendered. If I had selected my size and tapped Add to Cart, I'd see nothing. This is a showstopper. A 62-year-old is going to assume the site broke, not that he needs to wait or refresh.
- **Search "comfort shoes" shows Women's first.** I'm logged in. Both visible results — Contour Foam Cozy Fit Pure Comfort and Relaxed Fit Glide-Step Comfort — are labeled Women's. 1,448 results and they surfaced women's shoes at the top for a men's account. That's a personalization failure and it'd make me feel like the site doesn't know me.
- **Homepage hero misses my demographic entirely.** "GAME ON!" with bright athletic sneakers in green, blue, and red reads young and sporty. Nothing on the first screen signals comfort, cushioning, or walking ease. No older adult would see themselves in that image.
- **Low review count on the product.** 3 stars, 7 reviews on a $130 shoe. That's not enough social proof to pull the trigger, especially at that price. I'd back out and look at DSW where I can read more opinions.
- **Login state invisible in screenshots.** Step 3 looks identical to the homepage — I couldn't tell if I was actually logged in. If the personalized experience only kicks in silently, I'll never feel recognized.
- **312 results on the Slip-ins page with no default sort by "Best Match" or comfort rating.** That's too many choices for someone who just wants to find a good walking shoe. I'd need to filter, and filtering on mobile takes effort.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank white screen at checkout is a conversion killer. This is the single highest-priority fix.
2. **Gender-filter search results for logged-in users.** If I'm signed in as a male account, "comfort shoes" should surface Men's results by default, or at minimum show a gender toggle at the top.
3. **Swap the homepage hero for comfort messaging.** Something with an actual person walking — ideally someone who looks 55–65 — with copy like "Step in. No hands. All-day comfort." That speaks to me. The GAME ON campaign speaks to a 25-year-old.
4. **Surface a "Top Rated" or "Most Reviewed" sort on the Slip-ins category page** as the default. Comfort shoppers trust other comfort shoppers.
5. **Show a logged-in greeting or "Welcome back" signal** somewhere on the homepage so I know my account is active and the 20% Members discount will apply.
6. **Add more reviews to hero products.** 7 reviews on a $130 featured shoe is too thin. Consider surfacing higher-reviewed alternatives earlier in the journey.

The bones are here. Skechers knows what I need — slip-ins, arch fit, wide options, comfort tech — and they've organized the site around those things decently. But right now the cart is broken and search doesn't know I'm a man. Those two things alone mean I'm not buying today. I'd go to Famous Footwear's app where at least the cart works. **Score: 4/10. ## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware), evidenced by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` asset paths. Key infrastructure findings: Kasada bot protection is rate-limiting its own fingerprinting endpoint during this session (6 × 429 on the KapsSdk `/fp` path), the View Cart page renders a document shell missing required `<html lang>` and `<title>` — likely a hydration timing issue — and the TTFB spikes on Login and Men category (416ms vs. 30–38ms elsewhere) indicate CDN misses or server-side auth checks on those routes.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations across 3 pages. 2 critical, 2 serious per-page systemic issues.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Root Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA list/grid items exist outside their required parent container — likely a carousel or product grid component |
| Critical | `button-name` | Homepage | One or more `<button>` elements have no text, `aria-label`, or `title` — likely icon-only buttons (hamburger, close, search) |
| Serious | `link-name` | Homepage | `<a>` tags with no text content — likely image links with no `alt` on the child `<img>` |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` is empty or absent when axe scanned — suggests the cart page is a client-rendered shell that hasn't hydrated yet at scan time |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | Same root cause: shell HTML lacks `lang="en"` before hydration injects it |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | Heading hierarchy skips levels (e.g., `h1` → `h3`) |
| Moderate | Landmark issues (5 rules) | Homepage, Search | Multiple `<main>` landmarks, `<aside>` nested inside landmarks, content outside any landmark — consistent with a legacy template-based page structure that uses both native and ARIA landmarks redundantly |

The View Cart issues (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) all appearing together on a single page strongly points to a JavaScript-rendered cart that delivers a bare `<html>` shell and relies on client-side hydration. Any crawler or AT that reads before JS executes sees a broken document.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart `<title>` absent at scan time.** If Googlebot or social crawlers hit the cart URL, they index a blank title. Cart pages should carry a static `<title>Cart | Skechers</title>` in the server-rendered HTML regardless of hydration state.
- **`html-has-lang` absent on View Cart.** Google uses `lang` for language targeting; its absence can suppress international hreflang resolution.
- No `<title>` or `<meta description>` issues detected on product/category pages from the HTML samples.
- The `--vh` custom property is set inline on `<html>` via JavaScript, which is fine for SEO since it's a layout variable, not content.

## Performance

**TTFB anomalies:**

| Step | TTFB | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | 30ms | CDN cache hit |
| Men > Shoes | 38ms | CDN cache hit |
| Product detail | 32ms | CDN cache hit |
| Add to Cart | 32ms | CDN cache hit |
| Search | 35ms | CDN cache hit |
| View Cart | 143ms | Moderate — likely auth token check |
| **Log In** | **416ms** | CDN miss or server-side session check |
| **Men category** | **416ms** | CDN miss — category pages should be cacheable; this spike needs investigation |

The 416ms TTFB on Men category is the most actionable finding: category listing pages carry no user-specific data and should be fully CDN-cached. A consistent 416ms suggests either a cache-bypass rule (cookie presence triggering dynamic rendering) or a missing cache key configuration on the CDN.

**LCP not captured** for any step — the audit tooling didn't record it. LCP is the most user-visible Core Web Vital; it should be instrumented.

**Third-party script payload (Homepage):** At least 15 distinct third-party script origins fire on `<head>` load:
- Google Tag Manager + 3 gtag destinations
- TikTok Pixel (3 scripts)
- Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo (2×), Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Facebook, Taboola
- Monetate (2 scripts), Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, Impact, Tune, Pulse Insights, WebFontLoader

All are marked `async` which prevents render-blocking, but they still consume network bandwidth, main-thread CPU, and contribute to LCP delay. The Men category page loads a **superset** of these scripts (adds Snapchat, Reddit, Stape CDN, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola beyond the homepage set).

**WebFontLoader loads 6 weights of Mulish** (`n3` through `n8`). Unused font weights should be pruned; each weight is a separate network request.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh: 6.640000000000001px`** is set inline on `<html>` — this is a JavaScript viewport-height polyfill to work around mobile browser chrome (address bar) affecting `100vh`. Correct approach for mobile, but the floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) suggests it's calculated without `Math.round()` — minor issue, no functional impact.
- Font weight loading (6 variants of Mulish) is particularly costly on mobile connections.
- No `<meta name="viewport">` issues visible in the sampled HTML head (it's present via GTM injection pattern).
- No explicit mobile-specific issues beyond what's noted in Performance above.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate limiting on Kasada KapsSdk fingerprinting endpoint (5 hits):**
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is Kasada's bot protection SDK phoning home for fingerprint validation. Getting 429 here means Kasada's own rate limiter flagged this browser session. During automated audits this is expected, but if it reproduces in real user sessions it means Kasada is incorrectly classifying legitimate browsers as bots and rate-limiting their fingerprint submissions.

**429 on `/cart/` (1 hit):**
This is the most operationally significant error. A 429 on the cart page for a real user means they cannot load their cart — a direct revenue impact. Needs investigation: is Kasada or a WAF rate-limiting cart page loads per session, or is there a different rate limiter on the cart endpoint?

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics (outfit widget vendor) has not configured their CDN to allow cross-origin CSS fetches from `skechers.com`. Their `style.css` is being fetched with CORS mode but the response lacks the header. The outfit widget will render without its styles. This is a vendor configuration issue — Stylitics needs to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or `*`) to their CDN response headers for this asset.

**ERR_FAILED (1 hit):** One resource failed with a network-level error (not HTTP-level). URL not captured — likely a blocked third-party domain at the OS/network layer during the audit.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Revenue impact:**
1. **Investigate 429 on `/cart/`** — determine which rate-limiting layer (Kasada, WAF, SFCC) is returning 429 on cart loads and whether it affects real users. Add monitoring/alerting on 4xx rates for `/cart/`.

**P1 — Correctness:**
2. **Fix View Cart server-rendered shell** — ensure `<html lang="en">`, `<title>`, `<main>`, and `<h1>` are present in the initial server response, not only post-hydration. This fixes 4 of the 18 a11y violations and the SEO title issue simultaneously.
3. **Fix Homepage critical a11y** — `button-name` (add `aria-label` to icon-only buttons) and `aria-required-parent` (audit carousel/grid ARIA role structure). These are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures.
4. **Fix Homepage `link-name`** — add `alt` text to linked images or `aria-label` to link wrappers.
5. **Coordinate with Stylitics** to add CORS headers on their CDN for `style.css`.

**P2 — Performance:**
6. **Investigate Men category 416ms TTFB** — check CDN cache rules; confirm category pages aren't bypassing cache due to session cookies. If a cookie presence header is causing cache misses, consider cookie-based cache segmentation instead.
7. **Instrument LCP** across all steps — without it, Core Web Vitals compliance cannot be confirmed.
8. **Audit third-party script inventory** — 15+ third-party origins fire on every page load. Any redundant or unused tags (duplicate Yahoo `ytc.js` loaded twice is visible in the HTML) should be removed via GTM audit.
9. **Prune Mulish font weights** to only those actually used in CSS.
