# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 10, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers' mobile site gets a lot of things right in the middle of the funnel — the Men's navigation is clean, the Hands-Free Slip-ins landing page is focused, and the ADD TO CART button is the biggest, most tappable thing on the screen. But the visit falls apart at both ends. The homepage talks to a 25-year-old sneakerhead, not me. Logging in produced zero personalization. And the cart page — the one moment where I'm closest to handing over my card — loaded as a completely blank white screen. That's not friction. That's a dead stop.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **4/10**

No change from yesterday. The blank cart is a conversion-killer that erases whatever goodwill the slip-ins category page earned. Search returning women's shoes to a men's shopper compounds the miss.

---

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** The blue "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" banner rides the top of every single screen. For a price-conscious shopper doing value math, this is exactly where it needs to be — I don't have to hunt for the deal.

**Men's navigation menu is the best screen in the journey.** Large text, full-width tap targets, logical groupings. "Comfort Technologies" and "Hands Free Slip-ins" / "Wide Fit" / "Extra Wide Fit" as quick-links at the bottom — that's me, that's my language, that's what I came for.

**Hands-Free Slip-ins category page is focused and useful.** Big page title, fit selector buttons (Wide Fit / Extra Wide / Medium Fit) right at the top so I can filter before scrolling 314 results. That's smart.

**Size selector is senior-friendly.** The size grid buttons are well-spaced, no squinting required. "Don't See Your Size?" is a useful escape hatch. The "Please select a size" warning is clear.

**ADD TO CART button is a home run.** Full-width, dark blue, large text, pinned to the bottom of the screen. Even on a tired phone day with stiff fingers, I'm hitting that button.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The homepage doesn't know I exist.** Hero image is two young people in streetwear outside a Skechers building. I'm 62, I'm shopping for arch support — I might as well be on a different website. Not a single visual signal aimed at my demographic above the fold.

**Login produced nothing.** After tapping the account icon, the page looks identical to the homepage — same banner, same hero, cart still reads 0. No "Welcome back, Walker." No saved sizes. No loyalty points. If I'm logged in, show me something that proves it. Right now I have no idea if it worked.

**Product has zero reviews.** $120 shoe, "No Reviews." I'm not spending $120 without knowing at least three other guys said their feet thanked them. New Balance and Hoka always have hundreds of reviews on comparable product pages. This makes me hesitate.

**The cart page is a blank white screen.** Complete render failure. No error message, no spinner, nothing. I tapped "View Cart" and got a white rectangle. This is the single biggest regression possible — it's the checkout door being nailed shut.

**Search for "comfort shoes" returned women's shoes.** Both visible results — "Cozy Fit Pure Comfort" and "Glide-Step Comfort" — are labeled Women's. I'm a logged-in (supposedly) men's shopper searching for comfort shoes. The algorithm should know better. My cross-shop competitors (New Balance, Brooks) would have shown me men's results by default.

**No comfort tech details on the product page.** The product name has "Arch Fit" and "Skech-Air" in it but the screenshot shows no callout of what those actually mean — no cushioning stat, no arch depth, no weight. I need to know *why* this shoe is worth $120 before I scroll down to add it.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a P0 bug. A blank cart stops revenue dead. Every other improvement is irrelevant until this loads.

2. **Personalize the logged-in state.** Even a simple "Hi Walker" in the account icon tooltip or a "Your size: 10.5W" pre-selection on PDPs would signal that logging in was worth the tap.

3. **Filter search results by gender from session context.** If I navigated through Men > Shoes and then searched, surface men's results first. Basic session-aware ranking.

4. **Add a comfort proof block to the PDP.** Three bullet points: arch depth, cushioning foam name, slip-in entry ease. Doesn't need to be long — just something that earns the $120 ask before I scroll.

5. **Surface a review prompt or aggregate from related models.** Zero reviews is a dealbreaker for my age group. If this SKU is new, pull ratings from the Arch Fit family or add "Similar shoes rated X stars by X customers."

6. **Swap homepage hero seasonally for an age-diverse audience.** A single shot of someone my age walking a trail or a golf course costs nothing but signals "this brand is for me too."

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The middle of this funnel — finding my category, landing on slip-ins, picking a size — is genuinely solid. Skechers has clearly invested in the comfort-focused navigation. But the experience is bookended by irrelevance (homepage for 25-year-olds) and breakage (blank cart). I didn't buy. Not because I didn't want the shoe, but because the site wouldn't let me complete the transaction. Yesterday's 4/10 holds. Fix the cart and the score jumps to a 6 immediately.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

skechers.com runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), served through what appears to be Akamai/Kasada bot-protection middleware. The mobile journey surfaces three systemic issues: (1) Kasada bot-detection (`/fp` endpoint) is rate-limiting its own page requests, suggesting misconfigured rate-limit thresholds or a client-side fingerprinting loop; (2) the View Cart page renders a shell document missing critical HTML fundamentals (`<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, `<h1>`), indicating a client-rendered cart that doesn't hydrate correctly under rate-limit stress; (3) a third-party style injection (`stylitics.com`) is blocked by a missing CORS header on that vendor's CDN.

## Accessibility

**17 axe violations across 3 pages. Severity breakdown: 2 critical/serious on homepage, 4 serious on cart, 4 on search.**

| Rule | Severity | Page | Root cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aria-required-parent` | critical | Homepage | ARIA listitem/option elements exist outside their required container — likely a carousel or nav menu rendered outside `<ul>`/`<select>` |
| `link-name` | serious | Homepage | Icon-only `<a>` tags with no `aria-label` and no inner text |
| `document-title` | serious | View Cart | `<title>` is empty or absent in the cart shell document |
| `html-has-lang` | serious | View Cart | `<html>` has no `lang` attribute in the cart document — different document from the main shell |
| `heading-order` | moderate | Homepage | `<h3>` or lower appears before `<h1>` — common with above-the-fold promo banners |
| `landmark-main-is-top-level` | moderate | Homepage | `<main>` is nested inside another element (likely a wrapper div) |
| `landmark-no-duplicate-main` | moderate | Homepage | Two `<main>` elements present — suggests a modal or drawer injecting its own layout |
| `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` | moderate | Homepage + Search | `<aside>` nested inside `<main>` or another landmark |
| `landmark-unique` | moderate | Homepage + Search | Multiple landmarks of the same type without `aria-label` to distinguish them |
| `region` | moderate | Homepage + Search | Content outside any landmark region |
| `landmark-one-main` | moderate | View Cart | No `<main>` in cart document |
| `page-has-heading-one` | moderate | View Cart | No `<h1>` in cart document |

**Cart page is the most severe:** missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, and `<h1>` simultaneously suggests the cart is rendered in an iframe or a separate document fragment that bypasses the standard SFCC template entirely.

## SEO

- **View Cart `<title>` missing:** Cart pages are typically noindexed, but an empty `<title>` will produce garbage in browser tab history and social shares.
- **Log In and Men category pages return the same HTML root as Homepage** (all three show identical `<html lang="en">` with the same `--vh: 6.64px` inline style). The HTML snapshots suggest these may be single-page navigation states rather than distinct server-rendered pages — TTFB of 410ms on Log In / Men category vs. 24–31ms on all others confirms the login/nav pages are likely client-side transitions hitting a slow API, not a separate server render.
- **No canonical or hreflang visible in truncated source** — cannot fully assess, but the identical HTML structure across steps is a risk if Googlebot crawls intermediate states.

**Critical observation:** LCP and CLS are not captured. Given the third-party script volume on the homepage (20+ async scripts from TikTok, Google, Pinterest, Bing, Snapchat, Reddit, Yahoo, Taboola, Nextdoor, Quantum Metric, Monetate, PulseInsights, Impact, BrightEdge, Stylitics, Tune, WebFont Loader, Kasada), LCP is almost certainly degraded on mobile. WebFontLoader is synchronous-looking despite `async` attribute — the `wf-*` classes on `<html>` indicate it blocks font application and likely causes FOUT/CLS on text-heavy content.

The `--vh: 6.64px` CSS custom property (= `1vh` on a 664px viewport) is set inline on `<html>` via JS — standard mobile viewport fix, not a performance concern on its own.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport fix via JS (`--vh`)**: Correct pattern for mobile browser chrome offset, but it fires synchronously and the value is visible in the initial HTML, meaning it's set on the server or very early in the JS bundle. No issue if done correctly.
- **Third-party script proliferation**: 20+ tracking/analytics scripts load on every page, including on mobile. No evidence of lazy-loading or mobile-conditional loading. On a 4G connection this is a significant LCP risk.
- **WebFontLoader (`webfontloader.js` v1.6.28 from cdnjs.cloudflare.com)**: v1.6.28 is the latest release but the library is unmaintained since 2019. It adds a round-trip to load fonts via JS and applies `wf-*` body classes — if the script is delayed, fonts render with a fallback causing layout shift (CLS impact).
- **Stylitics widget blocked**: `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` is blocked by CORS. This is a vendor-side misconfiguration — Stylitics must add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN response. The widget will silently fail to render styles on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate-limit loop on Kasada fingerprinting endpoint:**

```
429 /149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```

This is the Kasada bot-protection SDK (`x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522`) calling its own `/fp` (fingerprint) endpoint and receiving 429s from Skechers' own origin. **Five out of six network errors are this same endpoint.** The sixth 429 is on `/cart/`. Likely causes:

1. The Kasada SDK is retrying aggressively on 429, creating a feedback loop.
2. The rate-limit rule applied to this path is too tight for the SDK's legitimate polling cadence.
3. The test session was flagged as a bot, and Kasada correctly challenged it — but the SDK's retry behavior then floods the console.

The cart 429 (`https://www.skechers.com/cart/`) is a separate issue: the cart page itself is rate-limited, which would silently block cart hydration for affected sessions.

**CORS block on Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
This is a vendor CDN issue, not a Skechers configuration issue. However Skechers should file a bug with Stylitics — the widget degrades silently on affected clients.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 blocking checkout:**
Investigate whether the `/cart/` 429 is triggered by Kasada misidentifying logged-in cart requests as bots. A Kasada session that fails the `/fp` handshake may get all subsequent requests blocked. Coordinate with Kasada to whitelist the cart endpoint or tune the challenge thresholds for authenticated sessions.

**P0 — View Cart document missing HTML fundamentals:**
The cart renders in a context without `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, or `<h1>`. If cart is in an iframe, ensure the iframe document uses the same SFCC base template. If it's client-rendered, the SPA shell must be pre-populated with these elements before JS hydration.

**P1 — Kasada `/fp` retry loop:**
The SDK is calling `/fp` 5+ times per session and receiving 429s each time, polluting the console and consuming network requests on mobile. Either the rate limit needs to be loosened for this endpoint, or the SDK version (`j-1.2.522`) needs to be updated if Kasada has patched the retry behavior.

**P1 — `link-name` / `aria-required-parent` on Homepage:**
Add `aria-label` to all icon-only anchor tags. Fix the ARIA role containment issue in the homepage carousel/nav — move ARIA child roles inside their required parent container.

**P2 — Third-party script audit:**
Audit which of the 20+ tracking scripts are truly required on every page load. Defer non-critical pixels (Reddit, Nextdoor, Taboola, Yahoo, Tune) until after `load` or first user interaction. This will directly improve LCP on mobile.

**P2 — Stylitics CORS:**
File a bug with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Until resolved, the Stylitics widget renders unstyled on affected clients.

**P3 — WebFontLoader replacement:**
Replace `webfontloader.js` with native `<link rel="preload">` + `font-display: swap` in CSS. The library is unmaintained and the JS-based class-swap pattern causes measurable CLS on slow connections.

**P3 — Capture LCP/CLS:**
All 8 steps are missing LCP and CLS measurements. Instrument these via PerformanceObserver or a RUM tool (Quantum Metric is already installed) before the next audit cycle — TTFB alone doesn't surface rendering problems.
