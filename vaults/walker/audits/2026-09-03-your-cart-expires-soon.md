# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- Same shoe, different day. That's the D'Lites - Spring Bloom again — pink floral, "you liked these and you weren't wrong" — and no, I did not like it, I never touched it, and I've now seen it in my inbox more times this week than I've seen actual news. If Skechers is tracking clicks, they'd know I've never so much as hovered over that thing.
- Below the fake cart, though, there's some real product — a men's Slip-ins Arch Fit and a men's Relaxed Fit Altman-Murdock, both cushioned, both slip-on, both things I'd actually consider. But they're buried three scrolls down under a floral sneaker and two boys' shoes I have zero use for. The 20% off for Skechers Plus members is a real, dated offer, which is good — but it's stuck near the bottom after I've already lost interest.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This is the same broken cart trick for the fourth or fifth time this week, and burying the men's shoes under junk I never touched tells me nobody's looking at what I actually browse.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus member deal has a real end date (thru 9/7) and clear mechanics — discount applied at cart, no code hunting.
- There's an actual men's Slip-ins Arch Fit and men's Relaxed Fit shoe in the grid, both hands-free/cushioned styles I'd genuinely look at.
- Clean layout, big readable text, one product per row — easy enough to read on my phone without zooming.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup up front, which I know and trust as options.

## What's weak

- The pink floral D'Lites is front and center again under a fake "you liked these" headline — I've flagged this same shoe in at least three prior emails this week and it has never been in my cart.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" implies I have something sitting in a cart. I don't. That's not urgency, that's just wrong.
- The men's shoes I'd actually buy are buried under a floral sneaker and two boys' Garage Sneakers — irrelevant to a 62-year-old guy.
- No arch support or cushioning callouts anywhere near the product images — just names and photos, no reason given to care.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake abandoned-cart framing entirely — if I didn't put it in a cart, don't tell me I did. Lead with a real "New styles you'll like" or go straight to the 20% off members deal.
- 2. Put the men's Slip-ins Arch Fit right at the top, not the floral D'Lites — that's the shoe matching my browsing, not hers.
- 3. Move the 20% off member banner up near the top — it's the strongest, most concrete offer in the whole email and it's stranded at the bottom.
- 4. Add one line under each shoe — "arch support," "slip-on," "all-day cushion" — so I know why I'd want it without guessing.
