# Last chance for 20% off your purchase
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- Now this is more like it — no pink floral sneaker chasing me around today. This one opens with my name, my 1,000 points sitting right there with a redeem link, and a real 20% off banner up top that doesn't make me hunt for it. It's laid out like an actual catalog — Casuals, Athletics, Wide Fit, Work & Safety — instead of one shoe I never clicked hammered at me for the fifth time this week.
- That Wide Fit section is the one that actually gets me — I've got a foot that needs room and a little give, and seeing that called out as its own category tells me somebody at Skechers thought about guys like me instead of just chasing a size-8 trend shoe. The models are appropriate too, a guy in his working slacks and boots, not some 20-something influencer shot.
- Where it loses me a little is that the 20% off is generic — same code I've apparently already seen twice this week per my own inbox history, so "last chance" doesn't feel like much of a last chance. I'd click through to Wide Fit or Athletics just to browse, but I'm not rushing. Wouldn't forward it to a buddy, but I'd probably click Wide Fit if I were already thinking about new shoes.

## What's working

- My name and points balance are right at the top — feels like they know I'm a member, not a stranger.
- The 20% off banner is big, clear, and impossible to miss scrolling in from the top.
- Wide Fit gets its own category — that's the first email this week that's actually spoken to a real comfort need instead of a single trending shoe.
- Categories are organized by how I actually shop (Casuals, Athletics, Work & Safety) rather than one hero product shoved at me.
- No broken images, no weird overlapping text — this one rendered clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The offer is the same 20% member code I already saw Tuesday and Wednesday — "Last Chance" doesn't land when it's the third time this week.
- No actual deadline printed anywhere visible — just "Last Chance" with no date, so I've got no real urgency to act today versus next week.
- Nothing in here talks about cushioning, arch support, or hands-free slip-on comfort specifically — the stuff that actually gets me to buy is implied by category names, not called out.
- No product details, sizing info, or reviews anywhere — just stock photography and category buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual expiration date or countdown on the 20% off banner — "Last Chance" with no date attached just reads as filler after three sends this week with the same code.
- 2. Lead the Wide Fit or Athletics block higher up with a line about arch support or all-day comfort — that's the actual hook for a guy like me, not just a category label.
- 3. Swap one of the generic lifestyle photos for a close-up of the cushioned sole or arch support callout — something that shows me why it's comfortable, not just what it looks like on a model.
- 4. Stop reusing the same 20% code send after send — rotate in something different (free shipping, bonus points multiplier) so it doesn't feel like the same email recycled.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is a standard SFMC-served Skechers promo with functional tracking pixels and links, but ships an empty personalization token in the greeting and multiple non-HTTPS image assets that risk being blocked by mail clients.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Open-tracking pixel present and correctly formed: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?OBOR3CRRUDNUNBSNVRQMLI4DZI.100228&d=100228&bmt=0`.
- Secondary tracking/beacon pixels (Kantar/Krux `beacon.krxd.net`, Return Path `pixel.app.returnpath.net`, Ink1000 `www.ink1000.com`) all present and use consistent campaign ID `MKG_US_NONPUR_U_CATEGORY_NONPURCH_EN_09132026_A` across vendors — good cross-vendor consistency.
- 34 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected behavior for redirect-domain links; not a confirmed defect, flagging as unverified rather than broken).
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif...` is missing the `http(s)://` scheme prefix in the source (`src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/..."` rather than `src="https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/..."`) — this will resolve as a relative path in most clients and silently fail to fire.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `o.gif` (Ink1000 tracking pixel) missing `alt` attribute — low impact since it's a 1x1 tracking pixel, but flagged per QA output.
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` missing `alt` text — this is a content image (not a tracking pixel), so this is a real accessibility gap for screen-reader users.
- Extensive mobile responsive CSS (`.hidden`, `.responsive-td`, `.widthFull`, breakpoints at 375px/480px/640px/680px/768px) is well-structured with standard Outlook/Apple Mail resets (`x-apple-data-detectors`, `ExternalClass`, `mso` patterns implied).
- No `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag — minor accessibility miss for assistive tech.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Confirmed FAIL**: Greeting renders with an empty name merge field ("Hi ,") — the personalization token resolved to an empty string rather than falling back to a default (e.g., "Hi there,"). This is a subscriber-record/data issue or missing fallback logic in the SFMC content block, not an HTML bug per se, but it is customer-visible and should be fixed at the template or data layer.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Truncated HTML does not include the footer/unsubscribe block, so unsubscribe link presence/physical address cannot be confirmed from the provided source — flagging as **unverified**, not fabricating a failure.
- Sender is `no-reply@emails.skechers.com`, a dedicated ESP subdomain — consistent with typical SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment setups, but authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC results) are not present in the provided data and cannot be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Campaign identifier `MKG_US_NONPUR_U_CATEGORY_NONPURCH_EN_09132026_A` is consistent across all tracking beacons (SFMC open pixel, Kantar, Ink1000), which supports reliable cross-platform attribution.
- Standard UTM query params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are not visible on the CTA/product links in the truncated source — cannot confirm landing-page UTM alignment from available data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the empty greeting merge field — add a fallback value (e.g., "there") for subscribers missing a first name.
2. Add missing `alt` text to `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` (content image) at minimum; optional for the tracking pixel.
3. Convert all `http://image.emails.skechers.com/...` and `http://www.ink1000.com/...` asset URLs to `https://` — several mail clients (notably Outlook/Gmail proxy) block or flag mixed-content/non-HTTPS images.
4. Add the missing scheme (`https://`) to the Return Path pixel `src` so it actually fires.
5. Confirm unsubscribe link and physical mailing address are present in the footer (not visible in truncated source) to validate CAN-SPAM compliance.
