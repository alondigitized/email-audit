# When's your birthday?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- They know my name. Top of the email says "Hi, Walker" with my member badge and 1,000 points sitting right there — and then... they ask me to tell them my birthday. That's the whole email. No shoes. No sale. No "here's something cushioned and slip-on for your aching feet." Just a form to fill out so they can promise me vague "surprises" later.
- I'm a Skechers Plus member. I have 1,000 points. The header teases that I have enough for a $5 reward "below" — and then I scroll down and there's no reward button. There's a grid of benefits icons (free shipping, extended returns), a big "ADD YOUR BIRTHDATE" button, and then a category nav list. That $5 dangled and disappeared. That's a frustrating bait.
- I would not click. I'd mark this as read, maybe half-interested in the points, and move on. I would not forward it — there's nothing in it worth sharing.

## What's working

- **Personalization landed.** "Hi, Walker" with my points balance at the top — they know who I am, and that earns a half-second of attention.
- **CTA button is crystal clear.** "ADD YOUR BIRTHDATE" is simple, big, and obvious. I know exactly what they want.
- **Benefits block is readable.** Four icons laid out cleanly — free shipping, extended returns, exclusive offers, birthday surprises. Easy to scan on a phone.
- **Layout is clean.** No overlapping elements, no broken images, text is a decent size. I didn't have to pinch-zoom.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is pure garbage.** What showed up next to the subject in my inbox was raw computer code — some kind of schema markup gibberish starting with `@context`. That alone almost made me skip opening it.
- **No product. Zero.** Not one shoe. Not one "check out this cushioned walker" image. For a comfort shoe brand emailing a guy with 1,000 points, that's a miss.
- **The $5 reward teaser goes nowhere.** The header says I have enough points for a $5 reward "below" — I scrolled the whole email and there's no reward CTA. That's a broken promise inside a single email.
- **"Surprises" isn't an offer.** Vague future-promise language is fine for a birthday card, not for a transactional email asking me to hand over personal data.
- **Nothing for a 62-year-old man.** No men's comfort imagery, no arch support mention, no walking shoe callout. The shop links say MEN but nothing visually says this email is for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately.** The JSON-LD schema is leaking into the inbox preview. That single issue tanks open rates before the subject line even gets a fair read. Real preheader copy should replace it.
- 2. **Show me one shoe.** A single hero product image — something cushioned, slip-on, men's — gives me a reason to stick around. Data collection emails can still sell.
- 3. **Redeem that $5 right here.** If the header tells me I have a $5 reward, put a button in the email body. Don't tease what you won't deliver.
- 4. **Make the birthday payoff concrete.** "Tell us your birthday and get X% off your birthday month order" beats "special surprises" every time. Give me a number.
- 5. **Add a men's featured section below the fold.** Even a two-column "While you're here..." block with a comfort walker and a slip-on would make this email pull double duty.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walker, unlock your birthday reward`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your birthday = a free treat from Skechers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members get a surprise all month long — tell us when to celebrate you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Add your birthday, unlock a month of member-only perks.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "When's your birthday?" (TRG_US_EN_BDAYCAPTURE_1_09082025_A)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One critical broken experience (empty name merge token rendering as "Hi ,") and a systemic HTTP image-hosting issue affecting 14 images across the email body and footer. The Return Path deliverability pixel has a missing protocol prefix, making it non-functional in all clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Protocol-stripped Return Path pixel — non-functional**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
Missing `https://` prefix. This pixel will 404 or be treated as a relative path in all clients, producing no deliverability signal for Validity/Return Path inbox monitoring.

**Ink1000 tracking pixel — HTTP**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=TRG_US_EN_BDAYCAPTURE_1_09082025_A
```
Served over HTTP. Will be blocked by Gmail and Outlook.com image proxies.

**27 click-redirect links** — all routed through `click.emails.skechers.com`. Destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be verified (probing skipped per QA config). See section 6.

**Subscriber ID exposed in plain text**
The second Krux pixel passes the raw numeric subscriber ID:
```
partner_uid=121173391
```
The first Krux pixel uses a SHA-256 hash (acceptable). The plain-text ID in the second pixel is unnecessary exposure.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**14 images served over HTTP** — will be blocked by Gmail proxy and Outlook.com after their forced HTTPS rewrites:
- Skechers logo: `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`
- App Store, Google Play, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Twitter, Facebook, Pinterest, YouTube icons — all under `http://image.emails.skechers.com/`
- Body image `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` (content unknown, no alt text)

The entire Skechers CDN domain (`image.emails.skechers.com`) appears to be configured HTTP-only for these asset paths. This is a systemic CDN config issue, not per-image.

**2 images missing alt text:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel — alt="" is acceptable for pixels, but absent entirely)
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt attribute; breaks screen readers and images-off rendering

**Dual media query blocks for `.show`** — `@media (max-width: 680px)` and `@media (max-width: 480px)` both define `.show` with conflicting scope. Minor, but creates ambiguity in clients that parse only one block (e.g., older Android).

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** — set globally on `*`. This disables iOS auto-sizing for all text, including small legal copy. Should be `100%` not `none`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[CRITICAL — FAIL] Empty name merge token**
QA confirms the greeting renders as `"Hi ,"` — the subscriber first-name field is null or unresolved for at least part of the send population. This is a broken customer-facing experience.

Evidence: `[FAIL] Empty greeting slot: Greeting has empty name field (e.g. "Hi ,")`

The template must include a fallback value in the merge tag syntax (e.g., SFMC AMPscript `@FirstName` with a `IIF(EMPTY(@FirstName), "there", @FirstName)` guard, or equivalent).

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / CASL:** HTML is truncated; physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism could not be verified from the provided source. The preheader is correctly hidden:
```html
<div class="preheader" style="font-size:1px;display:none !important">Tell us your birthday...</div>
```
No issues detectable in the visible portion. Full source review required to confirm footer compliance elements.

**Sender authentication:** From domain is `emails.skechers.com`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be verified from HTML alone — should be confirmed against MX/DNS records for this subdomain.

**Campaign ID date:** `TRG_US_EN_BDAYCAPTURE_1_09082025_A` encodes `09082025` (Sep 8, 2025). If this is a send-date reference and the email is being sent in May 2026, the campaign ID is stale and may cause reporting mismatches in SFMC and any downstream attribution.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 27 destination links pass through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects. UTM parameters on final landing URLs were not probed. Minimum expected parameters for this campaign type:
- `utm_source=email`
- `utm_medium=triggered`
- `utm_campaign=birthday_capture` (or equivalent)

The Krux DMP pixel carries `campaignid=TRG_US_EN_BDAYCAPTURE_1_09082025_A` — verify this matches the UTM campaign value used on-site for attribution consistency.
