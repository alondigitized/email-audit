# Holiday weekend🤝Holiday Savings. Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — Crocs in my inbox for what feels like the tenth time in two weeks. I'll give them this one small thing: the clogs I see in that hero image are actual classic clogs, not another studded platform slide for a college girl's TikTok. Red, gray, black — recognizable shapes. And "Up to 50% Off" in big bold text at the top? That lands. I see the offer before I even scroll.
- But the good feeling evaporates fast. "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" are right there staring at me, and after enough of these emails I know what that means — click through, find my size, find my style, discover it's excluded. The second half of the email is Jibbitz charms at $3 a pop. I am 62 years old with a wide foot and mild plantar fasciitis. I do not decorate my footwear. And the preheader in my inbox was completely blank — just whitespace where a real message should've been.
- I'd probably click through on the 50% off headline, but only because the last big-discount Crocs email I opened on the 20th actually had something worth seeing. This one's riding that goodwill. I wouldn't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- **"Up to 50% Off!" is the first thing the eye hits** — clean hierarchy, no competing for attention with a lifestyle photo of a 25-year-old.
- **Memorial Day framing is timely** — "Memorial Day Savings" at the top tells me this isn't some recycled promo from February.
- **Classic clogs in the hero** — first time in several sends I'm not looking at a women's wedge or a charm collection; these are the shoes I'd actually consider.
- **Buy 2 Take 30% Off stacked deal** — if I was going to grab a pair for me and replace my wife's garden pair, this math makes sense.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — everything rendered, text is large enough I don't need to pinch-zoom.

## What's weak

- **"Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" kill the lead** — they're right next to the headline and they immediately make 50% feel provisional.
- **Jibbitz charms section is dead weight for me** — $3 single charms, $7 packs. Hard pass. This belongs in a separate email to a completely different segment.
- **No comfort signals anywhere** — no mention of LiteRide, arch support, cushioning, men's widths, nothing that tells me which styles are actually built for a foot like mine.
- **Inbox preview was blank** — opened to zero preview text, just empty space after the subject. That's a wasted chance to tell me something useful before I clicked.
- **No urgency deadline** — "holiday weekend" implies it ends soon but never says when, so there's nothing pushing me to act today vs. Saturday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Monday night" or "Sale ends May 26" in the hero subhead would push me to act now instead of saying "I'll look later" and forgetting.
- 2. **Kill the Jibbitz section or bury it at the very bottom** — it's pulling focus from the main offer and it's aimed at a completely different buyer.
- 3. **Surface one men's comfort style with its name and a comfort callout** — even one line like "LiteRide Clogs included — built-in arch support" would make me trust the sale applies to what I want.
- 4. **Fix the preheader** — blank whitespace tricks are a cheap hack that just makes the send look broken in my inbox.
- 5. **Drop the app store badges** — I'm not downloading the Crocs app. This real estate could hold a deadline or a comfort proof point.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day: Up to 50% off select Crocs — ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off this weekend — men's styles included`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Classic clogs, slides, and LiteRide styles — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Memorial Day — comfort styles up to half off`
