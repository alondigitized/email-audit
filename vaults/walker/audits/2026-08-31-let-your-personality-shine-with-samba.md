# Let your personality shine with Samba
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Another Samba email — that's twice this week now, first the metallic ones on the 29th and now this. Same gold shoe, different words slapped on top. "Let your personality shine" — I don't need a shoe to express my personality, I need a shoe that doesn't hurt my feet by the second lap around the block. It's a flat suede-and-metal skate shoe with a hard rubber sole and zero arch support that I can see.
- The layout's clean at least, none of the clutter I've griped about before, but there's a whole lot of white space at the bottom with nothing in it — feels unfinished, like they forgot to add the rest of the email. No price, no sizing, no "shop now" under the actual product, just a banner up top that shops "best sellers" in general.
- I'm not clicking this one. Marking it read and moving on — I've got nothing to forward a friend even if I wanted to, there's no deal here to share.

## What's working

- The "Explore the latest best sellers" banner up top at least gives me a real button with a clear action, even if it's generic.
- Clean layout, nothing overlapping or broken that I can see.
- Big readable text — I don't have to zoom in to read "SAMBA GOES METALLIC."

## What's weak

- Still a flat suede skate shoe with no arch support or cushioning mentioned — not my category at all.
- No price, no percentage off, no deal of any kind — just a mood pitch.
- Big empty gap at the bottom of the email before the footer, like content is missing.
- Second Samba send in two days (the 29th was "Samba looks good in metal") — feels repetitive, like they're recycling the same shoe with new taglines.
- No second CTA button under the actual hero product — the only "shop" button is way up top, disconnected from the shoe I'm looking at.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a "Shop Samba" button directly under the product image and description — right now there's no way to act on the thing they're actually showing me.
- 2. Fill that dead space at the bottom with something useful — price, a second angle of the shoe, or a comfort-brand type callout (even if adidas doesn't sell arch support, at least show me sizing/fit info).
- 3. Slow down on the Samba cadence — two emails in three days pushing metallic colorways is starting to feel like noise, not news.
- 4. If there's a deal anywhere in this campaign, lead with it — the $50-for-$40 gift card email from the 25th got more of my attention than this one, and that's not a good sign for a "new colorway" launch.
