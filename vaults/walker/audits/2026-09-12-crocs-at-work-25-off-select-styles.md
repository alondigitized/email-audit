# Crocs at Work: 25% Off Select Styles!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- Now this one I can actually use. A plain black Crocs clog on a work-shift kind of pitch — 25% off, no cartoon charms, no teenager logos, no leopard print. I'm not on my feet all day at a job anymore, but my son-in-law works a warehouse floor and this is exactly the kind of thing I'd forward to him — sturdy-looking, all-business, comfort clog.
- That said, there's not a single button on this whole email. No "Shop Now," nothing to actually click. I'd have to go find Crocs.com myself and hunt for what's on sale, which I probably won't bother doing right this second.
- I'd read it, nod, maybe forward it to my son-in-law, but I wouldn't click through myself — there's nothing to click through to.

## What's working

- Plain black clog on a clean yellow background — easy on the eyes, no clutter, no gimmicks.
- "Crocs at Work" angle reads like an actual grown-up use case instead of a fashion stunt.
- 25% off is a clear, specific number right up top, easy to read without squinting.
- No cartoon nonsense this time — a welcome change from the Yu-Gi-Oh! and charm-bag emails they've been sending me.

## What's weak

- There is no "Shop Now" button anywhere in this email — I looked twice. Nothing to tap.
- "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" with no explanation of which styles — feels like a bait-and-switch waiting to happen.
- Huge amount of empty white space between the product photo and the app-store badges — feels unfinished, like half the email didn't load.
- No price shown, no member/loyalty pricing mentioned even though I get their emails regularly.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a big "Shop Work Shoes" button right under the product photo — that's the single biggest miss here, there's nothing to click.
- 2. Say which styles the 25% actually applies to, instead of just "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" — tell me up front so I'm not disappointed at checkout.
- 3. Tighten up all that dead space in the middle of the email — it makes it feel broken on a phone.
- 4. Show the discounted price on the clog pictured, since that's the specific offer I'd be evaluating.
