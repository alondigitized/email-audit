# $15 off! Gone at midnight! Hurry!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Eight. By my count, this is the eighth time DSW has landed in my inbox this week with some version of an urgency-clock deal — and now they've bumped the number from $10 to $15, which tells me the $10 "last chance" emails were never really last chances. I noticed the escalation, and not in a good way. It reads less like a genuine offer and more like a car salesman dropping the price after you walked toward the door.
- The email itself is clean and renders fine — countdown timer up top, colorful product rows below. The problem is every single row is women's shoes. Heels. Sandals. Wedges. Section headers like "THEY'RE JUST SOOOO YOU" and "MADE FOR SUMMER DAYS" with strappy block-heel photos. I scrolled the whole thing and couldn't find a men's comfort sneaker, a walking shoe, or a single reference to cushioning or arch support. DSW sells plenty of what I buy. They just didn't put any of it in this email.
- I'd mark it read and move on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it, and if one more "last chance" lands in my inbox by Monday I'm going to start wondering if my unsubscribe finger is getting enough exercise.

## What's working

- **The $15 bump is actually noticeable.** After a week of $10 offers, the higher number stopped my thumb for half a second — someone on the team made a real decision there.
- **Countdown timer front and center.** The "ENDS IN 12:25:26" clock is visible at the very top without scrolling; if the deal were for something I wanted, that timer would have worked on me.
- **Clean render, no breakage.** Images loaded, layout held, text was readable at mobile size without pinching. That's table stakes but DSW clears it consistently.
- **"SHOP BY SIZE" section at the bottom.** Appreciate that — I don't want to fall in love with a shoe and find out it stops at size 11.

## What's weak

- **The entire product grid is women's shoes.** Every section — heels, sandals, wedges — is aimed at someone who is not me. DSW knows my purchase history. This is a segmentation failure, not a creative one.
- **"THEY'RE JUST SOOOO YOU"** — that triple-O phrasing isn't how any man I know talks about footwear. This copy was written for a different customer and sent to the wrong list.
- **Three exclamation points in the subject line.** "$15 off! Gone at midnight! Hurry!" — after seven emails with the same energy, this reads as noise, not urgency.
- **The "one-day deal" preview is doing nothing.** "It's a one-day deal, people." is casual and punchy on its own, but it tells me nothing I didn't get from the subject. Wasted real estate.
- **Credibility is shot from the cadence.** When "LAST DAY" appears on a Tuesday and the deal is still running Thursday and you're calling it "last day" again on Saturday — I stop believing the clock.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Send men's product, full stop.** Every grid slot should be men's walking shoes, cushioned sneakers, or slip-ons. DSW carries New Balance, Hoka, Skechers comfort lines — fill the email with those and the $15 is actually compelling to me.
- 2. **Drop two of the three exclamation points.** One is urgency. Three is spam.
- 3. **Make the preview text earn its spot** — tell me what category is on sale, not just that it's a one-day deal.
- 4. **Reference the escalation.** "We raised it to $15" is more honest and more interesting than acting like last week's $10 never happened. Acknowledge the repeat customer who's been watching this unfold.
- 5. **One comfort-forward hero shot.** A men's cushioned sneaker or walking shoe as the hero image would have had me reading the whole thing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's comfort shoes, $15 off — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We bumped it to $15 — men's styles, one day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Skechers, Hoka — your kind of shoe, your kind of deal`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioning, arch support, $15 off — gone at midnight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Pinch-zoom is commonly used by low-vision readers on mobile.

**Duplicate/conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` in universal selector (confirmed):**
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* dead — overridden immediately below */
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* wins */
  text-size-adjust: none
}
```
The `100%` declaration is unreachable dead code. Harmless in practice but indicates a copy-paste error in the reset block.

**Non-standard `Src:` (uppercase) in all 10 `@font-face` blocks (confirmed):**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...woff") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, so compliant parsers accept this, but non-compliant email client CSS preprocessors (some older Android clients) may silently drop the declaration. Affects all four Futura PT and all six HelveticaNow faces.

**HelveticaNow fonts delivered as TTF only (confirmed):**
Futura PT uses `.woff`; HelveticaNow uses `.ttf`. No WOFF or WOFF2 fallback for HelveticaNow. WOFF has broader email client support. If TTF fetch fails, email falls through to system fonts with no intermediate step.

**Invalid `supported-color-schemes` CSS property in `:root` block (confirmed):**
```css
:root { color-scheme: light dark; supported-color-schemes: light dark; }
```
`supported-color-schemes` is not a valid CSS property — only `color-scheme` is. The meta tag equivalent is correctly present; this CSS line is dead weight.

**Two `<meta>` tags missing `http-equiv` attribute (confirmed):**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />         <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                           <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, both tags are non-functional. The charset declaration is particularly important — if the email client doesn't inherit UTF-8 from the `Content-Type` header, characters outside ASCII may render incorrectly.
