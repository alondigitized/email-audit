# Elevate your look with Adistar XLG 2.0
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email, another shot aimed at someone half my age. I saw "Elevate your look" in the subject and should have kept scrolling, but this brand has surprised me before — the $30 off deal back on June 19th was exactly what I needed — so I gave it a shot. Two young models, a chunky blue sneaker, and copy that talks about "a modern edge to your everyday look." That's not comfort-first shopping language. That's a streetwear catalog for a 28-year-old.
- The Adistar XLG 2.0 is right there in the hero, which I appreciate — no mystery about what's being sold. And buried in the body copy I spotted "Adiprene cushioning," which at least tells me adidas knows the shoe has something under it. But that one phrase is fighting against everything else on the screen: young talent, fashion framing, and zero pricing to do any math on.
- Then I scroll and there's the FIFA World Cup memorabilia giveaway. I've been keeping a mental tally on how many times adidas has pushed soccer content at me this month, and this is the latest entry. Bellingham, Dembele, Ronaldo — I don't know who half these people are and I don't need a signed kit. Mark as read. Forward to a friend? No.

## What's working

- Adistar XLG 2.0 is front and center — I know exactly what's being sold the moment the email opens.
- "Adiprene cushioning" is in the body copy — cushioning is a word that gets my attention, even when it's buried.
- SHOP NOW button is visible and clear — no hunting for where to tap.
- Clean render, no broken images, text is readable without pinching to zoom.
- adiClub giveaway acknowledges I'm a member — a small courtesy, even if the prize does nothing for me.

## What's weak

- "Modern edge to your everyday look" — I'm not shopping for an edge. I'm shopping for shoes my feet won't hate at hour three.
- Both models look like they're 25. I can't picture myself in this shoe based on what they're showing me.
- The FIFA World Cup section is still here. We've done this dance before — I do not want signed soccer memorabilia.
- No discount, no price, not even a "starting at $X." Just aesthetics and a contest for a sport I don't follow.
- Six adidas emails in ten days, and most of them feel built for someone else's inbox. The cadence is starting to wear on me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the cushioning, not the aesthetic.** "Adiprene cushioning" should be the headline, not a footnote. "Step all day in Adiprene cushioning — Adistar XLG 2.0 is here" is a line I'd actually read twice.
- 2. **Age up the models.** Show someone who looks like they're living in these shoes — walking a lot, on their feet, not posing. That's who buys comfort-forward sneakers.
- 3. **Pull the FIFA panel for this segment.** adidas knows my purchase history. If I've never bought a jersey or a soccer item, route me to a different second panel — a clearance strip, a "you might also like" comfort shoe row, anything.
- 4. **Add a price anchor under the hero.** Even "from $120" gives me something to evaluate. Right now there's nothing to decide with.
- 5. **One pitch, one CTA.** Cut the giveaway entirely for Walker-type segments. A tight single-product email with cushioning specs and a price would do more work.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Enlarged cushioned sole, molded cage — shop now`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "Elevate your look with Adistar XLG 2.0"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed QA failures: a broken click-tracked link and a missing plain-text MIME part. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources risk silent blocking in major clients, and one image is duplicated 8× in the source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — unrecognized URL type**
The QA prober rejected a link with path type `/g/` inside the click-redirect wrapper:

```
unknown url type: '/g/2b35a2ba-53e1-4456-84a5-2b4ee6f2b089?txn=dc9ea680-7077-11f1-b0c7-ae32f7ad8416'
Tracked via: https://dv.adidas.com/o/2b35a2ba-53e1-4456-84a5-2b4ee6f2b089?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The `/g/` path segment is not a recognized adidas redirect type (contrast with `/o/` used on all other links). This likely resolves to a dead or misconfigured destination. Needs manual click verification before send.

**[INFO] 35 tracking links skipped**
All use `dv.adidas.com` click-redirect with Salesforce Marketing Cloud parameters (`cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`). Normal — not a defect, but none were probed for destination health.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image source — 8 instances of the same asset**

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

This arrow icon appears to be reused across 8 CTA rows but is served over plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block non-HTTPS images. Should be `https://`.

**[WARN] HTTP open-tracking pixel**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ef726622a-eb2153a3-d566-4179-bd2b-e73657f022ba-000000/...
```

Open-rate tracking pixel is also HTTP. Gmail will proxy it, corrupting the true open signal.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- Dynamic content image: `2b35a2ba-53e1-4456-84a5-2b4ee6f2b089` (served from `dv.adidas.com/o/`) — may be a product image rendered server-side, not a tracking pixel. Needs `alt` if it carries visual content.
- Open-tracking pixel: `Z0oCevW5f4dMo1tvAsiZaH0yaTwM67fgdyCDoXpbcb4=452` — blank `alt=""` is correct for a 1×1 pixel; flag is technically a false positive here but the attribute must be explicitly present.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks in `<head>`. No functional breakage, but adds ~1.5 KB of redundant CSS. Consolidate into one block.

**[INFO] Malformed `<meta>` charset tag**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The correct form is:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, some older clients may ignore the charset declaration.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unresolved placeholder tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`) visible in the truncated source. Personalization fields appear to have rendered before delivery.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part missing**

```
Plain-text fallback missing or too short: Text version is 0 chars
```

CAN-SPAM does not strictly mandate a text part, but a 0-char text part is a strong spam-filter signal and breaks delivery to text-only clients. Many ESPs (including SFMC) auto-generate a text part — verify that the generation step ran.

**[INFO] `referrer` suppression**

```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```

This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on outbound clicks. Combined with UTM params embedded in the redirect payload, this is intentional and does not create a compliance gap — noted for landing page analytics context.

**[INFO] ROBOTS meta**

```html
<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />
```

Correct practice. No issue.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

All 35 probed links route through `dv.adidas.com/o/` with a base64-encoded `cp_tp=v3.eJzj...` payload that likely carries campaign attribution (SFMC standard). UTM surface is inside the redirect layer, not in the raw `href`, so the QA prober cannot inspect destination UTM params directly.

The one broken link (`/g/` path) is the only confirmed destination gap. If that link maps to a product page, that product's click attribution is lost entirely for this send.
