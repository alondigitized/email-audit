## What's working

- The BOGO 1/2 off banner at the top is bright and readable, no squinting required.
- Birkenstock "almost never goes on sale" line caught my eye — that's a real, specific reason to click, and it's a brand I actually own.
- The layout is clean, no overlapping text or broken images that I could see.
- Free shipping and Rewards join links are visible up top for anyone already looking for them.

## What's weak

- The whole middle of this email is a sneaker catalog by age group — nothing calls out comfort, cushioning, or arch support, which is the only thing that gets me to actually buy a shoe.
- Way too much scrolling before I hit anything relevant — by the time I got to Birkenstock and Crocs, I'd have already given up on my phone.
- "Back to school" framing again, same as the July 19th email — I'm 62, this isn't my shopping occasion, and it makes me feel like this is a mass blast, not something built for me.
- No mention of the men's comfort or walking shoe lines I'd actually shop for — it's all running and skate shoes in the Men section.
- Subject line "Let's gooooo" reads like it's talking to a teenager, not someone my age.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the deal that actually applies to my kind of shoe — comfort/slip-on brands like Skechers or Hoka — instead of burying Birkenstock and Crocs under a sneaker catalog.
- 2. Cut the bestseller grid down or split it by comfort priority (cushioning, arch support, hands-free) instead of just Women/Men/Kids — most of us don't want to scroll past a dozen running shoes to find our category.
- 3. Drop the "back to school" framing for sends that go to older shoppers — it signals immediately that the email wasn't built with me in mind.
- 4. Put the Birkenstock and Rewards $10 offer near the top — that's the strongest, most specific hook in the whole email and it's currently hidden two-thirds down.

# Technical Email Audit — Famous Footwear "Let's gooooo 📣 BOGO 1/2 off FTW"

## 1. Technical Summary
Table-based HTML4.01 Transitional email served via Salesforce Marketing Cloud–style infrastructure (`click.email.famousfootwear.com` redirects, `qs=` tokens). QA reports an 86% pass rate with one deliverability warning; source review found a malformed charset meta tag and a batch of images with missing/empty alt attributes.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Open-tracking pixel present and correctly formed: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?OIE4R3KEYKIUNJJGG3IYCK42OY.70251&d=70251&bmt=0`.
- All content links are wrapped in SFMC click-tracking redirects (`click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...`). QA skipped HTTP probing on **32 tracking links** (expected behavior for redirect domains), so final destinations are unverified in this pass.
- QA WARN: plain-text part is **76.7% URL characters** (12,499/16,298 chars) — the long encoded `qs=` tokens are bloating the plain-text alternate, a known spam-filter heuristic risk and a poor experience for plain-text readers.
- No malformed `href` syntax found in the visible markup.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed charset declaration**: `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` — as written this tag does not actually declare the charset to any client that relies on it, risking encoding fallback issues (relevant given the 📣 emoji in the subject line).
- Viewport meta sets `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0`, disabling pinch-to-zoom — an accessibility issue for low-vision recipients.
- QA flags **30 images with missing alt text**. Confirmed in the visible source: the header logo image (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) is wrapped in an `<a href>` link but has `alt=""` — since it's a functional/linked image, empty alt gives screen-reader users no context for the link target. The divider image (`4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg`, 2×450px) also has `alt=""`, which is acceptable since it's purely decorative. The remaining 28 flagged images fall outside the truncated source and couldn't be individually verified as decorative vs. content-bearing.
- Preheader div uses `display:none !important` rather than the more robust visually-hidden pattern (1px font + color-match + `max-height:0`/`overflow:hidden`). Some clients (notably Outlook desktop) don't surface `display:none` text as inbox preview text, risking the preheader ("Plus, save on Birkenstock, Nike, Jordan and more.") not displaying.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tags, AMPscript, or personalization placeholders (e.g. `%%FirstName%%`, `{{...}}`) found anywhere in the visible HTML. No issues found in the visible source, though the truncated remainder wasn't reviewable.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- Source is truncated before the footer — unsubscribe link and physical mailing address (both CAN-SPAM required) are not present in the provided excerpt and cannot be confirmed present or absent. Flag for follow-up with the full HTML.
- No SPF/DKIM/DMARC header data was supplied for this audit; authentication cannot be assessed from the given inputs.
- Sending domain (`email.famousfootwear.com`) is a dedicated subdomain, consistent with standard authentication practice, but this is not itself proof of a pass.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
All outbound links use opaque SFMC redirect tokens (`?qs=...`) rather than visible UTM query parameters, so landing-page destinations and UTM tagging can't be determined from source alone — all 32 were skipped by the automated prober. Recommend resolving a sample of tracked links to confirm final URLs carry proper `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` values and land on live, matching pages.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the charset tag: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.
