# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-29**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the browsing part of skechers.com on my phone without much trouble. The buying part didn't work. The homepage, the Men's menu, the Men's Shoes page, the product page and the search results all loaded and were readable. But I never got logged in, and my Add to Cart tap stopped at a red "Please select a size" warning. The cart page was a blank white screen. So I couldn't buy anything.

I only have yesterday's number (2/10), not yesterday's screenshots. Today looks slightly better because browsing and search work, but the cart is still broken.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 2/10, a small improvement).

Skechers gets credit for browsing, for the "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Arch Fit" entry points, and for search that returns comfort shoes. It loses most of the score because the cart was blank and I never got logged in. A shopper who can't check out is worth nothing to them.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort words are up front.** The top menu row on the homepage says "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning." Those are the things I look for.
- **The Men's menu is easy to follow.** It has big text and clear rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies. It has a Back button and an X, and each row is easy to tap.
- **The Men's Shoes page has shortcuts for me.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two picture tiles. That's how I shop.
- **The product page is clean.** The name, the star rating (about 4.5 stars, 96 reviews) and the $155 price are all large and clear.
- **The size buttons are big** and easy to tap with a thumb.
- **The main blue Add to Cart button is big and easy to hit.**
- **Search worked.** "comfort shoes" gave 1,574 results, and the first ones were Slip-ins and Relaxed Fit shoes with Contour Foam. That's what I meant.
- **Pricing is easy to read.** Search results show sale price against regular price, like $59.99–$88.00.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken.** Step 8 was a blank white page, with no items, no "your cart is empty" message, and no header. I don't know if my shoe was added or if the page just failed. I'd leave.
- **Add to Cart didn't go through.** In step 7 the red "Please select a size" warning was showing, and the cart badge still said 0. The site never guided me to pick a size first. It didn't look as though a shoe was added.
- **Login didn't happen.** Steps 2 and 3 look exactly like the homepage. I never saw a sign-in form or a "Hi, Walker." Search still said "15% off or Log In for 20% off," so I wasn't recognized. I got no personalization at all.
- **The homepage doesn't speak to me.** The big picture was a neon-green "Outrun the Night" running shoe. I'm a 62-year-old who walks, not a night runner. Nothing on the first screen mentions cushioning, arch support or slip-ins beyond the small menu row.
- **The featured shoe I landed on isn't comfort-led.** The Aero Burst is a $155 running shoe, and it says "Excluded from promotions." I didn't see any mention of cushioning or arch support in the part I could see. The page also shows a "Members Save 20%" banner, which makes the exclusion feel worse.
- **Some text is small.** The "Applied at Cart" promo line and the "Free pickup at Set Location" line are small and gray. The menu row cuts off at "Max Cus…" and needs a side-swipe I might not notice.
- **The first search results were women's shoes.** I'm shopping men's, and the site didn't know that.
- **No comparison against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and no price-versus-quality talk.** I saw no reviews summary, "why this costs more" note or side-by-side view.
- **751 results in Men's Shoes with no clear starting point.** The filter is a small icon on the left.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** Show my items, or at least say "Your cart is empty." Never show a blank page.
2. **Make Add to Cart forgiving.** If I haven't picked a size, scroll to the size buttons and highlight them, or open a size picker. Don't leave a red warning that's easy to miss.
3. **Make login work and show it.** After I sign in, show my name, and use my size and my past shoes. Right now the "Log In for 20%" line just nags.
4. **Lead with comfort on the homepage.** Put a "Slip-ins," "Arch Fit" or "walking" story in the first screen ahead of running promos.
5. **Put comfort facts on every product page.** Add a short line such as "cushioned, arch-supported, slip-in" near the price, and explain any "excluded from promotions" tag.
6. **Make the small text bigger,** especially the promo lines, and add an arrow or fade on the cut-off menu row.
7. **Remember I'm shopping men's** and show men's results in search.
8. **Add a simple "Is this worth $155?" section** with reviews, warranty and return details, so a shopper like me can do the price-versus-quality math without leaving.

## 6. Bottom Line

It's a good-looking site that's easy enough to browse, and Skechers does have the comfort products I want. But I got stuck at the last step. I couldn't get a shoe into the cart, and the cart page came up blank. If this happened to me at home, I'd probably open Hoka or New Brooks and buy there. Fixing the cart and the size prompt is worth more than any other change.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- The run was heavily affected by bot mitigation. Six requests returned 429: five to the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.797`) and one to `/cart/`. The cart response is the likely cause of the View Cart axe results.
- Web Vitals are incomplete. LCP and CLS were not captured for any of the 8 steps (`?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals verdict.
- Steps 2 and 3 (Log In, Men category) report `https://www.skechers.com/` as their URL and return the same HTML `<head>` as the homepage. Those steps probably did not navigate, so their data may duplicate the homepage.
- The 17 axe violations cover only 3 of the 8 steps (Homepage, View Cart, Search). Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Log In reported none, or weren't scanned. The data doesn't say which.

## Accessibility

**Homepage (9 violations):**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` (ARIA roles missing their required parent).
- **Critical:** `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
- **Serious:** `link-name` (links with no accessible name).
- **Moderate, landmark structure:**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main` (more than one `<main>`)
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-unique`
  - `region` (content outside landmarks)
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.
- Together these suggest nested or duplicated landmarks, likely from injected or third-party markup.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4 violations):** `aria-allowed-role` (minor), `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` (all moderate). These repeat the homepage landmark issues, which points to a shared template.

**View Cart (4 violations):**
- **Serious:** `document-title` (no `<title>`) and `html-has-lang` (no `lang` attribute).
- **Moderate:** `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- The `/cart/` request returned 429, so this is probably a bare block or error document rather than the real cart template. Treat these as an artifact of the block until the cart is re-tested unblocked.
- The homepage does set `lang="en"`.

## SEO

- **View Cart:** no `<title>` and no `<h1>` (see above). This only matters if the real cart page has the same defects. Carts are usually `noindex`, so search impact is low.
- **Homepage:** `<html lang="en">` is present. The truncated samples don't show the title, meta description, canonical or structured data, so those can't be assessed.
- **Duplicate snapshots:** Log In and Men category return identical homepage HTML at the same URL. If that reflects real behavior (client-side routing with no URL update), it would be a crawlability concern. It is more likely a capture problem. Verify.

## Performance

- **TTFB:**
  - Full loads: Homepage 464ms, Log In 410ms, Men category 410ms. Acceptable, and below the 800ms "good" threshold.
  - Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search: 25–26ms. These are implausibly low for origin responses, so they are probably client-side transitions or cache hits, not document TTFB.
  - View Cart: 135ms, and the response was a 429.
- **LCP/CLS:** not captured on any step. Instrumentation gap.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage `<head>` injects 20+ third-party scripts. These include:
  - Analytics and tag management: GTM, GA4, Google Ads/DoubleClick, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`).
  - Ad and social pixels: TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Taboola, Facebook, RTB House.
  - Other: Pulse Insights, Impact, Tune, `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
  - Most are `async`, but the volume adds main-thread and network contention. Consent-managed scripts use a `vice-managed-src` attribute.
- **Font loading:** fonts load through a JS loader (`webfontloader`) from a third-party CDN. This adds a dependency on the critical path, and a late font swap can cause layout shift (CLS is unmeasured).
- **Staging GTM container on the production page:** the GTM URL carries `gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x`. This loads a non-live GTM environment, which can ship unpublished tags. Confirm this is intentional.

## Mobile Optimization

- The captured viewport height is about 664px (`--vh: 6.64px`). The site uses a `--vh` custom-property workaround for mobile viewport height. `100dvh` or `svh` units would make that JS unnecessary.
- No viewport meta tag was visible in the truncated sample, so it can't be assessed.
- No other mobile-specific issues are evident from the data. Tap-target size and responsive image use were not measured.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6, all 429):**
- Five hits on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint `/149e9513-…/2d206a39-…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.797`. The repeats indicate rate-limiting or challenge failure for this automated client. If real users trip this, they would fail Kasada validation.
- One 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. The cart document itself was blocked.

**Console (24 total, 10 shown):**
- `Failed to load resource … 429`: these match the network errors above.
- `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (4 shown). Some script, likely third-party, registers `unload` handlers. This is being disabled in Chrome and blocks back/forward cache eligibility.
- `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified …` (2 shown). This comes from the browser's built-in AI API, not site code. It is probably an injected script or extension, or a Chrome feature. Confirm it doesn't come from the site's own or a vendor script.
- 14 console errors were not included in the sample.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run with mitigation cleared.** Use a whitelisted IP or header, or a slower cadence, so the Kasada 429s and the `/cart/` 429 stop invalidating results. Then re-test View Cart for real title, lang, `<main>` and `<h1>` defects.
2. **Fix instrumentation.** Capture LCP and CLS (for example, `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`, or the `web-vitals` library). Also capture document TTFB using navigation timing, and mark same-document transitions separately.
3. **Verify navigation for Log In and Men.** Assert the URL or DOM changed before recording a step.
4. **Fix homepage accessibility, starting with the critical items:**
   - Give buttons and links accessible names (`aria-label` or visible text).
   - Fix the ARIA roles that are missing required parents.
   - Remove the duplicate or nested `<main>` and `<aside>` landmarks.
   - Label landmarks so they are unique.
   - Apply the landmark fixes to the shared template so they also clear on Search.
5. **Audit the GTM environment.** Remove `gtm_preview`/`gtm_auth` from production if unintended.
6. **Trace the `unload` handlers.** Find which script registers them and move it to `pagehide`/`visibilitychange`. This restores bfcache eligibility.
7. **Trim third-party scripts.** Consolidate pixels through the tag manager, defer non-critical tags until after load or consent, and self-host or preload fonts instead of using a cdnjs loader.
8. **Extend axe coverage** to all 8 steps and report per-step results, including steps with none.
