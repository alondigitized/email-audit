# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-10**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked Skechers.com on my phone and never got a shoe into my cart. The home page and the Men's Shoes page looked fine. On the shoe I picked, I hit Add to Cart without choosing a size. I got a red "Please select a size" message, and the cart badge stayed at 0. Then the cart page came up as a blank white screen. Searching "comfort shoes" gave real results, but the first two were women's shoes.

Steps 1, 2 and 3 look identical. I never saw a pop-up get dismissed or a login happen. The page still showed an empty cart, and nothing on it was personal to me.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday's 3/10, so I see no regression and no improvement.

I have no record of yesterday's screens, so I can't compare details. The sale never completed, the cart was blank and nothing felt personal. Those problems keep the score low.

## 3. What's Working

- **Shortcuts for my kind of shopper.** The row under the header (Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning) is exactly what I shop for. The Men's Shoes page also has "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles. Someone paid attention to older, comfort-minded buyers.
- **Menu drawer.** It's a clean list with big text and wide rows. Men has Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections and Clothing. A 62-year-old can tap those without trouble.
- **Product page basics.** The title is large and readable. It has a 4.5-star rating with 175 reviews, a clear $165.00 price and a big blue Add to Cart button.
- **Size picker.** The size buttons are big and easy to hit, and sold-out sizes (12.5, 14.0) are crossed out. That is honest, and it saves me a wasted tap.
- **Search.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,596 results with Filter and Sort controls. It took the query seriously.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is a blank white page.** I couldn't tell whether anything was in it. For someone my age this feels broken, and I'd leave. I'd go to Hoka or Brooks.
- **Add to Cart fails without a size.** The size error shows, but the size was never chosen for me. It also sits well below the shoe photo, so a person tapping Add to Cart higher on the page might not see it. The cart badge stayed at 0 the whole time.
- **Mixed messages on price.**
  - The banner says "Extra 10% OFF Everything." The shoe I picked says "Excluded from promotions" at $165.
  - Search results say "Members Stack an Extra 10% OFF! Applied at Cart" in red.
  - I can't work out what I'd really pay. As a price-vs-quality shopper, this loses my trust.
- **No personalization.** I tried to log in, but the screen was the same as the logged-out home page. Nothing said "welcome back," showed my size or suggested a shoe. The product page still said "Sign in for free shipping & returns!"
- **Search ranked women's styles first** for a man's walk-through. Nothing told me these were women's until I read the small gray text.
- **Hero image is a running ad.** "Aero Burst, Designed to Cut Through the Wind" is aimed at runners. I want cushion and arch support for walking around. The comfort row is there, but it is cut off ("Max Cus…") and I have to swipe to see the rest.
- **No comparison content.** I saw nothing about how these compare with Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and nothing on arch support or cost per year of wear. The 10% off is fine, but price and quality math is missing.
- **Small text in places.** The orange "Excluded from promotions" line and the gray "Women's" labels are small. I'd have to zoom.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page now.** It should always show something: the items, or a friendly "Your cart is empty" with a shop button. A blank page costs sales.
2. **Make Add to Cart clearer.** Auto-select a size when only a few are left, or scroll to and highlight the size row when it's missing. Show a visible "added" confirmation and update the cart badge.
3. **Clean up the discount message.** Say one thing in one place, such as "10% off applies to this shoe" or "this shoe is excluded." Don't show both on one page.
4. **Actually show I'm logged in.** After sign-in, show my name, my saved size and width, and put Slip-ins and Wide Fit at the top.
5. **Default search to the shopper's category.** If I came from Men, show men's shoes first.
6. **Put comfort first on the home page.** Lead with slip-ins and arch support, and make that row wrap rather than cut off.
7. **Add trust and comparison content.** A short "Why Skechers vs. Hoka/Brooks" or "cost per wear" blurb would help me decide.

## 6. Bottom Line

The shelves are stocked with the right shoes. The Slip-ins and arch-fit shortcuts show Skechers understands me. But I couldn't finish a purchase, and a blank cart makes me trust the site less. The 10% off promise that doesn't apply to my shoe makes it worse. Today I'd walk away. The shoe that works and checks out without fuss wins my foot. Score: **3/10, unchanged from yesterday.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was partly blocked by bot protection, so some of the data is unreliable.
- **Bot challenge:** Kasada (`x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`) returned 429 on its `/fp` fingerprint endpoint five times. `GET /cart/` also returned 429. The View Cart step therefore audited a block or error page, not the real cart.
- **Navigation:** the HTML captured for "Log In" and "Men category" is labelled `https://www.skechers.com/` and has the same `<head>` as the homepage. Those two steps likely did not navigate, or the capture reused the homepage document.
- **Metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals cannot be assessed.
- **Confirmed issues:** these are homepage ARIA and landmark violations, a GTM preview environment loaded in production, a heavy third-party tag load, and an `unload` permissions-policy violation.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations in total. They fall into three groups.

**Homepage (real, likely reproducible):**
- **critical `aria-required-parent`:** an element with a child-only role (e.g. `listitem` or `tab`) is missing its required parent.
- **critical `button-name`:** one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- **serious `link-name`:** one or more links have no discernible text.
- **moderate landmark structure:** `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique`. Together these suggest a `<main>` nested inside another landmark, or a second `<main>`, probably from an injected or third-party widget.
- **moderate `region`:** content sits outside any landmark.
- **minor `aria-allowed-role`:** a role is invalid for its element.

**Search results:** `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` repeat the homepage pattern. This points to a shared template or header and footer.

**View Cart (probably an artifact):** `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail. That fits an empty 429 block page and is not evidence of a defect in the real cart template. Re-test with a session that passes Kasada.

## SEO

- **Homepage:** `<html lang="en">` is present. The samples are truncated before the `<title>`, meta description, canonical and structured data, so those cannot be assessed.
- **View Cart:** the missing `<title>`, `lang` and `<h1>` are attributable to the 429 response. The cart is not an indexable page anyway.
- **Not assessable:** the Log In and Men category captures are the homepage document, so category-page SEO was not tested.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** values range from 25 to 423ms. Everything is under 450ms, which is acceptable.
  - Log In and Men category share an identical 423ms, which supports the stale-capture theory.
  - The 25–30ms values on later steps suggest cached or client-side transitions, not full document loads.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured. Check that the `PerformanceObserver` was registered before navigation and that `buffered: true` was set.
- **Third-party load:** the `<head>` of every sample injects 20 or more third-party tags. They include:
  - Analytics and session replay: GTM, gtag (three IDs), Quantum Metric, Monetate (two scripts), Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Impact and Tune.
  - Ad pixels: TikTok (three scripts), Bing, Yahoo (`ytc.js`), Pinterest, Reddit, Snapchat, Taboola, Nextdoor, Facebook and Criteo/RTB House (`creativecdn`).
  - Duplicates: `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` and `tags.creativecdn.com` each appear twice in the same document.
  - Expect main-thread contention and a poor INP and TBT profile.
- **Font loading:** `webfontloader` comes from the `cdnjs.cloudflare.com` third-party origin. That adds a DNS and TLS hop, and text is hidden or swapped until the `wf-*-active` classes are set. Self-hosting with `font-display: swap` and `preload` would remove the dependency.
- **GTM environment:** the container is loaded with `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. That is a GTM environment (non-Live snapshot) snippet on the production site. It may serve unpublished container versions and bypasses the Live container's caching. Verify this is intentional.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` custom property is set inline on `<html>` (`6.64px`, so a 664px viewport). This is a JS workaround for mobile browser chrome and risks a layout shift when the viewport resizes. Use `dvh`/`svh` units instead.
- No viewport meta, tap-target size, or responsive image data was in the truncated samples. No other issues found in the captured data.

## Console & Network Errors

24 console errors were reported and 10 were provided. Of those 10, only three distinct messages occur:

| Message | Count in sample | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| `Failed to load resource: 429` | 3 | Kasada `/fp` fingerprint requests rejected; automation is being rate-limited or blocked |
| `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…` | 3 | Chrome built-in AI Summarizer API called with an unsupported language; the origin script is unidentified, so check which tag or extension invokes it |
| `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` | 4 | A script registers an `unload` handler, which is blocked by policy and deprecated. It is probably a third-party tag. Use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` instead |

Network errors: 6 in total, 429 on every one.
- 5 are the Kasada `/fp` endpoint.
- 1 is `/cart/`, which blocks the cart page itself.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit through Kasada.** Use a trusted session, an allowlisted IP, or a lower request rate. The cart findings, the 429s and the missing Log In and Men category data all depend on this.
2. **Fix the Log In and Men category steps** so they actually navigate. Then re-capture HTML, TTFB and axe results.
3. **Capture LCP and CLS.** Register the observers before navigation, or use `web-vitals`.
4. **Fix the homepage critical ARIA issues.** Add accessible names to the unnamed buttons and links (`aria-label` or visible text). Give the `aria-required-parent` element its correct parent role.
5. **Remove the nested or duplicate `<main>` and make landmarks unique** (distinct `aria-label`s). Check whether a third-party widget injects them.
6. **Review the GTM `gtm_preview=env-1` snippet.** Switch production to the Live container if it is unintentional.
7. **Cut the tag load.** Remove duplicate tags, consolidate the ad pixels behind GTM with consent gating, and defer non-essential tags until after load or interaction.
8. **Replace `unload` handlers** with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, and find which script registers them.
9. **Replace the JS-driven `--vh`** with `dvh`/`svh` and self-host the web fonts.
