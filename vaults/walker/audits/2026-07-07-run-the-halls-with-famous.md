# Run the halls with Famous 📚🎒
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Back to school. Backpacks. Jordan sneakers. Kids in hallways. I'm 62 years old with grown children and grandkids who buy their own shoes — and Famous Footwear just dropped a full back-to-school catalog in my inbox like I'm shopping for a second-grader. I scrolled through rows of ballet sneakers, kids' character shoes, and school performance runners looking for anything aimed at a man my age who wants a walking shoe with some arch support, and what I finally found was the same BOGO half-off clearance banner I've seen in every single email from these folks since at least July 2nd. That deal has been running so long it's practically a permanent fixture.
- To be fair, the BOGO clearance got my attention when I landed on it — that's real value, and I've bought off that rack before. But Famous buried it under the entire school shopping season first. I'm not the target audience for this send and they either don't know that or don't care.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and move on, mildly annoyed that I opened it expecting something new.

## What's working

- **BOGO 1/2 off Clearance is a proven hook** — same deal that's been running all week, but it's a real offer, clearly stated, with visible CTAs for Men and Women.
- **"SHOP MEN" button exists** — at least they gave me a lane to click even if I had to scroll past a school supply list to find it.
- **Email body renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum; the layout holds together on what looks like a mobile view.
- **Famous Footwear branding is consistent** — I know exactly whose email this is without guessing.

## What's weak

- **Every inch of hero real estate is kids content** — Jordan collabs, ballet sneakers, backpacks, kids characters — nothing signals to a 62-year-old male in the above-the-fold zone.
- **That preheader is still a raw tracking URL** — I've flagged this in every Famous Footwear audit going back to the June 27 send. It's been broken for weeks. Whoever owns the email template hasn't touched it.
- **BOGO fatigue is real** — This is the fifth or sixth iteration of the same clearance deal in about a week. "Stars, stripes and savings" as the BOGO subhead doesn't make it feel fresh; the 4th of July hook is stale on July 7th.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere** — Cushioning, arch support, wide widths, ease of entry — not a word of it. I'm supposed to trust Famous Footwear for my walking shoes but they gave me nothing to chew on.
- **Subject has zero urgency or offer** — "Run the halls with Famous" tells me absolutely nothing about why I should open this versus any other email sitting next to it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send** — A back-to-school email belongs in the inboxes of parents and grandparents buying kids' shoes, or at minimum, a men's version of this send exists without the school hallway hero. Sending the same blast to a 62-year-old male comfort shopper is lazy.
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately** — This is still leaking a raw click-tracking URL after multiple sends. That one fix alone upgrades every future open.
- 3. **Lead with the BOGO offer in the hero** — If the clearance deal is the real pitch, lead with it. Don't make me excavate it from the bottom of a kids' shoe catalog.
- 4. **Retire "Stars, stripes and savings"** — The 4th of July was three days ago. Update the subhead to something seasonless that holds through mid-July.
- 5. **Add one comfort-focused callout** — Even a single line — "Wide widths available" or "Cushioned insoles" — would give me a reason to click the SHOP MEN button instead of scrolling past it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL — HIGH VOLUME] 26 images missing `alt` text.** Every content image in the email has `alt=""`. Affected images include the logo (`3b6a7dc1`), hero images, product GIFs (`7ab9d644`, `bdfdb467`, `a832e677`), and all product imagery. When images are blocked — the default in Outlook desktop and many corporate environments — recipients see a blank email with no text fallback.

The logo image specifically (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) should carry `alt="Famous Footwear"` at minimum.

**[WARN] `user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />` prevents pinch-to-zoom. This creates an accessibility barrier for low-vision users and violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4. Gmail strips viewport meta anyway, but this is a sender-template-level problem to fix.

**[INFO] HTML4 Transitional doctype** — `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` is intentional for legacy Outlook compatibility. Not a defect.

**[INFO] `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing the `http-equiv` attribute** — should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Most clients infer UTF-8 anyway, but the malformed tag is technically invalid.
