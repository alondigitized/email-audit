# $10 off sandals that’re making 🌊🌊
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- DSW pulls me in with the subject — "$10 off sandals" sounds like a summer deal I might actually use. Sandals are my thing from May through September: wide toe box, cushioned footbed, easy on and off. So I opened. Then the hero hit me: "MAKE WAVES," and the first thing I see is strappy women's heels getting a water splash. Okay. Not exactly what I had in mind.
- I scroll a bit and it gets worse for me. The whole midsection is a Joan Miller x DSW collab with a header that literally reads "IT'S A GIRL, GIRL, GIRL, SUMMER." I'm not the target here. They used $10 off sandals as the hook — which I'll admit worked on me in the inbox — and then delivered a women's fashion lookbook. The Shop by Size and Shop Wide Widths links at the bottom are the only places this email acknowledges that men with wide feet exist.
- I'd mark it read and move on. DSW's been in my inbox every single day this week — yesterday was another "$10 off" push, the day before was "ICYMI: $10 off." I'm starting to feel like the offer isn't special anymore, it's just wallpaper. No forward to a friend, no click.

## What's working

- **Subject line is concrete.** "$10 off sandals" is real money and a real product — it got me to open, which is more than most promo emails can say.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** "MAKE WAVES" lands first, CTA buttons are clearly labeled and easy to tap on a phone. No clutter.
- **Shop Wide Widths / Shop by Size sections exist.** That's useful for a guy like me who can't always find 4E in store. At least they're there, even if buried.
- **No render bugs.** Email laid out fine on my screen — no broken images, no overlapping text.
- **Current season.** Summer sandals is the right beat for early June. They're not off-calendar.

## What's weak

- **Bait-and-switch demographic.** The subject says sandals, the email says "IT'S A GIRL, GIRL, GIRL, SUMMER." I feel like I was lured in under false pretenses.
- **$10 off is nowhere in the hero.** The offer that got me to open isn't called out above the fold. I had to scroll to find any trace of it.
- **Joan Miller collab means nothing to me.** A TV personality whose show I've probably never watched doesn't build any trust or desire. It's background noise.
- **Cadence fatigue.** This is at minimum the fourth "$10 off" email this week. The urgency they're trying to manufacture has completely worn out. I know there'll be another one tomorrow.
- **No men's comfort story anywhere.** Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or easy slip-on fit — the things that would actually make me buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men vs. women.** Send men a sandal email with men's sandals. A comfort-oriented 62-year-old getting "IT'S A GIRL, GIRL, GIRL, SUMMER" is just waste. This one change would probably double click-through for male subscribers.
- 2. **Put the $10 off in the hero.** If the offer is the subject line, it needs to be the first thing I see in the body — big text, right under the headline.
- 3. **Lead with comfort signals for a men's version.** Something like "cushioned footbed + wide widths" above the CTA would close the loop between my shopping priorities and the product.
- 4. **Rest the $10 off offer.** It's been running every day. Either put a real deadline on it or take a day off and let it feel scarce again.
- 5. **Lose the celebrity collab from the subject/preview if you're mailing to men.** Or swap it out entirely — this isn't a signal that builds purchase intent for me.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Wide widths, $10 off: shop men's sandals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned sandals that feel good all day — $10 off this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Easy slip-on styles, all sizes and widths. Save $10 today.`
