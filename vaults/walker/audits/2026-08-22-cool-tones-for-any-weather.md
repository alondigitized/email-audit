# Cool tones for any weather
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Alright, "the blue edit" — that's not a shoe email, that's a color palette email, and that's the problem. First thing I see is two young folks in matching electric blue outfits looking like they're headed to a rave, not the trail. My eye eventually finds the sneakers down the page — 1080v15, 880v15, the 740 — and those are the parts I'd actually stop for.
- There's a legit 25% off $125 banner up top, which is real money, and running/walking/lifestyle shoes do show up eventually. But the whole first screen is a fashion mood board before I get to a single price or a product I'd wear. I'm 62, I don't want "head-to-toe aquatic tones," I want to know if the 880 comes in a wide and what it costs.
- Compared to that "Bestsellers restocked" email from the 20th with the brown suede 9060 front and center — that one I'd have clicked. This one I'd scroll through fast, maybe pause on the shoe grid, and move on. Not deleting it, not forwarding it either.

## What's working

- The 25% off $125 banner at the very top is a real, specific offer I can use.
- Once I get past the fashion shots, there's a clean shoe grid — 1080v15, Fresh Foam X 880v15, 740, Fresh Foam 680v9 — with names I recognize.
- "Running Walking Lifestyle" category links at the bottom are useful shorthand.
- No render bugs — everything loaded clean, nothing broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- Hero models in matching neon-blue crop top and bike shorts sets — that's a young person's fashion shoot, not a comfort-shoe pitch for a guy my age.
- I have to scroll past two "outfit" hero images and a "the blue edit" title card before I hit an actual sneaker.
- No arch support, cushioning, or fit language anywhere — it's all about color coordination.
- This is the fourth or fifth New Balance email in a couple weeks and it's the second one built around a color theme instead of a shoe I might need.
- The "shop in store" photo at the bottom is just a storefront — not selling me anything new.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a shoe, not an outfit. Put the 1080v15 or 880v15 up top with the 25% off callout right next to it — that's the click driver, not the blue tracksuit.
- 2. Cut the fashion hero entirely or shrink it to a small banner; two full-screen lifestyle shots before any product is too much scrolling for a phone.
- 3. Add one line about comfort — cushioning, arch support, "all-day wear" — somewhere near the shoe grid so it's not just color and style.
- 4. Ease off the color-themed campaigns for a stretch; between "the blue edit" and the running-styles-on-sale from the 16th, it's starting to feel like the same shoes reshuffled under a new headline.
