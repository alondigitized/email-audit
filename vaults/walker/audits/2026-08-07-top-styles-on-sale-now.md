# Top styles on sale now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is actually one of the more usable New Balance emails I've gotten lately — and I've gotten a lot of them. "Top styles on sale now" doesn't tell me much, but once it opens up I get an actual grid: eight shoes, each with a name, an original price, and a marked-down price. That's the kind of straight math I can do in my head while I'm sitting in my recliner.
- The hero shot is just somebody's legs and feet in joggers — fine, I don't need to see a model's face to judge a shoe. What caught my eye were the chunky ones: the 9060 and the ABZORB 2010 look like they'd have some real cushion under the foot, which is what I'm after. No mention of arch support or comfort tech anywhere though, so I'm just guessing based on how thick the soles look in the photos.
- I'd click through on one or two of these — probably the 9060 or the 740 — just to check if they come in my size and to see how they feel described. Wouldn't forward this to a friend, but I wouldn't delete it without at least scrolling through the grid first, which is more than I can say for the last few "Night Lights" and back-to-school sends.

## What's working

- Real discount math right on the product tiles — original price crossed out, sale price next to it, no guessing.
- A proper grid of eight different styles instead of one outfit shot and a "shop now" button.
- The chunkier retro styles (9060, ABZORB 2010) look like they'd have decent cushioning, which is what actually gets my attention.
- Clear promo code (BTS26) and clear date window (Aug 6–20) right there — not buried in fine print I have to squint at.
- "Shop men" and "Shop women" buttons up top so I'm not wading through kids' stuff to get to my size.

## What's weak

- That random photo of two wooden folding chairs in the middle of a shoe email — I have no idea what that's supposed to be selling me.
- Not one word about comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere in the copy — just names and prices.
- Subject line "Top styles on sale now" is generic; every retailer sends some version of that every week.
- Nothing tells me if these are new markdowns or the same sale I saw in the July 22nd "25% off" email — feels like the same well getting re-dipped into with a slightly bigger number.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the subject line for something with the actual number and a deadline — "Up to 30% off, ends Aug 20" would've gotten me to open faster than "Top styles."
- 2. Cut the folding-chair photo entirely — it's dead space that could be another shoe or a comfort callout.
- 3. Add one line under each shoe name flagging cushioning or fit — even just "extra cushioned" or "wide-fit friendly" would separate this from every other rack of sneakers.
- 4. Lead with the code and date window higher up, near the hero, not just at the bottom near the fine print.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 30% off select styles — through Aug 20`
- **Subject Alt B:** `9060, 530, 574 — all marked down`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save with code BTS26, in stores and online`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on 8 fan-favorite styles`
