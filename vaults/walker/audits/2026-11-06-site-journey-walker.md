# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-06**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old guy on his phone looking for comfortable slip-ons, and I didn't get to a working cart. The top of the site is decent. The tabs for Slip-ins, Wide Fit and Arch Fit are what I'd look for. The men's menu is easy to read, and the "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile on the Men's Shoes page is what I came for.

Then it fell apart. I never saw a login take effect. The "Add to Cart" step showed a red "Please select a size" error and nothing was added. The cart page was a blank white screen. Search for "comfort shoes" put women's shoes first. I'd give up and go to Hoka or Brooks.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 3/10). This is unchanged, and the cart is still the biggest problem. Navigation and search are a bit better than nothing, but I never reached a successful add-to-cart or a real cart.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort-first header tabs.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…" sit right under the logo (Step 1). That's my vocabulary.
- **The men's menu is easy on a 62-year-old.** The text is large, rows are well spaced, and there's a Back button and an X (Step 4). "Comfort Technologies" is a good category name.
- **Hands Free Slip-Ins and Walking Shoes tiles** lead the Men's Shoes page, with 811 results and Filter, Sort, Size and Width chips (Step 5). Width is a big deal for my feet.
- **The product page is clear.** It has a big bold title, a $165.00 price, a 4.5-star rating with 184 reviews, and Klarna/Afterpay ("From $15/month") (Step 6).
- **The size buttons are big and easy to tap** (Step 7). The sold-out sizes (12.5, 13, 14) are struck through, so I don't tap them by mistake. The Add to Cart button is large, dark blue and easy to find.
- **Search shows "Members Get 20% OFF! Applied at Cart"** in red under the price (Step 9). The money message is easy to see.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken, or at least invisible.**
  - Step 7 shows "Please select a size" and the cart badge still says 0.
  - Step 8 is a completely blank white page. No items, no "your cart is empty," no checkout button.
  - I can't tell whether the site failed or the test never picked a size. Either way, it's not a working cart for a shopper like me.
  - The product page is where it breaks. Nothing is pre-selected and no size is highlighted for me.
- **Login shows nothing personal.** Step 3 is identical to the homepage, with no "Hi, Walker" and no points balance. The cart icon shows 0. I can't tell whether I'm signed in. Nothing feels personalized.
- **Step 2 (popups) is identical to Step 1.** It's not a problem for me, but the journey doesn't show anything useful there.
- **The homepage hero doesn't speak to me.** It's a loud "Skechers Britto" art collab with a woman in leggings and colorful sneakers. Nothing about comfort, arch support or hands-free. The comfort tabs are one swipe to the side and get cut off ("Max Cus…").
- **Mixed promo messages.**
  - The top banner says "20% OFF Regular Priced Items."
  - The strip below says "Holiday Checklist! 30% OFF Select Styles."
  - The product I picked says "Excluded from promotions."
  - Whether I'm getting 20%, 30% or nothing, I can't tell what I'll pay. I trust a brand less when the email, banner and product page disagree.
- **Search isn't tuned to me.** "Comfort shoes" returned 1,641 results, and the first two are women's styles ("Women's" is printed on each). I'm a man, and the site knows I came through Men.
- **The men's grid doesn't read as men's.** The first two Men's Shoes results are colorful Britto-style sneakers. They look more like fashion shoes than comfort shoes. I didn't see the Slip-ins I came for until I tapped the tile.
- **No comparison or value math.** Nothing on screen compares Skechers to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. There's no "cost per day of wear," and no arch-support or cushion rating on the cards.
- **The $165 price is steep for a casual shoe.** On top of that, it's excluded from promotions, which makes it feel even higher.
- **Small tap targets and text.**
  - The size chips are fine, but the filter chips (Filter, Sort by, Size, Width) are small.
  - The Width chip is cut off at the screen edge.
  - The promo-strip links ("Shop | Details") are small.
  - The "Members Get 20% OFF" red text is small.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** A shopper who hits Add to Cart without a size should be taken to the size picker, not left with a red error far down the page. The cart page must always show something: items, or "Your cart is empty" with a button back to shopping.
2. **Show the login state.** After sign-in, show "Hi, Walker" in the header, my points balance, and my usual size and width.
3. **Pre-select my size once I've shopped.** Show "Your size: 10.5, wide" at the top of the product page.
4. **Make the promos match.** Use one clear sale message across banner, strip, card and product page. If an item is excluded, say why next to the price, and don't show it under a banner that says 20% off.
5. **Make the homepage and search comfort-first for me.**
   - Put Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Wide Fit in the hero for a returning comfort shopper.
   - Apply my men's context to search, so the first results are men's.
   - Add badges on cards: "Hands-Free," "Arch Support," "Cushioned."
6. **Add a "Why this is worth $165" block.** Cover cushion and arch details, and cost per day of wear. Even a short comparison to other brands would build trust.
7. **Make the filter chips bigger and wrap them.** Don't let Width fall off the right edge. That's the filter I use most.

## 6. Bottom Line

The shopping part is fine: the menu is readable, the comfort categories are there, and the size picker is easy to tap. What's missing is the part where I actually buy something. The cart shows no items and then a blank page, I never see my login take effect, and the promos contradict each other. That's a 3/10 again, with no regression and no improvement I can point to. If I ended up here I'd go to Brooks or Hoka, because those sites let me buy shoes.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audit data is partly unreliable.
- **Navigation:** The HTML samples for Homepage, Log In and Men category all report `https://www.skechers.com/` as the URL. These steps probably did not navigate. They were likely overlays or in-page interactions, or the URL was captured before navigation finished.
- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS are `?` for all 8 steps, so no paint or layout-stability data was captured.
- **View Cart:** The step hit a `429` on `/cart/`. The resulting axe findings describe a rate-limit or error response, not the real cart page.
- **Third-party scripts:** The head is crowded with them (see Performance).

## Accessibility

17 axe violations were found across three steps.

**Homepage (9)**
- `button-name` (critical): at least one button has no accessible name.
- `aria-required-parent` (critical): an ARIA role is missing its required parent, e.g. `listitem` or `tab` without `list` or `tablist`.
- `link-name` (serious): at least one link has no discernible text.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` (moderate): there is more than one `main`, and at least one is nested inside another landmark.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` (moderate): landmark structure is inconsistent, and some content sits outside any landmark.
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor): a role is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.

**View Cart (4)**
- `document-title` (serious), `html-has-lang` (serious), `landmark-one-main` (moderate) and `page-has-heading-one` (moderate).
- Together these indicate a bare error document, consistent with the 429. They are not evidence about the real cart template.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- These repeat the homepage landmark and role problems, which points to a shared header or footer template. The two critical and serious homepage findings did not recur on search.

The homepage HTML sets `lang="en"` correctly. The `lang` and `<title>` failures are specific to the cart response.

## SEO

- **Cart error page:** It has no `<title>`, no `lang` and no `h1`. If a 429 is served to crawlers, the page can't be indexed meaningfully, and rate-limiting bots on `/cart/` risks crawl errors.
- **Duplicate `main` landmarks:** These can confuse the document outline, but this is a minor SEO concern.
- **Not assessable:** The HTML samples are truncated before any meta tags, canonical links or structured data, so I can't assess those.

## Performance

- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage, Log In and Men category are 338–343ms, which is acceptable.
  - Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search are 20–28ms.
  - View Cart is 126ms.
  - Values this low suggest cache hits, bfcache, or same-document navigation rather than real server timings. They can't be compared directly with the first three.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not measured, so no Core Web Vitals assessment is possible. Fix the collection first, since the observer may be registered after navigation.
- **Third-party scripts:** The homepage head already carries a large number of them, all async. I could identify:
  - **Tag management and testing:** GTM, Monetate (`entry.js`, `custom.js`), Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights.
  - **Ad and social pixels:** TikTok, Bing UET, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Yahoo (`ytc.js`), Facebook, Taboola.
  - **Other tracking and tools:** Impact (`impactcdn`), RTB House (`creativecdn`), Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Tune (`go2sdk`), Stape.
  - Combined main-thread and network cost is likely significant. The Men category page loads additional tags beyond those on the homepage.
- **Font loading:** Fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, which adds a third-party dependency and a render-blocking risk for text. The `wf-*-active` classes confirm the fonts did load.
- **GTM preview environment:** The GTM URL contains `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth=...`. That suggests a non-live GTM environment is being served on the production site, which isn't intended and can load unreviewed tags.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is set inline to `6.64px`, which corresponds to roughly a 664px-high viewport. The JS-computed `--vh` workaround is a legacy pattern. CSS `dvh`/`svh` units remove the dependency on script timing.
- **Unverified:** I have no evidence on tap target sizes, viewport meta or responsive images, since none of these were in the data.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** This is the one real network failure. It is likely bot or rate-limit protection triggered by the automated Add to Cart then View Cart sequence. It makes the cart step unreliable to test and could affect real users who reload the cart quickly.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…" (×5):** Chrome's built-in Summarizer API was called with an unsupported language. This is not a standard site API. It probably comes from a browser feature or extension, or from a script calling an experimental API without feature detection. Check whether any site or third-party script calls `Summarizer` and add a language-support check.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document." (×8):** A script registers an `unload` handler, which Chrome is deprecating. It is blocked by default and also disables bfcache eligibility. Likely sources are third-party tags. Replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the audit harness (high).** Confirm each step actually navigates. Three steps report the same URL. Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` registered before navigation (or via `addInitScript`). Retry or back off on 429s so the cart step reflects the real page.
2. **Remove `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth` from the production GTM snippet (high).**
3. **Fix the critical axe issues on the homepage (high).** Add accessible names to icon buttons (`aria-label`), add text or `aria-label` to the links flagged by `link-name`, and put required ARIA children inside their parent roles.
4. **Consolidate landmarks (medium).** Use exactly one top-level `<main>`. Make `<aside>` and `role="complementary"` top-level. Give repeated `nav` and `region` landmarks unique `aria-label`s. Wrap stray content in landmarks.
5. **Replace `unload` handlers with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` (medium).** Audit the third-party tags to find the offender. This restores bfcache eligibility.
6. **Audit third-party tag load (medium).** Remove unused pixels and defer non-essential ones until after interaction or consent. Self-host the web font loader or switch to CSS `@font-face` with `font-display`.
7. **Check the 429 on `/cart/` (medium).** Confirm the rate limit doesn't hit real users, and make sure any error response still includes `<title>`, `lang`, a `main` landmark and an `h1`.
8. **Guard the Summarizer API call (low).** Feature-detect it and check language support before calling it.
