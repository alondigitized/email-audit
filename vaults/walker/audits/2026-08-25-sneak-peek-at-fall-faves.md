## What's working

- The "Up to 60% off clearance" banner up top is a real, specific offer with a clear "Shop All Clearance" button.
- No fake urgency or all-caps DEADLINE nonsense for once — refreshing after this week's "LAST. DAY." run.
- Layout is clean, one product per section, easy to scroll on my phone without squinting.
- "Free shipping for VIPs," "in-store pickup," "opt-in to texts get 15% off" strip near the bottom is at least legible and low-friction if I wanted to poke at it.

## What's weak

- Every single hero product — suede mules, clogs, ankle-strap heels — is women's fall fashion. Nothing here says "comfort, cushioned, slip-on for a 62-year-old guy."
- The "Retro Sneakers / Tall Boots / Autumnal Browns" trend row at the bottom is the only spot that could plausibly include men's shoes, and it's three tiny thumbnails with zero detail — no price, no comfort callout, no "shop men's" link right there.
- The size-by-number grid (5–11) up top is a women's sizing convention; as a guy I glance right past it.
- Nothing in this email acknowledges I might be a men's shopper at all — feels like a stranger's mailing list, not a brand that's been sending me daily coupons all week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this send — if DSW knows I've only ever clicked men's/clearance content, don't send me the suede-and-clogs lookbook at all, or lead with a men's fall trend module instead of burying it at the very bottom.
- 2. Give the men's trend row real estate — actual product shots with prices and a "cushioned comfort" callout, not a thumbnail afterthought.
- 3. Swap the women's size grid for a men's one (or drop the grid entirely) when sending to a male subscriber.
- 4. Add one comfort/fit detail line near any men's product — arch support, slip-on, walking-shoe cushioning — since that's what actually gets me to click.
