# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker again — same one, sixth time this week, still not mine. "READY TO CHECK OUT?" like there's some cart out there with my name on it. There isn't. I don't shop for floral women's sneakers, and no amount of gold-foil urgency banner is going to change that.
- Buried under the phantom cart, though, is an actual sale — 20% off cozy fit and glide step styles, code UPGRADE — and a men's slip-ins section with an "Altman - Murdock" style that's exactly my lane: hands-free, casual, looks comfortable. If the email had opened with that instead of the ghost cart, I'd have kept reading. As it is, I'm scrolling past the top third annoyed before I even get to something relevant.
- Would I click? Maybe on the men's slip-in, once I fight past the fake urgency. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on — this is the same broken-record cart nag Skechers has been running on me all week, just with a discount code tacked to the bottom this time.

## What's working

- The 20% off / code UPGRADE banner is clear and specific — real offer, real deadline (8/26-8/31).
- Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit "Altman - Murdock" is actually my kind of shoe — slip-on, casual, no laces.
- Best-sellers grid at least mixes in men's and kids' styles, not just the one recycled sneaker.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup options are visible for anyone weighing price.

## What's weak

- Same pink floral D'Lites "cart" hero for the sixth straight email — I never put this in a cart, and the fake urgency is wearing thin.
- The one relevant men's product (Altman - Murdock) is a tiny thumbnail buried in a 5-shoe grid, no price, no size info.
- Nothing here says "member" or references my account at all — feels like a stranger blasted this to everyone with an abandoned session.
- The layout makes me scroll past a fake cart, a floral shoe, and a wall of kids' shoes before hitting anything I'd actually buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake abandoned-cart framing entirely — if I didn't put it in a cart, don't tell me I'm "ready to check out." Lead with the real 20% off sale instead.
- 2. Put a men's comfort shoe (like the Altman - Murdock slip-in) in the hero spot, with price and "arch fit / cushioned" callouts, not a floral sneaker I've never looked at.
- 3. Add basic segmentation — if I've only ever looked at men's styles, don't show me five product tiles where four are women's/kids'.
- 4. Show price and available sizes on thumbnails so I don't have to click through just to see if it's my size.
