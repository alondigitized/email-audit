# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that pink floral D'Lites sneaker, sixth time this week now, this time under "YOU LIKED THESE, AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG." I never liked it, I never clicked it, and telling me I "weren't wrong" about a shoe I've never once looked at twice doesn't land any better the sixth time than it did the first. It's a women's floral shoe being pushed at a 62-year-old guy who wears a men's slip-on — there's just no version of this that becomes relevant to me.
- Where it half-recovers is further down — men's slip-ins with an actual "Relaxed Fit: Altman - Murdock" name I could search for, plus that 20% off members code. But by the time I scroll past the D'Lites hero, the cart guilt trip, and a wall of unrelated kids' garage sneakers, I've mentally checked out. This is a mark-as-read, not a click, and I sure wouldn't forward it to anybody.

## What's working

- The men's slip-in and Arch Fit styles further down the grid are actually in my lane.
- 20% off with a clear code (EXTRA20) and a real deadline (9/14) is a legible offer once you get to it.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup are nice-to-haves I've noticed on other Skechers sends, at least it's consistent.

## What's weak

- The hero product is, again, a pink floral women's sneaker — the fourth or fifth time this exact shoe has anchored an email to me this week.
- "You liked these, and you weren't wrong" is a lie — I never liked or clicked that shoe, so the personalization claim actively erodes trust.
- No "Hi, Walker" greeting, no points balance up top — feels like a stranger blast, not a member email, unlike the 9/8 "End of Season" send that led with my name and 1000 points.
- Kids' garage sneakers and girls' glam racer sneakers in the grid have zero relevance to me and just add clutter before I get to anything I'd wear.
- Cart-abandon framing ("Complete Your Order") on a shoe I never put in a cart is the same broken-trust move I called out on 9/8's "Did you forget something?" send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the personalization engine — if I've never clicked or carted the D'Lites, don't headline an email with it and claim I "liked" it. Lead with the men's Arch Fit or slip-in styles I've actually shown interest in.
- 2. Bring back the "Hi, Walker" + points balance treatment from the 9/8 "End of Season" email — that one scored well specifically because it felt like it knew me.
- 3. Move the 20% off code above the fold — that's the one thing here worth my time, and it's buried after two rows of shoes I'd never buy.
- 4. Cut the kids'/girls' items from a men's-skewed cart-recovery email; segment those out.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walker, your 20% off code expires 9/14`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's Arch Fit styles are back in stock — 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members save an extra 20% on sale styles through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit favorites, now with code EXTRA20.`
