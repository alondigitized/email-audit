# 20% off + FREE shipping on shoes made for you 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Twenty percent off plus free shipping — and the email leads with it, big and bold, right up top. I'll admit that got me. After a week of "Spend & Save" countdowns and panic emojis, this one felt like a gear shift. No manufactured crisis, just a clean offer. And then it goes one step better with "We picked these just for you" — and I can actually see Skechers and New Balance and Brooks in there. Those are my brands. That section alone made me slow down.
- But then there's the preview text again. A raw tracking URL. The exact same garbage I flagged on the June 15th send, the 16th, the 17th, and again on the 21st. At this point I've stopped being annoyed and I'm just curious whether anyone at Famous Footwear actually reads their own inbox. Five-plus sends with broken preview copy and nobody's caught it. That's not a technical glitch anymore — that's a workflow problem.
- I'd click on the New Balance section or the Brooks Ghost. I wouldn't forward this one — the personalized picks angle is for me, not someone else. Mark-as-read with a click? Yes, probably.

## What's working

- **The 20% off + free shipping combo in the hero is immediately visible** — no scrolling, no hunting, the offer is right there on mobile.
- **"We picked these just for you" is doing real work here** — Skechers, New Balance, Brooks are exactly the brands I comparison-shop; this felt less like a mass blast and more like someone ran my purchase history.
- **New Balance gets its own 30% off section** — stacked on top of the 20%, that's serious value if it applies, and NB walking shoes are on my shortlist right now.
- **Brooks Ghost 17 shows a specific price ($119.95)** — that's the kind of concrete detail that makes me think "okay, I can evaluate this."
- **Breaking from the urgency-only cadence was the right call** — after several "last chance" screams in a row, a clean value offer felt like relief, not pressure.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is a tracking URL. Again.** I've called this out on at least five consecutive Famous Footwear sends. At this point it reads like the team doesn't dogfood their own product.
- **"Online only" is small print** — I shop in stores too, and if this deal doesn't work at my local Famous Footwear, I want to know that before I drive over. That caveat deserves more prominence.
- **No end date visible on the 20% offer** — "Starting Now" tells me when it begins, but not when it dies. I've been burned by "starting now" promos that expire before I get back to finish the order.
- **The personalized picks section is small on mobile** — the shoe thumbnails are tight and the product names are hard to read without zooming in. For someone my age squinting at a phone, that's a tap I might skip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text before anything else.** This is the single highest-leverage change — it's broken on every send and it's the first thing subscribers see. Set a real preheader: something like "20% off + free shipping — your picks are waiting."
- 2. **Add an expiration to the 20% offer.** Even "This week only" works. "Starting Now" with no endpoint creates hesitation, not urgency.
- 3. **Make "Online only" a prominent badge near the CTA** — not buried fine print. Customers who shop in-store need to know immediately so they don't feel baited.
- 4. **Increase the product card size on the personalized picks** — if you're betting on personalization as the hook, make those picks readable at arm's length. Bigger thumbnails, larger name text.
- 5. **Add a "why we picked this" micro-line** — even "cushioned sole" or "wide width available" under each shoe would make the personalization feel real instead of algorithmic filler.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off Brooks, New Balance + free ship`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks: 20% off + free shipping, this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers, New Balance, Brooks — picked for you. Offer ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off + free shipping on the comfort brands you actually wear.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` charset tag malformed** — missing `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration is ignored in some clients (Outlook, older webmail); can cause character rendering failures for special characters.

**`user-scalable=0` non-standard** — `0` is not a valid value per the HTML spec; should be `user-scalable=no`. Most mobile clients tolerate it, but it is technically incorrect.

**CSS typo — `lineapacing`** — invalid property in a content `<td>`:
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a CSS property. This rule is silently dropped; whatever spacing intent it had is lost.

**30+ images with empty `alt=""`** — confirmed by QA across all major content images including the logo, dividers, all product photography, and all 6 igodigital rec slots. In image-blocked environments (common in corporate email, Outlook defaults), all content reads as blank. The logo link in particular presents no accessible label:
```html
<a href="..."><img src="...3b6a7dc1....png" alt="" /></a>
```
