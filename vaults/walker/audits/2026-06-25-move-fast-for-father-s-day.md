# Move fast for Father’s Day
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- I'll be straight with you — the first thing I noticed after opening this is that it says "Order by 6/16" for Father's Day shipping. I'm reading this on June 25th. Father's Day was four days ago. The headline says "Right on Time," and I nearly laughed out loud. HOKA sent me a "move fast" email after the train already left the station.
- Setting aside the timing disaster, the bones of this email aren't bad. Four solid men's shoes — Bondi 9, Mach 7, Arahi 8, Arahi 5R — laid out in a clean grid. I can read the product names without squinting. The brand is one I'm already subscribed to and have bought from before. But the whole pitch is framed as "buy Dad a gift" — except I'm the dad. Nobody's buying me anything; if I want new HOKAs, I'm buying them myself. That framing pushes me sideways.
- I wouldn't click. I'd probably shake my head a little, wonder how HOKA let this sit in a queue a week past its useful life, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward this to anyone — there's nothing worth passing along when the offer is already expired.

## What's working

- **Clean product grid.** Bondi 9, Mach 7, Arahi 8, Arahi 5R — four recognizable shoes, names and colorways labeled, easy to scan on a phone screen.
- **Free expedited shipping is front and center.** The banner at the top earns its spot; I see it immediately.
- **"Dad-Approved" section header.** It's a little cheesy but it does the organizing work — I know I'm in a curated picks section without having to think about it.
- **Gift card fallback.** The eGift card block at the bottom is a smart hedge for people who genuinely didn't know what to buy. Good idea, just arrived too late.
- **Brand consistency.** HOKA looks like HOKA. Font, photography style, the neon accent on that gift card block — nothing feels off-brand.

## What's weak

- **The date is dead.** "Order by 6/16" is visible in the body copy and it's June 25th. "Right on Time" as a headline reads like a joke now. This kills the email's entire reason for existing.
- **"Move fast" subject line on a past-due offer is friction, not urgency.** I see that subject in my inbox after Father's Day and I'm already skeptical before I even open it.
- **Framed at gift-givers, not at me.** I'm not shopping for another dad — I am the dad. "Gift Guide" and "Not sure what to get Dad" both push me into the wrong role.
- **$250 floor for free shipping is steep.** One pair of Bondis runs around $165. I'd have to buy two pairs to unlock that shipping benefit, and the email doesn't acknowledge that math at all.
- **Zero comfort or fit language.** The Bondi 9 is the most cushioned shoe in the lineup — that's the one thing I actually want to hear. Not a word about it anywhere in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill this send or swap to a post-Father's Day self-purchase frame.** This email had a shelf life of about a week and was sent a week late. Next time, either suppress it after the deadline or rewrite the hero to "Treat yourself to what you wanted anyway" — turn the Dad recipient into the Dad buyer.
- 2. **Lead with comfort, not occasion.** "Bondi 9 — the most cushioned shoe we make" would move me more than "Gift Guide for Dad." Occasion hooks expire; product hooks don't.
- 3. **Drop the shipping threshold or explain the path.** If $250 requires two pairs, say "mix and match two pairs, shipping's on us." Otherwise the offer feels aspirational and out of reach.
- 4. **Call out fit signals in the product grid.** Even one line under the Bondi 9 — "max cushion, everyday comfort" — gives me a reason to click that specific shoe.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Bondi 9 is the most cushioned shoe we make`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `You deserve a pair too, Dad`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Free expedited shipping on $250+ — mix, match, go`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Max cushion. Stability. Walking comfort. Pick your pair.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">`:**
Two conflicting viewport declarations are present in `<head>`:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
The second overrides the first and contradicts it (`maximum-scale=1` vs. `user-scalable=yes`). Most clients will use the last declared value. Remove one; keep `user-scalable=yes` if pinch-zoom is desired.

**Bug — Malformed `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without it, some older clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**Bug — CSS mobile breakpoint has missing numeric values:**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width:480px)` block:
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Both rules are invalid — the numeric value before `px` is absent, and `border-top` has no color. These declarations will be silently ignored by all clients, meaning `.nav_padding` and `.nav_border` elements get no mobile override applied.

**`<title>` is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
In Gmail web and some preview panes, the `<title>` value is surfaced. An empty title can render as a blank tab label or fall back to the ESP's system name.

**`font-display: swap` in `@font-face`:**
Not parsed by any major email client. Harmless (ignored), but wasted bytes across five font declarations.

**`@font-face` client support:**
Custom fonts render in Apple Mail and iOS Mail only. Gmail (web and app), Outlook (Windows), and Samsung Mail all fall back to the system font stack. The fallback font stack is not visible in the truncated source — verify it is explicitly defined in all font-family declarations throughout the body.

**Alt text:** Cannot verify from truncated source. All `<img>` tags must carry `alt` attributes (even `alt=""` for decorative images) for accessibility and rendering in image-blocked clients.
