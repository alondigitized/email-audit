**Date:** July 17, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker Miles, 62, and I went through Skechers.com on my phone this morning looking for comfortable hands-free shoes. The navigation getting to Slip-ins was decent — Skechers knows their comfort story and puts it front-and-center in the menu. But by the time I added something to cart, the whole page went white and blank on me. Nothing. I'm standing there thinking my order went into a black hole. Then I searched "comfort shoes" and the first things they showed me were women's shoes. I'm logged in. They know I'm a man. That's a problem. The site has the bones of something good, but it keeps stumbling right when money is about to change hands.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**Score: 4/10** — Same as yesterday. No improvement.

The cart failure alone is a conversion killer. Every other issue compounds it. A guy like me who's already skeptical about buying shoes online needs zero friction at checkout. A blank cart page sends me straight to Brooks.com.

## 3. What's Working

I could see "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" all without scrolling frantically. Someone at Skechers designed this for people like me and it shows.

Homepage quick-links include "Slip-ins" right at the top. The Men > Shoes page leads with "Hands Free Slip-ins" as the page title and gives me 312 results. That's the right instinct — lead with the thing that makes Skechers distinctive.

**Fit filters on the category page (Step 5)**
Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit as pill buttons at the top of the results — I noticed those immediately. At my age, fit matters more than logo. That's smart placement.

**ADD TO CART button (Step 7)**
Large, full-width, high-contrast blue button. Easy to tap even with bigger fingers. The size picker had clear enough options. The "Please select a size" reminder with a warning icon was helpful — didn't let me proceed without making a choice.

**Promo visibility**
The "20% OFF Regular Priced Items Code: SUMMER" banner persisted across every screen I saw. Consistent. I didn't have to hunt for a discount.

After going through the whole journey — browsing, finding a product, selecting my details, tapping ADD TO CART — the cart rendered as a completely blank white page. Nothing. No items, no total, no "continue to checkout." This is a conversion-ending bug. I don't know if my item was added. I don't know if I should try again. **Only 2 reviews on the product (Step 6)**

The banner rotated from the summer sale message to an apparel discount message — that's fine — but I expected a modal or overlay to deal with. If a popup appeared and was dismissed too fast to capture, I missed it. If there was genuinely no popup, then this step is a no-op in the journey.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank white cart is a complete transaction failure. This needs to be the #1 priority regardless of what else is on the roadmap. Test across browsers and devices. Every day this is broken is revenue walking out the door.

2. **Filter search results by logged-in user's gender.** When a male member searches "comfort shoes," men's products should rank first. This is table-stakes personalization.

3. **Add a visible "logged in" state.** Show "Hi, Walker" or a name/initial badge on the account icon after login. Don't make me guess whether my session worked.

4. **Swap the homepage hero to lead with comfort, not sport.** "20 million feet can't be wrong" or "Walk all day" language would resonate with me far more than "GAME ON!" Show me a guy on a morning walk, not a colorful court shoe.

5. **Don't promote "20% OFF" sitewide and then land me on an excluded product.** Either exclude those products from "Slip-ins" category or clearly label them in the grid before I click in. Discovering the exclusion on the PDP feels like a trick.

6. **Boost social proof.** Surface more reviews — pull from verified purchases, add a "Most reviewed" sort option, show a "Comfort rating" breakdown (cushioning, arch support, ease of entry).

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Skechers.com runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), served via CDN. The audit covers 8 steps of a mobile purchase funnel. Key systemic issues: bot-mitigation (Kasada) is rate-limiting the automated session, the cart page renders in a broken iframe-based state missing core HTML structure, and the third-party tag load is excessive across every page.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations total. Critical items:**

- **`aria-required-parent` (critical, Homepage)** — ARIA child roles (e.g. `role="option"`, `role="row"`) rendered outside their required parent containers. Likely a carousel or dropdown component with broken markup.
- **`button-name` (critical, Homepage)** — One or more `<button>` elements have no text content, no `aria-label`, and no `title`. Screen readers will announce as "button" with no context.
- **`link-name` (serious, Homepage)** — Anchor tags with no inner text and no accessible label. Common in icon-only nav links without `aria-label`.
- **`document-title` + `html-has-lang` (serious, View Cart)** — The cart page is missing both `<title>` and `lang` on `<html>`. This indicates the cart renders inside a frameless iframe or a JS-injected shell that bypasses the standard page template. The `html-has-lang` violation on a page that clearly has `lang="en"` on the homepage suggests the cart loads a *separate document* without the attribute.
- **`landmark-main-is-top-level` + `landmark-no-duplicate-main` (moderate, Homepage)** — Multiple `<main>` elements or a `<main>` nested inside another landmark. Indicates component-level landmark misuse, possibly from Monetate/personalization injecting content outside the DOM hierarchy.
- **`heading-order` (moderate, Homepage)** — H-level skips (e.g. H1 → H3). Likely CMS-driven promo components with hardcoded heading levels.
- **`page-has-heading-one` + `landmark-one-main` (moderate, View Cart)** — Further confirms the cart page is structurally incomplete at render time.

---

## SEO

- **Cart page missing `<title>`** — Googlebot will index `/cart/` with no title signal. Not a major crawl priority but indicates template hygiene issues.
- **`--vh` CSS custom property set via inline style on `<html>`** — This is set to `6.64px` (= `window.innerHeight / 100`), computed JS-side. If the page is server-rendered or crawled without JS execution, any `100vh`-dependent layout breaks silently.
- **Duplicate tracker script tags** — `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` loaded twice on every page (visible in all HTML samples). No SEO penalty, but indicates tag management hygiene issues in GTM.
- **No `<link rel="canonical">` visible in truncated source** — Cannot confirm from samples alone, but worth verifying on category/PDP pages where facet URLs can duplicate content.
- **Multiple GTM containers** (`GTM-TDRBQZ`) plus direct `gtag` calls to multiple GA4 properties (`G-BH2DL4JXJL`, `G-XZTV9LQ9DQ`) — redundant measurement setup. Not an SEO issue directly, but signals measurement fragmentation.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` workaround** — Setting `--vh` via JS to correct for mobile browser chrome height is a known pattern but means any full-viewport component (hero, modals) requires JS to render correctly. Users on slow connections see incorrectly sized layouts until JS fires.
- **No evidence of `<meta name="viewport">` in truncated source** — Cannot confirm from samples. Must be present for mobile rendering to work at all; SFCC templates typically include it, but the truncation prevents verification.
- **Kasada bot-mitigation (`x-kpsdk-v`) fires on mobile sessions** — The `fp` fingerprint endpoint is being called and returning 429. On a real mobile device this would be transparent, but the 429 pattern suggests the fingerprinting payload is failing challenge resolution, which can degrade the session (slower page transitions, blocked cart writes). The cart 429 (`/cart/`) is the most severe — it implies the cart page load itself was rate-limited, which explains the missing HTML structure (`document-title`, `html-has-lang` violations).
- **Stylitics outfit widget** — `style.css` blocked by CORS. The Stylitics CDN (`web-assets.stylitics.com`) does not send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `skechers.com`. This means the outfit/style widget silently fails on every PDP load.

---

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting (6 network + 6 console):**
All 429s target the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/149e9513.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522`) and `/cart/`. This is the Kasada SDK retrying a failed bot challenge. In an automated audit context this is expected, but the `/cart/` 429 is not a fingerprint retry — it's the actual cart page returning 429, meaning Kasada blocked the cart request entirely. This would cause a complete checkout failure for any session Kasada classifies as suspicious.

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css...' 
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Root cause: Stylitics is serving the CSS with `<link rel="stylesheet">` cross-origin, and their CDN lacks the permissive CORS header. Fix requires Stylitics to add the header on their CDN, or Skechers to proxy the asset. The widget JS likely continues to execute but without styles.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 on real sessions:**
If Kasada's challenge fails for legitimate mobile users (e.g. VPN, privacy browser, aggressive ad-blocker), `/cart/` returns 429 and the cart page renders as a structurally broken document. Verify with Kasada whether the challenge threshold is tuned too aggressively for mobile User-Agents. The broken `document-title`/`html-has-lang` violations on View Cart are a symptom of this — the page that renders on 429 is an error page, not the real cart template.

**P1 — `button-name` and `link-name` (critical a11y):**
Unlabeled buttons and links are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures. Identify the specific components (likely icon-only nav, carousel controls, wishlist icons) and add `aria-label` attributes. This is also an ADA litigation risk.

**P1 — Stylitics CORS:**
File a ticket with Stylitics support to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or `*`) to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. No Skechers-side code change needed unless Stylitics does not resolve it, in which case proxy through a Skechers CDN path.

**P2 — Duplicate Yahoo pixel tag:**
`s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` loads twice per page via GTM. Deduplicate in the GTM container to avoid double-counting Yahoo conversions and wasting a network round-trip on every page.

**P2 — Cart page template integrity:**
The HTML structure violations on View Cart (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) suggest the cart renders inside a nested iframe or via a JS shell that bypasses the main SFCC page template. Audit the cart's rendering path — if it's an iframe, ensure the inner document has full `<html lang>` and `<title>` elements.

**P3 — Capture LCP and CLS:**
Re-run the audit with Core Web Vitals instrumentation (Lighthouse, WebPageTest, or PerformanceObserver in the test harness). TTFB alone is insufficient — LCP on mobile is almost certainly degraded by the third-party script volume, and CLS risk is high given the JS-driven `--vh` layout and Monetate personalization injections.

**P3 — Third-party audit:**
20+ third-party domains are loaded on every page. Audit each tag for business necessity and consolidate where possible (e.g. route social pixels through server-side GTM to reduce client-side requests). Quantum Metric, Monetate, and Bloomreach are the heaviest by payload.
