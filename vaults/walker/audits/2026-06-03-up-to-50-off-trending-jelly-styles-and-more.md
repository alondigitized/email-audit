# Up to 50% off trending jelly styles and MORE 🩴
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one out of habit more than anything else — Famous Footwear is in my regular rotation and 50% off usually means something. Then I landed inside and it was wall-to-wall jelly sandals and women's sizes. That's not me. The "It's jelly time" headline, the brightly colored slides, the lifestyle shot of a woman in a white linen set — none of that is aimed at a 62-year-old man who needs cushioning and arch support. I scrolled mostly out of curiosity, and I was glad I did, because buried near the bottom was a Brooks Ghost 17 at $110.99 — that's a shoe I'd actually consider. But I shouldn't have to dig for it.
- This one I'd mark as read and move on. The preview text was another raw tracking URL, same problem Famous Footwear has shown me on the May 12th, May 16th, and the other June 3rd sends. At this point it's not a one-off bug, it's a habit. And sending two emails in the same day — today there was already the Jordan slides one — is starting to feel like they're just blasting everything at everyone.
- Would I click? Probably not on the women's content. Maybe, maybe, I'd tap through on that Brooks Ghost if I were actively in the market. Would I forward it to a friend? No — unless I had a wife or daughter who was into jelly sandals, and I'd feel a little embarrassed sending it.

## What's working

- **50% off is real money.** The offer is clearly stated in the hero — "UP TO 50% off women's sandals" lands immediately without any mystery or fine print overhead.
- **Brooks Ghost 17 callout.** $110.99 for a Ghost is a number I can actually act on. That's a shoe I cross-shop, and naming it with a specific price is the right move.
- **Clean render.** Images loaded, layout held together, nothing overlapping or broken in the body.
- **Loyalty section present.** "Famous You Remember" shows up near the footer — good to see my status acknowledged even if the email didn't use it for anything personal.
- **Current season feels right.** Jelly sandals in early June is on-calendar — the creative matches the weather.

## What's weak

- **Entire email is women's.** From the hero to the size grid to every model shown, this is a women's email. The MEN link in the nav bar is decorative — it's not doing any work here.
- **That preview URL, again.** Third or fourth time this month Famous has leaked a raw tracking link into my inbox as a preheader. It's losing its ability to even annoy me at this point — now it just signals sloppiness.
- **"Jelly styles" is not my language.** I don't shop by trend categories; I shop by function. Cushioning, arch support, slip-resistant — none of that appears anywhere.
- **Two emails in one day.** The Jordan slides email already landed today. The cadence is getting heavy.
- **Brooks Ghost is buried.** The one thing in here that might actually get me to click is stuck below women's jelly content and a size chart I'll never use.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of the women's-only send.** If the entire email is women's sandals, don't send it to men. Or build a men's module — even a single row of comparable men's sandals or walking shoes — so the send has something for everyone on the list.
- 2. **Lead with the Brooks Ghost 17.** For the men's segment, that price-pointed product belongs in the hero, not the footer. "Brooks Ghost 17 — now $110.99" is a walker's headline.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** This is now a repeated failure across multiple sends. Write real preview copy that reinforces the subject.
- 4. **Consolidate same-day sends.** One email per day, maximum. Combine the Jordan slides and the jelly sale into a single send if both need to go out June 3rd.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brooks Ghost 17 dropped to $110.99 + up to 50% off sandals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Big summer savings: 50% off — including Brooks and Nike`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop men's and women's styles — sale prices on Brooks, Nike, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brooks Ghost 17 now $110.99. Plus sandals up to half off.`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Up to 50% off jelly styles" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: 25 of ~25 content images have empty `alt=""`.**
Every product image (`.jpg`) uses `alt=""` rather than a descriptive string. This is a blanket failure — not selective omission for decorative images. Affects screen readers and image-blocked rendering (Gmail "display images" off = blank email with no fallback text).

Evidence (representative):
- `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — logo image, `alt=""` (should be "Famous Footwear")
- `d66d009d-e00c-420a-bb21-02f697796005.jpg` — product image, `alt=""`
- 23 additional product/content images, same pattern

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is malformed.** The `http-equiv` attribute is missing:
```html
<!-- actual -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
<!-- correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Charset declaration is non-functional as written. Most clients will default to UTF-8 anyway, but this is a source defect.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta.** `user-scalable=0` is deprecated and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text). Should be `user-scalable=yes` or omit the attribute.

**`max-height:auto` in CSS** (`.header img`, `img` in mobile breakpoint) — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Browsers ignore it silently; no rendering break, but indicates template copy-paste debt.

**`lineapacing:20px`** is a typo in an inline style:
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
Should be `line-height` or `letter-spacing`. The property is silently ignored.
