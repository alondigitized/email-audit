# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Same cart, third time this week — Monday it was "Your cart expires soon," then "Your Items Are Waiting" again today, and it's still the Care Bears kids' sneaker and the hot pink women's high-top sitting in there like they belong to me. They don't. The one shoe up top, that white/navy/red Arch Fit D'Lites with the chunky sole, is the only thing in this whole email I'd actually put on my foot, and even that one I've already scrolled past twice this week.
- There's no offer here at all — no percent off, no free shipping line, nothing — just a "Complete Your Order" button sitting under three shoes, two of which aren't mine. That's not a deal, that's a nag. If the cart data's wrong this consistently, I stop trusting the "your cart" framing altogether.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm sure not forwarding this to anybody. Straight to read-and-ignore, same as Monday's.

## What's working

- The hero Arch Fit D'Lites shoe has that chunky sole and arch support look I actually care about — if it were the only thing shown, this'd be a decent reminder.
- "Complete Your Order" is a plain, unambiguous button — no guessing what it does.
- Clean white layout, big enough text, nothing crammed together — easy to read on my phone without zooming.

## What's weak

- Two of the three "cart" items are Care Bears prints and hot pink suede — not remotely something a 62-year-old guy ordered, and it undercuts the whole "we noticed you noticing us" line.
- No actual offer or discount anywhere in the email — just a bare reminder to check out, which doesn't move me the way a real price cut would.
- This is the same recycled cart from Monday's "cart expires soon" send — third nag this week, and it's starting to feel like a broken script rather than a sales team paying attention.
- The "more options" grid below is a wall of near-identical white sneakers with tiny captions — nothing there jumps out as relevant either.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the cart data so it only shows items I actually looked at — showing me kids' and women's shoes three sends running kills any credibility the "your cart" framing has left.
- 2. Put an actual discount or free-shipping line in the hero if you want a completed checkout — "your cart is waiting" with nothing sweetened isn't pulling me back in.
- 3. Stop resending the identical cart-reminder three times in one week — space it out or vary the creative, because right now it reads as an automated drip, not a real nudge.
- 4. Lead with the men's shoe that's actually relevant and drop the Care Bears/pink pairing from my version of this email entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Still deciding on the Arch Fit D'Lites?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size may sell out — cart reminder`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Finish checkout before it's gone — free returns on every pair.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Arch support, ready to ship. Complete your order.`
