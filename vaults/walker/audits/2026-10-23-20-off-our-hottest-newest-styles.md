# 20% off our hottest, newest styles
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and this is the fourth send this week still pushing 20% off with code JINGLE. The green "20% OFF*" banner is big and easy to read on my phone, and I'll give them that. But then the email goes almost entirely to women's shoes. Pumps, mules, a sandal and a slingback. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned, slip-on comfort, and I had to scroll a long way before I found anything for me.
- The "SHOP MEN" link up top is the only thing aimed at me, and it's small, tucked beside "Shop Women" and "Shop Kids." The hero section says "HEY, WHAT'S NEW?" and then offers one big "SHOP WOMEN" button. After yesterday's UGG slipper blitz and the three 20% emails on the 22nd, this one feels like a mailing for somebody else. I wouldn't click. I'd skim it and mark it read, and I wouldn't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF*" on green is huge and readable without zooming, and it's the first thing I see.
- The code JINGLE is printed right under the offer, so I know what to type.
- The "SHOP NOW" button and the three category links are clear and easy to tap.
- The "Shop by Size" block with wide width and wide calf options is a nice touch. Wide widths matter to me.
- The "Shopping Made Easy" row covers free shipping for VIPs, in-store pickup and Afterpay, so the trust and convenience cues are there.

## What's weak

- Nearly every product shown is women's: pumps, mules, a slingback, a boot. There's not one men's shoe in the grid, so it doesn't speak to me.
- "SHOP MEN" is a tiny text link, while "SHOP WOMEN" gets a big button. It feels like I'm an afterthought.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on ease. It's all "hottest, newest styles," and I don't shop by trend.
- The "Extra 20% off reg. price!" labels under the shoes are tiny red text I'd have to squint at, and they don't show any actual prices.
- The Shoebox Shuffle game is a lot of purple screen for a loyalty gimmick. I don't want to play games for Bonus Points.
- Four 20% emails in a few days makes the offer feel like it's always on, not something with a real deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. If I've shopped men's before, lead with men's comfort shoes. Put "SHOP MEN" on the same big button as the women's one.
- 2. Show real prices next to each shoe (for example "$79.99, now $63.99 with JINGLE") so I can do the price-vs-quality math.
- 3. Add a comfort row, like "Cushioned & slip-on" with Skechers, Hoka and Brooks, since that's what I cross-shop.
- 4. Give the offer a real end date, in a big font next to the code.
- 5. Cut the Shoebox Shuffle block or move it to the bottom. Don't send four nearly identical 20% emails in a week.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Braze-built, SendGrid-tracked (`ablink.dsw.com`) message with one confirmed broken CTA, the SMS opt-in link, which returned HTTP 400. It also has a malformed `<head>` and an accessibility-limiting viewport tag. SPF/DKIM could not be evaluated because of the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (FAIL):** "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" returned **400**. The URL is `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/dWX1tTVU49lCSoEee1D_kg~~/AAQRxRA~/l8in-Hu1jEsg...` (about 700 characters of encoded payload). It is one of only 12 links probed out of 72, and it is the only failure among them.
  - A 400 from a tracking redirector can mean a truncated or malformed payload. It can also mean the redirector rejects non-browser probes, or the target is an SMS deep link. QA data cannot tell these apart, so re-test it from a real client.
  - If the 400 is real, a revenue-relevant CTA (SMS list growth) is dead for recipients.
- **Plain-text part is bloated (WARN):** 27,197 of 31,927 characters (about 85%) are URLs. The long `ablink` tracking URLs dominate the plain-text alternative, which raises spam-filter risk and makes it unreadable.
- **Link coverage gap:** 60 links were not probed (footer, social and utility links). The pass rate is 62%, but the 400 above is the only recorded failure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images):**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/2OG3hEmQOpAjAEVoc5wGnA~~/AAQRxRA~/Afs4oL9k...` is a tracked image URL, probably a tracking image. The QA tool used the URL's filename-like stub as its identifier, which indicates no alt attribute.
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0.../o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261023_P251285_V3_WM-_Offer_MI_B` is an `o.gif` tracking pixel. Both should have `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom and conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 on supporting clients.
- **Malformed meta tags in `<head>`:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does not declare a charset.
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, so it has no effect.
  - The charset must then come from the MIME `Content-Type` header. If that header is missing or wrong, special characters such as ™ and ® may render incorrectly.
- **Web fonts:** The `@font-face` rules load `.woff` and `.ttf` files from `braze-images.com`. Gmail and Outlook ignore them, so the font fallback stacks need to be checked. They are not visible in the truncated source.
- Dark mode is declared correctly (`color-scheme: light dark` and `prefers-color-scheme` rules). No issues found there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered Liquid/Braze tokens (`{{ }}`, `{% %}`). The only subscriber-specific value visible is `mi_u=225090073` in the pixel URL. Any tokens in the truncated portion could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found (WARN):** SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The QA tool attributes this to the AgentMail relay stripping or not adding the header. This is a test-environment limitation, not a confirmed sender defect. Verify with the raw original headers (`dsw@e.dsw.com`).
- The unsubscribe link is always included in probing and was not reported as failing. The `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and the physical postal address were not in the data provided, so they are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Every link is wrapped in `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, so the destination URLs and any UTM parameters cannot be seen without following the redirects. Campaign identity is visible only in the pixel's `mi_ecmp=20261023_P251285_V3_WM-_Offer_MI_B`.
- The SMS opt-in CTA failed (400), so that email-to-landing path is broken or unverifiable.
- No other continuity issues are confirmed by the data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the "Opt-In to Texts" link with a real browser or mobile user-agent. If it still returns 400, regenerate it in Braze, and check whether the URL is being truncated or mangled.
2. Fix the head meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag.
4. Add `alt=""` to the two tracking images.
5. Shorten the plain-text version. Use fewer or shorter tracked links, or a hand-authored text part.
6. Check Authentication-Results, `List-Unsubscribe` and the footer address against the raw message. Follow the `ablink` redirects to confirm UTM parameters and landing-page consistency.
