# The Legend Returns. The Legacy Continues.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is actually the first adidas email in a while that made me stop and look — Billie Jean King, 82 years old, holding up a suede sneaker with that big grin. That's someone my age, not a teenager on a stone staircase. But then I read it and it's a suede tennis shoe — flat, no arch support I can see, no cushioning pitch, nothing about all-day comfort. It's a nostalgia/collector play, not a comfort shoe pitch. And then it swings right back into what's become the usual pattern from this brand — a soccer jersey shot on a pink background before the email's even done.
- So credit where due: an actual grown adult model, a real product front and center, no "shop the kids' collection" nonsense. But there's no price anywhere, no "shop now for $X," and the whole second half is a sweepstakes for a signed World Cup ball, which does nothing for me. I wouldn't click through on this one — I'd read it, register "oh that's a nice callback to Billie Jean King," and move on. Not forwarding it, not marking it read out of annoyance either. It's a wash.

## What's working

- Billie Jean King as the face of this — first time in weeks adidas has put someone my age front and center, and I recognize her.
- The shoe itself is right there, big clean product shot, no clutter.
- No kids' backpacks, no back-to-school junk this time — a nice break from the last few sends.
- Simple black-and-white "SHOP NOW" bar up top, easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- It's a flat suede tennis shoe — nothing about cushioning, arch support, or all-day comfort, which is the whole reason I'd buy a shoe.
- No price, no percentage off, no member discount anywhere — just "shop online or at your local store."
- That fine print about "purchase is not guaranteed" and "first-come, first-served" is a red flag before I even get to a register.
- The soccer jersey and sweepstakes section at the bottom has nothing to do with the shoe story and just dilutes the whole email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with why this shoe is comfortable to wear, not just why it's historic — I need to know if I could walk around Costco in it all day.
- 2. Put an actual price or discount in the hero section — "shop now" with no number attached doesn't move me.
- 3. Cut the World Cup ball sweepstakes entirely or send it separately — it drags a nice, focused story into a scattered one.
- 4. Drop the "not guaranteed, first-come-first-served" fine print higher up or soften it — feels like bait before I've even clicked.
