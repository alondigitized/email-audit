# Score an extra 20% off select styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Nike's back in my inbox, and at least this time they led with a real offer — 20% off is something I can actually use. The subject line was straightforward enough that I didn't delete it on sight. But the second I opened it, that big hero image of a young woman sprinting told me this wasn't really built for me. I'm 62. I'm looking for something comfortable I can walk around in all day. "Start Strong" in bold athletic type with a runner model feels like it belongs in someone else's inbox — maybe my grandson's.
- Scroll down and there's a second offer buried in there: 20% off an entire in-store purchase. That caught my attention for a second, until I noticed they cited a deadline of "through 6.31" — June has 30 days. That kind of sloppiness kills trust fast. If you can't proofread your own expiration date, I'm not confident you'll honor the offer at the register.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't forward it. If Skechers hit me with 20% off Slip-ins this week, Nike's email is already forgotten.

## What's working

- **Offer is front and center.** "EXTRA 20% OFF SELECT STYLES" is visible right in the hero — no hunting required.
- **Members banner up top.** "Free Shipping on Orders $89+" is visible immediately and adds a little loyalty feel.
- **Men's category tile is present.** The "Level Up Your Fit" grid at least shows a Men's tile, so I know there's something for me somewhere.
- **Clean layout.** No broken images, no overlapping text — it renders fine on my phone screen.
- **Nike branding is trustworthy.** Even if I'm not their core customer, I know who Nike is and the email looks legitimate.

## What's weak

- **Wrong demographic in the hero.** A young woman in full sprint is not who I see in the mirror. No signal that Nike knows a 62-year-old man opened this.
- **"Start Strong" / "Level Up Your Fit" language misses me completely.** I'm not leveling up anything. I need shoes that don't hurt by noon.
- **In-store offer requires a trip I'm not making.** I shop on my phone from the couch. "Find Your Store" is the last button I'm tapping.
- **"Through 6.31" is an impossible date.** June doesn't have 31 days. That's a proofreading failure that makes me doubt whether the deal is real.
- **Zero comfort or fit language.** No mention of cushioning, arch support, wide widths, or anything that maps to why I'd buy a shoe. It's all aesthetic performance talk.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero model for a man my age doing something casual.** Walking a dog, on a trail, at a farmers market. That one image change alone signals this email was meant for me.
- 2. **Fix the impossible date.** "Through 6.31" needs to be corrected before another send goes out. It erodes trust on a $0 fix.
- 3. **Add one comfort hook in the hero copy.** Even one line — "cushioned for all-day wear" or "slip-on comfort inside" — and I'm paying closer attention.
- 4. **Lead with the online offer for mobile users.** The in-store section is fine, but put the code-based discount higher and save the in-store block for desktop where people can realistically plan a store visit.
- 5. **Put a deadline on the online offer.** "Through Sunday" or "ends in 3 days" gives me a reason to act. Right now there's no clock on the 20% online code.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Nike's Memorial Day Start email (campaign `052126-na-e--dg-promo-memorialdaystart`) has a systemic click-tracking URL construction failure affecting all 8 navigable links, plus a missing plain-text fallback and one HTTP image asset — collectively degrading deliverability and click attribution for the entire send.

---

## 1. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Doubled-domain URL encoding bug (8 links affected)**

Every tracked link is malformed. The ESP click-wrapper (`click.em.nike.com/CL0/`) is receiving an already-encoded `https://www.nike.com/...` destination as a literal path segment rather than a properly wrapped redirect. Example:

```
https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F%3Fcp=...
```

The encoded `https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com` is embedded directly in the path, not as a `?url=` or similar redirect parameter. This pattern typically indicates a template double-substitution error — the merge tag was already resolved to a full URL before the click-wrapper was applied, or the click-wrapping step ran twice. Affected links: Nike.com (×2), Swoosh.com, Men, Women, Kids, Learn More, Get Help.

**Risk:** Links may redirect to a broken or 404 destination depending on how `click.em.nike.com` handles unrecognized path formats. Click tracking data will be corrupted regardless.

---

## 2. Rendering & Accessibility

**Warning — One image missing `alt` text**

Tracking pixel / open-pixel image at:
```
http://click.em.nike.com/CI0/0100019e4a897fd9-54dbbbeb-57b8-4356-92cb-fc7557ef9cc8-000000/hOOqEXTo8yLY_qjYCBe7fIg7QiviQ7...
```
Has no `alt` attribute. For a tracking pixel this is low severity, but it triggers accessibility linting failures.

**Warning — HTTP image asset**

Same pixel above uses `http://` instead of `https://`. Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content images. This will suppress open-tracking for a measurable portion of recipients.

**No issues found** — Responsive breakpoints (`max-width: 640px`), mobile layout utilities, and font stack fallbacks (`Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif`) are structurally present and correctly formed.
