# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-14**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front door and found Men's Shoes without trouble. Then the trip fell apart. When I tapped "Add to Cart," I got a red "Please select a size" warning and the cart badge stayed at 0. The cart page then loaded as a completely blank white screen. Search for "comfort shoes" worked, but the first two results were women's shoes. I never saw any sign that I was logged in.

The Men's Shoes page did have a "Hands Free Slip-Ins" tile and "Walking Shoes" right at the top. That is what I came for, and it's the best part of the site. But the checkout path I'd actually use doesn't work on a phone.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** (yesterday: 4/10).

That's a one-point drop. The cart is the reason: a blank cart page means a sale lost outright. I can't compare other details with yesterday, since all I have is the score. The browsing screens look about the same as a typical day.

## 3. What's Working

- **Text and header are easy on my eyes.** The logo, search, account, bag and MENU buttons are in a clear row. The MENU label sits under the icon, which helps.
- **The Men menu is the easiest part of the site.** Rows are big and well spaced, with arrows on the right: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections and so on. I could tap them with a thumb.
- **Comfort words are up front.** The top strip says "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…". Those are the things I shop for.
- **Men's Shoes starts with my kind of shoe.** The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles come first. Filter, Sort, Size and Width are all there too. Width matters to me.
- **The product page shows the basics clearly.** I could see the price ($90.00), the star rating (17 reviews), the Slip-ins logo, and Klarna and Afterpay payment options.
- **Search returned a lot of results.** It found 1,649 for "comfort shoes," and the prices were easy to read.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page is blank.** I saw only white, with no items, no error, and no way back. I can't tell whether that was a bug or a slow load, but either way a shopper walks away.
- **"Add to Cart" didn't add anything.** I got a red "Please select a size" error and the bag still showed 0. The error sat below the sizes, so I'd probably miss it. No size was preselected. The message also doesn't say that the button did nothing.
- **Most sizes were crossed out on that shoe.** Only about 11, 12, 13 and 14 were available. I got that far with no hint that it was nearly sold out.
- **The page shouts about sales the shoe doesn't get.** The banner says "BOGO 50% OFF" and "25% OFF Work & Safety," but the product page says "Excluded from promotions." That feels like a bait-and-switch.
- **Nothing felt personal.** Steps 2 and 3 look exactly like the homepage. There was no "Hi, Walker," no popup dismissal that I could see, and no sign I was logged in. The homepage photo is of two young models in dress clothes. It's labeled "AI-generated models," and that does nothing for me.
- **The search put women's shoes first.** Cypress and Relaxed Fit were both labeled "Women's." A man's account and browsing history should have shaped that.
- **There was no comparison or price-versus-quality help.** I saw nothing that compares Skechers to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, or explains why one costs more. I also saw no arch-support or cushioning details on the product page. I only looked at the top of the page, though.
- **The product I landed on was a painted-pattern "Britto" shoe.** It's loud, and it isn't a plain walking shoe.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart page right away.** If it can't load, show a friendly message with a "Continue shopping" button.
2. **Make "Add to Cart" forgiving.** Preselect the first available size, or scroll to the error and highlight the size row. Show a clear "Added to cart" message and update the bag count.
3. **Show sold-out sizes before I commit.** Give a "Low stock" notice up top, or suggest similar shoes that come in my size.
4. **Keep the banner honest.** Don't show a sitewide BOGO banner above products marked "Excluded from promotions." At the least, show the exclusion at the top of the page.
5. **Show that I'm logged in.** Put my name or a visible logged-in state in the header, and use my history to order search results.
6. **Put comfort first on mobile.** Add arch support and cushioning badges, a width selector, and a "Compare comfort" tool on product pages and search results.
7. **Use real people my age on the homepage.** Show people actually using the product, in plain walking and slip-in settings.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right shoes for me, and the Men menu is clear. But a comfort-first guy on a phone hits a failed add-to-cart and a blank cart page, and that's where I'd stop and go buy from Hoka or New Balance. I'd also be annoyed by the sale banners on a shoe that's excluded from the sale. **3/10.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Axe reported 18 violations across 3 of the 8 steps. Homepage has 10 (2 critical, 1 serious), View Cart has 4 (2 serious) and Search has 4. The captured data doesn't say whether the other 5 steps were scanned.
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. This is a gap in the test harness, not a finding about the site.
- The Log In and Men category steps both report the homepage URL (`https://www.skechers.com/`), and their HTML samples are the same DOM as the homepage. TTFB for Log In and Men category is identical (451ms). These steps probably didn't trigger a real navigation, so their metrics and violations are probably not independent.
- `/cart/` returned HTTP 429. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) are consistent with an error or interstitial body rather than the real cart page, so treat them as unverified.
- The homepage loads about 20 third-party tags. There are 21 console errors, 10 of which were captured.

## Accessibility

Homepage:
- **Critical:** `button-name` – one or more buttons have no discernible text.
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` – ARIA roles are used without their required parent roles, for example `listitem` or `tab` outside `list` or `tablist`.
- **Serious:** `link-name` – links have no accessible name, probably icon-only links.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` – there are multiple `<main>` landmarks and at least one is nested. This points to templates or components each rendering their own `<main>`.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` – content sits outside landmarks, and landmarks are non-unique or nested.
- **Moderate:** `page-has-heading-one` – there is no `<h1>`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` – an invalid role is set on an element.

Search "comfort shoes":
- The same `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` violations appear. This suggests a shared header or footer component rather than page-specific bugs.

View Cart (likely the 429 response body):
- **Serious:** `document-title` and `html-has-lang` are missing.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` fail.
- Re-test once the cart renders normally.

## SEO

- The homepage has no `<h1>`. The homepage source does set `lang="en"`.
- The truncated HTML doesn't include `<title>`, meta description, canonical, structured data or viewport meta, so those couldn't be verified.
- The cart response had no `<title>` or `lang`. This only matters if error or interstitial responses are served on a crawlable URL. Cart pages should be `noindex` regardless.
- The Log In and Men category steps never left the homepage URL, so category-page SEO elements weren't audited.

## Performance

| Log In | 451ms | Same value as Men category; likely a reused navigation entry |
| Men category | 451ms | As above |

- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS are `?` on all 8 steps. The likely causes are a `PerformanceObserver` registered after load, no `buffered: true`, or metrics read before they were reported.
- **Suspect TTFB:** the 22–26ms values are almost certainly SPA or same-document transitions, bfcache or cache hits, or stale `navigation` entries. They are not valid server timings.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage head carries about 20 async third-party tags: GTM, gtag (3 IDs), TikTok, Bing, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Yahoo, Taboola, Impact, RTB House, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Pulse Insights, Tune and Bloomreach. They are async, but they compete for main-thread time and bandwidth. This is the most likely LCP and INP risk, though it isn't measured here.
- **Font loading:** fonts are loaded through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, with `wf-mulish-*-active` classes toggled by JS. This adds a third-party connection and ties text rendering to JS execution.
- **Viewport height:** `--vh` is set inline on `<html>` by JS (`6.64px`), a JS-dependent 100vh workaround that runs before first paint.

## Mobile Optimization

- Viewport meta and touch-target sizes were not in the captured data, so they couldn't be assessed.
- The JS-computed `--vh` workaround suggests the layout depends on viewport-height scripting.
- The Log In and Men category steps didn't reach distinct pages, so mobile navigation, menu and login flows weren't verified.

## Console & Network Errors

- **Network:** `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. This is rate limiting or bot mitigation on the cart route, and it affects the View Cart results. It may come from automated-traffic throttling and might not affect real users.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`:** 6 of the 10 captured messages. Some script registers an `unload` handler, which is blocked by Permissions-Policy and is being deprecated in Chrome. The source script wasn't identified.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified, and the request was aborted`:** 4 of the 10 captured messages. A script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API with unsupported languages. This is probably a third-party or injected script, and the source wasn't identified.
- Only 10 of the 21 reported console errors are listed. The others weren't captured.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the measurement first.** Register LCP and CLS observers before navigation with `buffered: true`, and read `navigation` entries only for hard navigations. Then re-run the audit.
2. **Make the Log In and Men category steps navigate** (or capture the right URL), so they produce distinct DOM and metrics.
3. **Handle the cart 429.** Check the WAF or bot rules, and add retry or backoff and a response-status check to the harness. Re-run axe once `/cart/` returns 200.
4. **Fix the critical axe issues on the homepage.** Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links (`aria-label` or visually hidden text). Correct ARIA parent and child role structure.
5. **Clean up landmarks.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, unnest `<aside>` and `<main>`, give duplicate landmarks distinct `aria-label`s, and put page content inside landmarks.
6. **Add one `<h1>` per page**, starting with the homepage.
7. **Find the scripts registering `unload` listeners** and replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`. Find the caller of the Summarizer API and fix or remove it.
8. **Audit third-party tags.** Consolidate through GTM, defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or consent, and measure their main-thread cost.
9. **Self-host or preload the web fonts** with `font-display: swap`, and drop the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency.
