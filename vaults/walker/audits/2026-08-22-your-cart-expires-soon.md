# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- That pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom, right there at the top again — "You liked these, and you weren't wrong." No, I didn't, and no, I wasn't. That's the same women's sneaker Skechers has shoved at me for the better part of a week now, and I've never so much as clicked on it. Below the fold there's actually a decent mixed grid — some men's slip-ons, a Max Cushioning Arch Fit that's genuinely in my wheelhouse — and a real 20% off Skechers Plus code that's worth something. But I have to scroll past a phantom cart to find it.
- If they'd led with the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 and the 20% off code, I'd have opened this and probably clicked through. Instead the hero is a flowery women's shoe I never touched, wrapped in a "you liked this" lie for the fourth time this week. I'm not forwarding this, and honestly I'm getting close to just unsubscribing — the fake browsing-history angle is wearing thin.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus sitewide code with a real end date (8/25) is a legitimate reason to click.
- The grid underneath actually has men's product in it — the Delson 3.0 and Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 caught my eye, that's cushioned comfort-first stuff I'd wear.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup up front tells me they know their shopper wants flexible, low-friction ways to buy.
- Big, high-contrast "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" and "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" buttons are easy to tap and easy to read without squinting.

## What's weak

- Same fabricated "you liked these" cart pitch with the same pink floral women's D'Lites I flagged yesterday and the day before — I never touched that shoe, and repeating the lie doesn't make it true.
- Hero image and headline are 100% off my demographic — florals, pastel pink, women's sizing — before I ever see anything for me.
- The one thing that would actually move me, the 20% off code, is buried two full screens down under a shoe I don't want.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button sits right under a product I never added to a cart — that's the kind of mismatch that makes me trust the email less, not more.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake abandoned-cart framing for shoppers who never actually browsed that item — if there's no real cart, don't pretend there is one. Lead instead with the 20% off Plus code, which is a real, honest offer.
- 2. Swap the hero product to something from my own browsing or purchase history — if Skechers has ever seen me look at Arch Fit or Max Cushioning styles, put that up top instead of a floral women's shoe.
- 3. Move the "SHOP ADDITIONAL STYLES" categories (Men, Sale) higher, or at least put a "Men" quick-link near the top so I'm not scrolling past merchandise that isn't mine.
- 4. Cut the repetition — I've gotten some version of this exact email three or four days running. Space these out or vary the hook so it doesn't read as spam.
- **Subject Alt A:** `SKECHERS Plus: 20% off sitewide, ends 8/25`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's Arch Fit comfort, 20% off for Plus members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SKXPLUS gets you 20% off sitewide — online only, thru 8/25.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New men's slip-ons and Arch Fit styles just landed.`
