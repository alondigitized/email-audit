# Friday Deals + $20 Off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Another Friday, another Shoe Carnival in the inbox — honestly, I've lost count at this point. Eight emails in eight or nine days, and they've been flying the same "Inflation Relief" flag since the middle of May. The good news is that buried in the middle of all that red-and-yellow clutter, there are two things that actually speak to me: the Brooks Ghost 17 at $30 off and the Skechers Slip-Ins at 25% off. Those two alone are worth the scroll. Brooks has been on my short list since that "Price Break" email last week on the 16th, and if that Ghost discount applies in-store, I'd probably drive over this weekend.
- The bad news is Shoe Carnival keeps wrapping their best offers in a grab-bag of deals I have zero interest in — BOGO on Women's & Kids Sandals is about as relevant to me as a coupon for lip gloss. And the preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL, which is just sloppy. Made me feel like I'd gotten a forward from someone's spam folder before I even opened it.
- I'd click the Brooks section. Wouldn't forward this to a friend — too busy and too repetitive to bother explaining.

## What's working

- **Brooks Ghost 17 at $30 off** — that's the most targeted thing in this whole email for a guy like me; Brooks is a real comfort brand and a specific shoe + specific savings is exactly how you get me off the fence.
- **Skechers Slip-Ins at 25% off** — slip-ons, hands-free, and a real brand I recognize; this hits my comfort checklist.
- **$20 off $99.98+ threshold** — twenty bucks back on a pair of shoes is meaningful; it's not fine-print-buried, it's the hero, and the math is honest.
- **Shoe Perks $10 reward callout** — I noticed it at the bottom; loyalty acknowledgment matters even if it's small.
- **"Friday Deals" urgency** — the day-of framing at least suggests this isn't a two-week promo dragging on indefinitely (even if the Inflation Relief banner undercuts that).

## What's weak

- **"Inflation Relief" for the fourth or fifth time** — I've seen this exact banner in the May 18, May 20, and May 15 emails; at this point the phrase doesn't feel like relief, it feels like wallpaper.
- **BOGO Women's & Kids Sandals leads the "Friday Deal Roundup"** — that section has zero relevance to a 62-year-old man; it's the first thing after the hero and it's not for me.
- **The preview text was a raw tracking URL** — that's an embarrassing inbox experience; I almost didn't open it thinking it was a phishing attempt.
- **Eight emails in eight days is too many** — cadence fatigue is real; I'm starting to skim rather than read.
- **"Limited Time" on the $20 off is vague** — no end date, and after weeks of Inflation Relief promos, I don't believe the urgency anymore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the Friday Deal Roundup with the Brooks or Skechers section** — put the men's comfort offers first, not Women's & Kids BOGO; I'd read deeper if the first deal spoke to me.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately** — a tracking URL in the preheader is a basic QA miss; it actively damages credibility at the inbox level before a single person opens.
- 3. **Retire "Inflation Relief" for this campaign cycle** — it's done its job; a fresh hook tied to the Friday angle would feel less stale.
- 4. **Give the $20 off a hard deadline** — "Friday only" or "expires tonight at midnight" is credible; "Limited Time" after a month of the same banner is not.
- 5. **Segment the email or at least reorder for gender** — if Shoe Carnival knows I'm a male subscriber, Men's deals shouldn't be buried below Women's & Kids.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Friday Deals + $20 Off" (2026-05-22)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Three tracking URLs contain a U+200B zero-width space (ZWS) character that breaks ASCII URL parsing and will silently fail link resolution or tracking attribution for affected recipients. All other infrastructure appears standard but two additional accessibility gaps exist.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space (U+200B) embedded in emltrk.com tracking URLs**

The QA scanner confirmed ASCII codec failures at character position 74–75 in three URLs:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260522_friday_deals_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260522_friday_deals_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?p&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260522_friday_deals_AM​
```

The ZWS sits immediately after `friday_deals_AM` (invisible in rendered output). These appear to be the open-tracking pixel (`?p`), redirect tracker (`?rd`), and real-link tracker (`?rl`). The corrupt character will:
- Cause link resolution failures in strict ASCII HTTP clients
- Break the `t=` UTM passthrough parameter, dropping campaign attribution
- Potentially render the open-tracking pixel non-functional

**Root cause:** Likely a copy-paste artifact from a rich-text editor or an encoding mismatch when the campaign name token was injected into the URL template.

**26 tracking links skipped** — emltrk.com redirect domains were not probed. No pass/fail verdict on destination URL health.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text on content image:**

```
src: https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/d06be110-6c0c-4df3-aeee-e04bf8e18969.jpg
```

No `alt` attribute present. When images are blocked (common in corporate clients), this renders as a broken image icon with no fallback context. Also fails WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.1.1 for screen readers.

**Tracking pixels also lack `alt=""`:**
- `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&...`
- `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (explicit empty string) to signal decorative intent to assistive technology. Omitting the attribute entirely is a minor but technically incorrect pattern.

**Non-functional IE compatibility meta tag:**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

This is missing the required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. The correct form is:

```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, Outlook rendering engines that parse this tag will ignore it.

**Dark mode not declared:**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```

Only `light` is declared. Apple Mail and the Samsung email client will not attempt dark-mode color inversion, but this is a deliberate exclusion, not a bug — flagged for awareness only.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The `[UNIQUE]` token visible in the open-tracking pixel URL (`0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`) appears to be an unfired merge token placeholder. If this was sent with the literal string `[UNIQUE]` rather than a resolved per-recipient identifier, open tracking is non-functional for this send.

No other unresolved merge tokens are visible in the truncated HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The `t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260522_friday_deals_AM` parameter in the emltrk tracker is the mechanism for forwarding UTM values to the destination URL. Because all three affected emltrk URLs contain the ZWS corruption, UTM forwarding is broken for the open pixel and at minimum one redirect path. Campaign performance in GA4/analytics will be under-attributed to this send — traffic may land as `(direct)` or `(none)`.

The 26 untested redirect links could not be validated for UTM parameter consistency on landing pages.
