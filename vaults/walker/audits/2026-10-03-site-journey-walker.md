# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-03**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front door of Skechers on my phone, and the menu and search worked. I never got a shoe into my cart. On the product page I hit a size chart and a red "Please select a size" warning, and the cart icon still read 0. The cart page then came up as a **blank white screen**. I can't tell if the page was still loading or broken, and either way I'd have walked away. The men's menu is organized well, and the "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles speak to what I shop for. But I never saw a logged-in state and I never saw the cart work.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday, so I see no improvement and no clear regression. The browsing is decent, but the cart is the one place a purchase happens and it showed me nothing.

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's menu drawer (Step 4):** Large, clear rows with big arrows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. It's easy to read and tap, and "Comfort Technologies" is what I'd go looking for.
- **Men's Shoes landing (Step 5):** The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles are near the top and match what I want. The heading is large and Filter and Sort are easy to find.
- **Quick-filter strip on the homepage (Step 1):** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" sit right under the logo. That's my vocabulary.
- **Search (Step 9):** "comfort shoes" returned 1,577 results. The first two were a Slip-ins Contour Foam "Cozy Fit Luxe Comfort" and a Relaxed Fit "Glide-Step Comfort." Both are relevant, and prices are shown as ranges with the Buy 1 Get 1 50% note.
- **Product page header (Step 6):** The name, star rating, the $70.00 price, and the BOGO note are all readable without zooming.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart (Step 8):** A completely white page, with no items, no message, and no way forward. A cart is the last thing I want to have to wonder about.
- **Add to Cart didn't visibly work (Step 7):** The screen was a size grid with "Please select a size." The cart badge stayed at 0. The screen doesn't tell me what happened or whether my tap registered. A first-time visitor might take that as the button failing.
- **Size grid:** Half the sizes are crossed out, including M 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, 7.5, 8.5, 9.5, 10.5, 14, and 15. A man reading those labels in small type can't easily tell which are available. The "M 9 / W 10.5" double labeling adds clutter.
- **Popups and login (Steps 2-3):** The screens look identical to the homepage. I saw no sign-in form and no logged-in greeting or name. The account icon is the only hint, so nothing felt personalized.
- **Homepage hero (Step 1):** The first screen is a racing shoe ("Aero Elite, Built to…") with a promo bar above it. That isn't my shoe. Comfort, arch support, and slip-ins only appear in a small scrolling strip, and "Max Cus…" is cut off.
- **Product pick (Step 6):** I came through Men > Shoes and landed on a *unisex* shower-style slide at $70. It's a stretch for what a 62-year-old walker is looking for. The visible screen showed nothing about arch support, cushioning, or how it compares to Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance.
- **Promo language:** "Applied at Cart" shows up on every screen, and the cart was blank. That makes the promise hard to trust.
- **No price-versus-quality math:** No comparison or "why this costs what it costs" content appeared anywhere in the journey.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make sure it always shows something: the items, or an empty-cart message with a "Keep shopping" button. Never a blank page.
2. **Show a clear add-to-cart result.** After a tap, show a confirmation or move to the cart, and update the badge count. If a size is missing, scroll to the size grid and highlight it. Put the error message next to the Add to Cart button.
3. **Make unavailable sizes obvious.** Gray them out with a plain "Sold out" label, and put the available sizes first.
4. **Lead with comfort on the homepage.** Put a slip-ins or arch-fit block above the performance running hero, and let the quick-filter chips wrap or be bigger so "Max Cushion" isn't cut off.
5. **Make logged-in mode visible.** After login, show "Hi, [name]," recently viewed shoes, and my saved size.
6. **Add comfort facts on product pages.** Show arch support, cushioning level, weight, and slip-in or hands-free status near the top, with a short "compared to other brands" line.
7. **Keep the promo wording consistent.** If the discount only applies in the cart, show the final price there with a clear line like "You save $X."

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the Skechers shoes and the men's menu is easy to use. But if the cart is blank and the add-to-cart button doesn't visibly work, I don't buy, and I'd go to the DSW or Famous Footwear sale aisle instead. It's the same 3/10 as yesterday. Fix the cart and the add-to-cart feedback, and this could score much higher.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audit is only partly reliable, for two reasons.

- **Bot mitigation interfered.** Kasada (`x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825`) returned HTTP 429 on its fingerprint endpoint five times. `/cart/` also returned 429 (the sixth network error).
- **Several steps seem not to have navigated.** The HTML samples for Homepage, Log In and Men category all report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, so those steps likely captured the same page. Several steps also show TTFB of 25–28ms (Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart, Search), which suggests an in-page or cached transition rather than a document load.
- **No LCP or CLS values were captured** on any step, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed.

The View Cart violations below were measured on the 429 response, not the real cart page.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations in total:
- **Critical:**
  - `button-name` on the Homepage: at least one button has no accessible name.
- **Serious:**
  - `link-name` on the Homepage: at least one link has no discernible text.
  - `document-title` on View Cart.
  - `html-has-lang` on View Cart.
- **Moderate (Homepage):**
  - `heading-order`.
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, so there are multiple or nested `<main>` elements.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`.
  - `landmark-unique`.
  - `region`.
- **Moderate (Search):**
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`.
  - `landmark-unique`.
  - `region`.
- **Moderate (View Cart):**
  - `landmark-one-main`.
  - `page-has-heading-one`.
- **Minor:**
  - `aria-allowed-role` on the Homepage and Search.

The four View Cart violations (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) are the pattern you'd expect from a bare 429 error document. They probably reflect the block page, not the cart template. The `lang="en"` attribute is present on the homepage HTML.

The duplicate or nested `<main>` and repeated landmarks on the Homepage and Search recur across steps. They look like real template defects.

## SEO

- **Homepage:** the sampled `<html>` has `lang="en"` and the head is truncated, so title, meta description, canonical and structured data can't be verified.
- **Cart:** the missing `<title>` and `<h1>` came from the 429 response. Cart pages are typically `noindex`, so the practical SEO impact is low.
- **Audit limitation:** the Log In and Men category samples show the homepage URL. Category-page SEO (headings, canonicals, pagination) was not evaluated.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** 439ms on the Homepage, 391ms on Log In and Men category, 108ms on View Cart, and 25–28ms elsewhere. The sub-30ms values point to cached or client-side transitions, not full server responses.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured on any step, so no Core Web Vitals verdict is possible.
- **Third-party load:** the head injects a large number of async third-party scripts. These include:
  - GTM
  - Monetate
  - Quantum Metric
  - TikTok, Pinterest, Reddit, Snapchat, Facebook, Taboola and Nextdoor pixels
  - Bing
  - Yahoo
  - Impact
  - Pulse Insights
  - Bloomreach
  - RTB House
  - Tune
  - Webfont Loader from cdnjs
- **Why that matters:** this stack contends for main-thread and network resources. It also holds fonts behind a JS loader (`wf-*-active` classes), which risks FOIT/FOUT and layout shift.
- **Not measured:** actual main-thread cost, since no LCP, CLS or TBT data was captured.
- **Mobile layout:** the inline `--vh` custom property (`6.64px`) is set by JS at load, which is a layout-shift risk.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** the `--vh` value of 6.64px implies a ~664px-tall viewport, consistent with a mobile emulation.
- **Not verified:** the viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and responsive images, because the head was truncated and axe reported no target-size rules.
- **Concern:** the unnamed button and link (`button-name`, `link-name`) on mobile likely come from icon-only controls such as menu, cart or search icons, which hurts screen-reader users on mobile.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.825`):** 5 occurrences, one repeated per step or retry. The anti-bot challenge was rate-limited or rejected the automated session.
- **429 on `/cart/`:** the cart document itself was blocked. The cart step can't be treated as a valid test of cart functionality.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document.`:** 4 occurrences. A script on the page, probably a third-party tag, registers an `unload` handler. Chrome is deprecating `unload` and it also blocks bfcache.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…`:** 2 occurrences. A script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API with unsupported language options. It is probably a third-party or injected script, so its origin should be confirmed.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the journey with bot mitigation accounted for.** Use an allowlisted IP, a header or token agreed with the site owner, or slower pacing. Confirm each step actually navigates, by logging the URL per step.
2. **Capture LCP and CLS.** Use `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true` and wait for `visibilitychange` or `load` plus a settle period.
3. **Fix the unnamed button and link on the Homepage.** Add `aria-label` or visible text.
4. **Fix the landmark structure.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, make complementary regions top-level, and give repeated navs distinct `aria-label`s.
5. **Fix `heading-order` and `aria-allowed-role`.** Correct the heading sequence and remove redundant or invalid roles.
6. **Re-test the cart on a successful 200 response.** Treat the View Cart violations as unconfirmed until then.
7. **Find the source of the `unload` handler.** Replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, or ask the vendor to. This restores bfcache eligibility.
8. **Audit the third-party tag load.** Defer non-essential pixels through GTM consent or idle-time loading, and self-host or `preload` the fonts.
