# Extra 20% off select styles ends soon
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Nike's back with what feels like the same email I saw five days ago. The May 21st send said "Score an extra 20% off select styles" — this one says "Extra 20% off select styles ends soon." Same number, same concept, just with a countdown drum beat. I get what they're doing, but when you're pushing near-identical promos within the same week, the urgency starts to feel manufactured.
- The big "Start Strong" hero copy reads like it was written for a college kid training for a 5K, not a 62-year-old guy who just wants something that won't kill his knees walking the neighborhood. Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or comfort anywhere I can see. The in-store 20% off block lower in the email at least gives me a reason to drive over before 5/31 — that part's useful. But the overall vibe is still "performance athlete," and that's not me.
- I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- The 20% offer is plainly stated up top with a code (START) — no digging around to figure out the deal.
- "Ends soon" in the subject plus the hard 5/31 in-store deadline both give me a real clock to act on.
- The product grid at least labels "Men's" — they acknowledged I exist as a male customer.
- Free shipping for members is right at the top of the email, easy to spot.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout chaos on what appears to be a mobile-friendly stack.

## What's weak

- "Start Strong" hero is pure athletic performance language — nothing about comfort, cushioning, or walking, which is what would actually get my attention.
- No slip-ons, no arch support, no walking shoes called out anywhere in the product grid. Those runners look like they're built for someone younger and faster than me.
- This is essentially the same offer I got May 21st. Five days later and we're back to the same 20% pitch — that's lazy cadence and it trains me to tune out the urgency.
- The email has two separate deals stuffed in — online code vs. in-store percentage — and they don't connect. It feels scattered, like two emails crammed into one.
- The fine print block at the bottom is a wall of small text. That much legal copy makes me suspicious there's a catch hiding in there.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with comfort, not performance.** Ditch "Start Strong" and write a hero that speaks to a guy who needs cushioning — something like "More cushion, 20% off." If Nike's tracking what I buy, they know I'm not grabbing track spikes.
- 2. **Stop recycling the same offer inside a week.** Either run one send with a longer window or make the second send a different offer entirely. Sending the same promo twice in five days trains subscribers to wait and ignore.
- 3. **Anchor the CTA to a specific shoe.** Put one walking or comfort shoe front and center with a direct product link — not just a "Men's" grid of athletic runners.
- 4. **Pick one deal and own it.** Online code *or* in-store discount — not both in the same scroll. Split them into separate sends or at least give one clear primary CTA.
- 5. **Compress the fine print.** Summarize exclusions in two plain-English lines and link to full terms. A text wall signals there's something to hide.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: extra 20% off, code START`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-store deal too — grab both before May 31`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free member shipping included. Shop before it ends.`

## Technical Audit — Nike "Extra 20% Off" Memorial Day Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is deployed via Adobe Experience Platform (AEP) with click-tracking through `click.em.nike.com`. QA returned a 23% pass rate driven by 17 failures: 16 doubled-domain pattern matches against 8 unique links, a missing plain-text MIME part, and one HTTP-served tracking pixel with no alt attribute.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Doubled-domain flags (16 failures, 8 unique URLs)**

The QA checker fires on every link because `nike.com` appears in both the ESP wrapper host (`click.em.nike.com`) and the URL-encoded destination path (`https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...`). Each URL is flagged twice under two separate check names ("Doubled-domain bug" and "Doubled-domain merge error"), inflating the failure count.

This is consistent with standard AEP click-wrapping syntax and is likely a false positive from the checker's regex. However, the links **must be manually probed** to confirm end-to-end resolution — if the redirect handler expects a bare path and instead receives a full encoded URL, the destination will 404.

Affected destinations (all share the same `click.em.nike.com/CL0/` wrapper):

### 5. Compliance

**Plain-text fallback: FAIL**
QA reports 0 characters in the text/plain MIME part. CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(3) requires that the opt-out mechanism be "clearly and conspicuously" presented — a missing text part means the unsubscribe link is unreachable for plain-text clients and may fail deliverability filtering at ISPs that check for a multipart/alternative structure.

**Authentication headers:** Not supplied in input data — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC cannot be assessed. From domain `nike.com` / sending domain `official.nike.com` alignment should be verified against published DNS records.

**Physical address and unsubscribe link:** Cannot confirm from truncated HTML. Both are required by CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) and §7704(a)(3).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 8 unique links carry Nike's internal `cp` parameter with a consistent structure:
```
cp=nans_em_camp_nike_OTHER_20260522_c102490_..._052626-na-e--dg-promo-memorialdayfinalhours_AEP-NA-US-MW-Email-MemorialDay-FinalHours-Large-0526_CNT~USA_en_M_NCP-...
```

- Campaign date code `052626` and creative name `memorialdayfinalhours` are consistent across all links — no mismatched creative labels detected.
- **No standard UTM parameters** (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present. Attribution in Google Analytics / GA4 depends entirely on the `cp` parameter being parsed server-side. Traffic from this send will appear as direct/unattributed in any third-party analytics tool that doesn't recognize Nike's internal `cp` scheme.
- The two "Nike.com" links use different `cp` values (`c16b915a...` vs the shorter footer variant), suggesting they are intentionally tagged as separate placement segments — this is correct behavior.
