# VIPS: *TWO* days left for 20% off
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- Same DSW playbook, third or fourth time this week — big bold "20% OFF" up top, then a wall of shoes I'd never put on my feet: clogs, wedge sneakers, ankle booties, strappy heels, a pointy leopard print sneaker. "Shop Men" is buried as a tiny link in that top black bar and then never mentioned again. Not one product below the fold is a men's shoe.
- I get that VIPs get 20% off, and the countdown urgency ("two days left") is at least honest and time-bound, but urgency doesn't matter when there's nothing to buy. This is a mark-as-read, maybe an eye-roll. Wouldn't forward it — my buddy Earl doesn't want to see wedge booties either.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" banner is big, blue, and impossible to miss on open.
- "Two days left" gives a real deadline, not vague hype.
- "Shop Men" exists as a link in the nav bar, so the category isn't totally absent, just totally ignored below it.
- Shop-by-size grid near the bottom is a nice touch if I actually had a product to size.

## What's weak

- Every single featured product — nine of them — is a women's shoe: clogs, ankle boots, heels, sandals, ballet flats, a leopard sneaker.
- "HEY, WHAT'S NEW? Oh, just every single one of these styles" leads straight into "SHOP WOMEN" with zero acknowledgment I might be a guy.
- Category tiles at the bottom (Athletic & Sneakers, Boots, Heels, Women's Shoes) are all still women's imagery — no men's tile at all.
- No cushioning, arch support, or comfort-fit language anywhere — nothing that speaks to how I actually pick a shoe.
- This is basically the same layout and offer as Monday's and Tuesday's VIP emails, just with a new deadline slapped on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment men's vs. women's browsing history and swap the entire product grid — if DSW knows I've looked at men's shoes before, don't show me nine women's items in a row.
- 2. Add a men's category tile alongside Athletic & Sneakers / Boots / Heels — right now men don't exist past the top nav.
- 3. Work in one line of comfort-relevant copy — "cushioned insoles," "all-day support" — somewhere near the hero, since that's what actually gets me to click, not the discount alone.
- 4. Drop or shrink the "SHOP WOMEN" mega-banner for male subscribers; a generic "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" would at least not feel like a wrong-gender miss.
- **Subject Alt A:** `VIP: 20% off men's comfort shoes, 2 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your VIP discount expires Friday — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off new markdowns in your size, before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned, comfortable, 20% off for two more days`
