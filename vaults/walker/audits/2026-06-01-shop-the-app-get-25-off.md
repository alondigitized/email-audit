# Shop the app ➡ Get 25% off!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- DSW pivots on me again. Last time it was a Pride email after weeks of BOGO sandal blasts — now it's a "Trend Report." I'll give them credit for trying something different. The numbered five-trend layout is visually organized and clean; the email doesn't look like a dumpster fire. But the moment I actually look at what's being pitched — Refined Rustic, Boot Cut Sandals, Short Things, Cool Running, High-Key Jelly — I feel like I wandered into my daughter's closet. Every model I can see in this email is a young woman in a fashion-forward outfit. None of this speaks to a 62-year-old guy looking for something comfortable to walk in.
- The bigger problem: the subject promised me 25% off, which is the only reason I opened this thing. But when the email loads, I'm hunting for that offer. The hero is a trend editorial, not a discount banner. I had to scroll to find the "SHOP NOW" button, and even then I'm not sure the 25% off is prominently attached to it. That bait-and-switch feeling is the fastest way to lose my trust — and DSW has been chipping at it all month with repetitive sandal emails.
- I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. Maybe if the 25% was front-and-center on men's comfort shoes, but this email isn't even trying to talk to me.

## What's working

- **Clean layout.** The numbered 1–5 trend sections give the email a clear structure. My eye moves top to bottom without confusion.
- **DSW brand recognition.** The logo reads instantly and I know this store — that alone got it opened.
- **Orange "SHOP NOW" button.** Stands out against the white background. No ambiguity about where to tap.
- **"Shop by Size" row.** That's an actual useful feature — find your size and go. Closest thing to something practical in the whole email.
- **No broken images or layout chaos.** Renders cleanly, which after years of janky retail emails, I notice and appreciate.

## What's weak

- **100% women's content.** Every model, every category — Jelly sandals, Refined Rustic, Boot Cut Sandals. None of it is for me. DSW has men's shoes. I know they do. So why am I getting this?
- **25% off is buried or invisible in the body.** The subject line hooked me with a real discount; I open the email and get a trend editorial. That feels like a bait-and-switch, even if it isn't intended as one.
- **"Trend Report" framing is completely wrong for me.** I don't care about trends. I care about whether my feet hurt at the end of the day.
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere.** No "today only," no "offer ends Sunday," nothing. The 25% just floats out there with no reason to act now.
- **Zero comfort language.** Not a single word about cushioning, support, arch, or fit. That's the entire reason I shop DSW for shoes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 25% off offer in the hero, not just the subject.** A prominent "25% OFF YOUR APP PURCHASE — TODAY ONLY" banner at the top of the email body would immediately validate why I opened it and give me a reason to act.
- 2. **Segment this send.** I should not be getting a women's trend report. DSW knows I'm a male subscriber — send me men's comfort walkers, casual sneakers, wide-width options. That's table stakes.
- 3. **Add one credible deadline.** "Offer ends June 3" removes the inertia that kills clicks for me.
- 4. **Lead with a comfort-forward men's product**, even one. A Skechers slip-on, a New Balance walking shoe — something I'd actually consider. The "Cool Running" category is the closest, but it disappears into the trend grid.
- 5. **Reframe the app push with a benefit I care about.** "App exclusive: 25% off + save your size for faster checkout" speaks to me more than a trend report.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's comfort shoes: 25% off in the app today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size, 25% off — DSW app only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New walkers, wide widths, and slip-ons — tap to save`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `App exclusive deal expires soon — open to claim`

## Technical Audit — DSW "Shop the app" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze, uses XHTML 1.0 Transitional with dark mode support, and loads custom web fonts from `braze-images.com`. Several invalid HTML/CSS attributes are present in the `<head>`, and one accessibility violation in the viewport meta is confirmed from the available source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit — HTML is truncated before anchor tags appear.** Verify the following before send:
- All CTAs carry consistent UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`)
- App deep-links use a fallback URL for users without the app installed
- No raw redirect chains that bypass the ESP's click-tracking pixel

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a. `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Removing `maximum-scale=1` costs nothing on render; it only prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom.

**b. Both `<meta>` tags missing their `http-equiv` attribute**
```html
<!-- as written — attribute will be ignored -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, neither declaration takes effect. The charset meta is the higher-risk omission — Outlook desktop parses it at MIME level so it's normally fine, but it can cause mojibake in some webmail renderers if the HTTP header doesn't also specify UTF-8.

**c. `Src:` (capitalized) in every `@font-face` block**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
`Src` is not a valid CSS property name — `src` is. Apple Mail accepts it; other parsers (Gmail, Outlook) will silently drop the declaration. Because those clients already ignore custom fonts, this is low impact, but it is a spec error in all eleven font declarations.

**d. `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` in the `*` reset**
```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The property is set twice in the same rule; `none` wins via cascade and disables Windows Phone text-size adjustment rather than normalizing it to `100%`. Likely a copy-paste artifact.

**e. Alt text — unverifiable**
Source is truncated before `<img>` tags. Confirm all images carry descriptive `alt` values; decorative images should use `alt=""`.
