# Open for the easiest $10 Off Ever 📱
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- Well, the first thing I noticed scrolling through this one is there isn't a single pair of men's shoes in the whole lineup. New Balance kicks for the wife, Adidas for the kids, a running shoe for the ladies, more kids sneakers — this is a straight-up Back to School blast and I don't have anybody in school anymore. That "$10 Off Ever" subject line got me to open it, I'll give it that, but once I'm in there's nothing here for a 62-year-old guy looking for something cushioned to slip on.
- Then there's this fake text-message screenshot in the middle of the email trying to get me to sign up for their SMS list too — "Want $10 off? ... we only text the good stuff!" I already let you into my inbox every single morning, now you want my phone number too? That felt like a lot.
- The kicker is at the very bottom, buried in the fine print under that big blue $20/$10 off banner: New Balance, Nike, Brooks, Crocs are all excluded from the coupon. Those are literally the brands I actually buy. So the one offer in this email that might've mattered to me doesn't even apply to me. Not clicking, not forwarding, deleting this one.

## What's working

- The "Lowest Price Guarantee" badge up top is a real trust cue — that's the kind of thing that makes me believe I'm not overpaying.
- The $20 off $99.98+ / $10 off $74.98+ banner is bold and easy to read at a glance, with a clear "Get Coupon" button.
- Physical barcodes right in the email mean I could just walk into the store and scan my phone — no printing, no fumbling with codes.
- Consistent Shoe Carnival branding throughout, so I know exactly who this is from.

## What's weak

- Zero men's shoes anywhere in the product grid — every item is kids' or women's. This is a family back-to-school email, not a "Walker" email.
- The coupon excludes New Balance, Nike, Brooks, and Crocs — the exact comfort brands I cross-shop — and that only shows up in tiny print at the very bottom.
- The fake text-thread graphic pushing SMS sign-up felt like a bait-and-switch inside my own email; I opened for a deal, not another channel to subscribe to.
- The preview text in my inbox was just a raw tracking link, not real copy — looks broken before I even open it.
- This is roughly my fifth or sixth Shoe Carnival email in a row I can remember reviewing — after the July 24th "A+ Deals Roundup" repeat, the daily grind is starting to feel less like a deal and more like noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put at least one men's comfort shoe in the grid with this offer, or don't send this version to guys my age at all — segment by past purchase category.
- 2. Stop excluding the major comfort brands from the coupon, or if you must, say so up top ("excludes select brands") instead of burying it in six-point fine print at the bottom.
- 3. Cut the fake text-message SMS opt-in graphic — it eats space that could've shown me a shoe I'd actually consider.
- 4. Fix the preview text so it's real, relevant copy instead of a leaking tracking URL.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's Comfort Picks: $10 Off $74.98+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your walking shoes just got $10 cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Skechers & Hoka comfort styles — plus your $10 code inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned, slip-on, arch support — shop it before the code expires`

# Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "$10 Off" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard multi-client HTML4.01 Transitional email template with the usual Outlook/Hotmail/Gmail CSS resets; QA scan found 8 images with missing `alt` attributes and 31 tracking links that could not be probed (redirect domains). No broken links, malformed tables, or missing unsubscribe mechanics were confirmed from the available data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 31 tracking/click-redirect links were **not verified** — QA explicitly skipped HTTP probing for these domains (`[WARN] 31 tracking link(s) skipped: Tracking/click-redirect domains`). This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed failure; link destinations remain unverified.
- Two tracking-pixel beacons are present as `<img>` tags rather than link-based tracking:
  - `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260803_sms_AM` — note the query param `t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260803_**sms**_AM` tags this as an SMS campaign inside an **email** beacon. Worth confirming this isn't a copy-paste artifact from the SMS variant of this campaign, since it will misattribute engagement analytics if sent as-is.
  - `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` — see Section 4, unresolved merge token.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` is missing the `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute, so this tag has no effect in Outlook/Word-rendering engines — the compatibility directive is silently inert.
- `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` — the `content="text/html;"` attribute is orphaned (no `http-equiv`), redundant with the separate `charset="utf-8"` attribute; harmless but dead markup.
- 8 images missing `alt` text (QA-confirmed):
  - 6 product images from `i8.amplience.net` (`.../144406_308584_SET`, `.../157154_342182_SET`, `.../187166_418055_SET`, `.../178735_393540_SET`, `.../152229_355028_SET`, `.../162278_410117_SET`)
  - 2 content images from `image.email.shoecarnival.com` (`9521013e-...jpg`, `a97db5d3-...jpg`)
  - This affects screen-reader users and any client with images disabled by default. The 2 `emltrk.com` tracking-pixel entries also flagged for missing alt are non-content beacons — lower priority, but should carry `alt=""` for spec compliance rather than no attribute at all.
- `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes` are both pinned to `light` — intentional dark-mode suppression, not a defect.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- One image src contains an unresolved literal token: `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`. If `[UNIQUE]` is meant to be substituted server-side per recipient (e.g., a subscriber ID for open tracking), confirm this substitution actually runs before send — if this literal bracket syntax reaches inboxes, per-recipient open tracking on this beacon will be broken for all recipients (identical/non-unique key).
- No other merge fields (name, loyalty ID, dynamic content blocks) are visible in the HTML provided; truncation limits full verification of the body content.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- HTML was truncated before any footer/unsubscribe block, physical mailing address, or preference-center link — **cannot confirm or deny** CAN-SPAM footer compliance from the data provided.
- No SMTP/authentication header data (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) was included in this audit input — not assessable here.
- Sender uses a dedicated subdomain (`email.shoecarnival.com`), consistent with standard ESP domain-reputation isolation practice — no issue.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot fully assess: the 31 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the QA probe (Section 2), so destination UTM parameters and landing-page alignment are unverified.
- The one visible anomaly is the `sms`-tagged UTM value inside an email tracking pixel noted in Section 2 — flag for campaign-tagging QA before send.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 6 `amplience.net` product images and 2 `image.email.shoecarnival.com` content images; add `alt=""` to the 2 `emltrk.com` tracking pixels.
2. Fix the malformed `<meta content="IE=edge" />` tag by adding `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` (or remove it if not needed for current Outlook targets).
3. Confirm the `[UNIQUE]` token in the `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` beacon resolves to a real per-recipient value before send — verify in a rendered/test-send copy, not just the template source.
4. Verify the `utm_campaign=SCA_20260803_sms_AM` value on the `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` beacon is intentional for this email send, not an SMS-template leftover.
5. Re-run link QA with redirect-domain probing enabled (or manually spot-check a sample of the 31 skipped tracking links) to confirm destination URLs and UTM-to-landing-page alignment before send.
6. Request the full (untruncated) HTML to verify CAN-SPAM footer elements (unsubscribe link, physical address) — not assessable from the truncated source provided.
