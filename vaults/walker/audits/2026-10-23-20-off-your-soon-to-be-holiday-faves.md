# 20% off your (soon-to-be) holiday faves
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and it's the same 20% off with code JINGLE I've seen all week. The green "20% OFF*" banner is big and easy on my eyes. After that, the email is women's shoes from top to bottom: flats, heels, a fuzzy boot, and sandals. I scrolled the whole thing and didn't find one pair of men's shoes. "Shop Men" is in the top banner and the nav, but nothing below it is for me.
- The offer doesn't work harder than the earlier sends did. It's the fifth or sixth 20% email since the 21st, and the Shoebox Shuffle game is a distraction. I'm not playing a game to win bonus points on shoes I'll pick by fit. If I'd been shopping for my wife, this would work. For my own feet, I'd glance at the banner and archive it.
- I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. If a men's walking or slip-on section had been up top, I might have.

## What's working

- The giant "20% OFF*" on green is readable on my phone without zooming.
- The code JINGLE is printed right under the offer, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The "Shop by Size" block has wide width and wide calf buttons. Wide widths matter to a lot of guys my age.
- The "Shopping Made Easy" icons for free shipping, in-store pickup and text offers are clear and simple.
- The buttons are large, high-contrast black on white, and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- The product grid is entirely women's. There's nothing for a 62-year-old man.
- There's no comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on messaging anywhere, and that's what I shop for.
- The "Extra 20% off reg. price!" tags are tiny red text. I can barely read them on a phone.
- The fine print is a wall of exclusions, and the brands I cross-shop (Skechers, Clarks) appear in the excluded list. That feels like a bait-and-switch.
- The Shoebox Shuffle game takes up a big purple block and isn't a reason to buy. It's also the fourth or fifth near-identical 20% send this week, so it feels like noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender and category. Send men like me a top row of men's comfort and walking shoes, and drop the women's flats.
- 2. Put a "Comfort Picks" row with cushioned and slip-on styles right under the banner.
- 3. Name the exclusions next to the offer, or at least say which brands are in. Don't bury them in the fine print.
- 4. Cut the cadence. One strong 20% email beats five that look the same.
- 5. Make the "Extra 20% off" tags bigger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off men's comfort shoes, code JINGLE`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cushioned walkers, 20% off through 10/25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Slip-ons, walkers and wide widths. Use code JINGLE through 10/25.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wide widths in stock. Take 20% off before Sunday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders from a Braze/SendGrid-style template with click tracking on `ablink.dsw.com`. One high-priority CTA ("Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off") returned HTTP 400 when probed. The HTML source was truncated, so several checks (unsubscribe, UTMs, body-level merge tokens) can't be confirmed either way.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (confirmed by probe):** The link with text "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" returned **400**. The URL is `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/ufBofT3xQIKETyF7t6RrmQ~~/AAQRxRA~/6Kq1Cf1q2e-…`. The failure appears in the QA output with an empty label before the link text, which suggests the anchor's href or title is blank or odd.
  - A 400 from a tracking-redirect host can be a real malformed or expired token. It can also be the redirector rejecting the automated probe's request. Re-test in a real client and with a browser user agent before treating it as a send-blocking defect.
- **Long tracked URLs:** The plain-text part is 27,137 of 31,870 characters of URLs (85%), which is why the WARN fired. Each wrapped link is several hundred characters. This inflates the text part and makes it hard to read.
- **Coverage gap:** Only 12 of 69 links were probed and 57 were skipped. The other links are unverified, not known-good.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Ineffective meta tags (HTML source):**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it doesn't declare the charset.
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` also has no `http-equiv`.
  - Both should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" … />` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" … />`. Clients that sniff the encoding could mis-render special characters (e.g., ™, ®, curly quotes).
- **Missing alt text (2 images, QA-confirmed):**
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb024fdcb2cc855b9ed672710d4/o.gif?…` (`o.gif`)
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/Edhl0lFvBzV7A4YDnT8Jag~~/AAQRxRA~/fnCLijGG…`
  - Both look like open-tracking pixels. Impact is low, but they should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Two tracking pixels:** The `ablink.dsw.com/q/…` and `mi.dsw.com/…/o.gif` pixels both track opens, so there is redundant tracking and an extra third-party request. Whether that's intentional is unconfirmed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. The visible `mi_u=225090073` is a resolved numeric value, and no unrendered `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`-style tokens appear in the truncated HTML. Body content past the truncation point is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** The `Authentication-Results` header was not found. The QA tool expects it via the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown rather than failing. This is a tooling gap, not a confirmed defect. Check the raw headers.
- **Unsubscribe:** The QA tool always probes the unsubscribe link, and its only link failure is the SMS opt-in CTA. That suggests the unsubscribe link didn't fail, but the link itself isn't visible in the truncated source, so it isn't confirmed. The same applies to the physical postal address and any `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers.
- **Sender:** `dsw@e.dsw.com` is on a dedicated subdomain, consistent with proper sender-domain segregation. Alignment isn't verifiable here.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All probed links are wrapped in `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, so the destination URLs and any UTM parameters are not visible in the supplied source or QA data. UTM presence and landing-page alignment can't be assessed.
- The campaign identifier `20261023_P251285_V1_WM-_Offer_MI_2` appears only in the `mi.dsw.com` pixel. If it isn't also carried on destination URLs, attribution is split across systems.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the "Opt-In to Texts" link manually. If it is truly 400, fix or regenerate the tracking token or SMS opt-in destination before send. This is the only confirmed functional defect.
2. Change the meta tags to `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels, and consider dropping one of them.
4. Shorten or reduce the link count in the plain-text part, or add a hand-authored text version.
5. Inspect the raw headers for `Authentication-Results`, `List-Unsubscribe`, and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`. Also confirm the unsubscribe link and postal address in the full HTML.
6. Raise the probing depth, or resolve the redirects, so destination URLs and UTMs can be audited. The 62% pass rate reflects mostly skipped checks, not 38% broken content.
