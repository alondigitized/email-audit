# The BEST Black Friday Deals are waiting for you ‼️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again, and now it's Thanksgiving morning. I've had Black Friday email from them since the 13th, and this one hasn't changed much. The "HAPPY THANKSGIVING!" banner and "STORES CLOSED TODAY, SHOP ONLINE!" are a nice human touch, and I can read them without my glasses. After that it's a long scroll of gold and red "DOORBUSTER" tiles, mostly women's and kids' boots and cozy slippers.
- For a 62-year-old guy who wants cushioned, arch-supported, slip-on comfort, there's very little here. The Skechers tile at $39.98 is the closest thing, and it's buried halfway down with no shoe pictured. The Nike 30% off tile is fine, but it's a logo with no shoe. The "Extra 25% off, code GIVETHANKS" near the bottom is the best offer in the whole email, and I'd only find it by scrolling.
- Would I click? Maybe on Skechers, but only if I was already shopping. I wouldn't forward it. I'd leave it unread, same as the last few.

## What's working

- The "Happy Thanksgiving" header and "stores closed today, shop online" are timely and honest about the day.
- The big white price numbers like $39.98 and 30% off are easy to read on my phone.
- The sender is a store I know, and the layout matches the other Black Friday emails, so I trust it.
- The SHOE P·E·R·K·S strip at the top shows member rewards without crowding the page.
- The bottom nav has a MENS link I can tap.

## What's weak

- Nearly all the hero images are women's and kids' boots and slippers. There's no men's walking, slip-on or cushioned shoe I can see.
- The Skechers $39.98 tile has no shoe in it. I can't tell if it's the hands-free slip-ons or something else.
- The best deal, 25% off with code GIVETHANKS, sits near the bottom after about ten tiles. I'd lose interest before getting there.
- There are too many tiles and prices ($14.98, $34.98, $39.98, 30%, 40%, 60%). I can't tell which deal is the real one.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny and I can't read it without zooming. It's a long list of exclusions, and that makes me trust the "25% off everything else!" claim less.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the 25% off code and one clear "Shop now" button at the very top, right under the Thanksgiving line. One offer is easier than a dozen.
- 2. Send men's and women's versions, or at least show one men's tile above the fold. Show a real comfort shoe, like a slip-on or walking shoe, with the name and "cushioned" or "arch support" in the copy.
- 3. Put a shoe photo in the Skechers tile and name the style. Add something like "Slip-ins, hands-free, $39.98."
- 4. Cut the tile count by half. Stop sending nearly the same email every day.
- 5. Make the fine print bigger or link it.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a standard table-based, responsive ESP template with working viewport/media-query scaffolding. The source is truncated and only 1 automated warning was raised, so unsubscribe, footer, headers and landing-link checks can't be confirmed either way. The one concrete defect is an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in a tracking-pixel URL.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved placeholder:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` contains a literal `[UNIQUE]` token. It looks like a merge/uniqueness variable the ESP failed to substitute, which likely breaks per-recipient open attribution or dedupe for that pixel. It may be a harmless ESP default, so confirm in the ESP.
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261126_thanksgiving_MIDDAY_RESEND​` has two problems:
  - It nests `utm_campaign=` inside the `t=` value, which is a non-standard query structure.
  - It ends with a trailing zero-width space (U+200B) after `RESEND`. That character can corrupt campaign-name matching or get percent-encoded as `%E2%80%8B` in reporting.
- **Campaign name mismatch:** The campaign string reads `thanksgiving_MIDDAY_RESEND`, but the subject is "The BEST Black Friday Deals…". This may be intentional, but it will skew campaign reporting.
- **Unverified links:** 32 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe (emltrk redirect domains). Their destinations and status codes are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** Two images have none, `ieezzy5m` and `0di86k1v`. Both are `emltrk.com` tracking pixels. Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip them. The impact is low.
- **Links not visually distinguishable:** The global rule `a, a:link, a:visited… { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` strips underline and color from all links. Links are then indistinguishable from body text unless individually restyled (WCAG 1.4.1 risk). I can't confirm per-link overrides in the truncated source.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text { font-size: 12px !important; }` at `max-width: 600px`. This is small for body copy on mobile.
- **Disabled tel links:** `a[href^="tel"], a[href^="sms"] { pointer-events:none }` makes tel/sms links non-tappable on mobile.
- **Malformed meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` lack `http-equiv`. Both are harmless, since `charset="utf-8"` is still honored.
- **Doctype/namespace mismatch:** The HTML 4.01 doctype is paired with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`. It is non-blocking.
- **Positives:** `lang="en"` is set and the viewport meta is present.
