# Rewards members save an extra 20%
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-30**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear is dangling a 20% member discount, which sounds good on paper. But when I actually opened this thing, it felt like somebody sent me the women's shoe catalog with a sticky note slapped on top saying "you save 20%." I scrolled past sandals for women, sneakers I don't recognize, and product tiles that are too small for me to make out on my phone. The offer is real and the headline is clear enough — but the email doesn't treat me like a man who buys comfort shoes. It treats me like a generic rewards inbox. I'm not annoyed enough to unsubscribe, but I'm not rushing to click either.
- The discount is there but the targeting isn't. Most of what's in this email doesn't speak to me, and what does is buried.

## What's working

- **The 20% headline is immediately readable.** Big, bold, right at the top. I knew the offer in two seconds. That's all I need to decide whether to keep scrolling.
- **"Starting Now" urgency** — that little label above the discount creates just enough of a nudge that I don't feel like I can come back to it tomorrow and still get the deal.
- **Rewards member framing** — being called out as a member feels like I earned this, not like I'm being spammed. That matters to me. I've been shopping at Famous Footwear for years.

## What's weak

- **Women's sandals section is front and center.** I see a big "40% off women's sandals" module taking up a large chunk of the email. I'm a 62-year-old man. Why am I looking at women's sandals? That's real estate wasted on me.
- **Product tiles are tiny.** The shoe thumbnails in the grid are small on a phone screen. I'd have to zoom in to see what I'm looking at, and at my age I'm not doing that. If I can't see it clearly, I scroll past it.
- **No men's comfort or slip-in callout.** The whole reason I shop there is comfort shoes — walking shoes, slip-ins, that sort of thing. Nothing in this email said "hey Walker, here's something for your feet." It felt like a department store flyer.
- **The reward points section near the bottom** — there's a "2X" or points multiplier module buried down there. That's actually something I'd care about as a Plus member, but by the time I got there I'd already mentally checked out.
- **The preview text is a tracking URL.** That's just ugly. When I glanced at my inbox I saw a jumbled link instead of any actual message. Almost made me think it was spam.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a men's comfort pick.** Swap the hero or first product module to show one or two men's walking/slip-in shoes. Even a simple "Great for guys like you" module would make me feel seen.
- 2. **Move the women's sandals module below the fold** or skip it for male member segments entirely.
- 3. **Make product tiles larger.** Especially for mobile. I should be able to tap the shoe I want without squinting.
- 4. **Surface the points bonus earlier.** Put the 2X rewards callout right under the 20% headline — that's the double reason to buy, and it's currently hidden.
- 5. **Fix the preview text.** Any real sentence is better than a tracking URL. Something like "Your member discount is live — shop men's comfort now" would've gotten me to open faster.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Rewards members save an extra 20%"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally functional but carries two confirmed mixed-content vulnerabilities (HTTP image sources) and pervasive missing alt text across ~30 images. Deliverability is at risk from a URL-heavy plain-text part.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**28 tracking links unprobed** — all CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click redirect). QA skipped HTTP probing on all of them; broken destination URLs cannot be ruled out without a live check.

**Open pixel** uses a separate domain/path pattern:
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?4H3F2FYZCFTENCGOFZT53HS3EE.70253&d=70253&bmt=0
```
The `bmt=0` parameter is present but the `alt=""` attribute is correctly set to empty — no issue beyond confirming the pixel fires.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 2 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources** will be blocked by most modern email clients that enforce mixed-content policies (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook.com):

```
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NjJ9AAcAAAAAAL41VqgTmZZt6HIV...
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NjJ9AAcAAAAAAL41VqgVx0PDuvvBV...
```
These appear to be LiveContent (dynamic/personalized) blocks served over HTTP. Recipients will see broken images or a fallback block. Fix: migrate LiveContent endpoint to HTTPS or replace with static fallback.

**~30 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. Affected images include the logo (`3b6a7dc1`), all product recommendation images from `igodigital.com` (8 images), and most editorial/promotional images. With images disabled (common in B2B/Outlook environments), the email renders as a blank canvas. At minimum, the logo, hero, and product tiles need descriptive alt text.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is malformed** — the `http-equiv` attribute is missing:
```html
<!-- incorrect -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
<!-- correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Most clients tolerate this but it is technically invalid and may affect charset detection in strict parsers.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — an accessibility regression flagged by WCAG 1.4.4.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g. `%%FIRST_NAME%%` or `{{field}}` literals) visible in the truncated source. Product recommendation slots are populated via iGoDigital (`7222883.recs.igodigital.com`), which resolves at send time — no static fallback images are present for the 8 recommendation slots. If the recs engine returns no results, those `<img>` tags will resolve to broken images.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Plain-text part is URL-saturated** — QA flags 9,457 of 12,970 characters (73%) as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_COUNT`, Barracuda) penalize this pattern. A human-readable plain-text version with URLs moved to line-end or stripped to one canonical URL per section is required.

No unsubscribe link, physical mailing address, or CAN-SPAM footer is visible in the truncated HTML. These are mandatory; cannot confirm presence or absence from the truncated source — must be verified against the full render.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 28 CTAs pass through the SFMC click-redirect domain (`click.email.famousfootwear.com`). Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to the final destination URL cannot be confirmed without probing the redirect chain — the QA run skipped all tracking links. This is a gap in coverage that should be closed with a live link-check pass.
