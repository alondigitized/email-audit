# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Journey Review
### Date: April 14, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 3/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Another frustrating trip. The Men's navigation actually impressed me this time — they've got "Hands Free Slip-ins" right there as a quick link in the menu, which is exactly what I'm looking for. And the product page looked sharp. But then the wheels came off completely: the cart page was a **blank white screen**. Nothing. Just white. That's not a minor glitch, that's a wall between me and my wallet. Search still shoves Women's shoes in my face first. And the site keeps acting like I'm not a Plus member even though I logged in. Two steps forward, one giant step into a void.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **4/10**

Up one point from yesterday. The Men's navigation and product presentation are genuinely better. But a blank cart page is a conversion killer — no amount of good product pages matter if the checkout path doesn't load.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's Category Navigation (Step 4)** — Clean, readable list. Large enough text. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there as a quick-tap shortcut at the bottom, alongside Wide Fit and Extra Wide Fit. I didn't have to dig around. That's the best screen of the entire journey.

**Hands Free Slip-ins Category Page (Step 5)** — Landed exactly where I wanted. Clear heading, 262 results, filter buttons for Wide/Extra Wide/Medium fit. Good explanation of the Heel Pillow technology. Relevant and reassuring.

**Product Page (Step 6)** — Solid. Max Cushioning Glide-Step, $115, five stars, 65 reviews, BOGO 50% deal clearly called out, the Hands-Free Slip-Ins badge right on the page. Big images, left/right swipe for more views. I felt good about this shoe.

**BOGO 50% Deal Visibility** — Consistently shown top-of-screen across every page. I noticed it. As a price-conscious guy, this matters.

**ADD TO CART Button (Step 7)** — Big, blue, full-width. Easy to tap. No complaints there.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart Page is a Blank White Screen (Step 8)** — This is the biggest problem on the entire site. After going through the whole journey, finding my shoe, selecting my size — I get a completely empty white page. No items, no totals, no checkout button. Nothing. If I were a real customer, I would have called my daughter to complain and then gone to Zappos. This is a critical failure.

**Login Had No Confirmation (Step 3)** — The login step looks identical to the homepage. No "Welcome back, Walker." No name, no account recognition. The cart even shows "0" in the badge. If I logged in, the site didn't bother to tell me.

**Skechers Plus Upsell Showing to an Existing Member (Steps 7, 2)** — Step 7 still shows "Join for Free Shipping, Free Returns" even though I'm already a Plus member. Step 2 shows "Free Shipping with Skechers Plus — Join Now for free." I already joined. Stop asking me to join. This is a personalization failure that erodes trust.

**Homepage Is All Mother's Day, Zero Men's Content (Steps 1–3)** — The hero image is a mother and daughter in pink shoes. The sub-banner says "Get Mom a Gift She'll Love!" There's nothing for me on the front page. A 62-year-old man who just logged into his account sees zero content relevant to him. No "Welcome back," no men's picks, no comfort tech call-out.

**Search Returns Women's Results First (Step 9)** — I searched "slip in." Got 1,012 results. The first two products I see are both Women's — Arch Fit Arcade and Contour Foam Cozy Fit, both labeled "Women's." I'm logged in. The site knows I'm a man. Why is it showing me women's shoes first?

**Size Grid Has a Gap (Step 7)** — The size options shown jump from 8.0 straight to 10.5. Sizes 9.0, 9.5, and 10.0 are missing from view. Could be a scroll/cut-off issue, but it's confusing at first glance and could cause someone to think their size isn't available.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank white cart is a zero-revenue screen. This is the top priority. Investigate whether it's a login session issue or a rendering failure.

2. **Suppress the Plus upsell for active Plus members.** If I'm logged into a Plus account, replace "Join Now" with "You have free shipping" or a member benefit reminder. This is a quick personalization win.

3. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in users.** Even a simple "Welcome back, Walker — check out Men's Slip-ins" banner would dramatically improve the experience. The Mother's Day hero is fine for non-logged-in visitors, but logged-in members deserve relevant content.

4. **Filter search results by the logged-in user's gender preference.** When a male customer searches "slip in," men's styles should appear first. This is basic merchandising.

5. **Make the login confirmation visible.** Show the user's name or a "Signed in" state after login. The current experience leaves me wondering if it worked.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are getting better. I can navigate to my Slip-ins section without too much trouble, the product page is solid, and the deal is clearly communicated. But the cart doesn't load. That's not an inconvenience — that's a broken checkout. Until that's fixed, none of the other improvements matter for actual sales. And the site still doesn't know who I am even after I log in, which makes the whole "Plus member" value proposition feel hollow. Score: **4/10** — one point better than yesterday, but the ceiling is low as long as the cart is broken.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). Bot-protection layer (Kasada/kpsdk) is generating repeated 429s that propagate into both the console and the cart endpoint, causing a measurable TTFB spike at checkout. A third-party style widget (Stylitics) has a broken CORS configuration, silently disabling that feature. The page carries 20+ async third-party scripts per load with at least two duplicate script injections observed. LCP and CLS were not captured, leaving Web Vitals coverage incomplete.

## Mobile Optimization

- Custom CSS property `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set inline on `<html>` — this is a JS-driven fix for the `100vh` mobile browser-chrome problem. The floating-point precision artifact (`6.640000000000001` vs `6.64`) suggests the calculation `window.innerHeight / 100` is being stored without rounding. While functionally minor, it produces unnecessary DOM attribute churn on every resize/scroll event.
- `vice-managed-src` attribute pattern is used as a consent-gate mechanism for several scripts (Pinterest, Facebook, Yahoo, Nextdoor, Taboola, Impact). This is a non-standard attribute and depends on a first-party consent orchestration layer to swap in the real `src` — if that layer fails or is blocked, all gated scripts silently do not load with no fallback.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting (5 hits):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
```
The Kasada SDK is polling its own fingerprinting endpoint and being rate-limited. This is an internal loop — the bot-protection service is throttling its own client-side probe. Every 429 here means the SDK retries, adding network overhead and console noise.

**429 — Cart endpoint:**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself is receiving a 429. If this is also Kasada-gated, a failed fingerprint challenge is blocking cart rendering — directly causing the 168ms TTFB regression and potentially blocking add-to-cart completion for affected sessions.

**CORS — Stylitics widget (2 errors):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
Access to XMLHttpRequest at same URL blocked by CORS policy
```
The Stylitics hotspot/theme widget is making a cross-origin CSS fetch that `stylitics.com` does not permit from `skechers.com`. Both the stylesheet load and the XHR fallback fail. The widget renders without its styles.

## Recommendations

1. **Kasada 429s (critical):** Coordinate with the Kasada integration team — the `/fp` endpoint is returning 429 to legitimate browser sessions, which cascades to the cart endpoint. Investigate whether the kpsdk version (`j-1.2.308`) has a known polling-frequency bug or whether a rate-limit configuration on the Skechers origin is misconfigured.

2. **Cart 429 (critical):** Isolate whether `GET /cart/` is behind the same Kasada challenge. If a failed challenge blocks the cart response, affected users cannot complete checkout. Add session-level monitoring for 429 rates on `/cart/`.

3. **View Product accessibility — 4 critical violations:** Audit the product page component for: (a) icon buttons missing `aria-label`, (b) `<img>` tags missing `alt`, (c) broken ARIA widget structure. These are WCAG 2.1 Level A failures with legal exposure under ADA/Section 508.

4. **Stylitics CORS:** Either (a) have Stylitics add `skechers.com` to their `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` allowlist, or (b) proxy the stylesheet request through the Skechers origin. The current failure silently breaks the widget.

5. **Remove/fix placeholder GTM ID `G-1234567890`:** Audit GTM container configuration. This ID should not appear in production HTML.

6. **Deduplicate third-party scripts:** Remove the duplicate `creativecdn.com` and Yahoo `ytc.js` injections. Audit the tag manager configuration for the duplication root cause.

7. **Round `--vh` value:** Apply `Math.round(window.innerHeight / 100 * 10000) / 10000` or equivalent to prevent floating-point noise in DOM attribute updates.

8. **Capture LCP and CLS:** The Web Vitals instrumentation is not returning LCP or CLS for any step. These are Google ranking signals and the primary UX health indicators — their absence means the current monitoring is incomplete.
