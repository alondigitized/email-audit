# Long days. Longer runs.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — "Long days. Longer runs." is a tight two-liner, and the rhythm of it caught me for a second. Then I opened it and saw "Chasing Sunshine" over what looks like a pink-salmon trainer that was clearly designed for someone twenty years younger and without my foot problems. HOKA had me pretty well dialed in last week with that "Stability without the bulk" send — that subject felt like it was written for me specifically. This one reads like it was written for a 35-year-old who tracks mileage on Strava.
- The Bondi 9 is in here, and I've had my eye on that shoe because the cushioning reputation is real. But nothing in this email tells me that. It's just named, shown in a grid with five other road shoes, and left to fend for itself. No mention of what makes it worth $200. The whole email is pitched at people stacking summer miles — I'm stacking grocery runs and a round of golf. Those aren't the same thing.
- I won't click. I'll scroll the grid for maybe ten seconds, recognize the Bondi 9, and close it. I'd definitely not forward this — nothing in here would help a friend make a buying decision. Mark as read and wait for the next one.

## What's working

- **HOKA brand trust is already established.** After the June 17 stability email, I'm primed to open from them — they've earned some goodwill in my inbox.
- **The "Turn Up the Volume" grid labels both Men's and Women's** for each model — at least they're showing me the men's version of the Bondi 9 and Arahi 8, not just the women's.
- **The socks banner is visible immediately** — spending threshold is clear ($200+), the reward is specific (3-pack), no guessing required.
- **Clean layout, no render issues.** Reads fine on a phone screen, text doesn't overlap, images loaded.
- **"Shop Road Styles" button is obvious.** Doesn't say "Discover" or "Explore" — it tells me exactly where I'm going.

## What's weak

- **Hero shoe is clearly a women's style.** That salmon/pink colorway with the feminine silhouette is the first thing my eye lands on. I don't need a men's shoe to be brown and boring, but I do need it to not look like it was designed for my granddaughter.
- **"Stacking miles all season long" isn't my language.** I'm not training. I'm walking. This copy treats me like a serious runner and I'm not even close to that.
- **The Bondi 9 — HOKA's most cushioned shoe — gets zero description.** If they'd put one line under it like "max cushion, all-day comfort," I might have clicked right there. Instead it just says "Women's / Men's" and nothing else.
- **JOIN THE CLUB section is completely irrelevant to me.** Young people jogging in a pack with a "Read the Story" CTA. I don't run clubs. This takes up a third of the email.
- **No price anywhere.** Not a single dollar figure in the shoe grid. The $200 sock threshold hints at price range, but I can't do any value math without clicking through.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Bondi 9 as the comfort hero, not as a grid item.** Give it the full hero treatment — one shoe, one line about max cushioning, one CTA. That shoe IS Walker's shoe; the email just doesn't know it.
- 2. **Swap the hero colorway to a men's neutral.** Put the navy Bondi 9 (men's) up top instead of the salmon trainer. Immediate demographic signal.
- 3. **Kill or shrink the JOIN THE CLUB section.** That real estate is wasted on lifestyle content that doesn't convert comfort shoppers. Replace it with a comfort-feature callout or a simple "wide widths available" line.
- 4. **Add prices to the grid.** Even just the MSRP. I need to know if I'm window-shopping or actually in range.
- 5. **Tighten the body copy toward utility, not aspiration.** "Stacking miles" is runner talk. "On your feet all day" is Walker talk.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Duplicate conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` tags**

```html
<!-- tag 1 -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<!-- tag 2 -->
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```

Two viewport declarations are present. Tag 1 suppresses user scaling (`maximum-scale=1`); tag 2 re-enables it (`user-scalable=yes`). Client behavior is undefined when both exist. Additionally, `maximum-scale=1` fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) — users with low vision cannot scale the viewport. Remove tag 1; keep tag 2.

**[MEDIUM] Malformed `<meta>` charset tag — missing `http-equiv`**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no effect. Outlook and older mail clients rely on this declaration to set document encoding; its absence risks character rendering errors (mojibake) for non-ASCII content.

**[HIGH] Invalid CSS — `.nav_padding` and `.nav_border` have empty length/color values**

```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```

Both rules are syntactically invalid (`px` with no preceding number; `solid` with no color). The entire `@media (max-width:480px)` block may be partially discarded by strict parsers. These classes will silently fail on mobile. Intended values need to be filled in (e.g., `padding-top: 10px`, `border-top: 1px solid #000`).

**[LOW] `@font-face` declarations — no system-font fallback defined globally**

Custom fonts (`TheFutureHOKA`, `TheFutureHOKAMono`) are loaded from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/`. Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (Windows), and Samsung Mail strip or ignore `@font-face`. No global `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the `<style>` block, meaning affected clients will fall back to the user-agent default (typically Times New Roman or serif). A `font-family: "TheFutureHOKA", Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` fallback stack should be applied to all text-bearing `<td>` elements.

**[LOW] `a { color: #000001; }` — near-black hex, not black**

```css
a { color: #000001; }
```

`#000001` (blue channel = 1) instead of `#000000` is atypical. This is sometimes used to defeat Gmail's blue link auto-coloring, but it can also indicate a copy-paste error. Confirm intent; if black is desired, use `#000000`.
