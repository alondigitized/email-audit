# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Site Journey Review
**Date: April 5, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Another rough day on skechers.com. The front half of the trip — homepage, navigation, product page — held up reasonably well. Slip-ins are right there front and center on the homepage, which I appreciate. But then I tried to check my cart and got a completely blank white screen. Nothing. Just... gone. That's the same core breakdown I hit yesterday. And when I searched "slip in," the results came back showing women's shoes first — even though I'm logged in as a Skechers Plus member. This site still doesn't know who I am once I hit checkout or search.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The cart failure alone kills this score. A customer who can't see their cart can't buy anything. That's a conversion dead end.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Slip-ins front and center on the homepage.** The hero section shows "HANDS FREE Slip-ins" in big, bold letters right below the fold. I saw it immediately. That's exactly what I'm looking for. Good start.
- **BOGO 50% OFF deal is persistent and visible.** That blue banner stays at the top across every page. As someone who pays attention to value, I noticed it every step of the way. That's effective promotion.
- **Men's navigation menu is clean and readable.** The hamburger menu opens to a simple, well-spaced list. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins" — all there, all legible without squinting. Touch targets feel comfortable. This is one of the better-designed parts of the mobile experience.
- **Product page (Arch Fit 2.0 - Azusah) is solid.** Large product name in readable font, price ($135) displayed clearly, the BOGO discount called out directly on the page, and a big product image. The "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge is a nice trust signal. 4 stars and 10 reviews visible immediately — that helps me feel confident.
- **Size grid is functional.** Good-sized tap targets for each half-size from 7.0 to 14.0. I could tap these without fumbling. The "Please select a size" error message appeared clearly in red — the system caught that I hadn't picked a size yet.
- **ADD TO CART button is large and unmissable.** Full-width, bold blue — exactly how it should be on mobile.
- **263 results for Men's Hands Free Slip-ins.** Good selection depth. Knowing there are over 250 options gives me confidence I'll find my fit.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 showed a totally white screen — no products, no totals, no checkout button, nothing. This is a critical failure. I added a shoe to my cart and then had nowhere to go. This is the same problem as yesterday and it has not been fixed.
- **Search shows women's shoes first.** I searched "slip in" and the top results were both labeled "Women's." I'm a logged-in male Skechers Plus member. The site knows this. There's no excuse for surfacing women's styles at the top of my results. It returned 1,037 results — great selection — but the ordering completely ignores my gender.
- **Login didn't change anything visible.** The Step 3 login screenshot looks identical to the homepage. There's no visible "Welcome back, Walker" moment, no personalized banner, no Skechers Plus member acknowledgment. After logging in, the site looks exactly like it does for a stranger.
- **"Dismiss Popups" step didn't show a real popup.** The screenshot between homepage and logged-in state just shows a banner change (from "BOGO 50% OFF" to "Free Shipping with Skechers Plus"). There may have been a popup that didn't fully render, or the experience was underwhelming — but I never got a moment that said "hey, we see you're a Plus member."
- **"Spring Style Guide" promotion is irrelevant to me.** That's the secondary banner message I kept seeing. That's for a different customer. I want comfort tech, slip-ins, wide fit. Spring style guides feel aimed at fashion shoppers, not someone like me who just wants shoes that don't aggravate my feet.
- **Medium width filter gets cut off on the Men's Shoes page.** The width filter buttons show "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit," and then "Medium" is clipped at the right edge of the screen. A customer who needs medium width might not even see that option without scrolling horizontally — which nobody expects to do.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a revenue blocker. Every customer who adds an item and goes to checkout is hitting a blank wall. Priority one, full stop.
2. **Respect gender in search results.** A logged-in male user searching "slip in" should see men's results first. Basic personalization.
3. **Create a logged-in "welcome back" moment.** Show my name, my Plus status, maybe my points balance. Make me feel like a member, not a stranger.
4. **Fix the width filter cutoff on the Men's Slip-ins category page.** "Medium" width is being clipped — add horizontal scroll indicator or wrap the filter buttons.
5. **Swap the "Spring Style Guide" banner for something relevant to a returning Plus member.** BOGO is already doing the deal work — use the secondary banner for loyalty messaging or comfort tech highlights.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The site knows how to show me what I want at the top of the funnel — slip-ins, BOGO deals, clean men's navigation. But it completely falls apart when it matters most: the cart is broken, search ignores who I am, and logging in gives me nothing. I'm a loyal Skechers Plus member and the site treats me like a first-time visitor the moment things get serious.

**Score: 4/10 — No change from yesterday.**

The cart failure is unchanged. Until that's fixed, this journey cannot score higher than a 5 regardless of everything else. One step forward on the homepage, same brick wall at checkout.

## Accessibility

**Critical (4 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA roles used outside their required parent container — likely a slider/carousel component with misstructured list roles.
- `aria-required-children` (View Product): Element with role that requires specific child roles contains none (e.g., `role="listbox"` without `role="option"` children).
- `aria-valid-attr-value` (View Product): One or more ARIA attributes set to invalid values — needs targeted DOM inspection to identify which attributes.
- `button-name` (View Product): Buttons with no accessible name (no text, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`). Common in icon-only UI elements like close/expand buttons.

**Serious (5 violations):**
- `link-name` (Homepage): Anchor elements with no discernible text — likely image links with no `alt` text on the child `<img>`.
- `link-in-text-block` (Men's Shoes): In-text links distinguished only by color, not underline or other non-color indicator. Fails WCAG 1.4.1.
- `aria-dialog-name` (View Product): Modal/dialog missing `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. Screen readers cannot announce dialog purpose.
- `aria-input-field-name` (View Product): ARIA input fields (e.g., `role="spinbutton"` for quantity) without accessible name.
- `color-contrast` (View Product): Text failing minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio (WCAG AA).

**Moderate (11+ violations):**
- Landmark structure broken across Homepage and Men's Shoes: duplicate `<main>`, nested `<aside>` not top-level, non-unique landmarks, content outside landmark regions. Indicates components rendering their own `<main>` or `<aside>` in isolation without considering page-level structure.
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Heading levels skip (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`), likely from reusable components that assume a different heading hierarchy.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — Kasada Bot Detection (5 errors):**
- Endpoint: `/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308`
- This is Kasada's client-side fingerprinting SDK (`kpsdk`). The 429s indicate the bot detection layer flagged the audit session and rate-limited its fingerprinting calls. These errors will occur in any automated/headless context. However, if real user sessions are triggering these (e.g., fast navigation, VPN users), it could degrade client-side bot token acquisition and interfere with protected endpoints.

**429 Rate Limiting — Cart Page (1 error):**
- Endpoint: `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`
- The cart page itself returned 429. Combined with the elevated 140ms TTFB, this suggests the cart endpoint has aggressive rate limiting that may affect legitimate users under load or with fast repeated navigation. Needs verification against actual rate limit thresholds.

**CORS Violation — Stylitics Widget (2 errors + 2 net::ERR_FAILED):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&version=320dd7a42c'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
- Stylitics is a third-party shoppable styling widget. Their CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `skechers.com`. The widget's CSS fails to load, causing the widget to render without styles (or not render at all). This is a vendor-side configuration issue — Stylitics needs to add `skechers.com` to their CORS allowlist, or the request mode needs to change from CORS to `no-cors` (though that prevents reading the response). File a support ticket with Stylitics.

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Critical accessibility (WCAG blocker):**
1. Fix `button-name` on View Product: add `aria-label` to all icon-only buttons.
2. Fix `aria-required-children` on View Product: ensure `role="listbox"` / `role="tablist"` wrappers contain correctly-roled children.
3. Fix `aria-valid-attr-value` on View Product: audit all ARIA attributes in the product page component for invalid values.
4. Fix `aria-required-parent` on Homepage: likely a carousel — wrap `role="option"` or `role="tab"` elements in their required parent roles.

5. Contact Stylitics to resolve CORS on `web-assets.stylitics.com` — the widget is silently broken.
6. Investigate cart 429 threshold: determine whether the rate limit on `/cart/` is triggering for real users or only automated sessions. If the former, raise the threshold or move bot detection upstream.

7. Capture LCP and CLS for all 8 steps. Without these, Core Web Vitals compliance cannot be assessed. Use Chrome DevTools, Lighthouse CI, or a real-user monitoring tool.
