# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-19**
## Executive summary

- **From:** SKECHERS `<hello@msgs.skechers.com>`
- **Subject:** Your cart expires soon!
- **Date:** 2026-04-19
- They remembered me, and they got the shoe right — a white Slip-in with arch cushioning, exactly the kind of thing I'd actually consider. That headline, *"You Liked These And You Weren't Wrong,"* is friendly and not pushy, which I appreciate. The "Complete Your Order" button is big, clear, and right in front of me. But then the email loses the thread. The secondary product row below my cart item looks like women's shoes — colorful, strappy things that have nothing to do with me. The navigation puts WOMEN before MEN. And the 20% off banner, which should be the biggest lever they have to close this sale, sits below the fold like an afterthought. Good start, sloppy finish.
- The hero does its job. The offer exists. But missed targeting in the secondary content and poor offer placement leave conversion on the table.

## What's working

- **The hero product is actually my shoe.** Skechers Slip-ins: Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 — that's a men's comfort slip-in with arch support. Whoever pulled my cart did it right. First thing I see is the shoe I was already looking at.
- **"Complete Your Order" CTA is impossible to miss.** Dark button, plain text, centered. I don't have to hunt for what to do next.
- **"You liked these and you weren't wrong"** — this is a decent line. It's not aggressive. It's like a buddy nudging me back to something I already decided I wanted.
- **20% off is real money.** I notice it, even if it showed up too late.
- **Curbside Pickup and Find a Store** — I like having options. Sometimes I'd rather go try the shoe on.

## What's weak

- **The secondary product row looks like women's shoes.** Right below my cart item, there's a row of colorful sneakers that don't look men's to me. Red, bright blue, pink-ish. No labels I can read clearly at this size. If those are unisex or men's, the styling doesn't signal that. I'm already tuning them out.
- **WOMEN listed before MEN in the nav.** Small thing, but it adds to the feeling that this email wasn't really built for me. I'm 62, male, and I came here for men's shoes. Put me first.
- **The 20% off offer is buried.** I've already scrolled past my cart product and a product row before I hit the discount banner. If there's a financial reason to complete my order today, lead with it — or at least put it right next to the CTA.
- **Fine print at the bottom is tiny.** I'm on my phone. That block of text at the very bottom — the legal stuff about the Spring Flag Sale — is unreadable without zooming. I don't read it, but I notice it, and it makes the email feel cluttered at the end.
- **"Let's Get Texting" module feels like a detour.** I'm here to finish buying a shoe. Signing up for texts is a different ask entirely. Wrong moment.
- **App download prompt, BNPL options, social icons** — this is a cart-recovery email. All of this stuff is friction. Every module that isn't "here's your shoe, here's the deal, here's the button" is pulling me away from the close.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 20% off offer above the fold, next to the CTA.** "Complete your order and save 20% today" should be one message, not two separate sections.
- 2. **Audit the secondary product row for this persona.** If those are men's shoes, they need to look like men's shoes. If they're women's, pull them from this send entirely.
- 3. **Flip the nav order to MEN first** for male recipients. Simple personalization, easy win.
- 4. **Cut the clutter below the offer.** For a cart-recovery email, everything after the CTA should be minimal. App download, BNPL, texting sign-up, social icons — these can live in a regular promotional email. Here they dilute the one thing you want me to do: buy the shoe.
- 5. **Make the expiration feel real.** The subject says "your cart expires soon" but I don't see a timer, a date, or a specific deadline anywhere in the email body. If there's urgency, show it.
- 1. **Force HTTPS on Attentive tracking base URL** — contact Attentive or update ESP configuration; do not ship HTTP click URLs.
- 2. **Verify UTM survival through redirect** — use a link-checker tool against the resolved destination URLs before deployment.
- 3. **Add `title` content** — even `SKECHERS — Your Cart` prevents blank titlebar in Outlook.
- 4. **Restore underline on inline text links** — scope the `text-decoration:none` rule to button/CTA selectors only, not globally.
- 5. **Confirm CAN-SPAM footer completeness** — physical address, unsubscribe link, and List-Unsubscribe headers must be present in the full source.
- 6. **Validate SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment** for `msgs.skechers.com` before send.
- 7. **Simplify preheader padding** — remove soft-hyphen characters; keep variation selectors only.

## Technical Audit — SKECHERS Abandoned Cart Email

### 1. Technical Summary
Table-based layout sent via Attentive ESP (`attentivemail.com`); responsive via CSS media queries targeting `max-width:620px`. HTML is structurally sound but has several link, accessibility, and compliance items to verify.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking links (high priority)**
All links route through `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?...` — plain HTTP, not HTTPS. If Attentive's redirect does not immediately 301 to HTTPS, the click-path exposes a tracking token in cleartext. Example:
```
href="http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6..."
```
Confirm the redirect chain ends with `https://` on the final destination; if not, update the base tracking domain to `https://`.

**UTM parameters not confirmed**
UTM params are encoded inside the Attentive `upn=` redirect blob and are not directly readable from source. Verify via link-checker or Attentive dashboard that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` survive the redirect to skechers.com.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Outlook desktop) display the title in the window titlebar. An empty title is a minor but flaggable gap.

**Global link text-decoration removal**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This removes underlines from all links, including the unsubscribe link further down. Links relying solely on color for identification fail WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color). CTAs with button styling are fine; inline text links are not.

**Preheader padding method**
Preheader uses two distinct invisible character types to push hidden text past the preview cutoff — Unicode variation selectors (`͏` U+034F) followed by soft hyphens (`­` U+00AD). Both are standard techniques but using both is redundant. Some aggressive spam filters flag heavy use of soft hyphens; consider consolidating to variation selectors only.

**`lang="en"` present** — no issue.
**Viewport meta present** — no issue.
