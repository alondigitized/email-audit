# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-16**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the front door and found Skechers' comfort language right away: Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit and Max Cushioning sit in a bar under the logo. After that the trip fell apart. I tapped Add to Cart without picking a size and got a red error. The next screen, the cart, was completely blank white. I never saw a shoe in a cart, a price total or a checkout button. The search for "comfort shoes" worked, but it led with women's loafers and wedges. I'm a 62-year-old man, so that wasn't a good sign. A shopper like me would have gone to Hoka or the Famous Footwear aisle by that point.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday's 3/10, so nothing improved and nothing visibly got worse. I only have today's screenshots and yesterday's number, so I can't point to a specific regression. The score stays low for two reasons. The cart screen showed nothing at all, and that's the point where money changes hands. The "comfort" content is also more of a label than something that helps me decide.

## 3. What's Working

- **The comfort bar under the logo.** "Slip-ins · Wide Fit · Arch Fit · Max Cus…" is exactly what I shop for, and it's in the first screen. It scrolls sideways, so I'd have to know to swipe.
- **The Men menu.** It's a clean slide-out panel with big, well-spaced rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. There's a clear Back arrow and an X. This is easy for older eyes and thumbs.
- **Men's Shoes tiles.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two picture tiles. That's my shopping list.
- **Filters up top.** Filter, Sort by, Size and Width are all there, and Width matters to me. The count of 817 results is shown clearly.
- **Product page basics.** The price ($85.00) is large and bold, and the star rating is easy to read. The Klarna and Afterpay lines show monthly payments, which is good price math.
- **The size grid.** The sizes are big boxes. The sold-out 6.5 is struck through, so I'm not left guessing.
- **The Add to Cart button.** It's wide, dark blue and easy to hit.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart was blank (Step 8).** I saw a white screen with no item, no total and no message. If this is real and not a screenshot timing problem, it's the biggest failure on the site. I'd assume the site is broken and leave.
- **The popup and login steps looked the same as the homepage (Steps 2 and 3).** I couldn't tell whether I was logged in. The cart badge shows "0" and nothing greets me by name. I saw no sign of personalization anywhere.
- **The first shoes in Men's Shoes are colorful Britto art sneakers.** For a comfort-first man, bright novelty shoes up front say "this isn't for you." There's nothing about cushioning or arch support in view.
- **The product page buries the shoe.** I got a breadcrumb trail, a title, reviews, a price, Klarna and Afterpay, and a promo note before the first sliver of the shoe. The breadcrumb reads "Technologies › Collections › Website Premieres," which means nothing to me.
- **The promo messaging contradicts itself.** A banner at the top says "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart," but the product page says "Excluded from promotions" in orange. That undercuts trust, and I'm not going to do the math to sort it out.
- **No brand comparison and no value story anywhere.** Nothing says why this is worth $85 compared with Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. Nothing mentions arch support or cushioning on this shoe.
- **Search returned women's shoes first.** The 1,659 results opened with Women's Cypress and Women's Relaxed Fit, which are a loafer and a wedge. The results don't speak to me as a man.
- **The text is small in places.** The red "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" lines and the shoe sub-labels are small. The color swatches are tiny tap targets. The Size and Width chips are cut off at the right edge.
- **Several stacked promo bars take up the top of the screen.** There's a blue banner and a grey "Limited Time! 25% OFF Work & Safety" bar. That's a lot of clutter before any shoes appear.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make sure it always shows the item, the quantity, the price and the checkout button. Add a loading state and an error message so it's never a blank page. Check whether the blank screen is real or only a capture problem.
2. **Put comfort first on listing pages.** Default Men's Shoes sorting should lead with slip-ins, Arch Fit and walking shoes. Novelty collaborations belong further down.
3. **Show a short comfort summary on each product page.** Something like "Cushioning: high · Arch support: yes · Hands-free: yes · Wide widths: available" would do it. Put the shoe image above the details so I can see the product on arrival.
4. **Honor the shopper's gender in search.** If I'm browsing Men's, "comfort shoes" should return men's shoes first.
5. **Make the promos agree with each other.** Don't show a site-wide BOGO banner and then mark a product "Excluded from promotions" without explaining which offers apply.
6. **Make the Add to Cart error friendlier.** Scroll to the size grid, highlight it, and say "Pick your size to continue."
7. **Increase text size on offers and labels.** Enlarge the color swatches too. Drop one of the two promo bars on mobile.
8. **Make it obvious I'm signed in.** A "Hi, Walker" greeting and "Pick up where you left off" or "Your size: 10 Wide" would show some personalization.

## 6. Bottom Line

The menus and filters are friendly and the comfort vocabulary is right where I'd look. But a blank cart, women's-first search results, a flashy art shoe in the lead spot and conflicting promo messages mean I wouldn't buy here today. Fix the cart and put comfort details on every shoe, and this site could win my foot as easily as anyone's.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey surfaced three groups of problems:

- **Accessibility:** 18 axe violations, including 2 critical on the homepage (`button-name`, `aria-required-parent`).
- **Infrastructure:** a repeating `502` from `vice01.skechers.com` and a `429` on `/cart/`. The cart response also lacks basic document structure.
- **Data gaps in the audit itself:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, so Core Web Vitals can't be judged. Several steps look like they didn't navigate.

Caveats on the evidence:

- **Steps that may not have navigated.** The Log In and Men category steps report `https://www.skechers.com/` as their URL. Their HTML samples are identical to the homepage's, including the same `<html>` attributes and script list. Their TTFB (682ms) is also almost identical to the homepage's (675ms). These steps probably did not load new documents, so their metrics and axe results may describe the homepage again.
- **The cart page may not be a real cart.** The View Cart step shows no `<title>`, no `lang` attribute and no `<main>` landmark, and the network log shows `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. This is most likely a rate-limit or error response served instead of the cart. That is an inference, since the cart's HTML wasn't sampled.

- **Homepage landmarks:** `landmark-no-duplicate-main` and `landmark-main-is-top-level` indicate more than one `<main>`, one of them nested. This is likely an injected or personalization layer, or a component wrapping a second `<main>`.

- **Search page:** it repeats the complementary-landmark, unique-landmark, region and `aria-allowed-role` violations. These look like shared template issues rather than page-specific ones.
- **Cart page:** the missing `<title>` and `lang` match an error or non-templated response (see Technical Summary).
- **Pages with no results:** none are reported for Men, Men > Shoes, Product detail or Add to Cart. Either those steps were not scanned or they share a page with another step.

## SEO

- **Missing `<h1>`:** `page-has-heading-one` fails on the Homepage and View Cart.
- **Missing `<title>`:** `document-title` fails on View Cart. If this response is ever served to crawlers, it is a problem.
- **Nested or duplicate `<main>`:** this can confuse content-extraction heuristics. It is mainly an accessibility concern.
- **Not verifiable from the data:** canonical tags, structured data, meta descriptions and hreflang. The HTML samples were truncated before these would appear.
- **Homepage `<html>`:** `lang="en"` is present.

## Performance

- **Web Vitals not captured:** LCP and CLS are `?` for all 8 steps. There is no basis for a Core Web Vitals assessment. Likely causes are the `PerformanceObserver` being attached after the navigation or late-attached observers. Re-instrument with `buffered: true`.
- **TTFB, by step:**
  - **Home / Log In / Men:** 675–682ms. This is under the 800ms "good" threshold but the slowest in the journey.
  - **Men > Shoes:** 47ms. This suggests a cache hit or a client-side route change.
  - **Product detail / Add to Cart / View Cart:** 120–139ms.
  - **Search:** 93ms.
- **Heavy third-party tag load on the homepage.** The head already contains injected scripts or tags for:
  - **Tag management and testing:** GTM, Monetate.
  - **Analytics and session replay:** Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`).
  - **Ad and social pixels:** TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Pinterest, Nextdoor, Bing, Yahoo, Taboola, Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Tune (`go2sdk`).
  - **Consent:** TrustArc.

  All are `async`, which limits render blocking, but main-thread and network cost will grow with each additional tag.
- **GTM preview environment on what appears to be the live site:** `gtm.js?id=GTM-TDRBQZ&gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1`. This is a GTM environment snapshot, not the default live container. Confirm it is intended for production.
- **Font loading:** fonts load through `webfontloader` from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`. This adds a third-party dependency and an extra connection to the font path. The `wf-*-active` classes confirm JS-driven loading, which risks FOIT/FOUT and layout shift.
- **bfcache:** repeated `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` errors mean a script registers an `unload` handler. This typically disables the back/forward cache.

## Mobile Optimization

- The page uses a JS-set `--vh` custom property (`6.64px`, i.e. a ~664px viewport) for viewport height. This works, but modern `dvh`/`svh` units remove the need for it.
- Nothing else about mobile can be assessed. The viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and responsive images were not in the truncated samples.

## Console & Network Errors

26 console errors and 6 network errors. The console messages come from a small number of distinct causes:

- **`502` from `https://vice01.skechers.com/skecherssecurity/skechers/1.23.10.1/`** (5 of the 6 network errors; the console shows `Failed to load resource: 502` twice). This is a first-party security or bot-management endpoint, and it was failing throughout the session. The path suggests a client-side security or fraud script, so its failure may degrade protection or cause downstream blocks.
- **`Refused to display 'https://vice01.skechers.com/' in a frame because it set 'X-Frame-Options' to 'sameorigin'`.** The site embeds `vice01.skechers.com` in an iframe, but that host forbids framing from the parent origin. The two ends conflict (`www.skechers.com` and `vice01.skechers.com` are different origins), so the embed fails every time.
- **`429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`.** The cart was rate-limited. This is probably the automated journey tripping bot or rate protection. It may also affect real users who hit the cart quickly after add-to-cart, so check the thresholds.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`** (repeated). A script, probably third-party, uses an `unload` handler.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`** (repeated). Some script calls the browser Summarizer API with unsupported languages. It is probably a third-party widget or a browser-level feature. The source is not identified in the data.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the audit instrumentation first.** Capture LCP and CLS with buffered observers, and confirm that the Log In and Men steps actually navigate. Their URLs, HTML and TTFB currently match the homepage.
2. **Investigate the `vice01.skechers.com/skecherssecurity` 502s.** Check upstream health and decide whether failure of this script should degrade gracefully.
3. **Resolve the iframe conflict.** Either serve `vice01.skechers.com` with a `frame-ancestors` policy that allows `www.skechers.com`, or remove the iframe.
4. **Review the `/cart/` 429 behaviour.** Confirm whether real users can hit it, and capture the actual cart HTML to check whether the missing `<title>`, `lang` and `<main>` are real or just an error page.
5. **Fix the critical and serious accessibility issues:**
   - **Names:** add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`).
   - **ARIA:** correct the roles that fail `aria-required-parent` and `aria-allowed-role`.
   - **Landmarks:** reduce the page to a single top-level `<main>`, make complementary and navigation landmarks unique and top level, and wrap content in landmarks.
   - **Headings:** add an `<h1>` to the homepage and the cart.
6. **Audit the GTM container** (`gtm_preview=env-1`) and the third-party tag set. Remove the preview environment from production if it is unintentional, and prune tags that are no longer used.
7. **Find the `unload` handler** (likely in a third-party script) and replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` to restore bfcache eligibility.
8. **Consider self-hosting fonts** or using `font-display: swap` with preloaded WOFF2, instead of `webfontloader` from a CDN.
