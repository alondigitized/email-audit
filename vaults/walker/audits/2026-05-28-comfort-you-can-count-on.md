# Comfort you can count on
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Alright — this one stopped me. "Podiatrist Approved" right there at the top, big and bold, with that APMA Seal of Acceptance badge sitting in the corner. For a guy like me who's been dealing with aching arches and tired feet since before HOKA was even a thing, that is the right opening line. That is the hook. I didn't need a sale to pay attention.
- That said, once my eyes got past the hero, I noticed two things pretty quick: no price anywhere on this whole email, and a "Find Your Best Fit" quiz section at the bottom with a young runner doing sprint work on city pavement. I'm 62 and I'm looking for something that won't destroy my feet on a three-mile walk to the farmers market. That bottom section isn't for me. But the top half? That's a legitimately useful credibility email.
- I'd click the "Shop APMA-Approved" button — not to buy immediately, but to browse the Arahi and Bondi options with fresh eyes. I would not forward this one. The gift-guide email from yesterday felt more shareable. This one I'd keep to myself and open a second tab.

## What's working

- **"Podiatrist Approved" hero** — three words that speak directly to my foot-health anxiety. That's the single best subject-to-hero handoff HOKA has sent me in weeks.
- **APMA Seal badge** — visible in the corner, not buried. Third-party endorsement is real social proof; I trust a podiatry association badge more than a brand telling me their shoe is comfortable.
- **Named models front and center** — Arahi 5R, Bondi 9, Bondi 5R. I know those names. Clean product grid, clear Men's / Women's labels so I'm not hunting.
- **"Shop APMA-Approved" CTA** — better than a plain "Shop Now." The verb + noun + qualifier does the job.
- **Clean layout** — no broken images, no overlapping text, reads fine on my phone without zooming in.

## What's weak

- **No price, no offer** — after a string of "40% off" and "Final Hours" emails, landing in a full-price editorial with zero dollars off feels like a bait-and-switch in reverse. I came expecting something; I got a certification badge.
- **"Find Your Best Fit" quiz section** — that neon yellow block with a sprinter is aimed at a 26-year-old who just discovered running. I already know I need the Bondi. Don't waste the real estate on me.
- **Back-to-back send** — got the Father's Day guide yesterday, this today. Two quality emails in a row is better than the sale-fatigue stretch from earlier in the month, but my patience is thin from that run of eight-plus emails in two weeks.
- **No loyalty call-out** — I'm a member; nothing here acknowledges that. A single line like "as a Hoka member, these are your approved styles" would have made this feel personal instead of broadcast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor** — even "From $120" or "Member price inside" gives me a reason to click now vs. bookmark for later. The credibility play is strong; it just needs a hook to convert.
- 2. **Cut the quiz section or move it to a PS-style footnote** — the top half of this email is tight. The quiz block breaks the rhythm and talks to a different customer.
- 3. **Acknowledge the subscriber** — a single "Your next go-to walking shoe, Walker-approved" style callout in the subhead would close the gap between broadcast and personal.
- 4. **Leverage the brand history** — I've been getting HOKA emails for weeks; this would land harder with a line like "Built for the long days" rather than a quiz pitch.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some older clients ignore the charset declaration.

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">`**
Two viewport declarations appear in `<head>` with slightly different values (`user-scalable=yes` vs. absent). The second wins, but duplicate tags are dead weight and a flag in some spam filters.

**CSS syntax errors in mobile stylesheet (`@media only screen and (max-width:480px)`)**

| Rule | Raw value | Problem |
|------|-----------|---------|
| `.nav_padding` | `padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important;` | Missing numeric value — unit with no number; rule is silently dropped |
| `.nav_border` | `border-top:px solid ;` | Missing pixel value *and* missing color — rule dropped |

These will cause nav spacing/borders to fall back to whatever the desktop value is on narrow screens.

**`font-display: swap` in `@font-face`**
Supported in browsers; ignored in all major email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook). Not harmful, but noise.

**Custom `@font-face` stack**
`TheFutureHOKA` and `TheFutureHOKAMono` load from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/…`. Gmail (all surfaces) and Outlook 2007–2021 strip `@font-face` entirely — fallback stack must be solid. Not visible in the truncated source whether a safe fallback is declared on each element.

**Universal `border:none` reset**
```css
* { border:none; … }
```
Strips borders from every element including `<hr>` and any explicitly bordered table cells. This can mask layout dividers if inline styles are absent.

**`a { color: #000001; }`**
Near-black (`#000001` vs `#000000`) is a known workaround to prevent Gmail/iOS auto-link blue override. Functionally fine; worth documenting so QA doesn't flag it as a color error.
