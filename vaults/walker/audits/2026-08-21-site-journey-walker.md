# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 1/10 (prev 1/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-21**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

Second day in a row I got stopped at the exact same two walls: I can't log in, and I can't see my cart. Browsing is pleasant enough — the Men's Shoes page leads with hands-free slip-ins right up top, which is exactly my speed — but the account icon does nothing when tapped, and the cart page renders as a blank white screen after I supposedly added a shoe. The cart badge sat at "0" through all nine steps. I could window-shop on my phone all day, but I still can't buy anything or even confirm what's in my basket.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**1/10** — unchanged from yesterday. Discovery and browsing work fine, but both conversion-critical moments (login, cart) are still completely broken. This is a hard stop for any real purchase.

## 3. What's Working

- Homepage hero images are big, sharp, and legible without zooming — good for my eyes.
- The Men flyout menu (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, Collaborations, Clothing & Accessories) is plain-English and easy to scan on a small screen.
- Men's Shoes category page puts "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," and "Athletic Sneakers" right at the top — that's precisely the comfort-first framing I'm shopping for.
- Product detail page for the Slip-ins Contour Foam shoe shows a clear price ($65.99, marked down from $88), 5-star rating with review count, and a large product photo.
- Size selector buttons (7.0–13.0) on the PDP are big and easy to tap — no squinting or fat-thumb misses.
- Search for "comfort shoes" returned a large, mostly on-topic result set (1,506 results) with real product cards, pricing, and color swatches.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login still does nothing.** Tapping the account icon leaves me on the identical homepage — no sign-in form, no modal, nothing. I can't access order history or a saved size.
- **Cart page is still a blank white screen.** After the add-to-cart attempt, View Cart shows nothing at all — no items, no "empty cart" message, no error. This is where I'd give up and leave.
- **Add to Cart looks like it silently failed again.** The screenshot right before checkout shows a red "Please select a size" warning still active, and the cart badge reads "0" in every single screenshot across the whole trip, including after the add attempt and the search step.
- **"Excluded from promotions" sits right under a big 20%-off banner.** I scroll in expecting the discount to apply and then get told this specific shoe doesn't qualify — feels like a bait-and-switch to someone comparing price against Hoka or New Balance.
- **No add-to-cart confirmation anywhere.** Even if it had worked, there's no toast or checkmark telling me so.
- Search results mix genders — a women's shoe (GO WALK Arch Fit 2.0 - Cassy) shows first, ahead of a men's shoe, even though my whole session was in Men's Shoes.

## 5. Recommendations

1. Fix the account icon so it actually opens a sign-in screen — this has now failed two days running and blocks every personalization benefit (saved size, order history, loyalty points).
2. Fix the cart page rendering — a blank white screen after "adding to cart" is a guaranteed lost sale, and it's happened both days I've checked.
3. Block "Add to Cart" until a size is actually selected (or show a clear on-page error) instead of letting the tap silently no-op while the badge stays at zero.
4. Add a lightweight "Added to cart ✓" confirmation toast so shoppers get feedback the action worked.
5. Weight search relevance toward recent browsing context (I'd just spent the whole session in Men's) or let me filter by gender before scrolling through mixed results.
6. Clarify "Excluded from promotions" earlier — ideally before the shopper commits to a specific size/color — so the price math doesn't feel like a switch.

## 6. Bottom Line

No change from yesterday: good bones, broken checkout. The comfort-first framing, big photos, and readable text tell me Skechers knows who I am as a shopper. But login and cart being down two days in a row isn't a one-off glitch anymore — it's a standing hole in the funnel. Today, like yesterday, I could browse but I could not buy. Still a 1/10.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
Mobile journey audited across 8 steps (Homepage → Log In → Men category → Men > Shoes → PDP → Add to Cart → View Cart → Search). TTFB is acceptable-to-good throughout (25–485ms), but LCP and CLS were not captured for any step — a measurement gap that blocks a full Core Web Vitals assessment. The site loads 20+ third-party script origins (analytics, tag managers, pixel trackers) synchronously into every page. A bot-mitigation/fingerprinting endpoint (Kasada, `kpsdk`) returned 429 on 5 of 6 logged network errors, including one against `/cart/` itself. Notably, the "Log In" and "Men category" steps resolve to the same URL as the homepage (`https://www.skechers.com/`), consistent with client-rendered overlay/modal UI rather than distinct page navigations.

## Accessibility
18 violations total (2 critical, 5 serious, 9 moderate, 2 minor):

- **Homepage (10):** `aria-required-parent` (critical) and `button-name` (critical) are the highest-priority fixes — ARIA roles missing required parent context, and buttons with no discernible text will not announce correctly to screen readers. Also `link-name` (serious), plus a cluster of landmark issues (`landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `region`) indicating duplicated/nested `<main>` and non-top-level landmark regions, `heading-order`, and `aria-allowed-role` (minor).
- **View Cart (4):** `document-title` (serious) and `html-has-lang` (serious) — this page state renders without a `<title>` or `lang` attribute, unlike the sampled pages which correctly set `lang="en"`. Also missing a single `<main>` landmark (`landmark-one-main`) and an H1 (`page-has-heading-one`).
- **Search "comfort shoes" (4):** Same landmark/region pattern as homepage (`landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`) plus `aria-allowed-role` (minor).

The repeated landmark violations across three separate pages point to a shared layout/component issue (likely a header, footer, or aside included in a way that produces duplicate or non-top-level landmarks) rather than isolated per-page bugs.

## SEO
- `View Cart` is missing both `<title>` and `html lang` — a page state indexable/shareable content should not ship without these.
- "Log In" and "Men category" steps did not produce distinct URLs — both loaded under `https://www.skechers.com/`. If these are genuinely client-side overlays (not full navigations), the underlying category/login views are not independently crawlable or deep-linkable; confirm this is intentional.
- `heading-order` and duplicate `<main>` landmark violations on the homepage indicate malformed document structure, which can affect how search engines parse content hierarchy.

## Performance
- TTFB is good across the board: 25–128ms on most steps, with Homepage (425ms), Log In (485ms), and Men category (485ms) noticeably higher than the rest — worth investigating server-side (these three share the same URL, so the higher TTFB may reflect first-load/cold-cache behavior vs. the faster subsequent client-rendered transitions).
- **LCP and CLS were not recorded for any step.** Without these, mobile load-perceived performance and layout stability cannot be evaluated — this should be treated as an instrumentation gap to close before this audit can make Web Vitals pass/fail judgments.
- The `<head>` loads 20+ third-party origins (TikTok Pixel, Pinterest, Google gtag ×3, GTM, Monetate ×2, Quantum Metric, Impact, CreativeCDN, Bazaarvoice/brsrvr, Pulse Insights, Bing, cdnjs webfontloader). Most are `async`, but the sheer count adds parse/execution and main-thread contention overhead on mobile, independent of the missing LCP data.
- Repeated 429s against the Kasada fingerprinting endpoint (`kpsdk`) suggest the client is being rate-limited/retried during the session; one 429 hit `/cart/` directly, which is a functional endpoint, not just telemetry.

## Mobile Optimization
- The document sets a JS-computed `--vh` custom property inline (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`), the common pattern for correcting mobile browser chrome/URL-bar viewport issues. This works but depends on JS execution/resize listeners rather than native `dvh`/`svh` CSS units, adding a layout-recalculation dependency on mobile.
- Viewport `<meta>` tag presence could not be confirmed — the HTML samples were truncated before this portion of `<head>`.
- Web font loading uses `webfontloader` from cdnjs with FOUT-style class toggling (`wf-mulish-n*-active` added post-load to `<html>`), which can cause a visible font swap on mobile connections; cannot confirm impact on CLS without the missing metric.

## Console & Network Errors
- **429 (Kasada bot-detection endpoint, ×4)** — `.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.711` repeatedly rate-limited during the session.
- **429 on `/cart/` (×1)** — a functional page/resource, not telemetry, was rate-limited; if this occurs for real users it can block cart access.
- **CORS-blocked stylesheet** — `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` failed with no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header, followed by `ERR_FAILED`. This is the Stylitics outfit-recommendation widget's stylesheet; the widget will render unstyled or fail to render.
- **Summarizer API warnings (×4)** — Chrome's built-in Summarizer API is being invoked with unsupported language parameters and aborting every time. Not user-facing, but indicates a script (site code or extension) making calls that fail on every load.

## Recommendations
1. Fix the Stylitics CORS misconfiguration (add proper `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on the stylesheet response, or proxy/self-host it).
2. Investigate the Kasada 429s, particularly the one against `/cart/` — confirm rate-limit thresholds aren't being tripped by legitimate mobile traffic patterns.
3. Fix critical accessibility issues first: add discernible text to icon-only buttons (`button-name`), correct ARIA role/parent nesting (`aria-required-parent`), then resolve the landmark structure (single top-level `<main>`, unique/top-level `aside`) shared across Homepage and Search.
4. Ensure the View Cart state sets `<title>` and `lang` — likely a client-rendering gap where this route doesn't fully hydrate document metadata.
5. Instrument LCP and CLS collection — the current data set cannot support a Web Vitals verdict without it.
6. Audit and trim the third-party script list loaded on every page; consider deferring non-critical pixels/trackers on mobile.
7. Guard or remove the Summarizer API call with unsupported language parameters to stop the recurring console errors.
8. Confirm whether "Log In" and "Men category" are intentionally client-side overlays; if they should be independently navigable/crawlable, address the shared-URL behavior.
