# Adizero Evo SL. Designed for fast culture.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Well, adidas is back with another one. I'll give them credit — this time they sent me a shoe. The Adizero Evo SL is right there at the top, it's white, it looks like a running shoe, and the name is at least in the right category. But "designed for fast culture" — I don't even know what that means. I'm sixty-two. I want to know if my feet are going to ache after a Costco run, not whether I'm part of some culture.
- Then I scroll down and — there it is — another cleat. A coral-pink one this time, with a contest to win a signed version from the "Road to Glory Pack." I've been tracking adidas for a few weeks now and the World Cup stuff just doesn't quit. The Predator email, the Mexico jersey, the soccer jerseys for Father's Day. This cleat section feels copy-pasted from a different campaign entirely, and it absolutely does not belong in an email that opens with a lifestyle running shoe.
- The preheader text at the very top looked garbled and made no sense at all — "A sweet blanket ban designed to help you feel, and we off the track" or something close to that. If I'd read that in my inbox before opening, I would've assumed the email was broken. Mark as read and move on. Not clicking anything here.

## What's working

- The Adizero Evo SL image is clean and large — the shoe is front and center, no clutter, and at least it looks like something I'd try on.
- The headline "THE ADIZERO EVO SL IS BACK" is big and legible, no squinting required on my phone.
- The email is visually tidy — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing embarrassing.
- The "Shop Now" banner at the top is a clear enough tap target if I actually wanted to act.

## What's weak

- "Fast culture" is not my language. I want "all-day comfort" or "cushioning for your walk." Speed culture copy speaks to a thirty-year-old, not me.
- The cleat section feels like a completely different email accidentally stapled on — the pivot from running shoe to soccer giveaway is jarring and wastes half the scroll.
- No price. No sale. No discount. No "members save X." There is literally no financial reason to click right now.
- That preheader text is either garbled or pulled from the wrong content block — it reads like scrambled copy, and it's the first thing that would show up in my inbox.
- The body copy tells me this shoe is for "bold style" and "fast culture." I want to know about the cushioning, the arch, the width options — zero of that is here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "fast culture" with a comfort hook.** The copy should call out cushioning, all-day wear, or walking comfort. Something like "Built for the miles you actually log" — that speaks to me.
- 2. **Cut the cleat section entirely.** Walker Miles does not want a signed cleat. That section belongs in a different list segment. A clean single-product send would be more effective.
- 3. **Add a price or offer.** Even "from $X" or "free shipping today" gives me a reason to tap. Right now there's nothing pulling my wallet out.
- 4. **Fix that preheader.** Whatever is showing at the top of this email before the logo is not intentional marketing copy. That needs to be a clean one-liner that finishes the subject's thought.
- 5. **Add a real hero CTA button** in the shoe section — not just a text link with an asterisk. I'm on a phone; a tappable button matters.

## Technical Audit — Adidas Adizero Evo SL

**From:** adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com
**Subject:** Adizero Evo SL. Designed for fast culture.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken click-redirect URL and a missing plain-text fallback. Six HTTP (non-HTTPS) image references and several missing `alt` attributes add rendering and deliverability risk across privacy-enforcing mail clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken relative URL in click-redirect**

The QA prober received an `unknown url type` error for a path-only value passed to the redirect handler:

```
/g/2e158291-c472-4490-a621-142b584c271f?txn=97828800-66f9-11f1-980a-3efb318f62ec
```

This was resolved by the redirect to:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/2e158291-c472-4490-a621-142b584c271f?cp_tp=v3...
```

The `/g/` path is being treated as a relative URL rather than an absolute one inside the click-redirect layer. If the ESP's link-wrapping logic fails to absolutize this path at send time, clicks on the associated CTA will 404 or error. **Confirm the wrapped final URL resolves cleanly in a staging send before deployment.**

**[INFO] 30 tracking links skipped**

All 30 links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Not a bug, but full destination validation requires resolving through the redirect chain — these were not probed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 6× HTTP image sources (same asset, repeated)**

The arrow icon is loaded via HTTP across all instances:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail (via Google Image Proxy), Apple Mail with image blocking, and some corporate firewalls will either block or strip HTTP asset loads. The fix is a simple protocol swap to `https://`.

**[WARN] Open pixel also served over HTTP**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ebf60ae5d-.../BlrtKePLQH...=452
```

Open-tracking pixels over HTTP are blocked by most modern clients (Gmail proxies to HTTPS; Apple MPP rewrites to its own CDN). This may cause open-rate undercounting beyond the baseline MPP distortion.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on two images**

- `2e158291-c472-4490-a621-142b584c271f` (linked image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `BlrtKePLQH-YNXdWM7Rb49zoUWMqChCsXrh6gcOQ_6M=452` (open pixel)

Both render as blank boxes in image-off environments. The open pixel can safely carry `alt=""` (decorative); the product/CTA image should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and Outlook's image-blocked default state.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice in separate `<style>` blocks. Both blocks fall back to `local('Arial')` / `local('Helvetica')` — no web font is actually fetched. This is harmless but adds ~1 KB of dead CSS.

**[INFO] `font-display: swap` in email CSS**

`font-display` is a web property with no effect in email clients. Minor dead code.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback absent**

QA measured **0 characters** in the `text/plain` MIME part. CAN-SPAM does not technically require a plain-text part, but:

- RFC 2822 / ESP best practices strongly recommend a `multipart/alternative` structure to avoid spam filter penalties.
- Certain enterprise mail gateways (Proofpoint, Mimecast) flag HTML-only messages as higher-risk.
- Screen readers and terminal clients fall back to plain text.

The plain-text part should contain at minimum the subject, a short summary, all CTA URLs in plain form, and the unsubscribe link.

**[INFO] Unsubscribe mechanics**

The HTML source is truncated before the footer, so the unsubscribe link could not be directly verified. Given this is a commercial promotional send, CAN-SPAM §5 requires a functioning opt-out mechanism. Confirm the footer contains a working unsubscribe URL (not image-based) before send.

**[INFO] `ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `referrer: no-referrer` present**

Both meta tags are email-standard and correctly implemented to suppress web-archive indexing and referrer leakage.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[INFO] UTM parameters not visible in truncated source**

The `dv.adidas.com` redirect URL contains a large `cp_tp` payload (base64/compressed), which likely encodes campaign parameters server-side rather than as visible UTM querystring values. This is a valid ESP pattern (SFMC encoded tracking), but means standard GA4 UTM attribution depends entirely on the redirect correctly unpacking `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` into the final landing URL. **Verify a decoded click resolves with UTM params intact** by stepping through the redirect chain manually.

No mismatched campaign/product alignment is inferable from the truncated source alone.
