- Same shoe, same story. I don't have a women's shoe in any cart, I've never clicked on one, and there's nothing here that tells me this is a mistake versus just a template Skechers blasts to everyone regardless of what's actually in their cart.
- The one thing that did catch my eye was buried below the fold — 20% off boots and outdoor styles, valid through 9/28. ## What's working

- The boots/outdoor 20% off banner has a real number and a real deadline (thru 9/28) — that's the kind of concrete offer that gets my attention if it were up top.
- "Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit: Altman - Murdock Sneaker" is at least one product in my actual category, buried in the grid.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — text is big enough to read without zooming on my phone.
- Curbside pickup and "Find a Store" icons are handy for someone like me who still likes to try shoes on before buying.

## What's weak

- That pink floral women's D'Lites sneaker again, same as three of the last four emails I've gotten from this brand — I don't have that in a cart and never will.
- The product grid is five women's/kids' shoes to one men's shoe — feels like a stranger got my email address, not a company that knows what I buy.
- The real offer (20% off boots/outdoor) is buried past a giant "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" banner and six product thumbnails — by the time I'd get there on my phone I've probably already bailed.
- "READY TO CHECK OUT?" headline assumes urgency I don't feel, since it's not my cart to begin with.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or comfort features anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to buy a shoe.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending cart-abandonment templates when the cart item doesn't match the recipient's gender/purchase history — lead with the 20% off boots/outdoor deal instead, since that's the only thing in here relevant to me.
- 2. Swap the hero image from the pink floral sneaker to a men's boot or slip-in tied to the actual discount.
- 3. Move the 20% off banner above the product grid, not below six irrelevant thumbnails.
- 4. If personalization data is unreliable, default to a generic "browse our best sellers" email rather than a fake cart reminder — it undercuts trust every time it's wrong.
- 5. Add one line of comfort/fit language (arch support, cushioned sole) near the men's product — that's the actual purchase trigger for me.
