# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 2/10 (prev 1/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-23**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I went to buy myself a pair of Skechers on my phone — wanted to see if I could log in, find a comfortable men's shoe, throw it in the cart, and check out. Browsing was fine. Buying was not. I tapped "Add to Cart" without picking a size (easy mistake — nothing nudges you to pick one first), got a small red warning I almost missed, and when I went to "View Cart" anyway, the page came up **completely blank** — nothing there at all. My cart badge stayed at "0" through the entire rest of the trip. I never got a shoe in my cart. I also tried to log in and nothing visibly happened — the screen looked identical to the homepage. Search worked well and pointed me at genuinely comfortable options, which is the one bright spot.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**2/10**

Compared to yesterday's 1/10, this is barely better — the category menu and search are solid, relevant, and easy to tap through on a phone. But the two things that actually make Skechers money — logging in and getting a shoe into a cart — both failed outright. A broken cart page isn't a UX nit, it's a revenue leak. If I can't buy the shoe, none of the nice category browsing matters.

## 3. What's Working

- **Hamburger "Men" menu** is clean, well-spaced, easy to tap with a thumb (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, etc.) — good text size, clear chevrons.
- **Men's Shoes landing page** surfaces "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" right up top as tappable tiles — that's exactly the kind of comfort-first shortcut I want.
- **Search relevance** for "comfort shoes" is genuinely good — it returned Arch Fit and Max Cushioning styles even though neither literal word appears in most product names. That's smart matching.
- **Add to Cart button** itself is big, blue, thumb-friendly — no complaints about the tap target.
- **Sale banner** (20% off, code SKXPLUS) is visible and persistent across every screen, so I always know a deal is on.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart is broken.** After tapping Add to Cart, "View Cart" loaded a totally blank white page. No items, no "your cart is empty" message, nothing. This is the single biggest problem in the whole walkthrough.
- **Cart never actually got the shoe.** The cart badge read "0" from step 7 all the way through step 9 — the add-to-cart attempt silently failed because no size was selected, and the site let me proceed to checkout intent without one.
- **Size-selection warning is easy to miss.** "Please select a size" showed up in small red text below a grid of size buttons — at 62, with the screen already busy, that's not a strong enough nudge. No size was pre-highlighted, no shake/scroll-to animation, nothing forcing my eye there.
- **Login is a dead end, visually.** Tapping to log in produced a screen that looks pixel-identical to the homepage. If a modal or page did open, it wasn't visible in the viewport — as far as I could tell, nothing happened when I tried to log in.
- **"Excluded from promotions"** on the Aero Burst product page is a letdown right after seeing 20%-off banners everywhere. Small orange text, easy to miss until you're at checkout wondering why the discount didn't apply.
- **752 results** on the Men's Shoes page with no obvious "sort by comfort" or "best for arch support" quick-filter — that's a lot to wade through on a phone for someone who just wants "the cushioned one."

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page — urgently.** A blank page where a cart should be is a hard stop on revenue. This needs to be treated as a P0 bug, not a UX polish item.
2. **Require or default a size before enabling Add to Cart**, or scroll/highlight the size grid automatically when the warning fires — don't rely on small red text alone.
3. **Make login state visibly obvious.** Show a confirmation, a name/greeting, or a modal that's clearly different from the homepage so I know something happened when I tap "Log In."
4. **Flag promo exclusions earlier** — on the product tile in search/category results, not just buried on the PDP.
5. **Add a "Most Comfortable" or "Best Arch Support" sort/filter** on category pages with 700+ results — I shouldn't have to scroll forever to find what your homepage already promises (Arch Fit, Max Cushioning).

## 6. Bottom Line

Browsing feels built for me — big tiles, comfort-forward categories, relevant search. But I couldn't actually buy anything, and I couldn't tell if I was logged in. A comfort shopper my age doesn't have patience for a blank cart page; I'd bail and go check Hoka or New Balance instead. Fix checkout before anything else — right now this site loses the sale at the finish line.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Mobile journey through skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / Demandware storefront) was captured across 8 steps: Homepage, Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart, View Cart, Search. The most significant finding is that the **View Cart step (`/cart/`) returned HTTP 429** from the server, and the captured HTML for that state is missing `<title>`, `lang`, and `<h1>`/main-landmark markup — consistent with the client receiving a degraded/rate-limited response rather than the full cart template. A bot-mitigation fingerprinting endpoint (`x-kpsdk-v`, Kasada) also 429'd four separate times during the session, and a third-party stylesheet (Stylitics) failed to load due to a CORS misconfiguration.

## Accessibility

18 axe-core violations recorded, concentrated on Homepage (10), Search results (4), and View Cart (4).

- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` on Homepage — one or more buttons lack accessible names, and an ARIA role is used without its required parent role, both of which break screen-reader navigation.
- **Serious:** `link-name` on Homepage (links with no discernible text); `document-title` and `html-has-lang` on View Cart.
- **Moderate:** Landmark structure is broken on both Homepage and Search — `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, and `region` all fire, indicating multiple/non-top-level `<main>` and duplicated landmark roles. `heading-order` fails on Homepage. `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` fail on View Cart (no `<main>`, no `<h1>`).
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role` on Homepage and Search (invalid `role` values on non-matching elements).

The View Cart violations (missing title/lang/h1/main) correlate directly with the 429 response observed on that request — the page markup present in that state looks like a stripped error/rate-limit response, not the normal cart template, which would explain the missing baseline document structure.

## SEO

- `document-title` missing on View Cart — an empty or absent `<title>` on a rate-limited response will not index correctly and breaks browser tab/history labeling.
- `html-has-lang` missing on View Cart — `lang="en"` is present on every other captured page (Homepage, Log In, Men category) but absent here, again consistent with a non-standard/error response body being served instead of the templated page.
- No heading-one on View Cart (`page-has-heading-one`), which also affects on-page content signals for that URL.
- No other on-page SEO defects observed in the truncated HTML samples (canonical/meta tags weren't visible in the truncated `<head>` output — recommend re-capturing full `<head>` for a complete check).

## Performance

- TTFB was only reliably captured for the first three steps: Homepage 586ms, Log In 378ms, Men category 378ms. 586ms is elevated for a TTFB baseline and worth investigating server/edge-cache behavior on first load.
- Men > Shoes (27ms), Product detail (31ms), Add to Cart (31ms), and Search (25ms) show implausibly low TTFB for a server-rendered Demandware storefront — this pattern usually indicates responses served from a warm edge/browser cache rather than fresh origin renders. Worth confirming these aren't cached/stale responses skewing the metric.
- View Cart TTFB (107ms) should be discounted — it was captured against a 429 response, not a successful cart render, so it doesn't reflect real page-load performance for that step.
- **LCP and CLS were not captured for any step** (`?ms`/`?` throughout). This is a data gap, not a "no issues" result — Core Web Vitals cannot be assessed from this run. Recommend re-running with a tool that reports `LargestContentfulPaint` and `LayoutShift` PerformanceObserver entries (e.g., Lighthouse or web-vitals.js injection) before drawing performance conclusions.

## Mobile Optimization

No mobile-specific layout defects are observable from the data provided (no viewport meta, tap-target, or responsive-breakpoint data was captured in this audit). The `--vh` custom property set inline on `<html>` indicates a JS-based viewport-height fix (common iOS Safari 100vh workaround) is in use — functioning as expected, no issue. Cannot assess touch-target sizing or responsive image delivery without additional mobile-specific instrumentation (not included in this dataset).

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 rate-limiting on bot-detection fingerprinting** (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.711`, Kasada) — fired 4 times across the session. Repeated 429s from a bot-mitigation endpoint typically indicate the client is being throttled/flagged, which can degrade or block legitimate automated/mobile sessions.
- **429 on `/cart/` itself** — the actual cart page request was rate-limited, directly causing the malformed View Cart response described above. This is the most actionable network error in the set.
- **CORS failure**: `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` blocked by CORS policy (missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`) — this third-party outfit-styling stylesheet fails to load on every page it's referenced, correlating with the `net::ERR_FAILED` console entry.
- **Chrome Summarizer API errors** ("Unsupported Summarizer API languages") fired 4 times — a script on the page is invoking the browser's built-in `window.ai`/Summarizer API with unsupported language parameters, causing repeated aborted calls. This is console noise from a misconfigured feature-detection or AI-widget script rather than a user-facing failure, but it's a wasted API call on every affected step.

## Recommendations

1. **Investigate the `/cart/` 429** as the priority item — determine whether this is rate-limiting from the Kasada bot-mitigation layer misfiring against legitimate mobile traffic, or a backend capacity issue. This single error cascades into the View Cart accessibility/SEO failures (missing title/lang/h1/main).
2. **Fix the Kasada fingerprinting 429s** — review the bot-detection service's rate-limit thresholds for mobile sessions, or check whether the client is issuing duplicate/redundant fingerprint calls that trip the limiter.
3. **Fix the Stylitics CORS configuration** — add `www.skechers.com` to `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on `web-assets.stylitics.com`, or self-host/proxy the stylesheet.
4. **Remediate the Homepage landmark/ARIA structure**: consolidate to a single top-level `<main>`, ensure `role` attributes are only used where their required parent role exists, and add accessible names to all interactive buttons/links (critical/serious axe violations).
5. **Remove or gate the Summarizer API calls** so they aren't invoked with unsupported language parameters, eliminating the repeated console errors.
6. **Re-run the audit with LCP/CLS capture enabled** — the current dataset cannot support any Core Web Vitals conclusions; treat the performance section above as TTFB-only until that gap is closed.
7. **Investigate the anomalously low TTFB values** on Men > Shoes, PDP, Add to Cart, and Search (25–31ms) to confirm they reflect real origin/edge timing and not cached/stale responses skewing the metric.
