## Executive summary

- Well, New Balance again — third or fourth one this week if I'm counting right, and this one's actually running shoes, which is more than I can say for the wooden chairs they sent me on the 14th. There's a big legible grid of eight running shoes, "Up to 30% off," and a code right there — that part I can read fine without squinting. But it's a running email, same as the one on the 12th, and I already told them then: I'm not their guy for performance racers. These look like lightweight trainers for people logging miles, not a walking shoe with real arch support for a fella like me who's on his feet at the mall, not on a track.
- The two models at the top are young — slim ankles, colorful compression socks, nothing that signals "this is comfortable enough for an all-day shoe." No mention of cushioning, arch support, or all-day comfort anywhere, which is the stuff that actually gets me to click. And then out of nowhere, there's a photo of two wooden folding chairs sitting in the middle of a shoe email — I don't know if that's an ad glitch or what, but it did nothing for me and just made the whole thing feel unfinished.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Not forwarding it, not clicking. If they want my click, put a walking or lifestyle shoe with a comfort call-out front and center, not another Ellipse racer.

## What's working

- The "Up to 30% off" offer is bold, legible, and impossible to miss — that's a real number, not vague marketing speak.
- Product grid is clean, one shoe per row with names underneath — easy to scan even for my eyes.
- "Shop men" / "Shop women" buttons are big, high-contrast, easy to tap on a phone.
- Promo code (BTS26) and valid dates are spelled out plainly near the bottom — no hunting for fine print.

## What's weak

- Every shoe here is a performance running shoe — no walking, lifestyle, or comfort-cushioned option for someone like me who just wants to be comfortable on my feet all day.
- Both hero models are young and lean, styled for speed, not for a 62-year-old who wants slip-on ease and arch support.
- That folding-chair photo mid-email is bizarre and off-brand — looks like the wrong image got dropped in, and it broke my trust in the email for a second.
- No loyalty or member-pricing callout even though I've bought from them before — feels like a mass blast, not a "we know you" email.
- Zero mention of comfort features (cushioning, arch support, all-day wear) that would actually get my attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero and product grid for walking/lifestyle styles (Fresh Foam 680v9 is already in here, lead with that instead of the racing Ellipse) and call out cushioning/arch support in the hero copy — that's the single biggest lever for a guy like me.
- 2. Cut or replace the wooden chairs image — it reads like a mistake and undercuts the whole email's credibility.
- 3. Add a loyalty/member-pricing line if I'm a known customer — a stranger-treatment feel kills the click.
- 4. Segment running vs. walking shoppers so I stop getting racer emails three times in one week.
