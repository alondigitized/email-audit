## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 6, 2026 | **Compared to yesterday's score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old guy who knows exactly what he wants: Men's Hands-Free Slip-ins, a good deal, and a checkout that just works. Today's visit had some bright spots — the Men's nav menu is genuinely the best screen on the whole site — but I landed on a homepage screaming "Mother's Day" at me, couldn't tell if I was actually logged in, and then hit a completely blank white cart page when I tried to buy. A blank cart is a showstopper. If I can't see what's in my cart, I'm not buying. Full stop.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The Men's nav got a little cleaner in my head, but the blank cart cancels out any goodwill. Skechers is one misfire away from losing a loyal Plus member to Amazon or a local sporting goods store.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's nav menu** is the star of this whole journey. Big text, wide tap targets, clean list. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there as a quick link below the fold — exactly where I want it. "Comfort Technologies" is visible too. Somebody designed this part right.
- **BOGO 50% OFF** banner persists across every screen. As a price-conscious shopper, I noticed it every single time. That's effective.
- **Product detail page** — the Kynley shoe — has a clean layout. Price ($120), the BOGO deal in orange, star rating, and the Hands-Free Slip-Ins badge badge are all visible without scrolling much. Readable text, no squinting required.
- **Size selector on Add to Cart** is a proper grid with big enough boxes for my fingers. The sticky "ADD TO CART" button pinned to the bottom is smart design — I didn't have to hunt for it.
- **"Customers say this item feels true to size"** is a helpful nudge right above the size grid. Good for someone like me who's always second-guessing whether to size up.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Homepage — wrong audience, wrong message.** First thing I see is a Mother's Day theme with flowers, a mom and her kid, pink everything. I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for his own shoes. There's zero men's content above the fold. I have to scroll past this whole display just to find my way. It feels like I walked into the wrong store.
- **Login — no visible confirmation.** The login screenshot looks identical to the homepage. I can't tell if I actually logged in. There's no "Welcome back, Walker" moment, no name in the header, no Plus member badge. If you're going to make me sign in, at least tell me it worked.
- **Cart page: completely blank.** This is the single most damaging thing on the site today. I added a shoe I actually liked, went to view my cart, and got a white screen. Nothing. No product, no price, no checkout button. That's a broken experience. I would close the browser and give up right there.
- **Search for "slip in" shows women's first.** 1,021 results is already overwhelming, but the two products I can see immediately are both labeled Women's. I'm logged in (supposedly) as a male shopper. Why am I seeing women's shoes at the top? This should be filtered or sorted to Men's by default.
- **Only 7 reviews on the product.** For a $120 shoe, I want to read what other guys my age think. Seven reviews doesn't give me enough confidence.
- **No Skechers Plus personalization anywhere.** I'm a member. There should be something — points balance, member pricing, "you're 50 points away from a reward" — that makes me feel like the membership is worth it. I see nothing.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a P0 bug. A blank cart breaks the entire purchase funnel. Nothing else matters until this works.
2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in men.** If I'm signed in, show me Men's Slip-ins, not Mother's Day gifts. Even a simple "Welcome back, Walker — Shop Men's Slip-ins" banner would change the whole feeling.
3. **Show the logged-in state clearly.** Put my first name or a Plus member indicator in the header so I know the login worked.
4. **Default search results to my gender.** A logged-in male shopper searching "slip in" should see Men's results first, or at minimum a clear Men's filter pre-applied.
5. **Add a Plus member widget.** A small points balance or reward progress indicator somewhere on the homepage or product page would reinforce loyalty and make me feel valued.
6. **Reduce search result count anxiety.** 1,021 results for "slip in" is paralyzing. Auto-apply a Men's filter and surface the top Slip-in families (Max Cushioning, Arch Fit) as curated picks above the grid.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are decent — the Men's nav is genuinely good, the product page is clean, and the BOGO deal is well-displayed. But a loyal 62-year-old Skechers Plus member who just wants to buy a pair of Slip-ins hit a homepage selling him flowers, no sign he's recognized as a member, and a blank cart when he tried to check out. That's not a friction problem, that's a trust problem. **Score holds at 4/10 — same as yesterday. No regression, no improvement. The cart bug must be fixed before anything else moves the needle.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). TTFB is healthy across most steps except View Cart (326ms, ~10× higher). The cart page is being actively rate-limited by the bot-detection layer. LCP and CLS are unsampled — coverage gaps prevent full Core Web Vitals assessment. Third-party script sprawl is severe. Two serious accessibility regressions exist on the cart page (missing `<title>` and `lang`). The Stylitics outfit widget is broken due to a misconfigured CORS header.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (1)**
- `aria-required-parent` on Homepage: ARIA role used on an element without its required ancestor role present in the DOM. Screen readers will misinterpret the widget's structure.

**Serious (2, both on View Cart)**
- `document-title`: Cart page renders with an empty or absent `<title>`. Likely a client-side navigation issue where the document title is not updated on SPA-style cart open.
- `html-has-lang`: The `<html>` element on the cart page lacks a `lang` attribute. The homepage HTML shows `lang="en"` correctly, indicating the cart loads in a different render context (iframe or injected fragment) that strips the attribute.

**Moderate (12)**
- Duplicate `<main>` landmark on Homepage (`landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`): multiple `<main>` elements coexist, violating HTML spec.
- `<aside>` elements not at top level on Homepage and Search results.
- Heading order skipped on Homepage.
- Cart and Search pages lack an `<h1>`.
- Content outside landmark regions on Homepage and Search.

---

## SEO

- View Cart page missing `<title>` (serious axe). Cart is typically `noindex`, but any accidental crawl or sharing of a cart URL will produce an untitled page.
- All three HTML samples show the same URL (`https://www.skechers.com/`) for Homepage, Log In, and Men category — URL capture appears to have failed for the latter two steps, making it impossible to verify canonical tags, `hreflang`, or structured data on those pages.
- No issues observed with `lang="en"` on pages where it is present.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set inline on `<html>` — a JS-computed custom property to work around the iOS Safari dynamic toolbar bug (100vh mismatch). The value is per-pixel unit, not percentage, and the floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) suggests it is computed directly from `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding. No functional impact but worth normalizing.
- No `<meta name="viewport">` tag visible in the truncated HTML samples — cannot confirm proper mobile scaling config from provided data.
- Mulish loaded via WebFont Loader's `wf-mulish-*` CSS classes — font is active by render time based on class names, so FOUT is handled.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate limiting — Kaspersky bot fingerprinting endpoint (5 hits)**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.391
```
The path pattern (`149e9513...`) is Kaspersky Fraud Prevention SDK's device fingerprinting beacon. The automated browser is being throttled by the bot-detection layer. Real users are not affected, but this confirms bot detection is actively deployed and will interfere with any automated monitoring or synthetic testing tools that don't handle the Kaspersky challenge flow.

**429 on `/cart/` (1 hit)**
This is the likely cause of the 326ms TTFB spike on View Cart. The cart endpoint itself is being rate-limited during the session, not just the fingerprinting beacon. This suggests the bot detection challenge was not resolved before the cart request fired, causing the server to reject or delay the response.

**CORS block on Stylitics stylesheet**
```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics outfit/hotspot widget CSS is being fetched as a cross-origin stylesheet without a CORS header on the response. The widget will render without its styles or fail to load entirely. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration — their CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or `*`) on the CSS asset.

**ERR_FAILED (1 hit)**
One resource fetch failed with a network-level error (not HTTP). Insufficient data to identify the resource from the provided logs.

## Recommendations

1. **Cart 429 / TTFB regression** — Investigate whether Kaspersky SDK challenge completion is required before cart API calls are made. If the fingerprinting beacon must succeed first, ensure the cart fetch is gated behind it or that the session token is pre-established. Priority: high — this is a real checkout latency risk for users on slow networks where the beacon takes time.

2. **Stylitics CORS** — File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` to `web-assets.stylitics.com` CSS responses, or self-host the stylesheet to avoid the cross-origin fetch entirely.

3. **View Cart missing `<title>` and `lang`** — If the cart is a full-page render (not an iframe), the template is missing these elements. If it is a modal/overlay injected into the DOM, update the JS that opens it to also set `document.title` and verify `<html lang>` is inherited. Priority: serious — impacts screen reader users.

4. **Duplicate script tags** — Remove duplicate `ytc.js` (Yahoo) and `creativecdn.com` registrations. These cause redundant network requests and double-fire for every pixel event.

5. **Capture LCP and CLS** — The audit has no paint or layout stability data. Instrument Real User Monitoring or synthetic Lighthouse runs to fill the gap before drawing performance conclusions.

6. **Resolve `aria-required-parent` (critical)** — Identify the homepage component generating an ARIA role without its required parent context (likely a widget or carousel) and fix the DOM structure.

7. **Consolidate landmark structure** — Audit the homepage template for duplicate `<main>` tags. A single `<main>` per page is an HTML spec requirement, not just an ARIA best practice.
