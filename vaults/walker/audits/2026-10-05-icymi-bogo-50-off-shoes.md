# ICYMI: BOGO 50% off shoes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. This is the third BOGO email I've had today. I got "BOGO 50% off starts…NOW!" and then "Gifting season starts… NOW!" earlier. This one is "ICYMI," which is a funny thing to say to a man who has already been told twice. The big orange "BOGO 50% OFF*" is easy to read, I'll give them that. But I know this banner now, so I scrolled instead of reading.
- The "Long Weekend, Big Deal" headline also confuses me. The fine print says the deal runs October 5–7, which is Monday through Wednesday. That's no long weekend I know of. After that the email turns into women's boots and clogs, and I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned slip-ons. I'd skim it, maybe tap "Shop Men," and not forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- The orange hero is huge and the BOGO 50% offer is clear in one glance.
- The nav and the "Shop Men / Women / Kids" links up top are big enough for my thumbs.
- The fine print lists Skechers, Clarks and other brands I cross-shop as qualifying brands. That tells me the deal covers brands I'd actually buy.
- The "Shop by Size" block with "Shop Wide Width" is useful to me. A wide foot is a real thing at my age.
- The green box with Price Matching and Expert Shoe Repair reassures me they're a store that stands behind what they sell.

## What's weak

- All six featured shoes are women's styles (clogs, flats, ankle boots, a fuzzy bootie). Nothing here is for a man like me.
- No comfort language anywhere. There's no cushioning, no arch support, nothing about slip-on or walking.
- The asterisk is doing a lot of work. The "1st pair only" label under one shoe and the long list of exclusions ("Final Sale," "Excluded from any promotions") make me wonder what I'd actually get.
- The terms are in tiny type at the bottom, and I'd have to squint or zoom to read them.
- "ICYMI" and "Long Weekend" don't match the dates in the fine print or the three BOGO sends I've already had today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show men's comfort styles in the product grid, like slip-ons, walking shoes and cushioned sneakers. A segmented send for male subscribers would do it. Add one line like "Soft cushion, all-day arch support."
- 2. Put the real deadline in the hero ("Ends Wednesday, Oct. 7") and drop "Long Weekend."
- 3. Send one BOGO email per promo window, not three. A reminder is fine, but it should add something new.
- 4. Give the fine print bigger type, and move the biggest exclusions up near the button.
- 5. Make the CTA say what it does, like "Shop men's BOGO shoes," instead of "Shop Now."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's comfort shoes: buy one, get one 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Walking and slip-on styles from Skechers, Clarks and more. Ends Oct. 7.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick two pairs, save on the second. In stores and online through Wednesday.`

# Technical Audit: DSW, "ICYMI: BOGO 50% off shoes"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one failed link probe (the "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" CTA returned HTTP 400), an unusually URL-heavy plain-text part (84.6% URLs), and malformed `<meta>` declarations in the head. The HTML was truncated and no headers were supplied, so the compliance, personalization and UTM checks are limited to what was visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (HTTP 400):** The link text is "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off".
  - URL: `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/imGazxJsvpvCxQdQg0fodQ~~/AAQRxRA~/t0ze9oRKb78f…`
  - It is a roughly 700-character click-tracking redirect.
  - The QA report's label field for this failure is empty.
  - A 400 from a tracked redirect can come from a malformed or truncated token, or from the probe itself. Re-test it in a real client before treating it as definitely broken.
  - If it is broken, subscribers hit an error page on the 15% SMS incentive.
- **Probe coverage:** Only 12 of 69 links were probed and 57 were skipped. The unsubscribe link is always included in the probe, and no failure was reported for it. The other 57 links are unverified.
- **Two tracking pixels:**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/qMVzKyyWttKcULzhbdBLAQ~~/…`
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=…&mi_ecmp=20261005_P261361_V1_WM-_Offer_Stan…`
  - Two open-tracking beacons from two different vendors or systems (ablink and mi.dsw.com) is redundant. Confirm both are intended.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed meta tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`.
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
  - Both are inert as written, so the charset is never declared in the document. Clients that rely on the HTTP header are fine, but any that sniff the document may mis-render special characters such as ®, ™ and curly quotes.
- **Missing alt text (2 images):** Both are the tracking pixels listed above, so the impact is low. Add `alt=""` to both so screen readers skip them.
- **Plain-text part:** 25,821 of 30,534 characters (84.6%) are URLs, well over the 70% threshold. The long ablink redirects are the likely cause.
  - Spam filters can penalize this ratio.
  - The text version is also hard to read for anyone who sees it.
- Dark mode (`color-scheme`, `prefers-color-scheme`) and viewport meta are present.
- Web fonts have fallbacks as far as the visible source shows.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible HTML contains no unrendered tokens such as `{{ }}` or `%%`. The source is truncated, so the body was not fully checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
No issues found in the supplied data.
- The unsubscribe link was probed and no failure was reported.
- The sender is `dsw@e.dsw.com` and the tracking domain is `ablink.dsw.com`, so both are on-brand and aligned.
- **Not verifiable from the supplied data:** SPF, DKIM, DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and the physical postal address in the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Every link goes through `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, and the destination URLs were not visible in the supplied data. Whether UTM parameters are present and consistent could not be assessed.
- The `mi_ecmp=20261005_P261361_V1_WM-_Offer_Stan…` value on the pixel suggests campaign tagging exists for the send. It is the only campaign identifier visible.
- The 400 on the opt-in CTA breaks the continuity of the 15% offer if the failure is real.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the opt-in redirect from a normal client. If it still returns 400, fix the link in the ESP before the next send, or replace it with a direct opt-in URL.
2. Fix the head meta tags:
   - `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`
   - `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Reduce the URL share of the plain-text part by shortening the tracked URLs or trimming the text version. Aim for under 70%.
5. Confirm both open-tracking pixels are intended and drop one if not.
6. Re-run QA with full HTML, raw headers and a wider probe. That would verify the other 57 links, UTM consistency, SPF/DKIM/DMARC and the one-click unsubscribe header.
