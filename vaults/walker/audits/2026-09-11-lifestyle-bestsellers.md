## Executive summary

- Well, now this is more like it. No young folks running through city streets at dawn, no marathon gear, no signature athlete I never heard of — just shoes. A whole grid of them, actually, with the model numbers printed right under each pair, which I appreciate. The hero shot up top is legs and shoes, not some fitness model's face, and that's fine by me.
- That said, it's still a lot of chunky dad-shoe styles (the 9060, the 530) that skew younger and trendier than what I'd actually wear to Home Depot. Nothing here says "cushioned," "arch support," or "all-day comfort" anywhere — I'm left guessing whether any of these are actually good for standing on concrete all day, which is my main criteria.
- I'd probably scroll through this one instead of deleting it immediately — the offer's clear up top and the product grid is easy to browse on my phone. I might click "Shop now" out of curiosity to see the 950 in black, but I wouldn't forward it to anybody. It's a catalog page, not a pitch.

## What's working

- The banner offer — "Spend $125 and get up to 25% off select styles" — is right at the top, clear, with a visible "Shop now" link, no hunting for it.
- Product grid shows the model numbers (9060, 530, 327, 740, 950) right under each shoe, so I know what I'm looking at without clicking in.
- No influencer or athlete cluttering up the hero — it's just shoes and a plain outfit, which reads more neutral to me than the marathon or Sydney Levrone emails.
- Layout is simple, one column, easy to scroll through on my phone without pinching to zoom.

## What's weak

- Not one word about comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere in this whole email — for a guy like me, that's the actual selling point, and it's just missing.
- These are mostly chunky retro styles (9060, 530, 327) that read more "sneakerhead" than "walk the dog comfortably" — nothing here looks like a 990 or a clean walking shoe.
- "New arrivals" section with Kids and Clothing tiles is dead weight for me — I'm not buying my grandkids' shoes or a jacket in this email.
- Two "More bestsellers" sections feels repetitive — I already saw six shoes, then it circles back to "more bestsellers" with another big image and another "Shop now."

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one line of comfort-focused copy near the top — something like "All-day cushioning, easy on-and-off" — because right now there's zero language addressing why a shoe is comfortable, just what it looks like.
- 2. Cut the "New arrivals" Kids/Clothing/All tiles from this send or split it into a separate email — it dilutes a footwear-focused email with stuff I don't care about.
- 3. Consolidate the two bestseller sections into one — showing the same six-plus shoes twice with two separate "Shop now" CTAs feels padded.
- 4. Call out which of these styles has the most cushioning or support directly on the product tile — even a small badge like "extra cushion" would help me pick faster.
