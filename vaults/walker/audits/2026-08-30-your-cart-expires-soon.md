## Executive summary

- Same pink floral D'Lites, "you liked these," "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button — same darn shoe, sixth time this week by my count. "You weren't wrong" is a strange thing to tell me about a shoe I never once clicked on. There's a men's slip-in buried down in the grid — the Altman-Murdock, gray, actually looks decent — but it's competing with kids' garage sneakers and a basketball shoe pre-order, so my eye never really lands anywhere useful.
- The one thing that actually makes me pause is that 20% off banner further down — "Cozy Fit & Glide Step," code UPGRADE. That's real money and it's plain English. But I have to scroll past a whole flower garden to get to it.
- I'm not clicking this one. Mark as read, maybe forward the 20% code to my wife since she still shops there.

## What's working

- The 20% off banner is clear — a number, a code, a "shop now" link, no games.
- Men's Slip-ins Altman-Murdock shows up in the grid, gray and simple, my kind of shoe.
- Afterpay/Klarna and "find a store" strip at the bottom is useful if I ever do want to go try something on.
- Layout's clean enough, nothing broken or overlapping that I can see.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites hero — again. Never in my cart, never will be, and "you weren't wrong" is just insulting at this point.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button sits under a shoe I never ordered — makes the whole email feel like it's not even about me.
- Product grid is a grab-bag — boys' sneakers, a basketball pre-order, women's Bobs — none of it says "we know what a 62-year-old guy wants."
- The real offer, 20% off comfort styles, is stuck two-thirds down the page instead of up top where I'd actually see it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the pink floral hero entirely for my account — lead with the 20% off comfort banner instead, that's the actual reason to open this.
- 2. Put the men's slip-in comfort shoe up top as the hero image, not buried in a six-pack grid with kids' sneakers.
- 3. Drop "you liked these" language when I've never once engaged with the item — it just proves the targeting's broken.
- 4. Trim the product grid down to styles that match past behavior instead of a random store-wide mix.
