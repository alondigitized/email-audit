# $15 OFF TODAY ONLY 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Nine. By my rough count, this is the ninth time DSW has hit my inbox in roughly three days, and now they've bumped the number from $10 to $15. I noticed that immediately — the offer's gone up, which tells me the $10 "midnight deadline" they screamed about yesterday wasn't the real last chance after all. When a store keeps rolling out "today only" deals day after day, I stop believing any of them.
- The countdown timer is bold, the Summer Savings banner is clean, and $15 off is a real number. But then I scroll down to the product grid and it's more of the same: heels, ballet flats, an animal print slide. I spotted one shoe that might be men's — a brown sneaker buried in the middle of the second row — and there's a SHOP MEN link under the headline, so they haven't completely forgotten I exist. The size chart running from 5 to 11 reads female to me; men's sizing in my world goes up to at least 13 or 14.
- I'd click the SHOP MEN link if I were already planning to buy, but this email alone wouldn't pull me off the couch. Mark as read, maybe — after going back to look at that wide-width button, which is the one thing here that actually speaks to how I shop.

## What's working

- **$15 is a bump from last send.** I noticed the escalation — it signals DSW knows the $10 pitch is losing steam, and this one at least feels like a stronger pull.
- **Countdown timer gives me a concrete deadline.** Six hours and change when this loaded; that's more precise than "midnight" language and I can actually feel the clock ticking.
- **SHOP WIDE WIDTH button is hiding a gem.** That one call-to-action matters more to my feet than any other line in this email. Wish they'd led with it.
- **SHOP MEN link is visible and above the product grid.** Easy enough for me to tap and get out of the women's section.
- **VIP section at the bottom is a real benefit reminder.** Free shipping and rewards — I like seeing that spelled out, not just assumed.

## What's weak

- **"BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" buries the lede.** Subject promises "$15 OFF" but the body immediately pivots to a tiered spend-to-save structure with code WAVES. That bait-and-switch feeling bothers me even if it's technically disclosed.
- **Six products in the hero grid; five are clearly women's.** I'm 62, I wear a men's 10.5 wide — I don't have a use for a leopard print kitten heel or a nude ballet flat.
- **The preview text adds nothing.** "Talk about a hot deal…" tells me exactly what the subject already told me, just fuzzier. Wasted real estate.
- **Fatigue is real.** Eight or nine of these in a week and I've stopped trusting "TODAY ONLY." If every day is the last day, no day is.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the men's comfort story.** Put a walking shoe, a Skechers Arch Fit, or a cushioned loafer in the hero. One men's shoe above the fold beats six women's shoes on the entire scroll.
- 2. **Kill the tiered language from the subject/hero.** "$15 off your first pair over $X" is cleaner and more honest than "$15 OFF" followed by "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE."
- 3. **Promote the WIDE WIDTH CTA up to the hero.** That one button is my reason to click — it should not be sitting below a size grid that starts at a women's 5.
- 4. **Rest the list.** Two more DSW emails this week and I'm hitting unsubscribe. Frequency is destroying the urgency they're trying to manufacture.
- 5. **Swap the preview text for something that earns the click.** Tell me what the deal actually covers — men's, comfort brands, clearance — instead of "hot deal" filler.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$15 off men's comfort shoes — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wide widths, walking shoes: $15 off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers, New Balance, Hoka — use code WAVES at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's wide widths included. Spend $X, save $15 — today only`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` (capital S) in all `@font-face` blocks** — CSS property names must be lowercase. Braze's editor appears to uppercase the first letter of `src:`. While most CSS parsers are case-insensitive, this is non-standard and will silently fail in strict parsers.

```css
/* All 10 @font-face blocks contain: */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
/* Should be: */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

**Missing `http-equiv` on two `<meta>` tags** — both are syntactically malformed:

```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```

Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4, Success Criterion level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` values** in the reset block:

```css
* {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;  /* set first */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;      /* overrides with none — incorrect */
  text-size-adjust: none;          /* same problem on standard property */
}
```

`none` disables text scaling entirely. Both non-prefixed and `-ms-` values should be `100%`.

**Single font format per family, no `woff2`** — Futura PT serves only `.woff`; HelveticaNow serves only `.ttf`. Modern clients (Gmail app, Apple Mail) prefer `woff2` for smaller payloads and faster render. TTF with no `woff2` fallback means slower font load for HelveticaNow on mobile.

**Inconsistent cache-busting** — HelveticaNow font URLs carry a `?1755182939` timestamp; Futura PT URLs do not. If Futura PT fonts are updated, clients with warm caches will not pick up the new files.
