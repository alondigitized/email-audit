# Stack the summer savings ☀️💰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Look, Famous Footwear is one of my regular stops — real stores, fair prices, and when they run a deal I usually pay attention. The $20 off a $100 purchase caught my eye about two seconds in, and that's a reasonable deal for me. I've spent $100 at Famous Footwear more times than I can count.
- But then the whole email pivots to the Adidas Samba. I'm a 62-year-old guy who needs cushioning, arch support, and ideally something I can get on and off without bending over. The Samba is a flat canvas sneaker that college kids are wearing right now because it's having a moment. It's not bad-looking, but it has nothing to do with my feet or my back. The entire hero section — big photo, big type, prominent placement — is aimed at someone 30 years younger than me.
- And then there's the preheader. Raw tracking URL. Again. I called this out on the May 16th send. I called it out before that. Famous Footwear has been doing this for months and it still isn't fixed. At this point it's just part of how their emails look, and that's a bad look. I'd click the deal if I needed shoes this week, but I wouldn't forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- **The $20 off $100+ hero offer is up front and legible** — I didn't have to scroll or hunt for the discount, it's right there in a clean box at the top.
- **"Starts now!" + "Limited time!" gives the offer energy** without resorting to all-caps screaming — feels like a real promotion, not desperation.
- **The general shoe grid below the Samba section** shows a range of styles and what looks like sale pricing — gives me something to browse if I scroll.
- **Familiar sender name and layout** — I know what a Famous Footwear email looks like and this one doesn't surprise me. That's a small but real form of trust.

## What's weak

- **The Samba is the wrong hero for me entirely** — a flat, fashion-forward sneaker aimed at trend-chasers is not where my wallet lives. If the offer is site-wide, lead with comfort or walking shoes, not youth culture.
- **Preheader is a raw tracking URL — same bug, same brand, same frustration** — I've now seen this from Famous Footwear on at least four sends. At this point it looks like they just don't care.
- **"Check back this Friday!" is a tease with no payoff** — I see a product image but no context on what the deal actually is. Either tell me what's coming or don't mention it.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere in the visible copy** — zero mention of cushioning, arch support, wide widths, slip-on options. That's the whole conversation for someone like me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** This is a recurring broken window. One line of preview copy — "Take $20 off shoes built for summer comfort" — would outperform a tracking URL every single time and it costs nothing to fix.
- 2. **Swap the Samba hero for a comfort-category lead** — even a generic "Skechers, New Balance, Hoka — $20 off when you spend $100" framing would land better with my half of the customer base.
- 3. **Add a single comfort-cue to the offer box** — something like "Cushioned walkers, slip-ons, and more" right under the discount would tell me the deal applies to what I actually buy.
- 4. **Give "Check back this Friday!" a specific hook** — "Up to 50% off Skechers starts Friday" is a reason to come back. "Check back this Friday!" is filler.
- 5. **Lean into the brand history** — I've bought from Famous Footwear before. A line like "Your kind of deal is here" or a nod to the shoe categories I browse would make me feel like a known customer, not a stranger.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Stack the summer savings" (2026-06-04)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but carries a systemic accessibility gap (26 images with empty `alt` attributes) and one mixed-content violation. No broken links were detected, though all 33 click-redirect URLs were unprobed.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**33 tracking links skipped** — all click-links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` with opaque `?qs=` parameters. This is expected for Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget), but it means:
- No independent verification that final destinations resolve correctly.
- If SFMC link wrapping misconfigures a destination URL, it won't surface until clicks arrive.

**No broken or malformed `href` values** detected in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: 26 images have empty `alt=""`**

Every product/content image uses `alt=""`. This includes the logo (`3b6a7dc1`), hero images, product shots, and UI elements (divider `4ccff4ad` appears 4 times). Screen readers skip all of them silently; image-off rendering (Outlook default, many corporate clients) leaves the email content-free.

The logo image specifically should carry `alt="Famous Footwear"` at minimum.

**Mixed-content violation (HTTP asset):**
```
http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=...
```
This LiveContent image (likely a dynamic/live content block from SFMC) is served over HTTP. Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) will either block the image silently or show a security warning. It should be `https://`.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is malformed** — missing the `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<!-- current (broken) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />

<!-- correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no effect; charset declaration relies entirely on the server/MIME header.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta is non-standard (`0` vs `no`). Most parsers handle it, but `user-scalable=no` is the correct value per spec.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `[FIRST NAME]`) visible in the truncated source. The preheader is static: `"Save big with adidas under $50."` — no personalization applied there.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe:** The truncated source does not show the footer. A full-source audit is needed to confirm physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are present. The QA summary reports 0 hard issues and 86% pass rate, suggesting the unsubscribe infrastructure is present, but this should be verified against the complete HTML.

**Sender authentication:** Sending domain is `email.famousfootwear.com`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML alone — no authentication headers were included in the QA input. Should be verified at the MTA level.

**Open pixel** (`open.aspx`) is present and correctly sized at 1×1px with `alt=""`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected** on any click-through URLs in the visible source. All links use the SFMC redirect wrapper (`click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...`), which may or may not append UTMs at redirect time. If UTMs are not injected by the redirect handler, GA4/analytics will attribute all traffic as direct rather than email.

This needs to be confirmed by following one redirect chain to its final destination URL and checking for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` parameters.
