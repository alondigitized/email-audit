# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-31**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked skechers.com on my phone and got as far as picking a shoe I'd consider, the Slip-ins Contour Foam Cozy Fit. Then I got stuck. Add to Cart didn't add anything, because I hadn't picked a size. The cart page then came up as a completely blank white screen. I never saw a login form, and I never saw a popup. Steps 2 and 3 look identical to the homepage.

The browsing part is decent. The menu is clear and the product page is readable. The one step that makes Skechers money, getting the shoe into the cart, failed for me. Search for "comfort shoes" works, but the first results lean toward women's styles.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday's 3/10, so I see no improvement. I only have yesterday's number, not the screenshots, so I can't say exactly what changed. Today's biggest problem is the blank cart screen. A shopper who gets that screen leaves.

## 3. What's Working

- **The Men menu is easy to use.** The big, well-spaced rows are Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. Each has an arrow, and there's a clear Back and X. I could tap those without trouble.
- **"Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cushioning" sit in a bar right under the header.** That's what I shop for, and it's on the first screen. The bar cuts off at "Max Cus…" and I'd have to swipe sideways to see the rest.
- **The product page is readable.** The price ($88.00) is big and bold, and there are 4.5 stars from 1,954 reviews. That many reviews makes me trust it. The "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge is on the photo.
- **Size buttons are large.** They're easy to tap with a thumb, and sold-out sizes are crossed out with a bell icon. The "Please select a size" message is in red with a warning icon, so I'd know what went wrong.
- **The Add to Cart button is big, dark blue, and clearly the main action.**
- **Search found "Slip-ins: Contour Foam – Cozy Fit" and "Relaxed Fit"** shoes. That's on topic for comfort. It says 1,653 results.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is a blank white page (Step 8).** There's no header, no message, and no "your cart is empty." I can't tell whether the site is broken or still loading. This is the worst problem on the site.
- **The cart badge stayed at 0 the whole time.** That's partly my doing, since I never chose a size. But the page never helped me get there. The size chooser sits below the fold, and the "Please select a size" message only appeared after I'd already tried to add the shoe.
- **The "Dismiss Popups" and "Log In" steps looked just like the homepage.** I never saw a login screen, so I can't say whether the site knows me. Everything says "Login to Unlock Mystery Discount." It's on the homepage banner, the product page, and under every search result. I'm not logged in, so none of it feels personal.
- **The Mystery Deal wording is confusing.** I don't know what the discount is until I log in. As a 62-year-old, I just want the price. The black "trick or treat?" banner is Halloween-themed, but it's October 31st, so the season is nearly over.
- **The homepage hero is two models in skull-print hoodies and sneakers.** It's pretty, but it has nothing to do with comfort, arch support, or slip-ons. The first screen doesn't speak to me, apart from that small tab bar.
- **The Men's Shoes list shows a Day of the Dead "UNO" sneaker and a colorful Britto collab block** in the first two rows. That's fashion, not comfort. The 811 results have no obvious "Comfort" or "Slip-ins" starting point.
- **Search puts women's shoes first.** The first two results are labeled "Women's." I don't know if the site can tell I'm a man, and it didn't use that.
- **There's no comparison with Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance anywhere, and no price-vs-quality information.** That's what I'd want, but I wouldn't expect it from Skechers. The reviews and star ratings are the closest thing.
- **The filter bar is cut off.** On search, "Size" and "Wi…" for Width run off the right edge. Width matters for my feet.
- **The text is small in places.** The "New to Sale! Up To 50% OFF" strip, the product-card text, and the Klarna/Afterpay line are all small. I'd have to zoom to read the fine print.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page now.** If it's loading, show a spinner. If it's empty, say so, and add a "Continue Shopping" button. A blank page loses sales.
2. **Add the size picker to the first screen of the product page, or let the size error scroll me to it.** On a phone, I shouldn't have to scroll past the photo to find out I needed a size.
3. **Show the actual Mystery Discount price, or drop the login-for-a-discount line.** Repeating a red "Login to Unlock" under every product feels like a trick.
4. **Make the homepage speak to comfort.** Put a "Slip-ins / Arch Fit / Wide Fit" block with real shoes above the lifestyle photo. Make those category tabs wrap or get larger instead of cutting off.
5. **Use the shopper's gender and past browsing.** Showing men's results first on a men's search, and a "Welcome back" when logged in, would help.
6. **Make the filter bar fit the screen**, with Width visible, since fit is a big deal for me.
7. **Increase the font size** on prices, promo strips, and filter labels for older shoppers.
8. **Make the step that dismisses popups and the login step actually do something**, or report clearly when they didn't. Today they just repeat the homepage.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right shoes for me, and the menu and product page are good. But if I can't get a shoe into a cart and the cart page is blank, I'll go to Hoka or Brooks, or to Famous Footwear. It's a 3/10 again, with no change from yesterday, until the cart works.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The audit is partly invalid because of bot-mitigation blocking and incomplete instrumentation.

- **Kasada blocking:** The Kasada bot-defense fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`) returned HTTP 429 six times. `/cart/` also returned 429. The automated session was probably throttled or challenged. The cart step was likely evaluated against an error or interstitial page, not the real cart.
- **Navigation did not advance:** The HTML samples for Homepage, Log In, and Men category all report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and the markup is the same homepage. These steps probably either did not navigate or recorded the URL before the route changed. The samples do not show Log In or Men-category pages being rendered. Findings for those steps may really be homepage findings.
- **Web Vitals are incomplete:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any step, so only TTFB is available.
- **Page weight is third-party heavy:** The sampled head contains dozens of async third-party tag scripts.

## Accessibility

Axe reported 18 violations, 10 on Homepage, 4 on View Cart, and 4 on Search.

**Critical**
- `button-name` (Homepage): one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA roles are used without their required parent roles.

**Serious**
- `link-name` (Homepage): links have no discernible text.
- `document-title` (View Cart): the page has no `<title>`.
- `html-has-lang` (View Cart): the `<html>` element has no `lang` attribute. The homepage has `lang="en"`, so the cart page was probably not the real cart template. It was probably a 429 or challenge response (see Console & Network Errors).

**Moderate**
- Landmark structure problems on Homepage and Search:
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-unique`
  - `region`
- `page-has-heading-one` (Homepage, View Cart): no `<h1>`.
- `landmark-one-main` (View Cart): no `<main>` landmark.

**Minor**
- `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search): a role attribute is not valid for its element.

**Patterns**
- Duplicate or nested `<main>` on Homepage suggests injected or third-party markup, or nested templates.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` and `landmark-unique` recur on Homepage and Search, so they likely come from shared template components.

## SEO

- **Homepage** has `lang="en"`, but it has no `<h1>`.
- **View Cart** has no `<title>` and no `lang`. This is likely from the 429 response and not a real template defect. Cart pages are normally `noindex` anyway, so the SEO impact is low.
- **Not verifiable from the samples:** the truncated HTML does not include meta description, canonical, Open Graph, or structured data. Nothing can be concluded about them.
- **Missing step coverage:** the Log In and Men category samples show the homepage URL, so category-page SEO was not evaluated. This includes titles, canonicals, and faceted-navigation handling.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** values are good: 24–107ms on most steps and 351–352ms on Homepage, Log In, and Men category.
  - The 351–352ms steps are consistent with uncached or edge-miss responses. The sub-30ms steps (Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart, Search) suggest cache hits or client-side/SPA transitions that never made a real document request.
  - The 24–27ms values are unlikely for full cold navigations through bot protection.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured on any step. Core Web Vitals could not be assessed.
- **Third-party load:** the head injects many tag-manager and ad-tech scripts. All are `async`, so they should not block parsing, but they still compete for main-thread and network time on mobile.
  - **Tags and trackers:** GTM, GA4/gtag, Google Ads, Floodlight, TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Yahoo, Facebook, Snap, Reddit, Nextdoor, Taboola, RTB House, Impact, Tune, and Monetate.
  - **Session and survey tools:** Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, and Pulse Insights.
  - **Fonts:** a web-font loader (`webfontloader.js` from cdnjs) is loaded for Mulish. This adds a third-party round trip before fonts apply (FOIT/FOUT risk).
- **GTM environment:** the GTM snippet references `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth`. This is a non-default GTM environment, which may be a staging or preview container served in production.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` custom property is set inline to `6.64px`, which implies a very small viewport height (~664px). This is a JS-computed viewport-height workaround and can cause layout shift on mobile browsers when the URL bar collapses.
- No viewport meta or touch-target data is in the truncated source, so those cannot be assessed.
- `data-passkey-available="true"` indicates WebAuthn is detected on the device.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (7)**
- 6 × `429` on `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`. This is the Kasada client fingerprint endpoint.
- 1 × `429` on `/cart/`, a document-level failure.
- The cart 429 explains the View Cart axe results: missing title, missing `lang`, and no main landmark are what an error or challenge page looks like.
- The automation traffic is being rate-limited or blocked. The cart step cannot be treated as a valid test of the real cart.

**Console (27)**
The list shows 10 entries:
- `Failed to load resource: 429` (3×), the same Kasada/cart failures.
- `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (5×). A script registers an `unload` handler, which is deprecated and blocked by Permissions-Policy. It is almost certainly a third-party tag. It also prevents bfcache eligibility.
- `Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (2×). This comes from Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer API being called with unsupported language options. It is probably from a script or extension, not skechers.com first-party code.

The 17 remaining console entries were not provided.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit:** use an allow-listed IP, header, or test mode for the Kasada/bot-defense layer. Confirm each step lands on its intended URL; Log In and Men category currently report the homepage URL. Treat the View Cart results as unreliable until then.
2. **Capture LCP and CLS:** fix the PerformanceObserver injection or timing. Record them after load, with `buffered: true`.
3. **Fix the critical a11y defects:** give every icon-only button and link an accessible name (`aria-label` or visually hidden text). Correct the ARIA parent/child role structures.
4. **Fix landmarks:** keep a single top-level `<main>`, and add a single `<h1>` per page. Give repeated `nav`/`aside` landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put content inside landmarks.
5. **Confirm the real cart template:** check that it has `<title>`, `lang`, and `<main>` once the 429 is out of the way.
6. **Remove `unload` handlers:** find which third-party script registers them and replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`. This restores bfcache eligibility.
7. **Audit third-party tags:** consolidate or defer low-value ones to post-interaction or idle. Self-host the web fonts, or use `font-display: swap` with preload, to remove the cdnjs dependency.
8. **Verify the GTM environment:** make sure production is not loading `env-1` (preview/staging) container parameters.
