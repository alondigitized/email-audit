# Happening now: Up to 65% off sandals
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. That's where I've lost count this week with DSW — and this one shows up with the same sandals sale I've been seeing since Monday, just wearing a new subject line. "Happening now" — okay, fine, I opened it. Thirty seconds later I'm looking at a grid of strappy heels and slide sandals aimed squarely at a woman half my age, a CTA that literally says "SHOP ALL WOMEN," and not one mention of anything that matters to me: cushioning, arch support, a men's walking sandal, a wide-width that fits my foot.
- The deal is real — 65% off is real money and DSW is a store I actually walk into. The "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button caught my eye because my feet are wide and I've been burned by narrow shoes before. But by the time I scrolled down to it, I'd already clocked that everything above the fold was women's product shot after women's product shot. The wide-width button felt like an afterthought, not a promise made to me.
- I'd mark it read and move on. I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. If DSW has a men's walking sandal at 65% off this week, I don't know about it, and they made no effort to tell me.

## What's working

- **65% off is a genuine number** — visible immediately under the banner, no fine-print ambiguity in the hero.
- **"SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button** — that's the one thing here that speaks to me; wide feet are real and most brands pretend they don't exist.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — renders fine on a phone, text is legible, nothing is overlapping or clipped.
- **VIP rewards callout** — buried at the bottom but it's there; I do have a DSW account and "3% back + free shipping" is a real perk worth seeing.
- **Size range displayed** — sizes 5 through 11 on one scroll means I can at least see if my size exists before clicking anywhere.

## What's weak

- **Every single product shot is a women's sandal** — strappy heels, platform slides, floral toe-rings; not one men's shoe in sight.
- **"SHOP ALL WOMEN" is the secondary CTA** — that's not a store section I'm shopping, and seeing it printed big tells me DSW forgot who they were emailing.
- **"Today feels like a good day to shop"** — that preview line is the kind of copy a brand writes when they have nothing to say; it tells me nothing about why today is different from yesterday's identical email.
- **Thirteen emails in one week** — I keep a rough count and DSW has been relentless; this cadence is burning whatever goodwill the rewards program built.
- **No comfort signal anywhere** — no mention of cushioning, arch support, or anything a 62-year-old man standing on a concrete floor would care about; just "styles within your budget."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of the women's-only send** — if this email can't show me one men's sandal or walking shoe, it shouldn't land in my inbox. Send it to the women; send me a version with Hoka, New Balance, or Skechers Sport Sandals.
- 2. **Lead the hero with comfort language** — "Wide-width. All-day cushion. Up to 65% off" beats "THE PRICE IS RIGHT" for someone like me who shops with his feet, not his eyes.
- 3. **Move "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" above the product grid** — right now it's buried after a dozen women's product tiles; if that's my hook, put it high.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence** — one email every two days this week would have felt like a brand; every four hours feels like a retailer in trouble.
- 5. **Give the preheader something real** — tie it to the offer ("Your size is in stock — 65% off ends tonight") instead of a throwaway mood line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's sandals: up to 65% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wide-width sandals, 65% off — your size is in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide fits, walking cushion, summer prices — shop men's now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `65% off ends soon — your size might not last`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — invalid CSS property casing in `@font-face`:**

All 10 `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` (capital S) instead of the required lowercase `src:`. This is an invalid CSS property name and will cause the declaration to be silently dropped in strict CSS parsers (including several webmail clients). Fallback system fonts will render instead.

```css
/* All 10 @font-face blocks contain: */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
/* Should be: */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

**Confirmed issue — contradictory text-size-adjust directives:**

The global `*` reset block simultaneously sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` (preserve browser scaling) and `text-size-adjust: none` (suppress scaling). These are contradictory and produce undefined behavior on Safari/iOS:

```css
* {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* line 1 */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;       /* this is fine — targets IE only */
  text-size-adjust: none;           /* conflicts with webkit line above */
}
```

**Cannot fully audit** — image `alt` text, color contrast ratios, and link tap-target sizing require full body HTML.
