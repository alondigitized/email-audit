# Back-to-school-ready kicks 👟
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- My first thought scrolling this one: I am not who this email was made for. It's a back-to-school blast — kid in a backpack, teenager on the floor lacing up sneakers, "first-day approved," the whole nine yards. Fine for a mom with three kids, but I retired a while back and the last first day of school I dealt with was my grandkids', and I sure didn't buy their shoes off a DSW email.
- That said, DSW usually knows how to needle me with a real number, and buried down past all the backpack talk there is a 25% off sitewide code, which is the kind of thing that'll get me to click if I already need shoes. But compared to yesterday's "40% off rarely-on-sale brands" hitting Nike, New Balance, Birkenstock — brands I actually cross-shop — this one feels like it landed in the wrong inbox.
- I'd scroll past this one. Not deleting it, not forwarding it to anybody — my buddies and I aren't exactly shopping for backpacks. If I circle back it's for the 25% code, not because anything here spoke to my feet.

## What's working

- The 25% off code (SCHOOLDAY) is a real, clear number once you get to it — I know exactly what to do with that.
- "Shop by Size" grid with Wide Width and Wide Calf call-outs — that's actually useful to a guy my age with wide feet, more than most emails bother to mention.
- Layout's clean, no broken images or garbled text, easy enough to read on the phone even with all the sections.
- VIP rewards footer reminds me DSW knows I'm a member — 5% back, free shipping, birthday gift, that stuff registers.

## What's weak

- Every model in this email is a kid, a teenager, or a young mom — nothing here signals "this is for you, Walker."
- The whole theme is backpacks and "first-day approved" — zero mention of comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease, which is the only thing that actually moves me to buy a shoe.
- The 25% off offer is way down the page — I'd have scrolled past on my phone before I even found the reason to keep reading.
- Only Nike and adidas shown in the hero shots — no Skechers, Hoka, Brooks, the comfort brands I actually compare.
- "Regular-price styles only" restriction on the 25% off feels like the fine print that always trims down the deal once you're in the store.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this list — send back-to-school to parents, and send guys like me a comfort-brand promo instead. One email trying to do both just misses everybody.
- 2. Move the 25% off code up near the top, above the backpack section, so it's visible without scrolling.
- 3. If DSW wants my click, put a comfort/arch-support shoe — Skechers Arch Fit, Hoka, New Balance — front and center instead of leading with kids' sneakers.
- 4. Keep the Wide Width/Wide Calf callout but move it higher too — that's a real differentiator for me and it's currently buried near the bottom.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Comfort kicks, 25% off. No kids required.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Slip-on, cushioned, arch support — all 25% off with code SCHOOLDAY.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your wide-width favorites just got 25% cheaper.`
