## Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Walker Miles | 62 | Comfort-First Shopper | June 5, 2026**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Today's run was a mixed bag — and honestly, a frustrating one. The navigation menu is genuinely good for someone my age, and getting to the Hands Free Slip-ins section was smoother than I expected. But then the wheels came off. The cart page loaded completely blank. Nothing. A white screen where my shoe should've been waiting for me to check out. And when I searched "comfort shoes," the first two results were women's shoes — even though I'm logged in. Skechers knows I'm a man. It just doesn't act like it. The deal is good (BOGO 50% off), the product itself looks comfortable, but I can't buy what I can't cart. Same frustration, different day.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The navigation improved in feel, but the cart is broken and search personalization failed. A site that can't complete a transaction scores no higher than a 4, regardless of anything else. The underlying comfort story is there — Skechers just keeps fumbling the execution.

---

## 3. What's Working

**The Men's menu is actually well built.** When I tapped into the men's section, I got a clean full-screen menu with large text, generous tap targets, and — right there at the bottom — "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" as quick links. That's exactly what I need. I didn't have to hunt. Whoever laid that out was thinking about me.

**The BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** The blue banner at the top, the red tag on the product page, the "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" copy — it's consistent and prominent. That's the kind of math that makes me pay attention. I appreciate that it carries through from homepage to product page without disappearing.

**The product page text is readable.** The name, the price ($88.00), the deal callout — all legible without zooming. No squinting required.

**The ADD TO CART button is a big blue bar pinned to the bottom of the screen.** That's exactly right. I know where to tap. No guessing.

**The size grid is clear and tappable.** Each size button is large enough that I'm not worried about accidentally hitting the wrong one. The "Please select a size" warning in red is visible and helpful — it stops me from adding nothing to cart by mistake.

**299 results in Hands Free Slip-ins.** Good selection. I know they have options.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is blank. Completely blank.** Step 8 loaded a white screen — no product, no total, no checkout button, nothing. This is a transaction-killing bug. If I were shopping for real, I would've closed the browser and gone to DSW. This is the single most damaging thing on the site right now and it's the same problem class as yesterday.

**Search returned women's shoes.** I typed "comfort shoes" and the first two results — Arch Fit Arcade See Ya There and Contour Foam — both say "Women's." I'm logged in. Skechers has my account. There's no reason the top of a 1,475-result search page should be showing me women's product when I'm a male member. That's a personalization failure that makes me feel invisible.

**The homepage hero doesn't speak to me.** "GAME ON!" with bright red, green, and blue court shoes. That's a 25-year-old's ad. Where's the walking shoe? The Arch Fit? The "your feet deserve a break after 60 years"? I'm not who they're talking to on that front page, and that disconnect starts the visit on the wrong foot.

**No reviews on the product.** "No Reviews" on a $88 shoe is a yellow flag. I don't buy shoes I can't read about first. Hoka and New Balance both show hundreds of reviews on their comparable products. Skechers needs to seed this page or I'm going to cross-shop somewhere that has social proof.

**Login didn't visibly confirm anything.** After the login step, the page looked identical to before — same homepage, same "Login" link in the member banner. If I'm now logged in, tell me. Show my name. Show my points balance. Right now it feels like nothing happened.

**1,475 search results with no men's pre-filter.** That's an overwhelming number, and the first things shown are women's styles. I need Skechers to help me narrow this down, not dump everything at me and let me figure it out.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is priority one. A blank cart is a lost sale. There's no softer way to say it.

2. **Apply gender filtering to search results for logged-in users.** If I'm in my account and I search "comfort shoes," show me men's results by default. This is table-stakes personalization.

3. **Swap the homepage hero for comfort-first messaging.** A walking shoe on pavement. A man my age smiling. Copy that mentions cushioning, arch support, or slip-ins. The athletic court shoe hero is not converting my demographic.

4. **Show post-login confirmation.** After I log in, greet me: "Welcome back, Walker — you have X points." Make me feel like the site knows me.

5. **Seed reviews on new products.** Fifty reviews at launch would make a product like the Kiroh credible. Zero reviews makes me hesitate and cross-shop.

6. **Surface the "Comfort Technologies" menu item more prominently.** It's in the men's nav but buried mid-list. A 62-year-old shopping for comfort wants to tap that first.

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are decent — the menu is clean, the deals are clear, the size selector works fine. But you can't sell me a shoe when the cart won't load, and you can't call this a personalized experience when a logged-in male sees women's shoes at the top of his search. Until those two are fixed, Skechers is leaving real money on the table with buyers like me. Score holds at **4/10**. No regression from yesterday, no meaningful improvement either.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware) platform. Eight-step mobile journey reveals three distinct problem clusters: a Kasada bot-protection SDK rate-limiting its own fingerprint endpoint, a critical View Cart 429 that blocks checkout, and a View Cart page with incomplete document structure (missing `<title>` and `lang`). LCP and CLS are unsampled across all steps — performance assessment is partial.

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` **(critical)** — orphaned ARIA role elements. Likely a carousel or tab widget whose inner items render outside their required parent container. Breaks screen reader traversal.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` + `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-unique` — multiple `<main>` elements present, at least one nested. SFCC's Monetate or A/B injection is the likely culprit inserting a second document region.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` — `<aside>` nested inside another landmark.
- `heading-order` — heading hierarchy breaks (e.g., h3 before h2). Common in CMS-assembled template pages.
- `region` — page content outside all landmarks.
- `aria-allowed-role` **(minor)** — role applied to element type that doesn't permit it.

**View Cart (4 violations — most severe):**
- `document-title` **(serious)** — `<title>` is empty or absent. Combined with the 429 rate-limit on `/cart/`, this page is likely rendering a partial/error shell rather than the real cart DOM.
- `html-has-lang` **(serious)** — `<html>` has no `lang` attribute on the cart page. The homepage HTML shows `lang="en"`, so this is a cart-specific rendering failure, not a sitewide omission.
- `landmark-one-main` + `page-has-heading-one` — no `<main>` and no `<h1>`, consistent with an error/loading shell being captured.

**Search (3 violations):** Same landmark nesting pattern as homepage — `<aside>` not top-level, duplicate landmarks, content outside landmarks. Likely the same template component issue.

---

## SEO

- **Placeholder GA4 property** — `G-1234567890` fires alongside the real `G-XZTV9LQ9DQ` on every page. This is a GTM misconfiguration that sends duplicate pageview events to a non-existent property, introducing noise and potential data loss if a real property is ever assigned that ID.
- **View Cart missing `<title>`** — cart pages are typically `noindex`, but a missing title still breaks tab labeling and any non-headless crawl.
- **Duplicate script tags** — `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` injected twice on Men category; `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` injected twice on every page. Both load synchronously-registered third-party pixels twice, doubling their event firing.

---

## Performance

TTFB is within acceptable range on most steps. View Cart spikes to 134ms vs. 27–31ms baseline — consistent with a server-side 429 adding retry/timeout overhead before returning a degraded response.

**LCP and CLS are not captured** — the most user-impactful metrics are missing. Cannot assess rendering performance or layout stability without them.

**Third-party script payload (observed on every page):**
TikTok pixel (3 files), Pinterest, GTM, GA4, Bing, Reddit, Quantum Metric, Monetate (2 loaders), Snapchat, Facebook Pixel, Nextdoor, Taboola, Bloomreach (brsrvr), Impact, Tune/Go2SDK, PulseInsights, Stape, WebFontLoader — **19+ distinct third-party script origins** loaded on every page navigation. Each is `async` but still contends for network and main-thread parse time, particularly on mid-range mobile hardware.

**WebFontLoader v1.6.28** (from cdnjs) loads 6 Mulish weights. The `wf-*-active` classes on `<html>` indicate it completed, but the dependency on an external CDN-hosted loader is an unnecessary indirection.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set via JS — this is a correct workaround for the iOS Safari `100vh` viewport bug (sets 1vh = window.innerHeight/100). The floating-point tail is harmless.
- `vice-managed-src` attributes on ad pixel scripts indicate a consent management platform (CMP) is deferring script injection until consent is given. This is correct behavior for GDPR/CCPA compliance on mobile.
- No `<viewport>` meta is visible in the truncated source; assumed present but not confirmed.
- Six Mulish font weights loaded sitewide — mobile connections will feel this. Subsetting or reducing to 3–4 weights would reduce font payload.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 storm on Kasada fingerprint endpoint (6 occurrences):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.443
```
This is the Kasada KPSDK bot-protection fingerprint challenge endpoint. The SDK is rate-limiting its own polling requests — likely because the session token is not being accepted and the SDK retries in a tight loop without backoff. This is a Kasada configuration or SDK version issue on Skechers' side.

**429 on `/cart/`:**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself is rate-limited. Given Kasada's fingerprinting is failing, the WAF/bot-protection layer may be classifying the automated session as a bot and blocking the cart route. This is a hard conversion blocker in an automated test environment — and a risk signal that misconfigured Kasada settings could affect real users who trigger the same fingerprint failure path.

**CORS block on Stylitics stylesheet:**
```
https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=777c217f34
```
Stylitics (outfit recommendation widget) CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on its stylesheet. This breaks the widget's styling. Fix is on Stylitics' CDN configuration — Skechers needs to raise this with their Stylitics account team. Versioned URL (`777c217f34`) suggests a recent deploy may have dropped the CORS header.

**`net::ERR_FAILED`:** One unresolved resource load failure — insufficient detail in the provided data to identify the origin URL.
