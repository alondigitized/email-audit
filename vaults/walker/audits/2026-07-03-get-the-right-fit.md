# Get the right fit
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- The subject line "Get the right fit" had me nodding before I even tapped it — fit is everything at my age, and wide sizes are something I've hunted for in every store from DSW to the HOKA site itself. So I was actually *hopeful* opening this one.
- Inside, the hero delivers. "The right fit means every walk, run or full day on the job feels better from start to finish" — that sounds like it was written for a guy with a wide right foot and a long day ahead, not a marathoner. Seeing the Bondi 9 and Gaviota 6 in the Men's column is the right answer: cushion and stability, both of them. HOKA has been slowly finding its way back to me after a trail-and-performance heavy stretch through June, and the "Now walk it out" send on 06-26 started that pivot. This one keeps it going.
- But the bottom third loses me. "New Bondi 7 colors" is a color story, and two days ago they sent me the whole "New blues" email (07-01) which was *also* a color story. I feel like I just saw this movie. I'd probably tap the wide-sizes grid before closing the app — but I wouldn't forward this to a friend. Nothing here is urgent enough to share.

## What's working

- **Wide sizing is exactly my language** — hero copy calling out Regular, Wide, and X-Wide addresses a real frustration I have every time I shop for comfort shoes. This feels targeted.
- **Bondi 9 and Gaviota 6 are the right shoes to anchor the grid** — max cushion and stability are my priorities; seeing them labeled Men's front-and-center is a good call.
- **"Shop Wide Sizes" CTA is specific** — not "Discover" or "Learn More," tells me exactly where I'm landing.
- **Free expedited shipping banner is visible immediately** — right at the top, impossible to miss even if the $350 threshold is steep.
- **Clean layout, large images** — easy to scan on a phone without zooming; the hierarchy flows naturally.

## What's weak

- **"New Bondi 7 colors" undercuts the comfort narrative** — HOKA ran a color-drop email two days ago ("New blues," 07-01). Another color story in the same send makes it feel like two different teams are talking to me.
- **No price off anything** — free shipping on $350 orders is a thin incentive. I'm not buying three pairs at once. A "10% off wide-width styles" callout would've pushed me to the cart.
- **The Mach 7 feels out of place** — it's a performance trainer in a comfort-fit email. When I see "Mach" I think speed, not support.
- **No deadline, no urgency** — "Shop Wide Sizes" is an invitation to act any day. Nothing in this email tells me today matters more than next Tuesday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the "New Bondi 7 colors" section or hold it for its own send** — it muddies the comfort/fit message and repeats the color story from 48 hours ago. Earn back that real estate with a second comfort shoe or a sizing guide callout.
- 2. **Add a real price incentive** — even "10% off wide-width styles, today only" doubles click urgency. Free shipping at $350 is too far up the funnel to move me.
- 3. **Swap the Mach 7 for the Clifton or Transport** — a walking/everyday shoe that reinforces the fit theme, not a performance racer.
- 4. **Drop one customer review quote in the hero** — something like "Finally found a wide shoe that doesn't squeeze" does real work for a guy who's been burned by sizing before.
- 5. **Add a credible deadline** — even "Wide styles ship free through Sunday" gives me a reason to tap now.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your wide-fit HOKA is in stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Regular, Wide, and X-Wide — cushion without the squeeze`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bondi 9 and Gaviota 6 in wide widths — free expedited shipping on $350+`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tag** | Two declarations present — first: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`; second: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes`. Some clients may apply only the first, silently dropping `user-scalable=yes`. |
| **Empty `<title>` tag** | `<title></title>` — several clients (Samsung Mail, some WebKit views) fall back to displaying the title; an empty value can produce a blank or garbled subject preview. |
| **Malformed CSS — `.nav_padding`** | `padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important;` — missing numeric values. Rule is silently dropped; any nav padding at `max-width:480px` is non-functional. |
| **Malformed CSS — `.nav_border`** | `border-top:px solid ;` — missing width value and color. Rule is silently dropped. |
| **`font-display: swap` in `@font-face`**| Not a supported property in any email client. Harmless but dead code in all five `@font-face` blocks. |
| **Web fonts (`TheFutureHOKA`) via `@font-face`** | Supported only in Apple Mail and a small subset of clients. Gmail (Web, iOS, Android), Outlook (Windows), and Samsung Mail will fall back to system fonts. Fallback stack not auditable from the truncated source — verify `font-family` stacks in `<td>` / text elements have a reliable web-safe fallback (e.g., `Arial, sans-serif`). |

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<!-- Current (missing attribute) -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some older Outlook and Lotus Notes versions may misread the charset declaration.

**No issues found:** `img { display: block }`, `border-collapse: collapse`, `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override, and `-webkit-text-size-adjust` are all correctly implemented.
