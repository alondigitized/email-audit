# Make it a double for $50
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again, and it's the same "2 for $50" I got this morning ("Black Friday Hot Deal: 2 Pairs for $50"). The only change is the subject line. I can read the big "2 for $50" on my phone without squinting, and that part I like. But this is the third Crocs email today, and the one before it said the 20% code can't be combined with this offer. So I don't know which deal is the real one.
- There are no shoes in it, just a purple box with a number and a button. I can't tell whether I'd be buying a clog, a slip-on or a sandal. For a man who wants cushioning and arch support, that's a lot of guessing. I wouldn't click. I'd mark it read and move on, and I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The "2 for $50" is huge and easy to read on my phone. It's the first thing I see.
- It's one simple offer, with no code to copy and no percentage math.
- The black "Shop Black Friday" button is big and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The black "Free shipping on qualified orders" bar at the top is a nice extra.
- The end date, November 29th at 11:59pm MT, is in the fine print. At least it's stated.

## What's weak

- There's no product at all, so I can't tell whether this is a comfort shoe or something I'd wear.
- It repeats this morning's email almost word for word. I've lost count of the Crocs emails, and this is the third one today.
- The deadline is buried in small print at the bottom. The top should say "ends Sunday."
- "Exclusions Apply" and "select styles only" make me wary. Which shoes count?
- "Black Friday is here! These holiday savings are too good to miss!" tells me nothing.
- Nothing mentions arch support, cushioning or slip-on comfort, so nothing here is aimed at me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put two or three actual shoes in the box, with the price on each, and show a comfortable men's clog or slip-on. I want to see what $25 each buys me.
- 2. Move the end date up under the headline: "Ends Sunday, Nov 29."
- 3. Say which styles count instead of "Exclusions Apply."
- 4. Send one email a day, not three, and don't contradict the earlier code.
- 5. Use a button that names the product, such as "Shop Men's Clogs 2 for $50."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has solid responsive scaffolding: `lang="en"`, a viewport meta, an Outlook/Gmail client-fix CSS block, and a hidden preheader. The confirmed findings are a malformed `<meta>` pair, an unverified authentication status, and an alt-less tracking pixel. The provided HTML is truncated, so the footer, body links and unsubscribe markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **12 tracking links were not probed.** The QA run skipped them as click-redirect domains (`click.crocs-email.com`), so their destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- **The Adobe Audience Manager pixel has no alt attribute.** QA flagged `<img>` with src `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f…`. It is a tracking pixel, so the impact is low. It should still carry `alt=""` and explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
- **The ESP open pixel is correct.** `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…` has `width="1" height="1" alt=""`.
- **The demdex pixel sends a third-party event on open** (`c_emailopened=Yes`). This is a privacy and tracking disclosure item rather than a defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Two `<meta>` tags are malformed:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`.
  - `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`.
  - Without those attributes, both tags are inert.
  - The charset is probably still declared in the HTTP/MIME headers, but this is an error in the source.
- **`<title>` is the generic "Crocs".** It is minor, but it is what screen readers and web-view tabs announce.
- **Things that are in place:**
  - `lang="en"` on `<html>`.
  - Viewport meta.
  - `-webkit-text-size-adjust`.
  - The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset.
  - The `u + .email-body` Gmail hack.
  - `font-display: swap` on all three `@font-face` weights.
- **Web fonts load from `media.crocs.com` as woff/woff2.** Most clients will fall back to system fonts, so confirm the font stack in the inline styles. The truncated HTML didn't show it.
- **The preheader and spacer padding are consistent.** They use `display:none`, `max-height:0` and the zero-width/soft-hyphen character filler.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or in the QA output.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.** The QA warning says `Authentication-Results` was not found and is "expected via AgentMail relay". This is a limit of the capture path rather than a confirmed sender failure. Verify against the raw headers from a direct inbox, for example Gmail "Show original".
- **Unsubscribe link, physical address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers cannot be assessed.** The HTML is truncated before the footer and no headers were supplied. No violation is asserted.
- **Sender domain:** `email@crocs-email.com` matches the click-tracking domain `click.crocs-email.com`, so the tracking domain aligns with the sender.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. No destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the visible source, and the 12 tracking links were not probed. The `qs=` open-pixel token is opaque, so campaign-level attribution is hidden behind the ESP redirect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the meta tags:** `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
2. **Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel**, along with `width="1" height="1"` and `style="display:block"`.
3. **Re-run QA with the full HTML.** Use the untruncated source and raw headers to verify the footer, unsubscribe link, List-Unsubscribe headers, UTMs and the 12 redirect destinations. Resolve the redirects with a follow-redirect probe on a real click.
4. **Capture auth headers from a direct delivery.** That confirms SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass and alignment, instead of leaving the relay-induced warning unresolved.
5. **Make the `<title>` descriptive**, for example by using the subject line.
