# You'll want these best sellers & a freebie
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- DSW's in my inbox again — they're a store I actually walk into, so I don't delete these on sight. But the moment I opened this one, I felt like the computer got me mixed up with my wife. Almost every shoe I can see is women's — sandals with ankle straps, heeled dress shoes, strappy flats. The 30% off offer buried near the bottom is for Kelly & Katie beaded *handbags*. I'm a 62-year-old guy who needs something cushioned and easy to slip on. I don't own a handbag.
- The free cooler tote at the top caught my eye for half a second — "oh, a gift with purchase?" — but then I kept scrolling and never found what the qualifying purchase was, or whether any of these shoes are worth buying in the first place for my feet. There's a "Shop Sneakers" section that actually has some gender-neutral-looking athletic shoes, and a "Shop By Size" block at the very bottom, but you have to dig for it. There's nothing here that says DSW knows I care about arch support, wide widths, or a brand like Skechers or Hoka.
- I'd scroll past this. Mark it read, move on. I wouldn't click, and I'd definitely never forward it — my friends don't want beaded handbag deals either.

## What's working

- **DSW is a brand I trust and visit in person** — I didn't immediately trash this; the sender name has real equity with me.
- **"Summer's Best" is clean, seasonal, and legible** — the hero layout renders well, text is big enough to read without pinching.
- **Free tote teaser at the top is a real hook** — a freebie banner is the kind of thing that makes me at least look.
- **"Shop By Size" section near the bottom** — for a man who needs a specific width, that's a useful navigation feature when I can find it.
- **No broken images or layout disasters** — the email rendered clean; nothing fell apart.

## What's weak

- **Every shoe I can see appears to be women's** — heels, strappy sandals, beaded flats. Nothing that looks like it was picked for me.
- **The 30% off is for women's handbags** — the preview even says "30% off beaded Kelly & Katie handbags." That's the featured offer. I don't carry a purse.
- **The freebie conditions are invisible** — I can see a cooler tote is on offer but I cannot find the threshold or qualifying category without squinting.
- **"Best sellers" means nothing to me** — whose best sellers? For what type of foot? My top question when I open a DSW email is "do they have something that won't hurt my feet by 2 p.m.?" That question goes unanswered.
- **No comfort or fit language anywhere** — not one mention of cushioning, arch support, slip-on, or wide fit. These are the only words that make me click in a shoe email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the men's list separately.** This email is a women's catalog with my name on it. A men's "Summer Comfort Picks" send — Skechers slip-ons, Brooks walkers, Hoka recovery shoes — would get an immediate click from me.
- 2. **Lead the hero with comfort language, not season-speak.** "Summer's Best" is forgettable. "All-Day Comfort, All Summer" with a cushioned sneaker front-and-center would stop my thumb.
- 3. **Put the freebie terms in plain sight.** Tell me: "Spend $75, get a free cooler tote." That's a clear deal I can act on. Mystery conditions make me distrust the offer.
- 4. **Move the sneakers section to the top of the product grid.** That's the one block that might have something relevant for me, and it's buried after sandals and dress shoes.
- 5. **Drop the handbags entirely from the men's send** — or at minimum push them to a footnote, not the featured offer.

## Technical Audit — DSW "You'll want these best sellers & a freebie"

### 1. Technical Summary

Braze-sent email with custom font CDN dependency, dark mode support, and standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional structure. Several malformed meta tags and a CSS property misuse were found; the body is truncated so compliance and link audits are partially incomplete.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Partially assessable** — body HTML was truncated before product links appeared.

- Sending domain `e.dsw.com` is an ESP subdomain; DKIM/SPF alignment with the root `dsw.com` domain cannot be confirmed from HTML alone — requires header inspection.
- Font assets are loaded from `braze-images.com` (third-party CDN). Any CDN outage silently degrades fallback fonts; not a link-rot risk, but a fragile external dependency.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three confirmed defects in the `<head>`:**

| # | Location | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| 1 | `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` | Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Some older clients may not honor charset without the attribute. |
| 2 | `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` | Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Rendered inert in Outlook without it. |
| 3 | All `@font-face` blocks | `Src:` is capitalized. CSS property names are case-sensitive in strict parsers; `src:` (lowercase) is the spec-compliant form. Most clients tolerate this, but it is technically invalid. |

**Additional rendering concern:**

- `*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: none }` — value should be `100%`, not `none`. Setting to `none` disables automatic text size adjustment entirely on some Windows Phone / older IE mobile clients, which can cause unreadably small text.

**Font format efficiency:**

- Futura PT variants are served as `.woff` only; HelveticaNow variants as `.ttf` only. Neither uses WOFF2 (20–30% smaller). Not a rendering bug, but increases load time, particularly on mobile.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Not assessable from the truncated source — no body content with Liquid/merge tags is visible. No unresolved tokens (`{{`, `${`, `<%=`) were present in the visible `<head>` block.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Partially assessable** — footer not present in truncated source.

- Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are required by CAN-SPAM; neither is visible in the provided HTML. Confirm they exist in the full footer.

The CSS declaration is a no-op but indicates a copy-paste error between meta and style contexts.
