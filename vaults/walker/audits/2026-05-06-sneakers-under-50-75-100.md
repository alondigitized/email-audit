# Sneakers Under $50, $75, $100 ..
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival sent me a price-tier email built around a Mother's Day coupon. The layout is clean enough, and the price points caught my eye for about two seconds. But then I see the hero — some young woman posing in oversized street clothes — and I'm already halfway out the door. Nothing in this email says "men's," nothing says "comfort," and the preview text is literally a raw tracking URL dumped into my inbox. This model doesn't look like anyone I know, and she's definitely not wearing the kind of shoes I shop for. "ON TREND UNDER BUDGET" is the kind of language my grandkid uses, not me. The Mother's Day angle makes sense as a seasonal hook but it's not about buying for me — it's pitched as a gift for mom. I might pick something up for my wife, but the email hasn't made me think about myself at all.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tag — missing `http-equiv` (IE compat directive has no effect):**
```html
<!-- as sent — no http-equiv, directive is ignored -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />

<!-- correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**`<meta>` tag — malformed charset declaration:**
```html
<!-- as sent — mixes HTML4 content-type pattern with HTML5 charset attribute -->
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />

<!-- correct HTML5 form -->
<meta charset="utf-8" />
```
The `content="text/html;"` value without `http-equiv="Content-Type"` is inert. Charset is declared twice in conflicting patterns; most clients will recover, but this is spec-invalid.

**Dark mode explicitly disabled:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Apple Mail and Outlook on iOS/macOS will not apply dark-mode overrides. If background colors or image assets don't account for this suppression, forced-light rendering may produce harsh contrast for dark-mode users. This appears intentional but should be confirmed against the brand's dark-mode policy.

**Four separate `<style>` blocks in `<head>`:** Gmail strips `<head>` styles on some surfaces; having them split across four blocks rather than one consolidated block increases the risk of partial stripping. Consolidate into a single `<style>` tag.

**Subject line encoding:** Subject is `Sneakers Under $50, $75, $100 ..` — trailing `..` (two dots + trailing space before it) rather than `…` (U+2026 ellipsis) or `...`. If this is truncation in the audit feed it's fine; if it reflects the actual sent subject, fix the trailing space and character.

**Accessibility (cannot fully assess):** Image `alt` attributes, heading hierarchy, and link text are not visible in the truncated source. Verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` text and all CTAs have meaningful anchor text (not "click here").
