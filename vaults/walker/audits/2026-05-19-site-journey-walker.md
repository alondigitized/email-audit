# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 19, 2026 | **Prior Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The middle of the funnel — product discovery, category navigation, and the product detail page — held up reasonably well today. Touch targets in the Men's menu were generous, the Slip-ins landing page was exactly where I needed to be, and the product card communicated price and promo clearly. But the cart is completely broken — blank white screen, nothing rendered — and that alone tanks the whole visit. On top of that, the login never visibly stuck (I'm still seeing "Login" prompts throughout), and the search results for "comfort shoes" served me women's shoes first. Three critical failures out of nine steps. Compared to yesterday's 4/10, the middle improved slightly but the back end of the journey got worse or stayed broken.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The cart failure is a conversion killer. If I can't see what's in my cart, I'm not buying — I'm closing the tab and calling the 800 number or heading to DSW.

## 3. What's Working

- **Hero messaging is on-point.** The homepage hero leads with "HANDS FREE Slip-ins GLIDE-STEP" — that's exactly the product I care about. No burying the lede. Large image, clear brand call, prominent "SHOP NOW" button with a touch target big enough for my thumb.
- **Men's category menu is genuinely good.** Full-width rows, large readable text, "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" surfaced as quick links without having to dig. This is the best screen of the journey. A 62-year-old can navigate this without squinting.
- **Comfort Technologies as a menu item.** That's smart taxonomy for my shopping mindset. I didn't tap it today but I noticed it and it built trust.
- **Product detail page is clean.** "Skechers Slip-ins: Glide-Step Pro," $100.00, 4.5 stars / 381 reviews, BOGO promo — all above the fold on mobile without zooming. The Hands-Free Slip-ins badge in the product image reinforces the category.
- **"Customers say this item feels true to size"** — that one line does a lot of work. As someone who can't try these on before buying, that's reassuring.
- **BOGO banner is persistent.** The blue "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" bar never disappears. I trust a deal more when it follows me around rather than disappearing after the homepage.
- **Width filter chips on the category page.** Wide Fit and Extra Wide Fit are right there at the top of 281 results. No hunting required.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart is a blank white screen.** Step 8 rendered nothing — pure white. I have no idea if my item was added, if it's saved, or if the session dropped. This is the most important screen in the entire purchase flow and it completely failed. If I were a real customer, I would assume my order didn't go through.
- **Login never visibly completed.** Step 3 screenshot looks identical to the pre-login homepage — same "Login | details" link, same hero, no confirmation, no name greeting, no "Welcome back, Walker." Steps 5 and 9 still show the Login prompt in the loyalty banner. I'm either not actually logged in, or the site doesn't acknowledge me anywhere after authentication. Either way, there's zero personalization payoff from logging in.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" shows Women's first.** 1,441 results is impressive breadth — good signal that the catalog is deep. But the first two visible products on the results page are both tagged "Women's." If I'm a man who just searched "comfort shoes," I should be getting men's results, or at minimum a gender toggle at the top. This is a failed personalization moment.
- **Width selector missing from Add to Cart view.** The step-7 screenshot shows only three sizes (7.0, 7.5, 8.5) and a "Please select a size" error — but no width selector is visible in the viewport. For a shoe brand whose menu proudly calls out Wide Fit and Extra Wide Fit, burying width selection below the fold on the purchase screen is a miss.
- **Homepage defaults to Women's tab.** The bottom tab bar shows Women underlined as the active category. As a male shopper, I'm immediately off-persona on arrival.
- **"Members Only" loyalty banner requires a Login tap inside a small hyperlink.** The word "Login" is a small, underlined blue link embedded in a sentence of smaller text — not a button. For a shopper with older eyes on a phone, that's easy to miss or mistap.
- **No arch support or cushioning language on the product page (visible viewport).** The product name and hero are great, but I don't see "arch support," "cushioning," or "memory foam" in any visible text above the fold. Those are the words I'm shopping for, and they're not in my face.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart — this is P0.** A blank cart screen is a broken checkout. Investigate whether it's a session/auth issue tied to the login failure. A guest user should see their cart; a logged-in user should see their cart plus saved items.
2. **Confirm login with a visible greeting.** After login, show "Hi, Walker" or "Welcome back" somewhere persistent — header or loyalty banner. Right now logging in feels like it does nothing.
3. **Gender-filter search results.** When a logged-in male user searches "comfort shoes," default to Men's results or auto-apply a gender filter. Show a toggle to switch — don't make me wade through women's shoes.
4. **Promote width to the same viewport as size.** Size and width should be on screen together, not stacked. These are inseparable decisions for comfort shoppers.
5. **Swap Women's default tab to Men's when male user is logged in.** Homepage should open on "Men" for a known male customer.
6. **Add one comfort keyword above the fold on PDP.** "Air-Cooled Memory Foam" or "Arch-Fit insole" in a badge or subtitle line — before the scroll — converts comfort shoppers faster.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The product is right and the middle of the funnel shows real improvement in navigation clarity — the Men's menu is a genuine win. But a blank cart and a login that leaves no trace are deal-breakers. I'd have closed the browser and gone to New Balance's site. Score holds at **4/10** until the cart and auth are fixed. Fix those two and this jumps to a 6 overnight.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey on skechers.com (SFCC/Demandware platform). TTFB is consistently excellent (26–30ms) except at cart (138ms). LCP and CLS data are absent for all steps — the most critical render-quality metrics are unsampled. Six consecutive 429s on the Kasada bot-protection endpoint indicate the automated session triggered rate-limiting, which directly caused: the cart page TTFB spike, the cart 429 network error, and the View Cart accessibility violations (document rendered as a degraded shell without `<title>` or `lang`). A secondary Stylitics CORS misconfiguration breaks the outfit-styling widget site-wide.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (1)**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA roles without required parent container — screen readers will misreport element relationships.

**Serious (2)**
- `document-title` + `html-has-lang` (View Cart): Both are absent. Root cause is almost certainly the 429 on `/cart/` serving a degraded SSR response rather than a full HTML document. WCAG 2.1 failures (SC 2.4.2, SC 3.1.1).

**Moderate (12)**
- Duplicate `<main>` landmark (Homepage): Two `<main>` elements present — invalidates landmark navigation.
- `<main>` nested inside another element (Homepage + View Cart): `landmark-main-is-top-level` / `landmark-one-main` — likely caused by a widget or modal injecting its own document structure.
- `<aside>` not top-level (Homepage, Search): Complementary landmarks nested inside `<main>` or other sectioning elements.
- `landmark-unique` (Homepage, Search): Multiple landmarks of the same type without distinguishing labels.
- `region` violations (Homepage, Search): Page content outside any landmark — typically third-party inject points.
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Heading levels skip (e.g., h1 → h3), breaking document outline.
- `page-has-heading-one` (View Cart): No h1 present — again consistent with a partial/shell render under rate-limiting.

**Pattern:** The View Cart cluster (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) is almost certainly one root cause — the rate-limited cart response — not four independent bugs.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart `<title>` missing**: Googlebot will see a titleless cart page if it crawls a rate-limited session (unlikely in practice but indicates fragile SSR).
- **Heading hierarchy broken (Homepage)**: Skipped heading levels reduce crawlability of page structure.
- **Heavy third-party script injection via GTM**: Two GTM containers (`GTM-TDRBQZ` plus a second via `gtag/destination`) and 15+ ad pixels fire on most pages. At scale this delays `document-interactive` and can push LCP past 2.5s on mid-range mobile hardware despite fast TTFB.
- **Webfont loader (cdnjs, v1.6.28)**: Client-side font loading via WebFontLoader causes FOUT; `wf-*-active` classes on `<html>` confirm fonts are not preloaded — this can shift content and hurt CLS.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS variable**: The site uses a JS-driven `--vh` unit (common SFCC pattern for 100vh on iOS Safari). Floating-point value (`6.640000000000001px`) suggests `innerHeight / 100` without `Math.round` or `toFixed`. No functional breakage but worth normalizing.
- **Consent-gated scripts (`vice-managed-src`)**: TrustArc CMP defers Facebook, Pinterest, Yahoo, Impact, Taboola, Nextdoor pixels until consent. This is correct behavior — scripts fire only post-consent. On mobile, this means the consent banner itself must render and be dismissed before these pixels fire, which is the intended flow.
- **No mobile-specific viewport issues detected** in HTML source (standard `<meta name="viewport">` assumed; not visible in truncated source but `--vh` polyfill implies it's present).

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada fingerprinting (5 hits):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.412
```
This is the Kasada bot-detection SDK (`kpsdk`) submitting client fingerprints to validate the session. Repeated 429s mean the session was flagged as automated and the server is refusing to issue a valid bot-protection token. Downstream effect: `/cart/` also returns 429, breaking the cart page render.

**429 — Cart page (`/cart/`):**
The cart endpoint itself is rate-limited. This is the direct cause of the View Cart TTFB spike and the missing document metadata (server returned a non-200 shell, not the full cart HTML).

**CORS — Stylitics widget CSS:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics (outfit-styling widget) CSS is loaded as a cross-origin stylesheet. The Stylitics CDN is not returning the required `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or wildcard) header. The widget will render unstyled. This is a Stylitics CDN configuration issue — requires Stylitics to add the CORS header, or Skechers to proxy the asset.

**`ERR_FAILED` (1 hit):** Almost certainly the Stylitics CSS load that failed after the CORS rejection — same resource, two console entries.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix cart availability under bot-protection challenges**
The Kasada SDK is rate-limiting the cart endpoint. Real users on slow networks or privacy-focused browsers (canvas fingerprinting blocked) may also hit this. Ensure the cart page has a fallback render path that serves valid HTML (with `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, `<h1>`) even when the Kasada token is not yet validated. The bot-protection layer should gate *behavior* (add-to-cart actions), not *page rendering*.

**P0 — Collect LCP and CLS for all steps**
TTFB data alone is insufficient. Instrument field data (CrUX or RUM) or lab data (Lighthouse mobile throttled) to capture LCP and CLS — these are the metrics Google uses for ranking and the ones most impacted by the third-party script load.

**P1 — Fix Stylitics CORS headers**
File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. As a workaround, proxy the CSS through a Skechers-owned CDN path to avoid the cross-origin constraint entirely.

**P1 — Remove duplicate third-party script tags**
- `tags.creativecdn.com` fires twice on Men category and Log In
- `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` fires twice on Log In
Audit GTM container triggers — these duplicates are almost certainly GTM tag misconfiguration (tag firing on both Page View and DOM Ready triggers). Each duplicate doubles pixel spend and adds unnecessary parse/execute time.

**P2 — Resolve Homepage landmark structure**
Two `<main>` elements and nested `<aside>` elements indicate a widget or third-party inject (likely Monetate or a personalization layer) is inserting its own document structure. Audit Monetate/personalization campaign templates to enforce single-`<main>` constraint.

**P2 — Fix heading hierarchy (Homepage)**
Ensure h1 → h2 → h3 progression. This is an SFCC template issue — audit the homepage component slot rendering order.

**P3 — Normalize `--vh` value**
Change `window.innerHeight / 100` to `(window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(4)` or similar to eliminate floating-point noise in the CSS variable.

**P3 — Replace WebFontLoader with `<link rel="preload">`**
Remove `webfontloader.js` CDN dependency. Serve Mulish from a self-hosted or Google Fonts `font-display: swap` + preconnect pattern. Eliminates the cdnjs dependency, removes FOUT, and reduces a render-blocking request chain.
