# A gift he’ll love: FIFA World Cup 26™ jerseys
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Alright, adidas. Father's Day is around the corner, and this is what you came up with for me — soccer jerseys. Again. I've been keeping count, and this is somewhere around the third or fourth World Cup-themed send in the past couple weeks. The "HE'S A KEEPER" headline is cute — I see what you did there, the goalkeeper pun — and I'll admit it made me half-smile for about two seconds. But then I looked at the rest of it and remembered I'm a sixty-two year old man who hasn't worn a jersey since the '94 Super Bowl party, and that's not even the right sport.
- Here's the other thing that bugs me: the email is written like it's being sent to someone shopping FOR me, not to me. "A gift he'll love" — well, who's "he"? I'm the one reading this in my inbox. So adidas pitched me on buying myself a soccer jersey as a Father's Day gift to... myself? Or maybe they think I'm going to buy this for my own father, who's in his eighties and would have even less use for a FIFA jersey than I do. Either way, the targeting math doesn't add up.
- I'd mark this read and scroll on. No click, no forward, no regrets.

## What's working

- "HE'S A KEEPER" is legitimate wordplay — the goalkeeper/keeper-as-a-compliment double meaning is charming, and I respect the copywriter for landing it.
- The Father's Day gifting angle is at least a logical strategic frame; using a cultural moment to sell product makes sense.
- Clean render on mobile — no broken images, text doesn't overlap, layout holds up on a phone screen.
- The two-section structure (hero + "KICK-OFF READY FOR DAD") gives the email visual pacing without feeling chaotic.

## What's weak

- This is the third or fourth World Cup / soccer jersey push in two weeks — the cadence is burning me out on adidas right now, and I nearly deleted this without opening based on the subject alone.
- The "he" framing in the subject is backward — I'm the subscriber, the man, the one holding the phone. Writing to me like I'm shopping FOR a man I don't know feels like the wrong persona.
- No price anywhere visible. No deal. No discount. "Here are jerseys" is not a compelling Father's Day offer.
- The hero model is a young guy. Not a dad, not someone my age. Nothing in this visual says "this email is for you, Walker."
- Not a single shoe in sight — after getting the Adistar XLG 2.0 email two days ago, dropping completely back to jerseys with zero comfort-footwear content is a missed layup.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the email on a deal.** "Up to 30% off Father's Day gifts" gives me a reason to act. Right now there's no financial hook anywhere visible, just product showcase.
- 2. **Broaden the gift assortment.** At least one comfort shoe or casual sneaker alongside the jerseys — plenty of dads want gear for themselves, not just a jersey to hang in a closet.
- 3. **Rewrite the "he" framing.** If adidas is emailing me, speak to me. "A gift you'll actually wear" or "treat yourself this Father's Day" lands better than writing me out of my own email.
- 4. **Show the deadline.** I know Father's Day is around the corner but I need to see a date to feel urgency — "Shop before June 15" does more work than any clever headline.
- 5. **Rotate off the World Cup content.** Three-plus soccer sends in a row is enough to make me start hitting "unsubscribe." Vary the product focus.

## Technical Audit — Adidas FIFA World Cup 26™ Jerseys Email

**From:** adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com | **Date reviewed:** 2026-06-09

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken destination URL and a missing plain-text fallback are the two confirmed failures (25% fail rate per QA). Four HTTP image sources and two missing alt attributes add rendering and accessibility risk but are non-blocking.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Malformed destination URL**

The QA resolver encountered `unknown url type: '/g/840620a4-5911-45b4-9b99-85902937d0c4?txn=a7197c80-642c-11f1-9fc6-4e8366552ae6'` inside the Movable Ink wrapper:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/840620a4-5911-45b4-9b99-85902937d0c4?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The inner destination is a relative path (`/g/...`) rather than an absolute URL. The outer tracking wrapper resolves, but the final redirect target is broken. Clicks on this link will produce a 400/404 depending on how the redirect server handles relative paths. This likely affects a jersey product or CTA block — high-visibility failure.

**INFO — 25 tracking links not probed**

All click-redirect domains (`click.comms.adidas.com`, `dv.adidas.com`) were skipped by the HTTP prober. No secondary broken links confirmed, but destination URLs behind these wrappers cannot be verified without a full click-trace.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Four HTTP image sources (mixed content)**

The same arrow image is referenced four times via HTTP:

```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail (Chrome-rendered) and Outlook.com will block or proxy-upgrade this. Apple Mail on iOS 15+ blocks HTTP images by default. All four instances appear to be the same asset — fix once at the source.

A fifth HTTP image appears on the tracking/open pixel:

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ead27c8cb-...
```

Tracking pixels served over HTTP will be blocked silently, suppressing open-rate data for clients that enforce HTTPS.

**WARN — Two images missing `alt` text**

- `840620a4-5911-45b4-9b99-85902937d0c4` (Movable Ink rendered image — likely hero or product shot)
- `LN5Lk2wX2oY-iM5j9_lNA0_hs...` (tracking pixel — `alt=""` is correct here, but empty is different from absent)

Missing `alt` on the product image means screen readers skip it with no description, and image-off rendering (Outlook desktop default) shows a broken icon with no label.

**INFO — Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `src: local(...)` fallbacks and no remote font URL. The declarations are functional no-ops (local fonts will never match on subscriber devices), but the duplication adds ~600 bytes of dead CSS.

**INFO — `<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Should be:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, some older Outlook and Lotus Notes versions will not apply the charset declaration. Low risk in 2026, but a trivial fix.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{...}}`, `[%...]`, `*|...|*`) visible in the truncated HTML source. Cannot confirm full template for blocks outside the truncation window — spot-check the footer and subject line token in the full source before send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**FAIL — Plain-text fallback is 0 characters**

QA confirmed the `text/plain` MIME part contains no content. CAN-SPAM and GDPR best practice both require the plain-text part to include at minimum the physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism. A zero-length plain-text part also increases spam score on SpamAssassin-based filters (`MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule).

Cannot verify physical address or unsubscribe link presence from the truncated HTML, but those elements must appear in the rendered email body.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are visible in the decoded tracking URLs. The wrapper uses adidas/Movable Ink proprietary parameters (`cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`) which capture ESP-side click data but will not appear in Google Analytics or any UTM-dependent attribution. If GA4 / site analytics is expected to credit this send, UTM parameters need to be embedded in the final destination URLs before the tracking wrapper is applied.

The broken `/g/` destination URL (see §2) means at least one product landing page is unreachable regardless of UTM status.
