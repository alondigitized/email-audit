# Become a VIP & get $20 OFF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting DSW birthday emails all week — the $20 off VIP offer specifically showed up at least twice yesterday alone. So when this landed with almost the same pitch in the subject line, my thumb was already hovering over the archive button before I even opened it. The email itself is clean and renders fine, and yes, the $20 off hero pops right away. But here's my problem: I scroll down past that birthday banner and I'm looking at heeled sandals, block mules, strappy dress shoes, wedges — a women's footwear parade. The one "Shop Sneakers" link is buried in the middle of a grid that's otherwise aimed at someone shopping for her summer wardrobe, not my wide-width walking routine.
- The brand logos down near the bottom — I spotted New Balance and Adidas, two brands I actually cross-shop — but they're tiny and nowhere near the hero. If DSW had led with "New Balance and Brooks are in the birthday sale," I'd have clicked immediately. Instead I had to hunt.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Wouldn't forward it. At this point I'm starting to wonder if DSW knows I'm a 62-year-old guy at all.

## What's working

- **$20 off hero is impossible to miss** — birthday banner plus the dollar amount lands above the fold, no scrolling required.
- **Subject line is short and specific** — 26 characters, clear dollar figure, clean on mobile. No gimmicks.
- **DSW is a sender I recognize** — I'm in their loyalty program; the name alone keeps me from unsubscribing.
- **"Shop by Size" section at the bottom** — that's the right idea for someone like me who needs to filter to comfort widths quickly.
- **No render bugs** — everything loaded, images are crisp, layout held together.

## What's weak

- **Almost the entire product grid is women's shoes** — heels, wedges, block mules, strappy sandals. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned walkers; this email isn't talking to me.
- **"Peep these best sellers" preheader is disconnected** — the subject promises VIP + $20 off; the preview pivots to best sellers. Two different messages before I even open it.
- **Third time this week on the same VIP/$20 pitch** — yesterday I got "BTW: VIPS GET $20 OFF EARLY >>>" and "Want First Dibs on $20 off?" — this is the exact same offer wearing a slightly different shirt.
- **"SHOP IT" CTA is too vague** — shop what? Women's sandals? The sale generally? A button that said "Shop Men's Sale" would have put my finger on it.
- **No deadline visible** — the birthday sale has been running all week; there's no "ends tonight" or "last day" to make me move now versus closing the tab.

- 1. **Segment or surface men's product prominently.** Even a single men's comfort shoe block above the fold — Hoka, New Balance, or a Skechers wide — would transform this from "not for me" to "let me look closer."
- 2. **Replace "SHOP IT" with "Shop Men's Sale"** — or at minimum "Shop the Birthday Sale." One word of context is all it takes.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Birthday sale ends Sunday" somewhere in the hero. I've been conditioned by a week of birthday emails to wait — give me a reason to stop waiting.
- 4. **Lead with brand logos if you have New Balance and Adidas in the sale.** Those are names I trust. Pull them up from the footer into the hero or sub-hero row.
- 5. **Retire the VIP/$20 subject angle for one cycle.** I've seen it three times. Try a product-first subject — what specific shoe is on sale?

### 1. Technical Summary

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `<meta>` tags — confirmed bugs:**

```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv attribute -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```

Both tags are missing their `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, these tags are inert — the charset declaration does nothing and the IE compatibility mode hint is not applied.

**Non-standard CSS property casing in `@font-face`:**

```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

All 10 `@font-face` blocks use `Src:` (capital S). CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but this is non-standard and will fail strict parsers. The conventional form is `src:` (lowercase).

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text). Low-vision users cannot zoom the email in mobile clients that respect this meta.

**Font format gap:**

Futura PT fonts serve `.woff` only; no `.woff2` fallback. HelveticaNow fonts serve `.ttf`. WOFF2 has ~30% better compression and broader email client support. Neither format has a web-safe fallback declared in the `@font-face` block itself (fallback is handled in inline `font-family` stacks, which couldn't be verified in the truncated source).

**Mixed-case `Margin` hack:**

```css
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li { margin: 0 !important; Margin: 0 }
```

The capital `Margin: 0` is an intentional Outlook VML hack (Outlook's layout engine reads the proprietary `Margin` property). This is a known workaround, not a bug, but worth documenting.
