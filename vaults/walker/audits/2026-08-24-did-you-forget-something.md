# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that same pink floral D'Lites Spring Bloom sneaker, front and center under "READY TO CHECK OUT?" That's the fifth time this week I've seen this exact shoe in my inbox, and I want to say it one more time for the people in the back: I never put that in a cart. It's not my size, not my style, and not remotely close to what a 62-year-old guy needs on his feet for a walk around the block.
- Below the fold there's actually something useful — a row of men's slip-ins, a basketball shoe, some boys' sneakers, and a 20% off Skechers Plus code — but I have to scroll past a women's floral shoe to get there, which tells me nobody's paying attention to who's actually opening this email.
- Compare this to Sunday's "Classics Made for Every Step" send, which led with a guy about my son's age in a comfortable brown shoe — that one I'd have clicked. This one's going straight to read-and-ignore. Not forwarding it, not clicking it, not buying anything off the back of a phantom cart.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus sitewide code (SKXPLUS) is a real, specific offer with a visible deadline of 8/25 — that catches my eye as a member.
- The "Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit: Altman - Murdoch Sneaker" thumbnail is the kind of hands-free comfort shoe I'd actually consider.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup icons are there if I wanted a no-fuss way to buy — not that I do here.

## What's weak

- Leading the whole email with a pink floral women's sneaker I never looked at, again, is just wrong — this is the fifth near-identical "you forgot this" nudge this week and it's always the same wrong shoe.
- "Ready to check out?" implies I actually have something in a cart. I don't. That's not a reminder, that's a guess, and a bad one.
- The men's and comfort-focused items are buried below a wall of unrelated boys' and women's product — nothing in this email leads with what I'd actually buy.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning, or fit details anywhere — just names and thumbnails.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the cart-recovery logic — if I never added that shoe, stop showing it to me. This is the core problem across the last several sends, not just this one.
- 2. Lead with a men's comfort shoe (like the Altman - Murdoch slip-in already in this email) instead of a women's item that has nothing to do with my browsing history.
- 3. Add one line of copy under the hero item calling out arch support / slip-on ease — give me a reason beyond "you forgot this."
- 4. Move the 20% off Skechers Plus banner up near the top — that's the strongest real offer in the email and it's buried two-thirds down.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New men's slip-ins just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members save 20% sitewide with code SKXPLUS — ends tomorrow`

- Broken link (400) on "Unsubscribe" — URL returns 400 Bad Request. This is the highest-priority fix given CAN-SPAM implications (see §5).
