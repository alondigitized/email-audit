# Save up to 40% off right now
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's a step up from what adidas has been sending me lately — an actual clean offer, "40% off summer staples," right at the top, no soccer jerseys, no kids' backpacks. But it's still that fabric close-up hero image with stretched-out spaced-out lettering that I have to squint at to read as "SUMMER ESSENTIALS SALE," and there's not one single shoe pictured in the whole email. For a brand that makes footwear, that's a swing and a miss for a guy like me who's shopping by what I can put on my feet, not by a mystery fabric swatch.
- The gift card push in the second half — buy $50 for $40 — is honestly a decent deal, but it's buried behind another unreadable stretched-text image of a jersey, and by then I've mostly checked out. I'd scroll past this one, not click, not forward it. It's a "mark as read and move on" email.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 40% OFF SUMMER STAPLES" headline and "no code needed" copy is clear and easy to read — better than the past few sends.
- One concrete number — 40% — right up top, no digging for the offer.
- The $50-for-$40 gift card deal is a real, honest, straightforward discount with a clear asterisk pointing to the terms.
- Clean black-and-white layout, decent size text on the body copy, easy enough to read on my phone without zooming.

## What's weak

- Not a single shoe anywhere in this email — just two stretched-out fabric/jersey close-ups. I can't tell if "summer staples" means shoes, shirts, shorts, or all of it.
- The stylized spaced-out lettering ("S U M M E R E S S E N T I A L S S A L E") is hard to read fast on a phone — I had to slow down and piece it together.
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support, or anything about walking shoes — nothing here says "this is for you, Walker."
- Huge gaps of empty white space between sections make me think something didn't load right, like images are missing.
- "Exclusions apply" with no hint of what's excluded — feels like there's a catch I'd only find out at checkout.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual shoe — men's, casual or walking — in that hero image instead of a fabric close-up. That's the single biggest fix; right now I have zero idea if this applies to shoes at all.
- 2. Ditch the spaced-out stylized type treatment. Just say "Up to 40% Off Summer Styles" in normal readable type over the photo.
- 3. Add one line about what's actually included — "shoes, apparel, and accessories" or whatever's true — so I'm not guessing.
- 4. Tighten up those big blank gaps between sections; it reads like broken layout on my phone.
- 5. If there's a comfort or walking shoe angle in this sale, say so — even a small callout like "including Cloudfoam and Puremotion comfort styles" would get my attention.

- QA flagged a "doubled-domain" pattern on: `https://click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.adidas.com%2Fus%2Fhelp%2Fus-promotions-vouchers%2F...`. On inspection this is the expected SFMC `CL0` redirect format (tracking domain + URL-encoded destination), not a broken/duplicated domain — likely a QA false positive from pattern-matching two domain strings in one URL.
