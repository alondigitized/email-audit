# Nail the dorm dress code in UGG
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Well, DSW's stuck in the back-to-school aisle again — same theme as yesterday's UGG email, just a different subject line this time. This whole thing is "dorm dress code," Reebok Classics, a college-age model doing some kind of yoga pose in athletic gear, a water bottle tumbler, sunglasses — none of it is aimed at a 62-year-old, and none of it is what I open a shoe email hoping to see.
- The one shoe that's actually in my wheelhouse is that tan UGG clog up top — that's a real hands-free slip-on, and if they'd led with "comfortable slip-on, arch support, easy on-off" I'd have paused. Instead it's buried under "SEMESTER STAPLES" and a green fluorescent sneaker that looks like it belongs on a teenager's foot, not mine.
- I'd mark this one read and move on. Not forwarding it — nobody in my circle has a kid starting a dorm this fall, and even if they did, this isn't the email I'd send them.

## What's working

- The tan UGG clog thumbnail is genuinely a "hands-free slip-on" shoe — that's my category, even if the campaign framing missed it.
- Size grid (5 through 11) is right there, no digging — I use that every time.
- "SHOP UGG" button is a clean, specific CTA if I do want to chase that one shoe.
- Loyalty callout at the bottom (5% back, free shipping, birthday gift) is at least visible, even if it's an afterthought.

## What's weak

- The whole "dorm," "semester," "major in style" framing tells me flat out this isn't for me — I'm not shopping for a dorm.
- The model, the pose, the neon Reebok sneaker, the water tumbler — none of that speaks to a guy my age looking for cushioned, arch-supported comfort.
- No price, no percentage off, no dollar figure anywhere in the whole layout — just "new" and "shop." I like knowing the number before I click.
- "Deploy the dorm troopers" is trying too hard to be clever — doesn't land with me and just adds noise around the sunglasses.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the audience — send a men's comfort-shoe version of this UGG launch instead of burying one relevant style in a back-to-school theme. That single change would matter most.
- 2. Put a price or percentage on the UGG clog directly under the product shot — I won't click without knowing the ballpark.
- 3. Swap the college lifestyle imagery for a straightforward product shot with a callout on cushioning/arch support when targeting my segment.
- 4. Drop "Deploy the dorm troopers" — replace with plain, direct copy about the shoe itself.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New from UGG: an all-day slip-on clog`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The UGG clog we think you'll wear daily`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, easy on-off, arch support built in`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No laces, no fuss — just slip in and go`
