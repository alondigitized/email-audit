## Executive summary

- Another day, another New Balance email in my inbox — this makes it what, six or seven this month? The subject line's got that stutter where it says the same thing twice, which either means their system's broke or somebody's phone screen is showing me a glitch, and either way it's not a good first impression. Once I get past that, though, it's a straightforward one: shoeboxes stacked in a photo, 25% off if I spend $125, ends August 24th. No models, no lifestyle nonsense, just boxes and product — I can get behind that.
- Problem is 25% off only kicks in once I clear $125, and everything I actually scroll to at the bottom — the Numeric skate shoes, the T500, the 2010 Lace — those are the kid/skater lineup or a lifestyle sneaker, not the cushioned walking shoe I'm hunting for. The Ellipse looks like it might have some real sole to it, but there's nothing telling me about arch support or all-day comfort, just a picture and a price tier.
- I'd mark this one read and move on. Not annoying enough to unsubscribe over, but not compelling enough to click either. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here says "hey, thought of you."

## What's working

- The subject and preview are honest about the deadline — August 24th, no games there.
- Product-first hero shot (shoeboxes) instead of another 20-something model in bike shorts, which I appreciate.
- Price-tier shopping buttons (Under $50, $50-$100, Over $100) are a clean, thumb-friendly way to browse on my phone.
- Free shipping banner over $75 is legible and up top where I'd actually see it before scrolling.

## What's weak

- The subject line repeats itself word for word — looks like a broken merge, makes me trust the email less before I even open it.
- None of the "More to explore" picks are the comfort/arch-support shoe I actually shop for — it's skate shoes and lifestyle sneakers, not walking shoes.
- $125 minimum to unlock the 25% is a real barrier — that's not an impulse buy, that's "buy two things you don't need."
- No callout for men's specifically or any comfort/cushioning language anywhere in the copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the duplicated subject line — that's a technical embarrassment, not a style choice, and it should've never gone out the door.
- 2. Swap at least two of the six "More to explore" shoes for something in the walking/comfort lane — a Fresh Foam or similar — so a guy like me sees something relevant without hunting.
- 3. Add one line of copy near the hero tying the offer to a use case ("Stock up on your go-to walking shoe") instead of leaving it as bare boxes and a percentage.
- 4. Consider a lower spend threshold or a tiered offer (15% at $75, 25% at $125) so it doesn't feel like they're forcing a bigger basket than I want.
