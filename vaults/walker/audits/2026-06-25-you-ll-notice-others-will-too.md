# You’ll notice. Others will too.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Adidas sent me another product launch email, and I'll say this upfront — "You'll notice. Others will too." is not how I think about shoes. I think about whether my feet will hurt by the third hole at the golf course, or whether I can get in and out of the car without bending over to tie something. Noticing, and having others notice, is not in my top ten.
- What I'm looking at here is the Adizero Adios Pro 4, done up in some collaboration with a brand called SATISFY. The email tells me the soles are "mismatched by design" and introduce "a deliberate imbalance." I'm sixty-two. I'm not in the market for deliberate imbalance. The copy reads like something out of a design journal, not a shoe ad.
- I'm marking this as read and moving on. I'd never forward this — there's nobody on my contact list who's in the market for a fashion racing flat with mismatched soles.

## What's working

- adidas is a brand I recognize, so I at least opened it out of habit.
- The preview text — "Introducing the adidas Adizero Adios Pro 4 SATISFY running shoe" — at least tells me what the product is, which the subject line completely refused to do.
- The email renders cleanly. No broken images, no overlapping text. Clean layout, I'll give them that.
- The main photo puts the shoe front and center, so I know what I'm looking at immediately.

## What's weak

- "Mismatched by design" and "deliberate imbalance" are the exact opposite of what I want in a shoe. This copy actively pushed me away.
- No price anywhere. Not a number in sight. I don't even know if this is a $90 shoe or a $250 shoe.
- No discount, no offer, no "Shop Now" button in the body — just a small bar at the top. Nothing to act on.
- The whole visual treatment — dark moody tones, editorial photography — reads like it's aimed at a 28-year-old, not me.
- I just got the $30 off $100 email a week ago (June 19). Going from a clear savings offer straight to a design-philosophy product launch is a gear shift I wasn't ready for.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price and a button in the body.** If you're going to launch a product, tell me what it costs and give me somewhere to tap. A small "SHOP NOW" link buried in the header bar is not a CTA.
- 2. **Lead with something I can hold onto.** If this shoe has any story about cushioning, energy return, or all-day comfort — lean into that. "Race-day platform" is not it.
- 3. **Cool it on the fashion editorial.** The "deliberate imbalance" angle is for a different customer. I want to know if my foot will feel good in it.
- 4. **Respect the cadence.** This is the sixth or seventh adidas email I've gotten in two weeks. I need a breather between the pet collection and the philosophy lecture.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken click-tracking redirect and a missing plain-text alternative. Several non-HTTPS image sources and missing alt attributes represent deliverability and accessibility risk.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking redirect**
The encoded URL resolves to a `/g/` path that the redirect service cannot handle:
```
unknown url type: '/g/8beb2f3a-d437-43ee-b491-e66c49a13753?txn=d5fc3900-704f-11f1-9db7-ae32f7ad8416'
```
Outer URL: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/8beb2f3a-d437-43ee-b491-e66c49a13753?cp_tp=v3.eJzjYuII...`

The `dv.adidas.com/o/` wrapper decoded the payload correctly but resolved to a `/g/` path that the redirect handler rejects. Any click on this link produces a dead end. Likely a mis-configured dynamic content block or corrupted `txn` token.

**[WARN] 24 tracking links not probed** — click-redirect domains were skipped. Broken redirect above is the confirmed instance; others could share the same template defect.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× duplicate HTTP image — same asset, same defect**
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
`alt="arrow"` is present, so alt text is not the issue here. The `http://` scheme will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail when proxy-loading images over HTTPS — the arrow icon will render as a broken image in most clients. This asset is used 4 times (same src each time).

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 2 images**

| Image | src fragment |
|---|---|
| Tracking pixel / hero | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/8beb2f3a...` |
| Open-pixel | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ec828bcbf...` |

The open-pixel (`CI0/...`) is expected to have no alt, but the `dv.adidas.com/o/` image appears to be content-bearing and lacks `alt=""` even as an empty placeholder — a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure for screen readers.

**Font declarations duplicated across two `<style>` blocks** — `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` rules are declared twice with identical `src: local(...)` fallbacks. No rendering breakage, but doubles CSS weight unnecessarily.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` missing `http-equiv` attribute** — should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, some legacy clients ignore the charset declaration.

### 5. Compliance

CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its complete absence degrades deliverability scoring (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule adds score). More critically, recipients using plain-text-only clients or accessibility tools receive a blank email. Standard practice for bulk senders of this volume is to include at minimum a stripped-text version.

Recommend confirming `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are set at send time (RFC 8058 one-click).
