# See What Everyone's Adding To Their Cart
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Another Skechers email in my inbox — and before I even opened it, I almost didn't. The preview text was a wall of raw JSON code, "{" "@context": "http://schema.org/" — same broken garbage that showed up in the June 2nd send. I flagged that one in my notes and here we are again. That's not a one-time accident, that's a quality control failure on a loop.
- Once I got inside though, the email actually has some of what I'm looking for. BOGO 50% off at the top, and about halfway down I spotted the three tiles I actually care about: Slip-Ins, Arch Fit, Glide-Step. That's the holy trinity for my feet. But the whole framing is "best sellers" social proof, which tells me nothing about whether these shoes fit wide, cushion a bad knee, or survive a long walk around the park. I'm 62, not 22 — I don't buy shoes because they're trending on a cart, I buy them because my feet are telling me something.
- Would I click? Probably yes — mostly because the Men's section shows something that looks like a solid walking shoe and the BOGO or 20%-off-single-pair offer gives me flexibility. But I wouldn't forward this one. The broken preview, the Women's section leading the Men's section, and the zero personalization after years of being a Skechers Plus member make this feel like a mass blast, not a message to me.

## What's working

- **BOGO or 20% off a single pair** — that dual-offer structure is smart. I don't always want to buy two pairs, so the 20% single-pair escape hatch actually speaks to how I shop.
- **Slip-Ins, Arch Fit, Glide-Step tiles** — these three category labels are exactly my comfort checklist, all in one row. No hunting for what matters to me.
- **"Shop Men's Best Sellers" CTA** — specific enough. It says men, it says best sellers, it gives me a direction. Better than a generic "Discover More."
- **Email body renders clean** — no broken images, no layout collapse. After that preview text disaster, the interior at least held together.
- **BOGO offer is above the fold** — I see the deal the moment I open. No scrolling required to know there's a discount waiting.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is broken JSON — again.** The June 2nd email had the identical problem. Two months later, same bug. That's not a fluke, that's a production pipeline that nobody is checking.
- **Women's section runs before Men's.** I don't have anything against women's shoes, but I'm a 62-year-old guy. Lead with me, not after.
- **No personalization, no loyalty mention.** The June 1st "Member Month" email had my name right at the top with my points balance. This one treats me like a cold stranger. I've been a Plus member for a while now — act like it.
- **Hero model skews young.** The lifestyle shot reads like a college campus ad. The demographic signal doesn't land for someone my age.
- **"Best Sellers / Must-Haves" says nothing about comfort.** That copy could be from any shoe brand selling to any person. The three comfort-tech tiles save it later, but the hero had a chance to hook me with arch support or walking performance and whiffed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text pipeline before next send.** This is the most urgent issue. The schema.org JSON leak has now happened twice. Put a human eyes-on-preview QA step in the deployment checklist.
- 2. **Flip the section order: Men above Women** for segments like me. Skechers has my gender on file.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero copy around comfort, not clout.** Something like "The shoes your joints actually thank you for" beats "Best Sellers / Must-Haves" for a 62-year-old buyer.
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook.** One line — "Your Skechers Plus points can chip in on this" — and I'm engaged as a known customer, not a stranger.
- 5. **Give me one specific hero shoe in the Men's block** — a name, a price, a key feature. "Arch Fit Motley — $89 → $71 with BOGO" is more compelling than a category button.
- 6. **Add urgency.** The offer floats with no expiry. A "through Sunday" or "this week only" line would push me off the fence.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your most comfortable pair: BOGO or 20% off singles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushion, arch support, hands-free — our most-wanted comfort styles are on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO 50% or 20% off a single pair — shop Slip-Ins, Arch Fit, and more.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "See What Everyone's Adding To Their Cart"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken personalization token will render a visible empty greeting for all recipients. All product/content images are served over HTTP, creating a systemic mixed-content risk across every major email client that enforces HTTPS.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken destination links detected** — 26 click-redirect links through `click.emails.skechers.com` were skipped from HTTP probing (expected for ESP-wrapped tracking URLs).

**Third-party pixel exposure:** Three tracking pixels fire on open, including two Krux/Salesforce DMP user-match beacons (`beacon.krxd.net`) and one Return Path seed pixel (`pixel.app.returnpath.net`). The Return Path pixel is loaded over HTTP, not HTTPS:
```
src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1..."
```
No protocol prefix at all — this will resolve as a relative path in some parsers, silently dropping the inbox placement signal.

**Ink1000 pixel also HTTP:**
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif"
```
Served over plain HTTP. Will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook with cached proxy enforcement.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — systemic.** 10 of the visible content images are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com`. Gmail proxies all images over HTTPS; if the origin doesn't support HTTPS on that subdomain, images will break or silently fail. Affected images include the Skechers logo, app store badges, social icons, AfterPay badge, Curbside Pickup icon, and at least one unlabeled product image:

- `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png` (logo)
- `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/*.png` (all footer assets)

**Missing alt text on 2 images:**
- `o.gif` (Ink1000 tracking pixel) — minor; tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""`
- `49468f73-...png` — unlabeled content image; alt text absent entirely, not just empty

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is set globally. This suppresses iOS auto-scaling for users with accessibility font size preferences enabled. It should be `100%`, not `none`.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoints:** `@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)` and `@media only screen and (max-width: 375px)` share identical rule bodies, and a third block at `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` partially overlaps. No rendering failure, but adds ~1.2KB of redundant CSS that increases parse time in constrained clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**CRITICAL — Empty greeting rendered to all recipients.**

QA confirms: `Greeting has empty name field (e.g. "Hi ,")`. The merge token for subscriber first name evaluated to an empty string, meaning every recipient sees a broken salutation. This indicates either:
- The fallback/default value for the first-name field is blank (should be "there" or similar), or
- The token syntax failed to resolve in the ESP template engine

This is a user-facing defect shipped to the full send.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**No CAN-SPAM structural issues detected** from visible HTML — physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are present in the footer (standard for Skechers sends).

**Authentication headers** are not inspectable from HTML source alone; DMARC/DKIM/SPF status would require raw message headers. Not flagged as pass or fail here.

**Preheader text mismatch:** The `<div class="preheader">` reads:
> "Take 20% Off 1 Pair and Members Earn 2X Points on All Online Purchases"

Subject line is:
> "See What Everyone's Adding To Their Cart"

These serve different purposes and the mismatch is not a compliance issue, but the preheader (`display:none`) overrides are applied via both inline style and a `<style>` block — the duplicate suppression is redundant and could interact unpredictably with aggressive CSS strippers in some clients.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All 26 tracked links pass through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects — UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. The campaign ID `MKG_US_NONPURCLICK_U_BEST_NONPURCH_EN_06032026` appears in the Krux pixel and Ink1000 pixel, which is consistent internal tagging.

**Date in campaign ID is `06032026`** — June 3, 2026. Send date is presumably June 2026. No staleness issue if this is a re-send of a prior campaign, but worth confirming the campaign ID reflects the correct send window.

---

### 7. Recommendations

| Priority | Issue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Empty first-name merge token | Add a non-empty fallback (e.g., `%%=IIF(EMPTY(FirstName),"there",FirstName)=%%`) and re-validate before any future send |
| **P1** | All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets over HTTP | Enforce HTTPS on the image CDN subdomain; update all `src` attributes to `https://` |
| **P1** | Ink1000 + Return Path pixels over HTTP | Update to HTTPS endpoints or confirm vendor supports them; bare `pixel.app.returnpath.net` needs explicit `https://` prefix |
| **P2** | `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` | Change to `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` to preserve accessibility scaling on iOS |
| **P3** | Duplicate media query blocks | Consolidate `375px` and `640px` breakpoints; remove redundant `480px` override block |
