# These boots are only $49.99?! 😮
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- The subject got me. Boots at $49.99 sounds like a deal. The hero shows two ankle boots with "starting at $49.99" in pink, and I can see that. Then the email turns into UGG Season. Most of it is slippers and fuzzy boots, with a lot of women's and kids' product. The slip-on slippers did catch my eye, since I like something I can step into without bending over.
- This is the same DSW I've heard from all week: 20% off with JINGLE, then the $15 off, and now the "buy more, save more" ladder with code SOBRIGHT. It's the same offer as yesterday's "Have you used your $15 off yet?" and this morning's "Buy more = SAVE more." The boots headline is new, but the deal underneath is not.
- Would I click? Maybe once, if I wanted slippers. I'd skip the boots, because the ones pictured look like women's styles. I wouldn't forward it. I'd leave it unread for now.

## What's working

- The $49.99 boots banner is big and easy to read on my phone.
- The "Buy more, save more" ladder is simple: $15 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $199. I can do that math in my head.
- The shop-by-size grid with "Shop wide width" is useful. I have wide feet, and that's the kind of thing that gets me to click.
- The "Shop Men's UGG" button is there, and so is a "For Him" tag in the product photos.
- The layout is clean, with a clear top nav and plenty of white space.

## What's weak

- The $49.99 boots in the subject are not the star of the email. After the first banner it's all UGG, and the UGG prices are mostly missing. I only see one "$99.95" tag.
- Almost all the models and shoes are women's and kids'. The men's UGG section is a small button, and I saw no men's shoes pictured.
- There's nothing about cushioning, arch support, or walking comfort, and no comparison with Hoka or Brooks.
- The SOBRIGHT offer is buried below the UGG section. It's the same deal as yesterday and this morning.
- The fine print is long and the type is tiny. I'd need to zoom in to read the exclusions, and I can't tell whether UGG is excluded from the discount.
- The "Claim $15 off" button is fine, but I don't know if it applies to the boots.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the email about the boots the subject promised. Show a men's boot at $49.99, or say right away that the price is for women's. Don't switch to UGG in the second screen.
- 2. Put the savings ladder right under the hero, and say plainly whether UGG is included.
- 3. Add a men's row: slip-on slippers, a comfort boot, and a walking shoe, with prices.
- 4. Add a short line on comfort, like "cushioned insole" or "easy slip-on."
- 5. Vary the offer. After a week of the same code, I'm tuning out.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is structurally sound (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, dark-mode CSS, MSO resets). The one confirmed defect is the SMS opt-in CTA, which returned HTTP 400 when probed. Authentication status and most of the footer and compliance markup can't be verified from the data supplied. QA shows a 62% pass rate, with 1 failure and 2 warnings.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Broken link (400)** on the CTA "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off". The URL is an `ablink.dsw.com/f/a/OdCM8c8EishOXawpBFSD_g~~/AAQRxRA~/bNR5BTvx…` click-tracker. A 400 from the ESP redirect domain means the wrapped link was rejected. Likely causes are a malformed or truncated token, or an unresolved destination in the wrapped URL.
  - The probe may have been rejected as a non-browser request. Confirm with a real-client click before treating it as definitely dead for subscribers.
  - If it is dead, this is the highest-value CTA to fix, because it is an acquisition offer.
- **Coverage gap:** only 12 of 69 links were probed (57 skipped). The remaining links, including most product and footer links, are unverified.
- **Tracking pixels:** two are present. One is the `ablink.dsw.com/q/ULhWWhsiinNbO62ctO9Ruw~~/…` open pixel. The other is `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261027_P261610_V1_W-_Offer_Trend`. Running two separate open-tracking systems is redundant. The second also puts a user identifier (`mi_u`) in a query string.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images, both tracking pixels):** `ptPQ3uwEKeoPTDeIafLvmuIb-pnouwNsehxy8XqCeXJP6J0LIP…` and `o.gif`. Add `alt=""` so screen readers don't announce the filename or URL.
- **`<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>`** blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text) concern.
- **Ineffective meta tags:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it declares nothing.
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` has no `http-equiv` either.
  - The charset is probably covered by the Content-Type header, so the practical risk is low.
- **Plain-text part [WARN]:** 22,038 of 26,388 characters (about 84%) are URLs. The long tracking URLs dominate the text alternative, which is a spam-filter and readability risk for text-only clients.
- The rest of the markup couldn't be assessed because the HTML is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `{% %}`, `%%…%%`) appear in the visible source, though the body is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The `From` domain is `e.dsw.com` and the click-tracking domain is `ablink.dsw.com`, so check alignment at the source.
- The QA run says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but the findings don't report a result for it. The 12 probed links produced no unsubscribe failure, so there is no evidence of a problem. `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and the physical postal address aren't visible in the supplied data and can't be confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All links are wrapped in `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, so final destination URLs and UTM parameters can't be seen without following the redirects.
- The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=20261027_P261610_V1_W-_Offer_Trend`, inside the pixel URL. No UTM data was provided.
- Whether the "$49.99" boot claim in the subject matches the landing pages is unverified, because the probing didn't report landing-page content.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" link in a real browser. If it still fails, regenerate it in the ESP; it's the confirmed breakage.
2. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
4. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` to the two meta tags, or remove them.
5. Shorten the plain-text part by using shorter or non-wrapped links, and add more descriptive text, to bring the URL ratio down.
6. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC and the `List-Unsubscribe` headers from the raw message source. The relay stripped them, so they aren't in this data.
7. Raise probe coverage beyond 12 links, and resolve the redirect chains to verify UTM parameters and landing pages.
