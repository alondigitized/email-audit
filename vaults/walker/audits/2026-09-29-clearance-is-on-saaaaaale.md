## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders with a standard responsive and dark-mode scaffold, but the SMS opt-in CTA ("Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off") returned HTTP 400 in the QA probe. The plain-text part is also almost entirely tracking URLs. The source was truncated and only 12 of 71 links were probed, so some checks below can't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (FAIL):** The link text "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" points to `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/0qQ9CYMUOCMHOT6Vmy-cHw~~/AAQRxRA~/UNm91L28…Q~~`, which returned **400**.
  - This is a tracked redirect on the `ablink.dsw.com` click-tracking domain. A 400 there usually means the tracking token is malformed, truncated, or expired, not that the destination page is down.
  - It could also be a probe artifact. The probe may have rejected a recipient-specific token, so re-test it from a real inbox click.
  - If it is real, it's the highest-impact defect, because this CTA promises a 15% incentive.
- **Coverage gap:** 59 of 71 links were not probed, mostly footer, utility, and social links. Their status is unknown.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20260929_P251284_V1_WM-_Offer_MI_D` is a personalization or open pixel. It carries a numeric subscriber identifier (`mi_u`) in the query string, which is expected for this kind of pixel.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images):**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/8yuQ_gLm_Y--VjK_ufdrCg~~/AAQRxRA~/r_JnsXA8dZxEoM1qTKTF4tAaLaVl5-4I1FLsCNxYb5sTAKAAW8…`
  - `…/o.gif` (the tracking pixel)
  - The pixel is low impact but should have `alt=""`. The first image is a tracked link, so it is likely a content or link image that needs real alt text. If it is a linked image with no alt, screen readers will announce the raw URL.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom where the client honors it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Malformed meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither declares anything. The charset is normally supplied by the MIME `Content-Type` header, so the practical risk is low.
- **Plain-text part:** 27,648 of 32,395 characters (about 85%) are URLs, over the 70% warning threshold. This comes from long tracked URLs and can hurt deliverability scoring.
