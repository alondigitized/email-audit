# Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your November Newsletter
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas email. That makes seven or eight since the 14th, and this one doesn't sell me anything. It's a newsletter full of giveaways: an MLS fan prize, a shoe-of-the-month raffle, a Billie Jean King thing, and a cartoon Toca Boca collab. I'm 62 and not shopping for a pink soccer jersey or an Apple TV subscription. After the Black Friday "up to 60% off" emails, this felt like a different brand wandered in.
- The one thing I liked was the BW Army shoe. It's a plain, cushioned-looking sneaker and the closest thing here to my feet. But it's a drawing for a prize, not a price. Nothing in this email gives me a reason to buy this week.
- I'd mark it read and move on. I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. I might scroll back later for the "member appreciation weekend" countdown, but that's 15 days away.

## What's working

- The BW Army shoe is a clean, low-profile casual sneaker. It's the only product here that looks like something I'd wear.
- The black "ENTER NOW" buttons are big and easy to see. I didn't have to squint or hunt for them.
- The headlines are in big plain caps, so I can read them on my phone without zooming.
- The adiclub branding and the member weekend countdown at the bottom tell me I'm being treated as a member, not a stranger.

## What's weak

- There's no price, percent off, or sale anywhere. That's odd given what they've been sending since the 14th.
- The banner at the top is cut off, so it reads "OUR adiclub NEWSLETTER • Y". It looks sloppy.
- The MLS jersey and the Toca Boca cartoon characters are aimed at a much younger crowd than me.
- Almost every button is a vague "ENTER NOW". I can't tell what I'm entering or what I'd win without reading the small gray text.
- The Billie Jean King and Toca Boca blocks have no shoe detail. I get nothing on cushioning, fit or comfort.
- The asterisk on the shoe giveaway sends me to fine print at the very bottom, which makes the whole thing feel less honest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one clear member perk, like a discount or free shipping, instead of three giveaways. A newsletter should give members something to use.
- 2. Put a comfort or everyday-walking shoe at the top. Show the BW Army with a price and a "shop now" button, not only a raffle entry.
- 3. Fix the clipped header banner so the full title shows.
- 4. Make the buttons say what happens, like "Enter to win BW Army shoes", and move the countdown higher so the deadline is visible.
- 5. Cut the cartoon block, or move it out of a newsletter going to a mixed-age audience.
