# PSA: BOGO 50% OFF IS ON
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- DSW knows I'm going to open this — at this point it's muscle memory. Their name in my sender column and my thumb is already moving. "BOGO 50% OFF IS ON" is the same core deal they ran three days ago on June 22, just dressed up with a "PSA" label and a "HOT DEAL SUMMER" badge. Same math, different wrapper. When I audited that June 22 send I gave it a 9 because the BOGO framing was fresh and the math clicked. Third time seeing this offer in a week, the needle starts to move.
- The hero itself is easy on my eyes — oversized bold text, I can read it without my reading glasses — and BOGO is the kind of math that actually works on me at the register. Buy one, get the second half off. My brain immediately starts scanning what I'd pair together: the slip-ons my feet have been asking for, maybe a walking shoe alongside. That's exactly how they want me thinking.
- But then I scroll past the big black headline and hit the "So New, So You" product grid, and it's heeled sandals and strappy fashion shoes. That section may as well be blank for a 62-year-old man looking for cushioned walkers. The size picker at the bottom — including wide width — tells me DSW hasn't forgotten about feet like mine, but the visual middle of this email is pointed at a different customer entirely. I'd mark it read and might come back if the deal is still running when I actually need a second pair — but I'm not clicking today.

## What's working

- **Hero is bulletproof for legibility.** "BOGO 50% OFF*" in massive bold type — readable without zooming, the math hits instantly.
- **"HOT DEAL SUMMER" seasonal badge** makes it feel timely, not like recycled inventory sitting in a template.
- **SHOP BY SIZE grid** is genuinely useful — I know my size, I want to filter fast, and having it right in the email saves me tapping around the site.
- **SHOP WIDE WIDTH button** is a real signal. Comfort shoppers like me need that option, and it's visible without digging.
- **VIP free shipping reminder** in the Shopping Made Easy section reinforces that my loyalty is paying off — good soft touchpoint for retention.

## What's weak

- **Same BOGO deal, third delivery in four days.** June 22 was "starting NOW," and now it's a "PSA." Repackaging the same promotion with a different subject wrapper is starting to feel like they're running out of ideas, not like a real event.
- **"So New, So You" grid is the wrong product for me.** Heeled sandals, strappy mules — that's not my aisle at DSW. A man shopping for walking shoes scrolls right past that section.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is vague.** Shop what — the full site? Men's sale? Just dumps me at the front door. I want a shortcut to where I actually shop.
- **No deadline anywhere.** "This deal won't last" is not a deadline. I need "ends Sunday" or "ends tonight" to believe I shouldn't come back tomorrow. Vague urgency produces deferred clicks, which means no clicks.
- **Preview text leaks garbage characters.** "And hurry — this deal won't last" is solid copy, but the wall of filler characters after it renders as inbox clutter on my phone. It looks broken.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real deadline in the email.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" somewhere visible — in the hero subtext or stamped on the CTA button. That converts "I'll check later" to "I should click now."
- 2. **Swap the "So New, So You" grid for men's comfort picks.** Two or three cushioned walkers or slip-ons with a brief callout — memory foam, arch support, whatever DSW leads with — and this email suddenly feels built for me.
- 3. **Strengthen the CTA.** "SHOP MEN'S BOGO PICKS" or "SHOP COMFORT STYLES" beats "SHOP NOW" every time. Give my thumb a reason to believe the click goes somewhere worth it.
- 4. **Clean the preview text.** "And hurry — this deal won't last" is good copy; the filler junk after it is not. Cut it so the inbox preview looks like a professional send.
- 5. **Acknowledge the repeat.** If this is the third BOGO send this week, lead with something like "Still running — but not for long." Don't pretend I haven't seen this before; I have a sent history.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Invalid CSS property name in all `@font-face` blocks**

Every `@font-face` declaration uses a capitalized `Src:` instead of the required lowercase `src:`. CSS property names are case-sensitive in some parsers and invalid per spec. This will silently fail in strict parsers, causing fallback to system fonts.

```css
/* Found 9 times: */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
/* Should be: */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```

**Issue: `text-size-adjust: none` overrides `100%` in the same rule**

```css
* {
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* ← duplicate, overrides the 100% above */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

`none` disables text auto-scaling entirely. On mobile, this can cause unreadable small text in some clients if the email viewport isn't fully controlled. The safe value is `100%`.

**Issue: Font format mismatch — TTF fonts loaded as `.ttf` without `woff2` fallback**

The HelveticaNow family loads `.ttf` files only. All modern mail clients that support web fonts also support `woff2`, which is ~30% smaller. No `woff2` source is provided, adding unnecessary payload weight.
