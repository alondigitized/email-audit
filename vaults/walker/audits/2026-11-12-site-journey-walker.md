# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-12**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker, a 62-year-old comfort-first shopper on his phone, and this walkthrough didn't get me to a shoe in my cart. Getting to a product was fine. The Men menu is clear, and the Men's Shoes page has a "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile. The Slip-ins Contour Foam page shows $88, 4.5 stars and 1,977 reviews. Then it stopped working for me. When I hit **Add to Cart**, the page said "Please select a size" and the bag icon still read **0**. The cart screenshot is a completely blank white screen. The "comfort shoes" search returned 1,657 results, but the first two were women's shoes. I never saw a logged-in state on any screen.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10** (yesterday: 3/10)

I only have yesterday's score, not its write-up, so I can't list exactly what changed. This looks like a small improvement because the browsing path holds together and search returns results. The score stays low because the add-to-cart step didn't complete and the cart screen was blank. A shopper who can't get a shoe into the cart is a lost sale.

## 3. What's Working

- **The Men menu is easy to use.** Large text, one tap per row, and each row has an arrow (Shoes, Shop by Activity, **Comfort Technologies**, Collections). A man my age can use it without zooming.
- **The top chips speak to me.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cus…" are what I'm shopping for.
- **The Men's Shoes page has a "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile and a "Walking Shoes" tile.** That's the quickest route to what I want.
- **The product page is readable.** The price is big and bold, and the star rating and review count are easy to see. The Slip-ins logo is shown, and the Klarna and Afterpay lines are clear.
- **The size buttons are big enough to tap.** They are roughly thumb-sized, and sold-out sizes are clearly crossed out. The **Add to Cart** button is wide, dark blue and unmissable.
- **The search results page is tidy.** Prices are large, and each shoe shows its colors and the "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF" offer.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Add to Cart didn't work.** The page showed a red "Please select a size" warning and the bag count was still 0. The error message is fair, but it sits below the size grid, so I wouldn't have seen it if I'd tapped from the top. The test also never picked a size.
- **The cart screenshot is blank white.** I can't tell whether the cart page failed to load, loaded slowly or crashed. Either way, I'd assume the site was broken and leave.
- **The search results didn't fit me.** I searched "comfort shoes" as a man and the first two results were a Women's wedge and a Women's slipper. Skechers knows I browsed Men's. Nothing narrowed the results to Men's.
- **Nothing here is personal.** The login step looks the same as the homepage, with no "Hi, Walker," no saved items and no recent views. I can't tell whether I was logged in.
- **The homepage doesn't say "comfort."** The hero is a holiday photo of two young people in dressy clothes, with an "AI-generated models" caption in the corner. Nothing on it addresses my foot, so I'd have to find the chips myself.
- **There are too many banners stacked at the top.** "BOGO 50% OFF," "25% OFF Work & Safety" and the chip row all take up space before any shoe appears. On a phone, that's a lot of scrolling before I see a product.
- **The Men's Shoes grid starts with loud, multicolored fashion sneakers.** I wanted a calm slip-in or walking shoe. The page also shows 810 results with no hint of where to start.
- **There's no comparison or price-vs-quality content.** Nothing compares Skechers to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and nothing helps me weigh $88 against a pair I'd wear for years. "Applied at Cart" discounts also make me do the math myself.
- **Some small things are hard to use.** The filter chips (Size, Width) run off the right edge. The bell icons on sold-out sizes are tiny. "Check in-store availability" needs a color, width and size first, and nothing explains why.
- **The step names don't match the screens.** Steps 1, 2 and 3 show the identical homepage image, and step 4 shows the Men menu drawer rather than a category page.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make sure a successful add updates the bag count, and make sure /cart/ never renders blank. Show a visible error or a loading state if something goes wrong. Fixing this matters more than anything else on the list.
2. **Make the size error visible.** Scroll to it, or put the message right at the Add to Cart button. For a size-first test, select a size before adding.
3. **Keep the shopper's context in search.** If someone browsed Men's, default "comfort shoes" to Men's, or show a one-tap Men's / Women's toggle at the top.
4. **Add a comfort-first landing path on the homepage.** Put "Hands-Free Slip-ins," "Arch Support" and "Walking" in the first screen, with real shoes shown. Replace or supplement the holiday fashion hero.
5. **Show that the shopper is logged in.** A simple "Hi, Walker" and "Recently viewed" would be enough.
6. **Trim the stacked banners.** Combine the two offers into one line so shoes appear sooner.
7. **Add a short "Which comfort shoe is right for me?" guide.** Cover cushioning, arch support and slip-in, with price-per-year-of-wear math and an honest note about how Skechers compares to other comfort brands. That would earn a shopper like me.
8. **Make the small targets bigger.** That means the filter chips, the bell icons and the "Check in-store availability" row.

## 6. Bottom Line

I got to a shoe I'd consider, the Slip-ins Contour Foam at $88, but I couldn't buy it. The shopping path is easy to follow, and an empty cart behind a blank screen undoes that. Skechers has the right products and the right words in the menu ("Hands Free," "Arch Fit," "Comfort Technologies"). It needs a cart that works and a search that remembers I'm a man shopping for comfort. Until then, I'd go to Brooks or New Balance. I'm up a point from yesterday's 3/10, mostly because the browsing path works, and the cart failure keeps the score low.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- Scope: 8 mobile journey steps with 18 axe violations (2 critical, 3 serious), 21 console errors, and 1 network error (429 on `/cart/`).
- **Data gaps:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. TTFB is the only metric available.
- **Cart step is probably invalid:** `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` returned HTTP 429. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare rate-limit or error page, not the real cart. The 429 is probably the monitor being throttled by bot or rate protection. The cart's accessibility is therefore unaudited.
- **Step URLs are unreliable:** The Homepage, Log In and Men category HTML samples all report `https://www.skechers.com/` with near-identical `<head>` content. Either those steps are in-page overlays or SPA transitions, or the URL and HTML were captured before navigation completed. Per-step conclusions for those steps are weaker as a result.
- **Third-party load:** The homepage loads a large number of third-party tags. These include GTM, Monetate, Quantum Metric, TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Taboola, Reddit, Nextdoor, Facebook, Bing, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House, Bloomreach and PulseInsights.

## Accessibility

**Homepage**
- **[critical] `aria-required-parent`:** an element with a child-only ARIA role (e.g. `listitem`, `tab` or `menuitem`) is not inside its required parent.
- **[critical] `button-name`:** one or more buttons have no accessible name, probably icon-only controls.
- **[serious] `link-name`:** one or more links have no discernible text, probably image or icon links.
- **[moderate] Landmark structure:**
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`: more than one `main`.
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level`: `main` is nested inside another landmark.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: `aside` is nested.
  - `landmark-unique`: duplicate landmarks with no distinguishing label.
  - `region`: content sits outside landmarks.
  - These suggest the page is assembled from stacked templates or components that each render their own `main`.
- **[moderate] `page-has-heading-one`:** no `<h1>`.
- **[minor] `aria-allowed-role`:** a role is set on an element that doesn't permit it.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- Same pattern: `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`.

**View Cart**
- **[serious] `document-title`** and **`html-has-lang`**, plus **[moderate] `landmark-one-main`** and **`page-has-heading-one`**. These likely come from the 429 response, not the real cart (see Technical Summary).

**Not covered:** Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart reported no axe violations. Given the URL and HTML ambiguity above, don't treat that as confirmed clean.

## SEO

- **Homepage `<h1>` missing:** `page-has-heading-one` fires on the Homepage and View Cart.
- **`lang` set correctly on the homepage:** `<html lang="en">` is present. `html-has-lang` fails only on the cart response.
- **Titles and canonicals:** The truncated HTML samples don't include the `<title>`, meta description, canonical or structured data. Those can't be assessed.
- **Possible empty cart response:** The 429 response had no `<title>` (`document-title` fails). If that response is served to crawlers, it would hurt indexing. `/cart/` is normally noindex, so the impact is probably low.

## Performance

- **TTFB (good, all under 800ms):**
  - Homepage 483ms
  - Log In 399ms
  - Men 399ms
  - Men > Shoes 297ms
  - View Cart 267ms
  - Product 26ms
  - Add to Cart 26ms
  - Search 21ms
- **TTFB of 21–26ms** on Product, Add to Cart and Search is implausibly low for a document request. It suggests cache or bfcache hits or same-document navigation, so those figures likely don't reflect real server response time.
- **LCP and CLS missing** for every step. This is likely an observer or timing issue in the harness, such as the `PerformanceObserver` not being registered before load or the metric not being read. It should be fixed before drawing conclusions.
- **Third-party script weight:** 20+ async third-party scripts (see Technical Summary), several of them ad-tech pixels loaded on every page. They compete for main-thread time and bandwidth, which can hurt INP and LCP.
- **GTM preview environment in production:** The GTM snippet is `gtm.js?id=GTM-TDRBQZ&gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x`. The `gtm_preview` and `gtm_auth` parameters load a non-default environment snapshot instead of the Live container. Confirm this is intentional. If not, production may be serving unpublished tag changes.
- **Webfont loading:** Mulish loads through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, which is async and a third-party dependency. This means a font-loading delay and an extra DNS and TLS connection. The `wf-*-active` classes appear, but FOUT or layout shift can't be assessed without CLS data.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport:** `--vh: 6.64px` indicates the viewport-height variable was set, consistent with a ~664px-high mobile viewport, so the JS vh workaround is active.
- **Viewport meta, touch targets and responsive images:** Not verifiable from the truncated HTML or the axe output. The unnamed buttons and links above are also likely to be hard to use with a screen reader on mobile.
- **Navigation reliability on mobile:** The journey shows the same URL across steps, and the cart step failed with a 429. Both should be confirmed on a real device.

## Console & Network Errors

- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (5 occurrences):** This is Chrome's built-in Summarizer API rejecting the requested language. Something on the page, probably a third-party script, calls the API without specifying a supported language. It aborts harmlessly, but it shows a script calling experimental browser AI APIs. Identify and fix the caller.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document.` (≈8 occurrences, between the Summarizer errors):** A script registers an `unload` handler, which Chrome is deprecating and blocking via Permissions-Policy. This is typically third-party analytics. It prevents bfcache eligibility and will break once enforcement tightens. Find the source via the stack trace.
- **Exact counts:** The header says 21 errors, but only 13 lines are listed here. The remaining 8 weren't provided, so the breakdown above covers only the listed ones.
- **Network: `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/`:** Rate limiting, probably against the automated browser. This invalidates the View Cart step, and it matters for real users behind shared IPs or aggressive retries.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the harness first.** Capture LCP and CLS (and INP). Record the real URL after each navigation, and retry View Cart after a back-off or with a realistic session so it isn't rate-limited. Re-run the cart audit.
2. **Investigate the 429 on `/cart/`.** Check whether the bot/WAF rate limit is too aggressive for legitimate users, and whether the error page is served with a proper title, `lang` and landmarks.
3. **Fix the critical axe issues on the homepage.**
   - Add accessible names to icon buttons and links (`aria-label` or visible text).
   - Correct the parent/child ARIA structure.
4. **Fix the landmark structure.**
   - Use one top-level `<main>`.
   - Make `aside` elements top-level.
   - Label repeated landmarks (for example, `aria-label` on each `nav`).
   - Wrap remaining content in landmarks.
5. **Add one `<h1>`** per page. Fix the invalid `role` usage (`aria-allowed-role`).
6. **Verify the GTM container on production.** Remove `gtm_preview=env-1` and `gtm_auth` if it isn't intentional, and publish tag changes through the Live environment.
7. **Audit third-party tags.** Remove unused pixels, load the rest after interaction or via GTM with consent gating, and measure the effect on LCP and INP.
8. **Remove `unload` listeners.** Replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, and find which vendor registers them.
9. **Identify the caller of the Summarizer API** and pass a supported language, or remove the call.
10. **Self-host the Mulish webfonts** with `font-display: swap` and preload to remove the cdnjs dependency.
