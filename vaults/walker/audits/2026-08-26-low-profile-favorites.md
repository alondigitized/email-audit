# Low profile favorites
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- Six New Balance emails deep this month and this one's another product grid — "Low profile favorites," which tells me nothing about who it's for. The hero shot is somebody's feet up on a couch wearing 471s, fine, comfortable-looking sneaker, but then it's six shoe tiles — three of which are the same style in two colors — followed by a young gal in a crop top and midi skirt modeling clothes I have zero interest in. That "Complete the look" section with the sports bra and cropped jacket tells me this send wasn't built with a guy my age in mind at all.
- I'd glance at the 204L in that dark brown — that's a chunkier, more supportive-looking shoe than the skinny 471s — but there's no price, no discount, no mention of arch support or cushioning, nothing that tells me why today's the day to buy. Not clicking, not forwarding this one. Straight to "read and forgotten."

## What's working

- The hero image of the 471 on the couch is a nice lifestyle shot, low-key and not overly staged.
- I recognize the sender immediately — New Balance is a brand I already cross-shop.
- The "471" and "204L" and "T500" styles are laid out clean with clear product shots, easy to scan.
- Free shipping banner up top ($75+) is at least something concrete.

## What's weak

- No offer or discount anywhere — just "shop now," which after seeing three sale emails from these guys this month feels like a letdown.
- The clothing section (sports bra, cropped jacket, midi skirt) is aimed at a young woman, not me — makes the email feel like it wasn't built for my inbox at all.
- Six product tiles with no info on comfort, fit, or arch support — the stuff that actually gets me to buy a shoe.
- No price shown on any shoe, so I can't do my price-vs-quality math without clicking through.
- "Low profile favorites" as a headline doesn't say who favors these or why I should care.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "Complete the look" apparel block entirely for a segment like mine, or replace it with men's casual pieces — showing me women's activewear tells me this wasn't targeted.
- 2. Add price and one comfort callout (cushioned insole, arch support) under at least the hero shoe so I don't have to click to know if it's worth my time.
- 3. Lead with an actual offer — even 15% off would beat "shop now" with nothing behind it, especially after three straight sends this month that did have a discount.
- 4. Trim the product grid to 3-4 styles instead of 6 near-duplicates; showing the same shoe in two colors twice feels like padding.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The 471 and 204L, back in stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, slip-easy styles men are grabbing this week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what's new in the 471 and 204L lineup`
