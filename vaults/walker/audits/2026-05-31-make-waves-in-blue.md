## What's weak

- "Blue now trending" in the hero is fashion talk — that's not why I open Crocs emails; I want to know if my feet will feel better, not what color is hot
- No actual offer anywhere above the fold — no price, no discount percentage, nothing concrete
- The preheader is completely invisible noise (those spacer characters again); wasted real estate that could've teased the comfort angle
- Four shop buttons at the bottom (Clogs, Jibbitz/Charms, Sale, Sandals) is one too many — Jibbitz and Sandals aren't why I'm here, and cluttering the footer dilutes the Clogs/Sale buttons I'd actually use
- After ten-plus Crocs emails in May, another send with no meaningful offer feels like they forgot I exist as a customer and not just a click statistic

Gmail (webmail over HTTPS) will block mixed-content HTTP image loads; these icons will render as broken images for Gmail users. All product/hero images correctly use `https://media.crocs.com/`.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute** — `<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1" src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">` has no `alt=""`. Screen readers will attempt to read the full URL string. Fix: `alt=""`.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta** — `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. Some older clients use this tag for charset negotiation. Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`.

**Custom font fallback** — `@font-face` declarations load `TT Crocs` (400/700/900 weights) from `media.crocs.com`. Gmail and Outlook do not support web fonts and will silently fall back to the system default. No explicit `font-family` fallback stack (e.g., `Arial, sans-serif`) was observed in the `@font-face` blocks. Verify that fallback rendering is tested and acceptable.

**`font-display: swap`** — valid CSS but ignored by all email clients. Not a bug, just dead code.
