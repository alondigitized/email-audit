# Dressy styles from $34.99!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- My thumb opened this the same way it's opened every DSW for the past two weeks — pure reflex. But then the email loaded and I read "Va Va Va Bloom" over a spread of pink and gold heeled sandals, and whatever residual interest the "$34.99" subject-line hook gave me evaporated instantly. I'm sixty-two, I'm looking for something that won't make my arches scream on a long walk, and DSW just served me a runway editorial for women's strappy heels. The brand-sale header at the top — New Balance, Crocs, up to 25% off — that's actually relevant to me. The clearance section at the bottom is useful. Everything sandwiched in the middle is for someone who isn't me.
- I'll mark it read and maybe scroll back to the clearance banner when I actually need shoes. I would not forward this to anyone; there's nothing here worth passing along to a buddy who's also hunting for a comfortable walking sandal.

## What's working

- **Brand-sale header lands first.** New Balance and Crocs are right there above the fold with a 25%-off callout — two brands I actually cross-shop, front and center.
- **Clearance section with 50% off** is a legitimate pull for a value shopper like me. I always drift to the sale aisle first, so knowing clearance is restocked matters.
- **Shop by Size grid** is a nice functional touch. I can tap my size and skip the browse friction. That's something an older shopper appreciates on a phone.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, nothing confusing on the layout front. Easy enough to read at arm's length.
- **Price anchor in subject.** "$34.99" in the subject is specific — it made me curious for about three seconds.

## What's weak

- **Va Va Va Bloom is not my aisle.** The hero creative is women's fashion heels in candy colors. Once I see that, the email has already lost me emotionally — I feel like I opened someone else's mail.
- **"Dressy" in the subject is a red flag.** I'm not shopping for dress shoes. I want cushioning and slip-on ease. The word "dressy" is actively a signal to scroll past.
- **No comfort callout anywhere.** Arch support, cushioning, memory foam, slip-resistant sole — none of it appears. These words cost nothing to add and they're the whole reason I'm on DSW's list in the first place.
- **Preheader is wasted space.** "Your summer of savings starts now" is so generic it tells me nothing I couldn't have guessed from the DSW sender alone.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is somewhere around the eighth email in ten days. Even with the pivot away from the "20% off" drumbeat, my goodwill is running thin. The June 15 Dad's Day send was a genuinely fresh hook — this one just feels like filler volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men's emails away from women's fashion heroes.** A Men's Summer Sale email with a comfort-shoe hero — Brooks, New Balance, a Crocs slide — would have me in the CTA before I finish reading the first line. The Va Va Va Bloom creative shouldn't reach my inbox.
- 2. **Lead the hero with a comfort or walking-shoe story, not "dressy."** If $34.99 is the price anchor, show me a walking shoe or casual slip-on at that price, not a platform heel.
- 3. **Give the clearance section more real estate.** That 50% off clearance mention near the bottom is buried. For a price-sensitive shopper, that should be at least co-hero.
- 4. **Put a concrete comfort attribute in the preheader.** Something like "New Balance + Crocs deals — cushioned styles from $34.99" earns the tap before I even open.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject for the right customer segment.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Comfort brands up to 25% off — New Balance, Crocs + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's styles from $34.99 — NB, Crocs, Brooks on sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 50% off clearance + top comfort brands — shop your size`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Walking shoes, sandals + more — grab your DSW deals before they're gone`

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv`** (malformed tag):


**`@font-face` uses capitalized `Src:` property** across all 10 declarations. CSS properties are case-insensitive per spec, but this is non-standard and trips strict XML parsers (the doctype is XHTML, which is XML):
```css
/* current — non-standard capitalization */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
/* should be */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** prevents user zoom — WCAG 1.4.4 failure on mobile:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**`-ms-text-size-adjust: none`** in the universal `*` reset disables text scaling entirely in legacy Windows Mail clients, rather than the standard `100%` value. This conflicts with the `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` on the same rule:
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* correct */
   -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
   -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides the line above — should be removed or unified */
```

**`supported-color-schemes` in CSS `:root` is not a valid CSS property** — it belongs only in the `<meta>` tag (already present). The declaration in `:root` is a no-op and should be removed to avoid confusion:
```css
:root {
  color-scheme: light dark;          /* valid */
  supported-color-schemes: light dark; /* invalid here — remove */
}
```
