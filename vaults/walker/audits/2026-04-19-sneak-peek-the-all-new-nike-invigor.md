# Sneak Peek: The All-New Nike Invigor
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-19**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival missed the mark with me entirely. I'm a Skechers guy — comfort, slip-ins, nothing too flashy — and this email is wall-to-wall Nike Air Max and Jordan. That "Take Flight" section with the basketball shoe? I don't play basketball. I'm 62. The whole thing felt like it was built for a 22-year-old sneakerhead, not someone like me who just wants a good-looking shoe that doesn't kill my feet after a few hours of errands. I scrolled through it on my phone and it was cramped, busy, and nothing jumped out and said "hey Walker, this one's for you." I wouldn't click anything here.
- Low relevance to my profile. One decent coupon at the bottom almost saves it, but the rest is noise I'd swipe past.

## What's working

- **Coupon at the bottom** — "$10 or $25 off" with an online code is the only thing that made me pause. I like a deal. That part speaks my language.
- **"Back & Better" Reax 8 section** — $59.98 price point is reasonable and the shoe didn't look outrageous. Not my style, but at least it's grounded.

## What's weak

- **Wrong shoes, wrong persona.** Air Max Invigor and Jordan "Take Flight"? That's not me. I need comfort tech, cushioned insoles, maybe a wider fit — none of that is visible anywhere.
- **Too many sections competing for attention.** Hero, then Reax 8, then athletic sale, then Jordan, then a points promo, then a sweepstakes, then a coupon — that's six different asks in one email. On my phone it felt like scrolling through a cluttered flyer.
- **Points multiplier (2-3X) — irrelevant to me.** I'm a Skechers Plus member. I don't have a Shoe Carnival rewards account. This whole section means nothing to me.
- **Text is small.** The fine print and even some section headers looked tiny on a phone screen. I'd have to pinch-zoom.
- **No men's comfort or casual angle anywhere.** Nothing said "wide widths," "memory foam," "slip-on," or "all-day comfort." Those are the words that get my attention.
- **Jordan brand skews way too young.** I associate Jordan shoes with my grandkids, not with me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by purchase history.** If I've never bought Nike running shoes, don't lead with a Nike running shoe email. Lead with something closer to what I've actually looked at.
- 2. **Swap the hero for comfort/casual men's.** A slip-on walking shoe or a lightweight casual sneaker with cushioning would stop my scroll.
- 3. **Surface the coupon higher.** That "$25 off" offer buried at the bottom? That's your hook for a price-conscious customer like me. Lead with it or at least put it in the top third.
- 4. **Simplify the layout for mobile.** Pick two or three sections max. One hero, one offer, one CTA. I don't need six competing buckets.
- 5. **Use bigger, bolder text.** Subheadings are too small for phone browsing. I shouldn't have to squint.
