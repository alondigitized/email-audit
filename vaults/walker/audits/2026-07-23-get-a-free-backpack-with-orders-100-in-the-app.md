# Get a free backpack with orders $100+ in the app
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Well, I'll give adidas credit for one thing: this is the first email they've sent me in weeks that doesn't have a soccer jersey or a World Cup trophy in it. Every send since mid-July — July 17th "THE FINAL AWAITS," July 19th Spain wins, July 21st Juventus kit — has been football this and FIFA that. So when I saw "free backpack" in my inbox, I thought maybe they finally remembered I have feet, not a dribbling habit.
- But I opened it and realized they just swapped one miss for another. The whole email is about a bag. A backpack. I'm 62 years old — my backpack days ended around 1988. There's not a single shoe in this email. No cushioning, no walking comfort, no arch support, nothing about the foot. It's a gift-with-purchase offer that feels designed for a college kid heading into September, not a retired guy who walks the neighborhood every morning and wants to know if those Hoka Bondi 9s are worth the price bump.
- On top of that, the offer only works in the app. I use my phone fine, but I don't want to download and log into an app just to chase a backpack I don't need. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **Offer is legible and clear** — "FREE BACKPACK WITH ORDERS $100+" is in big, readable type. I didn't have to squint or zoom to understand the deal.
- **No soccer content** — I know that's a low bar, but after six straight weeks of football emails, the absence of a jersey is genuinely a relief.
- **No spam energy** — Clean subject line, no excessive caps or exclamation points, doesn't feel like a scam. That counts for something.
- **Fine print is present** — The offer terms are in the footer. I respect that they didn't hide the "while supplies last" caveat entirely.

## What's weak

- **Not a single shoe in the email** — For a footwear brand emailing someone subscribed for footwear, this is a fundamental miss. Zero product I'd actually buy.
- **App-only offer is friction I won't push through** — Requiring me to open or download an app to redeem a deal on a bag I didn't want anyway? Forget it. That's a deal-killer for my generation.
- **Young model, wrong audience** — The hero image reads like it was designed for a 22-year-old commuter. Not one demographic signal aimed at me.
- **The backpack itself is the star** — I don't need a bag. I need shoes. Using the bag as a hero rather than the shoes I might actually buy has the whole thing backwards.
- **No urgency in the body copy** — "While supplies last" is buried in fine print. If they wanted me to act now, they should have made the deadline visible above the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with shoes, not the bag** — Flip the hero: show a shoe I'd actually buy (something from the Ultraboost or Response Super line, anything with cushioning and walking credibility), then call out the free backpack as a bonus for hitting $100. The shoe sells; the bag closes the deal.
- 2. **Drop the app requirement or offer a fallback** — Either extend the offer to the website or add a clear "shop on adidas.com" secondary CTA. Locking the deal to an app burns older shoppers like me every time.
- 3. **Put a real deadline on it** — "July 25 only" or "this weekend" in the body copy, not buried in fine print. Time pressure works; fine print does not.
- 4. **Target the offer to footwear browsers** — If they know I've been clicking on walking shoes, put a pair I've looked at in this email. The bag as a reward only works when I want the main product badly enough to add to cart.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free bag on us — grab it before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $100 on shoes, we'll throw in the bag`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New kicks + a free backpack. App-only, limited supply.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free backpack when you spend $100 this weekend only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "Free Backpack" Promotional Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a malformed internal tracking link using a relative-style path scheme, and a missing plain-text fallback. Six repeated HTTP image warnings point to a single non-HTTPS asset used multiple times.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Malformed click-redirect URL**
The QA probe flagged `unknown url type: '/g/74a47bcf-...'` on this destination URL:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/74a47bcf-6a8d-4607-af9b-5c7e4e1eade9?cp_tp=v3...
```
The `/g/` path in the pre-redirect target suggests a relative or protocol-stripped URL is being passed as the redirect destination inside the ESP click-wrapper. If the ESP constructs the final URL from this value, it will produce a non-navigable link for recipients. This needs to be verified against the ESP link-building template.

**WARN — 28 tracking links skipped**
All outbound links pass through `click.comms.adidas.com` (ESP click-wrapper). The HTTP probe could not validate destination reachability for any of them. No broken destinations were confirmed, but the malformed `/g/` link above is within this pool.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Non-HTTPS image asset (repeated 6×)**
The same arrow image is embedded six times via HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail and Outlook 365 proxy images over HTTPS; other clients (Outlook desktop, some iOS configurations with strict mixed-content policies) may block or show a broken image. The six-occurrence count suggests this asset is reused across multiple content rows — a single asset fix resolves all instances.

**WARN — Two images missing `alt` text**
- `74a47bcf-6a8d-4607-af9b-5c7e4e1eade9` (served from `dv.adidas.com`) — no `alt` attribute
- `4Q9yEnUpw84ciQKByIED6W38izm-uf2DDVd3CMkmbcU=452` (served from `click.comms.adidas.com`, likely a tracking pixel) — no `alt` attribute

The tracking pixel omission is low-risk; the `dv.adidas.com` image is likely a visible content asset and should carry descriptive alt text.

**INFO — Custom fonts fall back to system fonts only**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared via `@font-face` but `src` points exclusively to `local()` references (Arial, Helvetica, Calibri). No web font URL (`url()`) is present. This is intentional for email (avoids external font load latency) but means the brand typeface never renders unless already installed on the device — confirmed by design, not a bug.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `*|`, `%%`) detected in the HTML source excerpt. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**FAIL — Plain-text alternative is absent**
The QA check confirmed `Text version is 0 chars`. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a plain-text part, but its absence degrades deliverability scoring (SpamAssassin penalizes HTML-only messages) and breaks accessibility for text-only mail agents.

**INFO — `NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `no-referrer` present**
```html
<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
Both are correct practice for email HTML — prevents indexing of web-view mirrors and suppresses referrer leakage from click-throughs. No issues.

**INFO — Sender domain**
From address is `adidas@us-news.comms.adidas.com`. DMARC alignment requires SPF/DKIM on `comms.adidas.com`. Not verifiable from HTML source alone; assume ESP handles authentication. Flag for infrastructure team to confirm alignment pass rate if deliverability issues surface.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The destination URL for the broken link contains a `cp_tp` parameter (ESP tracking payload) but no visible UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) in the decoded query string. If UTM attribution is expected on the landing page, it should be appended before the ESP wraps the URL — post-redirect injection is unreliable.

The 28 click-wrapped links could not be probed for UTM presence. Recommend spot-checking two or three unwrapped destination URLs in staging to confirm attribution is intact.
