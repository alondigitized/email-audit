## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 11, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old guy who came to Skechers.com to find a comfortable slip-on shoe. I got here, navigated to the right category, found a product — and then the cart page went completely blank. Couldn't see what I put in my bag. On top of that, when I searched "comfort shoes," Skechers served me women's styles first despite the fact that I was logged in. The navigation menu improved since yesterday, but a broken cart is a broken store. You can't buy what you can't check out.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**Holding flat from yesterday.** The Men's category menu got cleaner and the Hands Free Slip-ins landing page with fit filters is genuinely good for someone like me. Those are real improvements. But the cart page loading as a blank white screen is a critical regression — that's a lost sale, full stop. The search personalization failure and the youth-skewed homepage wash out any gains. Net result: same score, different problems.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** is the clearest win today. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" are all right there in a slide-out panel with text big enough to read and rows tall enough to tap without squinting. I didn't have to hunt. That's exactly how a menu should work for someone my age.

**Hands Free Slip-ins category page (Step 5)** does something right: it immediately surfaces Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, and Medium Fit as big blue pill buttons at the top. That's comfort-shopper language. My feet don't always fit standard width, and knowing I can filter by that before I even start scrolling builds trust. 313 results is too many, but at least the tools to narrow it down are visible upfront.

**BOGO 50% OFF banner** is persistent across all pages and hard to miss. As someone who cross-shops on price, seeing that deal front and center every time I load a page is a nudge that works.

**Add to Cart page (Step 7)** had clear, well-spaced size buttons and a prominent blue "ADD TO CART" button at the bottom of the screen. The "Please select a size" error in red was obvious — I knew what I did wrong and how to fix it.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is completely blank (Step 8).** This is the single biggest failure in the entire journey. I tapped Add to Cart, navigated to my bag, and got a white screen. Nothing. No items, no price, no checkout button. If I were a real shopper and not a test, I'd assume the item didn't save, get frustrated, and close the app. This is a broken conversion funnel.

**The hero image on the homepage (Steps 1–3) is aimed at someone 40 years younger than me.** Two people in their 20s posing in front of a Skechers store. No man my age wearing comfortable walking shoes. No mention of cushioning, arch support, or anything comfort-related above the fold. I see "Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs" as quick links — that's fine — but the visual language is completely off for the customer most likely to buy a $90 slip-in.

**Login didn't change anything I could see (Step 3).** Before and after logging in, the homepage looked identical. No "Welcome back, Walker." No "Based on your history..." No men's comfort shoes surfaced for me. If Skechers has my account data, they're not using it here. That's a missed opportunity every single time.

**The product I landed on (Step 6) was a pop-art collaboration shoe excluded from promotions.** The Britto UNO is a bright, colorful artist-edition sneaker at $90 with only 2 reviews. It's "Excluded from promotions" — so I can't even stack the BOGO deal on it. I navigated through Hands Free Slip-ins and ended up on what amounts to a collector's item. There's nothing wrong with the shoe existing, but it shouldn't be what a comfort-seeking 62-year-old lands on first.

**Search for "comfort shoes" showed women's products first (Step 9).** I'm logged in. Skechers knows I'm a man. The first two results on a 1,432-item search are both labeled "Women's." That's a basic personalization failure. The result count itself — 1,432 — is so large it's paralyzing. No "Men's" filter defaulted for me, no comfort technology callouts, nothing to help me narrow without manual work.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is an emergency — it's revenue walking out the door. Whatever JavaScript or data-fetch is failing needs to be diagnosed and resolved today. This is the number one priority above everything else.

2. **Default search to the logged-in user's gender.** If I'm signed in as a male, "comfort shoes" should surface men's results first. This is table stakes for any e-commerce site with account data. At minimum, add a gender filter that auto-populates from my profile.

3. **The homepage hero needs a men's comfort lane.** Not asking for full personalization — just a second hero slot or a featured banner that speaks to walking comfort, slip-on ease, or arch support for men. Right now the page reads as if Skechers' core customer is a 23-year-old.

4. **Surface personalized "For You" content after login.** Even a simple "Men's Slip-ins you might like" rail based on my past browsing would make the logged-in state feel worth something. Right now there's no payoff for creating an account.

5. **Add comfort technology callouts to the product detail page.** The Britto UNO page showed price, stars, and a warning about promotions — but nothing about Air-Cooled Memory Foam, cushioning, or what the insole actually feels like. That's the detail that closes the sale for someone like me.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right products and the right categories. The Slip-ins menu is well-organized, the fit filters are smart, and the deal banners are visible. But the experience breaks at the moment that matters most: the cart. Combine that with a homepage that doesn't speak to me, a login that doesn't remember me, and a search that shows me women's shoes — and I'm walking back to New Balance's site to see if they've got something in Extra Wide. **4/10 — same score as yesterday, cart failure cancels out the navigation improvements.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey through skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / Demandware). Critical blocking issues: cart page returning HTTP 429 (purchase funnel broken), View Cart rendering without `<title>` or `lang` attributes (broken page shell), and a KountSDK fingerprinting endpoint being rate-limited across the entire session (6 failures). Third-party script payload is extreme — 20+ vendors loaded before first meaningful paint.

---

## Accessibility

**17 violations across 3 pages (1 critical, 3 serious, 10 moderate, 3 minor)**

**Homepage — 9 violations:**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical]: ARIA role used outside its required parent container. Likely a carousel or list component with role mis-assignment.
- `link-name` [serious]: One or more `<a>` elements have no discernible text — empty icon links with no `aria-label`.
- `heading-order`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `region` [moderate]: Multiple `<main>` landmarks, nested `<aside>` inside non-top-level context, and content outside any landmark. Suggests a modal or overlay injected by Monetate/personalization layer is duplicating structural elements at runtime.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor]: Invalid `role` on an element that already has implicit semantics.

**View Cart — 4 violations (highest severity per page):**
- `document-title` [serious]: `<title>` is empty or absent. The cart page is either a JS-rendered partial that fails to set the document title, or a separate server template missing the tag entirely.
- `html-has-lang` [serious]: `<html>` element lacks `lang` attribute on the cart page. Contrast with homepage which correctly has `lang="en"`. Indicates the cart shell is a different (broken) template.
- `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one` [moderate]: No `<main>` or `<h1>` on cart — consistent with a skeleton/error state being served.

**Search — 4 violations:**
- Same landmark/region pattern as homepage, likely from the same injected overlay components.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Googlebot would index this as an untitled page. Cart pages shouldn't be indexed, but absence of `<title>` also signals a broken template that could propagate to other routes.
- **View Cart missing `html[lang]`**: Hreflang and language signals are dropped for this page variant.
- **Duplicate `<script>` injection**: `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` appears twice in the Men category `<head>`. GTM misconfiguration or vendor tag duplication — adds payload and risks double-firing conversion events.
- **`dns-prefetch` used but not `preconnect`**: `d.monetate.net` and `sb.monetate.net` only get `dns-prefetch`. These are first-party-critical personalization origins; `preconnect` would save ~100ms of TLS handshake on mobile.

LCP and CLS were not captured — these are the two most user-impactful Web Vitals and should be instrumented.

**TTFB anomalies:**
- Login and Men category both hit 477ms — 10–18× slower than other pages. These pages are likely bypassing CDN edge cache (session-authenticated or not cacheable due to cookie presence), hitting origin cold. At 477ms TTFB, LCP on a mid-range mobile device will blow past the 2.5s good threshold before the browser has painted anything.

**Third-party script budget (homepage `<head>` alone):**
20+ async scripts from: GTM (×4), TikTok Pixel (×3), Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Stape, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Monetate (×3), PulseInsights, Go2SDK, Quantum Metric, Bloomreach, Bing, Yahoo (×2), Impact, CreativeCDN, CloudFlare WebFontLoader. All are `async` but still consume parse, network, and main-thread time. On mobile, this is a sustained CPU tax during scroll.

**`--vh: 6.640000000000001px` CSS variable**: JS is computing viewport height and setting it as a CSS custom property — a standard iOS Safari 100vh workaround. If this runs on every resize event without debounce, it causes layout recalculation storms and contributes to CLS. The floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) confirms it's a raw `innerHeight / 100` calculation.

**WebFont loading**: 6 weights of Mulish (n3–n8) loaded via `webfontloader.js` from Cloudflare CDN. No `<link rel="preload">` for font files in source. This guarantees FOUT or FOIT on first visit, contributing to CLS and LCP delays.

## Mobile Optimization

- **No `preload` for critical fonts or hero images** visible in `<head>` source. Mobile connections will fetch fonts reactively, causing visible text flash.
- **Viewport height via JS**: `--vh` calculation is JS-dependent. If the script is blocked or slow (e.g., during the 477ms TTFB pages), layout dependent on `var(--vh)` will not render correctly until JS executes.
- **Snapchat, Reddit, Stape, Taboola, Nextdoor scripts** are loaded on the Men category page but not the homepage. These are retargeting/ad pixels with no user-facing value on mobile — they add network requests and CPU on the heaviest page in the funnel (product grid).
- **Monetate entry point uses protocol-relative URL**: `//se.monetate.net/js/2/.../entry.js` — on HTTP/2 mobile this works but is a legacy pattern; the browser must make an additional DNS lookup to determine scheme.

## Console & Network Errors

**KountSDK rate limiting (5× 429):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/
  2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is the KountSDK (KPSDK v1.2.522) device fingerprinting endpoint proxied through the Skechers origin. It is being rate-limited on every page load after the first. The SDK is likely retrying on failure, generating additional requests, which triggers further rate limiting. This is a runaway retry loop. KountSDK is used for fraud scoring at checkout — if it fails to fingerprint, fraud signals are absent at the point of purchase.

**Cart page 429:**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself returned HTTP 429. A real user hitting this would see an error or blank cart — a hard conversion blocker. This may be the same rate-limit regime as the fingerprint endpoint, or a separate bot-protection threshold triggered by the session's request volume.

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=48eb0a00f5
```
Stylitics (outfit/style recommendation widget) CSS is blocked by CORS. The `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header is absent or misconfigured on the Stylitics CDN for the `skechers.com` origin. The widget will render without styles — broken layout or invisible component.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**
1. **Investigate cart 429**: Determine whether KountSDK retry loop is exhausting rate limits that also gate `/cart/` responses. Add exponential backoff with jitter to the KountSDK integration, or whitelist the fingerprint endpoint from the WAF rate limiter.
2. **Fix View Cart page shell**: The cart template is missing `<html lang>` and `<title>`. This is a server-rendered template bug — locate the cart page template in the Demandware/SFCC storefront and add both tags. A missing `lang` attribute is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.

**P1 — High impact:**
3. **Resolve Stylitics CORS**: Coordinate with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or wildcard if appropriate) to their CDN response headers for `web-assets.stylitics.com`.
4. **Fix `aria-required-parent` [critical]**: Audit the homepage carousel/slider component for ARIA role hierarchy errors. This breaks screen reader navigation of the entire component.
5. **Fix empty `link-name` violations**: Add `aria-label` to all icon-only links (social, wishlist, etc.) on homepage.
6. **Instrument LCP and CLS**: Neither metric was captured. These are Core Web Vitals — Google Search ranking signals. Add RUM instrumentation (Quantum Metric is already present; enable CWV collection there or add to GTM).

**P2 — Performance:**
7. **Upgrade `dns-prefetch` to `preconnect`** for Monetate origins (`d.monetate.net`, `sb.monetate.net`, `se.monetate.net`) — saves one RTT per origin on mobile.
8. **Add `<link rel="preload">` for Mulish font files** (at minimum n4 and n5 — body and medium weights). Remove WebFontLoader dependency for the primary font; load directly via `@font-face` with `font-display: swap`.
9. **Deduplicate CreativeCDN script tag** on Men category page — two identical `<script src="https://tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js">` tags will double-fire all events tagged there.
10. **Debounce the `--vh` resize handler**: Add a 100ms debounce to the viewport height recalculation to prevent layout thrash on scroll/orientation change.
