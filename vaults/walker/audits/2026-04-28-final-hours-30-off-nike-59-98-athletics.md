# FINAL HOURS: 30% off Nike & $59.98 Athletics
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-28**
## Executive summary

- This is from Shoe Carnival, not Skechers — so right off the bat I'm a little thrown. I'm a Skechers Plus member and that's where my loyalty lives. Shoe Carnival is fine, I've bought there before, but I don't think of myself as a Shoe Carnival guy. The email is a wall of deals — Nike, Brooks, Skechers sandals, Crocs, fashion sneakers, pink sticker clearance — all stacked vertically in a kaleidoscope of colored sale tiles. The countdown timer at the top creates urgency, and there's a Skechers sandal section buried in the middle that's actually relevant to me. But I have to scroll past a lot of stuff that isn't before I find it. The $10 off coupon at the bottom is useful. Mostly this feels like a Sunday circular from a mall shoe store, not an email that knows who I am.
- There's real value in here for me — Skechers at $39.98 and a $10 off code are both things I'd use. But the noise-to-signal ratio kills it. Too many brands, too many offers, none of it feels like it was written for a 62-year-old guy who's been buying Skechers Slip-ins for three years.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer** is front and center. "Final Day Deals" with 12:59:00 showing — I see that immediately and I know I need to move if I want the deal. That works on me.
- **Skechers Sandal Sale at $39.98** — that tile actually speaks to me. I recognize the brand, I know what Skechers sandals feel like, and under $40 is a fair price. That got a second look.
- **$10 off $74.95+ coupon** at the very bottom — I'm price-conscious enough that a coupon code keeps me scrolling. "EXTRAS" is easy to remember.
- **Crocs sandals at $39.98** — not my brand, but I know guys my age who swear by Crocs. That price is probably worth mentioning to a buddy.

## What's weak

- **Not my store.** I'm a Skechers guy. When Shoe Carnival leads with Nike and Brooks and athletic sales, I feel like I wandered into the wrong section. The stuff I care about — the Skechers tile — is buried third or fourth down.
- **Too busy for a phone.** This email on a phone screen is a lot. There are six or seven colored sale tiles stacked on top of each other. Each one is fighting for my eyeballs. I'm 62 and I'm not squinting through a wall of promotions to find my stuff.
- **Pink Sticker Clearance is in-store only.** They give it the biggest, boldest banner in the whole email — 50% off in huge text — and then it's in-store only. I'm not driving to the mall. That's a letdown after building up that urgency.
- **"Trending styles for you" module** shows small product thumbnails but they're tiny and dark on a phone. I can barely make out what I'm looking at. There appear to be three shoes listed but I couldn't tell you the style name or price without zooming in.
- **No comfort messaging.** No mention of memory foam, Slip-ins, Air-Cooled technology — nothing that signals this email understands why I shop shoes. It's all price and brand names.
- **Fashion Sneakers at 50% off** — that section is not for me at all. I'm not buying fashion sneakers. That tile is real estate I'm just scrolling past.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with well-layered responsive CSS and broad legacy-client compatibility hacks. Two issues require immediate follow-up before treating this campaign as fully verified: an unresolved merge token in a tracking pixel and unknown SPF/DKIM authentication status.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel**
QA captured a literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in the second tracking pixel URL:
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
If this value was not substituted at send time, the pixel fires with no subscriber identity, making open attribution on this pixel useless for the full send. Verify against a live-delivered sample whether `[UNIQUE]` was populated.

**Possible stray character in first tracking pixel UTM string**
The QA report shows the campaign value for the first pixel as:
```
utm_campaign=SCA_20260428_nike_skechers_brooks_crocs_AM​
```
There is a Unicode zero-width space (U+200B) appended after `_AM`. If this is present in the actual URL (not a QA artifact), it will corrupt the UTM parameter, silently splitting attribution. Confirm by inspecting the raw pixel URL in a delivered copy.

**26 click-redirect links — destinations unverified**
All 26 tracked links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they route through a click-redirect domain. No broken link can be confirmed or ruled out from this audit. A manual spot-check of 3–5 primary CTAs (Nike promo, Athletics hero, footer nav) against the published offer page is recommended.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dark mode not declared**
`<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` lock the email to light mode only. No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` media query is present in any `<style>` block. On Apple Mail and supported clients, this prevents OS-level dark mode from applying, which may cause rendering issues for users with system dark mode enabled. Not a breaking defect, but a rendering gap.

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional**
`<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` is legacy but standard practice for broad email client support. No issue, noted for record.
