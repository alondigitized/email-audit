# Score big on the best gear, now up to 40% off.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's adidas with a sale email — at least it's not another World Cup nostalgia trip or a soccer jersey I have zero use for. The hero is stark: big "SUMMER KICKOFF EVENT" text over some dark swirling abstract graphic, then "UP TO 40% OFF STARTS NOW" in plain block letters below. Straightforward enough that even I, browsing on my phone after breakfast, can read it without squinting.
- But here's my problem: after the offer line, there's nothing. No shoes. No products. No button that takes me somewhere useful. Just body copy that essentially says "up to 40% off" again in a full sentence, some footer links I'll never touch, and that's it. It reads like someone remembered to set up the discount but forgot to design the rest of the email.
- I've gotten a lot of adidas emails this spring — that "Score big" subject actually made me pause because it sounded like a soccer headline at first, and after last week's FIFA World Cup Icons debacle I almost filed it without opening. I'm glad I looked, but I wouldn't click because there's nothing here worth clicking *toward*. Mark as read and maybe visit adidas.com on my own later if I remember. Probably won't.

## What's working

- The 40% off headline is large, legible, and immediately visible — I didn't have to hunt for the offer
- No broken images, no layout chaos; it rendered clean on mobile, text was readable without zooming
- The Summer Kickoff framing at least feels current and seasonal, not a recycled promo asset from February
- adidas logo is instantly recognizable at the top — I know who sent this

## What's weak

- No product images whatsoever — I have no idea if this sale covers shoes, tracksuits, soccer balls, or all three; "gear" tells me nothing
- No CTA button anywhere I can see — the most critical design element in an email sale is missing
- That abstract dark wavy-stripe hero is moody and vague; it doesn't say "comfortable walking shoes at a discount" to me at all
- Completely generic — not a single signal that adidas knows I care about cushioning, arch support, or casual men's footwear
- "Up to 40%" is repeated twice in the visible email with slightly different words; that's a lazy copy edit

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a CTA button.** This is the single most damaging omission. "Shop Men's Sale" or "Shop Walking Shoes" — anything clickable above the fold. An email with no button is a dead end.
- 2. **Show me at least one shoe.** A single product hero image — a men's walking shoe or a slip-on — makes this concrete. Right now it's abstract art attached to a coupon.
- 3. **Segment the offer toward comfort footwear.** Even "up to 40% off comfort shoes, running shoes, and more" would have made this feel like it was written for me rather than a stadium full of strangers.
- 4. **Kill the body-copy repetition.** The paragraph that says "discounts up to 40%" is just the headline wearing a trench coat. Replace it with something specific: "Including men's walking shoes, cushioned sneakers, and more."
- 5. **Put a deadline on it.** "Now" isn't urgency. "Through Sunday" is urgency.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Adidas "Score big on the best gear, now up to 40% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures confirmed: a broken click-tracker that returns a relative URL instead of a destination, and a completely absent plain-text body. Seven image assets are served over HTTP and will be blocked in security-enforcing clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link resolver**
The QA probe on `https://dv.adidas.com/o/ad4237a9-c327-4aca-8f76-c1da5036c5e2?cp_tp=v3...` returned an unknown URL type:
```
/g/ad4237a9-c327-4aca-8f76-c1da5036c5e2?txn=5db3d200-5fd3-11f1-a13e-4e8366552ae6
```
This is a relative path, not an absolute destination URL. Any subscriber clicking the associated CTA will land on a 404 or unresolvable address. The `txn` token suggests a session-ID lookup that failed to hydrate at send time.

**[WARN] 28 tracking links unverifiable** — all routed through click-redirect domains; probe skipped. No confirmed failures in this group, but coverage gap exists.

**[WARN] `referrer: no-referrer` meta tag present**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
Suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound clicks. UTM query-string parameters will still fire, but referrer-based attribution fallback (used by some analytics platforms) is disabled.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Seven HTTP image sources — will be blocked**

The same arrow asset is embedded 6× via HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
One tracking/open-pixel also over HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e9106351b-b41d31fc-7fe0-4437-a6d6-a86feb3b901c-000000/...
```
Clients that enforce HTTPS-only image loading (Outlook 2016+, some iOS mail configurations) will block these. The open-pixel failure also undercounts opens.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- Tracking/open pixel: `ad4237a9-c327-4aca-8f76-c1da5036c5e2`
- Click pixel: `V39bBfZCkLGIecx6o8WtCx1oA4rYPPND49ibxjx3Mm4=452`

Both are functional tracking images rather than content images; `alt=""` should be explicitly set.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) are declared identically across two separate `<style>` blocks. All declarations resolve to local system font fallbacks — no remote font URLs are loaded. No rendering impact, but adds ~800 bytes of redundant CSS payload.

**[INFO] Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Some older Outlook/Lotus clients won't apply the charset without the `http-equiv` attribute.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[NAME]`) visible in the available HTML. The broken link in §2 is a resolver failure, not a token substitution failure.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback absent**
QA confirms the text/plain MIME part is 0 characters. This is a deliverability and compliance risk:
- RFC 2045/2046 best practice requires `multipart/alternative` with a valid text/plain part.
- Major spam filters (SpamAssassin, Gmail, Proofpoint) penalize HTML-only messages.
- CAN-SPAM does not strictly mandate plain text, but the unsubscribe link and physical address must be accessible — if they exist only as HTML, image-blocked recipients in plain-text-only clients cannot access them.

Unsubscribe link and physical mailing address presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but footer-related CSS classes (`mobile-footer-padding-block`, `net-footer-logo-v2`) indicate a footer block exists in the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] `no-referrer` suppresses server-side referrer attribution** (see §2). If landing pages rely on `document.referrer` or server-side `Referer` header for channel attribution, those signals will be empty. UTM parameters embedded in link query strings are unaffected.

Cannot verify UTM parameter coverage across the 28 tracked links — all were skipped by the probe. At minimum, the broken link in §2 delivers no UTM data at all since it doesn't resolve.
