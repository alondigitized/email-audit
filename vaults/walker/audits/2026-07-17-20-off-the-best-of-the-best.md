# 20% OFF + the best of the best
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. I've lost count of how many DSW emails have hit my inbox this week — I'd put it at somewhere between seven and fifteen — and this one shows up with "20% OFF + the best of the best." Fine, I'll open it. Twenty percent is real money on shoes, and that's been my Pavlovian trigger with DSW for a while now. I know their game: blast me twice a day until I cave.
- But when I get inside, what I find is a long scroll of summer sandals — most of them clearly aimed at someone younger and female — some sneakers, a dress shoe row, and then a cart-abandonment block telling me there's still room in my cart. The "best of the best" framing promised me curation. What I got was the same product grid I've seen in every one of the last dozen DSW sends. There's a "Shop by Size" section near the bottom, which is genuinely useful for a guy like me who shops by fit, but you have to dig to find it.
- I wouldn't click anything here. I'd skim the sneaker row, note that nothing jumped out as a walking shoe or comfort fit, and close it. I wouldn't forward it — nothing in here that screams "hey Dad, check this out."

## What's working

- **DSW is a known sender** — I recognize the name and I'm a subscriber, so I opened without hesitation.
- **"SUMMER SAVINGS" banner** is clean and above the fold — at least I know there's a deal.
- **Sneaker section is present** — "Shop Sneakers" with recognizable athletic silhouettes is the one part of this email that's in my wheelhouse.
- **"Shop by Size" section near the bottom** is smart — I always shop by size first, so that's a link I'd actually use if anything else caught my eye.
- **Cart abandonment module** is a good tactic; if I actually had something in my cart, this would be a solid nudge.

## What's weak

- **Sandals dominate the visual real estate** — strappy women's heels and flip-flops are the first thing my eyes hit; this doesn't look like an email for me.
- **"The best of the best" is a hollow promise** — the content is the same category grid I've seen in every send this week; there's no editorial curation or "why these specific shoes" reasoning.
- **20% OFF is in the subject but I can't find it prominently stated in the email body** — the banner says "Summer Savings" which is vague; I had to rely on the subject line to know the actual discount.
- **Multiple competing CTAs** — Shop Sandals, Shop Sneakers, Shop Dress Shoes, Shop All Sneakers, cart module — no single thing wins my eye; the hierarchy is flat and fatiguing.
- **Inbox fatigue is real** — this is close to the same promo as yesterday's "20% off summer's hottest styles" and the day before's "Here's the deal: 20% OFF!" I'm starting to tune out the number.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the men's walking/comfort shoe angle** — if DSW has a curated "best of the best" promise, prove it with a hero product or category that isn't strappy sandals. One great Skechers or New Balance walking shoe front and center would have gotten my thumb moving.
- 2. **Show the 20% off explicitly in the email body** — don't make me remember what the subject line said; put the discount in the hero banner so it lands right away.
- 3. **Collapse the product rows** — four separate shop-by-category grids plus a cart module is exhausting. One strong hero section plus one secondary category is enough.
- 4. **Earn the "best of the best" label** — show a bestseller badge, a star rating, or a "most-reviewed" callout on at least one featured product. Right now it's editorial copy with no editorial substance.
- 5. **Rest the cadence** — DSW has hit me with essentially the same 20% off email two or three times in the past 48 hours. That erodes the offer's credibility even when the discount is real.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, supportive, on sale — see what made the cut`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` restricts user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Pinch-zoom is blocked on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Level AA) requires text to be resizable to 200% without loss of content. This is a confirmed accessibility defect.

**Contradictory `text-size-adjust` declarations in CSS reset:**
```css
-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased;
-ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides the 100% above */
text-size-adjust: none;        /* non-standard value */
```
`-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice — `100%` then `none` — with `none` winning via cascade. `text-size-adjust: none` is non-standard (valid values: `none`, `auto`, `<percentage>` — but `none` disables OS-level text scaling). Net effect: text scaling is suppressed on both IE/Edge legacy and standard-compliant engines.

**Incorrect CSS property capitalization in all `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Futura_PT_Regular';
  Src: url("...") format('woff');  /* should be lowercase src: */
}
```
All 10 `@font-face` declarations use `Src:` (capital S). CSS property names are case-insensitive in most implementations, but this is non-standard and will fail in strict parsers. Affects all four Futura PT and six Helvetica Now variants.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset `<meta>`:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag has no normative effect. Charset declaration is not enforced.
