# 🚨FINAL DAY: 30% OFF  Nike & Jordan 🚨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-08**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again, and this is the sixth or seventh send I've gotten from them in about two weeks. The hero is easy to read on my phone without my glasses. "FINAL DAY," "30% OFF" and the Nike and Jordan swoosh logos are huge. But the shoes lined up on the right look like a teenager's closet. There are pink sneakers and a red-and-black Jordan, and nothing aimed at a 62-year-old.
- Below the hero it falls apart for me. The product grid is two Nike sneakers, one men's and one women's, then another men's and a kids' pair. Nothing says cushioning, arch support or walking. Then comes a second sale banner, "Black Friday Preview Sale," then the $10 or $25 off coupon, then a wallet coupon with barcodes. That's three different offers stacked on top of each other, and I can't tell which one I'm supposed to use. The "FINAL DAY" urgency also feels weak when the fine print says the Nike sale expires 11/8 but the Black Friday deal runs through 11/17 and the coupon through 11/27.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I wanted Nike Air Max for walking. The Men's Air Max Excee is a chunky cushioned shoe, so it's the one thing that caught me. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it and move on.

## What's working

- The big "30% OFF" and "FINAL DAY" are readable on my phone without squinting.
- The "Shop Nike & Jordan" link under the hero is clear about where it goes.
- The product grid labels are plain and say men's, women's and kids', so I can find the men's pair quickly.
- Nike is a brand I do cross-shop, and the Air Max Excee looks like a cushioned walking shoe.
- The Shoe Perks $10 reward and the coupon codes are easy to find if I want to stack savings.

## What's weak

- The hero shoes are mostly flashy sneakers, women's and kids' styles, and a Jordan. None of them look like they're meant for me.
- The email is overloaded: a 30% Nike sale, a Black Friday Preview, a $10/$25 coupon, a wallet coupon, Word Stride, SMS, Shoe Perks and Afterpay. Too many asks.
- There's no mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on. Nothing speaks to how I shop.
- Half of the product grid is women's and kids' shoes. That's wasted space for me.
- The "FINAL DAY" claim doesn't match the Black Friday and coupon dates lower down, and the asterisk and "select styles" make me doubt the 30%.
- The barcodes and fine print are tiny and hard to read on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this down to one offer. Lead with the 30% Nike sale and drop the stacked coupons, or send the coupon in its own email.
- 2. Show men's walking and comfort styles, like Air Max or Pegasus, with a small "cushioned" or "all-day comfort" tag. Segment so I see men's shoes first.
- 3. Make the deadline match. Say "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM ET" on both the hero and the fine print, and keep the other dates out of the way.
- 4. Make the fine print and barcodes bigger, or move the coupon to a tap-to-add button.
- 5. Give each product a price, with the sale price shown next to the original.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned Nike styles for all-day wear. Sale ends at 11:59 PM tonight.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based ESP template (emltrk.com tracking, `image.email.shoecarnival.com` image host). The data shows no hard failures (0 issues, 1 warning, 86% pass). The visible findings are missing alt text, a malformed or unresolved tracking-pixel URL, and head markup that doesn't do what it intends. The supplied HTML is truncated, so the footer and unsubscribe block couldn't be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 35 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe because they are click-redirect domains. Destination health, redirect chains, and 404s are unverified.
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261108_nike_AM​`
  - The `t=` parameter holds a nested `utm_campaign=…` value. That is a malformed query string, and the nested `=` is unencoded.
  - The URL ends in a zero-width/invisible character (U+200B). It may break parsing of the campaign value in analytics.
- **Possible unresolved token:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`. The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder looks like it wasn't substituted at send time. If it wasn't, open tracking can't be attributed per recipient.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on content images (QA-confirmed):**
  - `23a03225-7c93-49fa-988c-8821675cac1b.jpg`
  - `fe4e293c-bccf-488a-bbf7-db06bf5745de.jpg`

  Both are on `image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/`. With images blocked or on a screen reader, they convey nothing. If they carry the offer or CTA, that content is lost.
- **Tracking pixels without alt:** the two `emltrk.com` pixels also lack alt. This matters less, but they should use `alt=""` with `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Ineffective meta tags:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, and `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute still works, but the IE=edge directive does nothing.
- **Doctype mismatch:** the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is paired with `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` and self-closing `/>` tags. It isn't harmful in practice but is inconsistent.
- **Link styling:** the global rule `a, a:link, … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes default link affordance. Any text link therefore depends on inline styling to be distinguishable.
- **Breakpoints:** the CSS uses three overlapping breakpoints (`max-width: 660px`, `450px`, plus the `600px`/`601px` `.responsive-text` rules). The 600/601 and 660 rules overlap, which can make font and layout behavior inconsistent between 600 and 660px.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The `d=[UNIQUE]` pixel parameter is a likely unrendered token (see section 2).
- No other unresolved tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FNAME]`) are visible in the truncated HTML.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found.** The QA flags this as expected via the AgentMail relay, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. This is a limitation of the test path, not a confirmed sender defect.
- **Footer not checkable:** the physical postal address and the unsubscribe link and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are not in the supplied data. Nothing is flagged, but it isn't verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The only UTM evidence is `utm_campaign=SCA_20261108_nike_AM`, which sits inside a malformed pixel parameter.
- Link destinations are hidden behind the 35 skipped redirect links. UTM propagation (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_content`) and landing-page alignment with the "30% off Nike & Jordan" offer couldn't be verified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two content images (`23a03225…jpg`, `fe4e293c…jpg`). Use `alt=""` plus explicit 1×1 dimensions on the tracking pixels.
2. Fix the `ieezzy5m` pixel by URL-encoding the `t=` value or splitting it into proper parameters, and removing the trailing U+200B.
3. Confirm that `[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v` pixel is substituted at send time. Check the delivered message, not just the template.
4. Correct the meta tags to `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` and `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`.
5. Resolve the 35 tracking links (follow the redirect chain, using a headless click-through) to confirm destinations return 200 and carry the expected UTMs.
6. Run a send test through a path that preserves headers (direct SMTP or a seed inbox) to verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment and the `List-Unsubscribe` headers.
7. Capture the full HTML so the footer, unsubscribe link, and postal address can be audited.
