# 25% off Everything They Need for School!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Day fifteen. Fifteen straight mornings of Shoe Carnival in my inbox, and today I open to a back-to-school family sale. I'm sixty-two with grown kids — "everything they need for school" does nothing for my feet. The hero is a pile of athletic sneakers that look like they belong on a teenager, and the section below it pitches backpacks, lunchboxes, and strollers. I don't need a lunchbox.
- The only thing that gave me pause was the New Balance logo in the athletics section — I've worn New Balance for years and the 25% off caught my eye. But that section's so small on my phone screen I couldn't tell if the shoes were men's walking styles or kids' court shoes. Didn't feel worth the tap to find out.
- Before I could even open this thing, my inbox showed me a broken tracking URL instead of preview text. That's the first thing I see on my phone next to the subject line, and it looked like spam. Mark as read, maybe. Click? No. Forward to a friend? Only if they have a kid heading back to school.

## What's working

- The **30% OFF family sale hero** reads immediately — big number, big font, no confusion about what the deal is.
- **Shoe Perks loyalty block** with the FREE +$10 reward is visible and relevant — I've got a Shoe Perks account, and any reminder of free money is worth a glance.
- **$20 off in-store coupon** with a clear barcode and the online code SAVEMORE are both easy to find — I appreciate a coupon I can actually use without hunting for it.
- **Brand identity is clean** — Shoe Carnival's logo and color scheme are consistent. I know exactly who sent this.

## What's weak

- **"Everything they need for school"** — I'm not shopping for a kid. This subject line told me before I opened that this wasn't for me.
- **Preview text is a broken tracking URL** — first impression on my phone was spam-level noise. Bad start.
- **Backpacks, lunchboxes, strollers** — a full category section that is one hundred percent irrelevant to me as a retired adult shopping for comfortable shoes.
- **Zero comfort language** — no cushioning, no arch support, no "easy on/easy off," no walking shoes. The one brand I trust (New Balance) appeared once, tiny, in an athletics section that looked like it was aimed at kids.
- **Fifteen emails in fifteen days** — the fatigue is real. I'm starting to feel like these are automated carpet bombs, not messages sent for a reason. After the Birkenstock email two days ago (which I actually liked) and the Nike deal before that, this one feels like a step backward in relevance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send away from childless adults.** If Shoe Carnival has any purchase or browse data on me, they know I've never bought kids' shoes. This email should not have landed in my inbox.
- 2. **Pull New Balance men's walking styles into the hero or at minimum a dedicated men's section.** That's a shoe I'd actually click on. The 25% off signal is there; the product isn't.
- 3. **Fix the preview text immediately** — a tracking URL in the inbox preview is a trust killer and looks like phishing. Any real copy is better than that URL.
- 4. **Add a comfort or "made for all day" callout** even in a family sale — if even one section had "men's comfort and walking" alongside the kids' content, I'd have something to click.
- 5. **Vary the back-to-school angle** — this is the fourth or fifth BTS email this month. Even one sentence acknowledging men's lifestyle footwear would break the monotony.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival: "25% off Everything They Need for School!" (SCA_20260715_backpacks_nike_athletics_AM)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Four tracking links fail due to a zero-width space (U+200B) embedded in the UTM parameter string, which will break click tracking and corrupt downstream analytics attribution. Six images lack alt text, degrading accessibility and image-off rendering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in tracking URLs (4 links)**

The QA errors report `'ascii' codec can't encode character '\u200b' in position 86–87`. The UTM value appended to the emltrk redirect contains a trailing zero-width space:

```
utm_campaign=SCA_20260715_backpacks_nike_athletics_AM​
                                                      ^ U+200B here
```

Affected link types (all routing through `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m`):
- `?rd` — desktop redirect click
- `?rl` — list-unsubscribe click
- `?p` — profile/preference link
- `?f` — forward-to-friend link

**Impact**: These links will 400/500 or silently fail depending on the tracking server's tolerance, dropping click data. The source of the U+200B is almost certainly copy-paste from a rich-text tool or a spreadsheet cell into the ESP template.

**Fix**: Strip non-ASCII characters from all UTM values before insertion into the template. Grep the template source for `\u200b` or `%E2%80%8B`.

**NOTE — `[UNIQUE]` token not resolved in open-tracking pixel**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

The literal string `[UNIQUE]` appears in the open-tracking pixel src. If this is an ESP merge token that should be subscriber-ID at send time, confirm it is being resolved by the ESP before deployment. If it goes out unresolved, open-rate data will be invalid or attributed to a single phantom subscriber.
