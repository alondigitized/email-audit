# 2 for $100 Ends Tonight!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's a mess of merchandise but not much of a "you" — I opened it because it's Skechers again (three, four times this week now?) and "Ends Tonight" still gets my thumb moving out of habit. But the second the page loaded I could tell this isn't for me — it's a Back to School bundle stacking kids' pink sneakers with a backpack, and the "head-to-toe" section with the guy in the sweater on a rooftop looks like he's my grandson's age, not mine.
- There's a real deal buried in here — 2 for $100 plus 15% off for Plus members is decent math if I were buying for myself and a grandkid — but nowhere does it say "arch support" or "cushioned" or anything that tells me these are the comfort shoes I actually care about. It reads like a generic store-wide sale email that got a Skechers logo slapped on top.
- I'd skim it, maybe glance at the "Shop Men's" button out of habit, and move on. Not forwarding this one to anybody — there's nothing here worth sharing, and after the last five emails this week I'm starting to feel like they're just cycling through every promo they've got rather than talking to me specifically.

## What's working

- "2 for $100" and "Ends Tonight" are clear, concrete, and easy to read at a glance — no squinting needed.
- The blue "SHOP NOW" button and the category buttons (Women's/Men's/Kids/All) are big enough for my thumb, no tiny tap targets here.
- The 15% off member code (SAVE15) is called out clearly with a real discount box — I like when they don't bury the member perk.
- Layout's clean, nothing broken or overlapping that I could see.

## What's weak

- This is a Back to School bundle email, plain and simple — pink kids' sneakers, a backpack, a young guy on a rooftop. Nothing here says "62-year-old man who wants cushioned slip-ons."
- Not one word about arch support, cushioning, or hands-free comfort — the stuff that actually gets me to click on a Skechers email (see the July 19th "Hands Free Slip-ins" send, which I opened in about two seconds flat).
- The "15% off (Almost) Everything Else" line with the asterisk makes me pause — sounds like there's a catch, and I've learned to read those footnotes before I get my hopes up.
- Fourth or fifth Skechers email this week — after "25% off Members," "Selling Out Fast," and yesterday's "Extra 10% off," this is starting to feel like noise, not a conversation with a customer they know.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero — lead with a men's Arch Fit or Hands Free Slip-in bundle (2 for $100 on shoes I'd actually wear), not the back-to-school kids/backpack combo. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. Add one line of real comfort copy under the hero — "cushioned, arch-supported, ready in seconds" — since that's the language that's worked on me before.
- 3. Cut the "(Almost)" hedge on the 15% off — either say what's excluded plainly or drop the qualifier, it reads as bait-and-switch.
- 4. Ease off the daily cadence — I've had a Skechers email every single day this week. Space it out or I start tuning them all out.
- 5. Fix the preview text — right now it's showing raw code/schema junk in the inbox instead of a sentence, which looks broken before I even open it.
