# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-29**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked the site on my phone and got through browsing, but I couldn't buy anything. Pages load, the menu is easy to read, and the shoe photos are good. The trouble came when I tried to act.

- **Login:** After the "log in" step the page looked exactly like the homepage. I was still logged out, with the same "Login or Join to Reveal" banner.
- **Add to Cart:** The red message "Please select a size" was showing, and the cart badge stayed at **0**.
- **Cart:** The cart page was a completely blank white screen. I had no idea whether I'd bought anything.
- **Search:** Searching "comfort shoes" gave 1,648 results, and the first ones were women's shoes. I'm a man looking for men's comfort shoes.

I don't have yesterday's screenshots in front of me, so I can't point to specific changes. The result today is no better than yesterday's 3/10.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday, with no clear improvement and no clear regression. The shopping path ends at a blank cart. A customer who is ready to spend $165 can't, and I'd leave for Hoka, Brooks or New Balance.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu:** The Men menu (step 4) has big, well-spaced rows with an arrow on each: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections and so on. It also has Back and X buttons, and I'd have no trouble using it with my thumb.
- **Comfort tabs:** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushion" sit right under the logo on the homepage. That's what I shop for, and I wish more sites did it.
- **Product page:** It shows the price ($165), 4.5 stars with 182 reviews, and monthly payment options. The size buttons are large and easy to tap.
- **Collection page:** The page for Men's Shoes (step 5) is clean. It shows the count (806 results) and Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons. The Width filter matters for my feet.
- **Add to Cart button:** It's wide, dark blue, and hard to miss.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login seems to have done nothing.** Steps 1, 2 and 3 look identical. I was never recognized, and nothing showed that I was signed in. The "mystery deal" banner kept asking me to log in.
- **Add to Cart failed without telling me clearly.** The screen showed "Please select a size" in small red text. The cart badge stayed at 0. I'd miss that message on a phone.
- **The cart page was blank white.** There was no items list, no "your cart is empty," no total, and no checkout button. This is the worst problem. I can't tell whether it's broken or still loading.
- **Search put women's shoes first.** The top results were a women's pink sneaker and a women's wedge. Nothing showed my comfort needs, like arch support or cushioning, in the first screen.
- **"Excluded from promotions"** appears on the product page in orange, right under the price. That feels like the site telling me I can't get a deal, and it sits next to the "mystery deal" banners.
- **Trick-or-treat banner and "Mystery Discount" text:** They make me log in to find out what I'd save. I don't want to play a guessing game to learn a price.
- **Text sizes:** The small print (the "Klarna" and "Afterpay" lines, the size chart link, the "Login to Unlock Mystery Discount" text) is small for my eyes. I'd be pinching to zoom.
- **Homepage hero:** The first big picture is a Halloween hoodie and sneakers. It says nothing about comfort, cushioning or support.
- **Brand comparison:** I found no comparison of comfort brands or "price vs. quality" math. I have to work that out myself.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It should always show something: items and a total, or a clear "Your cart is empty" message with a button back to shopping.
2. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to the size area and highlight it in a large, bold message. Better, preselect the most popular size or add a "Pick your size" prompt to the button.
3. **Show a change after login.** Display "Hi, Walker" or similar, and remove the "Login to Reveal" banners once I'm in.
4. **Put comfort first on the homepage and in search.** Lead with arch support, cushioning, slip-ins and wide widths, and keep men's items on top for men's searches.
5. **Show prices and deals up front.** Skip the "mystery" gimmick, or at least show the regular price and the sale price clearly.
6. **Make small text bigger,** especially on pricing, payment and promo lines.
7. **Explain "Excluded from promotions"** and put the reason next to it.
8. **Add a simple "How Skechers compares" section** with price, cushioning and support, to help the shopper who cross-shops Hoka and Brooks.

## 6. Bottom Line

The site looks good and the menu is easy for an older shopper like me. But I tried to buy a $165 pair of shoes and ended up on a blank page with 0 items in my cart. If the cart doesn't work, the rest doesn't matter. Fix the cart and the sign-in, then the comfort-first content. Score is **3/10**, no better than yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was only partly valid. Bot protection (Kasada, `x-kpsdk`) rate-limited the automated session with HTTP 429 responses, including on `/cart/`. Several findings below are therefore harness or bot-wall artifacts, not site defects. Core Web Vitals were not captured (LCP and CLS are `?` for all 8 steps).

Measurement problems:
- The Log In and Men category HTML samples both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/` and have the same `<head>` as the homepage. These steps probably didn't navigate, or they captured stale DOM.
- TTFB drops to 23–24ms on steps 4–6 and 8. That is consistent with same-document or cached navigation entries, not real server timings, so treat it as unreliable.
- The only trustworthy TTFB values are 337ms, 349ms and 123ms. They look healthy.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across 3 pages:

- **Homepage (10)**
  - Critical: `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
  - Serious: `link-name` (links with no accessible name).
  - Moderate: `page-has-heading-one` (no `<h1>`), `region`, and four landmark problems (`landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`). Together these point to a nested or duplicated `<main>` and `<aside>` structure.
  - Minor: `aria-allowed-role`.
- **Search "comfort shoes" (4)**: the same landmark and role issues as the homepage (`aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region`). The shared header and layout template is the likely cause.
- **View Cart (4)**: `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`. This page returned 429, so axe most likely scanned the bot-wall or error response. Don't treat these as real cart-page defects until re-tested with a successful 200.

## SEO

- The homepage has no `<h1>` (`page-has-heading-one`).
- `lang="en"` is present on the homepage.
- The truncated HTML samples don't show `<title>`, meta description, canonical, or structured data, so those can't be assessed.
- The `document-title` and `html-has-lang` failures on View Cart come from the 429 response. They would only matter if crawlers also get 429s.
- Googlebot or other crawlers could be challenged by the same Kasada layer. This is worth verifying with a crawler user-agent.

## Performance

- LCP and CLS were not collected, so there are no data-backed conclusions on them.
- The `<head>` loads many third-party tags:
  - GTM, gtag (3 destinations), TikTok (3 scripts), Pinterest, Bing UET, Yahoo `ytc.js`, Snap, Reddit, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola (3), RTB House, Impact, Tune, Bloomreach, Quantum Metric, Monetate (3) and Pulse Insights.
  - All are `async`, but this is a large main-thread and network cost on mobile.
- `https://s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is injected twice, which is redundant.
- Fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs (the `wf-mulish-*-active` classes). That adds a third-party dependency and a risk of font-swap layout shift on the critical path.
- The `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` errors (4 of 10 console errors shown, repeated across pages) indicate a script registering an `unload` handler. This is deprecated and disables bfcache, which slows back and forward navigation.

## Mobile Optimization

- No issues found that can be verified from the data.
- The `--vh` custom property is set (6.64px), which is a standard viewport-height workaround.
- The viewport meta tag isn't visible in the truncated HTML, so it couldn't be checked.

## Console & Network Errors

- **Network (7 errors):** all HTTP 429.
  - 6 are `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.940`, the Kasada fingerprint endpoint.
  - 1 is `GET /cart/`, so the cart page itself was blocked.
- **Console (27 total, 10 shown):**
  - `429` resource failures, matching the network errors.
  - `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` (repeated).
  - `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…`. This comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called with an unsupported language. It could be a third-party script or a browser feature, and it is not a first-party functional failure.
- Repeated 429s on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint suggest the client is retrying aggressively, which makes the rate-limiting worse.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the test harness first.** Allowlist the monitor's IP or user-agent with Kasada, or throttle the journey. Re-run to get a real cart page, Web Vitals and valid step transitions.
2. **Fix step navigation.** Confirm that Log In and Men category actually change the URL and DOM before capture. Re-collect LCP and CLS, and reset the performance baseline per step.
3. **Critical a11y fixes:**
   - Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links.
   - Fix the ARIA parent-child roles.
   - Keep a single top-level `<main>` and `<aside>`, and give duplicate landmarks unique labels.
4. **Add an `<h1>` to the homepage.**
5. **Remove the `unload` listener.** Use `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` instead, to restore bfcache eligibility.
6. **Deduplicate the Yahoo `ytc.js` tag** and audit the tag load. Defer non-essential marketing pixels until after interaction or consent.
7. **Consider self-hosting the Mulish font** with `font-display: swap` and preload, instead of loading it through `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
8. **Check crawler access.** Verify that bot protection doesn't return 429 to search-engine crawlers.
