- Well, would you look at that — the pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom is back for round seven this week, this time under "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US..." I noticed nothing, because I never clicked on that shoe in the first place. It's a women's sneaker with flowers embroidered on it, and I'm a 62-year-old guy who wants a slip-on with arch support. This isn't a cart reminder, it's the same broken record with a new sticker slapped on the front.
- The one thing that saved this from a flat-out delete is the grid underneath — a couple of men's slip-ins actually showed up in the "more great options" section, so somebody's algorithm knows I exist even if the hero shot doesn't. ## What's working

- The men's Slip-ins: Relaxed Fit Altman - Murdock Sneaker in the grid is actually in my wheelhouse — slip-on, cushioned-looking, my size range.
- "UP TO 40% OFF SUMMER STYLES" banner is a real, specific number with a clear "SHOP THE SALE" button.
- Sender is Skechers, a brand I know and check on.
- No layout glitches — images loaded, text is clean, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- The hero product is, again, the pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom — a shoe I've never once looked at, now the seventh appearance this week under yet another new headline.
- "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US" is a lie — I noticed nothing, there's no real cart here.
- The grid mixes in "Boy's Garage Sneaker" twice — I've got no kids in that age range, so half the "recommendations" don't apply to me either.
- Long wall of nav links (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing, New Arrivals, Sale) before I even get a real reason to tap anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake-cart hero entirely and lead with the men's comfort styles that are already sitting in the email — put the Slip-ins Altman up top with the 40% off banner right under it.
- 2. Stop sending the identical D'Lites cart-abandonment email under a new headline every day — I've now seen this shoe in seven straight sends across two names ("Did you forget something?", "Your cart expires soon!", "This is perfect for you!", now this one).
- 3. Segment the "more great options" grid by gender/size history — pull the kids' sneakers out for shoppers with no kids items in their history.
- 4. Make the 40% off sale banner the hero, not an afterthought halfway down — that's the one honest, actionable offer in the whole email.
