# Complete your school fits with must-have sneakers
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Fourth back-to-school email from adidas in two weeks, and this one's the same story again — teenagers in ankle-length skirts and platform sneakers, a metallic blue backpack on some stone steps, nothing here for a 62-year-old guy who wants a comfortable walking shoe. The "ICON ENERGY" low-profile sneakers look like something my granddaughter would wear, not shoes with any arch support I can see, and there's no mention of cushioning, sizing, or comfort anywhere on the page.
- The one thing that stopped my thumb for half a second was the sheer color on those metallic shoes — the wine-red and cobalt-blue pairs actually pop against the tile — but that's a design compliment, not a reason to click. This is a straight pass, not opening the link, not forwarding it, marking as read and moving on same as the last three of these.

## What's working

- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner up top is a clean, unambiguous CTA button.
- The hero shot with the metallic red and blue sneakers has good color contrast and catches the eye.
- No render bugs — images loaded fine, text is legible, layout is clean and simple to scroll.

## What's weak

- Every model shown is young — teens or twenties — nothing signals this is for a man my age.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support, or sizing — the copy just says "eye-catching," which isn't what sells me a shoe.
- Fourth back-to-school themed email in two weeks; I don't have kids in school and this whole campaign doesn't apply to me.
- No price, no discount, no offer at all — just "explore" and "find your go-to backpack." Nothing concrete to act on.
- The backpack section has nothing to do with the subject line about sneakers — feels like two unrelated emails stapled together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending Walker back-to-school creative — segment by past purchase/browse behavior so a 62-year-old gets comfort-shoe content, not teen fashion and backpacks.
- 2. Put an actual price or percentage off in the email — right now there's no offer anywhere, just "shop now."
- 3. If sneakers are the hook, show the comfort features — cushioned sole, arch support, slip-on ease — since that's what actually moves guys like me to click.
- 4. Cut the mismatched backpack block from a "sneakers" themed send; it dilutes the message and adds scroll for no payoff.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard adidas ESP template with functioning responsive/dark-mode CSS, but the send has a missing plain-text MIME part and several unencrypted image sources.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
No issues found — 18 tracking/redirect links were present and correctly routed through `click.comms.adidas.com`; they were skipped for HTTP probing as expected for redirect-domain links, not flagged as broken.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Non-HTTPS image sources: the "arrow" asset is served over plain HTTP (`http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`), repeated across at least 3 placements. Many mail clients (Outlook, some webmail) block mixed-content/non-HTTPS images by default, so this asset may fail to render.
- A second non-HTTPS image was found using a tracking-redirect path (`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...WVQdR_mIPRwctk7P-UK20NwH_...`) — same blocking risk, and unusual for a click-tracked pixel/image to be served over HTTP.
- Missing alt text on two images: `e4949485-c138-4414-a16d-5f750b9d84c5` (dv.adidas.com asset) and the `WVQdR_mIPRwctk7P...` image above. Both will render as blank with no accessible fallback text if images are blocked.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found — no unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) present in the truncated HTML source.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
No unsubscribe link, physical address, or authentication headers (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) were present in the provided HTML/QA data to evaluate — the source was truncated before the footer, so this cannot be confirmed either way from available evidence. Flagging as unverified rather than a confirmed defect.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from available evidence — the truncated HTML did not expose CTA destination URLs (only tracking-redirect domains, which were not probed), so UTM parameters and landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed.

7. Recommendations
- Serve the "arrow" image asset over HTTPS instead of `http://image.link.adidas.com/...`.
- Convert the `click.comms.adidas.com` image reference to HTTPS.
- Add descriptive `alt` attributes to the two images currently missing them (`e4949485-c138-4414-a16d-5f750b9d84c5` and the `WVQdR_mIPRwctk7P...` asset).
- Generate a proper multipart/alternative plain-text version — current text part is 0 chars, which hurts deliverability/spam scoring and breaks accessibility for text-only mail clients.
- Re-audit with the full (untruncated) HTML to confirm unsubscribe/CAN-SPAM footer and authentication headers, and to validate UTM parameters on final CTA destinations.
