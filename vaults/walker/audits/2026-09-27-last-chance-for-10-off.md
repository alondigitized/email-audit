# Last chance for $10 off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Same DSW playbook I've seen all week, folks. Tiered dollars-off deal, six pairs of shoes up top that are every one of them women's — pointy heels, slingbacks, ankle booties — and not a single sneaker or walking shoe in sight. The barcode is nice for in-store, I'll give them that, but I'm not driving to the mall for a shoe I can't even see pictured.
- The "$10 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $199" ladder is easy enough to follow, and the code COZYUP works both online and in-store, which is more than I can say for some of these emails. But once again this is the fourth or fifth near-identical DSW send this month, and the item lineup doesn't have a thing for a guy like me. I'm marking this read and moving on — no click, no forward.
- Nothing here says "we know you." It's the same clearance banner treatment DSW gives everybody, and the products underneath prove it.

## What's working

- Tiered discount math is dead simple — $10/$20/$60 off, I don't need a calculator.
- Barcode right in the email means I could use it in-store without hunting for a printout.
- Size grid goes up to 11, which at least acknowledges bigger feet exist, even if the products above it don't.
- Free shipping, Afterpay, and VIP rewards are all listed — I know what's on the table even if I'm not biting today.

## What's weak

- Every single "so new, so you" shoe is women's — heels, slingbacks, booties. Nothing for men, nothing comfort-focused, nothing I'd wear.
- "Last day" urgency banner up top but then the fine print says the code runs September 24–27 — feels like a stretch calling it "last chance" for a 4-day window.
- Zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or anything resembling comfort features — this brand still doesn't know what I care about.
- I've gotten this same clearance/percent-off format from DSW at least four times this month; the creative is just recycled with a new number bolted on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least half the hero product grid for men's casual or comfort shoes — if you're sending to a 62-year-old guy, show me something I'd actually wear.
- 2. Drop a comfort callout ("cushioned insole," "all-day support") somewhere in the body — right now there's nothing separating this from any other shoe retailer's blast.
- 3. Tone down the urgency claim — "last day" language should match the actual deadline in the fine print, not stretch it.
- 4. Vary the send cadence or creative — I'm getting shoe-store fatigue from the repetition alone.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's comfort picks: $10 off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size, your savings — up to $60 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned styles included — code expires at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy more, save more — in stores and online`
