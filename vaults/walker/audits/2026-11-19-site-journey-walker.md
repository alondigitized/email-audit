# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-19**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I went through Skechers.com on my phone, and the one thing I wanted to do, put a pair of shoes in my cart, didn't work. On the Britto Slip-ins page every size from 6.5 to 11.5 was crossed out. Only 12, 13 and 14 were open. When I tapped Add to Cart without picking a size, I got a red "Please select a size" message and the cart badge stayed at 0. The cart page then came up as a blank white screen. I was never logged in, so nothing felt personal. The slip-in and comfort information is there if you go looking, but the pages I landed on put loud sneakers and holiday fashion first.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That is the same as yesterday, so no improvement and no clear regression. I can only compare the score, because I don't have yesterday's screenshots. The menu and search are decent, but a blank cart and a sold-out size run would send me to Hoka or Brooks.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort links at the top.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are the first things in the scrolling row under the header. That is what I shop for.
- **The Men's menu is easy to follow.** It opens as a clean slide-out with large rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections, and so on. The text is big and each row is easy to tap, with a Back button.
- **Men's Shoes has useful shortcuts.** There are picture tiles for "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes," plus Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons. I like having Width, because my feet need room.
- **The product page is readable.** The $90.00 price is large and the 4.5-star rating shows 18 reviews. It also shows Klarna and Afterpay payment options. The Slip-ins badge is clear.
- **Search returned a good second result.** The "Skechers Slip-ins: GO WALK Glide-Step 2.0" for men, shown as "Also in Wide," is what I was looking for.
- **The Add to Cart button is big.** It is a wide, dark blue bar that is easy to hit.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken or empty.** After Add to Cart, the cart badge still said 0. Step 8 was a completely blank white page. I can't tell whether it failed to load or just had nothing in it, and either way I'd leave.
- **My sizes are sold out.** On the Britto shoe, sizes 6.5 through 11.5 are all crossed out. I'd have to pick 12 or bigger. The page leads with the shoe as if it were available, and the sold-out warning only appears after I scroll down to the size grid.
- **The error message is easy to miss.** "Please select a size" only shows up after the tap, in small red text.
- **The sale banner contradicts the product page.** The top banner says "Members Take 30% OFF Sitewide," but the product page says "Excluded from promotions." I'd feel misled. Search results do show "Members Get 30% OFF! Applied at Cart."
- **Nothing is personalized.** The Log In step looks exactly like the homepage. There was no greeting, no "welcome back," and no sign I was signed in. The popup step also looks identical to step 1, so I can't tell anything happened.
- **The homepage doesn't speak to me.** The first screen is a couple dressed up in front of holiday lights. The first screen has no cushioning, arch support, price comparison or hands-free message beyond the small links.
- **The Men's Shoes page opens with loud shoes.** The Britto graffiti-pattern sneakers aren't what a 62-year-old looking for comfort wants to see first.
- **Search results are mixed.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,626 results, and the first one was a Women's "Cypress - Chic Surge" loafer. I'm a man looking for men's shoes, so the top of the list should respect that.
- **The tap targets and text are small in places.** The icons in the header are small, and so are the "Shop | Details" links under the promo banner. The Size and Width filters are cut off at the right edge.
- **There's no comparison with other brands.** I cross-shop Hoka, Brooks and New Balance, and I found no price-versus-quality help.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make sure Add to Cart works and the cart page shows what's in it, with a visible empty-cart message if it's empty. A blank page should never ship.
2. **Show stock before the click.** Put "Sold out in your size" up near the title, or filter the Men's list to sizes that are in stock. Offer "Notify me" prominently, not just a tiny bell.
3. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to the size grid and show a large, clear message, not a small line of red text.
4. **Make the promotions match.** If a shoe is excluded, say so in the banner or tell me up front. Don't promise 30% off sitewide and then exclude the page I'm on.
5. **Show a signed-in state.** Add "Hi, Walker" or a points balance in the header, and surface Slip-ins and Wide sizes I've browsed before.
6. **Lead with comfort on mobile.** Put a "Comfort & Arch Support" block with large buttons near the top of the homepage, ahead of the lifestyle photo.
7. **Rank search for the shopper.** Put men's results first for a man, and relevant comfort shoes ahead of a fashion loafer.
8. **Bigger tap targets.** Make the header icons, filter buttons and promo links larger, and keep text at 16px or more.
9. **Offer simple price-versus-comfort math.** For example, "cost per year of wear," or a side-by-side comparison with other comfort brands.

## 6. Bottom Line

I came to Skechers wanting a comfortable slip-on and left with an empty cart. Everything good, like the Slip-ins links, the Men's menu and a sensible search result, was undone by a broken cart, sold-out sizes and a confusing sale message. Fix the cart and the stock messaging, and the score goes up fast. Until then, my foot goes to whoever lets me check out.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any of the 8 steps (`?`). Only TTFB is available, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed.
- **Cart step:** `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` returned **HTTP 429**. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match a bare error or rate-limit page, not a rendered cart. The cart step is likely invalid for audit purposes.
- **Accessibility:** 18 axe violations across 3 pages, including 2 critical and 3 serious.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage head injects 25+ third-party tags.
- **Navigation URLs:** the HTML samples for Log In and Men category both report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Those steps may not have navigated, or the URL was captured before navigation completed.

## Accessibility

18 violations in total: 10 on Homepage, 4 on View Cart, 4 on Search.

**Critical (Homepage)**
- `button-name`: one or more buttons have no discernible text.
- `aria-required-parent`: an ARIA role is used without its required parent role.

**Serious**
- Homepage `link-name`: links have no discernible text.
- View Cart `document-title`: no non-empty `<title>`.
- View Cart `html-has-lang`: no `lang` attribute. The homepage does have `lang="en"`, which supports the error-page theory.

**Moderate and minor, repeated across pages**
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (Homepage, Search)
- `landmark-unique` (Homepage, Search)
- `region` (Homepage, Search)
- `aria-allowed-role` (Homepage, Search; minor)
- `page-has-heading-one` (Homepage, View Cart)

**Homepage landmark structure**
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together mean nested or duplicate `<main>` elements.

**Pattern**
- The same landmark and role violations on Homepage and Search point to shared template or header code.

## SEO

- **Missing `<h1>`:** `page-has-heading-one` fails on Homepage and View Cart.
- **Missing `<title>`:** View Cart has none (likely the 429 response).
- **Unverified:** canonical, meta description, and structured data were not in the truncated source samples. They need a full-source check.

## Performance

- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage (443ms), Log In (429ms) and Men category (429ms) are acceptable.
  - Four steps are 22–26ms (Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart, Search). That is too fast for a server-rendered document, so these are probably SPA or client-side transitions or cache hits, not full document loads.
  - View Cart is 109ms.
- **LCP/CLS:** not collected (see Summary).
- **Third-party tags** in the homepage `<head>`:
  - Tracking and ad pixels: TikTok (3 scripts), GTM and gtag (4+ destinations), Bing, Yahoo (`ytc.js` loaded twice), Pinterest, Facebook, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Taboola (3), Impact, Criteo/RTB House (`creativecdn`), Tune, Stape.
  - Behavior and personalization: Quantum Metric, Monetate (2), Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`).
  - Duplicates: the Taboola, Pinterest, Yahoo and `creativecdn` scripts appear twice in the Men category source.
  - Most are `async`, but the volume is a likely main-thread and LCP risk.
- **Font loading:** webfontloader is fetched from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`. That adds a third-party dependency and a blocking chain before the Mulish weights are applied (six `wf-mulish-n*-active` classes).
- **GTM preview environment:** the GTM URL includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`. That is a non-default GTM environment on a production page. It may load unpublished container versions.

## Mobile Optimization

- No issues found in the captured evidence. `lang` is set, and `--vh` is set to 6.64px (a 664px viewport).
- **Unverified:** the viewport meta tag and touch-target sizes were not in the samples.

## Console & Network Errors

**Console (19 reported, 10 shown)**
- 7 × `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`.
  - Some script, most likely third-party, registers an `unload` handler. Chrome now blocks this by default.
  - Those handlers will not fire, which can silently drop beacons from analytics or ad tags.
- 3 × `Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`.
  - This comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API being called without supported language options.
  - It is likely a browser feature or extension, or an on-page script. The source is unattributed.
- The remaining 9 errors were not included in the data.

**Network**
- `429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/` is rate limiting or bot mitigation on the cart.
  - Real shoppers behind shared IPs or fast retries could hit the same limit.
  - This run's traffic is automated, so it may be an automation artifact.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the cart step.** Add backoff or retry on 429, and flag the step as invalid when the response is not 200. Rate-limit handling on `/cart/` is worth confirming with the site owner.
2. **Fix the instrumentation.** Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` using `buffered: true`, and read them after page settle. Confirm each step does a real navigation, since the URLs repeat and some TTFBs are 22–26ms.
3. **Fix the critical axe issues first:** unnamed buttons (`aria-label` or visible text), unnamed links, and ARIA roles missing required parents.
4. **Fix landmarks.** Use a single top-level `<main>`, keep `<aside>` at top level, give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put all content inside landmarks.
5. **Add an `<h1>`** to the homepage and cart templates.
6. **Check the GTM environment parameters** (`gtm_preview`, `gtm_auth`) and remove them from production if they aren't intended.
7. **Reduce third-party weight.** Remove the duplicate loads (Yahoo, Taboola, Pinterest, `creativecdn`), consolidate pixels through GTM, and defer non-essential tags until after interaction or LCP.
8. **Audit `unload` listeners.** Replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange` plus `navigator.sendBeacon`.
9. **Self-host the font loader** or use `font-display: swap` with preloaded Mulish files.
