# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-19**
## Executive summary

- Well, they caught me — I did leave something in my cart. The "Ready to Check Out?" hero is clear enough even for my old eyes, and I can see exactly what I abandoned: the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0. That's a solid Slip-in with comfort tech I care about, so the email is working. But the moment it gets past that one product, it turns into a catalog dump. Four tiny shoe thumbnails, a generic "Shop All Best Sellers" bar, a 20% off banner, then a long column of navigation links I'd never tap on a phone. By the time I hit "WOMEN / KIDS / CLOTHING" I felt like I was looking at the full website, not an email meant for me.
- The cart recovery trigger is exactly right and the product is a good fit for my profile. It loses points because the discount headline is vague (20% off what, exactly?), the product rec thumbnails are thumbnail-sized on a phone, and the lower half of the email is pure navigation filler that dilutes the single action they need me to take.

## What's working

- **Hero is unambiguous.** "Ready to Check Out?" with a big shoe photo and a "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA — I know in two seconds what this email wants from me. No confusion.
- **Product call-out is specific.** Seeing the Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 named and pictured confirms this isn't a blast email — they know what I looked at. That builds trust with me.
- **20% off banner is hard to miss.** Big, bold, gold. Even with my reading glasses on the nightstand I can spot that from across the room.
- **Slip-ins are present.** The hero product is actually a slip-in style, which is exactly what I shop for. Good targeting.

## What's weak

- **"20% off a single pair" — single pair of what?** Is that the cart item? Any shoe? Only sale items? I have to click to find out, and I shouldn't have to. At my age, ambiguity = skip.
- **Best Sellers thumbnails are tiny.** Four small product images in a row — on my phone those are barely finger-width. I can't read the names or tell the colors. Useless to me.
- **The navigation column below the fold.** WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / CLOTHING / NEW ARRIVALS / SALE — that's a website sitemap, not an email. I already know how to visit Skechers.com. This padding adds length and buries everything useful.
- **No mention of my Skechers Plus membership.** I'm a loyalty member. Not a word about points I'd earn, rewards I could apply, or a member-exclusive perk. Missed easy personalization.
- **"Let's Get Texting" feels off for me.** I'm not signing up for text blasts while I'm trying to decide on a shoe. Wrong moment, wrong audience.
- **AfterPay / Klarna logos at the bottom.** I'm not financing a pair of sneakers. This real estate could've reinforced the cart item or reminded me of free shipping.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lock the 20% offer to the cart product explicitly** — "20% off your Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0" removes doubt and closes the loop on the abandon.
- 2. **Replace the Best Sellers thumbnail strip with one or two alternate comfort/slip-in picks** that are clearly labeled with name and price. Give me a reason to consider them, not just thumbnails.
- 3. **Add a Skechers Plus callout** — even one line: "You'll earn X points on this order" or "Apply your $10 reward today." I respond to that.
- 4. **Cut everything from "Let's Get Texting" down to the navigation links.** The email should be: hero → product → offer → CTA → done. Clean.
- 5. **Bump product text size slightly.** The product name under the hero is readable but barely. On a phone that's rough for my eyes.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Did you forget something?" (Cart Abandonment)

### 1. Technical Summary
Cart abandonment email sent via Attentive's ESP (`msgs.skechers.com`, click-tracking through `skechers.attentivemail.com`). All links route through Attentive's redirect infrastructure; the HTML source is truncated so compliance and personalization sections are assessed against what is observable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP click-tracking domain (confirmed)**
All `<a href>` values use `http://` not `https://`:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001...
```
Modern email clients and security gateways flag unencrypted redirect hops. Attentive supports HTTPS for click domains — this should be enforced in account settings or the link domain configuration.

**No UTM parameters visible pre-redirect**
The raw `href` values are opaque Attentive redirect tokens. UTM attribution depends entirely on what Attentive appends post-redirect. This is not necessarily a bug, but it means UTM correctness cannot be audited from the HTML source alone — must be verified via a click-through test against a live send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag (confirmed)**
```html
<head><title></title>...
```
Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS) and some accessibility-focused email clients announce the document title. An empty title degrades the experience for assistive technology users and is a minor WCAG 2.1 failure (Success Criterion 2.4.2).

**Image alt text — unverifiable**
The `<img>` tags are inside the truncated portion of the source. Cannot confirm alt attributes are present. This must be manually verified — missing alt text on product images is a common cart abandonment email defect.

**Preheader padding technique**
The preheader uses a mix of `U+034F` (COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER) and `U+00AD` (SOFT HYPHEN) filler characters:
```
͏ ͏ ͏ ... ­ ­ ­
```
This is a known ESP pattern to suppress preheader overflow, but soft hyphens (`­`) can render as visible dashes in some older Outlook builds. Not critical, but worth testing in Outlook 2019.

**Mobile media query coverage**
CSS is present for `@media (max-width:620px)` with explicit column stacking and padding overrides — structurally sound for what is visible.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

```
You left something in your cart, get it before it's gone!
```

No first-name or product-name merge token in the preheader. For a cart abandonment trigger, first-name personalization in subject + preheader is standard and its absence is a missed signal — flagged here only because a broken or missing merge token (e.g., `{{first_name}}` rendering literally) would be a technical defect. Cannot confirm without full source.
