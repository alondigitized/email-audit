# Which One Will You Wear?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Yesterday it was the Mellow 2 — a chunky clog I could actually picture strapping on. Today I open "Which One Will You Wear?" and I'm scrolling through a color story for the Classic Crafted Clog. Fine, I get it, new palette. But by the time I reach the featured product at the bottom — "Pony Hair Espresso," which looks like someone upholstered a shoe in animal fur — I know this one wasn't written for me. The comfort callouts are there on the product annotation (adjustable strap, heel pillow, plush feel), and I do notice those. But the visual they chose to hero is clearly aimed at a woman, not a 62-year-old guy shopping for something to slip on when he walks the dog.
- The email is clean, no broken images, easy to scan on a phone. The "Shop Clogs" button is right there. But there's no price, no deal, nothing to turn curiosity into a tap. It's a color story dressed up as a reason to shop, and I don't need new colors — I need a reason to buy.
- I'd mark this one read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- The product annotation image is genuinely useful — "adjustable strap with heel pillow for a plush feel" is exactly the kind of detail I want before I spend money on shoes.
- "Shop Clogs" is a clean, unambiguous CTA — no guessing what I'm clicking into.
- The email renders cleanly on a phone; large enough images, no layout breakage.
- Tac Green Camo in the hero flatlay is the one colorway I'd actually consider — it's subtle and neutral enough for a guy my age.
- Consistent Crocs brand voice; no jarring tone shifts.

## What's weak

- "Pony Hair Espresso" is the featured hero product and it looks like a women's shoe — that killed my interest by the time I reached it.
- No price anywhere. No deal. Not even "Starting at $XX." Just vibes and color names. That's not enough to make me tap.
- The preview text is invisible junk characters — a completely wasted slot that could have told me what's new before I even opened it.
- "Which One Will You Wear?" doesn't tell me anything — no product, no offer, no hint whether this is for me.
- Two product emails in two days (Mellow 2 yesterday) is starting to feel like a push.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hero the right colorway for men.** Tac Green Camo is right there in the flatlay — make that the bottom product hero, not the furry one. Segment your list or at least rotate the hero by audience.
- 2. **Add a price anchor.** "Starting at $XX" or "New colorways from $XX" gives me a number to evaluate. Without it, the email is a catalog page, not a prompt to buy.
- 3. **Fix the preview text.** It's dead. Use it: "Four new colorways just dropped on the Classic Crafted Clog — find yours."
- 4. **Subject is too vague.** Rewrite it around the product or the benefit, not a rhetorical question.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Colors: Classic Crafted Clog Just Dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tac Green Camo Is Here — Shop the New Palette`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four new colorways. Adjustable strap, plush heel pillow. Find yours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Classic Crafted Clog just got a fresh look — see what's new.`
- 5. **Stagger the cadence.** If you're running product stories on back-to-back days, segment so different audiences lead the send — don't blast the full list two mornings in a row.
