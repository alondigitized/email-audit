# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- It's the pink flower sneaker again. The D'Lites "Spring Bloom" sits in the middle of my screen, and it's a women's shoe I never put in a cart. This is the same cart nag I've gotten since the 6th ("Your cart expires soon!", "Your Items Are Waiting!"). Now the subject asks "Did you forget something?" and the headline asks "Ready to check out?" I'm a 62-year-old man, and I don't recognize anything here as mine.
- The email does have one good thing. Way down in the grid there's a Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit Altman – Murdock sneaker, and that's the kind of shoe I actually wear. It's a tiny thumbnail with no price. Five of the six shoes in the grid are women's or kids' styles. The 10% member discount is real, but I'd have to scroll a long way to find it, and I don't let a points balance pick my shoe anyway.
- I wouldn't click. I'd mark it read and move on. I wouldn't forward it either, because there's nothing in it for a friend.

## What's working

- The SKECHERS logo is at the top, so I know who sent it right away.
- "Members stack an extra 10% off sitewide" is a clear number with a date (valid thru 10/11).
- The men's Altman slip-in relaxed fit is in the grid. It's hands-free and easy to wear, which is what I look for.
- The Men, Women and Kids links are big and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The fine print is in plain view at the bottom, so nothing feels hidden.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe is a women's floral sneaker I never touched. That makes "complete your order" feel false.
- The headline "READY TO CHECK OUT?" says I left something behind, but nothing here is a men's slip-in I looked at.
- The Altman is one of six tiny thumbnails with no price, no reviews and no mention of cushioning or arch support.
- The 10% offer is buried below the shoes and the best-sellers bar.
- There are two "complete your order" buttons, a big black "shop all best sellers" bar and a long list of links. That's a lot of choices for a nudge, and it isn't clear which one to tap.
- The text under the shoes and in the fine print is small for my eyes on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show the real item I looked at. If the data doesn't say, lead with a men's slip-in or walking shoe, not a women's style I never viewed. Put the actual price and a size or fit note next to it.
- 2. Move the 10% member offer up under the hero, and say how much I'd save on that shoe in dollars.
- 3. Cut the grid to three shoes that match what I browse, and add a short comfort line to each, like "Arch Fit, cushioned, no tying."
- 4. Stop sending near-identical cart nags every day. Space them out, or change the creative each time.
- 5. Make the product captions bigger.
