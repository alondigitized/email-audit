# VIP's 25% off ends *tomorrow*
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. I've lost count, but this is at least the eighth one in about three days. The giant white "25% OFF*" on purple is the same banner I got on 10/01 in "Happy VIP Days" and on 10/02 in "2 days left." My reaction was a shrug. The subject says it ends *tomorrow*, but the banner says nothing about tomorrow. The fine print says October 1-4, so the deadline is real. I'd have to scroll to the bottom to confirm it.
- The product grid doesn't help. It's wall-to-wall women's heels, clogs, a mary jane flat, and fur-trimmed boots. I count one slipper, a red plaid one, in all that. There's nothing for a 62-year-old man who wants a cushioned slip-on or something with arch support. The "Men" link is a small underlined word under the banner, so I'd have to hunt for it. The email has no comfort angle at all.
- Would I click? Probably not. I might tap "Shop Men" out of habit, since I do cross-shop the DSW sale aisle. I wouldn't forward it. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "25% OFF*" is huge and readable on my phone without zooming.
- The code VIPFUN is printed right in the banner, so I don't have to dig for it.
- The "Shop by Size" row has big, easy-to-tap numbers. "Shop Wide Width" is a button I actually care about.
- The "Free shipping for VIPs / In-store pickup" icons are clear and simple.
- The barcode at the bottom is handy if I walk into a store.

## What's weak

- Nothing in the email says "tomorrow." The urgency is only in the subject, and the real dates are in tiny print at the bottom.
- The product grid is almost all women's dress and fashion shoes. The one slipper doesn't make up for that.
- "Shop Men" is a small text link, the same size as Women and Kids. The "Become a VIP" button is the only real button up top.
- The offer says "Regular-price styles only," and the fine print lists a long set of exclusions. That includes Skechers, Clarks and others, which are the brands I'd actually buy. It feels like a bait-and-switch.
- It's another near-identical VIP email in a row. Three days of the same purple banner makes me trust the "ends soon" claim less.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender and category. Send me a men's comfort grid with walking shoes, slip-ons and wide widths, not women's heels.
- 2. Put the deadline in the hero. Something like "Ends tomorrow, Oct 4" next to the 25%.
- 3. Say up front which brands are excluded, since the list includes the comfort brands I shop.
- 4. Send one reminder, not eight. Cut the cadence.
- 5. Make "Shop Men" a real button, not a text link.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email has one failing tracked link, the SMS opt-in CTA, which returned HTTP 400 in the probe. It also has two missing-alt images, a URL-heavy plain-text part, and two malformed `<meta>` tags in the head. The QA run reports a 75% pass rate.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **[FAIL] SMS opt-in CTA returns 400.** The link text is "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off". The URL is `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/z4E9JBw6fE7rpWhoxEnLIA~~/AAQRxRA~/zSfmEjAPVuyb…` and the probe returned no status text. This is the highest-value conversion link in the message, so a real failure would lose every SMS signup from this send.
  - Verify manually. A 400 from a click-tracking redirect can come from the probe's request, such as a missing header or user-agent, and not from a dead link.
  - Check whether the link is a `sms:` deep link or a mobile-only redirect that rejects desktop clients. The probe cannot tell these cases apart.
- **Very long tracked URLs.** The redirect token for the opt-in link runs to roughly 700 characters. This is the cause of the plain-text warning in section 3.
- **Probe coverage is partial.** 12 links were probed and 63 were skipped. Only the probed links are confirmed.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **[WARN] Plain-text part is >70% URLs.** 29,382 of 33,959 characters are URLs. Spam filters weight this ratio. The plain-text alternative is effectively a list of tracking links.
- **Missing alt text on a content image.** The src is `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/JF32l4WVaD8AFkjPpHWjZw~~/AAQRxRA~/5FqSrritkRV-…`. If this is a visible image, screen readers and image-blocked clients get nothing.
- **Missing alt text on the tracking pixel.** The src is `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=…`. It should carry `alt=""` explicitly, and ideally `width="1" height="1"` as well.
- **Ineffective `<meta>` declarations.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither is functional. Use `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` instead. The charset is the one that matters, because without it some clients may mis-render special characters.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` blocks zoom on iOS and Android, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The rest of the body was not available to check.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
No issues found in the data provided. The QA notes say an unsubscribe link is always probed, and it raised no failure. No headers were supplied, so SPF, DKIM, DMARC and `List-Unsubscribe` were not assessed. The footer and physical address fall in the truncated portion of the source and could not be checked.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All inspected links go through the `ablink.dsw.com` redirect wrapper, so the final destination and any UTM parameters cannot be verified from the source. The wrapped destinations need to be resolved, or the probe's redirect-chain output reviewed.
- The Movable Ink pixel carries campaign ID `mi_ecmp=20261003_P251273_V3_WM-_Offer_MI_B`, which matches today's send date. No mismatch was found.
- The `mi_u=225090073` subscriber identifier is exposed in a plain image URL. This is standard for this vendor, but it makes the identifier visible to anyone who forwards or inspects the email.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Test the SMS opt-in link manually in a browser and on a phone. If it is truly returning 400, fix the destination before the next send. This is the only confirmed blocker.
2. Fix the `<meta>` tags by adding `http-equiv` to the charset and X-UA-Compatible declarations.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Add descriptive `alt` text to the content image, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
5. Shorten the plain-text version with a hand-authored text body that uses fewer, untracked or short-link URLs.
6. Rerun the probe with a higher link limit, or resolve the redirect chains, to confirm UTM and landing-page continuity for the 63 skipped links.
