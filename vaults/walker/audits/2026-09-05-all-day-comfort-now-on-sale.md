# All-day comfort, now on sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Now we're talking — "Hi, Walker" right at the top, my points balance sitting there, and then a straight-up 20% off banner for Plus members before I've even scrolled. And unlike that pink floral D'Lites nonsense that's been clogging my inbox all week, this one leads with Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, and Hands Free Slip-Ins — that's three sections in a row that are actually built for a guy like me who doesn't want to bend over and tie a shoe every morning.
- The guy in the red flannel on the stool looks like he could be my neighbor, not some 25-year-old influencer, and the shoe on his feet actually has a visible chunky sole. That's the kind of visual that makes me stop scrolling for a second.
- Where it loses me a little is the back half — it turns into a general apparel and markdown dump, sweatshirt model, six random shoes in a grid with no size or fit info, and by the time I hit "Shop New Markdowns" I've forgotten what the actual deal was. I'd click through to Arch Fit or the Slip-Ins section specifically. Wouldn't forward it to anybody, but I'd tap it and probably read past the fold, which is more than I can say for those cart-reminder emails Skechers has been hammering me with lately — compare this to Wednesday's "Discover the comfort of Arch Fit" send, which I gave a 7, and this one covers even more of my actual categories.

## What's working

- "Hi, Walker" greeting plus my 1000-point balance right at the top — feels like they know I'm a member, not a stranger.
- 20% off for Plus members is a clear, specific, honest-looking offer with no fine print buried at that point.
- Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, and Hands Free Slip-Ins are exactly my comfort priorities, each with its own product shot and its own "Shop" button.
- The lead model is a normal-looking guy in casual clothes on a stool — no gym-bro nonsense, no forced "lifestyle" shot.
- No pink floral D'Lites cart-abandonment junk cluttering this one up — nice change of pace.

## What's weak

- After the three comfort sections it turns into a generic sweatshirt/apparel push and then a six-shoe markdown grid with no sizes, no arch-support callouts, nothing to help me tell which of those six is actually a comfort shoe.
- That markdown grid mixes women's and men's styles together with tiny thumbnails — at my age, on a phone, that's squinting territory.
- No visible loyalty-specific pricing shown next to individual products, just the top banner — I'd want to see "20% off applied" stamped on the Arch Fit or Slip-In shoes themselves.
- Long email — by the time I get to "Find a Store" and the app download blocks I've lost the thread of the original offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email roughly in half — end it after the markdowns grid, drop the sweatshirt/apparel section and the redundant footer blocks. Comfort shoppers don't need three more scrolls of app-download nagging.
- 2. Stamp the 20% off directly on the Arch Fit / Cozy Fit / Slip-Ins product shots, not just in the top banner — make the discount visible right where my eye already lands.
- 3. Add one line of fit info under each shoe in the markdown grid (wide sizes available, arch support level) — that's the actual deciding factor for me, not just a price crossed out.
- 4. Separate men's and women's product grids instead of mixing them in one row — easier to scan on a phone without zooming.
