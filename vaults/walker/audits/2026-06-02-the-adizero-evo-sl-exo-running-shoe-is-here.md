# The ADIZERO EVO SL EXO running shoe is here
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Alright, adidas — I see what you did here. New product launch email, sleek photos, dynamic running shots. If I were a thirty-something competitive runner who tracks every mile on Strava and cares about shaving seconds off his 5K time, this might've stopped my thumb mid-scroll. But I'm not that guy. I'm sixty-two years old, I want a shoe that doesn't murder my arches by noon, and nothing in this email says anything to me about that.
- The ADIZERO EVO SL EXO. That's a mouthful of alphabet soup. And then right at the top — before I even get to the shoe — there's a World Cup ticket giveaway. I follow soccer about as much as I follow cricket. Adidas has sent me a small mountain of emails this spring: Arsenal titles, FIFA history, a Chinese New Year jacket that showed up in late May. This one fits right in with that confused parade.
- I'll mark it read and move on. I won't click, and I definitely won't forward it — none of my friends need a racing flat either.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy** — the hero shot of legs mid-stride leads the eye straight to the product name. No confusion about what this email is selling.
- **Product name is front and center** — the ADIZERO EVO SL EXO headline is big, bold, easy to read without glasses or zooming.
- **No render bugs** — images loaded, text sits where it should, nothing broken or overlapping. Technically clean send.
- **FIFA timing is current** — the World Cup sweepstakes at the top at least matches the calendar. Adidas connecting their shoe to a major June sporting event makes sense as a brand move.

## What's weak

- **Wrong shoe for me, full stop** — "EXOSKELETON upper," "precision support for a dynamic running experience" — this is a racing/performance flat. I need cushioning for a long walk at the mall, not race-day speed tech.
- **Not a single word about comfort, arch support, or ease of wear** — the copy leads with "Feel Fast," which is the opposite of my priority list.
- **The FIFA sweepstakes is noise at the top** — I had to scroll past a World Cup ticket contest before I even got to the shoe. Adidas has been hammering the soccer angle all spring; after the Arsenal email and the "soccer history since '94" send, I'm tuning this out reflexively.
- **No price, no offer, no discount** — there is nothing here that gives me a reason to click today rather than never. The Strava partnership section is aimed squarely at fitness trackers, not casual walkers.
- **Demographic mismatch** — the models are clearly young, high-performance athletes in motion. That imagery tells me who this email is for, and it's not me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this list** — if adidas has any data at all about my past purchases or browsing, they'd know I'm not a performance runner. Save the ADIZERO launch for people who clicked the "Ultraboost running" or "marathon training" category at some point. Send me something from the GRAND COURT or COMFORT FLEX family.
- 2. **Lead with one offer, not two competing messages** — the sweepstakes and the shoe launch are fighting for top-of-email real estate. Pick one.
- 3. **Drop "EXOSKELETON upper B system"** — that copy means nothing to me. Translate the tech into a benefit: does it flex? Does it breathe? Is it light enough that my feet don't ache after two hours?
- 4. **Give me a price or a deal** — even a "starting at $X" line would give me a reason to click.
- 5. **Retire the Strava block for non-runners** — showing me a Strava tracking integration when I've never once clicked a running content link is a dead giveaway that adidas isn't segmenting.

## Technical Audit — Adidas ADIZERO EVO SL EXO Launch Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken click-redirect resolving to an invalid relative URL, and a completely absent plain-text alternative. Eight content images are served over HTTP and will be blocked in security-conscious mail clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click redirect**
The QA probe on `https://dv.adidas.com/o/f5b5b87f-4194-49e7-b6f1-f864d3c10703?cp_tp=v3...&mi_mid=019e87c6-9900-7000-81e3-7e26bc541314` resolved to a relative path:

```
unknown url type: '/g/f5b5b87f-4194-49e7-b6f1-f864d3c10703?txn=2698c180-5e17-11f1-a229-8e0ec7ba23ea'
```

The `/g/` endpoint is not an absolute URL — the click-wrapper redirect chain is dropping the host, making the destination unreachable. Any subscriber clicking this link gets a dead end.

**[INFO] 37 tracking links not probed**
All links passing through `click.comms.adidas.com` and similar redirect domains were skipped. Link health for the majority of CTAs is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] HTTP image sources — blocked in strict clients**
The same arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is embedded 8 times via:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
The tracking pixel is also served over HTTP:
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e85a7c14b-d2d8d293-83dd-46be-8db8-fa5fb499fb2d-000000/yY0T3aD3B4wIcxfsYcbADC6pY...
```
Mail clients with mixed-content blocking (Gmail, Outlook 2016+, Apple Mail with MPP) will suppress both.

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 3 images**
- `ec93a4e0-ec25-4fb9-9aaf-abcc6b5afec5.png` — no `alt` attribute
- `f5b5b87f-4194-49e7-b6f1-f864d3c10703` (tracking pixel) — no `alt`
- `yY0T3aD3B4wIcxfsYcbADC6pY_L1fdbfS6PcjAPpJXQ=452` — no `alt`

The content image missing alt is an accessibility and image-blocking regression (renders blank in Outlook with images off).

**[WARN] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Most clients tolerate this, but it is technically invalid and may cause charset fallback issues in older Outlook/Lotus Notes environments.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` (400, 700) and `AdineuePRO` (400, 600) are declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `local()` fallback-only sources — no remote font URLs. The declarations load no custom typeface; they only alias system fonts. The duplication adds ~1.5 KB of dead CSS.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`) visible in the provided HTML. No issues found in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is absent**
QA reports the text/plain MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not mandate plain-text, but its complete absence degrades deliverability scoring (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY` rule adds positive spam score) and breaks accessibility for text-only clients.

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta suppresses browser referrer**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This strips the `Referer` header when a subscriber's mail client opens a link in a browser. Any landing page analytics that rely on the referrer header (non-UTM attribution) will attribute the session as direct traffic. This is intentional in some deployments but should be documented.

Unsubscribe link and physical address not visible in the truncated source — assumed present in footer, not flagged as failures without full HTML.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken redirect breaks conversion path**
The `dv.adidas.com/o/` → `/g/` redirect failure (§2) means at least one CTA leads to a dead URL. If this is the primary hero CTA, click-to-site conversion is completely broken for that link.

**[WARN] Referrer suppression affects non-UTM attribution**
If any links lack UTM parameters, the `no-referrer` meta (§5) will cause those sessions to appear as direct in GA4/Adobe Analytics rather than email-attributed. With 37 click-tracked links not probed, UTM coverage is unverified.
