# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** April 20, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 3/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm a 62-year-old guy who just wants to find a comfortable pair of Slip-ins, tap my size, and check out. Today's journey had some real bright spots — the Men's menu was clean, the product page was solid, and the BOGO deal was front and center. But the whole thing fell apart at the finish line. The cart page came up completely blank. Nothing. White screen. I have no idea if my shoe is in there or not. That's not just friction — that's a showstopper. I also never actually got logged in, so the whole experience felt like I was a stranger walking into a store where nobody knows my name, even though I'm a loyal Plus member.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**Up from 3/10 yesterday.** The product discovery path improved — the Men's menu is readable, "Hands Free Slip-ins" is findable, and the product page communicates value well. But the blank cart is a critical regression that would send me straight to the exit. A customer who can't confirm their cart exists does not complete a purchase.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** is genuinely good. Big text, full-width tap targets, "Hands Free Slip-ins" listed as a direct shortcut near the bottom. I didn't have to hunt.
- **Slip-ins category page (Step 5)** nails the headline. "Hands Free Slip-ins" in large type, a plain-English description of the technology, 267 results, and prominent fit filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) right at the top. This feels like it was built for me.
- **BOGO 50% OFF deal** is visible on nearly every screen — the persistent blue top banner works. I noticed it immediately and it kept my interest.
- **Product page (Step 6)** is clean. Price ($110), 5-star rating, 189 reviews, BOGO callout in red — all readable, no zooming required. The Hands-Free Slip-ins badge gives me confidence I'm in the right category.
- **Add to Cart button (Step 7)** is a big, bold blue bar across the full screen width. No way I'd miss it. Size grid buttons are adequately spaced for older fingers.
- **Search volume (Step 9)** — 1,006 results for "slip in" tells me the catalog is there.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Critical:**
- **Cart page is blank (Step 8).** Completely white. Nothing rendered. The cart icon still showed "0" after I tried to add a shoe. I have no confirmation the item was saved. For me, that's the equivalent of the cashier disappearing mid-transaction. I'm walking out.

**Serious:**
- **Login never happened (Step 3).** The screenshot shows the same homepage — no login form, no confirmation, nothing. I'm a Skechers Plus member and I got zero recognition. No "Welcome back, Walker." No saved size. No loyalty points shown. The whole session felt anonymous.
- **Search returns Women's shoes first (Step 9).** I typed "slip in" and the first two visible results are both labeled **Women's**. As a man, that's disorienting. Even if men's results are further down, what I *see* first is not for me.

**Moderate:**
- **Homepage defaults to Women (Step 1).** The tab row shows "Women" underlined and selected. The hero image is sandals on a woman and man, but the "Shop Now" button goes to sandals — not Men's Slip-ins. First impression is not targeted at me.
- **No personalization throughout.** Because login failed, nothing is tailored — no "your size," no "based on past purchases," no Plus member perks visible. The site doesn't know I exist.
- **Popup/banner step (Step 2)** is confusing. The "dismiss popup" step just swapped a promo banner — no actual popup appeared or was dismissed. If there was a cookie/newsletter modal, it wasn't captured. Minor, but the journey audit lost a step here.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a P0 bug. No customer completes a purchase if they can't see their items. Test across devices, especially after BOGO promotions are applied.
2. **Fix the login flow.** The login step should land me on a confirmed logged-in state. My Plus membership status, saved size preferences, and points balance should be visible somewhere — even just in the account icon area.
3. **Surface Men's content for men.** If my session or device can't be personalized, at minimum the search results should respect context. "slip in" typed from the Men's section of the site should default-filter to Men's. Show me what I came for.
4. **Persist the Men's filter into search.** Coming from Men's > Shoes > Hands Free Slip-ins, a search should carry my gender context. Women's products appearing first in that context is a merchandising miss.
5. **Homepage: rotate the hero or add a Men's shortcut.** A "Shop Men's Slip-ins" shortcut tile on the homepage above the fold would save me two navigation steps. I shouldn't have to tap into a hamburger menu to find my category.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The path from Men's category to product page is noticeably better than yesterday — I'd actually call it comfortable for a guy my age. Big text, sensible menus, good deal visibility. But the blank cart is a complete trust-killer, and the failure to log me in means I'm just another anonymous visitor. Skechers knows who I am. The site doesn't. Until the cart works and login sticks, this site is losing me at the moment it matters most.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Skechers.com is a Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC) deployment with a heavy third-party script stack. TTFB is fast across most steps (35–41ms), degrading at the cart (148ms). The critical issues are: Kasada bot-protection SDK rate-limiting its own fingerprinting endpoint (causing the cart 429), a broken Stylitics CORS integration, a suspected placeholder GTM destination ID in production, and 32 axe violations with 4 critical severity items on the product page. LCP and CLS data were not collected, leaving the largest unknowns in the performance picture.

---

| Critical | `aria-required-children` — ARIA role missing required descendants | View Product |

The product page has the highest density of violations — 7 of the top 9 critical/serious issues occur there. Unnamed buttons on Add to Cart flow is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.

---

## SEO

**Two confirmed issues, one likely issue.**

1. **Placeholder GTM destination ID in production.** The HTML contains `id=G-1234567890` as a `googletagmanager.com/gtag/destination` script parameter. This appears to be a test/stub value that shipped to production. Any conversion or GA4 events routed to this ID are silently dropped.

2. **Third-party script payload is a crawl performance risk.** The `<head>` loads 20+ third-party scripts synchronously or with `async`. While Googlebot handles async scripts, the sheer volume (Monetate ×2, tags.creativecdn.com ×2, TikTok pixel ×2 on category pages) increases time-to-interactive for the crawler and increases the chance of partial renders in Google's mobile-first index.

3. **No structured data visible** in the sampled HTML (no `application/ld+json`, no schema.org microdata) for product pages. Cannot confirm without full source.

- **View Product/Add to Cart TTFB jump (110ms):** 3× increase vs. category pages. Likely SFCC product detail page rendering or a backend API call (pricing, inventory) on the critical path.

- **WebFont loading:** `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs is loaded async but injects `wf-*` classes onto `<html>` at runtime. This is a known CLS vector if text renders before fonts are applied and reflows. The inline `--vh` property has floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001px`) — cosmetic but worth noting.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height hack present:** `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is injected via JS to work around the iOS Safari `100vh` bug. This is a standard workaround but the floating-point value (`6.640000000000001` instead of `6.64`) suggests the calculation is using raw `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding — harmless in practice but indicates no precision guard.
- **Script deduplication failures on mobile:** On the Men's Category page, `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` and the Yahoo/Yimg script (`vice-managed-src`) each appear twice in `<head>`. Duplicate script execution on low-memory mobile devices wastes CPU and memory.
- **No `<meta name="viewport">` visible** in sampled HTML (may be present below the truncation point — cannot confirm).

## Console & Network Errors

**Two distinct failure classes.**

### 1. Kasada Bot Protection — 429 Rate Limiting (6 errors)

```
429  /149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
429  /cart/
```

The UUID-namespaced `/fp` endpoint is Kasada's client-side fingerprinting SDK. It is rate-limiting its own requests — 5 of the 6 network 429s hit this endpoint. The cart page itself also returns 429, which means legitimate users hitting the cart during high-traffic periods are blocked by bot mitigation, not by server load. This is a Kasada configuration issue (token refresh rate / challenge rate too aggressive for real user sessions).

### 2. Stylitics CORS Failure (2 errors)

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css...' blocked by CORS policy:
  No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header present
Access to XMLHttpRequest at same URL blocked by CORS policy
```

The Stylitics outfit-recommendation widget CSS is being fetched both as a stylesheet `<link>` and via XHR, and Stylitics' CDN is not returning the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header for `skechers.com`. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration or the integration is using an expired/wrong API key version (`version=63ae28fe52`). The widget will silently fail to render for all users.

---

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Functional breakage**

1. **Kasada 429 on `/cart/`:** Coordinate with Kasada to adjust challenge thresholds for authenticated/cookie-holding sessions. Real users should not be rate-limited on cart page loads.
2. **Stylitics CORS:** Contact Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN response headers, or pin to a working widget version. The current integration is dead for all users.
3. **Remove or replace `G-1234567890` GTM destination:** Audit GTM container for any destination IDs that are placeholder values. Conversion data is likely being lost.

**Priority 2 — Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 Level A failures)**

4. **Product page button-name:** Every Add to Cart / size-selection button without visible text must have `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.
5. **Product page aria-required-children/parent:** Fix malformed ARIA widget trees (likely a carousel or tab component missing required child roles).
6. **Unnamed dialogs (`aria-dialog-name`):** Size guide / overlay modals need `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` pointing to their heading.

**Priority 3 — Performance**

7. **Collect LCP and CLS.** The current dataset has no layout shift or largest paint data — these are the two Core Web Vitals most correlated with conversion rate and Google ranking. Add field data collection (CrUX or RUM).
8. **Deduplicate third-party scripts:** Remove the duplicate Monetate, CreativeCDN, and Yimg script loads on category pages. Each duplicate is a wasted DNS lookup + download on mobile networks.
9. **Instrument View Product TTFB spike:** The 3× jump from category (37ms) to PDP (110ms) warrants a server-side trace — likely a synchronous inventory/price API call on the SFCC rendering path.
