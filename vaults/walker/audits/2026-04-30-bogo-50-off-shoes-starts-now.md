# BOGO 50% off shoes starts… now
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-30**
## Executive summary

- DSW dropped a BOGO 50% off deal and I'll be honest — the headline grabbed me. A deal like that on shoes sounds good on paper. But the moment I scrolled past that big text, the email turned into a women's fashion catalog. Heels, strappy sandals, espadrilles — nothing I'd wear to the grocery store, let alone put on my feet. I'm a loyal DSW shopper and they know I'm a guy, but this email treats me like a stranger who wandered into the wrong section. The deal is real, the execution is off-target.

## What's working

- **The BOGO headline is impossible to miss.** Big, bold, black text. "BOGO 50% OFF*" reads instantly even on a small phone screen. No squinting required. I got it right away.
- **"SOMETIMES YOU NEED A BACKUP…"** — that's a cute setup. Feels conversational, not corporate. I chuckled a little.
- **SHOP MEN link is present** in the category nav under the hero. At least there's a door I can walk through, even if the store layout doesn't point me there.
- **SHOPPING MADE EASY strip** is clean — free shipping for VIPs, in-store pickup, Afterpay. Those are quick reads and I appreciate knowing the logistics upfront.

## What's weak

- **Every product in the "SO NEW, SO YOU" grid is women's footwear.** Heels, strappy sandals, open-toe fashion shoes. Not a single men's shoe or comfort style in sight. That module might as well be invisible to me.
- **SHOP BY SIZE defaults to women's sizing.** The size range (5 through 11) and "SHOP WIDE CALF" button make it obvious this section wasn't built with me in mind. Men's wide widths exist too — I actually need those.
- **Mother's Day shipping banner at the top** is a reminder I'm not the target recipient of this particular campaign. Makes the whole thing feel mis-sent.
- **No comfort technology callout anywhere.** No mention of cushioning, memory foam, arch support — the things that actually make me pull out my wallet. DSW carries those brands. They just didn't show any of it.
- **The fine print block at the bottom is a wall of tiny gray text.** On a phone, that's unreadable without pinching and zooming. I don't bother with that.
- **DSW VIP pitch at the bottom** — I might already be a VIP and it doesn't acknowledge that at all. Another stranger-treatment moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men separately or split the product grid.** Show me men's new arrivals in that "SO NEW, SO YOU" module. Even two men's shoe images in a row would make me feel seen.
- 2. **Lead with a comfort angle for male recipients.** Something like "Your feet called. They want 50% off the second pair." Tie the BOGO to a comfort shoe category I actually browse.
- 3. **Fix the size grid for men.** Show me men's sizing (8–14), not women's. Or show both with a clear toggle.
- 4. **If this is a Mother's Day send, don't blast it to the guy who isn't buying for Mom.** Or at minimum, angle the men's message as a gift idea: "Get her a pair, then grab one for yourself — BOGO works both ways."
- 5. **VIP recognition.** If I'm already a member, say so. "As a DSW VIP, you already get free shipping — now stack that with BOGO." That one line would make me feel like a known customer instead of a cold lead.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug (confirmed): `Src:` capital-S in all 10 `@font-face` declarations**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
`src` is lowercase in CSS spec. While major browsers tolerate this, strict CSS parsers (older Outlook WebView, some Samsung Mail versions) will silently skip the rule and fall back to system fonts inconsistently. All 10 declarations are affected.

**Bug (confirmed): Contradictory `text-size-adjust` values**
```css
-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
...
text-size-adjust: none;
```
The unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` overrides the `100%` intent in the same rule block. Intended behavior is `100%` (preserve scaling); `none` suppresses it entirely. Pick one.

**Bug (confirmed): Missing `http-equiv` on meta tags**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                             <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, both metas are no-ops in older Outlook and IE-based clients that rely on them.

**Minor (confirmed): `.a6S` opacity set to `0.01` not `0`**
```css
.a6S { display: none !important; opacity: 0.01 !important; }
```
This class hides the Gmail "download" icon overlay. `opacity: 0.01` leaves a 1%-visible artifact. Should be `opacity: 0`.

**Font CDN dependency (confirmed):** All web fonts load from `braze-images.com`. No local fallback CDN. If Braze CDN is unavailable at render time, all 10 font faces silently fail and rendering falls through to system fonts — no error surfaced to the sender.
