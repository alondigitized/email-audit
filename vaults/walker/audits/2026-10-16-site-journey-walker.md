# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-16**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got as far as the shoe I wanted and couldn't buy it. The home page and the Men's menu are clear and easy on a phone. The shoe page is decent. But the login did nothing visible, "Add to Cart" only told me to pick a size, and the cart page came up as a blank white screen. The "comfort shoes" search also led with women's shoes. Comfort is what I came for, and the site never lets me compare it or do the price-versus-quality math.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That matches yesterday's 3/10. I can't tell from these screenshots what changed overall, so I'm calling it flat. A blank cart page loses the sale outright, and it caps the score.

## 3. What's Working

- **Header:** the Skechers logo, search, account, cart and MENU are all easy to find and tap. The cart badge is big enough to see.
- **Quick filters:** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushioning" sit right under the header on the home page. That's my shopping list in a row.
- **Men's menu:** the drawer has large, well-spaced rows with clear arrows. "Comfort Technologies" is its own entry, and I could tell where I was.
- **Men's Shoes page:** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first two tiles. There are Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons, and 784 results.
- **Shoe page top:** the 4.5-star rating with 176 reviews, the price ($165.00) and the monthly payment options are all clear. The size buttons are big, finger-sized and easy to tap. Out-of-stock sizes (12.5 and 14.0) are crossed out.
- **Search results:** search returned 1,601 results, and the first two shoes were relevant. The Contour Foam and Relaxed Fit labels tell me about comfort.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page:** it was totally blank white (step 8), with no message, no empty-cart notice and no way back. I can't tell if the cart is broken or just slow, and a customer won't care which. Either way I'd give up.
- **Add to Cart:** I tapped it without a size. The red "Please select a size" message is small, and it sits above the button. The cart badge stayed at 0.
- **Login:** steps 1–3 look identical. I saw no login screen, no name and no greeting. The size page still says "Sign in for free shipping & returns!", so I was never logged in. Nothing felt personal to me.
- **Mixed signals on price:** the banner says "20% OFF Sitewide," but the shoe page says "Excluded from promotions." I'd feel tricked. The shoe is $165 with no explanation of what that buys me.
- **Search results:** the first results were women's shoes (pink Contour Foam, a Parallel Lite wedge). I never said "women's." The sort and size row also runs off the right edge of the screen.
- **No comfort comparison:** nothing compares Skechers to Hoka or Brooks, and nothing helps me weigh price against quality. The home page's first screen is a lifestyle photo with no shoe name or price.
- **Small text:** the "Excluded from promotions" note, the red promo lines and the Klarna and Afterpay lines are small. I'd have to zoom.
- **Looks different from email:** the stacked banners ("20% OFF Sitewide" over "2 Pairs for $100") send two different deals. A loyalty email promising one deal will not match what I see here.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Show something on the cart page every time: the items, or "Your cart is empty," plus a "Keep shopping" button. Test it on a phone.
2. **Make Add to Cart say what's wrong.** If no size is picked, scroll to the sizes and highlight them in large red text. Update the cart count right after a successful add.
3. **Make login visible.** After login, show my name, my rewards and the free shipping I earned, and remove the "Sign in" nudge.
4. **Pick one deal and show it plainly.** If a shoe is excluded from the 20% offer, say so at the top of the page, near the banner.
5. **Default to my category.** In a Men's browsing session, search should lead with men's shoes, with a visible Men/Women toggle.
6. **Add comfort content.** Put an arch-support level, a cushion rating and a "slip-in, hands-free" badge on each product tile. Add a short "Skechers vs. Hoka vs. Brooks" comparison and a cost-per-wear line.
7. **Bigger text.** Make the price, promo notes and size error larger, and let the filter row wrap instead of running off the screen.

## 6. Bottom Line

I'd go to Hoka or Brooks. Skechers has the right shoes and the right words, like Slip-ins, Arch Fit and Max Cushioning. But a blank cart, a login that didn't register and results that ignored me say the site isn't ready for a guy who just wants to get his shoes. The score stays at 3/10 until the cart works.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey is affected by bot-mitigation rate limiting, so the later steps are unreliable. Five of the six network errors are HTTP 429 responses from the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904`). The sixth is a 429 on `/cart/`. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) match an error or challenge document rather than a real cart page.

Other measurement gaps:
- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any step, so only TTFB is usable.
- **URL tracking:** The Log In and Men category HTML samples both report `https://www.skechers.com/`, and the markup matches the homepage. These steps probably didn't navigate, or the URL was recorded wrongly.
- **Truncated samples:** The HTML is cut off before `<title>`, meta tags, the viewport tag and JSON-LD, so those could not be checked.
- **Console log:** Only 10 of the 24 console errors were provided.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across three steps. axe results appear only for Homepage, View Cart and Search, so it's unclear whether the other steps were scanned.

**Homepage (10)**
- **Critical `aria-required-parent`:** Elements with ARIA roles that need a parent role (for example `listitem`, `tab` or `option`) are not inside it.
- **Critical `button-name`:** Buttons have no accessible name. These are likely icon-only controls such as the menu, search or cart buttons.
- **Serious `link-name`:** Links have no discernible text.
- **Landmark structure:** `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together suggest a nested or duplicated `<main>`. `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` point to the same structural problem.
- **Moderate `heading-order`:** Heading levels are skipped.
- **Minor `aria-allowed-role`:** A role is set on an element that doesn't allow it.

**View Cart (4)**
- **Serious `document-title` and `html-has-lang`:** Both are missing, which is consistent with the 429 error response and not the real cart.
- **Moderate `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`:** Also consistent with an error document.
- **Cart coverage:** The real cart was probably never audited.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- These repeat the Homepage landmark problems, so they likely come from a shared header, footer or template.

## SEO

No issues found in the available evidence.
- **Homepage:** `<html lang="en">` is present.
- **Cart:** The cart response lacks `<title>` and `lang`. That is probably a 429 artifact, but check that crawlers aren't served rate-limit responses on indexable URLs. Verified bots are normally allowlisted in Kasada.
- **Not verifiable:** Canonical tags, meta descriptions, structured data and heading hierarchy, because of the truncated HTML.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** All steps are 444 ms or less, which is well under the 800 ms "good" threshold. Homepage is 444 ms, Log In and Men category 402 ms, and Men > Shoes 89 ms.
- **Suspiciously low TTFB:** Product detail (33 ms), Add to Cart (33 ms) and Search (31 ms) are very low. That suggests CDN or cache hits, or non-document requests such as SPA transitions or error responses. This is an inference, not a measurement.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured, so there is no Core Web Vitals verdict.
- **Third-party load:** The head contains roughly 25 third-party tags. They include GTM, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Pulse Insights, TikTok, Bing, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit, Pinterest, Facebook, Yahoo, Nextdoor, Impact, Criteo/RTB House, Bloomreach and Tune. Most are `async`, but they compete for main-thread time and bandwidth on mobile.
- **Layout shift risk:** Monetate (A/B testing) loads `custom.js` asynchronously. That is a known source of late DOM changes and layout shift, and CLS wasn't measured to confirm either way.
- **Font loading:** Fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, which adds a JS-dependent third-party step before text renders in Mulish. CSS `@font-face` with `font-display` and preload would avoid it.
- **Consent gating:** Some tags use `vice-managed-src` (Pinterest, Facebook, Yahoo, Nextdoor, Impact), which suggests consent gating. Others (TikTok, Bing, Taboola, Snapchat, Reddit) have a live `src`. This inconsistency is worth checking against the consent configuration.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height:** The page uses `--vh: 6.64px`, a JS-computed viewport unit that implies a 664 px-tall viewport. This is a legacy workaround for mobile `100vh`. Modern `dvh`/`svh` units would replace it.
- **Not verifiable:** The viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and responsive images, because the HTML is truncated.
- **Inaccessible controls:** The unnamed buttons and links flagged by axe are also an issue for mobile screen-reader users.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6)**
- **Kasada fingerprinting:** There are 5× 429 on `/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.904`. This is bot-mitigation fingerprinting being throttled for the automated session, and it has the biggest effect on the audit.
- **Cart:** One 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`, a downstream effect of the Kasada throttling. This is a hard block on the purchase path for this client.

**Console (24, 10 shown)**
- **429 resource failures:** 3× "Failed to load resource: 429", matching the network errors above.
- **Permissions-policy violation:** 4× "unload is not allowed in this document." Something registers an `unload` handler, which Chrome is deprecating and which makes the page ineligible for back/forward cache. It is probably third-party tag code, but the source wasn't captured.
- **Summarizer API:** 3× "Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…" A script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API without a supported language. It could come from a page script, a third-party tag or the test browser. It is non-fatal, but the source should be identified.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the test harness before drawing conclusions.** Cart and later steps were rate-limited by Kasada.
   - Get the audit client allowlisted or slow its request rate.
   - Mark steps that returned 429 as invalid, not as site defects.
2. **Fix navigation tracking.** Log In and Men category both recorded the homepage URL and HTML. Confirm each step navigated, and record `location.href` after navigation.
3. **Capture LCP and CLS.** Use `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`, and read the values after interaction settles.
4. **Fix critical a11y issues on the homepage.**
   - Give icon-only buttons and links accessible names, using `aria-label` or visually hidden text.
   - Fix ARIA parent/child role relationships.
5. **Fix the landmark structure.**
   - Use one top-level `<main>`.
   - Move `<aside>` elements to the top level.
   - Give repeated `<nav>` and `<aside>` elements unique `aria-label`s.
   - Wrap remaining content in landmarks.
6. **Re-audit the cart.** Run axe against a real cart response and confirm it has `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and an `<h1>`.
7. **Find the `unload` handler source.** Replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, or contact the vendor if it comes from a tag.
8. **Audit third-party tags.**
   - Consolidate or defer low-value pixels.
   - Make consent gating consistent across all pixels.
   - Check Monetate for layout shift.
   - Self-host the fonts and drop `webfontloader`.
9. **Replace `--vh` JS** with `dvh`/`svh` if browser support allows.
