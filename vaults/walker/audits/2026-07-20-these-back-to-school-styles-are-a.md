# These back-to-school styles are A+++
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- I've had a lot of DSW in my inbox this week — birthday sale, 20% off, more birthday sale — and now here's a back-to-school email. I opened it, took one look at young kids jumping around in high-tops with backpacks, and immediately felt like I'd wandered into the wrong section of the store. This is not for me. The "OWN THE CAMPUS" headline might as well be in a different language.
- The one thing that slowed my scroll was the sweepstakes — "You could win $5,000" caught my eye in the bottom third. That's a real number. But the rest of the email feels like I'm watching commercials meant for my grandkids. There's also a clearance mention in the preview, which is honestly more interesting to me than anything in the hero.
- Mark as read, maybe glance at the clearance section if I have 30 seconds. No click on the back-to-school stuff. Not forwarding this one to anyone.

## What's working

- **Sweepstakes hook** — "$5,000" is a concrete number and the "Enter for a chance to win" CTA is clear and unambiguous. That's the one thing that made me pause.
- **Clearance mention in the preview** — "see what's new in clearance" is actually speaking my language. I'd rather read that email.
- **DSW is a recognized sender** — I've shopped there, I trust the name. I opened it, which is something.
- **Shop by Size block** — the size-shop grid at the bottom is practical and easy to use on a phone. That's a layout decision I appreciate.
- **No obvious render bugs** — the email loaded clean, images showed up, nothing was broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Zero relevance to me** — back to school, "OWN THE CAMPUS," kids doing jumps in bright sneakers. This email was built for a teenager or a parent buying for one. I am neither.
- **Models are decades younger than me** — every figure I can see is young, athletic, posed like a catalog for a college freshman. No one who looks like my demographic anywhere in this email.
- **Competing messages with no clear anchor** — birthday sale, back-to-school campaign, AND a sweepstakes all in one email. I don't know what DSW wants me to do. Pick a lane.
- **No price discount I can see** — "BDAY SALE" banner is still up there from last week's run, but I can't find a clear % off. After six birthday-sale emails promising 25% off, I expected that number to show up again. It didn't.
- **DSW fatigue is real** — this is at least the seventh email from them in the last few days. Even when something's relevant, I'm starting to tune them out on reflex.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this away from me entirely** — if DSW has any data on my purchase history (walking shoes, wide widths, men's comfort brands), this email should not be landing in my inbox. A back-to-school drop belongs to a different list.
- 2. **Lead with the clearance, not the school campaign** — if you're going to send me something, the clearance section and the sweepstakes are the only two things that could make me click. Put those at the top.
- 3. **Kill the subject-line pun** — "A+++" is a grade joke aimed at a student, not a 62-year-old man. It reads as trying-too-hard to me.
- 4. **Cap the weekly send frequency** — seven emails in five days is too many. I've started automatically scanning for the delete button when I see DSW in the sender field.
- 5. **Show me the number if there's a sale** — if BDAY SALE is still running, tell me the percentage in the subject or hero. Don't make me hunt for it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` charset tag is malformed.**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Most clients tolerate this, but it is invalid XHTML per the declared DOCTYPE.

**`@font-face` declarations use capitalized `Src:` property (all 10 faces).**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
`Src:` is not a valid CSS property (must be lowercase `src:`). Most modern parsers are case-insensitive but this is technically invalid and could silently fail in strict parsers.

**`text-size-adjust: none` is non-standard.**
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; text-size-adjust: none }
```
The unprefixed `none` value is not in the spec; `100%` is the accepted value. The `-webkit-` and `-ms-` prefixes above it are correctly set to `100%`, so behavior is likely fine in practice, but the trailing `none` is semantically incorrect.

**`maximum-scale=1` disables user pinch-zoom.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
WCAG 1.4.4 (Level AA) requires that text can be resized up to 200% without loss of content. Blocking zoom fails this criterion on mobile.

**Font formats: no `woff2`, only `woff` (Futura) and `ttf` (HelveticaNow).**
`woff2` offers ~30% better compression. TTF files for HelveticaNow are being served (e.g., `original.ttf?1755182939`). All major email clients that support web fonts also support `woff2`; switching would reduce font payload meaningfully.
