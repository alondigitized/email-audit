# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that same pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom shoe I've never once looked at, now dressed up as "YOU LIKED THESE. AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG." with a "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button underneath it. I didn't like it, I didn't order it, and I don't own it. That's the third or fourth flavor of this exact fake-cart trick this week — different headline, same shoe, same nonsense.
- The one thing that saved this send from a flat-out delete: scrolling past the fake cart actually gets me a real product grid — men's Slip-ins, an Arch Fit sneaker, a 40% off summer sale banner. If they'd led with that instead of a made-up "you liked this" lie, I might've actually paid attention.
- Wouldn't click the top button, wouldn't forward it. If anything I might tap into the "Men" category link near the bottom just to see what's on sale, but that's me working around the email, not because of it.

## What's working

- The 40% off summer styles banner further down is a real, visible offer with a clear "SHOP THE SALE" link.
- There's an actual men's product shown — the Slip-ins Relaxed Fit: Altman - Murdock Sneaker — which is at least in my category.
- Shop by category (Men, Women, Kids, Sale) is easy to scan and tap on a phone.
- Text-to-subscribe, Afterpay/Klarna, curbside pickup, store finder — all legit useful info laid out cleanly at the bottom.

## What's weak

- That pink floral shoe with a fake "complete your order" button is just false — I have no cart, no order, nothing sitting there waiting.
- The hero of the whole email is a shoe that has zero to do with a 62-year-old guy shopping for comfort sneakers — wrong demographic, wrong style, wrong everything.
- Way too much scrolling before I hit anything relevant to me — the good stuff (men's Slip-ins, Arch Fit, the sale) is buried under a screen and a half of irrelevant product and a fake urgency banner.
- Subject line "Your cart expires soon" reinforces the same lie the last two emails told — I'm getting numb to it.

- 1. Kill the fake-cart gimmick entirely — I've now gotten this same pink shoe pitched to me under three different fake urgency headlines in one week (8/17 "Did you forget something?", 8/17 "Your cart expires soon" reused again, 8/16 "Your Items Are Waiting!"). It's not working and it's burning trust.
- 2. Lead with the 40% off summer sale and the men's Slip-ins/Arch Fit products instead — put those above the fold, not buried after a fake cart screen.
- 3. Segment by browsing/purchase history for real — if I've never clicked a women's floral shoe, don't keep sending it to me disguised as something I already picked out.
- 4. Bring back something closer to the 8/16 "Your Search Stops Here" format (8.00/10) — that one gave me a real category menu with a "Shop Men's" button up top, no fake cart nonsense.
