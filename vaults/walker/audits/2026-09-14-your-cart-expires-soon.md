# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that pink floral D'Lites sneaker, front and center like it's the only shoe Skechers sells. This has to be the sixth time this week that exact pair has shown up in my inbox chasing me around, and I never clicked on it the first five times either. "You liked these and you weren't wrong" — I didn't "like" it, I probably scrolled past it once.
- Now, to be fair, buried below that is a genuinely decent grid of men's and boys' shoes plus a real 20% off code for Skechers Plus members — that part I'd actually look at twice. But it's stapled onto the same tired floral-shoe hero for the umpteenth time, and that kills my interest before I even get to the good stuff.
- I'm not clicking this one. I've seen this exact bait too many times now — it reads like a broken recommendation engine, not a store that knows me. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The 20% off member code (EXTRA20) is clearly laid out with a deadline (valid thru 9/14) — that's honest and useful.
- The product grid below the hero has real men's options — the Arch Fit and Relaxed Fit Altman styles are exactly my kind of shoe.
- Afterpay/Klarna and "Find a Store" footer options are nice touches for how I actually shop.
- Clean layout, big readable text, no rendering bugs I can see.

## What's weak

- That pink floral women's D'Lites sneaker again — same shoe, same "you liked this" pitch, for what feels like the sixth send this week.
- "Complete Your Order" CTA sits directly under a shoe I never put in a cart, which just feels wrong — not personalized, just recycled.
- Nothing in the hero speaks to comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease — the stuff that actually gets me to buy.
- No sign this brand knows I'm a 62-year-old guy who's never once clicked that floral shoe.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop leading with the pink floral D'Lites — swap the hero for one of the men's Arch Fit or Relaxed Fit styles already sitting in the grid below; that's the shoe that'll get me to click.
- 2. Retire "You Liked These" messaging if the recommendation engine is clearly wrong — a generic "Members Save 20%" hero would outperform a mismatched personalization claim.
- 3. Move the 20% off member code up top, not two-thirds down the email — that's the real offer here.
- 4. Lead with comfort language — cushioned, arch support, slip-on — since that's what actually moves me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: 20% off sale styles, ends today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Arch Fit favorites are 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra savings on the comfort styles you've been eyeing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code EXTRA20 — valid through tonight only`
