# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-23**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers has the right idea for a guy like me. The nav chips say "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit" and "Arch Fit," and the Men's Shoes page opens with "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles. But I couldn't finish a purchase. On the product page I got "Please select a size" with the cart still at 0, and the cart page was a blank white screen. The "comfort shoes" search then led with women's shoes. I never felt logged in or recognized. The comfort story is good. Getting a shoe into the cart is the problem.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10**, the same as yesterday's 4/10, so I see no regression and no improvement.

- I only have yesterday's score, not its screenshots, so I can't say what changed underneath.
- The good navigation is offset by a cart step that gave me nothing.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort-first top nav.** "Slip-ins | Wide Fit | Arch Fit | Max Cushioning" sits right under the logo. That's what I shop for.
- **Menu drawer.** On the phone, the Men menu has large, bold, well-spaced rows. "Comfort Technologies" is one of them. I could read it without zooming.
- **Men's Shoes landing.** The "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles are exactly my categories. Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are right there, and Width matters to me.
- **Product page basics.** The price is big and clear. The Klarna/Afterpay line is easy to read. Size buttons are laid out in an easy grid, with the sold-out 6.5 crossed out.
- **Add to Cart button.** It's wide, dark blue and easy to hit with a thumb.
- **Skechers Plus box.** "Free Shipping, Free Returns, $5 Reward" is clear. It's a mild nudge, which suits me.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken or empty.** After "Add to Cart," the badge still read 0 and the only message was "Please select a size." The next screen, the cart, was a completely blank white page. No "your cart is empty" message and no help. A 62-year-old would assume the site broke and leave.
- **The size error is easy to miss.** It's small red text above the button, and no size is pre-selected. I'd tap Add to Cart, see nothing happen, and not know why.
- **Search missed me.** "comfort shoes" gave 1,642 results, and the first two were Women's, a pink sneaker and a black wedge. I'm a men's shopper and nothing asked me to filter.
- **Promo messaging contradicts itself.** The banner says "20% OFF Sitewide," but the product page says "Excluded from promotions" on a $100 shoe. That kind of mismatch costs trust.
- **No sense of being logged in.** The "Log In" step looked identical to the homepage. "Members Earn a $5 Reward… Login | Details" was still showing. No name, no saved items, nothing personal. The popup step only swapped the strip text to a "Work & Safety" promo that doesn't apply to me.
- **The hero is only a lifestyle photo.** It has no headline, price or button above the fold, and the people in it are young. Nothing says comfort, cushioning or arch support.
- **No evidence behind the comfort claims.** The product I landed on, a skull-print UNO, had no reviews, no arch-support or cushioning details in view, and no comparison with Hoka, Brooks or New Balance. I saw nothing like "what you get for $100."
- **Small text and targets.** "Applied at Cart" and "20% OFF for Members!" are tiny red text. Header icons and the Filter/Sort/Size/Width chips are on the small side. The Size Chart link is small.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the blank cart.** Always show cart contents or a clear "Your cart is empty — keep shopping" message, and never serve a blank page.
2. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to the size grid and highlight it when Add to Cart is tapped without a size. Or pre-select a size, or show a "Select your size" label on the button itself.
3. **Respect the category in search.** Keep the Men's context from the browsing path, or show Men/Women toggle chips at the top of results. Surface "Slip-ins," "Arch Fit" and "Wide" as quick filters for comfort searches.
4. **Resolve the promo contradiction.** Say "20% off applied at cart, excludes some styles," and flag excluded shoes in the listing, not after I've clicked in.
5. **Show comfort proof on the product page.** Put cushioning, arch support and a slip-in-ready label near the top. Add a simple "How it compares" line against other comfort brands, plus honest reviews.
6. **Make logged-in mean something.** Show "Welcome back, Walker," my saved size and width, and recently viewed shoes. Without that, the $5 reward is the only reason to log in.
7. **Enlarge small text.** Make the promo text, the Size Chart link and the filter chips larger.

## 6. Bottom Line

The comfort categories are there, but I never got a pair into a cart, and a blank page at checkout is a dealbreaker. I'd happily buy a good slip-in, but if the cart doesn't work I'll go to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, or the sale aisle at DSW, where the shoe that fits wins.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was partly blocked by bot mitigation. Kasada (`x-kpsdk`) returned HTTP 429 on 6 of 7 network errors, including the `/cart/` document, so the View Cart step audited a block response, not the real cart. Several findings are therefore test artifacts. The remaining real issues are on the homepage (2 critical and 1 serious axe violations, duplicate or nested `main` landmarks) and in third-party script weight.

Evidence limits:
- LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed.
- The Log In and Men category HTML samples report URL `https://www.skechers.com/` and have the same `<head>` as the homepage. Those steps may not have navigated, or the snapshot was taken before navigation.
- Steps 4–6 and 8 show TTFB of 23–26ms against 401–413ms for the first three steps. That points to same-document or cached loads, not fresh document requests.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations across 3 steps, all from the 9 distinct rules listed below.

**Homepage (9):**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent` (ARIA roles that need a parent role are not inside one).
- **Critical:** `button-name` (buttons with no accessible name).
- **Serious:** `link-name` (links with no accessible name).
- **Moderate:** `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main`. Together these suggest a nested or second `<main>`.
- **Moderate:** `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4):**
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor).
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` (moderate).
- These repeat the homepage landmark pattern, so they probably come from shared template or layout code.

**View Cart (4):**
- `document-title` (serious), `html-has-lang` (serious), `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` (moderate).
- These almost certainly describe the 429 block response, not the cart template. The homepage has `<html lang="en">`. Re-test with an unblocked session before filing them.

Axe results are reported only for these three steps. Steps with no entry were either clean or not scanned, and the data doesn't say which.

## SEO

No confirmed issues. The homepage has `<html lang="en">`. The truncated HTML doesn't include meta description, canonical, robots or structured data, so these can't be assessed. The missing `<title>` and `<h1>` on View Cart come from the 429 response (see Accessibility).

## Performance

- **TTFB:** 401–413ms on the document loads. That is within the good threshold (≤800ms). The 23–26ms values likely come from cached or same-document loads, as noted above.
- **LCP and CLS:** not measured.
- **Third-party load:** the homepage head injects 25 or more third-party tags. These include GTM, Monetate, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, and TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Facebook and Bing pixels. They also include Taboola, Yahoo, Impact, RTB House, Bloomreach and Tune. This is a likely main-thread and LCP risk, but it isn't confirmed without LCP data.
- **Web fonts:** fonts load through `webfontloader.js` from cdnjs, fetched asynchronously. The `wf-*-active` classes confirm JavaScript-driven font loading. This adds a third-party dependency and a font-swap risk (FOIT/FOUT, layout shift).
- **GTM environment:** the GTM URL carries `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…&gtm_cookies_win=x`. That is a non-default GTM environment snippet. Confirm it is intended for production.

## Mobile Optimization

No issues found in the data provided. The `--vh` custom property (6.64px, i.e. a 664px viewport) is set on `<html>`, which is a normal mobile viewport-height workaround. The viewport meta tag is not visible in the truncated source, so it can't be verified.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 responses (7):** 6 are Kasada fingerprint POSTs (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.936`) and 1 is the `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` document. This is rate limiting or bot mitigation triggered by the automated session. It is the only error that blocked a journey step.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed` (4 of the 10 logged):** some script on the page registers an `unload` handler, which the current document's permissions policy blocks. Chrome is deprecating `unload`, and handlers like this also disable bfcache. The source script isn't identified in the data.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages` (2 of the 10 logged):** the origin is unclear. It could be a page script, a third-party tag or browser tooling, and it is unattributed.
- Only 10 of the 27 reported console errors are listed above, so counts for other messages are unknown.

## Recommendations

1. Re-run the audit with the test traffic allowlisted or fingerprint-exempt in Kasada. Then recapture View Cart and re-validate the 4 cart violations.
2. Fix the homepage critical and serious violations. Give every button and link an accessible name, and supply the required parent roles for ARIA children.
3. Fix the landmark structure in the shared layout. Use one top-level `<main>`, make `<aside>` elements top-level, give repeated landmarks unique labels, and put page content inside landmarks.
4. Review the `role` attributes that trigger `aria-allowed-role`.
5. Find which script registers the `unload` handler and replace it with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
6. Capture LCP and CLS, for example with the `web-vitals` library and PerformanceObserver, and confirm each step really navigates.
7. Audit third-party tags and defer or consolidate the non-essential ones through the tag manager, based on measured main-thread cost.
8. Self-host the fonts, or use `font-display: swap` with preloads, instead of loading them through `webfontloader` from cdnjs.
9. Confirm the GTM `env-1` preview parameters are intended in production.
