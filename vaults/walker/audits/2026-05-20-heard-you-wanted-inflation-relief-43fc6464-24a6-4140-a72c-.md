# ​Heard You Wanted Inflation Relief??
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I've gotten so many of these from Shoe Carnival this week that I practically know the layout by heart before I even open it. And sure enough — same red-and-yellow color scheme, same stacked offers, same "Inflation Relief" language I already saw five days ago. That first one landed May 15th with "Inflation Relief Is Here!" and now they're asking me if I *heard* I wanted it. I heard the first time, fellas.
- That said, once I'm inside this one, I'll admit the Brooks Ghost 17 section stopped my thumb. Thirty dollars off a specific model I've been watching? That's the kind of concrete I can act on. The rest of the email is a lot of noise — a BOGO that's women's and kids' only, a Nike sale that doesn't say anything about fit or comfort, a sandal blowout leading with women's pricing. I'm not a women's shopper. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for cushioning and arch support. This email treats me like a ZIP code, not a customer.
- I'd click to the Brooks page. I would not forward this to anyone. I'd mark the sender with a mental note that they're sending too often and the targeting is sloppy.

## What's working

- **Brooks Ghost 17 with a specific discount** — "$30 OFF" on a named model is exactly the signal I need; that's a shoe I've been tracking and $30 off is real money.
- **Hero offer is clear and up front** — the $20 off $99.98+ with a coupon code (MAY20) is right at the top, no scrolling required.
- **Memorial Day framing lands** — seasonal context makes the deals feel legitimate rather than random, and it aligns with my mental calendar for shopping.
- **Shoe Perks reward visible** — the $10 reward with a barcode I can actually show at the register feels like something in it for me as a loyalty member.
- **No broken images or layout chaos** — at least the email renders cleanly; I can read it without squinting or pinching to zoom.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — in my inbox, next to "Heard You Wanted Inflation Relief??" I see "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com..." — that's embarrassing and it almost made me think it was spam.
- **BOGO is women's and kids' only** — the biggest, most colorful block in the email isn't for me; they wasted prime real estate on the wrong customer.
- **"Inflation Relief" is getting stale** — this is at least the second time this exact phrase has shown up in a subject line from them in five days; it's starting to feel like a department store sale that never actually ends.
- **Too many competing CTAs** — I count five or six different "Shop Now" or "Shop [X]" buttons and I lose the thread quickly; there's no clear "this is the one thing we want you to do today."
- **No comfort language anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, wide width, slip-on — nothing. The whole email could be for a 25-year-old just as easily as for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Brooks Ghost 17** — that specific shoe at a specific discount is the most actionable thing in this email for a customer like me; put it above the fold and make it the hero.
- 2. **Fix the preheader immediately** — a raw URL in the inbox is a trust killer; even "Memorial Day savings — Brooks, Nike, and more" would be a hundred times better.
- 3. **Segment the BOGO** — if my account shows I'm a 62-year-old man, don't put Women's & Kids BOGO as the second-biggest block; show me men's styles or drop that section for my segment entirely.
- 4. **Add a product detail line** — even one word: "cushioned," "wide widths available," "slip-resistant sole" — something that tells me this shoe is built for someone with my priorities.
- 5. **Retire "Inflation Relief" for a send or two** — it worked in May, but I've seen it twice already and it's starting to feel like a furniture store that's always going out of business.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed `<meta>` tag — IE compatibility:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing required `http-equiv` attribute. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag is a no-op and does not trigger IE edge rendering mode.

**Malformed charset `<meta>` tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />
```
The `content` value is incomplete (truncated after `text/html;`, missing `charset=utf-8` in content) and `http-equiv="Content-Type"` is absent. Browsers and email clients tolerate this, but it is technically malformed. Should be either `<meta charset="utf-8">` or `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`.

**Charset declaration placement:**
The `<meta charset>` tag appears *after* the first `<style>` block. Per spec, charset should be the first element in `<head>` to ensure correct encoding interpretation before any other content is parsed.

**Dark mode: not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Dark mode is explicitly excluded. Apple Mail and other clients that inject dark mode CSS may override colors unexpectedly. No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` media query present.

**Duplicate CSS property on anchor selector:**
```css
a, a:link, ... {
  color:inherit !important;
  color:inherit;
}
```
`color` is declared twice in the same ruleset. The second declaration (without `!important`) is dead code and should be removed.

**Five separate `<style>` blocks in `<head>`:** Not a bug, but increases parse overhead. Gmail strips `<style>` tags in some contexts; consolidating reduces surface area for stripping.
