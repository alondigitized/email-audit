# Raffia Edit from Naturalizer
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- I know Famous Footwear. I've been opening their emails for years. But the second I saw "Raffia Edit from Naturalizer" in my inbox, I already knew this one wasn't for me — and I was right. I scroll through the whole thing and every single product is a strappy women's sandal with a heel. Every single one. No men's section tucked at the bottom, no "also for him," nothing. Just tan leather slingbacks and woven wedges. I am a 62-year-old man shopping for walking shoes with arch support. This email sent me to the wrong zip code.
- And that preheader — I've been calling this out since the June 8 send, then June 13, June 14, June 15, June 16, June 17 — and here we are again on June 25 with the exact same raw tracking URL dumped right in my inbox preview. "https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7..." followed by the random word "Want." Famous Footwear has now done this at least six times in a row. At this point it's not a bug, it's a habit.
- I would not click this. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and wonder if someone at Famous Footwear has ever looked at my purchase history once in their life.

## What's working

- **Famous Footwear logo is immediately recognizable** — I know the sender in half a second, which is the only reason I don't trash it.
- **"Raffia Edit" photography is clean and professional** — the tan shoes are shot well against a matching neutral background; the email looks polished even if it's not for me.
- **Visual hierarchy is logical** — hero image up top, detail shots in the middle, size grid at the bottom; a reader who this email *is* for would move through it easily.
- **No render bugs** — the layout holds, images load, text is legible; technically sound send.
- **Size grid is a smart touch** — clicking right to a women's size is a good convenience feature for the person this was actually meant for.

## What's weak

- **Wrong demographic entirely** — I am a male customer; Naturalizer is a women's brand; every product shown is a women's heel or sandal. This should never have landed in my inbox.
- **The preheader bug is back. Again.** — Raw tracking URL as my inbox preview, same as the last six emails. I've said it every time: this is the first thing I see before I even open, and it looks like spam.
- **No offer anywhere in the email** — not a dollar off, not a points event, not free shipping. Just "shop this." I need a reason to click, not a catalog.
- **"Raffia" is women's fashion language, not my vocabulary** — I don't know what a raffia edit is and I don't need to. That subject line told me this wasn't mine before I even tapped.
- **Zero comfort or fit signals** — cushioning, arch support, slip-on, wide width — none of it. Even if I'd somehow stumbled into a pair of men's sandals in this email, there's nothing to tell me they'd be comfortable to walk in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader. Today. Not next campaign.** This is the same broken tracking URL I flagged June 8, 13, 14, 15, 16, and 17. It makes every email look like a phishing attempt before it's even opened. Whatever the technical root cause is, it needs to be escalated and fixed — not A/B tested around.
- 2. **Suppress men's customers from women's-only brand campaigns** — I should not be receiving a Naturalizer email. Segment by past purchase gender or at minimum by browsing behavior. A men's customer getting an all-women's catalog is a brand trust erosion, not a neutral send.
- 3. **Lead with a discount or event hook** — Even if someone like my wife were the right audience here, "Shop Naturalizer Sandals" is not a reason to click. "Extra $10 off your first Naturalizer pair" is.
- 4. **Add at least one comfort/fit callout in the copy** — Even for women customers, Naturalizer's brand equity is comfort. Mention cushioning, arch support, or all-day wearability somewhere in the body copy.
- 5. **Add urgency** — No deadline, no limited stock, no sale end date. Give me a reason to act now instead of closing the tab.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save on Naturalizer sandals — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Naturalizer comfort sandals: extra 15% off for members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Raffia, woven leather, and all-day cushioning — shop women's sizes 6–11`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer's most comfortable sandal is here — Free shipping on orders $49+`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound but carries two concrete defects: a malformed charset meta tag that leaves encoding undeclared at the HTML level, and a CSS property typo that silently discards a style rule. Accessibility coverage is poor — 24 content images have no alt text.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** — `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?IGKCPEMYXRRU5B4RLSGW2XO2ZQ.70257&d=70257&bmt=0` — present and structurally correct.

**Click tracking** — All 26 links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. QA skipped HTTP probes on all 26 (expected; click-redirect domains return 302 on bot UA). Destination URL health and UTM coverage cannot be confirmed without live probing under a real browser UA.

**Plain-text body** — 6,774 of 7,702 chars (88%) are raw URLs. This exceeds the spam-filter heuristic threshold (~70%) and will raise the message's spam score with Proofpoint, SpamAssassin, and similar filters. Root cause: the plain-text part appears to be auto-generated by dumping raw HTML href values rather than rendering link anchor text.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Charset meta tag is malformed (confirmed bug):**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is missing. Without it, the `content` attribute is ignored and no charset is declared at the HTML level. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Rendering falls back to the MIME header charset, which is likely fine in practice, but any client that relies on the meta tag (some webmail renderers) may misinterpret extended characters.

**CSS typo — silent rule drop:**
```css
lineapacing:20px
```
Found in the `.darkmode.padl10m` `<td>` inline style. Not a valid CSS property; the rule is silently ignored. Likely intended as `line-height` or `letter-spacing`.

**`max-height:auto` in mobile reset:**
```css
img { max-width:100% !important; height:auto !important; max-height:auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; the declaration is dropped. No rendering impact (the other rules cover it), but it's dead weight that can mask intent.

**`user-scalable=0`** — Non-standard value (`no` is the spec-compliant form). Broadly supported in practice, but technically incorrect and will trigger accessibility linting failures.

**Alt text — 24 images missing descriptive alt (confirmed by QA):**
Every content image (`090fe266...`, `963957c1...`, `ae842ce3...`, and 21 others) has `alt=""`. Screen readers skip these entirely, and image-blocking clients show blank boxes with no fallback text. The logo (`3b6a7dc1...`) also has `alt=""` — the brand name is lost when images are off.
