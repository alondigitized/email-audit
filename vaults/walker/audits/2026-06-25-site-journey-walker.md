# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-25**
## Full review
Now I have everything I need. Here's Walker Miles' full review.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I spent time on skechers.com today looking for a comfortable slip-on I can get in and out of without bending down — something with arch support, cushioning, decent price. The site got me to the right product without too much trouble. But when I tried to actually buy the thing, I hit a blank white screen where my cart should've been. That's not a hiccup, that's a wall. And when I searched "comfort shoes" to browse around, it served me women's shoes first — I'm a logged-in male customer. Small stuff adds up: the homepage was pushing some Britto art-shoe collaboration at a young woman, not at me. After everything I know Skechers does for comfort, today's site didn't feel like it knew me at all.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Up one point from yesterday's 3/10 — the navigation menu is genuinely better organized and the product page was readable. But the cart is blank. A blank cart is a dead end. Any improvement in the browse flow gets wiped out the moment someone can't complete a purchase. The search personalization failure is a separate punch to the gut.

---

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO banner is impossible to miss.** That blue stripe at the very top — "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" — is bold, clear, and readable without squinting. Good start. If I saw that in an email, I'd click through expecting it to work on whatever I pick.

**The Men's navigation menu is clean.** When I opened the hamburger menu and drilled into MEN, I got a well-organized slide-out panel. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" — these are exactly the words I look for. Big text, full-width rows, easy to tap with a thumb. That menu is the best screen in this whole journey.

**The Hands Free Slip-ins landing page set width options right away.** "Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit" as the first three buttons — smart. My feet have gotten wider over the years. Seeing that immediately made me feel like the right page found me.

**Product name and price are large and legible.** The D'Lites 330 page showed me the full name, $125.00 price, star rating, and a "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge right at the top of the screen. No zooming required.

**Size grid works fine on mobile.** Thirteen size boxes, half-sizes, comfortably tappable. The "Please select a size" error message in red with an icon is clear — I knew exactly what I missed.

**ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, dark blue, pinned to the bottom of the screen. Hard to miss, easy to hit with a thumb.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 is a white screen. Nothing. No items, no totals, no buttons, no error message explaining what happened. If I were a real shopper, I'd think I did something wrong, try again, maybe give up and go to DSW's app instead. This is the single most damaging thing on the site. I can't stress this enough — getting me to the cart and then showing me nothing is worse than never showing me the cart link at all. **This is a regression. I don't know if this happened yesterday, but it needs to be fixed today.**

**"Excluded from promotions" on a $125 shoe while the BOGO banner runs across every page.** That orange warning right under the price felt like a bait-and-switch. I came in seeing BOGO 50% Off. I found a shoe I liked. Then I'm told this one doesn't count. I don't know which shoes qualify — the site doesn't tell me. I'd leave and search for something else, or leave entirely.

**Search for "comfort shoes" returned women's products first.** I'm logged in as a male customer. The first two results I saw were both labeled "Women's." Skechers knows I'm a man — the account is there. This is a basic personalization miss. The results count was 1,427 which is overwhelming on its own, and when the top of that pile isn't even for me, I'm going to filter out fast or bail.

**Homepage doesn't know I exist.** The big hero image was a young woman in a Skechers Britto art-shoes collaboration. Colorful, splashy, clearly aimed at someone thirty years younger than me. No "Welcome back, Walker" moment. No "Based on your history, you might like..." Nothing. It's the same page a first-time visitor sees. After logging in, I expected something to shift. It didn't.

**Steps 2 and 3 showed no change at all.** The popup dismissal and login steps produced identical screenshots to the homepage — no popup appeared to be dismissed, and logging in showed zero change to the page I was looking at. Either the login happened invisibly (fine) or it didn't register (not fine). No confirmation, no greeting, no visual cue that my account is active.

**Only 3 reviews on the featured product.** For $125, I want to read what other men my age thought about fit and comfort. Three reviews isn't enough for me to feel confident. Hoka and New Balance product pages have hundreds. That comparison lives in my head every time I'm buying shoes.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a show-stopper. A blank cart means zero conversions from anyone who hits that step. Priority one.

2. **Filter search results by logged-in customer gender by default.** If I'm a logged-in male and I search "comfort shoes," men's shoes go first. Add a note that says "Showing Men's results — switch to Women's." Simple.

3. **Surface which products are eligible for BOGO.** Either mark excluded products clearly before I fall in love with them, or put a "BOGO-eligible" filter in the left rail so I can shop the deal with confidence. Right now the banner feels like a lie.

4. **Personalize the homepage hero for returning logged-in customers.** Show me Arch Fit or Slip-ins men's walking shoes, not a Britto collab aimed at a 25-year-old. Even a basic segment ("men's comfort") would feel like progress.

5. **Add a login confirmation state.** After I log in, show me something — my name in the account icon, a welcome banner, anything. Let me know the system recognized me.

6. **Push more reviews to comfort-focused products.** Email post-purchase requests. Surface the most helpful reviews about fit, arch support, and ease of getting on/off. Those details convert buyers like me.

Skechers makes the right shoes for me. The D'Lites 330 Arch Fit Slip-ins are exactly what I came for. But today's site treated the purchase moment like an afterthought — the cart broke, the BOGO deal doesn't apply to the shoe I wanted, and when I searched for more options it showed me women's footwear. I'd close the tab before completing the order. The site knows the words (Arch Fit, Hands Free Slip-ins, Wide Fit) but doesn't use that knowledge to take care of me as a returning customer. That gap is where Hoka and New Balance pick up my wallet.

**Score: 4/10** — up one from yesterday on the strength of a genuinely good navigation menu, but the blank cart is a critical new problem that offsets any other progress.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on **Salesforce Commerce Cloud** (evidenced by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/`). The audit surface reveals three structural problems: (1) the View Cart page is rendering a bare HTML shell missing foundational document metadata, (2) the Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint is rate-limiting itself during normal user sessions, and (3) 15+ third-party tracking scripts are loaded per page with several duplicated across requests.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (2)**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA role elements exist outside required parent containers — likely a carousel or listbox widget with incorrect DOM structure.
- `button-name` (Homepage): One or more `<button>` elements have no text content, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`. Common in icon-only CTAs (wishlist, close drawers).

**Serious (2) — View Cart**
- `document-title`: The cart page HTML response has no `<title>` element. This is not a hydration gap — it indicates a fallback/error shell is being served rather than the real cart template.
- `html-has-lang`: Same page is missing `lang="en"` on `<html>`. The homepage correctly includes `lang="en"`, so this is page-specific.

**Moderate (10)**
- `heading-order`, `landmark-*` violations across Homepage and Search are consistent with a page layout that nests `<aside>` and `<main>` inside other landmarks — likely a sidebar or modal rendered inside `<main>`.
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main`: Two `<main>` elements on Homepage — suggests a modal or drawer that misuses `<main>` as its container.
- `page-has-heading-one` missing on View Cart — further confirming it's a shell page, not the real cart template.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`**: Even if the cart is `noindex`ed, the absence of `<title>` in the served HTML suggests the page is either crashing or being served a bare error template. If search crawlers follow a link to `/cart/`, they receive malformed HTML.
- **`html-has-lang` missing on cart**: Hinders Google's language identification for that URL.
- **`heading-order` on Homepage**: Skipped heading levels can reduce crawlability of page structure for rich-result parsing.
- No other SEO metadata issues observable in the truncated HTML (homepage includes `lang="en"` and appears to have a structured `<head>`).

- **15+ distinct third-party domains** in `<head>` (TikTok, GTM×4, Pinterest, Bing, Reddit, Quantum Metric, Impact, Bloomreach, Monetate, Snap, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Pulse Insights, Stylitics, Tune/Go2SDK). Each is a separate DNS resolution + TLS handshake chain.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property** is set inline on `<html>` via JavaScript (`style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"`). This is a standard workaround for iOS Safari's dynamic browser chrome changing `100vh`, but the floating-point precision (`6.640000000000001`) indicates the value is computed as `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding — minor but avoidable.
- The JS-based viewport height fix means layout is recalculated on JS execution, introducing a render-blocking dependency for any element using `calc(var(--vh) * 100)`.
- No viewport meta tag visible in the truncated source — cannot confirm `<meta name="viewport">` is present, though its absence would be immediately obvious in testing.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada fingerprinting (6 hits)**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.513
```
Kasada's JavaScript challenge (`kpsdk`) is rate-limiting its own fingerprint submission endpoint during normal browser sessions. This suggests either: (a) the Kasada token isn't being accepted and the client is retrying, or (b) the rate limit threshold is set too low for legitimate multi-page sessions. This can silently break bot-protection token acquisition, leaving downstream API calls unprotected.

**429 — `/cart/`**
The cart URL itself returned 429. Combined with the missing `<title>` and `lang` on the cart page, this strongly suggests the cart is being served a rate-limit error page rather than the real cart template.

**403 — `survey.pulseinsights.com` (3 hits)**
Pulse Insights survey endpoint returning 403. The `udid` and `identifier` parameters are present, so the script is initializing correctly — the 403 likely indicates a misconfigured domain allowlist on the Pulse Insights account.

**CORS — Stylitics**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css' has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics's CDN is not sending CORS headers for cross-origin stylesheet requests. The Stylitics widget (outfit/look-builder) will render without its styles. This is a vendor-side configuration issue but needs to be escalated to their integration team.

**ERR_FAILED (1)**
One unresolved resource failure with no URL logged — likely a preload or beacon that was cancelled.

## Recommendations

**High priority**
1. **Investigate cart 429**: The `/cart/` endpoint returning 429 to a real browser session is the most severe functional issue. Determine whether Kasada's rate limiting is mis-triggering on the cart URL and whitelist or tune the threshold.
2. **Fix View Cart HTML shell**: The page is serving a response missing `<title>` and `lang` — diagnose whether this is the 429 response body or a SFCC template error. The real cart page must render full document metadata.
3. **Kasada retry loop**: 6 consecutive 429s on the fingerprint endpoint in a single session indicates the SDK is stuck in a retry cycle. Review Kasada SDK version and rate-limit configuration.

**Medium priority**
4. **Deduplicate third-party scripts**: Yahoo `ytc.js`, Monetate (v2 + v3), and Creative CDN are each loaded twice per page. Remove duplicates at the GTM or SFCC template level — double-firing inflates event counts and wastes bandwidth.
5. **Escalate Stylitics CORS**: File a support ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN responses for `web-assets.stylitics.com`.
6. **Fix `button-name` violations**: Audit icon buttons (wishlist, close, drawer toggles) for missing accessible labels — these are critical WCAG 2.1 AA failures.
7. **Fix `aria-required-parent`**: Locate the carousel/listbox component on Homepage with orphaned ARIA role children and correct the DOM nesting.

**Low priority**
8. **Round `--vh` value**: `(window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(4)` prevents the floating-point string; negligible but cleaner.
9. **Capture LCP and CLS**: The audit is missing the two most impactful Web Vitals. Add field data collection (CrUX or RUM) to establish baselines before any performance work.
10. **Pulse Insights 403**: Verify the domain allowlist in the Pulse Insights account settings includes `www.skechers.com`.
