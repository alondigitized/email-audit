# Here’s the deal: 20% OFF!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. Fifteen. I've lost count at this point, and today DSW shows up with "Here's the deal: 20% OFF!" — which at least has me landing on something I care about, a discount. I opened it. But the moment I'm inside, I'm staring at a campaign called "(UN)PLUG & PLAY" with a woman in a green tropical outfit and three rows of sandals I have no interest in. The 20% is real, the offer is there at the top, and it took about three seconds to find "SHOP MEN" buried lower in the layout. That's the best I can say.
- What really broke the spell was the Owala water bottle. Right there in the middle of the email — "BACK IN STOCK" in bold, with a green button. I don't know why DSW is selling me a water bottle, but I didn't come here for that. I came here wondering if there were Brooks or New Balance or Skechers slip-ons marked down 20%. The email never answers that. It just keeps being a sandals campaign wearing a different subject line.
- I won't click. I'll note the 20% and if my feet actually need something in the next few days I'll navigate there myself. I would not forward this — nothing here that my brother-in-law or neighbor would care about either.

## What's working

- The 20% off figure is the very first thing I read — orange banner, clean type, no burying the lead.
- "SHOP MEN" button is in the layout, so there's at least an on-ramp for me once I scroll.
- "Summer SALE IS ON" is seasonally correct — it's July, this makes sense.
- The "Shop by Size" grid lower in the email is genuinely useful if I'm trying to filter for my size fast.
- DSW is a brand I know — I opened it, and the email looked like DSW. No identity confusion.

## What's weak

- The Owala water bottle "BACK IN STOCK" section has no business being in a footwear discount email — completely broke my focus.
- The hero model is a young woman in a vacation outfit; I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for walking shoes — this creative isn't aimed at me.
- "(UN)PLUG & PLAY" repeated three times in giant text means nothing to me; I want to know what shoes are 20% off, not parse campaign wordplay.
- The preview text in my inbox leaked what looked like garbage filler characters after "Soak up the summer savings" — it looked broken before I even opened it.
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support, or even men's walking shoes anywhere in the visible body — I'd have to hunt to know if this sale applies to anything I'd actually buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull a men's comfort shoe into the hero** — one image of a cushioned Skechers or New Balance with "20% off men's walking shoes" would have me reading the whole email instead of skimming past sandal creative.
- 2. **Kill the Owala water bottle section** — or at minimum move it to the very bottom footer. A non-shoe product in the middle of the email breaks the browse flow and erodes trust in DSW's focus.
- 3. **Add a real deadline** — "through Sunday" or "ends tonight" somewhere near the 20% off claim would give me a reason to act now instead of later (which usually means never).
- 4. **Segment the creative** — a men's-specific version of this email exists somewhere in the data; I shouldn't be getting vacation-women-sandals imagery when my purchase history is walking shoes.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — spacer characters leaking into the inbox preview kills first impressions before I even open it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` capitalization in all `@font-face` declarations (critical)**
Every `@font-face` block uses `Src:` (capital S) instead of the required lowercase `src:`. This is invalid CSS and will cause all custom fonts (Futura PT and HelveticaNow) to silently fail to load in every client that attempts to parse them.

```css
/* Broken — all 10 @font-face blocks */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');

/* Should be */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```

**Italic `@font-face` variants declared with `font-style: normal`**
Three HelveticaNow italic variants are registered with incorrect `font-style: normal`:
- `HelveticaNow_MediumItalic`
- `HelveticaNow_BoldItalic`
- `HelveticaNow_Italic`

They will never be matched as italic faces. Any text relying on these variants will either fall back to the regular weight or render unstyled.

**Incomplete `<meta>` attributes**
Two meta tags are missing their `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, Outlook/IE will not apply the charset declaration or the Edge rendering directive.

**Near-black hex values in dark mode palette (likely typo)**
Three dark mode rules use `#00000F` or `#000001` instead of `#000000`:
```css
.dm-bg-black    { background: #00000F !important; }  /* blue channel = 15 */
.dm-font-black  { color:      #000001 !important; }  /* blue channel = 1  */
.border-top-black { border-top: 1px #000001 solid; } /* mobile dark mode  */
```
These values are visually imperceptible from black but are inconsistent with any intentional palette. Likely copy-paste or hex-editor error.

**WOFF-only for Futura PT (no WOFF2, no TTF fallback)**
Futura PT fonts declare only `format('woff')`. HelveticaNow correctly uses TTF. Neither uses WOFF2. Low impact given email client font support, but increases load latency where fonts *do* render (Apple Mail, iOS Mail).
