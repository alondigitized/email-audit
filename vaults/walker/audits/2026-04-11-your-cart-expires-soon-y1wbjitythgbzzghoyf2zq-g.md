# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-11**
## Executive summary

- A competent cart abandonment email that delivers the right skeleton — personalized product, recognizable headline, clear CTA — but then immediately undermines its urgency premise by loading up with generic marketing filler. The subject line promises "expires soon" but the email body never reinforces it. Below the fold is a near-complete marketing newsletter: category nav, app download, BNPL logos, find-a-store, and a text opt-in pitch. Skechers had the recipient's attention at the hero. Everything after the product grid dilutes it.
- Gets the job done for a returning, motivated buyer. Fails anyone who needed urgency to tip them over the edge.

## What's working

- **Headline lands.** "YOU LIKED THESE AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG" is confident and on-brand — better than generic "You left something behind" copy.
- **Hero product is specific.** The Slip-ins: Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 - Smooth is named and shown, not hidden in small type.
- **CTA is prominent and correctly placed.** "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" in teal directly under the hero image — no hunting required.
- **Best sellers grid adds optionality.** If the abandoned item isn't the one, there are alternative products visible before the promotional banner.
- **20% OFF offer is visible.** Even if the application is unclear, the discount is a real nudge.

## What's weak

- **No urgency signal in the body.** The subject says "expires soon" — but nothing in the email itself shows a timer, deadline, or countdown. The copy doesn't repeat the expiry message even once.
- **20% OFF module is ambiguous.** It's sandwiched between the product section and category nav with no explanation of whether it applies to the cart item or sitewide. Recipients will hesitate.
- **Category nav is newsletter-level filler.** WOMENS / MENS / KIDS / CLOTHING / NEW ARRIVALS / SALE is catalog content dropped into a transactional email. It siphons intent away from completion.
- **"LET'S GET TEXTING" has no place here.** An SMS opt-in acquisition pitch in a cart recovery email is a distraction at exactly the wrong moment.
- **Utility block overload.** Download Our App, Curbside Pickup, Shop Now Pay Later (Afterpay + Klarna), and Find a Store are all stacked together. Each module is individually defensible; together they read as an everything-but-the-kitchen-sink footer.
- **Product grid images are small.** The best sellers row is narrow and the products are difficult to evaluate at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an urgency callout in the body** — a simple "Your cart expires in 24 hours" line or a visual countdown directly under the CTA. Match the subject line promise.
- 2. **Anchor the 20% OFF offer to the cart item** — explicitly state whether the code applies to the abandoned product or clarify "sitewide." Ambiguity kills conversion.
- 3. **Remove or move the "LET'S GET TEXTING" module.** This belongs in a post-purchase or loyalty nurture flow, not a cart recovery email.
- 4. **Cut the category nav.** This is a one-job email; every exit path that isn't "Complete Your Order" is a cost.
- 5. **Consolidate the utility block** — pick the one most likely to reduce friction at checkout (BNPL is the strongest candidate) and drop the rest from this send.

### 1. Technical Summary

HTML is well-formed table-based layout sent through Attentive's platform (`skechers.attentivemail.com`). Two confirmed issues require remediation: insecure click-tracking links (HTTP) and a non-functional charset meta tag.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP click-tracking redirects (confirmed)**
All links route through `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...` using plain HTTP, not HTTPS. Example:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6VorXO...
```
This exposes click-tracking redirects to interception and can trigger mixed-content warnings in clients that enforce HTTPS. Attentive supports HTTPS tracking links — this should be enabled at the account/send level.

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
All destination URLs are encoded inside the `upn=` parameter. UTM attribution cannot be confirmed or denied without decoding. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag (confirmed)**
```html
<title></title>
```
Apple Mail and some screen readers surface the `<title>` element. An empty value degrades accessibility and can confuse tab labels in webmail clients.

**Non-functional charset `<meta>` (confirmed)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent, making this tag a no-op. Clients that rely on the meta tag for charset detection (notably older Outlook versions) may fall back to their default encoding. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Mixed preheader padding characters (confirmed)**
The preheader uses two distinct invisible Unicode characters interleaved:
- `͏` (U+034F, Combining Grapheme Joiner)
- `­` (U+00AD, Soft Hyphen)

Soft hyphens can render as visible `-` characters in Outlook on Windows, causing the preheader padding to bleed into the preview pane as a string of hyphens. Standard practice is to use only U+200C (Zero-Width Non-Joiner) or U+00A0 (Non-Breaking Space) for preheader stuffing, not soft hyphens.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>` — no issue**
Viewport meta present — no issue.
Image `alt` text — not verifiable from truncated source.
