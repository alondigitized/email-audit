# 20% off + the latest picks
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Another one. I've been watching DSW fire off 20%-off emails like they're running out of days in the year, and here comes "20% off + the latest picks" — which I almost didn't open because I could've sworn I just deleted the same email an hour ago. The preview text told me "Something special is coming tomorrow," which had nothing to do with the subject line's promise of today's deal. Mixed signals before I even got inside.
- Once I opened it, the first thing I saw was "HEY, WHAT'S NEW?" — friendly enough — but every shoe in that grid was a woman's heel, a strappy sandal, or a platform. I scrolled past eight women's dress shoes before I hit anything resembling what I'd actually wear. Down near the bottom there was an "ATHLETIC & SNEAKERS" tile and a "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" link — those are the only two things in this whole email that belong in my cart. The rest of it might as well have been addressed to my daughter.
- The 20% off is real and the button is clear. But I'm marking this as read and moving on. I'm not forwarding it to anyone, and I'm not clicking through to wade past another wall of heels to find a walking shoe.

## What's working

- The "CLAIM YOUR 20% OFF" button at the top is immediate — no hunting for the offer, it's right there.
- "SUMMER SAVINGS" hero is clean and loads fast; no broken images, no garbled text in the body.
- The Birkenstock loyalty banner at the very top reminded me points are in play — I noticed it.
- "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" link buried in the size section is actually exactly what I look for; just wish it weren't buried.
- The email renders cleanly at the size I view things — no squinting to read the discount.

## What's weak

- Eight women's heels and sandals greeted me before I saw a single shoe that could fit on my foot — this email was not built for me.
- The preview text ("Something special is coming tomorrow…") contradicts the subject ("20% off + the latest picks today") — I thought I was opening the wrong email.
- "GET YOUR PARTY SHOES READY" with a tomorrow-tease is bait; it's asking me to open yet another email tomorrow, and I'm already at my limit for this week.
- This is the seventh or eighth 20%-off DSW email I've gotten in the last three days — the "20% off + the best of the best" from yesterday was nearly identical, and that one landed a 6. At some point the discount stops feeling like a deal and starts feeling like background noise.
- No men's shoe shown hero-level — not a sneaker, not a loafer, nothing I'd actually wear shown above the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Send me a segmented email** — I'm a man who bought walking shoes last time. Show me men's new arrivals, not the women's heels grid. If you know who I am (and your loyalty program does), use it.
- 2. **Kill the tomorrow tease** — "SOMETHING BIG IS COMING TOMORROW" kills any click urgency I had for today's offer. Either deliver it or drop it.
- 3. **Lead with one specific men's shoe** — put a New Balance, Skechers GO Walk, or Hoka Bondi right in the hero, 20% off, with the size picker right there. Done. I click.
- 4. **Fix the preview text** — it's a completely different message than the subject, and the row of invisible spacer characters is visible in some clients. Align preview to subject.
- 5. **Give the offer a soft deadline** — even "Ends Sunday" would've pushed me off the fence.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on two `<meta>` tags** (head, lines 3 and 5):
```html
<!-- As written — non-standard, ignored by strict parsers -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- Correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Both tags are inert without `http-equiv`. The charset declaration is especially important — without it, Outlook and strict XHTML parsers may not apply the encoding.

**`Src:` (capital S) in all 11 `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but this will fail strict CSS validators and has been known to break font loading in some WebKit builds. Should be lowercase `src:`.

**No `woff2` format declared for any font family:**
- Futura PT (4 weights): `.woff` only — woff2 is 25–30% smaller and supported by all modern clients
- HelveticaNow (6 weights): `.ttf` only — TTF has the worst compression of any web font format and loads last in client priority order; should be `woff2` → `woff` → `ttf` fallback chain

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Common in email templates but technically non-compliant.

**Near-black hex workaround (`#000001`, `#00000F`) in dark mode classes:** These are intentional — preventing dark mode clients from inverting pure `#000000` to white. Correct technique, not a defect.
