- **Timely product moment.** This reads like a real new-arrival drop, not recycled clearance language from last week.

## What's weak

- **That preheader is a raw tracking URL. Again.** I flagged this exact problem on the July 12 flash sale email. Famous Footwear is now two-for-something on broken preheaders — it doesn't get less embarrassing on repeat.
- **Zero relevance to me.** Not one word about comfort, cushioning, arch support, or walking shoes. Every inch of this email is basketball sneaker culture.
- **Kids section eats up real estate.** Half the product grid is children's Jordans. I'm 62. My kids are grown.
- **No offer, no price.** "New Jordan in the bag" sounds exciting if you follow drops, but there's nothing to act on — no discount, no price anchor, no reason to click today over never.
- **Same Y2K app promo as July 13.** Same banner, same "3X Points," same "Download the App" ask. If it didn't move me three days ago, it's not moving me now.

- 1. **Fix the preheader pipeline.** Two broken preheaders in four days is a QA problem, not a fluke. The inbox first impression is destroyed before the email opens.
- 2. **Segment me out of Jordan sends.** A 62-year-old who has never purchased basketball shoes from this brand should not be on the Jordan new-arrivals list. Route me to comfort walkers and sale events instead.
- 3. **Give me a reason to act today.** Even a bare price anchor — "$89.99, just landed" — turns a browse into a consideration. No price, no urgency, no click.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "New Jordan in the bag" Email

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard table-based HTML build on an ESP (Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget, based on the `click.email.famousfootwear.com` domain pattern). No critical failures; the primary issues are missing alt text on multiple images and a plain-text part that is URL-heavy enough to raise spam filter concern.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links unverifiable.** All CTA links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` with encoded query strings. The QA probe skipped all of them — no broken-link confirmation is possible. This is expected for ESP click-tracking but means destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be independently verified.

**Open-pixel present.**
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?DA4LSL25XWEUFF22NUTYYPBLOI.70248
```
Standard 1×1 pixel tracker; correctly implemented with `width="1" height="1" alt=""`.

No issues with link structure beyond the skipped-probe limitation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 11 instances across 9 distinct images.** Two images (`4ccff4ad...jpg`) appear 4 times total with blank alt text each time. The logo image (`3b6a7dc1...png`) also has `alt=""`, which means screen readers get no brand identification from the header.

Affected files:
- `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png` — logo, should have `alt="Famous Footwear"`
- `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` — divider (4× reuse; `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative spacers, but confirm intent)
- `d7e6503d-9825-4765-8edf-571c1fac4638.jpg`, `d2128cf7...jpg`, `52ff4ac4...jpg`, `c9220297...jpg`, `8714175e...jpg` — product/hero images with no alt text (content-bearing images must have descriptive alt)
- `538d55be-8933-43c7-ad06-ce4b6ba89c70.gif` — animated asset, no alt text

**`<meta charset>` tag is malformed.** The charset meta is missing the `http-equiv` attribute:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
<!-- should be -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Most clients tolerate this but it is technically invalid and can cause character-decoding fallback behavior in legacy Outlook.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta.** This prevents zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility barrier (WCAG 1.4.4).

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE.** Declared but irrelevant to modern clients; no rendering impact, but signals the template is not being maintained to current standards.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** The truncated source cuts off before the footer; unsubscribe link and physical address presence cannot be confirmed from the provided HTML. The QA summary reports 0 issues, suggesting automated compliance checks passed — but this should be manually verified in the full source.

**Plain-text part is URL-saturated.** QA flagged:
> Plain-text is >70% URLs: 4535/6027 chars are URLs

A plain-text alternative where 75% of content is raw tracking URLs is effectively unreadable and will score poorly with spam filters that evaluate plain-text quality (SpamAssassin `URI_PHISHING`-family rules). The plain-text part needs human-readable content with links appended, not a URL dump.

**Authentication headers** (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) are not assessable from the HTML source alone — requires header inspection of a received copy.
