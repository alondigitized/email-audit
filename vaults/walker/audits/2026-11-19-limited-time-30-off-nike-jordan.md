- The "30% OFF" and "Skechers 25% OFF" numbers are huge and readable on a phone.
- Skechers Glide-Step is a brand and line I recognize, and it's in my comfort lane.
- The HEYDUDE Wally Stretch Jersey photo shows an easy slip-on, which is what I look for.
- The $10 off $74.98 and $25 off $149.98 coupon with the HOLIDAY code is clear, and the barcodes are handy.
- The SHOE P·E·R·K·S line at the top and the $10 reward at the bottom mention loyalty without making it the whole pitch.

## What's weak

- The hero is Nike and Jordan, which isn't me, and it takes up the top third of the screen.
- It's too long. I count about a dozen blocks, and it's hard to find what's for a 62-year-old man.
- The Crocs block is mostly women's and kids' shoes, with one men's clog in the middle.
- The fine print is tiny and I can't read it without zooming. "Select styles" and "some exclusions apply" make me wonder how honest the 30% is.
- It's the same coupon again, and nothing here says "you've seen this one already."
- There's nothing about arch support, cushioning or fit, so no reason to pick these over Hoka or Brooks.

- 1. Lead with comfort for someone like me: Skechers Glide-Step, Crocs and HEYDUDE slip-ons in the hero, not Nike and Jordan.
- 2. Cut the length and send me the three blocks I'd actually use.
- 3. Say what the shoe does: "cushioned," "arch support," "slip-in."
- 4. Make the fine print bigger, and tell me which brands are excluded.
- 5. Slow the cadence, or at least stop repeating the same coupon.
