# The 9060
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- Well, New Balance finally sent me an email that leads with a shoe, which is more than I can say for Skechers and its shopping-bag wall. The first thing on my phone is a big photo of a cream-colored 9060 with a pair of gray pants, then two black buttons, "Shop adults" and "Shop kids." Six sneaker photos sit below that in a tidy grid.
- It's a pretty email, but it's a fashion email. "Modern expressionism" and "timeless style" don't tell me about cushioning or arch support. There's no price and no sale, and nobody who looks like me. The only men I see are in the holiday gift photo at the bottom. I'd scroll through it and admire it, but I probably wouldn't click. I wouldn't forward it either.

## What's working

- The hero is a shoe, large and clear, and I know right away what's being sold.
- The grid of six 9060 colors lets me see the shoe from several angles without hunting for it.
- The red "Free shipping on all orders" line is something I can actually use.
- The top nav has Men, Women, Kids and Holiday, and the footer has "Walking," so I can find my category.
- The text is clean and the layout is simple, which suits my phone.

## What's weak

- Nothing says cushioning, comfort or arch support. This chunky shoe could be a comfortable walker, but the email never tells me.
- The six shoes in the grid have no names, prices or sizes, so I can't tell what I'd be paying.
- The "Complete the look" section shows a young woman in a hoodie and cargo pants. That's nowhere near my wardrobe.
- The buttons say "Shop adults" and "Shop kids." I'd like a "Shop men's" button instead.
- "Shop all" and "Shop now" are small and thin. They're hard to hit with a thumb.
- The holiday gifts photo has a man in it, but it comes at the very end, and the email never links it to a shoe I'd buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a comfort line under the headline. Say something like "All-day cushioning for walking" so I know why this shoe matters.
- 2. Add the name and price under each shoe in the grid, and make the grid tappable. Let the price tell me if it's worth it.
- 3. Change "Shop adults" to "Shop men's 9060" and make the buttons bigger.
- 4. Show a man closer to my age wearing the shoe, and put it higher up.
- 5. Make the free-shipping line more prominent, and say whether there's a minimum.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a reused, template-derived build with several leftover template artifacts (placeholder URL, duplicate CSS rules, non-functional `@font-face` blocks). Automated QA shows no hard failures, only 3 images without alt text and an oversized, URL-heavy plain-text part. The source was truncated, so findings below cover only what was visible or reported by QA.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- QA probed 12 material links and skipped 26. It reported no broken or redirecting links among the probed set, so the skipped 26 are unverified.
- The plain-text part is 11,290 of 12,776 characters URLs (about 88%). That points to long wrapped tracking URLs written out in full. This is a [WARN] from QA and can hurt spam scoring on plain-text-weighted filters.
- The `.bgwhite` CSS rule contains a leftover placeholder: `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')`. It is an insecure `http://` URL pointing to a non-existent host. It will 404 or trigger a mixed-content request wherever the class is applied.
- The tracking pixel `https://link.email.newbalance.com/mo/egR1f966kc_818512772_12597613_2527297_23799533.gif` is a standard open-tracking beacon. This is not a defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed):**
  - `ShippingIcons_06.png`
  - `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`
  - `egR1f966kc_…533.gif`: the tracking pixel should carry an explicit `alt=""`, which the check treats as missing.
- **Placeholder title:** `<title>New Balance Template</title>` is a leftover. It is shown as the tab title in web-view and is sometimes used by assistive tech.
- **Malformed meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv` attribute, so it does nothing.
- **Conflicting CSS:** `.bg1` is declared twice. `{background-color:#ffffff}` is overridden by `{background:#58546d}` later in the same sheet. The dark-mode override also sets `.bg1{background:#151415 !important}`, so the cascade is unpredictable.
- **Non-functional `@font-face` blocks:** every `@font-face` rule (proxima-nova, linotype-didot, ITC Garamond Book Condensed) has no `src:`. They load nothing and fall back to Arial/Times. One block also contains a stray duplicate `font-family` declaration (`proxima-nova, font-style: italic; font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif`).
- **Unquoted multi-word font names:** `ITC Garamond Book Condensed` and `Times New Roman` are unquoted in the CSS.
- **Doctype/markup mismatch:** the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is combined with an `xmlns` attribute and XHTML-style self-closing tags (`<meta … />`). It is harmless in most clients but inconsistent.
- **Dark-mode override:** `a {color:#cf0a2c !important}` forces all links to one colour in dark mode, regardless of per-component link classes such as `.la2`. That risks contrast problems on dark backgrounds.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[[…]]`) were visible in the truncated source or reported by QA. The `XXXXX` placeholder noted in section 2 is a CSS asset stub, not a personalization token.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication-Results header not found (QA WARN):** SPF/DKIM/DMARC status could not be verified. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay stripping or not exposing the header. It is an observability gap, not evidence of failure.
- **Unsubscribe:** QA states an unsubscribe link is always included in probing. No result was reported for it, and the footer was not in the truncated HTML, so I can't confirm its presence, the physical address, or `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers.
- **Sender:** `newbalance@email.newbalance.com` is consistent with the `link.email.newbalance.com` tracking domain, which is the same organizational domain.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Asset path:** the image paths contain `/NBUS/myNB_Welcome/UpdatedImages/`. These are assets from the myNB Welcome template, reused in a "The 9060" campaign. That suggests template cloning and raises the chance of other stale references.
- **Links:** CTAs are wrapped in the ESP click tracker (`link.email.newbalance.com`). UTM parameters and final landing URLs could not be verified because the visible source is truncated and QA did not report redirect targets.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to `ShippingIcons_06.png` and `ShippingBanner_DarkMode_03.png`. Add `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`) to the tracking GIF.
2. Remove the `.bgwhite` placeholder `http://image.XXXXX.png`, or replace it with a real HTTPS asset.
3. Replace the `<title>` with the campaign subject, and fix the meta tag to `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` or remove it.
4. Resolve the duplicate `.bg1` rule, and add `src:` to the `@font-face` blocks or delete them.
5. Shorten the plain-text version by using a shorter tracked link per CTA, or fewer links, to bring the URL ratio down from about 88%.
6. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC and `List-Unsubscribe` on the original message, not the relayed copy, since AgentMail drops `Authentication-Results`.
7. Raise the probe limit, or run a second pass, to cover the 26 unprobed links. Check final landing URLs and UTM parameters on those too.
