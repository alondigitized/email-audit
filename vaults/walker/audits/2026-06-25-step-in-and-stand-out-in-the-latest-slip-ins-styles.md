# Step in and Stand out in the latest Slip-Ins Styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — Skechers lands in my inbox and before I've even tapped to open it, I'm staring at that same wall of raw JSON garbage in the preview. Seventh straight email, at least, with that broken code showing instead of actual copy. At this point it's almost a signature of theirs. Annoying.
- Once I'm inside though, the story gets better. "Slip-Ins" is exactly what I'm shopping for — no laces, no bending down, foot just slides in. That hero banner delivers: a real man in casual clothes, a clean BOGO offer right up top, and "Shop Slip-Ins" buttons I can actually tap without squinting. The shoe in the Best Sellers block looks like something I'd genuinely put on my feet. That black slip-on? I'd click on that.
- But the email goes long and loses focus fast. The second hero block is a woman in workout tights, the 25% Off Apparel section does nothing for me, and by the time I hit the app download and payment options I've already mentally checked out. I'd probably tap the first "Shop Slip-Ins" button and call it done. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and maybe buy something? Probably.

## What's working

- **Slip-Ins front and center** — the subject line, the hero copy, the CTA buttons — all point directly at the one feature I actually care about: slip it on, walk out the door.
- **BOGO 50% off is unmissable** — it's the first thing my eye lands on, above the hero image. Concrete, simple, no math required.
- **Hero features a man** — the first lifestyle block has a guy in casual street clothes, not a twenty-year-old doing CrossFit. That actually registers with me.
- **Best Sellers block** — a black slip-on shoe, clearly photographed. That's a product I'd consider. Clean visual, easy tap.
- **"SHOP SLIP-INS" button copy** — specific verb + noun. Not "Discover More." Not "Explore." I know exactly where I'm going.

## What's weak

- **That preview text.** Still broken JSON. Has been every single Skechers email since early June. I've now audited six of these in a row and it hasn't been fixed once. Whoever owns that template needs to hear about it today, not next send.
- **The woman in the second hero block** — she's in black leggings doing something athletic. That's not me, and it breaks the "this is your email" feeling fast.
- **25% Off Apparel section** — I'm not here for socks and hoodies. Wasted real estate that pushes the Best Sellers shoe further down.
- **No comfort detail anywhere** — the email never mentions cushioning, arch support, or what makes Slip-Ins easy on the feet. "Fresh & Easy" is pleasant but vague. Tell me my feet won't hurt.
- **Email cadence is relentless** — this is the second email in two days. The one from June 14th was "Members Only BOGO" and this one is… also BOGO? Starts to feel like a permanent sale, and permanent sales stop feeling like deals.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately.** This is not a creative problem, it's a broken template. Every send with that JSON garbage trains subscribers to ignore it. Escalate it as a P1 bug before the next campaign goes out.
- 2. **Lead the second hero block with a man's shoe** — swap the woman-in-leggings block for a men's Slip-In with one comfort callout: "Memory Foam Insole" or "No-Lace, No-Bend." That's the one sentence that would make me lean in.
- 3. **Cut or collapse the Apparel section** — if it has to stay, push it below the fold. Shoe first, always shoe first.
- 4. **Add a real deadline** — "BOGO ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" would make the offer feel real. Right now the BOGO floats there with no urgency, which reads as "this will probably still be here next week."
- 5. **One comfort spec on the hero shoe** — even just a line under the product image: "Cushioned insole · Hands-free entry." Gives me a reason to click *that* shoe specifically.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Skechers BOGO ends soon — Slip-Ins are in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `No-lace comfort: Slip-Ins BOGO 50% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy one pair of Slip-Ins, get a second half off — cushioned, hands-free, easy on.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO 50% off Slip-Ins — the style that slides on and stays comfortable all day.`

## Technical Audit — Skechers Slip-Ins Email (06/17/2026)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud send with functional structure and responsive CSS. The primary technical risks are pervasive HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources across all hosted assets and a third-party pixel beacon loaded over HTTP — both of which will trigger mixed-content blocking in modern email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **26 click-redirect links** route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — QA correctly skipped HTTP probing these (redirect chains mask destination). Final destinations were not verified; recommend a separate link-checker pass against actual landing pages.
- **Open-pixel protocol mismatch:** The Salesforce open-tracking pixel (`click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?...`) is HTTPS. However, the secondary open pixel from `ink1000.com` is HTTP:
  ```
  http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165
  ```
  This pixel will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail's image proxy — open tracking from this vendor will be systematically undercounted.
- **Kruxd (Salesforce DMP) user-match beacons** fire in the hidden preheader table using `beacon.krxd.net`. These are HTTPS and structurally hidden (`display:none`) — acceptable, but note the `partner_uid=121173391` value appears to be a static/unhashed subscriber ID, not the SHA-256 variant. The companion beacon uses `sha25` (truncated — likely `sha256`) in the query param key name, suggesting an inconsistency in the DMP configuration.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — mixed content risk (high):**

All 13+ images hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com` use `http://` rather than `https://`. Examples:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png  (Skechers logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-...png  (App Store badge)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/9bb508d0-...png  (Google Play badge)
```
Clients that enforce HTTPS-only content (Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection, Outlook.com) will block or proxy these, causing broken images for a significant share of recipients.

**Missing alt text:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel) — minor, hidden pixel
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — visible image with no `alt` attribute; unknown content from truncated HTML, but if this is a product or promotional image it fails WCAG 2.1 AA (criterion 1.1.1) and renders blank in image-off environments

**CSS viewport lock:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user zoom — this is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and is flagged by screen reader tools. Remove the `maximum-scale` constraint.

**Duplicate responsive CSS blocks:** The `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` breakpoints contain identical rule sets. This is harmless but adds ~1.5KB of dead payload and indicates template maintenance debt.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is set globally. This suppresses iOS font scaling for visually impaired users — should be `100%` not `none`.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Cannot confirm from truncated source:**
- Physical mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5))
- Unsubscribe link presence and functional one-click mechanism (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3), and RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header for Gmail/Yahoo deliverability)

The send domain is `emails.skechers.com` with `no-reply@emails.skechers.com` as the From address. Authentication headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not included in the provided data — these should be verified against the received headers of a live send before deployment sign-off.

**Kruxd DMP pixel fires without explicit user consent signal** embedded in the payload. If any EU/UK recipients are in the send list, this constitutes a GDPR/UK GDPR risk depending on consent configuration upstream in the ESP.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The campaign identifier visible in two pixels is:
```
MKG_US_L30D_U_SLIPIN_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_06172026
```
This is an internal SFMC campaign tag, not a UTM parameter. The 26 click-redirect URLs were not probed, so it is unknown whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to destination URLs. If click-tracking redirects strip or fail to pass UTMs, GA4/Adobe Analytics will mis-attribute this traffic as direct.

Recommend: spot-check 3–4 destination URLs by following the `click.emails.skechers.com` redirect chain and confirming UTM parameters survive to the Skechers.com landing page.
