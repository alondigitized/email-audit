# This is it: $10, $20, or $60 OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- DSW in my sender column, and my thumb was already moving — that muscle memory is real at this point, I've opened almost every one of their sends for two weeks running. The subject was different this time though: tiers. Not just "$10 off" again but $10, $20, or $60 depending on how much I spend. That caught my brain, not just my thumb.
- Then the email loaded, and it was the same story I've seen in sends like the June 21st "$10 off trendy floral styles" one — wall-to-wall women's shoes. Strappy sandals, heeled wedges, open-toe everything. The section header literally says "THEY'RE JUST SOOO YOU," and it is absolutely, emphatically not me. I'm a 62-year-old guy hunting for something cushioned with arch support I can slip on — none of that showed up in the scroll.
- The tiered discount is genuinely interesting and I don't want to dismiss it. I kept reading partly because of those numbers — $60 off is real money. But nothing in the visuals gave me a reason to click through to men's. I'd mark this as read and maybe go to the DSW website directly on my own terms rather than via this email's CTAs. Wouldn't forward it — nothing to forward to another guy my age.

## What's working

- **Tiered discount is the strongest hook DSW has sent me in weeks** — $10/$20/$60 is more interesting than the flat "$10 off ends tomorrow" I've seen on repeat.
- **Offer hierarchy at the top is immediately readable** — the three tiers sit right in the banner above the fold, no scrolling needed to see the deal.
- **"We're in the final hours" preview** actually complements the subject instead of repeating it — that's better than most of their recent sends.
- **Crocs and Nike logos visible** in the brand section — those are names I recognize and actually shop.
- **No layout bugs** — email rendered clean, text over images was legible.

## What's weak

- **Every product shown is women's footwear** — heels, strappy sandals, wedge slides. Not one men's shoe visible in any section.
- **"THEY'RE JUST SOOO YOU" copy is aggressively feminine** — that language pushes me out of the email immediately.
- **Size grid at the bottom appears to be women's sizing** — if there's a men's shop-by-size option, I can't see it.
- **Zero comfort or fit language** — no mention of cushioning, arch support, or slip-on styling anywhere in what I can see.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is at minimum the fifth DSW email I've opened in a week with some variation of "$10 off ends soon." Even with the tiered upgrade, it starts to feel like the boy who cried wolf.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the send by gender** — send me men's shoes. The entire creative below the offer banner should be filtered to the subscriber's actual browsing history. I have never bought women's shoes from DSW; this shouldn't be hard.
- 2. **Add one men's comfort hero product above the fold** — a Crocs or New Balance slip-on with a "men's comfort" label under the discount banner would make the tiered offer feel like it's for me.
- 3. **Retire "SOOO YOU" for any send that hasn't been segmented** — it's only charming if it's true.
- 4. **Call out what I need to spend to hit each tier** — "$10 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $149" (or whatever the thresholds are) should be in the banner itself, not buried in fine print.
- 5. **Rest the cadence** — three emails a week from DSW is the edge of my patience; five-plus is where I start evaluating the unsubscribe button.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**`Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` blocks — invalid CSS**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/.../original.woff") format('woff');
```
The CSS property `src` is case-sensitive in some parsers. All ten declarations use `Src:` instead of `src:`. Most major email clients ignore web fonts entirely, but this is a latent bug if font support expands.

**Conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` in the `*` selector**
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none }
```
The property is declared twice in the same rule. The second declaration (`none`) overrides the first (`100%`), disabling text-size adjustment on IE/Outlook for Windows entirely. The correct value is `100%`.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, older Outlook/Lotus clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 violation (Resize Text, Level AA).

**HelveticaNow fonts served as `.ttf` only**
```
https://braze-images.com/.../original.ttf?1755182939
```
TTF is the heaviest font format. Futura PT at least uses `.woff`; HelveticaNow has no `.woff` or `.woff2` fallback. Since most email clients don't load web fonts this is low severity, but the download penalty hits the small number of clients (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS) that do.
