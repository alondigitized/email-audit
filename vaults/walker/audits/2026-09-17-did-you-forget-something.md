# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- There she is. Again. The pink floral D'Lites sneaker, big and centered right under the "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner, same as every other cart-reminder Skechers has fired at me this week. I don't have a cart with a women's floral sneaker in it — I've never so much as clicked on one — so this whole email opens on the wrong foot before I even get to the good stuff.
- Buried below, though, is content I'd actually look at: a "best sellers" grid with men's slip-ins, an Arch Fit Arcade shoe, and a "Relaxed Fit: Altman" slip-on that's exactly my lane — hands-free, cushioned, the whole reason I still open Skechers emails at all. And the 20% off for Skechers Plus members is a real, legible offer with a code and a date. But it's stapled onto the bottom of a cart-reminder email, not built around it.
- I'd scroll past the top, maybe tap on the Murdock slip-in, and forget the rest by lunch. Not forwarding this one — nothing here is worth showing anybody, and the repeated "your cart" gimmick is starting to feel like the brand doesn't know who I am.

## What's working

- The men's slip-in "Relaxed Fit: Altman" and the Arch Fit Arcade both hit my comfort-first, slip-on checklist without me having to dig.
- The 20% off Skechers Plus banner is clean — clear discount, clear code, clear dates, no fine-print maze up front.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" and "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" buttons are big, high-contrast, easy to tap on a phone.
- Store finder, curbside pickup, and "shop now pay later" icons at the bottom are legible without squinting.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe — pink floral D'Lites — has nothing to do with a cart I never filled, and I've now seen this exact pair chase me around all week.
- The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" urgency banner up top is fake urgency for me specifically; it just makes the brand look like it's guessing.
- Best-sellers grid mixes boys' and girls' shoes in with men's — fine for a general catalog, but it's noise between me and the two shoes I'd actually tap.
- The real offer, the 20% off, is scrolled way down past a wall of unrelated product tiles — most guys my age won't get that far.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending me cart-reminder emails for a shoe I never touched — if there's no real abandoned cart tied to my account, don't fake one. Lead with the 20% off instead, since that's the one thing here that's actually mine to act on.
- 2. Move the men's comfort picks (Arch Fit, the Altman slip-in) up top, right where the fake cart banner is now.
- 3. Drop the kids' shoes from a "best sellers" block aimed at someone browsing men's comfort — segment that grid by what I've clicked before.
- 4. Put the 20% off code right under the hero, not six scrolls down.
