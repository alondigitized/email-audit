# Happening now: $10 OFF!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Same muscle memory as always — I saw DSW in the sender column and my thumb was already moving. "$10 OFF" in the subject, "Happening now," fine, let's see it. And then the email opened and I got four trend reports on women's sandals. Jelly shoes. Minimalist slides with ankle straps. Something called "Natural" that looks like strappy cork sandals. Not a single shot of a men's shoe in the whole scroll.
- The tiered deal at the top is actually decent — buy more, save more, up to 60 bucks off if you spend $100. That's real money and I noticed it immediately. But the rest of the email spent all its energy showing me what "trends" to wear this season, and every single model was a woman in a summer sandal. I don't care what's trending. I care if my feet hurt after three hours at the hardware store.
- I would not click. I'd mark it read and move on. Wouldn't forward it to anyone — maybe if my wife was a DSW shopper, but she's not.

## What's working

- **Tiered offer is the clearest thing in the email** — $10/$20/$60 off depending on spend, right at the top, easy to parse on a small screen.
- **No broken images or layout junk** — rendered clean, text is legible, no overlap issues I could see.
- **Visual hierarchy works at a glance** — the "4 TOP TRENDS" header and the offer banner both land before you scroll; the structure isn't confusing.
- **Seasonal relevance** — summer sandals in late June makes timing sense, even if the selection misses me.

## What's weak

- **Every model is a woman, every shoe is a women's style** — Jelly sandals, minimalist slides, strappy sandals. I scrolled the whole email and found zero men's content.
- **"4 Top Trends & How to Wear Them" is a fashion magazine concept, not a comfort-shopper concept** — I don't need a trend report. I need to know if the shoe has cushioning and won't give me blisters.
- **Pride Month section mid-email** — I have nothing against it, but dropping a "Celebrate Pride Month With Us" block in the middle of a trend email feels like two different campaigns stitched together. It doesn't flow.
- **The $10 off is the hook but there's nothing to click to men's shoes** — the offer is real but if I click anything, I'm landing in women's trend pages. Wasted offer for me.
- **DSW has sent me roughly a dozen emails this month** — I've built the reflex to open, but the cadence is wearing thin. This one didn't earn the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me one men's shoe, or don't mail me this one.** A single "Men's Comfort Picks — $10 Off" block above the fold would have given me a reason to click. Segment it or sit it out.
- 2. **Swap "4 Top Trends" as the hero for the deal itself.** Lead with "Buy More, Save More — Up to $60 Off" and let the trends be the secondary content. The offer is the better hook.
- 3. **Cut the trend format entirely for the comfort-shopper segment.** "How to wear it" editorial is for a 32-year-old who follows fashion. Give me "best arch support under $80" and I'm in.
- 4. **The Pride Month block needs its own send or a smoother integration** — not dropped mid-email between Retro and Natural trend sections.
- 5. **Add one product detail** — even just "cushioned footbed" or "wide widths available" next to whatever men's pick you surface.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — invalid CSS property casing in all `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');
```
The CSS property is `src` (lowercase). All nine `@font-face` declarations use `Src:` with a capital `S`. The CSS spec is case-insensitive for property names so major browsers parse it, but it is non-standard and will fail strict validators. Recommend normalizing to lowercase.

**Bug — conflicting `text-size-adjust` in the universal selector:**
```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides the 100% above */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
`-ms-text-size-adjust` is declared twice in the same rule — `100%` then immediately `none`. The second declaration wins, so the effective IE/Edge value is `none`, suppressing automatic text scaling entirely. This will render small text at fixed size on mobile IE/Windows Mail without user zoom recovery.

**Accessibility concern — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality. This blocks that. iOS Safari 10+ ignores it for system accessibility settings, but it remains a compliance flag.

**Font format coverage:**
- Futura_PT: WOFF only — no WOFF2. Older but widely supported; not a breakage issue.
- HelveticaNow: TTF only — larger file size vs WOFF/WOFF2. Slower load on constrained connections.
