## What's working

- The FALL15 banner is big and the 15% is easy to read, even on my phone.
- The two coupon tiers ($10 off $74.98, $25 off $149.98) are a plain bit of price math. I can work out what a pair of sneakers costs after the discount.
- The Men's New Balance 515 is a brand I trust, and the shot is clean.
- The "Add coupon to your mobile wallet" block is handy for in-store shopping, and the barcodes are big.
- The orange section headers are easy to read, and the product names under each photo are a good size.

## What's weak

- Almost nothing in the grid is for me. It's women's flats, girls' sneakers, kids' clogs and leopard print. Only one men's shoe is really mine, and the Nike Court Vision is the other men's pair.
- The FALL15 and BOGO offers repeat from earlier in the week, so it all feels like the same email.
- There are two discount offers stacked at the top (15% off online, and dollars off in store). I had to stop and work out which one applied to me.
- There's no comfort language anywhere. I see no cushioning, arch support, walking or slip-on copy at all.
- The "Shop Now", "Get Coupon" and "Shop Favorites" links are small plain text, which is hard to tap on a phone.
- The fine print is long and tiny, and it lists lots of excluded brands. Brooks, Hoka-style comfort brands and New Balance all appear in that exclusions list.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a men's comfort row: walking shoes, slip-ons and New Balance, with a line like "Cushioned for all-day walking." Put it above the women's and kids' shoes.
- 2. Pick one offer and make it the headline. Don't run two discounts back to back.
- 3. Make the links real buttons with larger tap targets and a verb, like "Shop men's walking shoes."
- 4. Space the sends out. Don't repeat the same banner for a week.
- 5. Show the exclusions near the offer. Seeing New Balance on the excluded list after I was drawn in by the 515 felt like a letdown.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The HTML source is mostly well-formed and responsive, but only the head was available (the source was truncated). One tracking pixel has an unencoded, malformed URL, and another carries what looks like an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token. Link destinations, footer, and headers could not be verified.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 51 links go through the `emltrk.com` click-redirect domain. The QA probe skipped them all ("skipped HTTP probe"), so final destinations, status codes, and UTM passthrough are unverified.
- The open-tracking pixel `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261005_MWK_start_your_week_off_AM - 20261002_115` has two problems:
  - It contains raw spaces around the hyphen (`AM - 20261002_115`). These should be percent-encoded, and some clients or proxies may truncate the URL at the first space.
  - `t=utm_campaign=...` nests a second `=` inside a query value, which is non-standard parameter construction.
- The second pixel, `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]`, has the literal string `[UNIQUE]` in the `d=` parameter. If this is in the delivered HTML and not just a QA artifact, the ESP's per-recipient token was not substituted, so this pixel can't attribute opens.
- QA warns that the plain-text part is >70% URLs (9,437 of 12,536 chars). This comes from long tracking URLs inlined in the text alternative. It is a minor spam-filter and readability risk.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Two images have no `alt` attribute (QA): both `emltrk.com` pixels. Add `alt=""` (and ideally `width="1" height="1"`) so screen readers skip them. This is low severity because they are tracking pixels.
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, so it does nothing.
- `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute still works in most clients.
- The doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional, but `<html>` carries an XHTML `xmlns`. The mismatch is harmless in practice but inconsistent.
- The global rule `a, a:link, ... { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` removes default link styling. Any text link that isn't styled inline will be visually indistinguishable from body text, which is a WCAG 1.4.1 risk.
- `.responsive-text` drops to `font-size: 12px !important` at ≤600px, which is small for mobile body copy.
- Positives: `lang="en"` is set, the viewport meta is present, and the media queries and Outlook/Gmail/Samsung fixes are in place.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder in the pixel URL (see §2) is the only possible unresolved token in the visible source.
- The subject "Happy Monday, Friend!" looks like a generic fallback salutation, so the first-name field may have been empty. This can't be confirmed from the data provided.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
Cannot be assessed. The HTML was truncated before the footer, and no headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC `Authentication-Results`) were supplied. QA reported no authentication failures. No issues can be confirmed.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- The campaign tag `SCA_20261005_MWK_start_your_week_off_AM` matches the send date, 2026-10-05 (Monday), and the "Happy Monday" subject. This is consistent.
- It appears only in the pixel URL, in a malformed form (§2). Whether the 51 redirected links carry `utm_*` parameters to the landing pages is unverified, because the probe skipped them.
- The `20261002_115` suffix suggests the campaign was built on 10/02, which is plausible.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Percent-encode the spaces in the `ieezzy5m` pixel URL and fix the `t=utm_campaign=` parameter format.
2. Check the delivered message for the literal `[UNIQUE]` in the `0di86k1v` pixel. If it's present, fix the ESP merge-tag syntax or substitution.
3. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
4. Replace the meta tags with `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` and `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">`.
5. Restore a visible link treatment (underline or color) on text links, and raise the 12px mobile text size.
6. Shorten the plain-text version's URLs, or reduce the number of links in it, to lower the URL-to-text ratio.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML with headers. Resolve a sample of the `emltrk.com` links to check final destinations and UTMs, and verify the unsubscribe link, physical address, and `List-Unsubscribe` header.
