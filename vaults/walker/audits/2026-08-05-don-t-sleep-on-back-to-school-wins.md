# Don’t sleep on back-to-school wins
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Same neon basketball-court graphic, same "Back to School Sale" banner I saw twice already this summer — Nike's now on their third lap with this creative in under three weeks. I don't have kids in school anymore, my grandkids' parents handle that shopping, so "Day One Glow-Up" and "Varsity Savings" just aren't speaking to me at all.
- Scrolling through, it's the usual Nike lineup — a couple of sneakers, a windbreaker, fleece pants, then kids' and women's sections tacked on. Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or anything slip-on. These are runway-lean, college-age models in every shot, which tells me this brand still doesn't have a clue who I am even after all the emails I've opened from them.
- I'm not clicking this one. It's not for me, and honestly the repeat creative is starting to feel like Nike's just spamming their whole list with the same back-to-school push regardless of who's on the other end. Straight to archived, not forwarding it to anybody.

## What's working

- The "Members: Free Shipping on Orders $50+" line up top is at least something concrete for anyone who is a member.
- The 25% off with code DAYONE is a real, specific offer, not vague "save big" fluff.
- Layout's clean — no broken images, nothing overlapping, easy enough to scroll through on my phone.
- The in-store "25% Off Shoes" section at the bottom is at least footwear-focused, which is the one part of this email in my wheelhouse.

## What's weak

- Third back-to-school email with the same black-and-neon court graphic — I flagged this exact fatigue on the July 22nd send, and here it is again.
- Not one mention of comfort, arch support, or slip-on ease anywhere — the stuff that actually gets me to buy shoes.
- Every model is 20-something and athletic-built. Nobody in this email looks like they're shopping for themselves at my age.
- The "Shop" button is generic — doesn't tell me what I'm shopping for, and the actual dollar-or-percent offer isn't visible until I scroll past that big hero graphic.
- No sizing, fit, or comfort details on any of the featured shoes — just a name and a photo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the same neon-court hero for a third send in the same window — rotate the creative or retire the campaign, it reads as repetitive rather than urgent.
- 2. Segment this list — if I've never clicked a back-to-school email and I'm not buying kids' gear, put me in a comfort-footwear track instead.
- 3. Put an actual comfort callout (cushioned, arch support, all-day wear) on at least one product tile if you want guys my age to stop and look.
- 4. Make the CTA specific — "Shop Men's Shoes" beats a plain "Shop" button.
- 5. Move the 25% off number into the hero itself so it's visible before any scrolling.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off shoes — in-store only through 8/16`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your back-to-school shoe upgrade starts here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned comfort, 25% off — no code needed in store.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members save extra this week on select styles.`
