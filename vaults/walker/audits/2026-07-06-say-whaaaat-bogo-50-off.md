# Say whaaaat: BOGO 50% OFF?!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- After watching that 25%-off offer get "FINAL HOURS'd" into the ground — I counted at least six or seven of those through the Fourth before losing track — I'll give DSW this much: BOGO 50% off is a different offer entirely. It's not the same promo wearing a new hat. It's a reason to actually run the math in my head: what two pairs do I need?
- Inside, the email does a lot right. The offer is front and center in big clean type, the size grid is right there without digging, and — this is the part that actually made me slow down — "SHOP WIDE WIDTH" is sitting right below the products. I've been buying wide widths for twenty years. When a brand acknowledges that, I don't look away.
- That said, the product grid running down the page reads mostly women's to me. Flats, mules, a delicate flat sandal. I didn't see a single shoe I'd put on my foot in that "SO NEW, SO YOU" section. And "Say whaaaat" in the subject line — look, I know who I am. I'm 62. Nobody talks to me like that. I'll still click, but I'd feel better about it if they'd aimed this thing a little more my direction. Bottom line: I'd click through to the wide-width section specifically. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF is legible from across the room.** The hero hierarchy is clean and the offer lands before you read a single word of copy.
- **"SHOP WIDE WIDTH" is the most personally relevant button in the whole email.** That one button tells me DSW actually carries my sizes and cares that I can find them.
- **The size grid (5–11 with half-sizes) shows their range at a glance.** I don't have to click blind and then discover they're out of 10.5W.
- **Fresh deal type after a full week of 25% off.** The pivot to BOGO gave me a genuine reason to look instead of auto-archive.
- **VIP rewards section is a quiet but meaningful nudge.** I'm a member; seeing the program reinforced below the offer confirms there's reason to stay in it.

## What's weak

- **"Say whaaaat" is not how anyone over 50 talks.** Subject line reads like it was written for a 28-year-old, not the guy with a DSW Rewards card and a wide-width habit.
- **Product grid is women's-first.** Six shoes shown and I couldn't pick out one I'd wear. For a sale pitched to everyone, that's a miss.
- **No deadline visible anywhere.** BOGO without an end date doesn't push me to act today; I'll mean to come back and probably won't.
- **Cadence hangover is real.** Even with a new offer, my thumb has developed a DSW-skip reflex after the last two weeks. This one broke through — but just barely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a men's comfort shoe in the hero product row.** One cushioned walking shoe or slip-on above the fold makes this feel like it was meant for me — not forwarded from the women's team.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" gives me a concrete reason to act today instead of later-that-never-comes.
- 3. **Move SHOP WIDE WIDTH up to the hero secondary CTA.** It's your single best signal to older male shoppers and it's buried halfway down.
- 4. **Drop "Say whaaaat" from the subject.** The BOGO offer is strong enough on its own. Youth-speak undercuts trust with my demographic.
- 5. **One line of comfort copy in the hero.** Something like "Cushioned. Supportive. Half price on the second pair." That's what gets me from curious to clicking.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on two meta tags** — both will be silently ignored by Outlook and some legacy clients:

```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

**Capitalized `Src:` in all `@font-face` blocks** — CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but several strict parser implementations (some Outlook WebView builds) treat this as a parse error and skip the rule:
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/...") format('woff');  /* all 10 @font-face blocks */
```
Should be `src:` (lowercase).

**Font format gaps:**
- Futura PT (4 weights): served only as `.woff` — no `.woff2` (30–40% smaller, preferred by all modern clients) and no `.ttf` fallback for older Android.
- HelveticaNow (6 variants): served only as `.ttf` — no `.woff` or `.woff2`. TTF is heavier and uncompressed; clients that support web fonts will load ~2× more bytes than necessary.

**No font preloading** — custom fonts are not preloaded via `<link rel="preload">`. Clients that support web fonts (Apple Mail, some Gmail) will render system fallback fonts until fonts load (FOUT).

**Near-black color values** (`#000001`, `#00000F`) appear in both dark-mode and mobile CSS classes. These are functionally indistinguishable from `#000000` visually but will fail exact-match color comparisons in QA tooling and accessibility contrast checkers that expect `#000000`. No rendering impact, but flag for consistency.
