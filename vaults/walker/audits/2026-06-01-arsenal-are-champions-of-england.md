# Arsenal are champions of England
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one and immediately felt like adidas had the wrong address book open. Arsenal are champions — okay, good for them, I genuinely don't follow English football and I'm not sure why adidas thinks I do. The big red hero image is dramatic, I'll give them that. But I'm a 62-year-old guy in Columbus who wants comfortable walking shoes, not an Arsenal jersey.
- The second half of the email — the adiClub x Prime giveaway — is at least marginally interesting because it's aimed at members and there's real money attached ($2,500 in prizes). But it's a sweepstakes, not a sale. I have to enter and hope. That's not how I shop for shoes.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to anyone I know. I'd mark it read and move on, mildly annoyed that this is the second soccer email from adidas in two days — yesterday it was "making soccer history," today it's Arsenal winning England. One of those is already one too many.

## What's working

- The Arsenal hero image is bold and clean — no render bugs, text isn't overlapping, the email loads crisply even on a smaller screen.
- The adiClub giveaway section is at least loyalty-member aware — it acknowledges I'm a member, which is more than some recent adidas sends have bothered to do.
- The subject line is factual and spam-free — no exclamation points, no ALL CAPS, just a straightforward statement. Easy to read in the inbox.
- Visual hierarchy is decent: big red hero → "Champions" headline → giveaway block. My eye moves through it without getting confused.

## What's weak

- This entire email is built for an English soccer fan, which I am not. Arsenal jersey, Premier League trophy — nothing here connects to comfort shoes, walking gear, or anything I've ever signaled interest in.
- The giveaway is a sweepstakes, not a discount. I can't act on it the way I act on a sale. Enter and wait isn't shopping, it's a lottery.
- Yesterday's email was also soccer ("making soccer history"). Two soccer emails in two days from a brand I subscribe to for footwear deals is past the point of annoyance.
- "LEARN MORE" is the weakest possible CTA copy — I'd want to know what I'm learning more about before I tap anything.
- No shoe content whatsoever. Not one pair. Not a comfort line mention. Not even a "shop the collection" sidebar. Just jerseys and a prize box.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of soccer sends entirely.** If my purchase history and browsing say "walking shoes, arch support, men's casual," I should never see an Arsenal jersey email. The technology exists. Use it.
- 2. **Turn the giveaway into a hook for a real offer.** "Enter the giveaway AND save 20% on adidas walking shoes this week" gives me two reasons to click instead of zero.
- 3. **Swap the CTA copy.** "Enter to Win" is clearer and more honest than "LEARN MORE" for a sweepstakes call to action.
- 4. **Rest the soccer theme for at least a week.** Yesterday was soccer, today is soccer — it's fatiguing even for a fan, let alone someone who isn't.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed failures: a broken click-redirect URL and a missing plain-text fallback. Four HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and two missing alt attributes are additional rendering risks. All other checks pass.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — unknown URL type**

The click-tracking layer at `dv.adidas.com` is wrapping a destination with an unrecognized scheme:

```
unknown url type: '/g/a4528edf-031e-4040-975d-3e627471fbb6?txn=51730f00-5d5e-11f1-a324-26d090a92706'
```

Outer tracking URL: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/a4528edf-031e-4040-975d-3e627471fbb6?cp_tp=v3...`

The decoded destination begins with `/g/` (a relative path), not an absolute URL. The redirect server cannot resolve a relative path to a hostname — this link will 404 or error for any recipient who clicks it. The affected asset is the open-graph/tracking pixel at UUID `a4528edf-031e-4040-975d-3e627471fbb6`.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped**

All click-redirect domains were skipped during HTTP probing, so additional broken destinations may exist but are unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] 4× HTTP image sources — same file repeated**

`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt="arrow") is referenced four times over plain HTTP:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. All four instances of this arrow graphic will render as broken image boxes. Fix: change scheme to `https://`.
