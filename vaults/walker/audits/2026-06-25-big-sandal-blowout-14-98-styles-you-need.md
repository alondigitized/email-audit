# Big Sandal Blowout: $14.98 Styles You Need
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival shows up again — this time with a "Big Sandal Blowout" and the $14.98 price point I've seen from them before. The June 11 send hit that same number on Women's & Kids' sandals, so pulling it out again two weeks later doesn't exactly feel fresh. What I did notice right away: the Inflation Relief banner is gone. After weeks of that red-and-yellow block greeting me every single time I opened one of these, it's been swapped out for a cleaner tiered coupon setup at the top — $10 off $74.99+ or $15 off $99.99+. That's a real improvement in my book. Cleaner, more actionable.
- But here's my problem: this email is almost entirely Women's Sandals and Kids' Sandals. I'm a 62-year-old guy hunting for cushioned, arch-supported walking shoes. The Skechers "Comfort Go-Tos" block in the middle gave me a flicker of interest — that's actually my wheelhouse — but it's sandwiched between a women's blowout hero and a kids' section I have zero use for. I felt like a wrong-address delivery.
- The preheader is a disaster. What showed up in my inbox next to the subject was a raw tracking URL — not a word of copy. That alone killed my desire to even tap it. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **Tiered coupon block is clean and readable** — $10 off or $15 off with a code spelled out in plain text; I can actually use that without squinting.
- **Skechers Comfort Go-Tos section** — that's a brand and a product family I pay attention to; glad it was in here even if it wasn't the hero.
- **$14.98 price point is concrete and eye-catching** — easy to grasp on a phone screen without doing math.
- **Layout is clean, no broken images or overlapping text** — renders just fine, which is more than I can say for some emails I get.
- **Inflation Relief banner is finally gone** — the cadence had beaten that creative to death; the swap to coupon tiers feels like a reset.

## What's weak

- **The whole email is aimed at women and kids** — hero image, model, "Women's Sandal Blowout" headline — I felt invisible the moment I scrolled past the coupon bar.
- **The preheader is a broken tracking URL** — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." showed up verbatim in my inbox. That's a technical screwup that costs opens before I even see the email.
- **$14.98 sandals again** — I saw this offer in the June 11 email. Running the same number fourteen days later without anything new wrapped around it makes me feel like they forgot they already sent this.
- **No men's comfort story** — not a single word about arch support, cushioning, or wide widths anywhere in what I could see. Skechers was teased but the copy didn't tell me anything useful about fit or comfort.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic** — two words that could live on any button at any retailer; tells me nothing about where I'm going or why I should bother.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — a raw tracking URL in the inbox is an embarrassment and is the single highest-leverage fix. Write actual copy that teases the offer instead.
- 2. **Add a men's comfort lane** — even one module — "Men's Skechers Comfort Go-Tos from $XX.XX" with a direct Shop Men's button would give me a reason to click instead of scroll past.
- 3. **Retire the $14.98 hero or reframe it** — if you're going to run the same price point again, wrap it in a new angle: "Last chance," "New arrivals at $14.98," or at least different creative.
- 4. **Make the Skechers section do more work** — show a specific SKU, call out cushioning or arch support, give me a reason to care beyond just the logo.
- 5. **Swap "Shop Now" for something specific** — "Shop Men's Comfort," "Find My Size," "See Skechers Styles" — anything with a destination baked in.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed meta tags (two confirmed issues):**

- `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` — missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `content` value also truncates at the semicolon, omitting the charset from the MIME declaration. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`.
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` — missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Without the `http-equiv` attribute this tag is inert and does nothing for IE/legacy Edge rendering.

**Dark mode declared as unsupported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Apple Mail 13+ and iOS 13+ will force a dark-mode inversion on `light`-only emails rather than applying designed overrides. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Risk: text/background color inversions on images and white-background sections.

**CSS specificity conflict — Apple link color override vs. global reset:**
The global reset declares:
```css
a, a:link, a:visited, ... { color: inherit !important; }
```
This `!important` will override `.appleLinksWhite a { color: #ffffff; }` and `.appleLinksGray/Black` rules in Apple Mail, since the global selector is broader and declared later. The apple autolink fix blocks (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` and `#MessageViewBody a`) are similarly at risk of being neutered. These class-based overrides were likely intended to work but are structurally defeated.

**Duplicate property — harmless but sloppy:**
```css
color: inherit !important;
color: inherit;   /* redundant */
```

**`min-width: 100vw` on `.body` (450px breakpoint):**
```css
.body { min-width: 100vw; }
```
Can cause horizontal scroll bleed on narrow viewports in some Android webview clients where `100vw` includes scrollbar width.

**`pointer-events: none` on tel/sms links:**
```css
a[href^="tel"], a[href^="sms"] { pointer-events: none; cursor: default !important; }
```
Intentional suppression of tap-to-call, but this is an accessibility regression on mobile — users with motor impairments relying on assistive touch cannot activate these links at all.

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify, source truncated before `<img>` tags.
