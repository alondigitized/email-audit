# Are you stocked on Birkenstock?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Birkenstock. Okay, now we're talking. I've been wearing the same pair of Arizona sandals around the house for six summers — cork footbed, contoured arch, no laces to deal with. So when I saw this subject in my inbox, it actually caught my attention in a way the last dozen Famous Footwear emails didn't. The subject is a little weird — "are you stocked on" sounds like something a store manager says to a warehouse guy, not how a brand talks to a customer — but I knew what it meant.
- Then I tapped it open and the first thing I noticed was the preheader. Again. A raw tracking link. Same exact problem they had on that July 12th flash sale email. These folks are running the same technical mistake week after week and nobody's caught it. At this point I'm not sure if I should be annoyed or just feel bad for whoever manages their platform.
- The email itself is fine — Birkenstock sandals shown clearly, a "limited-time price drops" message, a clean shop button. I would probably scroll through it because the product is genuinely relevant to me. But I wouldn't forward it to anyone, and I'm not rushing to click — there's no "this deal ends at midnight" or "your size is still in stock" to push me off the couch.

## What's working

- **Birkenstock is a legitimate comfort brand for my feet.** Cork footbed, molded arch, wide toe box — this is exactly the kind of shoe I cross-shop. Subject actually pulled me in.
- **"SHOP BIRKENSTOCK" and "BIRKENSTOCK ON SALE" are clean, simple buttons.** No mystery what happens when I tap them. I appreciate that.
- **Sandals in July is timely.** Not a stale promo — this is peak season for me to be thinking about what I'm wearing to the farmers market or on a slow morning walk.
- **No layout breakage I could see.** The images loaded, the Birkenstock styles came through clearly, and the product grid at the bottom was readable even at small size.
- **"Limited-time price drops" is an honest framing.** Not screaming ENDS TONIGHT, just telling me prices have come down. I can work with that.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is a raw tracking URL. Again.** Famous Footwear sent me this same broken preheader on July 12th. I said something about it then, and here we are three days later. It makes the brand look sloppy before I've even opened.
- **The models are young — early-to-mid thirties, athletic, trendy.** Birkenstock has a huge customer base in older adults. Show me someone who looks like he's been on his feet for thirty years and just wants to walk comfortably.
- **The 25% off clearance banner buried in the middle feels tacked on.** We've had some version of clearance-ends-tonight in nearly every email this week. Feels like they couldn't help themselves — the Birkenstock email is doing something different and then they stuff the same old clearance pitch in there.
- **Jordans and back-to-school below the fold.** I have no interest in Jordan sneakers. When I get to that section my thumb is already heading for the exit.
- **No specific prices shown for the Birkenstock sandals.** "Price drops" is vague. Tell me the Arizona is now $79 and I'm paying attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** This has now been broken on at least two separate sends. It should read something like "Cork footbed sandals built for comfort — now on sale." That single fix would meaningfully lift open rates.
- 2. **Show a price.** Pick one hero Birkenstock style, put the sale price on it in the email body. "$89 → $69" in the hero stops more thumbs than "limited-time price drops" does.
- 3. **Cut the Jordan / back-to-school section entirely from this send.** When the email subject is Birkenstock, the content should stay in that lane. Stuffing in athletic sneakers halfway through is a distraction and it dilutes the message.
- 4. **Model swap.** Pull in a model who's 55–65, male, wearing the Arizona with shorts. Birkenstock has a strong older-male customer base — use it.
- 5. **Drop the buried clearance banner.** I've seen it every day this week. It's starting to feel like they paste it into everything regardless of whether it fits.
