# Meet the new Freehiker
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a hiking shoe email, which at least is a real category for me — better than soccer cleats or a K-pop star, I'll give them that. But it's still not it. The lifestyle shot is a young woman leaping around on a rope swing over a river while a couple of younger guys watch — that's not my hiking, that's not even close to my hiking. And there's not a single price anywhere on this thing.
- The shoe itself, the Terrex Freehiker, actually looks like something I'd wear — chunky, cushioned sole, looks like it'd have decent arch support. But they never say a word about comfort, cushioning, or fit, which is the whole reason I'd click on a hiking shoe in the first place.
- I'm not clicking this one. No price, no comfort language, and a lifestyle photo that skews 25 years younger than me. Not forwarding it either — this one goes in the "read and move on" pile.

## What's working

- The hero product shot of the shoe itself is clean, well-lit, and I can actually see the tread and cushioned sole — that matters to me.
- "From city streets to countryside paths and everything in between" as the preheader is at least a real, relevant line, not junk.
- It's a real outdoor/hiking product, which is a lot closer to my world than backpacks or soccer jerseys.
- Top black banner has a clear "SHOP NOW" button that's easy to spot.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere in this email — I have no idea if this shoe is $80 or $220, and that's a dealbreaker for whether I even consider it.
- The lifestyle photo is a young woman doing a rope swing stunt over a river with two younger guys watching — nothing about that scene is me or my kind of hike.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support — the stuff that actually sells a hiking shoe to a 62-year-old with foot issues.
- "WHAT THE HIKE?" as a headline is trying too hard to be clever; I'd rather just know what the shoe does for my feet.
- Two separate CTAs (top banner "Shop Now" and no bottom button after the story) feels unfinished — there's no clear button near the actual product I'd want to buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or starting price right under the hero shot — that's the single biggest thing missing and the first thing I look for.
- 2. Add one line about cushioning/support — "Boost midsole cushioning for all-day comfort" or similar — since that's what actually gets me to click on a hiking shoe.
- 3. Swap the rope-swing lifestyle photo for someone actually hiking a trail at a normal pace — closer to my age group if possible.
- 4. Add a "Shop the Freehiker" button directly under the product image, not just the generic banner at top.
