# Tick tock ⏰ Up to an extra $20 off ends tonight!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- My inbox preview was a dumpster fire — literally a long tracking URL where the preview text should've been. That's the first thing I saw before I even tapped to open. Already made me wonder if something was broken. Once I got inside, the email itself was cleaner: a big tiered "Spend & Save" deal right up top, clear dollar amounts, and a SHOP NOW button. I'll give Famous Footwear credit for that part — I understood the offer in about three seconds.
- But here's my problem: I scrolled through this thing and what I'm seeing is sandals, sandals, and more sandals — and not the kind I'm shopping for. The whole vibe skews women's. I spotted Crocs near the bottom, which perked my ears up, and Adidas too. But the hero product? Strappy sandals. That's not where my head is when I'm thinking about my next pair of shoes.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. The deal math is fine — $20 off $100 is worth keeping in mind — but nothing in here made me want to find my size and click through. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **Tiered deal is crystal clear.** $10 off $50 / $15 off $75 / $20 off $100 — I can do that math in my head without squinting, and that's a real offer, not vague coupon language.
- **Famous Footwear is a brand I recognize and shop.** I've walked into their stores for years. The logo and layout feel familiar — no trust issues with the sender.
- **Crocs got a feature spot near the bottom.** Those are on my short list for slip-on comfort, so that placement kept me from closing immediately.
- **Email body renders cleanly.** No broken images, no scrambled text — what I saw was legible.
- **Seasonal relevance.** It's May, people are thinking warm weather shoes. The inventory makes sense for the time of year.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL.** That's the very first thing someone sees after the subject line in their inbox — and this one showed me a wall of gibberish code. Makes the whole thing look like a scam or a broken send.
- **Wrong demographic in the hero.** The dominant product visuals were women's sandals. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for comfort walking shoes. I had to hunt to find anything for me.
- **Zero comfort messaging.** No cushioning, no arch support, no "easy on/off" — the copy that would actually speak to me was nowhere in the email.
- **"SHOP NOW" tells me nothing.** Shop what? Where? At least say "Shop Men's" or "Shop Cushioned Sneakers." That button could go to anything.
- **No loyalty acknowledgement.** I'm a Famous Footwear customer and there's no "Hey Walker" or any sign they know who I am. Treated me like a stranger on a cold list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately — this is ship-stopping.** A raw tracking URL in the inbox preview is the email equivalent of leaving the price tag on at a dinner party. Before anything else, get real copy in that slot.
- 2. **Segment men's comfort shoppers and lead with their product.** If I'm in the "comfort/men's" bucket, show me cushioned sneakers or slip-ons up top — not women's sandals. Even a tab or "Shop Men's" banner in the hero would help.
- 3. **Add one comfort-specific hook in the hero copy.** Something like "Extra cushion. Extra savings." ties the deal to the reason I'm actually buying. Right now SPEND & SAVE is pure math with no emotion.
- 4. **Make the CTA specific.** "Shop Men's Comfort Shoes →" beats "SHOP NOW" every time for someone like me. I want to know I'm going somewhere relevant before I tap.
- 5. **Add my name or a loyalty tier acknowledgment.** "Gold Member, tonight's the night" — something that tells me Famous Footwear sees me as a customer, not just an email address.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Tick tock" Promo Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-managed HTML blast with no catastrophic failures, but carries a systemic alt-text gap across 35+ images and a plain-text body that is nearly 83% tracking URLs — both of which elevate spam-filter risk and degrade screen-reader usability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text spam signal (HIGH)**
- QA: plain-text version is 15,033 of 18,037 chars of URLs (83.3%) — well above the 70% threshold that triggers spam classifier penalties at major ISPs (Gmail, Outlook).
- Root cause: ESP is auto-generating the plain-text part by dumping click-redirect URLs rather than stripping them to human-readable anchor text.

**31 click-redirect links unverifiable**
- All outbound links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (e.g., `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7...`). QA correctly skipped HTTP probing of these.
- No broken links detected in what was probed; redirect chain integrity is unconfirmed.

**Open pixel**
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?4BJ3HRCW5CRUPAM5N2S7637OTU.70253&d=70253&bmt=0
```
- Correctly implemented as `width="1" height="1" alt=""`. No issues.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text — 35 images missing descriptors (HIGH)**
- Every content image (`3b6a7dc1`, `fd389a3a` GIF, `32250b7b`, etc.) has `alt=""`. The logo (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) is the brand anchor and is linked — it needs at minimum `alt="Famous Footwear"`.
- The animated GIF (`fd389a3a-79d2-493b-a755-4e34daee5d0d.gif`) used as a visual headline has no alt — screen reader users get zero offer context.
- Decorative dividers (`4ccff4ad` — appears 4× in QA output) correctly use `alt=""` and are fine; those warnings are noise from the checker.

**`<meta charset>` malformed**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration is inert in some legacy clients (Outlook 2010/2013).

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0
```
Disabling zoom is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). Many ESPs use this pattern regardless — flag for awareness.

**`max-height:auto` — invalid CSS**
```css
img { max-height:auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values are `none`, length, or percentage). Browsers ignore it silently; remove to avoid lint failures and confusion.

**`lineapacing` typo**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
Typo for `line-height` or `letter-spacing` — the property is unrecognized and silently ignored.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All destination URLs are opaque behind `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...` encoded parameters. UTM parameter presence/absence on the final destination URLs cannot be verified without resolving the redirect chain. This is the single biggest attribution blind spot — if the ESP's redirect doesn't append UTMs before hitting famousfootwear.com, campaign analytics will be underreported.

**Recommended verification:** resolve one representative redirect (e.g., the header logo link) and confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are present on the landing URL.
