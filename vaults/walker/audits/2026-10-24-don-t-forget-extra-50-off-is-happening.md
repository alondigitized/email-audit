# Don't forget: Extra 50% Off is Happening!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- Crocs again. This is the same "Extra 50% Off" I got yesterday in "Wait...Extra 50% Off?!", with a "Don't forget" in front of it. I read the whole thing in two seconds. Big black "Extra 50% Off," one black button, and a giant SALE graphic with a navy clog. That part is clear. But it's the same offer a day later, so it feels like nagging.
- Nothing here tells me it's for a 62-year-old guy who wants cushioning and arch support. It's one generic Baya clog, "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply." That's a lot of hedging for a headline that big. I'd also like to know which shoes are half off. Is it the clog I'd actually wear, or just the odd colors? The deadline is only in the tiny print at the bottom: ends Sunday, October 25th at 11:59pm MT.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already shopping for a slip-on, since 50% off is real money. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably leave it for later, and since the same deal arrived yesterday, "later" is fine.

## What's working

- The "Extra 50% Off" headline is huge and I can read it on my phone without zooming.
- There's one button, "Shop the Sale," and it's big, black and easy to tap.
- The navy clog on the SALE graphic is a plain, sensible color. It's not a novelty or licensed style.
- Free shipping is stated in the top banner, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The email looks like the Crocs I know, so I trust it.

## What's weak

- I got the same offer yesterday, so "Don't forget" feels like pestering. That's three or four sends this week, and the offer keeps changing.
- "Select Styles" and "Exclusions Apply" tell me nothing. I can't tell whether the shoes I'd buy are included.
- The deadline is buried in the fine print. The body has no "ends Sunday" to make me act.
- There's no mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support or walking, which is what I shop for. It's one clog and a big SALE sign.
- "Refer a Friend: Give 25%, Get 25% Off!" is a competing second offer that muddies the 50%. Nothing here recognizes me as a returning customer.
- The font in the footer and fine print is small for my eyes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends Sunday night." Then show 3-4 specific shoes with their sale prices, including a men's slip-on or a cushioned style. That would help the most.
- 2. Say which styles are included and give a few examples, instead of "Select Styles."
- 3. Add a line about comfort, like "all-day cushioned comfort" or a review quote, so a man like me has a reason to care.
- 4. Don't send the same offer two days in a row. Fold the reminder into one send, or change the creative.
- 5. Move the referral offer below the fold or drop it for this send.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is structurally sound: it uses a responsive media-query setup, a hidden preheader, an `lang="en"` attribute, and a single-pixel open-tracking image. The QA run found 0 errors and 1 warning (86% pass rate). Technical issues are limited to insecure `http://` image sources, one missing alt attribute, and header and footer data that couldn't be verified from the truncated source.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 20 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they use click-redirect domains. The probe didn't test them, so their destinations and redirect chains are unverified.
- The open pixel is `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?qs=…&d=10199&bmt=0`. It is HTTPS, has `alt=""`, and sits in a 1px hidden div. This is fine.
- A second tracking image points to `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f…`. It has no `alt` attribute, which the QA check flagged. Add `alt=""` and explicit `width="1" height="1"` so it renders as a decorative pixel.
- It also passes a `d_dpuid` identifier to a third-party Adobe Audience Manager (demdex) domain. That is a data-sharing point to cover in the privacy disclosure. It isn't a code defect.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Mixed content:** 7 images load over `http://` from `image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`. They are the App Store badge, Google Play badge, Find Your Store, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube icons. Clients and proxies may block or rewrite non-HTTPS images, and Gmail proxies them. The result can be missing icons or "not secure" warnings. Serve them over `https://`, since the same host already serves HTTPS tracking.
- **Alt text:** The `http://` images have alt text (e.g. "Download on the App Store", "Instagram"). The only missing alt is the demdex pixel above.
- **Meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so they do nothing. The charset and X-UA-Compatible declarations are ineffective. Change them to `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" …>` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" …>`. The UTF-8 charset is normally still correct from the MIME header.
- **Web fonts:** The `TT Crocs` `@font-face` rules load from `media.crocs.com` over HTTPS with `font-display: swap`. Most clients ignore web fonts, so the font stack needs a web-safe fallback. The truncated source doesn't show whether one exists.
- **Preheader:** The preheader is hidden with `display:none` and padded with zero-width characters. This is standard.
- **Truncation:** The HTML source was truncated, so the remaining body markup wasn't audited. That includes table structure, `role="presentation"`, VML or MSO fallbacks, and any bulletproof buttons.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible source has no unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, or `[First Name]`. The tracking IDs are rendered values. The truncated portion couldn't be checked.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **Authentication:** The `Authentication-Results` header wasn't found, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The audit harness attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it's a limit of the test setup and not evidence of a sender misconfiguration. The result is inconclusive, not a failure.
- **Unsubscribe link and `List-Unsubscribe` header:** Neither the unsubscribe link, the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, nor the physical postal address appears in the truncated source or the QA output. I can't confirm compliance either way. Check the footer and raw headers.
- **Sender domain:** The From domain `crocs-email.com` matches the tracking (`click.crocs-email.com`) and image (`image.crocs-email.com`) hosts. That is good for alignment and for link-branding trust.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Not assessable. The 20 tracking links were skipped, and the truncated source shows no destination URLs. The links appear to use opaque `click.crocs-email.com` redirects, so UTM parameters are applied or stripped server-side. Resolve a sample of the redirects to confirm that the final URLs carry UTM parameters and that the landing page matches the "Extra 50% Off" offer.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Change the 7 `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` image URLs to `https://`. This is the highest-priority fix.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel `<img>`, along with explicit dimensions.
3. Add `http-equiv` to the charset and X-UA-Compatible `<meta>` tags.
4. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with raw headers, so the footer, unsubscribe, and `List-Unsubscribe` can be checked. Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC at the sending MTA, not through the relay.
5. Resolve the 20 redirect links, either with a follow-redirects probe or a manual sample, to validate their destinations and UTM parameters.
