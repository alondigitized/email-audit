# ICYMI 📣 Big styles this month
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-30**
## Executive summary

- **Persona:** Walker Miles, 62 | Reviewed: 2026-04-29
- Okay, I'll be straight with you: this one barely held my attention past the first scroll. Famous Footwear sent me a "don't-miss-it" recap of April, and honestly, I nearly missed the whole thing — because almost none of it was aimed at a guy like me. Hello Kitty Crocs. Rhinestone shoes. A Minecraft tie-in. And then the kicker — halfway down the email it says "BRING SUMMER BACK, **JANE**." My name is not Jane. That right there almost made me hit unsubscribe. When a brand can't even get my name right, I stop trusting they know anything about what I actually want to buy. The Jordan sneakers and the FlyX section were the only two things that even briefly caught my eye, and neither had enough detail to make me reach for my credit card.
- Wrong audience, wrong name, wrong vibe. This email would alienate most men over 50 who actually have a purchase history with this brand.

## What's working

- **The Jordans section** — I'm not a sneakerhead, but I know the Jordan brand and those white shoes looked clean and sharp. It's the one moment I thought "hm, maybe worth a look."
- **"The latest and greatest" framing** — that headline is simple and clear. Doesn't talk down to me, doesn't use jargon I have to Google.
- **Visual layout is mostly clean** — the sections stack reasonably well. It doesn't feel cluttered the way some emails do.

## What's weak

- **Wrong name — "BRING SUMMER BACK, JANE."** This is the single worst thing in the email. I'm a 62-year-old man named Walker. Seeing "JANE" in big bold letters mid-email is jarring. It makes me feel like I got someone else's mail. Kills any sense that this brand knows me at all.
- **Female-heavy content.** Rhinestone shoes. Denim sections. Hello Kitty and Friends Crocs. Even the lifestyle photo in the "Make Summer Iconic" section looks like it's aimed at a young woman. I have zero interest in any of this. I don't even have grandkids looking for Hello Kitty shoes.
- **Minecraft x Hey Dude.** I had to squint to read this section and when I figured out what it was, I scrolled right past it. Kids' stuff. Not for me.
- **No men's comfort content visible.** No slip-ons, no wide-width options, no cushioning callouts — nothing that speaks to why I actually shop for shoes. I've got knee issues. Comfort sells me. This email never tried.
- **Preview text is a raw URL.** That tracking link in the preview slot is embarrassing. Before I even opened it, my inbox showed me a wall of gibberish. I open a lot of Skechers emails — they don't do that.
- **FlyX section is too thin.** The headline "The deal wears FlyX" is vague. What's FlyX? What's the deal — is there a price off? Without more info, I skip it.
- **No price or discount shown.** I'm price-conscious. If there's a sale, tell me up front. "Big styles this month" doesn't tell me anything about value.

## Recommendations

- 2. **Segment by gender before sending.** Men's customers should not see Hello Kitty or rhinestone content in the hero rotation. Show me men's casual, comfort, or athletic — that's why I'm on the list.
- 3. **Lead with comfort or value for older male customers.** A headline like "Easy on your feet, easy on your wallet" or "Built for all-day wear" would get my attention far faster than "Make summer iconic."
- 4. **Surface a dollar-off or percent-off.** Don't make me hunt for whether there's actually a reason to click today.
- 5. **Fix the preview text.** Lose the tracking URL. Use that slot to give me one reason to open — even just "New men's styles + a deal worth a look."
- 6. **Reduce the number of sections.** Five or six distinct product stories is too many. I lose the thread. Pick two or three and make each one count.

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "ICYMI Big styles this month"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a systemic alt-text gap across all product images, a malformed charset meta tag, and a plain-text body that is 85% URLs — all of which compound into real deliverability and accessibility exposure. The truncated source prevents full CAN-SPAM footer verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **31 click-redirect links** routed through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` — all were skipped by the probe; destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from available data.
- **Open-pixel URL** present: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?5T64VOCRUHBUZDL56Y3C3JGOI4.70253` — standard tracking, no issue, noting for completeness.
- **Plain-text body is 85% URLs** (4,839 / 5,681 chars): QA flagged this as `[WARN]`. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY`) penalize plain-text alternates that are nearly pure link dumps. This is the highest-priority deliverability risk in the send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **`user-scalable=0` / `maximum-scale=1`** in the viewport meta tag:
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
  ```
  This blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). iOS Safari ignores it since iOS 10, but Android WebView does not.

- **17 images missing alt text** (16 unique assets, one divider image — `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` — appears 4× with no alt each time). The logo image (`3b6a7dc1`) has `alt=""` which is acceptable for decorative images, but product and promotional images have no alt text at all. Confirmed assets:
  - `b9b109c6` (product image)
  - `b6146610`, `3eaaa7ec`, `9a771355`, `9c840aa8`, `bd7a3f04`, `befed8a0`, `2ed1d6cc`, `8714175e` (promotional images)
  - `850f4f2d`, `252df0eb` (likely footer/social icons)

- **Malformed charset meta tag** — `http-equiv` attribute is missing:
  ```html
  <!-- Present -->
  <meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
  <!-- Should be -->
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
  ```
  Without `http-equiv`, some older clients (Outlook 2007–2013) may fall back to their default encoding.

- **HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE** — not a blocking issue in major clients, but is a legacy choice; some ESPs default to this and it can cause Outlook quirks-mode rendering.

---

This is a data gap, not a confirmed defect — recommend probing a sample of click URLs in QA to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` pass through correctly.
