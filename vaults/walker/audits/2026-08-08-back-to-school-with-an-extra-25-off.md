# Back to school with an extra 25% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least this one gave me a real code to use — DAYONE, 25% off — instead of just teasing "up to" some number. That's an improvement over some of the recent ones. But I opened this and it's the same neon green and black basketball-court motif Nike's been running since July, just recolored. This is the fourth "back to school" email from Nike I've gotten in the last month, and I don't even have kids in school — I don't know why they keep pounding me with this.
- The product shots are fine — I noticed the Air Max Plus and the P-6000, and there's a gray fleece hoodie set that actually caught my eye more than the shoes did, if I'm honest. But then it turns into models in shorts and tank tops, a woman in cropped leggings, kids in graphic tees — none of that is me. By the time I got to the second "25% Off Shoes" banner near the bottom I was just scrolling to get to the end.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were actually shopping for shoes right now, since the offer itself is concrete. Would I forward it to a friend? No. This is a scroll-and-close, not a mark-as-read-and-come-back-to-it.

## What's working

- The 25% off code (DAYONE) is right there, big, with actual dates on it — 7.19.26 to 8.15.26 — so I know exactly how long I've got.
- The Air Max Plus and P-6000 sneakers are shown clean, on white background, easy to see what I'm getting.
- "Members: Free Shipping on Orders $50+" up top — I am a member, so that's a real perk, not just filler.
- Two CTAs — "Shop" and "Find Your Store" — so if I wanted to walk into a physical Nike Store instead of ordering online, that's covered.

## What's weak

- Back to school, again. Fourth time this summer. I don't have kids in school anymore — my grandkids maybe, but this doesn't feel aimed at me.
- The whole middle section — kids in graphic tees, women in leggings, a shirtless-adjacent tank top shot — none of that reads as a 62-year-old guy's category.
- No mention of cushioning, arch support, all-day comfort — nothing that speaks to what actually gets me to buy a shoe.
- That second big black "25% Off Shoes" banner near the bottom just repeats the same offer I already saw at the top — feels like padding.
- Small print, tight lines — I had to squint on my phone to read the exclusions and terms at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop leading with "Back to School" for subscribers who've shown no signal they're shopping for kids — split the list and send a plain "25% off select styles, code DAYONE" version to someone like me instead.
- 2. Cut the middle "More Sale Styles" block with the kids/women's shots — for me, that's dead space between two shoe offers I might actually care about.
- 3. Add one line about comfort or fit near the shoe images — "cushioned, all-day wear" — something that speaks to why I'd pick Nike over Hoka or New Balance.
- 4. Drop the redundant second 25% banner — one clear offer stated once is plenty; repeating it just reads like filler.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Member deal: extra 25% off select styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $50+ for members, plus 25% off with DAYONE`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort picks from Air Max to fleece — 25% off through Aug 15`

# Technical Audit — Nike "Back to School" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on a standard Foundation-for-Emails/SFMC template with responsive breakpoints and a branded web font; QA flagged 23 items, but 21 are false positives from the doubled-domain heuristic misreading legitimate click-tracking/deep-link wrappers. Two confirmed defects remain: a missing plain-text MIME part and one non-HTTPS, alt-less tracking image.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **QA "doubled-domain" flags (21 instances) — false positive, not a bug.** Every flagged URL follows the pattern `https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/<url-encoded destination>/.../<hash>=452`, which is SFMC's standard click-tracking wrapper (`click.em.nike.com` is the ExactTarget/SFMC tracking subdomain; the encoded `https%3A%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2F...` is the real destination). The checker's regex is matching "two domains present in one string," which is inherent to any click-tracked link and not evidence of a merge/templating error. Example: `https://click.em.nike.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.nike.com%2Fmembership%3Fcp=...` correctly decodes to `https://www.nike.com/membership?cp=...`.
- The three product links additionally route through `nike.sng.link` (Singular deep-link service) with `_fallback_redirect` / `_ios_redirect` / `_android_redirect` parameters all pointing to the same `www.nike.com/t/...` product page — this is intentional platform-aware deep-linking, not a duplicated/broken destination.
- All tracking links share a consistent `cp=` campaign string (`AEP-NA-US-MW-Email-BackToSchoolSale-Medium-0808`), confirming links are wired to one campaign rather than stale/mismatched IDs.
- 24 tracking links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (expected — click-redirect domains are intentionally not probed).
