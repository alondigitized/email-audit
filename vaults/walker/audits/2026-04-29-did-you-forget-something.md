# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-29**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one got my attention right off the bat — "Did you forget something?" I recognized what was going on immediately. I must've been looking at those Slip-ins and didn't finish buying. The main shoe they're showing me, the Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro, looks like exactly the kind of thing I'd wear. Gray, sleek, slip-on — great. But then I kept scrolling and they showed me a bunch of women's shoes as "best sellers." That took the wind right out of my sails. I'm a 62-year-old man, Skechers knows me — I'm a Plus member — and they're pushing pink and floral women's sneakers at me? That felt like a mistake. The 20% off banner was a nice nudge, but not enough to overcome the feeling that this email wasn't really *for me*.
- The hook is solid and the hero product is right. But the women's product flood in the recommendations module burns the goodwill fast. Left feeling more confused than compelled.

## What's working

- **The abandoned cart hook is smart.** "Did you forget something?" and "READY TO CHECK OUT?" work together cleanly. I knew immediately what this was about, and I appreciated that they remembered what I was looking at.
- **The hero product is a genuine fit for me.** The Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro Lanzo is a men's Slip-ins with comfort tech — exactly my wheelhouse. One good photo, clear name, clear CTA.
- **The 20% OFF banner is a real incentive.** If that's my Plus member discount stacking on top, that's meaningful. I notice things like that.
- **Curbside pickup and Find a Store are useful.** I don't always want to wait for shipping. Those utility blocks at the bottom are the kind of no-fuss info I appreciate.

## What's weak

- **Every recommended product is women's.** That's the big one. I'm looking at pink slip-ons and floral sneakers labeled "Women's Slip-ins." I'm not buying those. It makes me feel like the algorithm forgot who I am. As a known Plus member, I expect Skechers to do better than this.
- **The hero CTA is buried after the product.** "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" appears above the fold but then again below the product. Fine — but the button styling isn't bold enough to really jump out on a small phone screen.
- **"Shop Additional Styles: Women, Men, Kids..."** is just a wall of text links. No visuals, no context. That section doesn't entice me to explore anything.
- **The fine print at the bottom is very small.** The Mother's Day Sale terms are in tiny font. I'd have to zoom on my phone to read the discount rules, which I'd probably just skip.
- **The 20% OFF banner is vague.** Is this my member benefit? A general sale? One pair only? I'm left guessing whether this applies to my cart item.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the recommendations module — show me men's items only.** This is a straightforward personalization failure. If I'm a male customer with a men's shoe in cart, every "best seller" shown should come from the men's catalog.
- 2. **Make the CTA button more prominent on mobile.** Darker color, bigger tap target — something that stands out on a 5-inch phone screen without squinting.
- 3. **Clarify the 20% OFF claim.** One sentence: "Your Plus member discount applies to your cart item." That turns a banner I scroll past into a reason to act.
- 4. **Add urgency to the cart reminder.** "This offer expires in 48 hours" or "Low stock" would push me to act now rather than save it for later.
- 5. **Swap the category link wall for 2-3 curated men's product tiles.** Give me something visual that fits my profile instead of a nav menu.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP redirect URLs (confirmed)**
All tracked links use `http://` at the entry point:
```
http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...
```
Even if the final destination resolves to HTTPS, the first hop is unencrypted. Some security-conscious mail clients flag or strip HTTP links. Attentive supports `https://` redirect domains — this should be enforced at the ESP config level.

**Click-tracking domain**
Redirect domain is `skechers.attentivemail.com` (CNAME'd subdomain of Attentive). Standard practice, no issue with the domain itself, but the HTTP protocol above applies to all links using this domain.

**No visible UTM parameters in link payloads**
The `upn=` parameters are opaque Attentive tokens. Whether UTM params are appended to final destination URLs cannot be confirmed from the source — see §6.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some screen readers (NVDA + Thunderbird) announce the tab/window title. An empty title degrades accessibility for those users.

**Global link style override removes underline**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This strips all visual link affordances inside Gmail's `#MessageViewBody` wrapper. Links become indistinguishable from body text unless button/CTA styles re-apply decoration explicitly.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` suppression**
```css
a[x-apple-data-detectors] { color: inherit !important; text-decoration: inherit !important }
```
Correct — prevents Apple Mail from auto-linking addresses/dates and overriding brand styles.

**Responsive breakpoint**
Media query at `max-width:620px` targets the standard mobile stacking breakpoint. No issues with the breakpoint value itself; column stacking classes (`stack .column`) are present.

**Table-based layout**
Expected for email. `mso-table-lspace:0; mso-table-rspace:0` on all tables — correct Outlook fix.
