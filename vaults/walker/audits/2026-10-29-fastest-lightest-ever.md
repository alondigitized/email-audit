- A racing shoe that weighs under 100 grams. It's a shoe built to go fast, and I walk for comfort. This is another adidas email, and I've stopped counting them since the 15th. This one at least has a shoe in it, which beats the Metallica tee on the 26th and the fashion spread with no footwear on the 23rd. The product photo is clean and easy to see.
- I don't run races, though, and nothing here speaks to my feet. There's no price and no discount. There's no mention of cushioning, arch support or all-day wear. "Lightest" is a nice word, but I want soft and supportive more than I want light. ## What's working

- The hero photo is a single big white shoe on a plain background. I can see it clearly on my phone.
- The numbered breakdown (upper, foam, stiffening element, outsole) explains the shoe in plain steps. I like being told what I'm paying for.
- The "SHOP NOW" buttons are big, black and easy to tap with a thumb.
- The text is large enough to read without zooming, and there's a lot of white space.
- "Sub-100-gram" is a specific claim. It's more concrete than "light and comfy."

## What's weak

- There's no price anywhere. A new super shoe will cost a lot, and I'd want to know before I read on.
- It's a carbon-plate racing shoe. Nothing says cushioning, arch support, walking or slip-on ease.
- The fine print says it's only at "select stores" in New York. I can't try it on, and "first-come, first-served" sounds like a tease.
- There are two CTAs stacked together, "Shop Now" and "Find Store Now," plus a black "best sellers" banner above the hero. That's three places to tap before I've learned anything.
- The loud all-caps headline and the "future of Running" copy feel aimed at someone 35 years younger than me.
- It's the same pattern as the last two weeks: a big brand moment with no reason for me to buy.

- 1. Put a price, and a way to compare, near the first button. Something like "$500, here's who it's for."
- 2. Split the list by buyer. Send the racing story to runners and send walkers a cushioned everyday shoe.
- 3. Drop the "Find Store Now" button unless stores near the reader carry the shoe. Two buttons side by side is clutter.
- 4. Add one line on comfort or fit, such as "Runs true to size" or "Cushioned for long miles," so a non-racer knows whether it's for them.
- 5. Move the "best sellers" banner below the hero so the shoe comes first.
