# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Same shoe again. That pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom is back for what, the sixth time this week, now under "YOU LIKED THESE. AND YOU WEREN'T WRONG." with a big "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button. I did not like these — I've never once clicked on them, never sized them, never put them in a cart. This is a women's floral sneaker being pushed at a 62-year-old guy who's never shown a hint of interest in it. I saw the exact same shoe under "YOU LIKED THESE" language yesterday too (2026-08-18, "Your cart expires soon!").
- The grid underneath is actually decent — Slip-ins, Garage Sneakers, a couple of men's styles, and there's a real "MEN" link further down. But I have to scroll past a fake cart pitch to get there. That's backwards.
- I'm not clicking the hero, I'm not forwarding this, and honestly I'm getting numb to Skechers mail at this point. If tomorrow's another "cart expires" reskin of this same pink shoe, I'm done opening these for a while.

## What's working

- The product grid below the fold has real men's items — Slip-ins Relaxed Fit and an Arch Fit Arcade sneaker, which is the kind of thing I'd actually consider.
- The "UP TO 40% OFF SUMMER STYLES" banner is bold, clear, and easy to read on my phone without squinting.
- "SHOP ADDITIONAL STYLES" with a plain MEN link means I can get to my category in two taps if I skip past the top.
- No layout glitches — everything rendered clean, buttons look tappable.

## What's weak

- The whole hero is built around a shoe I never touched — floral, pink, women's — dressed up as "you liked these," which is just false.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" implies I have something sitting in a cart. I don't. That's the same fake-urgency trick from yesterday's send, just reworded.
- This is at least the sixth near-identical email pushing this one shoe this week — the cadence is exhausting, not persuasive.
- Men's arch-support, cushioned options are buried two-thirds down the page instead of leading.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the fake-cart framing entirely — if I never added anything, don't tell me I "liked" it or that an order is waiting. Lead instead with the 40% off banner and a men's comfort hero, like the 08-16 "Your Search Stops Here" email did (that one scored well with me).
- 2. Use purchase/browse history for personalization — if there's no real signal I'm into that shoe, don't fabricate one.
- 3. Move the men's product grid up top, above the fold, so guys like me see relevant shoes first instead of scrolling past someone else's shoe.
- 4. Cap how often this cart-reminder template fires — six sends in a week for a shoe I never touched is going to get this whole sender ignored.
