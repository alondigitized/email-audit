# Final Hours! Our Summer Favorites Sale Ends Tonight
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- I've seen this sale before — the Summer Favorites at up to 40% off has been in my inbox since July 12th. "Final Hours" is doing the heavy lifting here, and I'll be honest, that landing did catch my eye for a second. But the moment I got to my inbox and saw that same wall of raw computer code sitting in my preview slot again, I sighed. Skechers has sent me seven emails in nine days, and every single one of them has that broken gibberish where a real preview should be. At this point it's like getting a letter with the envelope glued shut — I know what's inside, but it's still annoying.
- The body itself is clean and perfectly readable, and the 40% off sale plus the Skechers Plus member exclusive 20% off code is a genuine double-dip deal. I appreciate that they didn't bury the member perk. But the hero image is a woman in a bright casual outfit — not me. The SHOP MEN'S section is there, buried below the women's section, which tells me this email is designed around their biggest audience first and guys like me come second.
- I'd probably scroll through, click SHOP MEN'S, and check what's left in the sale. I would not forward this to a buddy because it's just a rehash of the same sale from last week.

## What's working

- The 40% off callout in the hero is impossible to miss — big, bold, right at the top before I even scroll.
- The Skechers Plus Member Exclusive block with CODE: SUMMER is a real added value. I know I'm a member and that 20% off regular-priced styles is meaningful on top of a sale.
- "LAST CHANCE" banner and "Final Hours" create genuine urgency — I know today is the day to decide or walk away.
- Men's sizes are right there in a size-selector widget. Practical. I can see if my size is even in the mix without clicking through multiple pages.
- The email body renders clean — no broken images, no text pileup, everything lines up. Whatever else is wrong with this send, the layout isn't it.

## What's weak

- That preview text is raw JSON code again. I have flagged this in my notes on the July 15th email, the July 14th email — it keeps happening. I don't know what's going on over there at Skechers but somebody needs to fix it.
- The hero model is a woman. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for men's walking shoes. The first human I see in this email is not me.
- No comfort language anywhere — no "arch support," no "Hands Free Slip-ins," nothing about cushioning. Compare that to the July 19th email which said "Hands Free Slip-ins" right in the subject line. That one I opened faster than a Wheel of Fortune rerun.
- This is the same Summer Favorites Sale from July 12th, just with "Final Hours" slapped on it. After seven emails in nine days I'm getting a little numb to the urgency.
- "SHOP THE SALE" is a generic CTA. It goes to everything — women's, kids', clothing. I have to do the work to find men's comfort shoes myself.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a men's-specific subject** — something that tells me this sale has what I actually want, not a generic sale close-out.
- 2. **Fix the broken preheader** — this has appeared in at least six straight emails. Every single send is burning the preview slot with raw schema.org JSON code. That's a half-second first impression, and right now it's a negative one every time.
- 3. **Move SHOP MEN'S above SHOP WOMEN'S**, or at least make it the second hero block. Men's comfort is Walker's lane; put it closer to the top.
- 4. **Add one comfort-specific callout** — even one line of "Arch Fit, Max Cushioning, and Hands Free Slip-ins — all on sale" would pull me in.
- 5. **Make the primary CTA category-specific** — "SHOP MEN'S SALE" instead of the generic "SHOP THE SALE."
- 6. **Show one specific shoe** — the hero right now is a woman and a lifestyle shot. If I see the Max Cushioning Elite 2.0 or the GOwalk Arch Fit on sale for $59.99 I'm clicking immediately.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Final Hours! Summer Favorites Sale Ends Tonight"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends cleanly and tracking fires, but every hosted image is served over HTTP rather than HTTPS, meaning most modern email clients will block or proxy all visual content. One tracking pixel has a malformed URL with no protocol scheme, rendering it a broken relative path.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path pixel — missing protocol scheme (broken)**
```
src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499"
```
No `http://` or `https://` prefix. Email clients will interpret this as a relative path, making the pixel a dead request. Inbox placement / deliverability signal from Return Path is lost for every recipient.

**Ink1000 pixel — HTTP**
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?..."
```
Served over HTTP. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com will proxy or block this on load.

**33 click-redirect links** — all routed through `click.emails.skechers.com`. Standard practice; not flagged as broken, but UTM passthrough could not be verified (see §6).

**Krux/Salesforce DMP beacons** — three pixels fire to `beacon.krxd.net` including a SHA-256-hashed email match. These are HTTPS and functional, but they do constitute cross-site identity matching — relevant if GDPR or CCPA scope ever applies to this list.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — systemic (14 images)**

All content images are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com`. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, app store badges, social icons, and at least one content image:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/*.png            (11 footer images)
```
Gmail has proxied HTTP images since 2013; Apple Mail on iOS 15+ blocks mixed content. The email will render image-broken in any client that enforces HTTPS-only.

**Missing alt text**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — alt="" is absent entirely (tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly for screen reader silence)
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt text; unknown what it depicts from the truncated source

**Meta tag attribute errors**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Both tags are syntactically invalid without `http-equiv`. Charset and IE compatibility hints will not be applied.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
Disables font scaling globally, including for users who have increased system text size for accessibility. Standard practice in email is to scope this to `body` only.

**Redundant breakpoint definitions**
Media queries are duplicated at `375px` and `640px` — identical rule blocks appear twice in the `<head>`. No functional regression, but increases parse weight.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens visible in the truncated source. The Krux pixel carries a hardcoded hashed subscriber ID (`partner_uid=9153f33f...`) and a plain subscriber ID (`partner_uid=121173391`), confirming per-recipient rendering fired correctly for this send sample.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated before the footer; cannot confirm physical mailing address or one-click unsubscribe link presence from source alone. QA pipeline should assert both are non-empty in the rendered DOM.

**Preheader** is present and hidden via `display:none !important` — correctly implemented:
```html
<div class="preheader" style="font-size:1px;display:none !important">Up to 40% off select styles…</div>
```

**Authentication headers** — not observable from HTML source; DKIM/SPF/DMARC pass/fail must be confirmed from raw message headers. `emails.skechers.com` sending domain should have alignment with `skechers.com` DMARC policy.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 33 destination URLs pass through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects. The campaign identifier `PRO_US_NA_U_SITESELECT_FULL_EN_07202026` is present in tracking pixels but whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to final landing URLs could not be verified — the QA probe skipped all redirect-wrapped links. If UTMs are injected at the redirect layer, this is fine; if they rely on landing page query strings in the HTML, they should be spot-checked against a decoded redirect.
