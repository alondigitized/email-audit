# What's up to 65% off? NEW SANDALS!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. I've lost count somewhere around there, and this one shows up using a question-format subject line — *"What's up to 65% off?"* — as if I haven't been watching DSW answer that question every single day this week. The answer is sandals. Women's sandals. It says so right in the hero banner, plain as day: **"UP TO 65% OFF WOMEN'S SANDALS."** I'm a 62-year-old man. I keep waiting for DSW to notice.
- The email itself is clean. The offer is loud and up top. There are four or five product sections below the fold, all organized by theme — "Just Dropped," "Made for Summer Days," "Everyone Loves These Names," "Top Picks Under $50." All women's products. The whole scroll is heels, strappy sandals, espadrilles. I'm not the customer for any of it.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. I've got nothing against DSW — I've bought walking shoes there — but this is fourteen emails into a campaign that never once aimed at me.

## What's working

- **Offer is prominent and immediate.** "UP TO 65% OFF" is the first thing I see in the hero. No hunting for the discount.
- **"SHOP BY SIZE" section at the bottom** is actually practical. If I were shopping, being able to filter by my size right from the email is the kind of shortcut I appreciate.
- **"THERE'S MORE IN STORE" / Find a Store section** is a smart touch for someone like me who'd rather try shoes on before buying them.
- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, no overlapping text, nothing jumbled. Reads fine on a phone.
- **"Top Picks Under $50" section** shows they know price sensitivity matters — that's a hook I'd respect if any of the product were for me.

## What's weak

- **"WOMEN'S SANDALS" is right there in the hero.** They're not hiding who this is for, and it isn't me. After two weeks of emails, they still haven't split the list by gender.
- **"What's up to 65% off? NEW SANDALS!"** — the question-format subject works once. It doesn't work on the fourteenth send of the same sale. I knew the answer before I opened it.
- **Zero men's product, not even a sidebar.** A "Men's picks" module below the fold costs them nothing and could have salvaged the click for me.
- **The asterisk on "UP TO 65% OFF WOMEN'S SANDALS\*"** makes me hesitate. Fine print attached to a headline offer always makes me read slower and trust less.
- **"Perfect for peak summer ☀️"** as preheader is vague. Tells me nothing I didn't know from "NEW SANDALS" in the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by gender, or add a men's module.** This is the fix that would matter most across every DSW send going forward. I've bought shoes from DSW — they know my account. Route men's subscribers to an email that leads with men's walking shoes, or at minimum add a "For the guys in your life" block below the fold.
- 2. **Retire the question-format subject.** "What's up to 65% off?" was clever the first time. By the fourteenth send it reads like a riddle with an obvious punchline. Use a declarative offer instead.
- 3. **Drop the asterisk or explain it immediately.** If the exclusions are narrow (specific brands), say so up front. Buried fine print signals bait-and-switch to me even when it isn't.
- 4. **Give the preheader real information.** "Perfect for peak summer" is filler. Tell me the deadline, a specific brand, or a product type — something that makes opening feel worth the 2 seconds.
- 5. **"Everyone Loves These Names" needs the name brands in the subject or preheader.** If it's Birkenstock or Hoka in that section, say so. That's what sells me, not vague brand-authority copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's & Women's Sandals — Up to 65% Off This Week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `65% Off Summer Sandals — New Arrivals Just Landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Sunday. Shop by size — wide widths included.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Top brands, up to 65% off. Find a store near you.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` (capital S) in all `@font-face` declarations — confirmed bug.**
CSS property names are case-sensitive in the spec. All 10 `@font-face` blocks use:
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
`Src:` is an unknown declaration and will be silently dropped by all conforming CSS parsers. Fallback system fonts will render instead of Futura PT and HelveticaNow in every client. Fix: lowercase `src:`.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**`-ms-text-size-adjust` set to `none` while `-webkit-text-size-adjust` is `100%`.**
```css
-ms-text-size-adjust: none;
-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
```
These are contradictory. The `none` value on the MS property suppresses text scaling entirely in legacy IE/Windows Phone. Should be `100%` consistently across both.

**Dark mode:** Implementation is present and structurally correct (`.dm-bg-*` / `.dm-font-*` classes with `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`). Cannot verify completeness without full source.
