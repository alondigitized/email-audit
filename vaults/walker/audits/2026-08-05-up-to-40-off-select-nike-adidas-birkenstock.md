# Up to 40% off select Nike, adidas, Birkenstock…
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, DSW's kept up the pace — three days ago it was "40% off adidas & more TODAY," yesterday was the "FOMO alert" version, and now here's basically the same 40%-off pitch dressed in back-to-school wrapping paper. My first read of the hero — Nike, New Balance, Crocs, adidas, Birkenstock logos lined up — actually caught my eye for a second since a couple of those are brands I do check when I'm shoe shopping. Then I scrolled.
- And scrolled. And it's heels. Wedges. Espadrilles. Strappy sandals in tan and black. A whole "Shop by Size" chart at the bottom that tops out looking like it's built for dress shoes, not a wide-fitting walking shoe. There is not one men's product shown in this entire email — not a single sneaker, slip-on, or anything with visible cushioning. The brand logos got my attention and then the email completely forgot I exist.
- I'm not clicking this one, and I'm sure not forwarding it — there's nothing here for me or anyone I'd send it to. Straight to "read and ignored," same bucket as most of this week's DSW mail.

## What's working

- The brand logo row up top (New Balance, Crocs among them) is a legit hook — those are brands I actually rotate through.
- "UP TO 40% OFF" in bold is impossible to miss — hierarchy is clean, no hunting for the deal.
- "Shop by Size" and wide-width/wide-calf buttons at the bottom show they at least think about fit, even if the products above don't match my fit needs.
- Layout's tidy — no broken images, no overlapping text, easy enough to scroll through on my phone.

## What's weak

- Zero men's product anywhere. Not one shoe pictured is something I'd wear — all heels, wedges, sandals, espadrilles.
- "Start the school year right" — I don't have a kid heading back to school, so that whole framing is dead air for me.
- The brand logos promise Nike/New Balance/Crocs/adidas/Birkenstock, but the actual featured product is entirely women's dress and sandal styles — bait-and-switch feel between hero and body.
- "Shop Women" is a visible category button but there's no "Shop Men" anywhere to balance it — tells me exactly who this send was built for, and it wasn't me.
- This is at least the fourth 40%-off-ish DSW email I've gotten this week, following the "FOMO alert" one yesterday and the actual adidas sale two days before that — starting to blur together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender before sending — if DSW knows I've clicked men's/comfort categories before, show me men's walking shoes, not wedges. This is the single biggest miss.
- 2. Add an actual men's product row if this brand-name hero is going out to male subscribers at all — right now the logos over-promise and the body under-delivers.
- 3. Cut the back-to-school angle for subscribers without that context, or at least don't lead with it if the offer is brand-wide.
- 4. Clean up the preview text — right now it's a real sentence followed by a wall of invisible characters, which is going to look broken in some inbox previews.
- 5. Ease off the frequency — three near-identical 40%-off sends in under a week trains me to stop opening.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's comfort styles now up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Balance, Crocs & more — 40% off starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, slip-on, wide-width — all in the sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your size, your fit, up to 40% off top brands`
