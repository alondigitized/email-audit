## Executive summary

- Well, this whole email is a women's athletic-wear catalog. Sydney Levrone — I had to squint, I don't know who that is, apparently a champion runner — and every single item pictured is a dress, a legging, a cropped tank, a "Define Fitted Tank." Not one thing here fits on my foot or is meant to. This isn't a near-miss like some of the others, it's a total swing and a miss.
- Honestly this is worse than the marathon email from Wednesday — at least that one had men's running shoes hidden in it somewhere. This is head-to-toe women's apparel from a "signature capsule," and I get zero use out of any of it. I'm not forwarding this to anybody, and it's getting deleted, not even skimmed twice.
- The one shoe up top, the 9060R, is actually a decent-looking lifestyle sneaker in a neutral cream — if New Balance had built the whole email around that, I might've clicked through. Instead they buried it under a wall of women's leggings and dresses.

## What's working

- The hero shoe shot — that cream 9060R — is a clean, well-lit product photo, the kind of neutral colorway I'd actually consider.
- Top nav is simple, big enough type, easy to tap — Men / Women / Kids / Sale, no clutter.
- "What's trending" footer block with the bestseller sneakers pulls me back toward something relevant, too little too late.

## What's weak

- Almost the entire product grid is women's apparel — dresses, leggings, crop tops — I'm a 62-year-old guy, none of this is for me.
- Signature athlete tie-in means nothing to me; I don't follow track and field, I don't know who Sydney Levrone is.
- No men's shoe, no comfort or arch-support language anywhere, no price, no percentage off — nothing that speaks to how I actually shop.
- Big stretch of model photos in a studio doing a "fashion shoot" vibe, not a product email — feels like I opened someone else's inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this by gender — send me the men's version of a "trending" or "bestsellers" email instead of a women's capsule drop.
- 2. Lead the whole email with the 9060R sneaker and give it its own real estate — specs, comfort features, price — instead of one small square in a grid.
- 3. Drop an actual offer in there. A capsule launch with no price and no discount gives me nothing to act on.
- 4. Cut the athlete-bio copy or replace it with plain comfort/fit language if you insist on sending this to men on your list at all.
