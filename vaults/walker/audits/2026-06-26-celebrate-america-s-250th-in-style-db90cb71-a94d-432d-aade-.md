## What's working

- **Seasonal timing is dead-on.** America's 250th — that's a real moment, not some made-up Skechers sale event. I respect a brand that pegs a promotion to something real.
- **"Men's Weekend Essentials" is front and center.** I don't have to dig past a women's section to find what's for me, and that matters on a phone.
- **SHOP SANDALS and SHOP CANVAS are specific.** Those are two words I understand — not "Discover" or "Explore," actual category names that tell me where I'm going.
- **Email renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text. Whatever's wrong with the preview isn't showing up inside the body.
- **Red, white, and blue creative is appropriate.** They didn't just slap a flag on a generic email — the whole thing feels put together for the holiday.

## What's weak

- **That preview text JSON problem. Again.** I don't know what to call it anymore — a bug, negligence, nobody home — but eleven-plus straight emails with the same broken code pasted where a pitch should go is embarrassing. I'm a loyal Skechers Plus member and this is what they show me in the inbox.
- **25% off apparel, not shoes.** Walker doesn't shop for Skechers shirts. I'm in this list for footwear. Lead with the shoe discount or keep the apparel offer secondary.
- **No comfort messaging whatsoever.** No arch support, no cushioning, no GO WALK, no mention of what makes these sandals and canvas shoes worth buying versus the DSW rack. I need a reason, not just a patriotic picture.
- **No Skechers Plus member hook.** I've been a member long enough that I've now audited a dozen of their emails. Not one line here acknowledging I'm a member or that I might have points or early access.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** This is the only item that matters before everything else. Every other improvement is invisible when my inbox shows JSON code. Until this is fixed, every email starts in a hole.
- 2. **Lead the offer with footwear, not apparel.** Make the 25% something like "25% off summer sandals" or put a shoe-specific discount up top. Move apparel to a secondary block.
- 3. **Add one line of comfort copy under the Men's hero.** Even "built for all-day comfort" or "cushioned sole, easy on — easy off" would anchor the shoe pic to why I actually buy from Skechers.
- 4. **Give me a Skechers Plus callout.** "Members get early access" or "Earn points on every pair" — one line is enough. Right now I feel like a stranger.
- 5. **Put a date on the deal.** "Offer ends July 4th" adds real urgency. The patriotic theme implies it but doesn't say it.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) with Krux DMP audience syndication. The primary technical risk is widespread use of HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources, which will cause broken images in clients that enforce mixed-content blocking (Outlook 365, Apple Mail with image proxying).

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links unverifiable** — all routed through `click.emails.skechers.com`; QA probes were skipped. Landing page UTM coverage cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Third-party audience-match pixels (Krux/Salesforce DMP):**
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=9153f33fe5cd...` — passes a SHA-256 hashed subscriber email to a third-party data broker at render time.
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_subid&partner_uid=121173391` — passes a raw subscriber ID to the same broker.
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif?confid=so3yexmpm&campaignid=MKG_US_NA_U_SEASONAL_FULL_EN_06262026` — impression beacon; campaign ID is correctly populated.

**ReturnPath/Validity inbox placement pixel served over HTTP:**
```
pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499
```

No `https://` prefix — will silently fail in clients blocking mixed content, defeating inbox placement monitoring.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 11 affected assets:**

All `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/...` images are served over HTTP. Affected named images:
- Skechers logo (`dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`)
- App Store badge (`0ec56c9f-...`)
- Google Play badge (`9bb508d0-...`)
- Curbside Pickup icon (`00100b23-...`)
- AfterPay icon (`07cb60b5-...`)
- Find A Store icon (`fc08601a-...`)
- Instagram icon (`b782e0aa-...`)
- Facebook icon (`a5c4cae0-...`)
- YouTube icon (`8c5738fc-...`)
- Unnamed content image (`49468f73-...`)

Gmail proxies these to HTTPS, masking the issue there. Outlook.com and Outlook desktop (2016+) may render broken images.

**Missing alt text — 2 images:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000.com pixel) — minor; tracking pixels conventionally carry `alt=""`
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — no `alt` attribute; appears to be a content image based on the large-format naming pattern. Screen readers will skip it entirely.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** applied globally via `* {}` selector. This suppresses iOS Mail's automatic text scaling, which may make body text unreadable at small viewport widths without explicit responsive font rules. The CSS does include `.mobileFont16` but it's opt-in per element, not a system-wide fix.

**Duplicate media queries:** The `.show` / `.mobile-hidden` rules are defined three times across two `<style>` blocks at breakpoints 680px, 640px, and 480px. Redundant; not a rendering bug in practice but increases parse weight.
