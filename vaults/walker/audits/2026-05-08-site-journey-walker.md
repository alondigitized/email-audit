# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 8, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I went through the whole site on my phone today, start to finish — browsing, logging in, finding Slip-ins, picking a shoe, trying to add it to my cart, and searching. Some parts were actually decent — the Men's menu was clean and easy, and the product page was laid out well. But three things hit me hard: the homepage pushed Mother's Day content at me like I don't exist as a customer, my cart page came up completely blank (nothing, just white), and when I searched "slip in" the first results shown were women's shoes. For a loyal Skechers Plus member, that's a rough experience. **Score holds at 4/10** — no meaningful improvement from yesterday.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same as yesterday. The cart failure alone could kill a sale. The homepage and search personalization gaps mean I'm being served content for everyone but me.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's menu is genuinely good.** When I tapped MENU and found Men, the submenu was clean and readable — large text, big tap targets, clear categories. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there at the bottom of the list as a direct link. That felt thoughtful.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category loaded properly.** 279 results, clear page title, width filter buttons (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) right at the top. That's useful for me — I always check wide fit first.
- **The product page was solid.** "Skechers Slip-ins: GO RUN Glide-Step Arch Fit" — $120, 4-star rating with 17 reviews, the BOGO deal called out clearly in red, big Slip-ins badge. Easy to read, no squinting required.
- **ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, blue, sticky at the bottom of the screen. I didn't have to hunt for it.
- **BOGO 50% OFF deal is visible everywhere.** Persistent top banner throughout the journey — I never forgot about the deal. That's good merchandising.
- **"Customers say this item feels true to size"** — small detail but I appreciate that. As someone who cares about fit, that saves me a phone call.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Homepage is for everyone but me.** I land on a full-screen Mother's Day promotion — a mom and daughter in pink — with a "Get Mom a Gift She'll Love" banner. I'm a 62-year-old man who wants Slip-ins. Nothing on the homepage speaks to me, and there's no sign the site knows I'm a Skechers Plus member. No "Welcome back, Walker" anywhere.
- **Login step wasn't visible.** The screenshot for the login step shows the same homepage I started on. Either the login flow didn't capture, or I couldn't tell I was logged in at all. The account icon looks the same before and after. If I logged in and the site doesn't acknowledge that anywhere visible, what's the point?
- **Cart page is completely broken — blank white screen.** Step 8 is a white rectangle. Nothing. No product, no total, no checkout button. This is a showstopper. If I added a shoe and then went to check out, I would give up and close the app. This is the most serious issue in the entire journey.
- **Size selection showed errors before I even tried.** The add-to-cart view showed two red warnings — "Please select a width" and "Please select a size" — displayed upfront before I touched anything. That's jarring. I don't need to be yelled at before I've done anything wrong.
- **Limited size availability shown.** I could only see sizes 7.0 through 10.5. I wear a 11. No 11, no 11.5, no 12. "Don't See Your Size?" is a link — but why is that my problem to chase down? Show me what's in stock clearly.
- **Search "slip in" returns women's shoes first.** 1028 results sounds impressive but the first two products shown are labeled "Women's." I'm a male customer browsing Men's. The site should know this. Women's results first is a disorienting and irrelevant start.
- **No Skechers Plus recognition anywhere.** I'm a loyalty member. At no point in this journey did I see my points balance, a members-only price, early access, or even a simple "Member" badge. That's a missed opportunity on every single page.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a broken checkout funnel — this needs to be the top engineering priority. No sale can close with that bug active.
2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in male members.** If I'm signed in, lead with Men's content and at minimum acknowledge my Skechers Plus status. Even a "Hi Walker — here's what's new in Men's Slip-ins" banner would be a step up from a Mother's Day shop.
3. **Fix the size/width validation messaging.** Don't show "Please select a size" before I've had a chance to select anything. Surface that only after a failed add-to-cart attempt.
4. **Filter search by gender when user is logged in.** A known male customer should see Men's results first by default for an ambiguous query like "slip in." Or at minimum put a gender toggle at the top of search results.
5. **Make Skechers Plus membership visible post-login.** Show my points and any member-specific pricing on the product page. I'm loyal because of that program — remind me it's working for me.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey audited across skechers.com (Salesforce Commerce Cloud / SFRA stack). Primary issues are: View Cart renders a broken HTML document (missing `<title>` and `lang`), Kasada bot-detection SDK is rate-limiting its own fingerprint calls which cascades to a cart 429, Stylitics CSS widget is silently broken due to a CORS misconfiguration, and the page head carries ~20 concurrent third-party script loads with two confirmed duplicates.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA role present on an element whose required parent role is absent. Screen readers will mis-announce or skip the element entirely.

**Serious**
- `document-title` (View Cart): `<title>` element is empty or missing. AT users and tab switchers have no page identity.
- `html-has-lang` (View Cart): `<html>` has no `lang` attribute. Language detection for screen readers and translation engines fails. *Both homepage and login pages correctly set `lang="en"` — this is a cart-page-specific regression, likely a template that bypasses the standard layout.*

**Moderate (8 violations)**
- Landmark structure is broken on Homepage and Search: duplicate `<main>`, `<aside>` not top-level, content outside any landmark region. Indicates heavy widget injection (Monetate, Quantum Metric) is appending DOM nodes outside the layout landmarks.
- Heading order skipped on Homepage.
- View Cart lacks a `<main>` landmark and an `<h1>`.

---

## SEO

- View Cart's missing `<title>` and `<html lang>` will cause Googlebot to treat it as a low-quality document. Cart pages are typically `noindex`, but if they are ever crawled (e.g., shared cart links), this is a signal hit.
- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` set inline on `<html>` style is a viewport-height JS shim — harmless for SEO but the fractional pixel value suggests the calculation runs on every resize without rounding, creating unnecessary style recalculations.
- No structural SEO issues observed in the product or category page HTML fragments provided.

LCP and CLS were not captured; those are the metrics most directly tied to mobile user experience and should be added to the next test run.

**Script count in `<head>`:** 20+ third-party scripts fired asynchronously on every page. The Men category page adds Snapchat, Facebook, Nextdoor, and Taboola on top of the baseline set. Each represents an additional DNS lookup + connection. With `dns-prefetch` only declared for Monetate, all other origins pay a cold connection cost.

**Duplicate script loads (confirmed in source):**
- `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` injected twice on Men category page.
- `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` loaded twice via two separate `<script vice-managed-src>` tags on Homepage and Login.

**Monetate double-load:** Both `se.monetate.net/js/3/...` (custom) and `se.monetate.net/js/2/...` (entry) load on every page — this appears intentional per their split-testing setup, but should be confirmed as not redundant.

**WebFontLoader:** `cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/webfont/1.6.28/webfontloader.js` is loaded from cdnjs. This library is deprecated and unmaintained since 2019. It is also a render-blocking concern even when loaded `async` because the `wf-*` body classes it sets can trigger FOUT on fonts already loading natively.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh` CSS custom property is computed via JS and set on `<html>` — a common workaround for the iOS Safari dynamic toolbar. Correct pattern, but the value `6.640000000000001px` (floating point artifact) should be `Math.round` or `parseFloat(...toFixed(2))` to avoid sub-pixel style churn on resize.
- No `viewport` meta tag or `preconnect` hints visible in the provided HTML head truncation; cannot fully verify without a complete `<head>` dump.
- `vice-managed-src` attributes on consent-gated scripts are a TrustArc pattern — correct for GDPR/CCPA compliance. No issues here.

---

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada KProtect 429s (5 of 6 network errors):**
All 429s target `/{uuid1}/{uuid2}/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.405` — the Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint. The test runner (headless browser or automation) is being identified as a bot and rate-limited by Kasada before it can establish a trusted session. The downstream effect is that `/cart/` also returns 429 because the session token that Kasada would have issued was never granted. This is Kasada working as designed, but it means any automated monitoring of the cart page will always see degraded TTFB and potential functional failure.

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics "Shop the Look" / outfit widget CSS is silently failing. The widget will render without its styles or not render at all on affected pages. This is a misconfiguration on Stylitics' CDN — either `skechers.com` was not added to their CORS allowlist for this version (`146f27f3c5`), or the version was updated and the allowlist was not. Skechers needs to file a ticket with Stylitics to update the CORS policy for this version hash.

**`net::ERR_NETWORK_CHANGED` (2 errors):** Network interface changed mid-test (Wi-Fi/LTE handoff or VPN reconnect during the audit run). Not reproducible in production; disregard.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix View Cart document shell (high, quick):** The cart page template is missing `<html lang="en">` and a non-empty `<title>`. This is almost certainly a separate Salesforce Commerce Cloud template (e.g., `cart.isml`) that doesn't inherit the standard `htmlHead.isml` include. Add the lang and title decorators to that template.

2. **Resolve Stylitics CORS (high, vendor action):** Contact Stylitics support with version hash `146f27f3c5` and request `https://www.skechers.com` be added to the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` list. Until resolved, the widget is non-functional.

3. **Remove duplicate script tags (medium):** Deduplicate `creativecdn` and `ytc.js` tags. These are likely firing events twice per page, inflating analytics data and adding unnecessary network overhead.

4. **Repair ARIA landmark structure (medium):** The `aria-required-parent` critical violation needs targeted fix — identify which widget is injecting the orphaned ARIA role element and either wrap it correctly or remove the role. The duplicate `<main>` issue points to a widget (likely Monetate or Quantum Metric) injecting a `role="main"` or `<main>` element into the DOM after page load; audit their injected markup.

5. **Stub Kasada in CI/synthetic monitoring (medium):** Automated cart-flow tests will always trip Kasada's bot detection. Use a Kasada-aware session seed or configure the monitoring tool's user-agent/fingerprint profile to pass the challenge, or exclude the `/fp` endpoint from the error budget in alerting.

6. **Instrument LCP and CLS (medium):** All 8 steps are missing these two Core Web Vitals. Add `PerformanceObserver` captures or use a tool with CrUX/field data support (e.g., Vercel Speed Insights, SpeedCurve) to fill the gaps before the next audit cycle.

7. **Replace WebFontLoader (low):** Migrate from the deprecated `webfontloader` library to native `@font-face` + `font-display: swap` in CSS. The existing Mulish font loading (`wf-mulish-*` classes visible in `<html>`) can be replicated with a `<link rel="preload">` + `font-display: optional` strategy without JS dependency.
