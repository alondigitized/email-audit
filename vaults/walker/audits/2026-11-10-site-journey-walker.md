# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-10**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got to the shoe pages fine. I never got a shoe into a cart. The homepage opened on a Christmas sweater photo, not a shoe. The Men's menu was easy to use. The product page was clear, but Add to Cart told me "Please select a size" and nothing was added. The cart page then came up completely blank, a white screen. Searching "comfort shoes" gave me 1,653 results, and the first two were women's shoes.

The slip-in and arch-support categories are there, which is good. But I never saw an order go through, and the cart is where this site loses me.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** It's the same as yesterday's 3, so I see no real improvement. I don't have yesterday's screenshots, so I can't point to a specific regression either.

The shopping path works up to the product page. The cart step failed, and a blank cart page costs the site sales.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu drawer (step 4).** The text is big and bold, and the rows are spaced well apart. "Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections" is easy to follow with a thumb. It also has a clear Back and an X.
- **Comfort shortcuts up top.** "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushioning" is the row I'd tap first. The same goes for the "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles on Men's Shoes. That's my kind of shopping.
- **Size buttons (step 7).** They're big, boxy and easy to hit. Sold-out sizes are crossed out with a little bell for restock alerts.
- **Add to Cart button.** It's wide, dark blue and impossible to miss.
- **Product page basics.** The price is large, and there are 4.5 stars with 186 reviews. The Klarna and Afterpay lines are clear. There's a "Check in-store availability" line, which I'd use.
- **Filters.** "Filter / Sort by / Size / Width" is right there. Width matters for my feet.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart (step 8).** It's a white page with no header and no "your cart is empty" message. I can't tell if it's loading, broken, or empty. I'd leave, and you'd lose me.
- **Size error.** The first tap on Add to Cart gives a red "Please select a size" and no item goes in. The size grid sits above the button, so this was avoidable. Preselecting a size or scrolling me to the grid would help. The cart badge also stayed at 0 all the way through.
- **Login did nothing visible (step 3).** The screen looks the same as the homepage, with no greeting, no name and no account menu. Nothing felt personal. The page I saw never showed that I was signed in.
- **Mixed promo messages.**
  - The top banner says "20% OFF Regular Priced Items."
  - The strip below it says "30% OFF Select Styles."
  - The shoe I picked says "Excluded from promotions" at $165.
  - I don't know what I'd really pay. For a price-vs-quality shopper, that kills trust.
- **Homepage lead image.** It shows sweaters and joggers for the holidays, not comfort shoes. I'm not shopping for loungewear, and nothing on that first screen speaks to cushioning or arch support.
- **Search results.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,653 results. The first two, a Women's wedge loafer and a Women's slipper, aren't for me. The search didn't know I'm a man, and the list isn't focused.
- **Small print.** The "From $15/month" and Klarna/Afterpay lines are small, and I'd squint at them. The "Max Cus…" tab is also cut off at the edge.
- **No comparison help.** There's nothing here that sets Skechers against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and no "is this worth $165" guidance.
- **Popups (step 2).** I saw none, which is fine. The screenshot is identical to step 1.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** Show the header and an item list, or "Your cart is empty — keep shopping" with a button. A blank page should never ship.
2. **Make Add to Cart forgiving.** Scroll to the size grid or preselect a common size on the first tap, and make the error easier to see.
3. **Make logging in visible.** Show "Hi, Walker" and a "Welcome back" bar with the last style I viewed or slip-ins in my usual size.
4. **Pick one promo message.** Show the real price after discount on the product page, or explain why an item is excluded.
5. **Lead with comfort on mobile.** Put a hero or tile on the homepage like "Slip-ins: step in, no hands" or "Arch Fit for all-day walking."
6. **Use what you know.** Once I'm looking at Men's, search for "comfort shoes" should show men's results first.
7. **Add a plain-language "why this one" line** on the product page, such as arch support, cushioning and weight.
8. **Make the small text larger.** That covers the payment lines and any fine print.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has the right shoes for my feet, and the menu points me to them well. But I couldn't finish a purchase. The size error got in my way, and the cart then went blank, so I'd go to Hoka or Brooks and spend my money there. Fix the cart and the promo confusion and this would be a real contender. As it stands, 3/10, the same as yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was only partly measured. LCP and CLS are not captured for any of the 8 steps, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. The cart step hit a 429, and its axe results (missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, `<h1>`) probably describe an error page, not the real cart. The homepage has the most substantive defects: 2 critical and 1 serious axe violation, plus a broken landmark structure with multiple `<main>` elements. The page also loads a very large number of third-party tags.

Evidence limits:
- The HTML samples are truncated inside `<head>`, so title, meta, canonical and structured data can't be verified.
- The captured URL for Homepage, Log In and Men category is `https://www.skechers.com/` for all three.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations were reported: 10 on Homepage, 4 on View Cart and 4 on Search.

**Homepage**
- **critical `button-name`**: one or more buttons have no accessible name. This is likely icon-only controls such as menu, search, wishlist or carousel buttons.
- **critical `aria-required-parent`**: ARIA roles such as `listitem` or `tab` are used without their required parent role.
- **serious `link-name`**: links have no discernible text, probably image-only or icon links.
- **moderate landmark violations**: `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. Together these indicate more than one `<main>` and landmarks nested inside other landmarks. This is consistent with injected or personalised content (Monetate) or a fragment embedded inside the page shell.
- **moderate `heading-order`**: heading levels are skipped.
- **minor `aria-allowed-role`**: a role is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- These match the Homepage, so they probably come from shared header, footer or aside templates.

**View Cart**
- **serious `document-title` and `html-has-lang`**, plus `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one`.
- All four are consistent with a bare error document, given the 429 on `/cart/` (see Console & Network Errors). The same HTML had `lang="en"` on the homepage.
- Re-run this step after the 429 is resolved before treating these as real cart defects.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`.
- Title, meta description, canonical, robots and structured data are not visible in the truncated samples, so they could not be assessed.
- The Log In and Men category steps report the homepage URL. If the real URL didn't change, category navigation is JS-driven without History API updates, which would hurt crawlability and deep linking. If it did change, the capture is stale. Verify which.
- View Cart has no `<title>` or `lang`. This only matters if that response is what crawlers or users actually receive, and cart pages are normally `noindex` anyway.

## Performance

- **LCP and CLS:** not captured on any step (`?`), so the vitals instrumentation or `PerformanceObserver` timing is failing. This is a gap in the audit itself.
- **TTFB:**
  - Homepage is 457ms and the first two later steps are 376ms. That is acceptable, but at the upper end of "good" (under 800ms).
  - Log In and Men category both show exactly 376ms, which suggests a reused navigation entry, not two separate measurements.
  - Steps 4–8 show 23–28ms (and 115ms for cart). That is too fast for server round-trips, so they were probably SPA transitions, bfcache or cache hits. They don't measure real document TTFB.
- **Third-party load:** the `<head>` alone has about 25–35 async third-party scripts. They include GTM, GA4/gtag, Google Ads, TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Snapchat, Reddit, Nextdoor, Yahoo, Taboola, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Pulse Insights, Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Impact, RTB House and Tune/go2sdk, and more on the category page. This risks main-thread contention, which hurts INP and LCP.
- **Fonts:** Mulish is loaded through the JS `webfontloader` from cdnjs (`wf-*-active` classes). Font discovery therefore waits on JS, which delays text paint. Self-hosted `@font-face` with preload and `font-display: swap` is faster.
- **GTM environment:** the GTM URL includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, so production may be serving a non-default GTM environment. Confirm this is intentional.

## Mobile Optimization

- Viewport handling uses a `--vh` CSS variable (6.64px, which implies a 664px viewport). This is a correct workaround for mobile browser chrome.
- The category page loads more tracking scripts than the homepage, which adds weight on constrained devices.
- No other mobile-specific defects are evidenced.

## Console & Network Errors

- **429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`**: this is rate limiting or bot mitigation, likely triggered by the automated session. It is the probable cause of the View Cart axe results.
- **"Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document"** (×6): a script registers an `unload` handler, which is blocked under Chrome's deprecation. It also makes the page ineligible for bfcache, and it is most likely from a third-party tag, though the first-party source is unconfirmed.
- **"Unsupported Summarizer API languages…"** (×4): a script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API with unsupported language options. The caller is unidentified (it could be an extension or a tag), but the repeated errors suggest an on-page script.

## Recommendations

1. Fix the audit harness. Capture LCP and CLS with `PerformanceObserver` (`buffered: true`), measure real document navigations, and retry the cart step with backoff or a whitelisted client.
2. **Critical a11y:** add accessible names (`aria-label` or visible text) to icon-only buttons and links, and fix the ARIA parent/child roles on the homepage.
3. Remove duplicate `<main>` landmarks and make sure `main` and `aside` are top-level. Give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and correct the heading order.
4. Find the origin of the `unload` handlers and replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
5. Audit the third-party tag inventory. Defer or consent-gate non-essential pixels, and load them after interaction or idle.
6. Self-host and preload fonts instead of using the JS font loader.
7. Confirm the GTM `env-1` setting is intentional for production.
8. Verify that URLs and titles change per step, and that the head metadata is complete, using the full HTML.
