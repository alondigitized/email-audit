# Did you see what's on clearance???
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- My thumb opened this the same way it's opened the last eight DSW emails — pure reflex, no thought involved. And then the email loaded, and there it was again: heels. Strappy sandals. A "SHOP WOMEN" button right below "What's New?" I'm a 62-year-old man who wore out a pair of New Balance 928s last month. Why is DSW showing me a runway?
- The one thing that slowed my scroll was the clearance section — "Up to 60% Off" is a real number, meaningfully bigger than the 25% off they've been recycling since Father's Day week. But they buried it two-thirds of the way down, underneath two full rows of women's shoes. The preview text promised "picks for Pops." I looked. They're not in here. That's not a miss, that's a broken promise.
- I'll mark this read. I won't click and I won't forward it. If the clearance section had been the hero, with men's comfort styles called out by name, this would have been a different conversation.

## What's working

- The hero brand logos — Nike, New Balance, Crocs — are exactly the names I comparison-shop; seeing them clustered at the top made me look twice
- "Up to 60% Off Clearance" is a genuine escalation from the 25% off offer I've seen on repeat; that number has weight
- The "Shop by Size" section near the bottom is a rare practical touch — I know my size, I just want my size; that's useful
- The email rendered cleanly on my phone — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing I had to pinch-zoom to make sense of

## What's weak

- "SHOP WOMEN" is literally the first CTA below the hero — I'm a 62-year-old man; that button is not for me
- The preview said "picks for Pops" and the email body contains no such thing — I scrolled looking for a men's curated section and found none
- Three question marks in the subject line ("???") read like coupon-spam, not a brand I've trusted for twenty years of shoe shopping
- Twenty-fifth-percent-off is now wallpaper — the June 15 send, the June 16 send, now this one; it stopped registering as an offer a week ago
- The clearance section, which is the only genuinely new and bigger number in this email, is buried past two rows of women's new arrivals

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 60% off clearance, not 25% off brands** — bigger number, bigger urgency, and clearance actually feels finite; flip the hero and the buried section
- 2. **Deliver on "Picks for Pops"** — if the preview text promises it, put a men's comfort block in the body: cushioned walkers, wide widths, slip-ons; even four products would have earned a click
- 3. **Kill the triple question marks** — one is fine, three reads as desperation; "Did you see what's on clearance?" is a perfectly decent subject without the pile-on
- 4. **Give the "Shop by Size" section men's-specific entry points** — right now it's generic; a "Men's sizes" label on the size buttons would let me tap directly to my category
- 5. **Vary the offer already** — after nine sends in ten days at 25% off, the discount has to change or you're training your list to wait for something better

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Malformed meta tags (two instances):**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```

Both are missing the required `http-equiv` attribute. Correct forms:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, these directives are inert. The charset declaration may not be recognized by Outlook/IE rendering engines, risking encoding fallback to ISO-8859-1 for non-ASCII characters.

**Issue — `Src:` capitalization in all `@font-face` blocks (10 declarations):**

```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```

CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, but several email-client preprocessors and some WebKit-derived renderers enforce lowercase. This is a known failure mode for web fonts in Samsung Mail and some older iOS builds. All 10 `@font-face` declarations are affected — should be `src:` (lowercase).

**Issue — Invalid CSS property in `:root`:**

```css
supported-color-schemes: light dark;
```

`supported-color-schemes` is only valid as an HTML `<meta>` attribute (`<meta name="supported-color-schemes">`), not as a CSS property. The correct CSS property is `color-scheme` (already set on the same `:root` block). This non-standard property is silently ignored but adds noise and could confuse CSS linters or future maintainers.

**No issues found** — viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`, and dark mode `color-scheme` meta are all correctly formed.
