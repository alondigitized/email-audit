# BOGO 50% off yet? Last chance!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. The orange BOGO banner at the top is easy to read, and I can see the deal right away. But it's the sixth BOGO note since the 5th. After "Psst...BOGO 50% off ends tomorrow" yesterday, a "Last chance!" today makes me doubt there's any last chance at all. The banner says "Long weekend, big deal," and it's a Wednesday. The email shows a "Your mid week mood boost" headline further down, so the email argues with itself.
- Past the banner, it's a long scroll of women's boots, flats and heels. I count maybe two or three men's shoes in the whole thing, and the "Shop Men" link is a small line of text under the banner. The one thing for me is the Brooks banner near the bottom, "Starting at $75." The preview text promised the Brooks Adrenaline up front, but I had to scroll nearly to the bottom to find it, and the banner shows no model name or price on the actual shoe. That is the one thing I'd click, and they buried it.
- Would I click? Maybe the Brooks button, if I'm curious about the price. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The orange BOGO 50% OFF banner is big and readable on my phone. I didn't need to zoom.
- The Brooks banner is a brand I actually cross-shop, and "Starting at $75" is a concrete number.
- The "Shop Men" link is right under the banner, so I can get to my section in one tap.
- The black "Shop" buttons are wide and easy to tap with my thumb.
- The "Shop by Size" block with a wide width button is useful. A lot of us need wide.

## What's weak

- The "Last chance!" subject runs against the fifth-plus BOGO email in two days. It doesn't feel credible.
- "Long weekend" in the banner doesn't match a Wednesday inbox, and the "mid week mood boost" headline below it makes it worse.
- Almost everything shown is women's boots, heels and flats. Nothing is slip-on, cushioned or arch-supported for a man.
- The only men's link is a small text line, and the lone Brooks shoe has no name or price tag on it.
- The fine print at the bottom is tiny and dense. The "Excludes" list with brands is where I'd expect to find my brands, and I can't read it without zooming.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Brooks Adrenaline in the hero, with the name and price. The preheader already promised it.
- 2. Send men a men's version: walking shoes, slip-ons, and comfort brands like Brooks, Hoka and New Balance, with a "Shop Men" button the same size as "Shop Women."
- 3. Fix the date copy so the banner matches the day, and only say "last chance" when the deal really ends.
- 4. Put the exclusions in plain words near the offer, such as which brands are in or out of the BOGO.
- 5. Cut the send count. One BOGO email a day is plenty.

- **Subject Alt B:** `BOGO 50% off ends Sunday: comfort walkers for men`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Brooks, Hoka and New Balance walking shoes. Buy one, get 50% off a second pair.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wide widths and cushioned slip-ons, now in the BOGO sale.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses Braze-style tracked links (`ablink.dsw.com`) and has one probe-confirmed 400 on the "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" link. Smaller issues are missing alt text on two images, a plain-text part that is mostly tracking URLs, and `<head>` markup gaps. The QA pass rate was 75%.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken link (400):** the "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" CTA returned HTTP 400 when probed.
  - URL: `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/qNk59fwfyxqXQ_Kc72SpnA~~/AAQRxRA~/9uVeb9UiB9DWpw…`. The token is about 700 characters long.
  - A 400 on a redirect-tracker link usually means the token is malformed or truncated, or that the link rejects non-browser probes. The QA data can't tell which, so re-test in a real client.
  - If the link is genuinely broken, the SMS opt-in offer is unreachable.
- **Probe coverage:** only 12 of 78 links were probed and 66 were skipped. The other 66 are unverified, including footer and social links.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb0…/o.gif?mi_u=225090073&mi_ecmp=20261007_P260598_V3_WM-_Offer_MI_B` carries a user ID (`mi_u`) in the query string. This is normal for open tracking, but note that this ID is tied to the recipient.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, tracked image:** `src=https://ablink.dsw.com/q/9XBNettNavdVJK8tiE1dHg~~/AAQRxRA~/tKTZGA4Bu1ESNRPpWCfUpW1FUfByiHc6xhRY0pYA9GTK6n8bY1…` has no alt attribute. If it is a content or CTA image, it is invisible to screen readers and to clients that block images.
- **Missing alt text, `o.gif`:** the tracking pixel should carry `alt=""` and `role="presentation"`. This is minor.
- **Charset meta tag:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it is not a valid charset declaration. Clients that ignore the HTTP header could mis-render special characters, such as the `sup` trademark marks.
- **IE compatibility meta tag:** `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` has no `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, so it does nothing.
- **Language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, which screen readers use to pick pronunciation.
- **Title:** `<title>DSW</title>` is generic. This is minor.
- **Web fonts:** `@font-face` rules load Futura and HelveticaNow from `braze-images.com` with no visible fallback stack in the truncated source. I can't confirm the fallbacks either way.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated HTML shows no unrendered tokens, such as `{{ }}`, `${}` or `%%`.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not verifiable:** no raw headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`) were provided. The unsubscribe link and physical address sit in the truncated footer. QA says an unsubscribe link is always probed, but it lists no result for one.
- **Plain-text part:** 30,930 of 36,085 characters (86%) are URLs. This is a spam-filter risk, because long tracking tokens like the one above inflate the text part. The plain-text version is also likely hard for a person to read.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. Every link is wrapped in an `ablink.dsw.com` redirect, so the final destinations and any UTM parameters aren't visible in the source. The one probed link failed, so its destination couldn't be resolved either.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the SMS opt-in link in a browser. If it fails, regenerate it in the ESP before the next send.
2. Add `alt` text to the `ablink.dsw.com/q/…` image, and add `alt=""` to `o.gif`.
3. Fix the head tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
4. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
5. Trim the plain-text part. Use shorter tracking links, or a hand-written plain-text version with fewer links.
6. For a fuller audit, provide the full HTML (footer and unsubscribe), the raw headers, and the resolved link destinations with UTMs.
