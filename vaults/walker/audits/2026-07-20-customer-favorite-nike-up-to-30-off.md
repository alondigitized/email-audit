# 🚨Customer Favorite Nike up to 30% off🚨​
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Twenty days straight. I said it last week, and I'll say it again — Shoe Carnival shows up in my inbox every single morning like it's got nowhere else to be. That part I've accepted. What I haven't accepted is sending me basically the same Nike 30%-off email two days after the last one. I opened an email from these folks on July 18th that said "Your favorite sale? 30% OFF NIKE" and gave it a solid nod. Today I open this and it's the same pitch — same hero, same percentage, same back-to-school frame.
- The image itself isn't bad. Nike up top, price visible, Crocs section in the middle (which actually caught my eye — those look like the kind of things I'd throw on to walk the dog). But then the email keeps going: Women's & Kids' Sandals at 70% off, School Essentials backpacks and lunchboxes at 25% off. I'm sixty-two with grown kids. I don't need a lunchbox. I need something that won't make my feet ache by 2 in the afternoon.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I already saw the Nike deal on the 18th. Nothing here makes me feel like Shoe Carnival knows who I am or what I'm looking for.

## What's working

- **Nike at 30% off is a real number.** Price-first heroes work on me, and the discount is visible the moment I open it.
- **Crocs "Stand Out!" section.** Crocs are comfort-first slip-ons — that's my lane. Nice that it's in there, even if it got buried mid-scroll.
- **Shoe Perks coupon block.** The $20-off and $10-off coupons at the bottom are the kind of thing I'll take a picture of with my phone before walking into the store.
- **Clean layout, no broken images.** The email renders fine top to bottom — no missing pictures, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Preview text leaked a raw URL.** Before I even opened this thing, my inbox showed "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." — looked like a phishing attempt. Almost didn't open it.
- **Second Nike email in three days.** I already got this pitch on July 18th. Running it again without changing the offer makes me feel like I'm on a list, not a relationship.
- **Women's & Kids' sandals at 70% off.** Completely irrelevant to me. Takes up real estate that could've shown me men's walking shoes.
- **Backpacks and lunchboxes.** School supplies. I'm done with school. I've been done with school for forty years.
- **Young model in the hero.** Kid in Nike gear, back-to-school energy. I don't see myself anywhere in this email.
- **🚨🚨 sirens in the subject line.** That's my nephew's texting style, not how I want to hear from a shoe store.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately.** A raw URL as the preheader is the single biggest trust killer in this send — it reads like spam before the email even opens. Use a line of actual copy there.
- 2. **Retire the identical offer for at least a week.** July 18th was Nike 30% off. July 20th is Nike 30% off. Either escalate the deal (35%? a specific model?) or swap the hero entirely. Repetition without escalation trains me to ignore you.
- 3. **Lead with men's comfort styles within the Nike section.** If you're going to sell me Nike, show me the ones with cushioning — Air Max, React foam, something my podiatrist would approve of. Don't make me hunt.
- 4. **Prune the women's/kids' content for men's segments.** I don't need to see Women's & Kids' Sandals at 70% off. That space could show me a men's walking shoe, or at minimum a comfort-focused sub-header.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Nike 30% off ends soon — comfort styles for men`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your Nike deal: up to 30% off, today only`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Walking shoes, everyday sneakers, and more — shop men's Nike now`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned, supportive, 30% off — men's picks inside`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival / Nike 30% Off (2026-07-20)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Four tracking links fail due to a Unicode zero-width space (`U+200B`) appended to URL strings, rendering them unresolvable by the tracking platform. The campaign token in those URLs also misidentifies this send as `crocs_backpacks_AM`, not a Nike campaign.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space poisoning all pixel/redirect URLs**

All four failing links share the same root cause: a `\u200b` (zero-width space) is appended immediately after the `utm_campaign` value. The QA tool correctly surfaces this as an ASCII codec error at character position 77–78.

Affected URLs (truncated for clarity):
```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260720_crocs_backpacks_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&...​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?p&...​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?f&...​
```

The `​` at the end of each URL is the invisible `U+200B`. This is typically introduced by copy-paste from a rich text source (Word, Slack, Google Docs) into the ESP template editor. The tracking platform (`emltrk.com`) cannot decode these links, meaning open/click events tied to these four endpoints will fail silently.

**WARNING — Campaign token mismatch**

All tracking URLs carry `utm_campaign=SCA_20260720_crocs_backpacks_AM`. The subject line is about Nike footwear. This indicates the template was cloned from a Crocs/backpacks send without updating the campaign parameter. Analytics for this send will be attributed to the wrong campaign in downstream reporting.

**INFO — 26 additional tracking links not probed**

QA skipped HTTP resolution of 26 redirect links (expected behavior for click-tracking domains). No additional broken links confirmed, but the `U+200B` pattern should be checked across all 26 manually before treating them as clean.

Missing `alt=""` on tracking pixels is acceptable (they should use empty alt). Missing alt on product images fails WCAG 2.1 AA (1.1.1 Non-text Content) and degrades inbox preview in image-blocked clients. The two Amplience product images in particular should carry descriptive alt text matching the product name.

**No dark mode support declared:** `color-scheme` meta is set to `light` only (`content="light"`). Images with white backgrounds will look correct, but recipients using dark mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook 2021+) will not get any color inversion hints. Low severity but worth noting if deliverability to Apple Mail is a priority.

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE:** The document declares `HTML 4.01 Transitional`, which is standard for legacy email compatibility. No issue.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`[UNIQUE]` token unresolved in beacon URL:**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

The string `[UNIQUE]` appears literally in the open-tracking beacon. This token should be substituted at send time by the ESP with a per-recipient unique identifier. If it renders literally in production, all open events from this beacon will be collapsed onto a single token, making open-rate data unreliable. Verify ESP substitution is firing correctly for this domain/pixel type.

No other merge tokens detected in the truncated HTML source.
