- Silver Arrows. I had to Google that to confirm — it's the nickname for the Mercedes Formula 1 team. adidas is sending me a fashion collab built around racing culture, and I'll be honest: I don't follow F1, I don't wear racing-inspired street clothes, and the "Legacy Unlocked" headline made me feel like I'd accidentally opened something meant for a 28-year-old who watches Grand Prix on a second monitor.

- **The 30% off offer is hard to miss** — big text, "SUMMER SAVINGS," promo code SVR5 right there. No scavenger hunt, no "click to reveal." That's how you do it.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no junk leaking into the layout. I've seen worse from brands with bigger budgets.
- **Offer context is honest** — "Exclusions apply" is right there in plain sight, not buried in microscopic footer type. That earns a small amount of trust.
- **The sender name is recognizable** — I know adidas, I'm subscribed, so I opened it.

## What's weak

- **"Silver Arrows" means nothing to me without context** — I had no idea this was an F1 thing until I read the body copy. If you're going to drop a niche brand name in the subject line, at least give me a hook in the subject.
- **The hero is a fashion/racing story, not a shoe story** — I came here for footwear. "Legacy Unlocked" with race car imagery is cool if you're the target. I'm not the target.
- **The preview text leaked junk** — "Review in inbox" is the first thing the inbox showed me after the subject. That's dead real estate. No pull, no hook, no reason to open beyond brand recognition alone.
- **No comfort signals anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, wide width, slip-on — zero of those words appear anywhere in this email. They're pitching a lifestyle I don't live.
- **Models and vibe skew young and fashion-forward** — the lifestyle shot in the summer savings section isn't speaking to a 62-year-old man shopping for walkable everyday shoes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 30% off offer in the subject line** — the Silver Arrows collection is the hook for a very specific audience; the discount is the hook for everyone else. Flip the hierarchy.
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader entirely** — "Review in inbox" is a wasted impression. Use that space to reinforce the offer before I even open.
- 3. **Put the summer savings section first** — or at minimum, feature comfort/walking styles in the sale callout. Make me feel like the 30% is on something I'd actually wear.
- 4. **Segment this better** — based on my browsing behavior (I was looking at Ultraboost), a sale email should lead with performance or cushioned walking, not motorsport fashion. ## Technical Audit — adidas "All new Silver Arrows fits"

### 1. Technical Summary

One link in the email contains an unresolvable relative URL path (`/g/`) that the click-tracking layer cannot process, producing a broken destination. The email also has zero plain-text content, which degrades deliverability and violates sender best-practice requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-tracked link**

The QA checker found a link whose `href` resolves to an unknown URL type:

```
href: /g/53713cb8-a07a-499f-9782-b6ab26461c5a?txn=28c7a600-775e-11f1-915b-764a3bc6abf2
wrapped click URL: https://dv.adidas.com/o/53713cb8-a07a-499f-9782-b6ab26461c5a?cp_tp=v3...
```

The `/g/` path is not a recognized scheme; the click-tracking redirect (Marigold/Cheetah) cannot resolve it to a valid destination. The wrapped `dv.adidas.com/o/` URL may itself be dynamic personalization content that renders only in a live session context — if that asset is unavailable or the `txn` token is expired, the link returns an error.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links not HTTP-probed**

All 29 remaining CTAs route through `click.comms.adidas.com` or similar redirect domains and were skipped by the HTTP prober. Destination integrity for those links is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Six instances of the same HTTP image asset**

The arrow/chevron icon `b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` is referenced six times via `http://`:

```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail (since 2023), Apple Mail, and Outlook.com enforce HTTPS for remote images. This asset will be blocked or replaced by a broken-image placeholder in those clients.

**[WARN] Open-tracking pixel served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f2b501e02-bf07d318-1da0-42bd-a795-7f1357f33599-000000/...
```

HTTP tracking pixels are blocked by major clients. Open-rate data for this send will be artificially suppressed.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `53713cb8-a07a-499f-9782-b6ab26461c5a` (dynamic content image, `dv.adidas.com`)
- `_tGgipO3PpZ_T2Sa09uCk3MMbA7TiLCX_Z-xmKDRSnE=452` (open-tracking pixel, `click.comms.adidas.com`)

Screen readers will skip these silently; when images are blocked the first renders as a blank linked area.

**[WARN] Invalid `<meta charset>` declaration**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The correct form is:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, some older Outlook and Lotus Notes renderers may ignore the charset declaration entirely.

**[WARN] Duplicate `@font-face` blocks**

AdihausDIN and AdineuePRO are each declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `src: local(...)` values. This is dead weight that inflates HTML payload but causes no functional rendering failure.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is empty (0 chars)**

CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate plain-text, but a zero-length text part is treated as suspicious by SpamAssassin and similar filters (`MISSING_TEXT` rule). Gmail's spam classifier also weights this negatively. All major ESP deliverability guides (Salesforce, Braze, Klaviyo) flag a missing text part as a compliance risk.

**[INFO] `referrer: no-referrer` suppresses click source data**

```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```

This is a deliberate sender choice, but it means any landing-page analytics that rely on `document.referrer` (vs. UTM params) will show `(direct)` traffic instead of email as the source.

No missing unsubscribe link or sender address was detected in the truncated source (footer compliance elements appeared present based on the CSS class names `.mobile-footer-padding-block`, `privacy`).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `/g/` link (section 2) means at least one CTA leads to a non-functional destination. The 29 remaining click-tracked links could not be probed for UTM parameter presence or landing-page alignment. If UTM params are embedded in the `cp_tp` encoded payload rather than appended as plain query strings, they will be invisible to server-side analytics until the redirect resolves — no issue if that is intentional, but worth confirming GA4/Adobe Analytics is reading them from the decoded destination URL.
