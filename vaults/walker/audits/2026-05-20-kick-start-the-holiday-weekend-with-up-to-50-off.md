# Kick start the holiday weekend with Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Alright, now *this* is more like it — or at least it's trying to be. After eight straight Crocs emails that felt like they were aimed at a college girl on TikTok, I finally see something that could get me to reach for my wallet: "Up to 50% Off!" in big bold letters, tied to Memorial Day weekend. That's a real reason to open an email. The hierarchy is simple enough that I could read it at arm's length without my glasses — headline, product shot, secondary deal. No confusion.
- That said, the one product they chose to show me is a blush-pink clog. Pink. I get that it's a bestseller, but after the Dylan Studded Slide debacle on May 18th and the floral clog push the day before, Crocs is still not connecting the dots that I'm a 62-year-old man shopping for something to wear on my morning walk. The Jibbitz charms section — $3 singles, $7 packs — is a complete waste of my screen space. I don't want decorations on my shoes.
- The "Buy 2, Take 30% Off!" deal buried below the fold is the most interesting offer in the whole email, and it barely gets any real estate. I'd probably click through to the sale page to see if there are any men's styles in the discount pile, but I'm not excited. I wouldn't forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- **The headline discount is real and easy to read** — "Up to 50% Off!" is large, bold, and the first thing my eye lands on. No hunting.
- **Memorial Day hook is timely** — I know the weekend is coming, so the urgency feels earned rather than manufactured.
- **"Buy 2, Take 30% Off!" is actually compelling** — If I need a pair for the house and a pair for the garage, this math works out. It's specific enough to feel like a real deal.
- **No render bugs or broken images** — The email loaded clean, single column, readable text. That matters more than people think for a guy squinting at his phone.
- **"Exclusions Apply" is disclosed right up front** — I've been burned by fine print before; at least they're not hiding it.

## What's weak

- **Pink clog as the hero product** — I'm not the customer for that color. A men's classic in slate or navy would have taken two seconds to swap and made this feel personal.
- **Jibbitz charms section is dead real estate to me** — $3 charms, $7 packs. I'm not decorating my footwear. This takes up a meaningful chunk of the email and I skipped right past it.
- **No CTA button anywhere** — The image is probably clickable, but there's no visible "Shop the Sale" button. I shouldn't have to guess where to tap.
- **Preheader is garbage** — Whatever's showing up in my inbox preview before I open is a wall of invisible characters. A wasted opportunity to reinforce the Memorial Day discount.
- **Eight emails in two weeks is too many** — I know that's not visible inside this email, but by the time I opened this one I was already fatigued. The welcome email on May 12th at 7/10 was the high point; everything since has felt like noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero product to a men's style in a neutral colorway** — Show me the Classic Clog in slate, navy, or khaki. Same sale, same copy, immediately more relevant to me.
- 2. **Add a visible "Shop Men's Sale" button** — A big, tappable button right below the hero image. Don't make me tap a product photo and hope.
- 3. **Fix the preheader** — Those invisible zero-width characters are embarrassing and waste the best real estate in the inbox besides the subject line.
- 4. **Lead with "Buy 2, Take 30% Off!" as the secondary call-out, not Jibbitz** — That deal appeals to the practical buyer in me. Jibbitz doesn't.
- 5. **Trim email frequency** — One strong sale email beats four mediocre ones. If I'm getting eight emails in two weeks, the 50% off news doesn't land with the impact it deserves.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy 2 and save 30% more — no code needed, discount in cart`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Holiday weekend sale starts now — exclusions apply`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 7 HTTP image URLs (footer block):**

All 7 are served from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:

| Image | Filename |
|---|---|
| App Store badge | `9c36c0ef-333c-4e6c-bec8-9e0ac799d475.png` |
| Google Play badge | `13246762-1111-4174-bca8-2f84a6bf62a5.png` |

| Facebook | `80f68cd8-0090-4258-b202-50e422ad1b5c.png` |
| TikTok | `51d8144e-818a-4a00-a812-5cf0429b2429.png` |
| YouTube | `1187ef58-07fe-4985-8349-93e338d86694.png` |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook enforce HTTPS-only for remote images. These will render as broken image icons for a substantial share of the list.

**Malformed charset meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Risk is low in modern clients but can cause encoding fallback in legacy Outlook.

**Body-scoped `<style>` block:** The `.preheader { display: none !important; }` rule is injected inside `<body>` rather than `<head>`. Gmail strips `<head>` styles but retains body-level styles, so this is intentional and correct for Gmail — no issue.

**Open-pixel alt text missing:**
```html
<img style="visibility:hidden;display:none" height="1" width="1"
  src="https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?...">
```
The image is non-visible by design, so missing `alt=""` has no user-facing impact, but it will trigger accessibility linters. Add `alt=""` to suppress false positives.

**Custom font (`TT Crocs`) via `@font-face`:** Source URLs are HTTPS and all three weights (400/700/900) declared with `font-display: swap`. Fallback behavior is acceptable — no action needed, but note Outlook desktop will ignore entirely.
