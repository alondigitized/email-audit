# Deeper markdowns
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- "Deeper markdowns" — okay, HOKA, you've got my attention for about half a second. I've been getting these emails almost every other day lately, and after that May 12th send that actually spoke to me about plush cushioning, this one feels like a step backward. The Summer Kickoff Sale banner is clean, the shoe photo is fine, but what I want to know is *how much* off and *which shoes* — and this email doesn't tell me either. Just "deeper markdowns on some of our most popular styles." That's not a deal, that's a tease.
- The free expedited shipping section is the second pitch in the email, and by the time I get there, I find out I have to sign up for texts *and* it only applies to my first full-price order. That's two conditions I have to read carefully to understand — on a phone, in smaller print — and it makes the whole thing feel like a bait-and-switch.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe check the sale page later out of habit, but I'm not clicking today. Nothing here pulled me toward a specific shoe that fits my needs, and there's no price to compare against.

## What's working

- **Sale language is timely.** "Summer Kickoff Sale" ties to the long weekend — that's relevant and seasonal, not stale.
- **"Shop Sale" button is clear and easy to tap.** One big button, not buried — I can find it without hunting.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Logo, headline, shoe photo, CTA — the eye follows the layout without confusion.
- **The shoe photo is a solid image.** Renders well, no broken images, looks sharp.

## What's weak

- **No actual discount number.** "Deeper markdowns" means nothing without a percentage or a dollar amount. I'm a guy who does the price math — give me 30% off or $40 off, don't make me guess.
- **Wrong shoe for me.** That green runner in the hero is a performance road shoe. I'm not training for a 5K — I want to know if the Bondi or Clifton is marked down.
- **Free shipping comes with a catch.** Sign up for texts *and* it's only on the first full-price order. That's buried in small print below the bold headline. That's not a gift, that's a signup bribe with fine print.
- **High cadence fatigue is real.** This is somewhere around my eighth HOKA email in twelve days. Even when the offer is solid, that pace starts to feel like noise.
- **No mention of comfort, cushioning, or fit.** Zero. That's the only reason I'd cross-shop HOKA at all — and this email never says the word.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real number in the offer.** "Up to 30% off" or "Clifton 9 — now $109" would make this email worth opening. "Deeper markdowns" is not actionable.
- 2. **Feature a comfort-category shoe in the hero.** Swap the performance runner for the Bondi or Clifton — something that looks like a walking shoe, not a race flat.
- 3. **Simplify the free shipping offer.** Either it's free shipping on the sale order or it isn't. The text-signup condition with a full-price restriction is too complicated for one email.
- 4. **Pull back on email cadence.** Two sends a week is plenty. Eight in twelve days is the kind of thing that earns an unsubscribe.
- 5. **Add one comfort-specific detail.** One line: "Extra cushioning. All-day support." That's the hook for someone like me.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably desktop Outlook, Apple Mail) surface the title in the window or notification. An empty value is a missed opportunity and can surface as blank in title-bar previews.

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tags:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
Two conflicting viewport declarations. The second also contradicts `maximum-scale=1` by adding `user-scalable=yes`. The second tag should be removed.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some legacy clients (Outlook 2007–2016) may not honor the charset declaration.

**Malformed CSS — `.nav_padding`:**
```css
.nav_padding {padding-top:px !important;padding-bottom:px !important;}
```
Missing numeric values before `px`. This rule is invalid and will be ignored by all clients, potentially causing layout collapse on mobile.

**Malformed CSS — `.nav_border`:**
```css
.nav_border {border-top:px solid ;}
```
Missing border-width value and color value. Invalid rule, silently ignored.

**`outline:none` on all elements:**
```css
* { outline:none; }
```
Removes keyboard focus indicators globally, which is a WCAG 2.1 Level AA failure (Success Criterion 2.4.7). While email keyboard navigation is limited, this is a blanket accessibility regression.

**Web font fallback — not visible in this excerpt:** The `@font-face` declarations load `TheFutureHOKA` from hoka.com. Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all Windows versions), and Samsung Mail block external font loading. The actual `font-family` stack used on text elements is not visible in the truncated source — if system-font fallbacks are absent on those declarations, text will render in the client default (typically serif on some clients).
