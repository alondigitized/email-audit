# Your $10 disappears tonight!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Seven. By my count, this is the seventh time DSW has hit me with some version of this same ten-dollar offer in the past three days. At this point, "Your $10 disappears tonight!" doesn't feel like urgency — it feels like the boy who cried wolf. I remember the "LAST DAY: $10 OFF!" email earlier today, which came after the "TILL MIDNIGHT!" one this morning. DSW has basically trained me to ignore their deadlines.
- And then I open this thing and I'm greeted with a full wall of women's heels, pumps, and T-strap shoes. Scroll down a little — there's a size selector that starts at 5 and 5.5. Then a "SHOP WIDE CALF" button. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for something comfortable to put on my feet. Nothing about that product grid is talking to me. There's a tiny little "SHOP MEN" text link buried above the grid, but I almost missed it in all the noise.
- To be fair, the $10 off with code POOLDAY is clear and the VIP section at the bottom reminds me I'm a member — I like that. But I'm not clicking today. I'm not even annoyed enough to unsubscribe. I'll just scroll past it like I've been scrolling past this same deal all week.

## What's working

- **Offer clarity is rock solid** — "$10 off" with code POOLDAY on regular and clearance prices is spelled out right at the top, no hunting required.
- **VIP card section is a good touch** — Seeing my loyalty card with the barcode reminds me I'm a known customer, not just another email address.
- **"SHOP WIDE WIDTH" button is relevant** — That's actually useful for guys like me who need extra room, and it's a detail most shoe emails skip.
- **No render problems** — Clean layout, images loaded, text is readable without squinting on my phone. That's not nothing.

## What's weak

- **Seventh time this week on the same offer** — "Disappears tonight" means nothing when I've heard "last day" twice already from the same sender in the past 24 hours. The urgency is completely burned out.
- **Product grid is almost entirely women's shoes** — Heels, pumps, kitten heels, ankle boots. The "BRAND NEW...AND SO YOU" section does not include me.
- **Size selector starting at 5 and 5.5** — Those are women's sizes. A man scrolling this on his phone is going to look at that row and feel like he's in the wrong aisle.
- **"SHOP WIDE CALF" is right next to "SHOP WIDE WIDTH"** — Fine for women's shoppers, but pairing them side by side without separation makes the whole section feel like it's aimed at women's boot shoppers, not me.
- **No comfort signal anywhere** — Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or walking comfort. That's how I actually decide what to buy. Could've been a line in the hero or even just one men's comfort pick as a hero product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the cadence dramatically** — One "last chance" email, not three. Every redundant send chips away at the credibility of the next one.
- 2. **Serve men a men's grid** — If DSW knows I'm a male VIP member, the "BRAND NEW...AND SO YOU" section should show men's casual and walking shoes, not a wall of heels.
- 3. **Add one comfort-forward men's hero product** — A single featured shoe with a line like "Built for all-day walking" would've stopped my scroll.
- 4. **Lead the men's CTA into the hero button** — Make "SHOP MEN" the main dark button, not a footnote text link above the product grid.
- 5. **Move the size selector to start at men's sizes** — Or at minimum show two parallel rows, one clearly labeled "Men's."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `http-equiv` on two meta tags:**

```html
<!-- Actual -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- Should be -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, these directives are ignored by the parser. The charset declaration in particular will not be honored, which can cause encoding failures on some clients if the Content-Type header is absent or mismatched.

**Issue — Non-standard CSS shorthand order:**

```css
border-top: 1px #000001 solid   /* incorrect order */
border-top: 1px solid #000001   /* correct: width style color */
```

Strict CSS parsers (notably some Outlook versions) may drop the rule entirely.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector:**

```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: none; text-size-adjust: none; }
```

`none` disables all automatic text resizing, which can violate WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) for users who rely on mobile browser zoom. The safer value is `100%` (used on the webkit prefixed variant in the same block — the final `text-size-adjust: none` overrides it).

**Minor — `Src:` capitalisation in `@font-face`:**

```css
Src: url("...") format('woff');
```

CSS property names are technically case-insensitive so this parses correctly, but it is non-standard and will cause linter failures. All ten `@font-face` blocks are affected.

**Minor — Spurious `supported-color-schemes` in `:root` CSS:**

```css
:root { color-scheme: light dark; supported-color-schemes: light dark; }
```

`supported-color-schemes` is valid only in the `<meta>` tag (where it appears correctly). In CSS it is an unknown property and will be silently ignored by all clients. No functional impact, but it adds noise.
