# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- **From:** SKECHERS | **Subject:** Did you forget something?
- Okay so this one's personal — Skechers is nudging me about something I left in my cart. I actually like that. The shoe they're reminding me about is the Max Cushioning Glide-Step Slip-ins in blue, which is exactly the kind of thing I shop for. Good start. But then the email goes sideways. The six shoes they show me below the hero are almost entirely women's styles. I'm a 62-year-old guy — I don't need to see women's sneakers in my cart reminder. The 20% off deal caught my eye and made me want to act, but then they frame it around Mother's Day, which feels like I walked into the wrong aisle at the store. Email does the right thing at the top, then loses me in the middle.
- The cart recovery mechanic is working, and the featured shoe is right. But the women-heavy product grid and the Mother's Day angle weaken the targeting for a male customer. This could easily be a 8 with a men's-only recommendation row.

## What's working

- **The hero is spot-on.** The blue Max Cushioning Glide-Step Slip-ins are front and center. That's the shoe I was looking at. Big image, clear name, bright orange "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button — I know exactly what they want me to do. No confusion there.
- **20% off is a real hook.** I'm a Skechers Plus member and I'm always watching for a deal. The black banner with 20% OFF in big white letters jumped out at me. That's the kind of thing that turns a browse into a purchase.
- **Simple navigation.** The category row — WOMEN, MEN, KIDS, CLOTHING — is easy to read. Even on my phone I can tap "MEN" without squinting.
- **Curbside pickup notice is useful.** I like knowing I can pick it up locally if I don't want to wait for shipping.

## What's weak

- **The product grid below the hero is mostly women's shoes.** I count at least four women's styles in those six slots. This is my cart reminder email. Why am I being shown Women's Bobs and Women's Arch Fit sneakers? That's not personalized — that's just filler.
- **Mother's Day framing lands wrong for me as the buyer.** The 20% off is tied to "Mother's Day Sale" in the fine print. I'm not shopping for my wife here — I'm shopping for myself. That framing doesn't make me feel like they know me.
- **Two "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" buttons feel pushy.** One above the product, one below it. I get it, I left something in my cart. You don't need to ask me twice in the same scroll.
- **The texting promo is noise.** "Text SKECHERS to 83808" — I'm not signing up for text messages in the middle of trying to buy shoes. That's clutter.
- **Very small fine print at the bottom.** The Mother's Day Sale terms are in tiny text. I'd need readers or to zoom in. That's annoying.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the product grid for men's recommendations only.** If they know I left a men's shoe in my cart, show me more men's shoes — especially other Slip-ins or Max Cushioning styles. Don't show me women's styles.
- 2. **Drop the Mother's Day angle for male segments.** Either remove it or reframe as "Treat yourself" — something that works for me as the shopper.
- 3. **Consolidate to one "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA.** One strong button is enough. Two feels like nagging.
- 4. **Move the 20% off higher or tie it directly to my cart item.** Something like "Your Slip-ins are waiting — grab 20% off today" would feel personal.
- 5. **Pull the text signup from this email.** It's a distraction in a cart recovery message. Save that pitch for a different send.
