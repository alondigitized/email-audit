# All in on adidas 🙌

## Executive summary

- Adidas Samba on a young woman in a mini skirt — that's the whole email in one image, and it tells me right away this wasn't built with a guy my age in mind. I've been getting Famous Footwear emails almost daily this summer, and this one swaps the usual BOGO grab-bag for an entire adidas showcase: Sambas, Forums, retro court shoes, a Mary Jane sneaker. Not one arch-support callout, not one "cushioned" or "walking" word anywhere.
- The BOGO banner and 2X points strip at the top is the exact same purple template I've now seen more times than I can count — same shape, same "In-store & online" fine print underneath. At least this time they didn't stack five more banners under it like some of their other sends.
- I'd scroll past this one without clicking. Nothing here is a shoe I'd put on my feet, and nothing tells me why today's the day to act. Straight to "seen it, skip it" — not one I'd forward to anybody.

## What's working

- The BOGO + 2X points banner is legible and sits right up top where my eye lands first.
- "Up to 25% off" with the adidas logo further down is a clean, easy-to-read secondary offer.
- The $10 Reward callout for store pickup is a nice loyalty touch if I were actually buying something here.
- Layout's clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, nothing glitchy.

## What's weak

- Every single shoe shown — Samba, Forum, Barreda Mary Jane, Retro 76 — is a fashion sneaker, not a comfort or walking shoe. None of it is for me.
- The hero model is a young woman in a skirt. I don't relate to that at all, and it tells me this send wasn't segmented by who's actually opening it.
- "Back-to-school BOGO" — I'm 62, nobody in my house is going back to school. That framing just isn't for me.
- It's the same BOGO/2X points banner I've seen from this brand five or six times this summer already. At some point it just becomes wallpaper I scroll past.
- Not one word about arch support, cushioning, or all-day comfort anywhere in this email — the stuff that actually gets me to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this one out for me entirely — if a customer's browsing/purchase history skews comfort or walking shoes, don't send an all-adidas-fashion email. Send me the comfort-brand version of this BOGO deal instead.
- 2. Swap the hero model and lead product for something a guy my age would actually wear — a slip-on or low-top comfort sneaker, not a mini-skirt streetwear shot.
- 3. Retire or refresh the BOGO banner art — it's the same purple block I've clicked past for weeks now. Even a new color or new headline would help.
- 4. Put a real offer or product hint in the subject line instead of just the brand name — give me a reason tied to my category.

## Technical Audit: Famous Footwear — "All in on adidas" Email

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- QA confirms 54 tracking links were skipped for HTTP probing (click-redirect domains), so live-link/404 validation is incomplete for this send — recommend a manual click-through pass before/after send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed charset declaration**: `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` (or a bare `charset="UTF-8"` attribute). As written, this tag is not a valid charset declaration and may be ignored by some clients, risking encoding issues (e.g., the 🙌 emoji in the subject line, curly quotes, etc.).
- **Invalid CSS property**: `lineapacing:20px` in the footer-adjacent `<td>` style block (`padding-top:10px;font-size:14px;lineapacing:20px;padding-bottom:1px;...`) is not a real CSS property — likely a typo for `line-height`. It will be silently dropped by renderers, so intended vertical spacing in that block won't apply.
- Responsive breakpoints at 480px/640px are present and cover standard fluid-image/hidden-column patterns (`.fluid`, `.responsive-td`, `.mobile-hidden`) — no issues found here.
- **Missing `alt` text**: QA flagged 24 image instances (16 unique assets, including the header logo `3b6a7dc1...png` and the hairline divider `4ccff4ad...jpg`) with empty/missing `alt`. The logo is wrapped in a link with no accessible text anywhere in the anchor, so screen-reader users get no label for that link.
