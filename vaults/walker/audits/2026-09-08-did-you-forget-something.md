# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- Well, would you look at this — that pink floral D'Lites is back, big as life, right under a gold "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner. This makes it five times this week I've seen this exact sneaker in my inbox, and I'll say it again: I have never clicked on it, never browsed it, wouldn't wear a pink floral shoe with vines on it if you paid me. Whatever "cart abandonment" logic is running here has me confused with somebody else, or it just doesn't care.
- Buried further down there's actually something useful — men's slip-ins, an arch-fit sneaker, a relaxed-fit style — and a real "20% OFF sale styles" offer with a code for Plus members. That's the stuff that would get my attention if it led the email instead of hiding under a shoe I never asked about.
- I'm not clicking on the hero, I'm scrolling past the fake urgency, and I'm not forwarding this to anybody. If I open anything, it's the small "Men's Slip-ins" thumbnail near the bottom, and only because I happened to keep scrolling out of habit.

## What's working

- The 20% off member offer with a real code (EXTRA20) and a "valid thru 9/14" date is something I can actually use.
- There's a men's shoe grid further down — slip-ins, arch fit, relaxed fit — that's closer to what I'd actually shop for.
- "Complete Your Order" button is big, high-contrast, easy to tap on a phone.
- Text is large enough to read without zooming.

## What's weak

- That pink floral D'Lites sneaker leading the email again — fifth time this week — and I've never so much as glanced at it. It's not my size, style, or gender, and it makes the whole "we noticed you" pitch feel fake.
- The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" hero implies I left something in a cart, which isn't true — that's the same guilt-trip trick from the last few sends, just reworded.
- Nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support called out up top — that's the stuff that actually sells me, and it's stuck in small print on product names way down the page.
- No mention of my Skechers points balance or member status at the top the way Wednesday's "Last Chance Labor Day" email did — that one felt more like they knew who I was.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake cart-abandon framing entirely and stop recycling the pink floral D'Lites — if I haven't clicked it in five sends, the algorithm should've dropped it by now. Lead with a shoe from my actual browsing or purchase history.
- 2. Move the 20% off member offer to the very top, above the fold, since that's the one thing in this email with real value.
- 3. Put a men's comfort shoe — arch fit or slip-in — in the hero spot instead of a floral sneaker, with a line about cushioning or all-day comfort.
- 4. Drop the gold-foil "big reveal" treatment on a banner that isn't actually revealing anything — it reads like a discount ad, not a personal reminder.
