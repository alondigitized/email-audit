# OG Nexus member pre-order opportunity!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- I open this one and — once again — my inbox preview slot is filled with that same wall of raw computer gibberish. At this point I've counted at least seven or eight Skechers emails in a row with that broken code sitting where the preview copy should be. I've stopped being surprised and started being genuinely irritated. It's one thing to slip up once; at this point it's just negligence.
- Inside the email the execution is actually clean. But here's the thing — I'm 62 years old, I shop for cushioned walking shoes and slip-ons that don't murder my arches, and Skechers just sent me a basketball player edition sneaker named after a "legendary tip-in." I don't follow the NBA. I don't know what an OG Nexus is. The hero image is a young man launching off a basketball court against a city skyline. I'm looking at that picture trying to find any version of myself in it, and I can't. This is a sneakerhead email that landed in a comfort-walker's inbox.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to any friend I have. I'd mark it read and keep scrolling, mildly annoyed that my "member pre-order opportunity" turned out to be for a shoe I'd never buy.

## What's working

- **Member callout is real.** The body copy explicitly says "additional opportunity for our Skechers Plus members" — that's the one signal that treats me like a known customer, not a cold lead.
- **CTA button is unmissable.** That orange "PRE-ORDER NOW" button pops off the dark hero. No hunting for where to tap.
- **Visual hierarchy is solid.** Logo → hero image → body copy → product shot → button. Eye goes exactly where it's supposed to.
- **No render bugs.** Unlike the preview, the email body itself displays cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything loads.
- **Product is specifically named.** "SKX Nexus NYC Blue Player Edition" tells me exactly what this is, even if it's not for me.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is broken. Again.** Raw JSON schema code, for what I now count as the eighth consecutive Skechers send. This isn't a typo — it's a systemic failure that nobody has bothered to fix.
- **Wrong shoe, wrong man.** This is a basketball court statement piece. I wear Skechers Go Walk. I want arch support, not "Championship Style."
- **The hero model isn't me.** Young athlete mid-jump against a city skyline. I'm looking for something that says "comfortable enough for a 5-hour day on your feet." Nothing here does that.
- **No price anywhere.** Not even a ballpark. I'm being asked to pre-order a shoe without knowing what it costs. That's a hard stop for me.
- **"OG" language.** I know what "OG" means, barely — my grandkids use it — but leading with it in a subject line aimed at a 62-year-old Skechers Plus member who buys Go Walks is a mismatch in every direction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text. This is the most important thing.** Seven-plus emails with raw JSON in the preview slot is embarrassing for a brand this size. Fix it before the next send, full stop. Every other improvement on this list is secondary.
- 2. **Segment this to your sneakerhead list, not your comfort-walker list.** If my purchase history is all walking shoes and slip-ons, I should never see a basketball player edition email. Send this to people who actually buy athletic performance footwear.
- 3. **Put the price in the email.** "Pre-order now" with no number tells me nothing. Even "Starting at $X" gives me something to hold onto.
- 4. **Find a model who looks like your Plus member base.** A hero image with a 55–65-year-old man walking confidently would have landed differently. This one bounced off me.
- 5. **Rework the subject for your comfort audience** — if you must send to them, at least speak their language.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members only: New Skechers drop — be first in line`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Plus access: exclusive pre-order just opened`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First pre-order sold out fast — here's your second chance as a Plus member.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This one's just for Plus members. Grab it before it's gone again.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "OG Nexus Member Pre-Order" (2026-07-15)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound and renders across breakpoints, but has a systemic HTTP-over-HTTPS image problem affecting 11 assets, a broken Return Path deliverability pixel (missing protocol), and third-party data-match pixels that warrant privacy disclosure scrutiny.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken Return Path pixel — missing protocol prefix**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
The `src` has no `http://` or `https://` prefix. Email clients will treat this as a relative URL and it will fail silently. This breaks inbox placement monitoring.

**ink1000.com open pixel served over HTTP**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8107766165&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NA_U_PREORDER_LOYL_EN_07152026
```
Plain HTTP will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS 15+), and Outlook.com when image proxying is active.

**25 click-redirect links skipped by QA** — standard for ESP tracking; no issues inferred, but destination UTM coverage is unverified (see §6).

**Three Krux/Salesforce DMP match pixels present:**
```
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif — two variants (SHA256 hash + raw subid)
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif
```
Fires on open; subscriber hash (`partner_uid=9153f33f...`) and raw ID (`partner_uid=121173391`) are both sent to a third-party ad network. See §5.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images served over HTTP** (`image.emails.skechers.com` domain). Gmail and most modern clients proxy or block mixed-content images. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, a content image (no alt), App Store badge, Google Play badge, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons.

**Missing alt text on two images:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — low severity for a 1×1 pixel
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — unknown content image; if this is a product/hero image, missing alt text is a screen reader failure

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** restricts user zoom on mobile — accessibility concern for visually impaired users.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** in global CSS disables browser font scaling; this overrides user accessibility preferences.

**Duplicate breakpoint CSS blocks** — `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` define identical rule sets in two separate `<style>` blocks. Functionally redundant; adds ~1.5KB to payload.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Subject line targets "OG Nexus members," implying list segmentation rather than inline merge tokens. The truncated HTML shows no unrendered `%%TOKEN%%` or `{{token}}` syntax in the visible portion — no broken merge fields detected.

Raw subscriber ID `121173391` appears to be rendered correctly into the Krux pixel `partner_uid` param (not a broken token).

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / CASL:** Cannot confirm physical mailing address or unsubscribe link presence from the truncated source — both are required. These are standard Skechers footer elements and likely present below the truncation point, but should be verified against the full render.

**Third-party data sharing — potential disclosure gap:**
The Krux `usermatch` pixels fire a SHA256-hashed email and a raw numeric subscriber ID to `beacon.krxd.net` (Salesforce/Krux DMP) on open, before any user interaction. This is behavioral data shared with a third party. Confirm this is covered by Skechers' privacy policy and email consent language.

**Authentication headers:** Cannot be verified from HTML source alone. Given the sending domain `emails.skechers.com`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment should be confirmed via header inspection of a received copy.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `MKG_US_NA_U_PREORDER_LOYL_EN_07152026` is consistently applied across all tracking pixels — good.

UTM parameter coverage on the 25 click-redirect links (`click.emails.skechers.com`) cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML. QA skipped these due to redirect domain detection. Spot-check 3–5 destination URLs to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are appended after redirect resolution.
