## Executive summary

- Eight emails in eight days, and Shoe Carnival is back to Back-to-School — same pivot they pulled two days ago with "Early Back-to-School Deals Start NOW." At least the $10 off offer is right at the top where I can see it, and I'll give them credit for that. But everything below that offer is family athletic shoes, kid-friendly Nikes, Asics, and New Balance — none of which have anything to do with why I open Shoe Carnival emails. But by the time I scroll past three back-to-school banners and a family athletics sale, I've mentally checked out. The offer is buried where most people my age have already scrolled away.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and move on, hoping tomorrow's send has something more relevant — though at eight days straight, I'm starting to wonder if unsubscribing is the smarter move.

- **$10 off hero offer is front and center** — I see it without scrolling, the threshold ($74.99+) is right there, and the "Shop Now" button is obvious. That's how you open an email.

## What's weak

- **Back-to-School is not my season** — I said this after the July 6 send and I'll say it again. I don't have kids in school. Two back-to-school pushes inside three days, and neither one has anything for me.
- **Every featured shoe is a kid or family athletic shoe** — The hero Nikes, the family athletics section, the Asics — none of these are the comfort-cushion walking shoes I care about. Demographic miss.
- **The preview text is a tracking URL** — What showed in my inbox next to the subject line was raw link garbage. That is the digital equivalent of a butt dial. It looks broken, and it is.
- **Eight emails in eight days** — The cadence has crossed from "active loyalty program" into "please stop." No single email can overcome that kind of fatigue.
- **Brooks is buried** — The one product I might actually click is at the very bottom, after I've already decided this email isn't for me.

- 1. **Lead with Brooks, not kids' Nike** — If there's a comfort brand deal in this email, make it the hero. Move the $89.98 Brooks price break to the top section, right under the $10 off offer. That's the one thing that could keep a 60-something scrolling.
- 2. **Separate the back-to-school sends from the comfort-walking sends** — These are two different customer segments. If Shoe Carnival has segmentation data, use it. I shouldn't be getting a kids' backpack-season email.
- 3. **Fix the preheader immediately** — A tracking URL leaking into the preview text is an embarrassment. The inbox impression is what decides the open; right now it actively undermines the subject line.
- 4. **Give the offer a deadline** — "Early Access" implies there's a window, but I can't see when it closes. A specific date would push me to act rather than defer.
- 5. **Dial back the cadence** — Skip a day. The 9th email is less valuable than the 7th; every send from here erodes trust.

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Early Access Back-to-School" (2026-07-08)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two critical send-blocking defects exist: a zero-width space character (U+200B) corrupts five tracking URLs, and an unresolved `[UNIQUE]` merge token causes two redirect/unsubscribe links to time out. Accessibility coverage is also incomplete with five images lacking alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in campaign parameter (5 URLs)**

A U+200B character is appended to the campaign name in all `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com` links:

```
...utm_campaign=SCA_20260708_nike_athletics_brooks_AM​
                                                    ^-- U+200B here
```

Positions 83–85 (varies by endpoint: `?rd`, `?rl`, `?p`, `?f`, `?i`). This causes an ASCII codec error in the tracker's URL parser and likely silently drops click attribution or returns a 400. The character probably entered the template via a copy-paste from a rich-text tool.

**CRITICAL — Unresolved `[UNIQUE]` merge token (2 URLs, both timeout)**

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?rd&d=[UNIQUE]
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?rl&d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` is a literal string — recipient-specific identifier was never substituted. These links timed out in QA probes, which is consistent with the tracker rejecting the malformed token. The `?rd` endpoint typically handles read-receipt and `?rl` is often the unsubscribe redirect — if the latter is broken, unsubscribe flow fails (CAN-SPAM risk; see §5).

**INFO — 25 click-redirect links skipped**

These are redirect-chain URLs; skip is expected behavior for the probe. No action required, but they should be manually spot-checked in a seed inbox.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 3 content images (WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1)**

| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| Product (SKU 151900_398971) | `amplience.net/s/scvl/151900_398971_SET/1` |
| Product (SKU 180756_399002) | `amplience.net/s/scvl/180756_399002_SET/1` |
| Banner/creative | `image.email.shoecarnival.com/…/465aca37-a584-4d52-a5a1-61002d176bb3.png` |

The two tracking pixel `<img>` tags (ieezzy5m, 0di86k1v) lack `alt=""` as well, but those should use `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress screen reader announcement — minor fix.

**Meta tag defects**

- `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"` — the attribute is ignored without it.
- `<meta content="IE=edge" />` is missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` — same issue; IE conditional targeting is inert.

**Dark mode: suppressed**

`<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />` forces light-only rendering. No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` media query is present. Not a defect per se, but recipients in system dark mode will see forced-white backgrounds with no override.

**DOCTYPE/namespace mismatch**

Declared as HTML 4.01 Transitional (`<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN"`) but includes `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` on `<html>`. Low severity — most clients ignore this — but it can trigger quirks parsing in edge cases.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe)

**HIGH RISK — Unsubscribe link may be broken**

The `?rl` endpoint on `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` (which timed out) is the pattern used by several ESPs for the unsubscribe redirect chain. If this is the one-click unsubscribe path, the broken `[UNIQUE]` token means recipients cannot unsubscribe — a direct CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) violation and an RFC 8058 (List-Unsubscribe-Post) failure.

Verify: does `?rl` resolve to the unsubscribe page? If yes, this is a must-fix before any further sends.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link presence in the email body could not be verified from the truncated HTML — these must be confirmed in the full render.

Sending domain is `email.shoecarnival.com`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment should be verified against the sending infrastructure; not verifiable from HTML alone.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

The campaign identifier `SCA_20260708_nike_athletics_brooks_AM` is embedded in tracking URLs via a non-standard format:

```
?t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260708_nike_athletics_brooks_AM
```

This passes the entire `utm_campaign=…` string as the value of parameter `t`, rather than as a standalone query parameter. Whether the tracker correctly parses and forwards this as a UTM parameter to the destination URL depends on the tracker's implementation — confirm that GA4 / analytics is receiving `utm_campaign` correctly on click-through, not just `t`.

Additionally, the U+200B defect in the campaign name (§2) means even if the tracker does parse it, the campaign value passed downstream will contain a garbage character, potentially creating a phantom campaign entry in analytics.
