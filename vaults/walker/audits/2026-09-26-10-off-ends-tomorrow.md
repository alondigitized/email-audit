# $10 off ends TOMORROW
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- At least the "$10, $20, $60 off" tiered offer is dead simple to understand — that's a step up from another vague clearance banner. But I scroll down and it's the same story as every DSW email this month: wall-to-wall women's shoes — pointy heels, strappy sandals, patent flats — three, four rows deep before anything remotely unisex shows up. The "shop by size" grid tops out at 11, which tells me exactly who they think their customer is, and it's not a 62-year-old guy with wide feet looking for arch support.
- The tiered discount is genuinely decent if I were buying boots for my wife, and I'll give them credit for the "brand new" new-arrivals section instead of just recycled clearance copy again. But there's nothing here for me — no men's category shown at all in the product grid, no comfort or walking shoe angle, nothing about cushioning. I'm not clicking, and I'm sure not forwarding it unless it's to my wife. Straight to "read and ignored."

## What's working

- The tiered $10/$20/$60 off banner up top is clear and easy to read at a glance — no squinting required.
- One clean promo code (COZYUP) instead of five different codes for five different sales, which I appreciate.
- The "Buy Now Pay Later with Afterpay" and free shipping icons near the bottom are useful info, even if I have to scroll forever to find them.

## What's weak

- Every single product in the "Brand New" grid — twelve items — is women's footwear. Heels, strappy sandals, embellished flats, tall boots. Not one men's shoe in sight.
- The size grid tops out at 11 with half sizes, which is a women's sizing range — doesn't apply to me at all.
- The Hispanic Heritage Month banner is a nice cause, but it's dropped in the middle of a discount email with zero connection to the offer, which feels tacked-on and confusing.
- Nothing about comfort, arch support, or slip-on styles anywhere in this email — the stuff I'd actually stop scrolling for.
- I've now seen "up to 60% off" and tiered dollar-off codes from these guys nearly every day this week (per my last few opens) — the offers blur together at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment men's shoppers into a men's-only version of this email — show me comfort walking shoes or boots, not heels and sandals I'll never buy.
- 2. Move the "shop men" link (if it exists somewhere) up near the top instead of burying men's category entirely out of the visual grid.
- 3. Add one line of comfort-specific copy — "cushioned, easy on-off, all-day comfort" — since that's what actually gets me to click, not a dollar amount.
- 4. Drop the Hispanic Heritage Month block from a discount-code email or give it its own separate send — mixing a cause message into a "claim $10 off" email undercuts both.
- 5. Vary the subject line language — I've had a stack of near-identical DSW promo emails this week and this one doesn't stand out.
