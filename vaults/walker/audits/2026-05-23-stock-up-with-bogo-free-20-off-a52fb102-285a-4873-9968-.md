# Stock up with BOGO Free +$20 Off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Nine days, at least eight emails from Shoe Carnival — I know what the layout looks like before it even loads. And sure enough, same red-and-yellow "Inflation Relief" banner, same stacked offer structure. This one leads with $20 off $99.88+ and a big BOGO Free headline, which sounds appealing until you read the small print: BOGO Free is for **Women's & Kids' Sandals only.** I'm a 62-year-old man. Every product image below that banner is a strappy women's sandal or a little girl walking on grass. The "TOO CUTE!" callout on the child photo made me feel like I accidentally opened my daughter-in-law's email.
- The $20 off code (MAYO5) does appear to apply sitewide at $99.88+, so there's technically something in here for me — but I'd have to go digging for men's shoes on my own after the email pointed me entirely somewhere else. The Shoe Perks $10 reward at the bottom is a nice touch and the only part that felt like it was talking to a returning customer.
- I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward — unless I was shopping for a grandkid.

## What's working

- The $20 off offer is concrete and upfront — dollar amount, minimum spend, and coupon code all visible without scrolling.
- "BOGO Free" in the subject line is the kind of hard number that actually makes me tap to look, even if the payoff disappointed.
- Shoe Perks loyalty block at the bottom reminded me I have a $10 reward sitting there — that's the one nudge that almost got me.
- Layout is clean, no broken images, no overlapping text — renders fine on a phone, which is how I'm reading this.
- Memorial Day framing makes the sale feel timely and credible rather than manufactured.

## What's weak

- Every product featured is women's or kids' sandals — not a single men's shoe in the scroll.
- "TOO CUTE!" with a little girl photo is about as far from my world as it gets; felt like I was served the wrong email entirely.
- The BOGO offer, which is the headline, has nothing in it for me — that's bait-and-switch energy even if the $20 off technically applies.
- No comfort, cushioning, or fit language anywhere — my actual buying criteria don't appear once.
- Eight-plus emails in nine days is too many; I'm starting to tune these out the same way I ignore the circular that comes with the Sunday paper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before sending.** If Shoe Carnival knows I've never bought women's shoes, this email should never reach my inbox. A men's sandals or walking shoe version of this same BOGO offer would have had me clicking immediately.
- 2. **Lead the $20 off as the universal offer** rather than burying it under a women's-only BOGO headline. That code works for everyone — make it the hero.
- 3. **Add one men's product shot** — even a single Skechers or New Balance slip-on in the mix signals this email was meant for me.
- 4. **Show the Shoe Perks reward earlier.** That $10 dangling at the bottom is the most compelling reason for a returning customer to click — it should be in the top third.
- 5. **Pull back cadence.** One email every two or three days would land harder than daily noise at this point.

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Stock up with BOGO Free +$20 Off!" (2026-05-23)

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken tracking link caused by a Unicode zero-width space (U+200B) injected into a click-redirect URL, which will silently corrupt click attribution for any recipients hitting that link. Six product images carry no `alt` text, degrading accessibility and breaking the image-off render path.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Zero-width space in tracking URL**

The QA probe returned an ASCII codec error at character position 68 of this URL:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260523_bogof_AM​
```

The invisible `​` (U+200B) trails the `_AM` suffix. This is almost certainly a copy-paste artifact from a rich-text source (Word, Notion, Slack) introduced during campaign setup. Depending on how the redirect server handles non-ASCII query strings, recipients get either a 400 error or a successful redirect with a corrupted `utm_campaign` value — either outcome breaks click attribution for this link.

**29 tracking links skipped** — redirect domains (emltrk.com) were not probed for destination reachability. Final-destination 404s cannot be ruled out from this data.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text — 6 product images**

All four Amplience CDN product images and the primary hero asset carry no `alt` attribute:

| Image | src |
|---|---|
| Product 1 | `amplience.net/s/scvl/114229_235198_SET/1` |
| Product 2 | `amplience.net/s/scvl/155504_337919_SET/1` |
| Product 3 | `amplience.net/s/scvl/106172_212323_SET/1` |
| Product 4 | `amplience.net/s/scvl/186581_415747_SET/1` |
| Hero asset | `image.email.shoecarnival.com/…/d06be110-…e18969.jpg` |

Clients that block images by default (Outlook desktop, corporate proxies) will render blank spaces with no context. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 requires meaningful alternatives on all informational images.

**Tracking pixel missing `alt=""`**

```
src: https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=...
src: https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to signal decorative intent to screen readers. Omitting `alt` entirely causes some screen readers to announce the filename.

**`<meta http-equiv>` malformed**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

Missing the required `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute. Should be:

```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Dark mode not supported**

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```

Declared light-only. No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` media query present in the `<style>` blocks. Apple Mail and recent Gmail will not apply any dark-mode adaptation.

### 5. Compliance

**Sending domain** — `shoecarnival@email.shoecarnival.com` uses a dedicated subdomain, consistent with proper SPF/DKIM/DMARC segmentation. Authentication headers are not present in the supplied data so pass/fail cannot be confirmed here.

**`<meta charset>` malformed**

```html
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />
```

This conflates two different meta patterns. It should be either:
```html
<meta charset="utf-8" />
```
or:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Not a deliverability risk, but some older parsers may misread charset declaration.
