# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker, third time this week now, still sitting in a "cart" I never put it in. I don't know who Skechers thinks is opening this inbox, but it isn't a 62-year-old guy who buys arch-support walking shoes. At least this time they scrolled down and threw in some men's options — the Arch Fit Altman and the plain white BOBS slip-ons aren't bad — but I had to get past a giant floral shoe and a wall of boy's and women's sneakers first.
- The 20% off Skechers Plus banner near the bottom is the one thing that'll actually get my attention, same deal I already saw in the Labor Day email and the Monthly Summary. I'm not clicking "Complete Your Order" on a shoe that was never mine, but I might scroll straight past the top of this thing to the men's row if I bother opening it at all.
- This one's getting marked as read and forgotten. Wouldn't forward it — nothing here says "hey, this is for you" until you're two-thirds of the way down the email.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus member banner is clear and matches offers I've seen and trusted before.
- They finally worked in some men's shoes — the Arch Fit Altman-Murdock and the Slip-ins BOBS — further down the "more options" row.
- Big blue "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is easy to spot and tap on a phone.
- Layout is clean, text is legible, nothing broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe is, again, a pink floral women's sneaker I never touched — this is the third near-identical "your cart" email this week with that exact shoe.
- No cushioning, arch support, or comfort language anywhere near the top — the stuff that'd actually make me stop scrolling.
- My name isn't up top, no points balance, no "you're a member" greeting like the good emails had.
- Subject "Your Items Are Waiting!" is flat-out wrong — I never added anything, so it reads as bait, not personalization.
- Men's shoes are buried four rows down under a bunch of boys' and women's product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the abandoned-cart trigger — stop sending "your cart" emails to people who never actually put anything in a cart. That's the root problem, and it's showing up email after email.
- 2. Lead with a men's shoe if the customer's browsing/purchase history skews men's — put the Arch Fit Altman or the BOBS slip-on where the floral D'Lites is now.
- 3. Put the name and points balance up top like the Monthly Summary email did — that one scored well specifically because it felt personal.
- 4. Move the 20% off Skechers Plus banner higher, right under the hero — it's the strongest offer in the whole email and it's stuck near the bottom.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off for Skechers Plus members, thru 9/7`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size, your savings: 20% off ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members save 20% at checkout — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort picks in your size, plus a member discount`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Email is built on Attentive's ESP (`skechers.attentivemail.com`, `image.emails.skechers.com`). Two hard failures: the unsubscribe link returns HTTP 400 and there is no plain-text MIME part.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unsubscribe link broken (400)**: `https://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6VorXOZu5avyAvDTfGQbua6CZqR1E...anti_JgSMuu6woH0...` returns a 400 error. This is a hard compliance/functional failure, not just a warning.
- Two CTA links (`Complete you Order`, and the "We noticed you noticing us..." link) returned 429 (rate-limited) during probing — likely valid but inconclusive from this data; recommend manual re-check outside the QA tool's rate window.
- 26 of 38 links were skipped by the prober (footer/utility/social, deprioritized by design) — not a defect, just reduced coverage.
- All tracked links route through Attentive's redirect wrapper (`ls/click?upn=...`) rather than exposing final destination URLs — expected for this ESP, not an issue in itself, but means link-destination/UTM validation below is limited to what's visible in the wrapper.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 13 images served over `http://` instead of `https://` (e.g. `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png`, `.../m/5/dfcc8fd7-...jpg`, `.../m/11/49468f73-...png`, app store/social/payment badges). Mixed content on an https-loading client (most webmail/app clients) can cause these images to be blocked or flagged.
- 2 images missing `alt` text: `021bd784-11b5-4646-95f8-35c2bd27702a.gif` and `a6ec5198-5437-4eaa-8613-eacc775726f9.jpg` (the latter also affected by the http:// issue above). The tracking pixel (`wf/open?upn=...`) missing alt is expected/non-issue for a 1x1 open pixel.
- Responsive CSS (`@media max-width:620px`) and MSO/Outlook conditionals (`x-apple-data-detectors`, `mso-hide`) are present and structured correctly — no issues found there.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%...%%`) are present in the truncated source — copy appears static/segment-based rather than 1:1 personalized. No broken or unrendered tokens found in the visible HTML.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unsubscribe link is broken (400)** — this is a CAN-SPAM violation risk since the required functioning opt-out mechanism is not operational as sent.
- **No plain-text part**: QA reports the text version is 0 chars. CAN-SPAM doesn't strictly require plain-text, but its absence increases spam-filter risk and is a deliverability/accessibility gap (screen readers/text-only clients get nothing).

Would need the resolved/unwrapped destination URLs to assess.
