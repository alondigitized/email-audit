# Inflation Relief Is Here!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- When I saw "Inflation Relief Is Here!" in my inbox, I'll admit — the framing caught my attention. Groceries are still expensive, gas prices aren't doing me any favors, and anything promising price relief sounds worth a second. But then I notice the preview text is a wall of gobbledygook URL, and that immediately drops my confidence. Feels like a mistake slipped through.
- Inside the email, it's a yard-sale layout. Inflation Relief at the top promises 10% off — then I read "In Store Only" in smaller text underneath and feel slightly cheated. The Friday Deal Roundup beneath it has six or seven sections crammed together: Women's & Kids BOGO, Nike, Footbed sandals, Adidas headwear, Skechers, Kids' sandals. I'm scrolling and scrolling looking for something for me. The Skechers section is buried mid-email, and when I get there, it's the same $54.98 deal from yesterday's send. I just saw that.
- The $20 off coupon with code MAY20 at the bottom is the clearest, most useful thing in the email — and it's last. I'd mark this read, maybe go straight to the coupon code, but I'm not clicking anything else. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The "Friday Deal Roundup" label gives the email some organizing logic — at least each section has a header so I can scan quickly without reading every word.
- The $20 off code (MAY20) and the threshold ($99.98+) are spelled out clearly — that's actually useful, I can do that math.
- Shoe Perks loyalty benefit is visible — a free $10 reward shows the brand knows I'm a member, even if the email doesn't feel personalized beyond that.
- No broken images or layout meltdowns visible — the email at least renders cleanly on the screen.
- Afterpay "Pay in 4" at least acknowledges that shoes can be a big purchase — that's a practical touch.

## What's weak

- The preview text is a raw tracking URL. That's not a preheader — that's a technical failure, and it makes the whole send feel sloppy before I even open it.
- "10% Off In Store Only" leads the email. I'm on my phone, not driving to a store — this offer doesn't apply to me and shouldn't be the hero.
- Women's & Kids BOGO is the most visually prominent offer. I'm a 62-year-old man. I have no one to buy women's or kids' sandals for.
- The Skechers deal is $54.98 — the exact same as yesterday's email. Shoe Carnival just sent me this. Feels like copy-paste, not a curated send.
- Adidas headwear and accessories has zero relevance to my comfort-shoe shopping. It pads the length without adding value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately** — this is a defect, not a design choice. Wire in something like "10 deals, one great code: MAY20 saves you $20 today."
- 2. **Drop the "In Store Only" hero**. If the lead offer doesn't work online, don't lead with it for a mobile email send. Either make it online-eligible or bury it below the fold.
- 3. **Put the $20 off code at the top**, not the bottom. That's the most actionable offer for someone on their phone — lead with it.
- 4. **Consolidate for my gender/category** — two men's comfort sections max. No Women's & Kids, no headwear. If the platform has my purchase history, use it.
- 5. **Retire the $54.98 Skechers section for at least one cycle** — showing me the same price two days in a row makes it feel like there's no real deal happening.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$20 Off Today — Skechers, Nike & More`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code MAY20 at checkout. No minimum fuss, just $99.98+ and you're in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Skechers, Nike, Brooks — comfort picks on sale. Your $20 code is inside.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for broad client compatibility, with no broken renders or critical failures. Three confirmed issues warrant remediation before the next send: an unresolved merge token in the open-tracking pixel, missing alt text on a content image, and a malformed IE-compatibility meta tag.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel**
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder is visible in the raw HTML. If this was not substituted at send time by the ESP, every open event is attributed to the same null recipient, breaking individual open-rate data. Confirm whether this token resolves correctly in the ESP preview or delivered source.

**27 click-redirect links not validated**
All CTA links pass through an email click-redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity on the final landing pages cannot be confirmed from this audit. Manual spot-checking of 3–5 high-traffic CTAs is recommended.

**UTM parameter in open-tracking pixel (anomaly)**
```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260515_Friday_deals_AM
```
UTM params are appended to the open (image) pixel, not just click links. This is unusual and likely harmless, but if the ESP is also injecting these onto click-redirect URLs, verify there is no double-encoding that corrupts `utm_campaign` values downstream.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content image**
```html
src: https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3111737164047d701077/m/1/fcf7c998-c6f2-4f06-82af-785f6595003d.jpg
```
This appears to be a hero or product image based on the CDN path. Images-off rendering (Outlook default, corporate firewalls) will show a blank box. Add descriptive `alt` text.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels**
Both emltrk.com pixel `<img>` tags lack `alt=""`. Decorative/invisible images should carry an explicit empty alt to satisfy screen reader tools and strict HTML validators. Add `alt=""` to both.

**Malformed IE-compatibility meta tag**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
The `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` attribute is absent. Without it, this tag is inert — Outlook's MSHTML engine will not receive the edge-rendering directive. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Light-mode only color scheme**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) will force-invert colors rather than render the email's own palette. This is intentional only if dark-mode designs are explicitly out of scope.

**Legacy doctype**
```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
HTML 4.01 Transitional is functional but antiquated. Gmail and most modern clients normalize to their own rendering anyway, so this is low priority.
