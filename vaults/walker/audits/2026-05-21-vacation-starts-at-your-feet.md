# Vacation Starts at Your Feet
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Another Crocs email. I'll give them credit — "Vacation Starts at Your Feet" at least sounds like it might be about a comfortable sandal rather than a studded platform wedge. But I open this thing and what do I see? A woman's feet in thin thong flip-flops. That's the hero. The whole email is about something called the Miami Jelly Flip — a flat, strappy sandal for women heading to the beach. Not a word about arch support. Not a word about cushioning. No price, no offer, just vibes.
- What makes this sting a little more is that yesterday Crocs sent me a Memorial Day email promising up to 50% off. That had my attention. This one walks it all back — no deal visible anywhere, just "Shop Crocs / Shop Jibbitz Charms / Shop Sale / Shop Sandals" at the bottom like a menu at a restaurant that doesn't actually tell you the prices. The Jibbitz charms shoutout is a recurring theme in this brand's emails and it still doesn't speak to me.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Nothing here is aimed at a 62-year-old man who needs a walking sandal with some real support under his foot. Forward to a friend? No.

## What's working

- **Crocs logo renders cleanly and is immediately recognizable** — at least I know who sent it.
- **"Miami Jelly Flip" is a named product** — more specific than some of the lifestyle-only emails they've sent; there's something identifiable in the subject.
- **Seasonal timing makes sense** — Memorial Day weekend, beach trip imagery, vacation copy. The calendar fit is right even if the product fit for me is wrong.
- **Email renders without bugs** — no broken images, clean layout, readable sections even on a phone screen.

## What's weak

- **All women, all the time** — every model visible is female; the Miami Jelly Flip appears to be a women's sandal. I'm not the audience for this email and it shows.
- **Yesterday promised 50% off; today promises nothing** — after the Memorial Day sale email, this one has zero price information, which is a step backward in relevance.
- **Four CTAs with no hierarchy** — "Shop Crocs," "Shop Jibbitz Charms," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals" are all the same visual weight. I don't know where to tap.
- **Preheader is garbage** — whatever I see next to the subject in my inbox is blank or noise. No preview copy at all means I'm flying blind before I open.
- **Zero comfort language** — arch, cushion, support — none of it. A flip-flop that "lets all-day comfort" is the closest they get.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Run the men's sandal version of this email** — Crocs makes men's comfort sandals. Point me to those. Show me a guy my age actually walking somewhere in them.
- 2. **Bring back the offer** — yesterday's 50% off gave me a reason to open. If the sale is still running, lead with that again. If not, tell me what the Miami Flip costs. Anything.
- 3. **Kill three of the four CTAs** — pick one: "Shop Men's Sandals" or "Shop the Sale." Four buttons equal no button.
- 4. **Write a real preheader** — even one sentence like "The Miami Flip is built for all-day beach walks" beats invisible padding characters.
- 5. **Add one comfort spec** — "flexible, lightweight sole" or "contoured footbed" — something I can evaluate with my feet in mind.
