# Early. Black. Friday! 1000s of 👢, 30% off!

## Executive summary

- DSW again. The fifth boots email in about three days, and by now I'm skimming. The big green "30% OFF BOOTS*" banner is easy to read on my phone without my glasses, and the code BOOTFRIDAY is right there. I've seen that offer in "30% OFF BOOTS!? DECK YEAH!" and "Click for 30% off thousands of NEW boot styles." The only new thing is slippers.
- The slippers are the one thing that speaks to me. A moccasin slipper at $34.99 is the kind of comfort shoe I'd actually buy. But the whole lower half is pink and women's, with UGG, "Warm Wishes" and a pair of heels. The men's link is a small underlined line under the banner. Nothing talks about cushioning or arch support.
- I'd open it out of habit and maybe tap "Shop Men" once. I wouldn't forward it. It's the same pitch as yesterday, with a pink coat of paint.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF BOOTS*" headline is huge and the white SHOP BOOTS button is easy to tap with a thumb.
- The code BOOTFRIDAY is right under the headline, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The $34.99 moccasin slipper is a price I'd consider. It's the closest thing here to my hands-free comfort shoe.
- "Shop by Size" with wide-width and wide-calf buttons is a decent touch. I can read them.
- The free shipping, in-store pickup and "opt in to texts, get 15% off" strip is clear.

## What's weak

- Another boots email so soon. The subject, offer and banner are nearly identical to yesterday's.
- Most of the images are women's: the fuzzy slippers, the UGGs, the heels in the "And while you're here" row. "Shop Men" is a tiny underlined link under the banner.
- There's no mention of cushioning, arch support or slip-on comfort anywhere. I'm left guessing whether that slipper has a real sole.
- The asterisk on the offer leads to a wall of tiny fine print at the bottom. I had to squint, and I can't tell which brands are excluded without reading all of it.
- The "Warm Wishes" and "gift *these* to yourself" copy is cutesy and doesn't tell me anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a men's comfort block right under the hero, with a slip-on or a cushioned walking shoe and a "Shop Men's Comfort" button. That's the biggest fix for me.
- 2. Say which brands are excluded in plain words next to the offer instead of burying it.
- 3. Space out the sends. Two or three boots emails with different hooks beat five with the same one.
- 4. Show a rating or "easy on, easy off" detail on the $34.99 slipper so I can judge it quickly.

  - Two open-tracking beacons from different domains is redundant.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (2 images):**
  - `oLIcqcq_AVmaEC5f52pf3Ho4ZQGmcEjvJSc6QPdssY0x6hcXCq…` (src `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/…`)
  - `o.gif` (src `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/…/o.gif`)
  - Both are tracking pixels, so the impact is low. They should still carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Charset and compatibility metas have no `http-equiv`:**
  - `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`
  - `<meta content="IE=Edge" />`
  - Without `http-equiv`, neither declaration is valid. The charset won't be honored by parsers that rely on it. The subject contains an emoji (👢), so UTF-8 handling matters.
- **Zoom is disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Web fonts:** `@font-face` rules load `.woff` and `.ttf` files from `braze-images.com`. Many clients ignore them, so the font stack falls back. I couldn't see the fallback stack in the truncated source.
- **Plain-text part:** 26,213 of 30,823 characters (about 85%) are URLs, well over the 70% threshold. The tracking-wrapped URLs bloat the text part and raise spam-filter risk.

No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FNAME]`) appear in the visible source. The pixel URL carries a per-recipient identifier (`mi_u=225090073`), which is expected for tracking.
