# Psst…it’s STILL Birkenstock season
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's at least visually different from the wall of "40% off" and birthday-candle emails DSW's been lobbing at me all week. The big Birkenstock hero shot caught my eye for a second — I know that brand, I know they've got that contoured cork footbed that's supposed to be good for your arches, and that's exactly the kind of thing that gets my attention. Trouble is, the email never says a word about arch support or cushioning — it just assumes I already know why Birkenstock matters and skips straight to "summer's ending, buy sandals."
- Then I got to the actual people in the pictures — a teenage girl in plaid pants and a young guy in gym shorts playing basketball — and "SUMMER NIGHTS? SCHOOL DAYS? THEY DO IT ALL" and I lost interest fast. I don't have kids in school anymore, and I'm not shooting hoops in sandals. That's a back-to-school email wearing a Birkenstock costume.
- Bottom line: I'd scroll past the top section, maybe pause at the "Shop Men's Birkenstock" button out of habit, but I'm not clicking through. Not forwarding this to anybody — my buddy at the coffee shop who wears his Birkenstocks year-round might actually like it, but that's a stretch. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The hero shot is clean — one sandal, one clear background, no clutter, easy to see on my phone without squinting.
- There's a "Shop Men's Birkenstock" button sitting right next to the women's one, so at least they didn't forget I exist.
- The "Shop by Size" grid with wide width and wide calf options is the one part that actually spoke to a guy like me — I've got wider feet than most shoe racks account for, and seeing that upfront tells me DSW gets it.
- The donation offer ("Give Shoes, Get $10") is a genuinely different hook than the same old percentage-off blast — a little refreshing after this week's parade.

## What's weak

- Not one word about comfort, cushioning, or arch support — the actual reason a guy my age would care about Birkenstock over some other sandal.
- Every model in this email is a teenager. A girl in a schoolyard outfit and a kid playing basketball don't tell me anything about how these shoes fit or wear for someone my age.
- The "back to school" theme is completely irrelevant to me — no grandkids shopping angle, nothing.
- That $10 offer isn't really a discount — I have to drive to a store and donate shoes during a specific week just to earn a "VIP Bonus Reward," whatever that ends up being worth. That's a lot of steps for a vague payoff.
- After four or five nearly identical "40% off" emails this week (like the Aug 5 "Up to 40% off select Nike, adidas, Birkenstock" one), getting a whole separate email about the same brand feels like DSW's just hammering the same nail twice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real comfort callout right under the hero image — something like "Contoured cork footbed. All-day arch support." That's the actual selling point for someone like me, and it's missing entirely.
- 2. Swap at least one lifestyle photo for someone over 40 — even just a guy on a porch or walking a dog in Birkenstocks would make this feel like it includes me instead of my grandkids.
- 3. Lead with a real dollar-off or percent-off deal on Birkenstocks specifically, not a shoe-donation ask buried two-thirds down the email.
- 4. Move the "Shop by Size" / wide-width section higher — that's a stronger hook for a returning customer than another lifestyle shot.
- 5. Space these Birkenstock-specific sends out — I've now gotten Birkenstock mentioned in at least two emails this week; pick one lane per week.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your feet will thank you: Birkenstock, now in wide width`
- **Subject Alt B:** `All-day comfort, no laces required — Birkenstock is in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Contoured footbeds, wide-width options, sizes 5–11 in stock`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sandal your podiatrist would actually approve of`
