# Happy 4th! 🎆 Celebrate with BOGO 1/2 off Clearance
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Another July 4th blast from Famous Footwear, and I'll be straight — the BOGO half-off on clearance stopped my thumb from swiping. That's real money off, and "clearance" is where I actually find the everyday walkers I can justify. The hero is clean, patriotic, seasonally on-point, and the BOGO framing is something different than the steady stream of "20% off" emails they've been running all month. Points for changing it up.
- But before I even opened the email, I was staring at that same broken tracking URL in the preview slot. Again. I flagged it on the June 21 Samba send, the June 24 Naturalizer send, the June 26 Weekend Deals send, and the July 2 sandals send. Seven sends, same busted preheader. At this point it's not a mistake — it's who they are. And it reads to me like a brand that doesn't bother checking how their mail looks before they hit send.
- Inside, the email is a solid if busy catalog. There's a men's section, some sandals and casual shoes I'd actually consider, and a loyalty 2X points kicker at the bottom. I'd probably scroll through the clearance section and maybe click. I wouldn't forward this — the layout is too dense for me to explain over the phone to a buddy, and the broken preview URL would look sketchy in a forward.

## What's working

- **BOGO clearance is the right offer for me.** Half off on an already-marked-down shoe is the kind of math I can do at the register — feels like real savings, not fine-print games.
- **July 4th framing is crisp and timely.** The subject's holiday hook gives the offer a deadline I believe without needing a countdown clock.
- **Men's product section is present.** I can see men's shoes in the scroll — sandals, casual walkers — which at least puts some relevant product in front of me.
- **2X loyalty points visible.** I don't buy *because* of points, but if I'm buying anyway, seeing it reinforced is a nice nudge.
- **Hero visual hierarchy works.** BOGO lands first, big, before anything else competes for attention. No confusion about what this email is selling.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is a raw tracking URL. Still. Again.** I've been pointing at this for weeks. It's the first thing I see in my inbox before I even open the email, and it looks like spam. This is the single most fixable problem and they keep ignoring it.
- **No comfort-specific language anywhere.** The hero is savings-first, not shoe-first. Nothing says "cushioned," "arch support," "wide width," or "easy-on" — the things that would actually make me commit to a click.
- **The product grid is dense for a phone screen.** Small thumbnails, small text under each shoe. At my age, that requires zooming, and I don't do that for a sale email.
- **Clearance scope isn't defined.** "BOGO half off clearance" — okay, but *how much* clearance? Is it the same six women's sandals from last week? Or are there actually men's walkers in there? I have no idea until I click, and that uncertainty costs clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader.** This is non-negotiable and it's been broken across at least seven sends. One line of real copy — "Ends tonight: men's clearance walkers buy one, get one half off" — and every inbox score improves immediately.
- 2. **Put one comfort-anchored product in the hero.** Show me a Skechers or New Balance walker on the BOGO call-out. Something with "cushioned" or "slip-on" in the copy. I'll click faster when I see my category called out explicitly.
- 3. **Add a deadline line under the hero CTA.** The June 27 email had a countdown clock that worked. Even just "Offer ends July 4th at midnight" does the job and makes the urgency feel honest.
- 4. **Simplify the product grid for mobile.** Fewer, larger product tiles. I should be able to read the shoe name and price without pinching the screen.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `The BOGO you've been waiting for: clearance shoes, ½ off your second pair — 4th only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound but carries two meaningful technical defects: a CSS property typo that silently drops a layout style, and a malformed `<meta>` charset declaration. The dominant issue is pervasive missing alt text across 34 images, which is both an accessibility failure and a rendering risk when images are blocked.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**31 click links unverifiable.** All outbound links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...` with opaque base64-encoded `qs` parameters (SFMC pattern). QA skipped HTTP probing on all 31. Final destination URLs, UTM parameter presence, and 404 status cannot be confirmed from the source alone.

**Open pixel is present and correctly formed:**
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?RZSBGORLEMSULJ27C6AQZ7YOTM.70246&d=70246&bmt=0
```
1×1 px, `alt=""`, `width`/`height` attributes set — no issues.

**Plain-text part URL density: 85% (14,852 / 17,483 chars).** Plain-text body is essentially a URL dump. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO`, Gmail heuristics) penalize plain-text parts where URLs constitute the vast majority of content. This is a deliverability risk.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS typo — `lineapacing` is not a valid property.**
```html
<td style="...lineapacing:20px;...">
```
Should be `line-height` or `letter-spacing`. The style silently no-ops in all clients; intended spacing is not applied to that cell.

**Malformed charset `<meta>` tag — missing `http-equiv` attribute.**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the tag has no semantic effect. Charset declaration is effectively absent for clients that rely on it (Outlook on Windows falls back to system encoding).

**34 images missing `alt` text.** Every product/hero image in the send has `alt=""`. When images are blocked (default in Outlook, many corporate clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no readable content. The logo link (`3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`) also has `alt=""` — it wraps an `<a>` tag, so screen readers announce an unlabeled link.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta.** `0` is non-standard; the spec value is `no`. Works in practice but should be `user-scalable=no` for strict compliance.

**Legacy doctype.** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` is industry-standard for broad email client compatibility — not flagged as an issue.
