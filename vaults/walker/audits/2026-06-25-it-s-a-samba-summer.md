# It's a Samba summer ⚽☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again with the tracking URL in my preview text. I flagged this exact issue on the June 15th send, the June 16th send, the June 16th send, and the June 17th Spend & Save send. That's now four or five emails in a row with the same broken preheader, and Famous Footwear still hasn't fixed it. At this point it's not an accident — it's a process failure, and it's starting to make me trust the brand a little less every time I see it.
- Once I get past that and open it up — Samba. Adidas Samba. I know the Samba. Every kid in their twenties is wearing them right now. Flat as a pancake, zero arch support, looks like a vintage soccer cleat. My feet would be screaming by the third hour. I've got nothing against Adidas, but the Samba is somebody else's shoe, and somebody at Famous Footwear apparently doesn't know the difference between my purchase history and a trend-chasing fashion list.
- The Spend & Save tiers at the top are solid — I like the tiered math, no confusion — but they're in service of a shoe I have zero interest in. I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't forward it to a friend unless that friend is thirty-five and cares more about looking cool than walking without knee pain.

## What's working

- Spend & Save is front and center — $10/$15/$20 off tiers, the math is clear, I don't have to hunt for the offer
- Bonus Cash with Store Pickup is a nice loyalty touch — the QR code is visible and that kind of incentive gets my attention
- The Samba hero image is clean and well-lit — it's well-produced creative, even if it's the wrong product for me
- Famous Footwear is a sender I recognize, so I opened it rather than deleting on sight
- Summer timing is on-point — it's late June, the seasonal angle makes sense

## What's weak

- The Samba is a flat fashion sneaker for people thirty years younger than me — wrong shoe, wrong demographic, wrong priority entirely
- That tracking URL is back in the preview text — same broken preheader I've complained about repeatedly, still not fixed, still looks like spam garbage in my inbox
- Not one word about cushioning, arch support, wide widths, or anything that would make me think this shoe is built for a 62-year-old who walks three miles a day
- The email is dense — Samba hero, clearance grid, style of the week, product tiles — it all says "Adidas Samba" in different ways, which doesn't help when Samba isn't on my list
- Cadence is getting heavy; this is at least the sixth Famous Footwear email in ten days and my thumb is getting itchy near the unsubscribe button

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader. Today.** This is the fourth or fifth send in a row with raw tracking URL showing up as preview text. A marketer could patch this before the next send goes out. It's not a small thing — it's the first impression in my inbox.
- 2. **Segment me out of trend-driven sends.** If Famous Footwear has any click data on me, they know I gravitate toward comfort and walking shoes. The Samba blast belongs on a different list — not mine.
- 3. **If the Samba must stay in the email**, give me a comfort angle. Lead with the most cushioned insole version, call out "surprising support for a retro silhouette," or show me a men's walking colorway. Give me a reason to look twice instead of confirming my gut that it's not for me.
- 4. **Ease up on the email cadence.** Six-plus sends in ten days from the same brand is too much. I'm starting to tune out Famous Footwear as a category, which means I'll miss the emails I actually care about.
- 5. **Tie the discount to something I'd buy.** Drop a few New Balance, Hoka, or Brooks picks into the clearance rail so the Spend & Save has somewhere to land for a guy like me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save $10–$20 this week — men's comfort shoes on sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend & Save is on: $20 off when you spend $100`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Walk in, save more. Spend $50, $75, or $100 and get cash back.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bonus cash with store pickup. Spend & Save tiers start at just $50.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: 20 images with empty `alt=""`**

All product and hero images have `alt=""`, which is the HTML attribute present but blank — not the same as omitted. For purely decorative images this is acceptable, but at least the logo image (`3b6a7dc1...png`) and product images should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering (common in Outlook desktop).

Affected images (representative):
- Logo: `3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png`
- Hero/divider: `4ccff4ad-f402-43de-a027-5eb147b6f238.jpg` (appears 3×)
- Product images: `73bc3661`, `1d62dde4`, `8f69eb4e`, `f91c1840`, `f1219bfc`, `d1eb8a93`, `8381bbe6`, `34ceaea4`, `8714175e`
- UI elements (likely buttons/badges): `fae6608c`, `b01d2c4b`, `a7955336`, `0dd80858`, `79245af5`, `7dfedbb4`, `b357b658`, `45064e91`

**Mixed content — HTTP image load:**
```
src: http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=...
```
This is an ExactTarget LiveContent (dynamic content/coupon) block loaded over HTTP. HTTPS clients (Gmail web, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) will block this image or show a security warning. The LiveContent service should be served over HTTPS; this is a known ExactTarget configuration issue.

**Doctype:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` — intentional for email clients (Outlook Word renderer compatibility), no action needed.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern; WCAG 1.4.4 recommends against disabling zoom. Low severity for email vs. web, but worth flagging.
