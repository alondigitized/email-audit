# In here: brands you 🤎
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a whole different animal from the "25% off" banners DSW's been hammering me with all week. This one leads with "FALL (TREND) FORECAST" and a woman in patchwork suede boots — Miranda Patchwork by Frye — which, no offense to Frye, is about as far from what I'm shopping for as it gets. I scrolled a good ways before hitting anything that resembled comfort or my size wheelhouse, and even the "sooo down to earth" boot lineup is dress boots and loafers, not a cushioned walker in sight.
- The "Give Shoes, Get $10" donation drive caught my attention more than the fashion stuff, honestly — I like the idea of clearing out old shoes for a bonus reward, that feels like a real deal, not just a style pitch. But by the time I got there I'd already mentally filed this as "not for me."
- I wouldn't click past a curious scroll, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — this is a trend email for someone shopping for boots to match an outfit, not someone looking for arch support. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The donation program ("Give Shoes, Get $10") is a concrete, easy-to-understand offer with a clear reward tied to it.
- Shop by Size grid near the bottom is genuinely useful — I can see my size (10, 10.5, 11) laid out plainly, no hunting required.
- "Free shipping for VIPs" and "Buy now, pay later with Afterpay" icons near the footer are readable and relevant regardless of style.
- Layout is clean, no overlapping text or broken images that I can spot.

## What's weak

- The whole top half is fall fashion — patchwork boots, western boots, animal print — nothing about slip-on comfort, cushioning, or men's casual at all.
- The hero model and styling skew younger and more fashion-forward; doesn't read as aimed at a 62-year-old guy.
- "25% OFF" claim button sits way up top isolated with a lot of white space before the actual content loads in — feels like the email stalls before getting to the point.
- No men's category shown until the tiny nav bar at the very top; everything visual is women's boots and trend talk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero for a men's comfort silhouette — Skechers slip-on or Hoka walker — paired with a real percentage-off callout, not a "trend forecast" theme that skips half your customer base.
- 2. Move the "Shop By Size" and "Wide Width / Wide Calf" blocks up near the top — that's the most useful thing in the whole email for a guy like me and it's buried near the bottom.
- 3. Trim the dead space between the "CLAIM 25% OFF" line and the actual hero image — right now it just looks like the email is loading slow.
- 4. Add a men's comfort tile to "More Breakout Trends for Fall" instead of three women's/unisex fashion categories back to back.
