# Your cart expires soon!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-30**
## Executive summary

- That pink floral D'Lites "Spring Bloom" sneaker is back. I flagged it on 9/17, 9/27, all over 9/28 and again yesterday, and here it is in "Your cart expires soon!" It's a women's shoe with embroidered flowers, and I'm a 62-year-old man who has never wanted it. Skechers keeps treating me like I'm shopping for somebody else.
- The 15% for guests and 20% for members banner is the part that helped. Yesterday's "Fall Haul 20% off for members" email led with that offer, and it was a better email for it. Here the discount sits under a big pink shoe and a bunch of kids' sneakers, so I had to scroll to find it. I'm a member, so I noticed, but a points balance doesn't decide my purchase. The shoe that fits does.
- Would I click? Probably not. Forward it? No. I'd mark it read and move on. The only thing that caught my eye was the one men's slip-in, the brown Relaxed Fit Altman, and it's tiny and buried in the grid.

## What's working

- The 20% off for members banner is big and easy to read. I'm a member, so it feels like I'm being recognized.
- "Complete Your Order" is a clear, dark blue button. I know exactly what it does.
- The layout is clean, with nothing broken or overlapping. Everything loads and the headline is easy to read on a phone.
- The Men's Slip-ins Relaxed Fit Altman is a shoe I'd consider. Slip-ins and a relaxed fit are what I look for.

## What's weak

- The hero is the pink floral women's sneaker again. It's the sixth or seventh time I've seen it, and it's plainly not for me.
- Four of the six "best sellers" are women's or kids' shoes. Only one is a man's shoe, and nothing mentions cushioning, arch support or all-day walking comfort.
- The dates don't agree. The banner says "Thru 10/1," but the fine print says the offer was valid 9/23 to 9/28 and calls it "20% off boots & outdoor styles." Now I wonder if the discount even applies to my cart.
- The product captions are tiny, and the category links at the bottom are a long stack of text. On my phone I'd be squinting and hunting.
- "Your cart expires soon" comes with no deadline. I've had cart reminders almost daily, so the urgency isn't believable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop repeating the same abandoned item in every cart email. If I haven't bought the pink floral shoe after a week, show me something from my men's slip-in browsing instead. That would lift this email more than anything else.
- 2. Move the 20% member offer above the fold, and match the fine print to the banner date.
- 3. Add one line of comfort proof under the hero shoe, like "Arch Fit support, machine washable, Hands Free Slip-ins." Right now I get a photo and a name.
- 4. Use bigger captions and fewer categories.
- 5. Send fewer cart reminders. One per abandonment is enough.

- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off for members ends 10/1`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members save 20% through 10/1, and your cushioned pair is still waiting.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hands-free comfort, arch support, and your member discount, all saved for you.`
