# Top shoes $80 and under for the new school year
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's got a real number in it at least — "$80 and under" — and no teenagers, no jerseys, no back-to-school hallway shots. Just three flat lace-up sneakers sitting on some concrete steps. Problem is, those aren't shoes I'd wear. Skate-style suede slip-ons and low-profile leather trainers with gum soles — that's a young guy's shoe, no ankle support, no visible cushioning, nothing that says "walk around the mall for three hours and your feet feel fine." The preheader up top mentions "all-day comfort," which caught my eye for half a second, but the shoes themselves don't back that up.
- It's a clean-looking email, I'll give them that — no clutter, easy to read on the phone. But "back to school fit for the season" tells me right away this isn't for me, it's for somebody buying for a teenager. I wouldn't click, and I sure wouldn't forward it — nothing here to pass along to anybody my age.

## What's working

- The $80-and-under headline is a real, specific number — better than the vague "new drop" stuff they usually send.
- No people in the photo this time, so at least I'm not staring at a model thirty years younger than me.
- Layout's simple and the text is big enough to read without squinting.
- "Shop sneakers with all-day comfort and style" in the preheader is the right kind of pitch — just doesn't match what's pictured.

## What's weak

- The shoes are skate/retro flats — no arch support, no cushioning I can see, wrong category entirely for a comfort shopper like me.
- "Back to school fit for the season" — I don't have a kid in school, so this whole campaign skips right past me.
- Big empty gap in the middle of the email with nothing in it, looks unfinished.
- Only one CTA up top ("Shop Now") and it's generic — doesn't even point at these specific shoes.
- No price shown beyond the "$80 and under" headline — no actual number on a shoe, no comparison, nothing to chew on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero shoes for something with real cushioning and a chunkier sole — if you're going to say "all-day comfort," show me a shoe that looks like it delivers that, not skate flats.
- 2. Drop the "back to school" framing entirely for a send like this, or split the list so guys my age get a "comfort classics under $80" version instead.
- 3. Fill that dead space in the middle with actual product tiles — three or four shoes with prices, not just a paragraph and a big gap.
- 4. Add a second CTA under the shoe photo itself, tied to that specific style.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Comfort sneakers, $80 and under`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New markdowns: classic sneakers under $80`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Everyday comfort, prices that don't creep up`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned classics, all under $80`
