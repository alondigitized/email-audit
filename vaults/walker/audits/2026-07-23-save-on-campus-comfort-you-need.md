# Save on Campus Comfort You NEED
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three mornings in a row. I counted. When that "Shoe Carnival" name shows up in my inbox at this point it's like the neighbor's dog — I know it's coming, I've made my peace with it, and some days it surprises me. Today was not one of those days.
- I opened this one and the first thing I see is two teenagers on a running track, a "Back to School" badge, and the words "Campus Comfort." I am sixty-two years old. My campus days ended before these kids' parents were born. I don't need backpack straps or a meal plan — I need something with arch support I can slip on before my morning walk. Right away this email is talking to my grandkids, not me. The Brooks section down below almost saved it — I've been keeping an eye on the Ghost 17 ever since the Birkenstock send on July 15th reminded me I should be paying more attention to this store's comfort aisle — but you have to scroll past a Jordan brand ad to get there.
- I would not click anything, and I would definitely not forward this to a friend. I'd glance at the Brooks deal, mentally file it, and move on.

## What's working

- **Brooks Ghost 17 is right in my wheelhouse.** That "Price Break" callout with a real dollar amount ($40 off) is the kind of specific savings that makes me stop scrolling. Brooks is a brand I trust for walking comfort.
- **Multiple discount layers add up fast.** $40 off the Brooks, 25% off family athletic styles, AND a $20/$10 coupon code with "SAVEMORE" — there's real money on the table here even if the framing is off.
- **The email renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. It's a competent-looking email visually.
- **In-store barcodes at the bottom.** I actually appreciate that — I can walk into a Shoe Carnival and show them my phone. That's how I shop.

## What's weak

- **"Campus Comfort" and teen track stars are not my world.** The hero image — young woman in a crop top, young man in short shorts, running track — screams "back to school" at someone literally going back to school. I felt like I accidentally opened my daughter's email.
- **The preview text is broken.** What I see next to the subject in my inbox is a raw tracking URL — `https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7...` — instead of actual marketing copy. That alone makes me trust the email less before I even open it.
- **Three competing sections with no clear winner.** Brooks section, Family Athletics section, coupon codes — they're all shouting at the same volume. I don't know which thing they want me to do first.
- **"You NEED" in all-caps in the subject line.** I don't like being told what I need by a store. That phrasing reads like a teenager wrote it, which maybe tracks given the target audience, but it's not how you talk to me.
- **Twenty-three days in a row.** Fatigue is real. I open these out of habit now, not curiosity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the send.** Any subscriber over 55 who hasn't clicked a back-to-school offer in two years should get a completely different email this week — a comfort-walking focused hero with adult models, no campus language. The Brooks deal is genuinely good; it just needs the right frame.
- 2. **Fix the broken preheader.** This is the most basic thing. The preview text leaking a click-tracking URL signals either a technical failure or a QA miss. Whatever is causing this needs to be addressed before the next deploy.
- 3. **Lead with the Brooks deal for comfort shoppers.** If they insist on one template, flip the hierarchy: put the Brooks Ghost 17 price break as the hero for me, back-to-school as the secondary section. The $40 off is a genuinely compelling number.
- 4. **Drop "You NEED" from the subject.** It's pushy and it reads young. "Walker, $40 off Brooks Ghost 17 — this weekend only" would make me open immediately. Without personalization, even "Brooks Ghost 17: $40 Off This Weekend" beats what they sent.
- 5. **Add a credible deadline.** Every section — Brooks, Family Athletics, coupons — floats with no end date. "Through Sunday" or "48 hours only" would make me act today instead of "maybe later" (which is always never).

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 25% off select styles and a $20 off code — through Sunday only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three ways to save today, including a price break on Brooks`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel:**
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
`[UNIQUE]` is a literal string in the source — this token must be resolved by the ESP at send time to a subscriber-specific value. If it fires as-is, all opens will collide on the same anonymous key, making open attribution useless.

**Unencoded space in UTM campaign value (click-tracking pixel):**
```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260723_nike_brooks_AM - 20260722_101232
```
The space between `AM` and `-` is not percent-encoded. This will cause the parameter to be truncated or misread by analytics platforms. Should be `AM%20-%2020260722_101232` or restructured.

**Date inconsistency in UTM campaign string:** The campaign name encodes `20260723` (today) but the suffix contains `20260722` (yesterday). Likely a copy-paste error from the prior day's send template.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):

| Type | Count | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| Product images | 4 | `i8.amplience.net` |
| Content/hero images | 2 | `image.email.shoecarnival.com` |
| Tracking pixels | 2 | `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com`, `0di86k1v.emltrk.com` |

Product and content images must have descriptive `alt` text for screen readers and image-blocked rendering. Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` (empty string, not omitted) to suppress broken-image indicators.

**Dark mode not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` media query is present. Apple Mail and supported clients will attempt their own inversion on a light-only declaration, which can produce unintended color artifacts on images and backgrounds.

**Global link color override is overly broad:**
```css
a, a:link, a:visited, ... { color: inherit !important; }
```
This suppresses all link color everywhere in the email, including intentional CTA button text. Any button that relies on a link color for contrast must set color explicitly on the element itself, not via `<a>` cascade — otherwise it will be silently overridden.
