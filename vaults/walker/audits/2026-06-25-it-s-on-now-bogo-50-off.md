# It's on now: BOGO 50% OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- DSW in my sender column, and yeah, my thumb was already moving — that's just where we are after two weeks of daily opens from these folks. But the moment this one loaded, I stopped dead. Flats. Strappy sandals. A young woman with colorful heels around her neck. "Take a Breather." I'm 62 years old looking for a cushioned walking shoe with some arch support, and this email handed me the women's casual aisle.
- The BOGO 50% off deal is real and I recognize it — they ran the same headline on June 22 and again a couple days before that. The math still works: buy one pair, get the second half off. But the entire email is styled, modeled, and stocked for women. No men's shoe in sight. The "Jean Miller × DSW" collab section sealed it — that is not for me. I kept scrolling hoping to hit a men's section. Never found it.
- I'd mark this read. Wouldn't click. Definitely wouldn't forward to a buddy. If I have a wife or daughter, maybe I'd show them the BOGO deal and let them sort it out.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF is front and center in the hero** — I see it before anything else, no confusion about what the offer is.
- **"Starting at $49.99" gives me a price anchor** — I know immediately whether this is in my range, and $49.99 for a pair with BOGO math is worth doing.
- **DSW brand trust is real** — I've been opening these for weeks; the sender name alone carries weight.
- **"Shop Wide Width" is visible** — that's an actual fit callout that matters to me, even if it's buried and aimed at women's boot calves.
- **Email loads clean** — no broken images, no giant white gaps, layout holds together on what I imagine is a phone screen.

## What's weak

- **Every model, every shoe, every section is women's** — this is not a men's email; someone sent me my wife's email by mistake.
- **"Take a Breather / Easy breezy flats" hero copy** — not one word of that speaks to a 62-year-old guy looking for cushioning and support.
- **Jean Miller × DSW collab** — I don't know who Jean Miller is and I don't think I'm supposed to.
- **No urgency anchor** — "It's on now" tells me nothing about when it ends. The June 22 BOGO had the same problem; I never know if I have hours or a week.
- **Cadence fatigue is starting to show** — this is at minimum the third BOGO-style DSW email in about three days. The deal is real, but when every email is "BOGO is on," the urgency signal gets dull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by gender and send me the men's version.** Every shoe, every model, every CTA should map to my purchasing behavior. If they have my purchase history (and they do — I'm in their loyalty program), they know I'm not buying women's flats.
- 2. **Give me a hard deadline.** "It's on now" means nothing. "BOGO ends Sunday midnight" gives me a reason to act today, not someday.
- 3. **Hero copy should speak comfort, not fashion.** "Walking shoes built for all day" or "Cushioned comfort, BOGO 50% off" would have me leaning in immediately.
- 4. **Men's picks module, even one row, mid-email** — a "For Him" section with two or three walking shoe thumbnails would save this send for my demographic.
- 5. **Rotate the creative.** The June 22 email had nearly the same BOGO headline. Two identical-feeling BOGO sends in three days flattens the impact.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned walkers, slip-ons, wide widths — starting at $49.99`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` declarations — invalid CSS**
All font-face blocks use `Src:` instead of `src:`. CSS property names are case-sensitive in strict parsers. This will silently fail font loading in clients that enforce it, falling back to system fonts.
```css
/* Broken — appears 10 times */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/.../original.woff") format('woff');
/* Correct */
src: url("https://braze-images.com/.../original.woff") format('woff');
```

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Suppresses user zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Missing `http-equiv` attributes on two meta tags**
```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, these are unrecognized pragmas; charset declaration and IE compatibility mode hints will not apply in clients that require the attribute.

**Contradictory `text-size-adjust` rules on the `*` selector**
```css
*{
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* preserve scaling */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;       /* suppress scaling */
  text-size-adjust: none;           /* suppress scaling */
}
```
`-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` and `text-size-adjust: none` are semantically opposite. The `none` value risks unreadably small text on mobile clients that honor the unprefixed property.

**HelveticaNow fonts provided only as `.ttf`**
```
https://braze-images.com/.../original.ttf?1755182939
```
TTF has narrower email client support than WOFF. Futura PT uses `.woff` (correct); HelveticaNow uses `.ttf` only. Clients that don't render TTF will fall back to system fonts, producing a rendering inconsistency across the same email.

**Cache-busting query strings inconsistently applied**
HelveticaNow URLs include `?1755182939`; Futura PT URLs do not. Inconsistency suggests different asset upload dates — not a breakage, but a signal that the Futura PT fonts may not be the latest versions in Braze.
