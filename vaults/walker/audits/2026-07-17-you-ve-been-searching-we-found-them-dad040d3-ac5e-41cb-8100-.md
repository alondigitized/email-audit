# You've Been Searching 🔍 We Found Them
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — the subject line grabbed me a little. "You've been searching" is the kind of thing that makes me think they actually looked at what I've been clicking on, and for a minute I wondered if they spotted me eyeing those Arch Fit walkers last week. So I opened it.
- Once I'm in, there's a lot going on. The 40% off banner is right up top, which I appreciate — don't make me hunt for the deal. And the Arch Fit section lands exactly where I live. That's the reason I buy Skechers in the first place: arch support, cushion, something I can pull on without sitting down for five minutes to lace it up. Seeing "ARCH FIT" bold and proud with its own shop button made me feel like they know me.
- But then I scroll and hit BOBS. That's a women's line. So suddenly I'm wondering if they actually know me at all, or if this was just a mass blast with a clever subject line. Add in the wall of links at the bottom — Women, Men, Kids, New Arrivals, Download the App, Pay Later — and by the time I reach the end, the email has lost the thread. I'd click the Arch Fit button. I would not forward this to a friend; too much noise for a simple share.

## What's working

- **40% off is front and center** — first thing I see at the top, no scrolling required. That's how you open a deal email.
- **Arch Fit gets its own dedicated section** — that's my bread and butter. Cushion, support, the reason I pick Skechers over Brooks on a given day.
- **Male model in the hero** — the guy on the left in the casual pants looks like somebody my age. That's rare; brands usually skew younger and I notice when they don't.
- **Cozy Fit section** — slip-ons and comfort fits are right in my lane. Two of my three comfort categories covered.
- **"Only at Skechers" exclusives call-out** — I like knowing I'm not going to find this somewhere cheaper at DSW. Gives me a reason to buy here instead of waiting for a Famous Footwear coupon.

## What's weak

- **BOBS** — I don't know why that's in my email. That's a women's line. If they're tracking my searches, they should know I've never clicked on a BOBS shoe in my life. This makes the "we found them" promise feel hollow.
- **"So Popular" is not my language** — I don't care what's trending. I care what fits and doesn't kill my feet by 3pm. The hero copy missed the comfort message entirely.
- **Two separate discounts, no clarity on stacking** — there's a 40% off banner at the top and a 20% off code partway down. Are those the same deal? Different items? Can I use both? I shouldn't have to do math to figure out my savings.
- **Bottom half is a kitchen sink** — Women/Men/Kids/App download/Pay Later/Curbside/Find a Store. After the Arch Fit section I was ready to click; instead I got buried in navigation links. Kills the momentum.
- **Still no preview text fix** — based on every Skechers email I've opened this month, I already knew walking in that my inbox showed garbled code instead of a real preheader. They haven't fixed it. At this point it's less annoying and more just sad.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with comfort, not popularity** — swap "So Popular" for something like "Built for All-Day Comfort" or "Your Arch Fit Match is Here." I'm not shopping for what's trending; I'm shopping for what won't wreck my feet.
- 2. **Pull BOBS out of this send** — segment this for men. A men's comfort-focused send with Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, and maybe a hands-free slip-on block would feel like it was made for me. BOBS signals they didn't filter at all.
- 3. **Clarify the two-offer situation** — one discount, clearly stated. Or explicitly say "stack your 20% member code on the 40% sale." Don't make me guess.
- 4. **Cut the bottom half in half** — App download, Kids, Pay Later, Find a Store — pick one utility callout. The rest belongs in a footer, not featured content.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — I've said it every week. It's raw code in my inbox. Someone needs to look at this.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Arch Fit, 40% Off — Your Comfort Match is Here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We Think These Are the Ones for You — 40% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Arch Fit and Cozy Fit styles built for all-day comfort — now 40% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your most-searched styles found. Shop Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, and more — 40% off.`

## Technical Audit — Skechers "You've Been Searching" (07/17/2026)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one confirmed deliverability failure (missing plain-text fallback) and a pervasive HTTP-over-HTTPS image problem affecting 13+ assets. No broken links were confirmed, but the mixed-content and tracking pixel issues create meaningful rendering and reputation risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixels — mixed-content and protocol risk**

- `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — HTTP pixel; will be blocked silently by Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail image proxies. Also missing `alt=""` attribute.
- Return Path pixel: `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` — loaded without protocol (`src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/..."` — relative-looking value with no `http://` or `https://`). This will resolve as a relative path and **fail to fire** in all clients.
- Krux/Salesforce DMP pixels (`beacon.krxd.net`) — loaded via HTTPS, no issues.
- Open pixel: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?...` — HTTPS, no issue.

**26 click-redirect links** — all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Domain resolves correctly; no dead-link probe possible through the redirect layer, but the tracking domain itself is live.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (13 assets blocked in proxy-enforcing clients)**

All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets use `http://`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com rewrite or block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected images:

| Asset | Impact |
|---|---|
| Skechers logo (`dde00662...png`) | Header logo broken |
| Unnamed promo image (`49468f73...png`) | Missing alt text + HTTP |
| App Store badge | HTTP |
| Google Play badge | HTTP |
| Curbside Pickup icon | HTTP |
| AfterPay icon | HTTP |
| Find A Store icon | HTTP |
| Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons | HTTP (3 assets) |

**Alt text gaps**

- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — no `alt=""`.
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — no `alt` attribute; if image breaks, no fallback text renders.

**CSS `display` conflict on mobile/desktop toggle**

`.mobile-content { display: block }` / `.desktop-content { display: none }` are set globally, then overridden at `min-width: 768px`. Gmail strips `<style>` blocks in some render paths, meaning mobile content would display at all breakpoints in those clients with no desktop alternative.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — prevents user zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern for low-vision users; violates WCAG 1.4.4.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- Campaign ID `MKG_US_L60D_U_MULTI_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_07172026_B` appears in the Krux impression pixel and the ink1000 pixel — consistent campaign tagging at the pixel level.
- All 26 click links route through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects; UTM parameter carrythrough to `skechers.com` landing pages cannot be verified without resolving the redirects, but SFMC's standard link decorator typically appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`. **Recommend verifying** one resolved URL manually to confirm UTM params are populated and match the campaign ID above.
