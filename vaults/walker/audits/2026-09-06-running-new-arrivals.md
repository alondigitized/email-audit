# Running new arrivals
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- A pack of young folks running through some city street at dawn — that's the hero, right up top. Then "New arrivals, just landed." Nothing about cushioning, nothing about arch support, nothing that says "comfort" anywhere I can see. This is a running shoe email through and through, and I haven't run anywhere in twenty years.
- There's a decent product grid — eight shoes lined up clean, which I do appreciate, no clutter — but they're all performance runners: SuperComp Rebel, FuelCell Rebel v5, Ellipse. Racing flats and foam-stack trainers, not the walking shoe I put on to go to the grocery store. Then it swings into "performance running essentials" with a couple of twenty-somethings in short-shorts, which tells me exactly who this brand thinks its customer is, and it isn't me.
- Bottom line: I'm scrolling past this one. The "Shop the sale" at the bottom with up to 30% off is the only thing that gets my eyebrow up, but by the time I get there I've already checked out. Not forwarding it, not clicking, straight to read-and-ignore.

## What's working

- The product grid is genuinely clean — eight shoes, clear names underneath each, no visual clutter or overlap.
- "Up to 30% off select styles with LABORDAY" near the bottom is a real, specific offer with a code I can actually see.
- Two clear buttons throughout — "Shop now" and "Visit a store" — good if I were actually in the market for this stuff.
- No rendering bugs — everything loaded, nothing broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- Hero image is a crew of runners in athletic gear sprinting through a city — nothing about comfort, walking, or slip-on ease.
- Every shoe pictured is a performance running model — SuperComp Rebel, FuelCell Rebel, Ellipse — not a casual walker in the bunch.
- The "Performance running essentials" section doubles down with shorts and running tops modeled by two people in their twenties — I'm 62, that's not my aisle.
- The one offer that would actually get me to click (30% off, LABORDAY code) is buried at the very bottom after five other sections.
- Same beat as their 8/28 send ("Comfortable, versatile, reliable") — this brand keeps mailing me runner's gear like I'm training for something.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the sale and a comfort-lifestyle shoe up top instead of burying LABORDAY at the bottom — that's the one thing in here with pull for someone like me.
- 2. Swap the hero image for a walking/casual shoe on someone closer to my age, not a sprint pack in short-shorts.
- 3. Segment this list — if New Balance knows I've never clicked a running-specific email, stop sending me the RC Collection and Rebel line and start showing me the 574s, walking shoes, or something with real cushioning.
- 4. Cut the "performance running essentials" apparel block entirely for a comfort-shopper segment — it's dead weight if I'm not training.
