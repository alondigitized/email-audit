# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- There's that pink floral D'Lites again — fourth time this week, still under a fake "cart" banner. "We Noticed You Noticing Us..." is cute copy, but I never noticed that shoe, and I never had a cart. I clicked on nothing, put nothing in a bag, and yet here's Skechers telling me my order is "still waiting." It's not waiting, it's not mine.
- The one thing that actually caught my eye scrolling down was the grid of "more options" — I saw men's slip-ins in there, a boat shoe-looking thing, some Roth Shoes with actual arch support in the name. That's closer to my lane than a floral sneaker with embroidery on it. And the 40% off summer styles banner near the bottom is a real, dated offer — that's worth something.
- But leading with a shoe I have zero interest in, for the fourth straight day, buries the one part of this email that might've worked. Not clicking, not forwarding. This is getting marked as read and I'm getting closer to just unsubscribing at this point.

## What's working

- The 40% off banner near the bottom is a specific, credible offer with real terms in the fine print below.
- The "more options" grid actually includes men's slip-ins and arch-fit styles — that's product I'd consider, if it were up top instead of buried under the fake cart.
- Layout is clean, big readable text, one shoe at a time — easy enough to scroll on my phone without squinting.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup up front tells me they know their audience shops practical, not just impulse.

## What's weak

- Same D'Lites - Spring Bloom shoe, same "your cart" fiction, going on four-plus days now — I never touched this shoe.
- Hero product is a women's floral sneaker; nothing about it says "62-year-old guy" at all, so the whole top third of the email is dead space for me.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button implies I started something I never started — that's not a nudge, that's just wrong, and it makes me trust the brand less each time I see it.
- No arch support, cushioning, or comfort-fit language anywhere near the top — the stuff that'd actually get me to tap in.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the fake-cart framing entirely — if I never added anything to a cart, don't tell me I did. Lead with the 40% off sale and a men's comfort shoe instead.
- 2. Swap the hero image for one of those slip-ins or arch-fit shoes already sitting further down the email — that's the actual product-market fit for a guy like me.
- 3. Move the 40% off banner up near the top, above the fold, since that's the one piece with a real, time-bound offer.
- 4. Stop repeating this exact creative — I've seen this shoe and this "did you forget" pitch on the 11th, 12th, 14th, and now the 16th. Rotate in the actual new stuff, like that Workout Walker from yesterday.
