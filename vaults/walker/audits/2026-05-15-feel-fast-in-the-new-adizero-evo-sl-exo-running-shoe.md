# Feel fast in the new ADIZERO EVO SL EXO running shoe
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- I recognized adidas right away in the sender line — that's enough to get me to open it. But about two seconds in, I knew this email wasn't for me. "Feel fast" and "EXOSKELETON upper fit system" — that's race-day language. I'm not training for anything. I want my feet to feel *good* at the end of a walk, not fast at a finish line.
- The bottom half with "UP TO 40% OFF" caught my attention the way a sale sign in a window does — it slows me down. But by then I've already waded through a performance shoe I have no interest in and a FIFA World Cup giveaway that I'd have to drive to a store for. That's two things I don't need before I get to the one thing that might make me click.
- I'd probably scan it, wonder if there's a comfortable walking shoe buried in that 40% section, and close it. Wouldn't forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- **adidas is a brand I recognize** — I've worn their stuff before, so I didn't dismiss it outright
- **"UP TO 40% OFF" is clear and visible** — no guesswork about what the promotion is
- **Clean layout, no visual clutter** — easy to read, nothing jumping around or broken
- **World Cup giveaway with an actual date (June 19)** — at least one real deadline in the email, even if it's not for me

## What's weak

- **"Feel fast" is not my language** — I need to feel comfortable, not fast; this copy signals the shoe isn't built for me
- **EXOSKELETON / ADIZERO EVO SL EXO** — these product names mean nothing to me; I have to work too hard to understand what I'm even looking at
- **No comfort or support language anywhere** — cushioning, arch support, wide toe box — none of it; they never made the case I should care
- **No clear CTA button visible** — I see the sale headline but there's no obvious "Shop Now" button telling me where to tap
- **The models are clearly serious athletes** — nobody in this email looks like me, or shops like me

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero module for something in the sale section** — lead with the 40% off, put the performance shoe below for whoever wants it; that's the only reason I'd click
- 2. **Add one comfort-focused shoe to the "Favorites" grid** — even one walking shoe or Cloudfoam style in the sale block gives me a reason to tap through
- 3. **Drop the "EXOSKELETON" terminology** — if you want a broader audience, translate the tech into a benefit ("wrapped fit for all-day wear" reads better than EXOSKELETON to a 62-year-old)
- 4. **Add a CTA button** — "Shop the Sale" with a real tap target, large enough to hit without squinting

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas ADIZERO EVO SL EXO Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken click-redirect URL that will 404 for affected recipients, and a completely absent plain-text fallback. Eight repeated HTTP (non-HTTPS) image references and three images missing alt text are the primary rendering risks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect — unknown URL type `/g/`**

The QA probe resolved the destination URL to a path prefixed `/g/` which the redirect service reports as an unknown type:

```
unknown url type: '/g/494a7e65-47bf-4b29-9076-4b03725cce97
  ?txn=07c0bf80-500f-11f1-b096-82575556174b'
```

Outer redirect: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/494a7e65-47bf-4b29-9076-4b03725cce97?cp_tp=v3...`

This is a broken destination — clicks that route through this URL will land on an error page. The affected content block is the image/CTA associated with asset `494a7e65-47bf-4b29-9076-4b03725cce97`.

**[WARN] 37 tracking links not probed** — click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP checker, so additional dead links cannot be ruled out from this scan alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources — 8 instances of the same asset**

`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt: "arrow") is loaded over plain HTTP eight times:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-...jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. These arrow indicators will be invisible for most recipients.

**[WARN] HTTP tracking pixel**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e1ff13955-5d7aa918-4801-45e8-94f2-39db9517dbae-000000/cRRhrb91EodBbZXxSNlWkf9Nx...
```

This open/read-tracking beacon fires over HTTP. Major clients will block it, causing underreported open rates.

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 3 images**

| Asset | Source |
|---|---|
| `14986549-7195-4dbb-b2f8-4d5e4b2903b6.png` | `https://image.link.adidas.com/...` |
| `494a7e65-47bf-4b29-9076-4b03725cce97` | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...` |
| `cRRhrb91EodBbZXxSNlWkf9NxiXkjHB-JI5cVJEOhZQ=452` | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/...` |

Images without `alt` attributes render as broken boxes in image-off environments (Outlook default) and fail WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` blocks**

The `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` declarations appear in two separate `<style>` blocks with identical `src: local(...)` fallback chains. No web font URLs are present — the custom typefaces will only render if the recipient happens to have them installed locally. This is likely intentional (email font embedding limitations) but the duplication adds unnecessary payload.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unresolved merge token syntax (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`) visible in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is absent**

QA confirmed the text/plain MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a plain-text part, but its absence:

- Triggers spam filters that weight multipart/alternative ratio
- Violates best practice for WCAG and screen-reader accessibility
- Will be flagged by Gmail's spam classifier as a phishing/commercial signal

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are present in the footer (confirmed by footer CSS classes in source: `.mobile-footer-padding-block`, `.mobile-privacy-transactional`), so the CAN-SPAM structural requirements appear met.

`<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW" />` is correctly set — no search indexing risk.

`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` is set, which suppresses referrer headers on outbound clicks. This is intentional but worth noting as it means analytics platforms relying on `document.referrer` on landing pages will see direct traffic instead of email traffic for non-UTM-tagged links.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully verify — 37 redirect-chain links were skipped by the HTTP probe. The one link that was resolved (`dv.adidas.com/o/494a7e65...`) is broken at the redirect layer, so UTM passthrough to the destination is moot for that link.

No issues found in the HTML indicating raw/unparameterized `adidas.com` links that would bypass click tracking entirely, but the skipped-link gap means this is an incomplete check.
