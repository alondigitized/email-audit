# Soooo many deals inside ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Another one from Famous Footwear — at this point I recognize their sender address the way I recognize my mail carrier's truck. But "Soooo many deals inside" with a little arrow? That's the kind of subject line you write when you don't know what's actually in the email. And the preview text — I kid you not — is a tracking URL. A raw link. I can see it right there in my inbox before I even open. That alone almost made me swipe left.
- Once I got inside, the email is basically a department store clearance rack dumped in my inbox. Nike on sale, Jordan sneakers, 60% off kids sandals, women's clogs at $109.99. I'm a 62-year-old man who wants to walk the block without his knees complaining. Most of this email isn't built for me. Then I scroll down — and there it is. Birkenstock. "Limited time price drops." Yesterday they sent me one with Birkenstock in the actual subject line and it earned its place. Today they buried it three sections deep, below Jordan sneakers I haven't touched since 1996.
- I'd scroll to the Birkenstock section, maybe glance at the Brooks pricing, and then close it. I wouldn't forward this to anyone — there's nothing specific enough to say "hey, check this out." Mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- **BOGO banner is impossible to miss** — it's the first thing I see, big and bold, and the offer is real. Famous Footwear knows BOGO is their hook.
- **Birkenstock section is in here** — I've had their Arizonas for six summers and yesterday's Birkenstock email pulled me in; seeing a "limited time price drop" section here gives me a reason to scroll down.
- **Brooks shows up near the bottom** — that's a brand I actually trust for walking shoes; the price is visible and legible.
- **Clean layout, nothing broken** — text didn't overlap, images loaded, the "Shop Now" buttons are easy enough to tap on a phone screen.
- **Sender name is immediately recognizable** — I know Famous Footwear; there's no confusion about who sent this.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — I can read it in my inbox before I open. That's embarrassing and it nearly cost them the open.
- **Subject line tells me nothing specific** — "Soooo many deals" could be any retailer on any day. The extra O's don't make it more appealing; they make it feel lazy.
- **Kids sandals and women's clogs are filler for me** — I had to scroll past two sections completely irrelevant to a 62-year-old man to find anything I'd buy.
- **Jordan section is dead air** — they sent me a Jordan launch email five days ago, I gave it a 8 because of nostalgia, but I still didn't click. Featuring it again in a deal roundup doesn't change that.
- **No comfort-specific language anywhere** — not a single word about cushioning, arch support, or easy-on fit. The brands I care about are in here, but they're presented like any other item in the sale bin.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the brands that match the subscriber, not a catch-all BOGO** — if Famous Footwear knows I've been clicking Birkenstock and Brooks content, put those up front. The algorithm should do that work.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately** — a tracking URL rendering as the preheader is a technical failure. Any real copy — even "Birkenstock price drops + Brooks deals this week" — would have doubled my open intent.
- 3. **Kill the kids and women's sections or move them to a different send** — or at least ensure the men's comfort sections appear within the first two scrolls on mobile.
- 4. **Be specific in the hero** — instead of BOGO + vague arrows, tell me what's dropping in price. "Birkenstock Arizonas down" or "Brooks walking shoes under $80" gives me a reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Birkenstock & Brooks — prices just dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 30% off the brands your feet actually know`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Birkenstock limited-time drops + Brooks walking shoes on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO still on — plus Birkenstock price cuts inside`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Soooo many deals inside"

### 1. Technical Summary

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 27 images**
Every content image (hero GIF, product shots, brand badges) carries `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. QA flagged all 27. The logo image `3b6a7dc1-...png` is wrapped in an `<a>` tag and has `alt=""` — screen readers will announce the link as unlabeled.

**Logo link unlabeled**
```html
<a href="https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7...">
  <img src="...3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png" alt="" ...>
</a>
```
Needs `alt="Famous Footwear"` at minimum so screen reader users understand where the link goes.

**Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. While most clients tolerate this, the correct form is `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`.

**Zoom disabled on mobile**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` prevent users from zooming — a WCAG 1.4.4 violation and a friction point for low-vision users. Remove both constraints.

**DOCTYPE**
HTML 4.01 Transitional is expected for email; no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / physical address**: Footer is truncated in the provided source. Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link appear to be present (unsubscribe URL begins at end of truncated block: `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7...`). Cannot fully verify without complete HTML.

**Authentication headers**: Not auditable from HTML source. Sending domain is `email.famousfootwear.com` (subdomain). DKIM, SPF, and DMARC records for this subdomain should be verified separately via `dig TXT email.famousfootwear.com` and a live header capture.

**Plain-text URL density — deliverability risk**
QA flagged: `12,137 / 15,215 chars are URLs (80%)` in the plain-text part. SpamAssassin and Barracuda penalize plain-text bodies where URL content dominates over readable prose. The plain-text version should contain human-readable summary copy, not a dump of encoded tracking URLs.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 33 CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=[encoded]`. Because QA skipped all 33 tracking links, destination URLs were not probed — UTM parameter presence on landing pages is **unconfirmed**.

If UTM injection is handled server-side by the ESP's redirect, this is acceptable. If not, any sessions arriving on `famousfootwear.com` from this send will appear as direct traffic in analytics rather than attributed to email.

**Recommended verification**: Manually follow one representative CTA link and inspect the final URL for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters.
