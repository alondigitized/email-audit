# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** May 4, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score:** 4/10

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Today's journey was a mixed bag with one critical failure that would have stopped me cold. The men's navigation and product pages continue to work reasonably well for a guy like me — big text, clear categories, Slip-ins front and center in the menu. But three serious problems drag this down: the homepage has zero awareness that I'm a 62-year-old man who just logged in, the cart page rendered completely blank (a full crash), and search returned Women's shoes first for "slip in." That blank cart alone would have sent me to a competitor. Not an improvement over yesterday.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**Same as yesterday. The blank cart is a critical regression that blocks purchase completion.** The navigation improvements don't offset a checkout pipeline that literally shows nothing.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's navigation menu is genuinely good.** When I tapped the hamburger menu and drilled into Men, the layout was clean full-width rows with big tap targets. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is listed right there as a quick link — I didn't have to hunt. "Comfort Technologies" is a category I'd actually tap. This works.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category page is clean.** 272 results, clear heading, width filter chips (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) prominent — that matters to me at my age. Product thumbnails loaded fine.
- **Product detail page communicates value clearly.** The BOGO 50% OFF deal is called out right under the price, the Slip-ins badge is visible, 69 reviews and 5 stars are reassuring. $115 is steep but the deal softens it.
- **"Add to Cart" button is large and blue.** Full-width, high contrast — easy to tap with older fingers.
- **BOGO banner is persistent across the journey.** I can always see the deal at the top. Good for a price-conscious shopper like me.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Critical:**
- **Cart page is completely blank (Step 8).** White screen. Nothing. No product, no price, no checkout button. If I'd actually selected my size and tapped "Add to Cart," I would have landed on an empty page and assumed something went wrong. I'm not a tech troubleshooter — I'd close the app and give up. This is a lost sale.

**Significant:**
- **Homepage shows zero personalization after login.** The entire above-the-fold experience is Mother's Day: flowers, pink, a mother and daughter. I'm a 62-year-old man who just logged in as a Skechers Plus member. There is no acknowledgment that I exist — no "Welcome back, Walker," no men's featured content, nothing. The secondary banner is about Apparel I don't care about.
- **Login step didn't show a login screen.** Step 3 captured the same Mother's Day homepage as Steps 1 and 2. I can't tell if I was even recognized as logged in. No visual confirmation of my account status.
- **Search for "slip in" returns Women's shoes first.** The top two results visible are both labeled "Women's." I typed "slip in" — I'm shopping for myself. A logged-in male account should surface men's results first. 1,021 results with no immediate gender filter is overwhelming.

**Minor:**
- **Size selector in Step 7 shows a gap in sizes** — visible options jump from 7.5 to 10.5, skipping 8, 8.5, 9, 9.5, 10. Either those are sold out (which should be shown more clearly) or the grid was cut off in the screenshot. Either way, confusing.
- **Skechers Plus upsell appears on the product page** even though I'm supposedly already a member. Should say "You're earning points on this purchase" instead of "Join for Free Shipping."

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is P0. A blank cart is an unrecoverable failure. Whatever JavaScript error or data loading issue is causing this kills conversions.

2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in male users.** If I'm signed in, lead with Men's, Slip-ins, comfort content — not Mother's Day. Even a simple "Welcome back, Walker — shop Men's Slip-ins" strip above the hero would signal the site knows who I am.

3. **Gender-filter search results for logged-in users.** If my account is male, "slip in" search should default to Men's or at minimum show a prominent "Men's / Women's" toggle at the top. Showing Women's products first to a male shopper is a miss.

4. **Confirm login visually.** After logging in, show my name or a personalization signal in the header (e.g., "Hi, Walker" next to the account icon). Right now there's no feedback that login did anything.

5. **Acknowledge Plus membership on product pages.** Swap the "Join Skechers Plus" pitch for "You're a Plus member — free shipping applies" to reinforce loyalty value.

---

The plumbing for finding Slip-ins is decent — the Men's menu works, the category page is clean, the product page communicates the deal. But the cart is broken (blank page), the homepage ignores me entirely, and search doesn't know I'm a man. I'd have abandoned this purchase at the cart page. **Score: 4/10 — unchanged from yesterday.** The blank cart is new or at least newly visible; if that's a regression, this is trending worse, not better.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform: Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), evidenced by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths. 20+ third-party scripts injected in `<head>` across all pages. Bot-protection layer (Kasada SDK, identified by UUID path pattern and `x-kpsdk-v` query param) is actively rate-limiting the audit tool, masking real cart performance. LCP and CLS data was not captured — those are the two most user-impactful Core Web Vitals and should be treated as unknowns until re-measured.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): ARIA role used without required ancestor container. Likely a carousel or list widget where `role="option"` or `role="tab"` elements are orphaned from their required `role="listbox"` / `role="tablist"` parent.

**Serious**
- `link-name` (Homepage): Anchor elements with no discernible text — likely icon-only links (social icons, arrows) missing `aria-label` or `<title>` on inline SVG.
- `document-title` (View Cart): `<title>` element is empty or absent. This breaks screen reader page identification and is an SEO regression.
- `html-has-lang` (View Cart): `<html>` missing `lang` attribute on the cart page. All other pages set `lang="en"`. Suggests the cart is rendered via a separate template that skips the standard `<head>` scaffold — check the SFCC cart template (`cart.isml` or equivalent).

**Moderate**
- Duplicate `<main>` landmark and `<main>` not top-level (Homepage): Multiple `<main>` regions, likely from Monetate or CMS injection inserting a second `<main>` inside another landmark.
- `heading-order` (Homepage): Non-sequential heading levels (e.g., `<h1>` → `<h3>`), common when marketing components are assembled independently.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` (Homepage, Search): `<aside>` nested inside a non-body/non-section container, likely a sidebar or recommendation widget.
- `landmark-unique` (Homepage, Search): Multiple landmarks of the same type without distinguishing labels (`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`).
- `region` violations (Homepage, Search): Content outside any landmark — likely footer sub-sections or ad injection zones.
- `page-has-heading-one` (View Cart): No `<h1>` on cart page — confirms the cart template is structurally incomplete.

---

## SEO

- **Missing `<title>` on View Cart** is an indexability defect. If Googlebot crawls `/cart/` (it shouldn't, but canonical/robots enforcement should be verified), it will see an untitled page.
- The `<html lang="en">` attribute is correctly set on all pages except View Cart. Hreflang or language signals on the cart template should be patched at the SFCC template level.
- No structural SEO issues visible in the sampled HTML for category/PDP pages. SFCC's standard breadcrumb and canonical link patterns appear to be in use (not directly visible in the truncated source, but standard for the platform).

**Issues:**
- View Cart TTFB at 155ms indicates a synchronous server-side cart assembly (SFCC cart controller is not edge-cacheable by design). The 429 on `/cart/` compounds this — if Kasada challenges fire before the cart response, real-user TTFB is higher.
- **LCP and CLS are unrecorded across all 8 steps.** These are the highest-weighted Core Web Vitals for Google ranking. Missing data means the audit cannot assess render performance or layout stability.
- **Duplicate script tags detected:**
  - `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` loaded **twice** on every page (two `<script async vice-managed-src=...>` tags with identical src).
  - `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` loaded **twice** on the Men category page.
  - These duplicates fire two network requests and two script executions.
- **20+ third-party scripts in `<head>`**, including: TikTok Pixel (2 files), Pinterest, Google Tag Manager (3+ destination instances), Bing Ads, Reddit Ads, Yahoo (×2), Quantum Metric, Impact, Taboola, NextDoor, Snapchat, Facebook Pixel, Bloomreach tracker, Monetate (2 separate instances), Tune/GO2SDK, Pulse Insights, WebFontLoader (cdnjs). All are `async`, but combined they represent significant parser overhead and post-load CPU cost.
- **WebFont Loader (`webfont.js` v1.6.28 from cdnjs)** triggers a FOIT/FOUT cycle for Mulish (5 weights loaded). The `--vh` CSS custom property set inline suggests a JS-based viewport height polyfill is running synchronously.

---

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` set as an inline style on `<html>` — this is a JS polyfill for `100vh` on mobile Safari. The fractional precision (`6.640000000000001`) is floating-point noise; the value should be rounded. Modern mobile Safari supports `dvh`/`svh` units natively; the polyfill can be removed for browsers shipping since 2022.
- No `viewport` meta tag visible in the truncated source samples, but this is almost certainly present (SFCC standard templates include it). Needs confirmation.
- The `vice-managed-src` attribute pattern (used by TrustArc to gate scripts behind consent) means consent-blocked scripts load zero bytes initially but switch to full payloads on consent accept — on mobile networks this is a latency cliff after the consent banner interaction.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — Kasada Bot Protection (5 errors on fingerprint endpoint)**

The path `149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.391` is the Kasada client-side fingerprinting SDK calling home. The 429s indicate the audit environment is being flagged as non-human and throttled. **This is expected behavior for automated audit tools, but it also means:**
  - The cart 429 (`/cart/`) may be a downstream consequence of Kasada denying the session, not a load issue on the cart endpoint itself.
  - Real users on legitimate browsers will not hit these 429s under normal conditions.
  - The fingerprinting SDK fires on every page navigation, adding a network round-trip to every step of the funnel.

**CORS Block — Stylitics stylesheet**

```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers&version=a633fea7cb'
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```

The Stylitics "complete the look" / outfit hotspot widget cannot load its stylesheet. This is a misconfiguration on Stylitics' CDN (`web-assets.stylitics.com`) — their server is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com`. The widget will render broken or silently degrade. This requires Stylitics support to correct their CDN headers; Skechers can work around it by self-hosting the stylesheet or using a proxy endpoint.

**`net::ERR_FAILED`** — One resource failed with a network-level error (not HTTP error). URL not captured. Could be an ad pixel on a blocked domain in the audit environment.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before next release**
1. **View Cart template**: Add `<title>`, `lang="en"` on `<html>`, `<h1>`, and `<main>` to the SFCC cart ISML template. These are critical accessibility and SEO failures on the checkout entry point.
2. **Deduplicate scripts**: Remove the duplicate `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` tags (present on every page) and the duplicate `tags.creativecdn.com` tag on category pages. Audit the TrustArc / tag manager configuration that injects these.

**P1 — Near-term**
3. **Fix `aria-required-parent`** (critical axe violation): Audit carousel/tab/list ARIA widget markup on the homepage. Ensure `role="option"` / `role="tab"` elements are always wrapped in their required parent container.
4. **Fix `link-name`** (serious): Add `aria-label` to all icon-only links on homepage (social links, sliders, CTAs).
5. **Engage Stylitics** to fix `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on `web-assets.stylitics.com` for the `skechers.com` origin.
6. **Capture LCP and CLS**: Re-run the audit with Lighthouse or CrUX data. TTFB alone cannot assess Core Web Vitals; LCP and CLS are the ranking signals.

**P2 — Planned improvement**
7. **Remove `--vh` JS polyfill**: Migrate to CSS `dvh` / `svh` units. Eliminates one synchronous JS execution on every page load on mobile.
8. **Script audit**: Consolidate the 3+ GTM destination instances and 2 Monetate scripts into a single tag manager configuration. The current setup fires redundant requests on every navigation.
9. **Kasada + cart interaction**: Verify that Kasada challenge failures do not cascade into cart 429s for legitimate users — specifically, confirm `/cart/` has a separate rate-limit budget from the fingerprint endpoint.
