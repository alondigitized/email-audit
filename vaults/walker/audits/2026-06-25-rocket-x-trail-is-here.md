# Rocket X Trail is here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- HOKA dropped a new trail shoe in my inbox and I'll admit the name "Rocket X Trail" made me look twice — sounds fast, sounds aggressive. Then I opened it and saw "Gravel Rush" splashed across a hero image of what looks like a woman mid-sprint, and the copy starts talking about "demands of gravel racing," "carbon plate," "targeted traction," and "races that aren't for the faint of heart." I am 62 years old. I am absolutely for the faint of heart. The last race I ran was chasing a parking spot at the DSW on Black Friday.
- The email is clean and loads fine. HOKA knows how to build a good-looking send. But this shoe — carbon plate, ultralight racing geometry, gravel course specific — is aimed at someone who lines up at a starting corral at 5am. That is not me, and nothing in this email pretended otherwise. Even that top banner offering free socks for a $200 spend feels like I'm subsidizing someone else's race kit.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Won't click, won't forward. The one guy I'd forward a HOKA email to is my buddy Ken who does trail ultras — but he already knows about every new shoe before HOKA does, so he doesn't need me.

## What's working

- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, no text collisions, the shoe photography is sharp. HOKA's production quality is consistently good.
- **"Shop Rocket X Trail" button is unambiguous.** Clear verb, clear noun, hard to miss. They didn't write "Discover" or "Explore More."
- **New product announcement is timely.** This isn't a recycled Father's Day promo — unlike the "Just in time" email from June 20th that still smelled like stale gift-wrap, this one is at least current.
- **Product features are called out simply.** Specialized carbon plate / Ultralight and propulsive / Targeted traction — three bullets, no jargon overload. I could read it without zooming.

## What's weak

- **Wrong shoe for me entirely.** Carbon plate + gravel racing + "faint of heart" language — this is a performance racing product. I shop HOKA for the cushioning platform, not carbon propulsion.
- **Female model in the hero.** HOKA markets to everyone and that's fine, but I'm a 62-year-old man and the primary hero image doesn't put anyone who looks like me in the frame.
- **Free socks threshold is too high for a shoe I'm not buying.** $200 to unlock two pairs of socks is a deal only if I was already planning the purchase. I wasn't.
- **No comfort or cushioning language anywhere.** Not one word about what the foam feels like under your foot, how it handles all-day wear, whether it's walkable or strictly race-day only. That's the information I'd need.
- **Cadence is still heavy.** This is the sixth email in eleven days. "Stability without the bulk" on June 17th actually earned my attention — but they've been in my inbox so often lately that I'm starting to reach for the archive button faster each time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send away from comfort walkers entirely.** If I'm in the HOKA list because I bought the Bondi or the Clifton, don't send me a carbon-plate gravel racer. Send it to the people who bought the Speed Goat or the Tecton. This is a targeting miss, not a creative miss.
- 2. **If you must send it broadly, add one comfort bridge.** A single line like "Built fast — but the cushioning still feels like HOKA" gives me a reason to look twice. Right now the copy says nothing to a guy who just wants his feet to stop aching.
- 3. **Put a man in the email.** Not asking for a gray-haired retiree specifically, but a male model would help. The entire visual treatment reads female athletic.
- 4. **Ease off the cadence.** Six emails in eleven days is too many. Space them out; each one lands harder when it's not crowding the previous one.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` — conflicting values**
```html
<!-- tag 1 -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<!-- tag 2 (duplicate) -->
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
Tag 1 omits `user-scalable`; tag 2 sets it to `yes`. Client behavior on duplicate viewport tags is undefined. Remove one; keep whichever reflects intent.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset declaration**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Correct form for email (required by Outlook and legacy clients):
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Apple Mail, Gmail web) surface the title in notification previews or tab labels. Should be a descriptive fallback string, e.g. `Rocket X Trail is here — HOKA`.

**Malformed CSS in `@media (max-width: 480px)` block**
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Both rules have missing numeric values before `px` and `.nav_border` is missing a color value. These rules will be silently dropped by all parsers, meaning any element with class `nav_padding` or `nav_border` gets no mobile padding/border override. Verify whether these classes are used in the body; if so, the mobile layout will be broken.

**`font-display: swap` in `@font-face`**
Near-zero support across email clients (Apple Mail partial). Harmless but adds dead weight; remove to reduce CSS payload.

**Universal selector `border:none` reset**
```css
* { border:none; }
```
This strips borders from all elements globally, including any intentional hairline dividers. Scope border resets to `img, table, td` rather than `*`.
