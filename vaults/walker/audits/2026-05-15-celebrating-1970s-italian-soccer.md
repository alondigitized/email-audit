# Celebrating 1970s Italian soccer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see when I open this: a World Cup ticket giveaway. Okay, that pulls my eye — two tickets to a FIFA match in Los Angeles sounds interesting for about five seconds. Then I scroll down and realize the entire email is selling me a soccer jersey inspired by the Italian national team from the 1970s. Not a shoe. Not a slipper. Not even an athletic sandal. A jersey.
- I'm on Adidas's list because I bought a pair of their running shoes on sale a while back, not because I'm dressing like a Serie A supporter. The model in this email is half my age and looks like he's about to play a pickup match. That's not me. I wear comfortable casual shoes; I don't wear kit. This email doesn't know anything about why I opened it or what I buy.
- I'd mark this read and move on. Wouldn't click anything except maybe the giveaway link out of curiosity, and I wouldn't forward it to a single person I know.

## What's working

- The World Cup giveaway banner at the top is a genuine attention-grabber — free tickets are hard to scroll past cold.
- The Italy blue colorway is sharp and the layout is clean; nothing is cluttered or fighting for space.
- Adidas is a name I recognize and trust enough not to unsubscribe on the spot.
- The June 19 deadline gives the giveaway a credible, time-stamped urgency.

## What's weak

- There is not a single shoe in this email — completely off-base for how I use Adidas's list.
- The model is young, athletic, and dressed for a pitch; zero demographic overlap with a 62-year-old who shops for cushioning and arch support.
- "Learn More" as a CTA tells me nothing — learn more about the giveaway? The jersey? Both? Pick one and commit.
- No price, no discount, no shipping offer; the only hook is a sweepstakes, which is not a buying decision.
- The "1970s Italian soccer" nostalgia angle presumes I care about soccer heritage fashion — I don't, and most guys my age who shop Adidas for comfort shoes don't either.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with footwear, not apparel.** If you're emailing me, you know I bought shoes — make the hero product relevant to that. The giveaway can stay as a secondary block.
- 2. **Fix the CTA.** "Enter to Win Tickets" for the sweepstakes and "Shop Italy Collection" for the jersey — two clear buttons, two destinations. Don't make me guess.
- 3. **Show someone who looks like me.** A 50s–60s guy in comfortable casual gear wearing the brand would go a long way. The current model doesn't represent my purchase decision at all.
- 4. **Add a product offer.** A 20% off code, free standard shipping, or even an early-access price makes this email actionable. Right now it's a catalog page with a contest attached.
