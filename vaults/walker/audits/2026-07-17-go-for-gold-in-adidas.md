# Go for gold in adidas 🏆 ⚽️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear, again. At this point I could recognize their sender address in my sleep — this is at least the seventh or eighth email from them I've opened this week, and I'm starting to feel like the inbox equivalent of a junk mail pile. The subject promised adidas and gold, which made me think for half a second they were pushing something like a sale on the Adilette slides I've been eyeing. Wrong. I opened it and got hit with soccer. Cleats, field gear, the whole World Cup marketing machine.
- Look — I watched the World Cup. That doesn't mean I need soccer cleats. I'm 62. My feet need cushioning, arch support, and a shoe I can slip on without bending over. This email spent most of its real estate on youth-oriented athletic gear aimed at people half my age running around on grass. The BOGO offer up top is genuinely good — that's what saves this from the trash — but I had to wade through models who looked like college athletes before I got to anything remotely in my lane.
- I'd click the BOGO out of habit, not excitement. I would not forward this to a friend. I'd probably buy something and feel vaguely annoyed the whole time about the soccer theme.

## What's working

- **BOGO is big and impossible to miss.** The headline dominates the top of the email — no squinting required, no fine print to find first. That offer structure works on me.
- **"For off the field" section.** The casual adidas slides section down the scroll actually got my attention — that's the kind of footwear I'd consider. Smart to include it, even if buried.
- **Layout is clean and orderly.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no visual chaos. Email renders fine, easy to follow the grid of products even on a phone.
- **3X points callout.** I noticed the loyalty multiplier near the bottom. I won't let points drive my decision, but I like seeing it acknowledged — makes me feel like a known customer rather than a stranger.
- **Consistent brand voice.** Famous Footwear has a look, and this email matches it. After a week of their emails, I know what I'm getting visually.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL.** Before I even opened this email, my inbox was showing me a wall of `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=ABB7...` — that is not copy, that is a technical failure. It made the email look broken or suspicious before I even tapped it.
- **The soccer framing is almost completely irrelevant to me.** "Go for gold" + soccer ball emoji + field cleats = not my department. I'm shopping for walking shoes and comfortable casuals, not athletic performance gear.
- **Young, athletic models throughout.** I didn't see a single person in this email who looked like they were in my decade. Demographically, I felt invisible.
- **No comfort language anywhere.** Not a word about cushioning, arch support, or fit. For a brand that knows I cross-shop Hoka and New Balance, that's a missed opportunity.
- **The t-shirt section.** I came for shoes. A green graphic tee in the middle of a footwear email feels like filler — it breaks the scroll momentum and doesn't interest me at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** The raw tracking URL in the preview is the single most damaging thing in this email. A one-line preheader — even something generic like "BOGO ½ off ends soon — shop men's styles now" — would have gotten me more curious before I even opened it.
- 2. **Lead the soccer section with the off-field casual gear.** Flip the order: put slides, casual sneakers, and walking-oriented adidas styles up top. Let the soccer cleats live further down for whoever wants them — don't make me scroll past a sports catalog to find myself.
- 3. **Add one comfort signal to the men's section.** One line: "cushioned footbed," "arch-support insole," anything. That's the word that would make me stop and read instead of scroll.
- 4. **Show me a model who looks like me.** Doesn't have to be the whole email — even one lifestyle shot of a guy in his 60s walking a trail in adidas would make me feel seen instead of advertised at.
- 5. **Call out BOGO deadline clearly.** The offer is strong but I don't see a hard end date. "Ends Sunday" would push me to act now instead of closing the tab and forgetting.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a table-based ESP send, but carries a pervasive alt-text gap across all 22+ product images, a typo'd inline CSS property that silently drops a style, and a viewport directive that blocks mobile zoom. No broken links detected; all 28 click URLs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` as expected.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL ratio — deliverability risk**
QA measured 7,069 of 9,545 plain-text characters as URLs (74%). Spam filters penalize plain-text parts that are almost entirely redirect URLs with little human-readable content. The plain-text version needs substantive copy added or the URL density reduced.

**28 tracking links unverifiable**
All CTAs resolve through `click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=<encoded>`. Destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without decoding. This is expected ESP behavior but means destination hygiene must be verified in the ESP campaign builder, not from the HTML source alone.

**Open pixel**
Standard 1×1 pixel present:
```
https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?ZPRCOMRZDEREJAOIG3DYJZZOIE.70250&d=70250&bmt=0
```
No issues with this pattern.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta — silent failure in some clients**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without it, some older clients may not honor the charset declaration.

**`user-scalable=0` blocks zoom — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0`.

**22 images with empty or absent `alt` text**
QA flagged all product/banner images. Notable cases:
- Logo (`3b6a7dc1-...png`): `alt=""` — should be `alt="Famous Footwear"`
- Hero GIF (`721856e1-...gif`), all product shots, divider bars: `alt=""` or missing entirely

Decorative dividers (e.g., `4ccff4ad-...jpg` at `height="2"`) can legitimately keep `alt=""`. All product images and the logo must have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients.

**Typo'd inline CSS property — silently dropped**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px;..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended as `letter-spacing` or `line-height`). This rule is silently ignored by all clients, meaning the intended spacing is not applied to that `<td>`.

**`<style>` block inside `<body>`**
```html
<body ...><style>div.preheader { display: none !important; }</style>
```
Technically invalid HTML 4.01 (styles belong in `<head>`). Most modern email clients handle this, but Outlook desktop and some older clients may not apply or may strip body-level `<style>` blocks, potentially exposing the preheader text visually.
