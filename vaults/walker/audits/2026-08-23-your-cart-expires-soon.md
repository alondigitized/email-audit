- Below that there's a decent men's mix in the "best sellers" row — the blue Anubhav basketball shoe, the Delson 3.0 slip-in, the Max Cushioning Arch Fit — which at least tells me somebody's algorithm knows men shop this brand too. - The Skechers Plus 20% off sitewide banner is a real, specific offer with a code and a date — that part's clean and I can read it fine on my phone.
- Men's product mix does show up in the best-sellers grid — the basketball shoe, the Delson slip-in, the Arch Fit Azusah sneaker are all shoes I'd actually consider.
- Layout's simple, big buttons, no crowding — easy enough to scroll through one-handed.
- Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup icons are clear if I wanted to use them, though I don't.

## What's weak

- The hero shoe, again, is a women's floral D'Lites I never touched — same lie as last time, "you liked these, and you weren't wrong."
- The whole premise of the email — "complete your order" — assumes an abandoned cart that isn't mine. That's not a small miss, that's the entire email being wrong.
- This is the same cart-nag pattern from "This is perfect for you!" (8/21) and the earlier "Your cart expires soon!" (8/21) — same shoe, same guilt copy, just recycled again two days later.
- Nothing here — no arch support callout, no cushioning language, no men's comfort framing — speaks to why I'd actually buy a shoe. It's all urgency, no substance.

- 2. If there's no real cart, don't fake urgency — lead with the 20% off Plus member sale instead, that's a real offer I'd actually read.
- 3. Put a men's comfort shoe in the hero slot, not buried in row two of a grid — something with arch support or slip-in ease called out in the copy.
