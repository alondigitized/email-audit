# Stop the sleigh for up to 30% OFF!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- Shoe Carnival again, and this one doesn't match its own subject. The subject promised a sleigh and up to 30% off. The email opens on a big red "VETERANS DAY SALE!" with Extra 20% off, code VETSDAY, and an extra 10% for military. The 30% is only a Nike block further down, and a girl in a Christmas tree scene sells the Nike shoes. That's a mixed message. I like to see the email match what I saw scrolling, and this one doesn't.
- The Brooks block is the first thing that's actually my kind of shoe, and it comes about two-thirds of the way down. "Trace 4, $89.98" is a number I can do the math on. But it says "not eligible for additional discounts," so the big 20% banner at the top doesn't apply. That feels like a bait-and-switch. Nothing here talks about slip-ons, arch support or cushioning, and there's no Skechers or Hoka. It's a long scroll of banners for a guy who just wants a comfortable walking shoe.
- Would I click? Maybe the Brooks one if I'm in the mood. I wouldn't forward it. It's the 7th or so send in two weeks, after the Nov 9 "Early Black Friday Deals" and the Nov 8 "FINAL DAY" Nike email. I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" text is huge and I can read it on my phone without my glasses.
- The Veterans Day tie-in is timely, and the military 10% is a nice touch for the right guy.
- The Brooks "Trace 4 $89.98" block shows a real shoe at a real price, and Brooks is one of my brands.
- The bottom nav has big, simple tabs (Womens, Mens, Kids, Accessories), so I could find Mens easily.
- The Shoe Perks $10 reward box is a loyalty cue I noticed.

## What's weak

- The subject says "sleigh" and "up to 30%", but the hero is Veterans Day and 20%. They don't line up.
- The Brooks block says "Not eligible for additional discounts" in small print. That undercuts the 20% hero.
- The Nike block leads with a kid in a Christmas photo. Nothing is aimed at a 62-year-old man.
- There are no slip-on, cushioned or arch-support callouts. The shoes shown are mostly sneakers for younger folks and kids.
- The fine print at the bottom is a wall of tiny text I can't read without zooming.
- Barcode, Word Stride game and SMS boxes add clutter, and the tiny "Shop Nike" and "Shop Brooks" links are hard to tap.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject match the hero. Lead with the offer the reader sees first.
- 2. Put the men's comfort and walking section, Brooks and New Balance, right under the hero.
- 3. Say plainly which brands are excluded from the 20% right next to the hero, not in the small print.
- 4. Turn "Shop Brooks" and "Shop Nike" into big buttons, like "Shop Men's Brooks."
- 5. Cut the game and SMS boxes, or move them below the shoes.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Veterans Day: extra 20% off + 10% for military`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Brooks Trace 4 for $89.98, plus 20% off other styles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code VETSDAY in store or online. Ends 11/11.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 10% off for veterans and active military. Brooks, Nike and New Balance.`
