# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-24**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I walked through Skechers.com on my phone. The first half is decent. The second half fell apart. I couldn't buy a shoe.

The homepage is loud with Black Friday banners. The Men's menu is easy to follow. The Men's Shoes page has a "Hands Free Slip-ins" tile right at the top, which is what I look for. Then it went wrong:

- The product page made me pick a size, and "Add to Cart" didn't go through. The cart badge still said 0.
- The cart page was a completely blank white screen.
- I never saw a logged-in experience. The "Log In" step looked identical to the homepage.
- My search for "comfort shoes" opened with Women's loafers and wedges.

Skechers showed me deals but didn't get me to a purchase.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** Same as yesterday (3/10). I don't see a regression, and I don't see a real improvement either.

A blank cart and a failed add-to-cart are what keep this score low. A shopper who gets that far and hits a dead end goes to the Hoka or Brooks site.

## 3. What's Working

- **"Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," "Max Cushioning" sit in a strip under the header.** That is my shopping list, and it's right where I land.
- **The Men menu is easy.** Large text, big tap rows, and a clear "Back" and "X". "Comfort Technologies" is a good label.
- **Men's Shoes has "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" tiles.** They're friendly and visual.
- **Filter, Sort, Size and Width buttons are right there.** Width matters to me.
- **The size buttons on the product page are big and easy to tap.** Out-of-stock 6.5 is crossed out.
- **"Add to Cart" is a big, solid navy button.** The page doesn't hide the price or the star rating (4.5 stars, 9 reviews).
- **The Black Friday offer is easy to read** at 30-50% off, plus the code BF2026.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page was blank white.** I couldn't see what I'd picked, what it cost or how to check out. This is the worst finding.
- **Add to Cart didn't register.** The page flagged "Please select a size," but the cart badge stayed at 0. I had to guess what went wrong. The red message sits below the sizes, easy to miss, and it didn't scroll me up to the problem.
- **No logged-in experience.** The Log In step showed the same homepage. The account icon looked the same. Nothing said "Welcome back" and nothing was personalized to me.
- **Popup step showed no real popup.** Only the thin banner text changed ("Doorbusters... starting at $49.99").
- **Search led with Women's.** My first two results for "comfort shoes" were Women's Cypress and Parallel Lite. I'm a man shopping on a men's-shoe-focused journey. 1,664 results is a lot to wade through.
- **Discount confusion.** "30-50% off sitewide" and "Extra 15% with code" appear together. The product I opened said "Excluded from promotions." Search showed "30% off, applied at cart." I can't tell what I'd really pay.
- **The product I landed on was a Britto art sneaker (Women's-style colorways, "Website Premieres").** It's not a comfort or slip-in shoe. Nothing on screen told me about cushioning, arch support or slip-in features.
- **Small text.** Fine print like "Discount automatically applied at cart. Exclusions apply*" is tiny. The "Shop | Details" links under the banner are small touch targets.
- **No comparison content.** Nothing to compare against Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and no price-vs-quality math.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page now.** It must never render blank. Show an item list, a total and a big "Checkout" button.
2. **Make the size error obvious.** Scroll to the sizes, highlight them in red and keep the message right next to the Add to Cart button. Better, pre-select a popular size or show "Select size to add."
3. **Show a logged-in state.** Show the first name, "Welcome back" and maybe my last-viewed styles. A points balance is fine, but I won't buy because of it.
4. **Honor the shopper's department in search.** If I came from Men's, show Men's first or offer a "Men | Women" toggle at the top.
5. **Put comfort details on the product page.** Show Slip-ins, Arch Fit, cushioning level and a simple "who it's for" line.
6. **Simplify the discount math.** Show one final price: "$68.00 with code BF2026." Say plainly when a style is excluded.
7. **Bigger text and bigger tap targets** for the "Shop | Details" links and fine print.
8. **Surface comfort picks first** on the Men's Shoes page: a "Most comfortable" or "Best for walking all day" row.

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers looks good and speaks my language ("Hands Free," "Arch Fit"), but I couldn't finish a purchase. A blank cart is a deal-breaker. Until the cart works and the site shows me I'm signed in, a shoe from someone else wins my foot. That matches yesterday's 3/10.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

- **Scope limits:** LCP and CLS were not captured on any step, so Core Web Vitals can't be assessed. The HTML samples are truncated, so SEO tags (canonical, meta description, structured data) can't be checked.
- **Cart blocked:** `GET /cart/` returned **429**. The View Cart step's axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) look like a bare error response, not the real cart. That step is probably not a valid test of the cart page.
- **Accessibility:** The homepage has 2 critical and 1 serious axe violation. The landmark violations on both the homepage and search point to a shared template problem.
- **Third-party load:** The page loads about 20 third-party tags. These cause the console noise, the 403s, and probably the missing bfcache eligibility.
- **Tag manager:** The GTM URL contains `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, which is a non-production GTM environment on a production page.
- **Journey data:** The "Log In" and "Men category" steps both record URL `https://www.skechers.com/`. Either the step didn't navigate or the URL capture is stale, so those steps' metrics may describe the homepage.

## Console & Network Errors

24 console errors were reported and 10 were shown. In the shown set:
- **Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed** (×6). A script registers an `unload` handler, which is blocked by the document's policy. This is typically third-party tag code. It also makes the page ineligible for the back/forward cache.
- **Unsupported Summarizer API languages** (×3). Chrome's built-in Summarizer API is called without a supported language. It is likely from an injected script or browser feature, not the site's own code.
- **403 Forbidden** (×1, matching the network log).

Network errors (4):
- **`survey.pulseinsights.com/serve`** returned **403** three times (pageview_count 1, 2 and 3, from `/men/`, `/britto` and `/search`). The survey widget is failing on every page view, so it is a recurring request that never succeeds. The 403 may come from bot detection against the automation browser.
- **`www.skechers.com/cart/`** returned **429**. Rate limiting or bot mitigation blocked the cart request. It is unclear whether real users would hit this or only the automated client.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix measurement first:** Capture LCP and CLS (register `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true`), and record real navigation versus client-side route TTFB.
2. **Cart 429:** Investigate whether the rate limiter or WAF challenges real mobile users on `/cart/`. Whitelist or slow the test client so the cart page can be audited properly. Re-run the View Cart axe check once it renders.
3. **Homepage ARIA and names (critical and serious):** Add accessible names to icon-only buttons and links. Fix the role and parent relationships behind `aria-required-parent`.
4. **Landmarks:** Keep one top-level `<main>`, move `<aside>` out of nested landmarks, give repeated landmarks unique `aria-label`s, and put page content inside landmarks.
5. **Headings and cart metadata:** Add an `<h1>` on the homepage. Ensure error and cart responses always include `<title>`, `lang` and `<main>`.
6. **GTM:** Remove `gtm_preview` and `gtm_auth` from the production snippet and point it at the live environment.
7. **Third parties:** Audit the roughly 20 tags and defer or consolidate them through GTM or a consent gate. Find which tag registers `unload` listeners and have it switch to `pagehide`.
8. **Pulse Insights:** Fix the 403 (domain allowlist, device detection) or stop requesting it when it can't succeed.
9. **Fonts:** Self-host Mulish with preload and `font-display: swap`, and drop the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency.
10. **Test fidelity:** Confirm the "Log In" and "Men category" steps actually navigated, and use a fresh browser profile so `visit_count=174` doesn't skew results.
