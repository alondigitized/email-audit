# Stars, Stripes, and Savings
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Another Skechers Plus email, another morning I open my inbox and see that wall of raw JSON code sitting in the preview slot where words should be. By my count — checking my own memory against the six previous sends in my history here — this makes at least fourteen straight emails with that same broken preview. At this point I have to assume someone at Skechers marketing just doesn't look at their inbox the way a normal subscriber does, because if they did, that would have been fixed back in May.
- Inside the email itself? Honestly not bad. The BOGO 50% off is a real, tangible offer, the red-white-and-blue patriotic layout is well-timed for the weekend, and there's a clean "SHOP MEN'S" section with a men's shoe right in the middle of the email — I don't have to hunt for it. Text is readable without zooming, buttons are big enough to tap on a phone. The layout earns its keep.
- But here's the thing: there's not one word about why this shoe is comfortable. No mention of cushioning, arch support, or anything my feet actually care about. It's all price and patriotism. I'd probably click through on the BOGO deal because I'm a Skechers Plus member and 50% off a second pair is genuinely worth my time — but I'm browsing, not buying, because nothing told me which shoe in that men's section is going to feel good after three hours on my feet at a cookout. Mark it as read with a click to explore. Would not forward to a friend — the broken preview alone would make me look bad for sharing it.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% offer is immediate and unmissable.** Hero section leads with the deal in large type — I don't need to scroll or squint to understand what's being offered.
- **"Skechers Plus Exclusive" framing is smart.** Reminds me I'm a member getting something the general public doesn't; makes me feel like the email is for me, not a blast to everyone.
- **Timely creative for the Fourth of July weekend.** Red, white, blue, stars — appropriate for July 2nd. Feels current, not like a recycled template.
- **Men's section is visible without scrolling past the fold.** One of my biggest complaints with women's-first emails — they buried the men's section — is actually handled reasonably here, even if women's still leads.
- **Email renders clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Layout holds up.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is broken JSON. Again. Fourteenth email in a row.** This is the single most consistent failure in my entire Skechers inbox history. It's not a one-time glitch anymore — it's a process problem that nobody is fixing.
- **Zero comfort language anywhere in the email.** "Savings" and "Stars and Stripes" do nothing for me as someone shopping for cushioning and arch support. A shoe that looks patriotic and one that feels good after a long walk are not the same shoe.
- **Women's content leads and kids' content follows men's.** Women → Men → Kids is the structure. For a 62-year-old guy, I've already scrolled past content that isn't mine before I hit what I care about.
- **No product-specific detail on the men's shoe.** It's a nice-looking chunky athletic shoe but I have no idea if it's a walking shoe, a gym shoe, or something in between. No callout for the fit, the technology, nothing.
- **Afterpay / Klarna badges in the footer.** Not relevant to how I shop — I use a card or I don't buy. These take up space and skew young.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is the highest-priority item across all fourteen sends.** Insert a real preheader: something like "BOGO 50% off — use it on the comfort style you've been watching." Every email with broken JSON in the preview loses open-rate before the subject line even gets a chance.
- 2. **Add one comfort callout to the men's shoe featured in the hero section.** Even a two-word badge ("Memory Foam," "Ultra-Cushion," "Arch Fit") would give me a reason to click that specific shoe instead of just browsing category pages.
- 3. **Lead the men's section with a slip-on or hands-free style.** I shop that way. If the hero men's shoe happens to be easy-on, say so.
- 4. **Move the sale end date higher.** If it ends July 5th, put that in the hero — "Through Sunday Only" does more than the Afterpay badge for someone like me who needs a reason to decide now.
- 5. **Remove or de-emphasize Afterpay/Klarna** for this segment — replaces with a trust cue relevant to Boomers like a satisfaction guarantee or free returns note.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a pervasive HTTP image-serving problem affecting 13+ assets, including the logo and all footer icons, which will trigger mixed-content warnings or silent blocking in modern email clients. Two secondary accessibility violations compound the issue: viewport zoom is locked and text-size adjustment is globally disabled.

- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` — HTTP (no scheme shown in source, but no `https://` prefix). Return Path pixels normally support HTTPS; this should be verified against the actual rendered source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP images — likely to be blocked**

13 images served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com` (not HTTPS). Apple Mail, Gmail, and Outlook on iOS/Android enforce mixed-content blocking. Affected assets:

| Asset | src |
|---|---|
| Skechers logo | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../dde00662-....png` |
| Unlabeled image | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../49468f73-....png` |
| App Store badge | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../0ec56c9f-....png` |
| Google Play badge | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../9bb508d0-....png` |
| Curbside Pickup | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../00100b23-....png` |
| AfterPay | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../07cb60b5-....png` |
| Find A Store | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/.../fc08601a-....png` |
| Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons | `http://image.emails.skechers.com/...` |

`image.emails.skechers.com` almost certainly supports HTTPS — this is a template configuration issue, not an infrastructure one.

**Missing alt text**

Two images have no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`):
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel)
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` (unknown content image)

Decorative/tracking pixels must have `alt=""`. Content images must have descriptive alt text.

**Viewport zoom locked**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. This violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and will fail accessibility audits in regulated jurisdictions.

**Global text-size adjustment disabled**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

Applying `none` on `*` disables automatic text scaling in WebKit/Blink. The correct value for email is `100%`, which preserves layout without stripping user accessibility overrides.

**Duplicate CSS breakpoints**

The 375px and 640px media query blocks define identical rule sets. The 480px breakpoint in the second `<style>` block partially overlaps. This adds dead weight to parse time and creates a maintenance hazard.

**`<meta>` attributes missing required `http-equiv`**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />   <!-- missing http-equiv -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                      <!-- missing http-equiv -->
```

Both should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, these directives are silently ignored by parsers.
