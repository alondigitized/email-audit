# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-08**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker, 62, on my phone looking for a comfortable slip-on or walking shoe. The front half of the visit is decent. The homepage has "Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Arch Fit, Max Cushion" right at the top, and the Men's menu and Men's Shoes page are easy to use. The back half fell apart. The first shoe I landed on was a $225 racing shoe with no reviews. I couldn't add anything to the cart. The cart page was a blank white screen. The "comfort shoes" search worked but showed me women's shoes first. Logging in showed no change at all, so nothing felt personal to me.

**Versus yesterday (4/10):** I only have yesterday's score, not its write-up. I can't tell you which specific things got better or worse, so I'm calling today flat. The navigation and search work, but the cart failure cancels them out.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**4/10.** This is the same as yesterday. Browsing is solid, but a shopper like me can't complete a purchase. A blank cart page is where a sale dies.

## 3. What's Working

- **The homepage speaks my language.** The tabs "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Arch Fit" are the exact things I shop for. This is the best feature of the site. (The tab row is cut off at "Max Cus…" and I'd have to swipe to see the rest.)
- **The Men menu is easy.** It opens as a big, clear list. "Shoes," "Shop by Activity," and "Comfort Technologies" have wide rows with arrows, plus a clear Back and X. I can tap these without fumbling.
- **Men's Shoes starts in the right place.** The first tiles are "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," and "Boots." That's what I came for.
- **Size buttons are big and easy to tap.** The Add to Cart button is wide and dark blue, so there's no missing it. The "Please select a size" message is in red and readable.
- **Search shows its work.** It says "comfort shoes" with 1,591 results. Filter and Sort are right there, and prices and color dots are shown.
- **Offers are visible.** The banners say "Extra 10% OFF" and "Up To 40% OFF Select Styles," and search results say "Members Stack an Extra 10%."

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is broken or blank.** After I tried to add to cart, the cart icon still showed 0. When I went to the cart page, I saw nothing but white. I can't tell whether it's empty or the page failed to load. Either way, a customer would leave.
- **Add to Cart has no friendly path.** The page doesn't preselect a size. The red "Please select a size" error shows, and the cart count never moved. I had to figure that out myself. A 62-year-old may not notice the error and may assume the button is broken.
- **The product I landed on is wrong for me.** The Aero Elite is a $225 racing shoe with "No Reviews" and an "Excluded from promotions" warning. I'm looking for cushioning and arch support, not a carbon-plate racer. It was also the first item in the list, which hurts trust.
- **Login felt like nothing happened.** The "Log In" screen looked exactly like the homepage. There was no greeting, no name, no "welcome back," and no slip-on or wide-fit shortcuts.
- **Search put women's shoes first.** The first two results were a women's pink sneaker and a women's glide-step clog. I'm a man shopping men's comfort. Nothing asked or remembered that.
- **Small text.** Labels like "Women's," "Size Chart," and "Free pickup at Set Location" are small and grey. I would squint or zoom.
- **Nothing helps me compare.** There's no comparison to Hoka, Brooks, or New Balance, and no "cost per year of wear" or price-vs-quality information. I'm cross-shopping, and the site doesn't try to win me on that.
- **The top of every page is crowded.** Two banners stack above the header. The wording changed from "up to 40% off" to "25% off Apparel & Bags" between steps. That is confusing, and the offers don't match each other.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart first.** Make sure the page loads and shows what was added. If the cart is empty, show "Your cart is empty" with a button to keep shopping. Never show a blank page.
2. **Make size selection hard to miss.** Scroll to the size row and highlight it when someone taps Add to Cart without a size. Update the cart count right away when the item is added.
3. **Sort by comfort for people who arrive through comfort routes.** If someone comes from "Slip-ins" or "Arch Fit," show those shoes first. Don't start with a $225 racer. Put the best-reviewed comfort shoes at the top.
4. **Use the logged-in state.** Show a name, last-viewed shoes, saved size and width, and a "Your Slip-ins" shortcut.
5. **Respect gender and category in search.** If someone browsed Men's, search results should lead with men's.
6. **Add "why this one" content.** Show arch support, cushioning level, weight, and a "price per year" or "compared with" box for a comfort-first buyer.
7. **Increase text size and contrast** on secondary labels, and show one clear offer at a time.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like what Skechers sells, and the site understands me right up to the product page. But I couldn't buy anything. A blank cart, a size error that was easy to miss, and a racing shoe offered to a walker all add up. If it were my money, I'd go to a competitor whose cart works. The score stays at **4/10**.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The run was heavily affected by bot mitigation. Five requests to the Kasada endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`) and the `/cart/` document request itself returned 429. The View Cart step therefore audited a block or error page, not the real cart. The Log In and Men category HTML samples both show URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, so those steps may not have navigated and may be re-auditing the homepage. LCP and CLS were not captured for any step. The only usable performance data is TTFB.

Most findings below come from the homepage and search steps. View Cart findings are likely artifacts of the 429 response.

## Accessibility

18 axe violations across 3 steps. Homepage has 10, View Cart 4, and Search 4.

**Homepage**
- **Critical:** `aria-required-parent`. Elements with child-only roles such as `listitem`, `tab` or `option` are missing their required parent role.
- **Critical:** `button-name`. Buttons have no accessible text, likely icon-only controls.
- **Serious:** `link-name`. Links have no discernible text.
- **Moderate (landmark structure):**
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main`: multiple `<main>` landmarks, at least one nested.
  - `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`
  - `landmark-unique`
  - `region`: content outside landmarks.
- **Moderate:** `heading-order`.
- **Minor:** `aria-allowed-role`.

**Search "comfort shoes"**
- Four of the homepage violations recur: `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. This points to a shared template or header and footer problem, not a page-specific one.

**View Cart**
- `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail. That pattern matches a bare 429 response body with no `<html lang>`, `<title>`, `<main>` or `<h1>`. It is probably not representative of the real cart. Re-test it with an unblocked session.

**Not audited or no violations**
- Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart report no axe results. I can't tell from the data whether that means clean or not run.

## SEO

- The homepage has `<html lang="en">`, which is correct.
- The truncated HTML samples don't show `<title>`, meta description, canonical, hreflang or structured data, so these can't be assessed.
- The cart 429 response lacks `<title>` and `lang`. If a crawler gets this response, it would index a titleless page. Confirm that bot-mitigation responses send appropriate status codes and `noindex`.
- Heading-order and duplicate-`<main>` issues on the homepage weaken document outline for crawlers.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6)**
- Five 429s on `/{uuid}/{uuid}/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.864`. This is the Kasada bot-defense fingerprinting call, rate-limited or rejected for this client.
- One 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`.

**Console (30 reported, 10 shown, apparently de-duplicated into 4 distinct messages)**
- `Failed to load resource: 429`: corresponds to the Kasada calls.
- `Failed to load resource: net::ERR_FAILED`: no URL given, likely tied to the same blocked requests.
- `Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`: appears 4 times. A script, probably third-party, registers an `unload` handler. Chrome is deprecating `unload`, and it also breaks bfcache eligibility.
- `Unsupported Summarizer API languages…`: appears twice. This is Chrome's built-in AI Summarizer API called with an unsupported language. It is more likely from a browser feature or injected script than from site code, so attribute it before filing it against the site.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix the test harness before drawing site conclusions.**
   - The 429s mean the cart step and possibly others weren't tested against the real site.
   - Lower request rate, add backoff and retry on 429, and treat a 429 as a failed step. Don't audit its body.
   - Beyond that, getting past the bot defense would need allowlisting from the site owner, which isn't something this audit can assume.
2. **Verify navigation per step.** Log In and Men category both record the homepage URL. Assert `location.href` after each step.
3. **Capture LCP and CLS reliably.** Use `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true` and read the values after each real navigation. Take TTFB from the navigation entry for each document load, and mark SPA or in-page transitions separately.
4. **Site fixes (homepage and search templates):**
   - Add accessible names to icon buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`).
   - Give ARIA child roles their required parents.
   - Remove the duplicate or nested `<main>`, move `<aside>` elements to the top level, and give repeated landmarks unique labels.
   - Correct the heading order.
5. **Replace `unload` handlers with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.** Identify which tag registers them. This restores bfcache eligibility.
6. **Review the third-party tag inventory.**
   - Defer non-essential pixels until after interaction or idle.
   - Self-host or preload the web fonts to take cdnjs off the critical path.
7. **Re-run the audit** with the harness fixes to get valid cart, product, Log In and Men category results.
