# Early access: Torrent 5
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- HOKA's dangling something I don't usually get from a brand — a VIP seat at the table before the shoe even drops publicly. "Member Exclusive," "Get It First," members get first dibs — I appreciate that. It makes me feel like signing up for their emails was actually worth something. The email itself is clean: one big shoe, clear headline, two buttons. I can read everything on my phone without zooming. No complaints there.
- But here's where I hesitate: "daily trail miles." That's the phrase that started pulling me out of this. I'm not a trail runner. I'm the guy trying to get through the grocery store, the airport, a long afternoon on his feet without his arches screaming at him. The May 28 send — "Comfort you can count on" with that Podiatrist Approved badge front and center — that was written for my feet. This one feels like it was written for someone twenty years younger with a National Park pass. The Torrent 5 looks like a serious shoe. Too serious for what I'm doing on a Tuesday.
- I'd open this because I'm a HOKA subscriber and early access is intriguing. I might scroll through and look at the men's colorways. I probably wouldn't buy, and I definitely wouldn't forward it to my buddy Dave who's in the same boat as me foot-comfort-wise.

## What's working

- The "Member Exclusive" framing is the right instinct — it treats me like a known customer, not another cold email on a list
- Two CTAs ("Get Early Access" / "Join for Free") are a smart split — one for people already in, one to pull in fence-sitters; both buttons are visible and clear on mobile
- Men's colorways in the product grid are solid — that black/gray and the white/blue look clean, not flashy; I could see myself in either
- "Plusher ride than ever" is the one piece of copy that speaks my language — cushion language always catches my eye, even buried in trail-running context
- The "Find Your Best Fit" quiz at the bottom is a nice gesture; fit is always my hesitation when buying shoes online, so a tool like that earns points

## What's weak

- "Daily trail miles" — that's not my sport; HOKA knows from my browsing that I'm not lacing up for ridge runs, and the copy doesn't try to bridge the gap to casual walkers
- No price anywhere — "early access" is an empty promise if I can't see what I'm getting early access to; tell me it starts at $149 and I might actually feel the urgency
- The hero shot is a dramatic studio close-up of the shoe, no person in it — I can't picture myself in it the way I could with a walking lifestyle photo
- The "Find Your Best Fit" runner image is a young, fast-looking athlete; not a single model anywhere in this email who looks like me
- The $250 shipping threshold in the top banner is easy to miss and not a real perk for a one-pair purchase; that's not the benefit to lead with

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bridge trail to everyday** — rewrite "sidekick for daily trail miles" to something like "walks, hikes, and everything in between — plusher than ever" to pull in casual walkers who aren't self-identifying as trail runners
- 2. **Show a price** — even "Members unlock at $149" in the hero gives early access actual meaning; right now I don't know if I'm clicking into a $100 shoe or a $200 one
- 3. **Add one comfort credential** — a single line about the midsole foam or a cushion tech name in the hero copy would do what the Podiatrist Approved badge did in May: give me a medical-adjacent reason to trust my feet to this shoe
- 4. **Use a lifestyle model** — swap the runner in the "Find Your Best Fit" section for someone walking a trail path, not sprinting; a 55+ male would close the demographic gap fast
- 5. **Make the member benefit tangible** — "Members get first dibs" is vague; "Members unlock 48 hours early" or "Members save 10% on launch day" is the version that would make me click without hesitation

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` (lines 5–6)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
Two conflicting declarations. `maximum-scale=1` on the first tag suppresses pinch-zoom; the second tag adds `user-scalable=yes` but also retains `maximum-scale=1`, so zoom is still blocked. This fails **WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text)**.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Prevents system-level text scaling on iOS/Safari. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, users with low vision cannot increase text size. Should be `100%`, not `none`.

**CSS bug — empty numeric values in `.nav_padding`**
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
```
Missing numbers before `px`. This is invalid CSS; the rule is silently dropped in all parsers. Mobile nav spacing will be wrong on screens ≤480px.

**CSS bug — malformed `.nav_border`**
```css
.nav_border { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Missing width value and color token. Border will not render.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
No content. Some spam classifiers penalize blank titles; some preview panes expose it.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` for legacy Outlook/Lotus Notes compatibility.

**Web font support scope**
Five `@font-face` declarations load `TheFutureHOKA` variants from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/`. Supported in Apple Mail and iOS Mail only — Outlook, Gmail web, and Gmail Android will fall back to system fonts. This is expected behavior, but fallback stacks should be confirmed in the body CSS.
