# 250 Years 🇺🇸 One MEGA SALE🎆
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- July 1st, Shoe Carnival lands with a patriotic flag emoji and "250 Years." First reaction: 250 years of what? Is this their anniversary? Took me a beat to connect it to America's 250th birthday. That's a beat I shouldn't have to spend.
- Open the email and the hero is a bold red "MEGA SALE — IN STORE ONLY." Right there, that's where I mentally pump the brakes. I'm on my phone. I'm not driving across town based on a promotional email. The top of the email is pushing me toward a parking lot, not a checkout screen. But I keep scrolling and things get more interesting — Skechers Slip-ins at 25% off, Brooks at a flat $89.98. Those are two brands I actually cross-shop, at prices I can evaluate on the spot. That's the email I wanted. It just took too long to find me.
- The preheader is a raw URL — again. I flagged the exact same issue on the June 24th send. That's two times in less than two weeks. That's not a bug, that's nobody checking. I'd scroll past Skechers and Brooks and probably not forward this to anyone, but the Slip-ins link might get a tap.

## What's working

- **Skechers Slip-ins with 25% off is right in my lane** — hands-free, comfort-focused, and a real percentage off. That section earns a click from me.
- **Brooks at a concrete $89.98 is actionable** — I can decide on a number. "Up to X% off" is marketing; "$89.98" is a price.
- **The $10 off coupon at the top works online, not just in-store** — small save, but appreciated after the hero pushes me toward a store visit.
- **Timing is legitimate** — 4th of July, America's 250th, this email belongs this week. After the Father's Day zombie sends in late June, a timely hook is noticeable.
- **Shoe Carnival name is immediately recognizable** in the header — no confusion about who's talking to me.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL, same as June 24th** — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." is what shows in my inbox next to the subject. That's wasted real estate and it looks broken.
- **"IN STORE ONLY" on the hero kills momentum for a mobile shopper** — if the lead deal requires a car trip, you've already lost me before I see the online offers below.
- **"250 Years" in the subject needs one more word of context** — without it, my first read was a Shoe Carnival anniversary, not a patriotic sale.
- **Women's & Kids' Sandal Blowout takes a full section** — I have to scroll past it to get to Brooks and Skechers. That section isn't talking to me.
- **Seventh email in roughly seven days** — I've started counting Shoe Carnival sends the way I count spam. Cadence is killing my goodwill.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is the second broken one in two weeks.** "Up to 25% off Skechers Slip-ins + Brooks — shop online" takes five minutes and makes the inbox entry look like a real send.
- 2. **Move Skechers Slip-ins and Brooks above the Women's/Kids section.** The comfort-focused men's offer is your best hook for a customer like me — don't make me excavate for it.
- 3. **Swap the hero CTA from "Find Your Store" to a split layout** — in-store offer on the left, online coupon on the right. Don't lead with the path that requires car keys.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the sale.** "Sale ends July 4th" is all it takes to turn browsing into buying.
- 5. **Either cut the Women's & Kids' Sandal Blowout or push it to the bottom** — it's not hurting anyone down there, but it's costing me a scroll when it's in the middle.

- **Subject Alt B:** `America's 250th — Skechers Slip-ins & Brooks On Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop online now — comfort shoes on sale through July 4th`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — source truncates before any `<a href>` tags appear in the body.

One structural risk visible in `<head>` CSS:

```css
a, a:link, a:visited, a:hover, a:active { color:inherit !important; text-decoration:none !important; }
```

This blanket `!important` override will suppress any inline `color` or `text-decoration` on anchor tags. If any link is styled inline (common in template systems), the override wins and the visual state is hidden — making broken or mis-colored links harder to catch in QA.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dark mode not declared.** Both meta tags explicitly lock to light-only:

```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```

No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present. Apple Mail and Outlook on macOS/iOS will attempt an automatic inversion on white backgrounds — potential for unreadable reversed colors on white-on-white sections.

**Malformed charset meta tag:**

```html
<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />
```

Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The correct form is:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, some older Outlook/Windows Mail builds may not honor the declared charset.

**Five separate `<style>` blocks** are present in `<head>`. Gmail (non-app, webmail) and a handful of older clients only process the first `<style>` block. All responsive media queries after block 1 are at risk of being dropped in those clients. Consolidating into one block is safer.

**Tap-to-call disabled intentionally but silently:**

```css
a[href^="tel"], a[href^="sms"] { pointer-events:none; cursor:default !important; }
```

If phone numbers appear in the footer (common for customer service), this suppresses native dialing on mobile. Confirm this is intentional — if not, remove these rules.
