# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-20**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

I spent time on skechers.com today and came away frustrated more than impressed. The navigation to Hands Free Slip-ins was smooth, the product page read clearly on my phone, and the size grid was easy to tap. But two things killed the trip: the cart page loaded completely blank — I couldn't even confirm my item was in there — and when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results I saw were women's shoes. I'm logged in. They know I'm a man. That's a miss I can't overlook. The homepage hero is a young running athlete that says nothing to a 62-year-old looking for a comfortable walking shoe. Overall the site feels like it's built for someone 30 years younger than me.

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**Same as yesterday — no improvement, one new critical bug (blank cart).**

The cart failure is a conversion killer. If I can't see my cart, I'm gone. Search returning women's results to a logged-in male user undermines any trust that the site knows who I am.

## 3. What's Working

- **Promo banners are visible and clear.** "20% OFF Regular Priced Items Code: SUMMER" sits right at the top in blue — I'd notice that immediately. The discount messaging on the product page ("Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER") is well-placed.
- **Men's category menu is excellent.** Big tap targets, clean list. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" right there in the drawer — those are exactly the words I'd look for. No hunting.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins landing page is on point.** The fit filter pills (Wide, Extra Wide, Medium) across the top are smart — I've got wider feet and I'd tap that immediately. 311 results tells me they've got stock.
- **Product page text is readable.** Product name, price ($90.00), and the member discount line are all large enough to read without zooming. Five stars and "13 Reviews" visible — I check reviews.
- **Size grid on the add-to-cart screen is generous.** Each size button is a large square, easy to tap with a thick finger. Full-width "ADD TO CART" button at the bottom — can't miss it.
- **"Check in-store availability" option is appreciated.** I like knowing I can go try these on. That line of text is reassuring.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page loaded completely blank.** White screen. Nothing. No item, no total, no checkout button. If this happened to me for real, I'd assume something went wrong and either call someone or leave. This is the single biggest failure in the journey.
- **Search shows women's shoes to a logged-in man.** I typed "comfort shoes" and the first two results I saw — Contour Foam Cozy Fit and Glide-Step Comfort — are both labeled "Women's." 1,460 results and they surface women's shoes first? I'm logged in, I navigated through the Men's section — this tells me the site isn't using any of what it knows about me.
- **Homepage hero doesn't speak to me.** A young athlete on bleachers with a basketball and running shoes says "performance sport." I'm looking for "my feet don't hurt at the end of the day." Nobody on that hero image is my age or looks like they're shopping for a comfortable walking shoe.
- **Login confirmation was invisible.** After the login step, I was just back on the homepage. No "Welcome back, Walker" message, no name in the header, nothing to confirm I was actually signed in. The account icon looks the same whether I'm in or out.
- **Popup step showed almost no popup.** Step 2 looks nearly identical to step 1 — just a banner swap. If there was a real popup overlay, it either wasn't triggered or was captured after dismissal. Hard to evaluate, but if there was one I'd want it out of my way fast with a clear X button.
- **No cushioning/arch support language on the product page above the fold.** The product is called "Skech-Air Summits" — the air cushioning is the selling point — but I'm not seeing the comfort tech explanation in the screenshot. I see the Slip-ins badge but no "Air-Cooled Memory Foam" or "cushioned insole" in the visible portion. That language sells me.
- **"Please select a size" error appeared before I even tried to add.** The warning showed up pre-emptively on screen. That's fine as a validation, but it means the cart counter still shows 0 — and then the cart was blank anyway.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is an abandoned purchase. This needs to be the top P0 fix. If it's a render/hydration issue, it's costing real sales today.
2. **Filter search results by the logged-in user's gender.** I'm a logged-in male who navigated through Men's all session. "Comfort shoes" should default to Men's results or at least surface men's first with a "showing women's too" toggle below.
3. **Put comfort tech language above the fold on product pages.** "Air-cushioned," "Memory Foam insole," "arch support" — these are the words that make me click "Add to Cart." They should be in the first screen, not buried below the fold.
4. **Confirm login visually.** "Welcome back, Walker" or even my first name appearing in the account icon area would tell me the site knows who I am. Right now I'm not confident I was actually logged in.
5. **Update the homepage hero for the comfort/casual shopper.** Show someone my age, walking a trail or a mall, looking comfortable. Slip-ins and Arch Fit are your comfort story — the hero image should tell it.
6. **Make the member discount automatically applied, not a code.** Showing "Use Code: SUMMER" is a friction point. If I'm a member, just apply it. I might forget the code at checkout.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform is Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware), confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths. The journey loads 20+ third-party async scripts on every page. The View Cart page appears to render inside an iframe or dynamically-injected document fragment rather than a full HTML document — evidenced by missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<h1>`, and main landmark. The "Log In" and "Men category" steps show identical URLs (`https://www.skechers.com/`) in the HTML samples, suggesting either the capture tool missed navigation or both views are modals/overlays over the homepage document. Kasada bot-detection (KP SDK) is rate-limiting its own fingerprinting endpoint — 5 of 6 network errors are against the same Kasada path.

---

## Accessibility

**18 violations across 3 pages. Two are critical.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA list/grid items not wrapped in required parent role; screen readers will misread structure |
| Critical | `button-name` | Homepage | One or more `<button>` elements have no accessible label; AT users cannot identify the action |
| Serious | `link-name` | Homepage | Unlabeled `<a>` tags; likely icon-only links without `aria-label` |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` is absent — AT announces "untitled document"; also a crawler signal |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | No `lang` attribute; screen readers default to OS language, breaking pronunciation for EN content |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | Heading levels are skipped (e.g., h1 → h3), breaking screen reader outline navigation |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-one-main`, `region` | Homepage, Cart, Search | Multiple and nested landmark violations; `<main>` appears inside another landmark; `<aside>` not top-level |
| Minor | `aria-allowed-role` | Homepage, Search | `role` attribute used on elements where it is not permitted by ARIA spec |

**Root cause for View Cart cluster:** the cart is almost certainly rendered in an `<iframe>` or a SPA shell that injects a bare HTML fragment without a proper document skeleton. The four violations (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) are exactly what you'd see from a stripped frame document.

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`:** Even though cart is typically `noindex`, the absence of a title element is a defect in the document template that could affect other pages sharing the same shell.
- **Heading order violations (Homepage):** Skipped heading levels reduce crawlability of page structure and degrade content hierarchy signals.
- **Log In and Men category showing same URL as Homepage:** If these are SPA modal states with no `pushState` or canonical updates, search engines cannot distinguish them. Skechers likely relies on Demandware's server-rendered URLs for indexable pages, but any fragment-rendered state that shares a URL is invisible to crawlers.
- No structural SEO blockers on product/category pages based on available data.

**Third-party script load:** Every page fires 20+ async scripts at parse time including TikTok Pixel (2 files), Google Tag Manager, Google Ads, Pinterest, Reddit, Snapchat, Taboola, Impact, Bloomreach, Quantum Metric, Monetate (2 instances), Bing, Yahoo (loaded **twice** — `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` appears in `<head>` twice on every captured page), Tune, PulseInsights, Stylitics, and Kasada. Even async, this volume saturates the main thread and delays Time to Interactive.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height hack present:** `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set as a CSS custom property via JavaScript. This is a standard workaround for mobile browsers where `100vh` includes the address bar, but the fractional precision (`6.640000000000001`) indicates floating-point drift in the calculation. This value is recomputed on resize and could cause a forced layout/reflow on orientation change.
- **No evidence of adaptive image serving** in the truncated HTML. Cannot confirm without full source, but SFCC deployments frequently omit responsive `srcset` on mobile views.
- **Heavy synchronous SFCC bundle:** `us-skechers-prod.js` is loaded with `async` but is a monolithic bundle. If it's large, it will compete with LCP image loading on constrained mobile connections.
- **Font loading:** WebFontLoader (`webfontloader.js`) is used to load Mulish in 6 weights. This triggers FOUT (Flash of Unstyled Text) on mobile. The `wf-active` class in `<html>` confirms fonts eventually load, but the mechanism is JS-dependent rather than `font-display: swap` in CSS.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting (5 Kasada + 1 Cart):**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/149e9513.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522  (×5)
429 https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```

The `149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b` path prefix is the Kasada KP SDK bot-detection fingerprinting endpoint. Five 429s against this single endpoint indicates the test session's browser fingerprint was flagged as non-human and the endpoint is throttling it. In production this would silently degrade bot-protection coverage. The `/cart/` 429 is more severe — the cart page itself returning 429 to a user session means a real user could receive a blank cart, likely triggering a client-side error state.

**Stylitics CORS Block:**

```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...'
from origin 'https://www.skechers.com' has been blocked by CORS policy:
No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header is present
```

The Stylitics outfit widget CSS is being loaded as a cross-origin stylesheet but `web-assets.stylitics.com` does not return an `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header for the skechers.com origin. This breaks any Stylitics outfit recommendation UI — the widget renders unstyled or not at all. This is a Stylitics CDN misconfiguration that Skechers needs to report to Stylitics or work around by self-hosting the stylesheet.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**

1. **Cart 429:** Investigate why `/cart/` returns 429 to authenticated sessions. This is a revenue-blocking defect. Likely a Kasada false-positive or aggressive rate-limit on the cart endpoint.
2. **View Cart document structure:** The cart frame/modal must include `<html lang="en">`, `<title>`, `<h1>`, and `<main>` in its document template. These are both WCAG 2.1 failures and broken document hygiene.
3. **`button-name` (Critical a11y):** Audit all unlabeled buttons on the homepage and add `aria-label` or visible text. Critical violations.

**P1 — High priority:**

4. **Stylitics CORS:** Contact Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to `web-assets.stylitics.com`. Alternatively, proxy the CSS through Skechers' own CDN.
5. **Collect LCP + CLS:** The audit has no LCP or CLS data. Instrument Real User Monitoring (RUM) or run Lighthouse on a real device to get these Core Web Vitals — they are required for Google Search ranking signals.
6. **Deduplicate Yahoo pixel:** Remove one of the two `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` injections from the GTM container or the SFCC template.

**P2 — Planned:**

7. **Link/button labeling sweep:** `link-name` (serious) and `aria-allowed-role` (minor) violations across homepage and search — add `aria-label` to icon-only links.
8. **WebFontLoader → CSS `font-display`:** Replace JavaScript font loading with `@font-face { font-display: swap }` in CSS to eliminate render-blocking FOUT dependency on JS execution.
9. **Third-party script audit:** 20+ vendors fire on every page. Defer non-critical pixels (Reddit, Snapchat, Taboola, Nextdoor, Pinterest) until after first user interaction using a consent-gated loader — this is already partially in place via `vice-managed-src` attributes on some tags.
10. **Heading order fix:** Correct skipped heading levels on homepage to restore document outline for both AT users and crawlers.
