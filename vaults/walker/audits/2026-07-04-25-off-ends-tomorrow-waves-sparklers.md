# 25% off ends tomorrow *waves sparklers*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Ten. I think this is ten DSW emails in roughly a week. I'm not even mad at this point — I almost admire the audacity. When "*waves sparklers*" showed up in my subject line I stared at my phone for a second trying to figure out if I'd accidentally subscribed to something my granddaughter uses. That's not how I talk. That's not how anyone my age talks. But habit is a powerful thing, and I opened it anyway because I still have 25% off burned into my brain from every other email this week.
- What I saw inside: a wall of shoes, most of them clearly aimed at women — flats, little heeled boots, the kind of thing you see on a rack at Macy's in the women's section. Somewhere buried in there is a semi-annual clearance pitch that actually has some meat to it — extra 10% off with a code. That clearance angle is the only thing that made me pause. Clearance + DSW = maybe something comfortable hiding in there if I dig.
- Would I click? Maybe the clearance button, on a slow holiday afternoon. Would I forward to a friend? No chance. This email wasn't written for me or any guy I know.

## What's working

- The semi-annual sale banner is bold and unmissable — red block, large type, hard to scroll past without noticing
- "Extra 10% off clearance" with a code gives me a second reason to engage beyond the 25% that's been showing up all week
- "Ends tomorrow" lands cleanly — that's real urgency I can act on without overthinking it
- VIP perks section is visible, which at least acknowledges I'm a known customer and not a stranger off the street
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no obvious layout mess on what I can see

## What's weak

- Every shoe in that product grid looks like it's for my wife, not me — I counted one or two that might be unisex, and the rest are heels and feminine flats
- "*waves sparklers*" in the subject line is trying too hard; cute language like that reads young and a little silly to me
- "Brand New...And So You" is a weird tagline — I don't know what it means and it doesn't make me want to shop
- The shop-by-size grid is filler — knowing DSW carries size 5 does nothing for me; I want to see men's walking shoes, cushioned soles, something I can put on without bending over
- This is the same basic 25% off email I've gotten every single day this week; the sparklers costume doesn't change the fact that I've seen this movie before

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the women's new arrivals grid** — if DSW knows I buy men's comfort shoes, show me men's comfort shoes. One row of Skechers or New Balance with the 25% off tag would do more work than sixteen women's flats.
- 2. **Lead with clearance more aggressively** — the semi-annual sale is a stronger hook for a value-conscious guy like me than new arrivals; flip the hierarchy so clearance comes first, new arrivals second
- 3. **Kill the cutesy subject language** — "waves sparklers" is a younger person's joke; keep it plain and the numbers do the work
- 4. **Name the comfort category** — "Men's walking shoes, 25% off" in the hero would stop my scroll cold; right now I have to go hunting
- 5. **Add one featured men's comfort shoe** — a single product image with the price, the discount, and maybe a comfort callout would give me something to actually evaluate
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off ends tonight — men's walking & comfort shoes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance + 25% off: your size is still in stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Semi-annual sale on men's cushioned shoes — shop before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 10% off clearance. Ends tomorrow. Comfort brands included.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze with XHTML 1.0 Transitional, table-based layout, and a dual-font stack (Futura PT + HelveticaNow). Two structural markup errors are present in the `<head>`; the remaining categories cannot be fully audited because the HTML source is truncated before the body content.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot audit** — source truncated before `<body>`. Full link inspection (redirect chains, click-tracking wrapper domains, UTM presence) requires the complete HTML.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Malformed charset meta tag.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing the required `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without it, the charset declaration is ignored in some rendering contexts.

- **Invalid CSS property casing in all `@font-face` blocks.** Every `@font-face` rule uses `Src:` (capital S) instead of the spec-required lowercase `src:`. Most CSS parsers are forgiving, but it is non-compliant and will silently fail in strict parsers (e.g., some WebKit builds).

- **TTF-only format for HelveticaNow fonts; WOFF-only for Futura PT.** Neither family includes a WOFF2 source. Email client `@font-face` support is limited regardless, but TTF files are larger and unsupported in several webmail clients. Both families should include at least a WOFF fallback; the absence of WOFF2 is a missed optimization.

- **`alt` text on images** — cannot audit; body is truncated.

**No issues found:**
- Dark mode implementation is correct: `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` is paired with a `:root { color-scheme }` CSS declaration and a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block.
- Gmail-specific overrides (`div[style*="margin: 16px 0"]`, `#MessageViewBody`, `.a6S`) are present and correctly applied.
- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection` suppression are both present.
