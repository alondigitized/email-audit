# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Same movie, different day. There's that pink floral D'Lites - Spring Bloom sneaker again — I saw it on 8/11 and 8/12, and here it is a third time as the hero image telling me to "complete my order." I've never put a women's flowered sneaker in a cart, and at this point that's not a targeting miss, it's a broken record.
- The grid below is a little better — I can spot a couple of men's slip-ins in there, the Delson 3.0 - Roth looks like something I'd actually wear — but they're buried under two rows of women's and kids' product before I get to anything for me. The 25% off kids sale banner tells me plainly this email isn't built with me in mind at all.
- I'm not clicking. This is the third "your cart" email built around a shoe that isn't mine, and I'm starting to tune these out the same way I ignore the point balance — it's just noise until the shoe on the screen is actually something I'd wear.

## What's working

- The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" banner is at least legible and the orange "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is easy to spot on a phone screen.
- Buried in the product grid there are two shoes I'd actually consider — the Men's Slip-ins: Delson 3.0 - Roth and the Boy's Garage Sneaker looks like a kid gift, not for me but at least it's a guy's shoe style.
- Payment options (Afterpay, Klarna) and curbside pickup are called out clearly near the bottom, which is useful info if I were closer to buying.

## What's weak

- The hero product — again — is a pink floral women's sneaker with embroidered flowers, the same D'Lites - Spring Bloom from 8/11 and 8/12. That's never been in any cart of mine.
- This is the same "abandoned cart" gimmick run on 8/8, 8/9, 8/11, and now 8/12 and 8/13 — five times in six days with a shoe I've never touched.
- The 25% off banner is for kids' styles — I don't have young kids to shop for, so that's dead space for me.
- Nothing in the layout speaks to comfort, cushioning, or arch support — just "shop all best sellers" and category links.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop using this same pink floral women's shoe as the "your cart" hero — if there's no actual cart data for me, don't fake one. Lead with a men's comfort shoe instead, something like the Delson 3.0 that's already buried in the grid.
- 2. Segment the kids' sale banner out of my emails entirely if I've got no kids' purchase history — it's wasted real estate for a 62-year-old guy.
- 3. Move the men's product tiles to the top of the grid, not row two, so I don't have to scroll past shoes I'll never buy.
- 4. Add one line about cushioning or arch support next to whatever men's shoe leads — that's the actual hook that gets me to tap.
