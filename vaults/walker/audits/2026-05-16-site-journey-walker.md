The navigation skeleton is clean and the Hands Free Slip-ins message is front and center — that's real progress from where I've been on this site. But two things killed the session dead: the cart page rendered completely blank (I can't buy what I can't check out), and searching "comfort shoes" served me a wall of women's shoes even though I'm logged in as a man. ## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO 50% OFF is pinned everywhere.** That blue banner follows me across every step. I can't miss it, and it makes the $100 price feel more manageable — I'm already doing the math on getting a second pair. Good.
- **The Men's menu is genuinely easy.** Full-screen, large text, one tap per level. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are surfaced directly in the Men's menu — I didn't have to dig. That's exactly what I need.
- **The Hands Free Slip-ins category page leads with fit filters.** Wide Fit and Extra Wide Fit pills are right at the top above the grid. That matters at my age. My feet aren't getting narrower.
- **The size grid on the product page is excellent.** Each size tile is large, well-spaced, and easy to tap with a thumb. The "Please select a size" warning in red is clear and not buried.
- **The sticky ADD TO CART button is full-width and blue.** No hunting. No accidental taps on something else. That button does what a button should do.
- **The product page shows the BOGO offer inline.** "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" right under the price — I don't have to remember the banner from two pages ago.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart is a blank white page.** Step 8 is an empty white screen. Nothing. No items, no total, no checkout button — just white. If I had actually gotten there after adding a shoe to my cart, I would have thought the site crashed and closed the tab. This is the single most damaging issue on the site.
- **Search "comfort shoes" returns women's products.** I'm logged in. Skechers knows I'm a man. The first two results on a 1,445-item "COMFORT SHOES" page are both labeled Women's. That's not a minor oversight — that's the personalization engine failing at the most basic task. I'd bounce within five seconds.
- **The product has zero reviews.** "No Reviews" under the star rating is a trust killer for someone like me. I'm not Hoka or Brooks — I don't have brand loyalty baked in yet. I need other guys my age telling me their feet felt good after a three-hour walk. Without that, $100 is a harder sell.
- **The login step is invisible.** The Step 3 screenshot shows the same homepage I was already on. There's no sign that I'm recognized — no "Welcome back, Walker" or even a name in the account icon. If I logged in, the site isn't acknowledging it.
- **The homepage defaults to the Women's tab.** The bottom tab bar shows Women | Men | Kids' | Recently Viewed, with Women underlined. I tap the site as a man — why is Women my starting point?
- **The hero image skews young.** A young man and a young woman modeling Glide-Steps. I get that they're selling aspirationally, but I'm 62. Show me someone my age doing this comfortably. I trust that more.
- **No comfort language above the fold on the product page.** The PDP leads with name, price, and BOGO. I have to scroll to find out anything about cushioning, arch support, or what makes this shoe actually good for my feet. The Hands Free badge is there, but that's one piece of a three-part story I care about (hands-free + cushioning + arch support).

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page — this is P0.** A blank cart is a complete checkout failure. Nothing else matters if I can't buy the shoe. Investigate and fix immediately.
2. **Apply gender filter to search results for logged-in users.** If I'm logged in as a male, "comfort shoes" should default to Men's results, or at minimum interleave them. Surface a "Men's" quick-filter pill at the top of the results.
3. **Personalize the homepage tab default by account gender.** Flip the default tab to Men for male-identified accounts. This is a one-line fix that signals the site actually knows who I am.
4. **Add a "Welcome back" signal post-login.** Even just the account icon changing to a first name initial tells me the login worked and the site remembers me. Right now I can't tell.
5. **Surface comfort tech language earlier on the PDP.** Before I scroll, I want to see "Air-Cooled Memory Foam" or "Arch Fit" — whatever this shoe has. Put that immediately below the price, above the size grid.
6. **Seed some reviews.** 1,445 results on a comfort shoes search but zero reviews on a featured product is an inconsistency. Prioritize products with real reviews in the PDP spotlight, or run a post-purchase review campaign for recent buyers of this SKU.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Skechers.com runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). The mobile journey shows healthy TTFB on most pages (23–35ms), a notable spike on View Cart (133ms), aggressive bot-protection rate-limiting that is hitting legitimate traffic, a Stylitics CORS misconfiguration, duplicate third-party script loads, and a View Cart page that appears to render without a proper HTML document frame (missing `<title>` and `lang`).

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — An ARIA role requiring a parent container is orphaned. Most likely a `role="option"` or `role="listitem"` rendered outside its required parent by a carousel or dropdown widget.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — A native element has an ARIA role that conflicts with its implicit role.
- `heading-order` [moderate] — Heading levels skip (e.g., h1 → h3), breaking screen-reader document outline.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level` + `landmark-no-duplicate-main` + `landmark-unique` [moderate] — Multiple `<main>` elements detected, likely from lazy-loaded content or A/B test variants inserting a second document skeleton.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` [moderate] — `<aside>` nested inside another landmark instead of being a direct child of `<body>`.
- `region` [moderate] — Content exists outside any landmark region, making it unreachable via landmark navigation.

**View Cart (4 violations):**
- `document-title` [serious] — The cart page has no `<title>`. Combined with the 429 errors, this strongly indicates the cart loads in a fragment/overlay that does not update the document `<head>`. Screen readers announce "untitled" and browser history is broken.
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — No `lang` attribute on the `<html>` element. Screen readers cannot select the correct language profile. This paired with the missing title confirms the cart is rendered outside the main document (iframe or injected SPA shell without head management).
- `landmark-one-main` [moderate] — No `<main>` landmark present.
- `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<h1>` in the cart view.

**Search (3 violations):**
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` — Same pattern as homepage; likely the same sidebar/filter component.

**Root cause for cart violations:** The cart page is almost certainly rendered in a client-side overlay or modal that injects markup without updating `document.title`, `<html lang>`, or providing a full document skeleton. This needs to be fixed at the SPA routing layer.

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`** — If Googlebot ever crawls a cart URL (e.g., via canonical leakage or sitemap error), it will index an untitled page.
- **Duplicate Monetate script injection** — `se.monetate.net/js/2/...` and `se.monetate.net/js/3/...` are both loaded. If Monetate injects content, duplicate execution can produce duplicate DOM nodes that confuse crawlers.
- **`--vh` inline style on `<html>`** — Applied via JS after load, not a direct SEO issue, but signals no server-side viewport hint is being sent.
- No canonical, hreflang, or structured data issues are detectable from the truncated source, but the volume of injected scripts increases render-blocking risk for crawlers with execution time budgets.

**LCP/CLS not captured** — Incomplete dataset. The `--vh` CSS custom property (`6.64px`) is set via JS, which means any element sized relative to `--vh` will cause layout recalculation after first paint, contributing to CLS. This is a structural issue.

**Third-party script load:**
The `<head>` on every page loads 20+ distinct third-party scripts from ~15 origins before or during parse:

- TikTok pixel (`identify` + `main` + `events` = 3 requests)
- Google Tag Manager + 4 gtag destinations (5 requests)
- Monetate × 2 endpoints (duplicate — 2 requests)
- Yahoo ytc.js × 2 (duplicate — 2 requests)
- Pinterest, Bing, Reddit, QuantumMetric, Impact, CreativeCDN, Bloomreach, Snapchat, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Stape, PulseInsights, Tune/Go2SDK, WebFontLoader, TrustArc

Even with `async`, each origin requires DNS + TCP + TLS. At 15 origins × ~50ms per cold connection on mobile, this is 750ms+ of parallel network overhead on first visit. Main thread execution of all these pixels compounds it.

**Confirmed duplicates requiring immediate fix:**
1. Yahoo `ytc.js` loaded twice from the same URL in the same `<head>`
2. Monetate loaded from both `/js/2/` and `/js/3/` path variants

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` workaround** — The `style="--vh: 6.64px"` on `<html>` is a well-known JS workaround for the iOS Safari 100vh bug. It requires JS execution before correct viewport units can be used, which delays any above-the-fold element that uses this variable. The modern fix is `svh`/`dvh`/`lvh` CSS units (supported in iOS Safari 16+).
- **No LCP/CLS data** — Cannot fully assess image lazy loading, font rendering, or scroll jank without these metrics.
- **TrustArc consent overlay** — Loads fonts from `consent.trustarc.com` via a `@font-face` injected into `<head>` as an inline `<style>`. This is an additional blocking font load on first visit for all users in consent-required regions.
- **`vice-managed-src`** — TrustArc's consent management replaces `src` with `vice-managed-src` on conditional scripts (Pinterest, Facebook, Yahoo, Impact, Nextdoor, Taboola). This is correct behavior for CCPA/GDPR compliance.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting — KnowledgeScript (KPSDK) fingerprinting endpoint:**

```
GET /149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.412
```

This UUID path is the HUMAN Security / KnowledgeScript (`x-kpsdk`) bot detection fingerprinting endpoint, hosted on the Skechers origin. It is returning 429 on **5 consecutive requests**. This means the bot-protection layer is rate-limiting its own fingerprinting probes — a configuration error in the KPSDK policy, likely overly aggressive rate limits on the `/fp` route itself. Since fingerprint validation gates other requests, failed probes can cascade into blocked cart and checkout interactions.

**`/cart/` returning 429** — This is the most critical finding. A real user attempting to load the cart page received a 429. This is a direct conversion failure. The KnowledgeScript rate limiting policy needs to be audited; the `/cart/` path must be excluded from or given a higher threshold in the rate limit rules.

**Stylitics CORS block:**

```
Access to CSS at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: 
No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header present
```

The Stylitics outfit-recommendation widget's stylesheet is being served without `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or `*`). The widget will render unstyled or not at all. This is a misconfiguration on Stylitics's CDN/origin that Skechers needs to raise with Stylitics as a vendor issue.

**`net::ERR_FAILED`** — One resource failed with a network-level error (not HTTP error). Without the URL, likely a blocked third-party resource from a browser content blocker or an internal fetch that threw before a response.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately (conversion impact):**
1. **Audit KnowledgeScript rate limit policy.** The `/fp` fingerprinting route and `/cart/` are returning 429 to real users. Increase the rate limit threshold for the fingerprinting endpoint and verify `/cart/` is not caught by bot-protection rules that target unauthenticated sessions.

**P1 — Fix within sprint (accessibility / SEO compliance):**
2. **Fix View Cart document structure.** The cart is rendering without `<title>` and `html[lang]`. If it's a modal/overlay, use `document.title` updates on route change and ensure the cart shell is not a bare document fragment. If it's a full-page route, the server must render a complete HTML document.
3. **Fix `aria-required-parent` on Homepage [critical].** Locate the widget producing orphaned ARIA roles (likely the carousel or dropdown) and wrap the children in the required parent container.

**P2 — Fix within two sprints:**
4. **Remove duplicate script loads.** Yahoo `ytc.js` appears twice in `<head>`. Monetate loads from both `/js/2/` and `/js/3/`. Deduplicate via the GTM tag configuration.
5. **Migrate `--vh` to native CSS viewport units.** Replace the JS-computed `--vh` workaround with `svh`/`dvh` which are supported in all current mobile browsers, eliminating a JS-dependent layout dependency.
6. **Fix Stylitics CORS.** File a vendor ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN configuration.

**P3 — Ongoing optimization:**
7. **Consolidate third-party pixels under GTM.** At least 10 pixels are loaded independently outside GTM. Routing them through GTM reduces origin count, enables consent signal propagation, and simplifies deduplication.
8. **Capture LCP and CLS in monitoring.** The audit lacks LCP/CLS data for all steps. Instrument Real User Monitoring (RUM) via QuantumMetric (already present) or a dedicated Web Vitals pipeline to surface these metrics per page type.
