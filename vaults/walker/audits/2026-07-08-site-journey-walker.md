# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-08**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

I landed on skechers.com looking for comfort — slip-ins, cushioning, something that'll feel good on my feet all day. The navigation menu is clean, the Hands-Free Slip-ins category was easy enough to find, and the product page itself is solid. But I hit two walls that killed the trip: the cart page came up completely blank when I tried to checkout, and when I searched "comfort shoes," every result shown was Women's. A 62-year-old guy on his phone isn't going to dig through 1,400 results to prove a point. He's going to DSW.

**vs. Yesterday: No improvement. Score holds at 4/10.**

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same as yesterday. The broken cart alone is a zero-conversion event. Add gender-blind search results and this site is actively losing Boomer male buyers on two separate purchase paths.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO deal is front and center.** That blue top banner ("BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart") is readable and persistent across every screen. I noticed it. That's how deals should work.
- **Men's menu is genuinely useful.** The drawer has large, well-spaced rows. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are listed as direct links — no hunting required. Touch targets are finger-friendly.
- **Product page layout is clean.** The Skech-Gel page shows the name big, price big, star rating visible, and the Members deal in red. Easy to read without zooming. The size grid buttons are large enough to tap confidently.
- **"ADD TO CART" button is sticky and full-width.** Pinned to the bottom of the screen in a bold blue. That's right.
- **317 results in Hands-Free Slip-ins** — good inventory depth, and fit filters (Wide, Extra Wide, Medium) are shown as prominent pill buttons at the top.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is a blank white screen.** Step 8 is pure white — nothing rendered. I tried to buy a $105 shoe and got an empty page. This is a complete checkout failure. Revenue = zero.
- **Search ignores my gender.** Typing "comfort shoes" returns 1,424 results, and the first two shown are both labeled Women's. I'm logged in (or at least walked through login). The site knows enough to show me Men's navigation — it should know enough to rank Men's products first in search.
- **Login step produced no visible change.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 are identical screenshots — same page, same cart badge (0), no "Welcome back" greeting, no personalized hero. If I logged in, the site didn't acknowledge it.
- **Hero image talks to someone 35 years younger than me.** Two young people in streetwear outside Skechers HQ. Nothing about comfort, cushioning, or walking. That's fine for younger buyers, but there's zero signal up front that this brand gets what I need.
- **"Comfort Technologies" is buried in the Men's menu mid-list.** It's below Shoes and Shop by Activity. For my priorities, that should be higher — or at least linked prominently on the homepage.
- **Product page has only 6 reviews.** I want social proof before spending $105. Six reviews doesn't tell me much. No comfort callouts visible above the fold (no "Air-Cooled Memory Foam" or arch support language in the initial viewport).
- **No personalization anywhere.** After logging in, nothing — no name, no saved shoes, no "picks for you," no points balance. My loyalty should mean something.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is P0. A blank cart means zero revenue from anyone who gets that far. Reproduce on mobile and hotfix.
2. **Filter search results by logged-in gender.** If a user is in the Men's section or has a Men's account history, default search rankings should surface Men's products first. This is table stakes personalization.
3. **Acknowledge login with a visible signal.** Even "Hi, Walker" or showing my points balance in the header goes a long way. Right now the logged-in state is invisible.
4. **Surface comfort language on the product page above the fold.** "Air-Cooled Memory Foam," "Arch Fit," "Cushioned insole" — I need to see that before I scroll to price. Move it up.
5. **Swap the hero image periodically for the comfort-shopper demographic.** A Boomer walking a trail or doing errands converts better for me than urban streetwear models. Even if it rotates, I should see myself somewhere on that homepage.
6. **Move "Comfort Technologies" to the top of the Men's menu** — above or equal to "Shoes." That's the unique reason someone picks Skechers over New Balance.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones are decent — the menu works, the product page is legible, and the deals are visible. But I literally could not complete a purchase: the cart page was blank. And when I tried a different path and searched "comfort shoes," the site served me Women's products. Two paths in, two dead ends. At $105 a shoe, I'm not playing detective — I'm going somewhere that works on the first try.

**Score: 4/10. No change from yesterday. The cart failure is the same critical gap.**

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware), confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` asset paths. The test session triggered Kasada bot-detection middleware, producing 429s that contaminate the performance and network data. The View Cart page is missing both `<title>` and `lang`, indicating a broken or partial document template. Stylitics widget stylesheet has a CORS misconfiguration. Third-party script payload is extremely heavy (15+ vendors injected in `<head>`).

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (1)**
- `aria-required-parent` — Homepage has ARIA role elements missing their required parent container. This breaks screen-reader tree traversal.

**Serious (2)**
- `document-title` — View Cart renders without a `<title>`. Likely a SPA modal/partial template that never sets `document.title`.
- `html-has-lang` — View Cart `<html>` element has no `lang` attribute. The homepage correctly sets `lang="en"`; the cart page does not inherit it, suggesting it's rendered in a separate document context (iframe or document.write).

**Moderate (10)**
- `heading-order` — Homepage heading hierarchy is non-sequential.
- `landmark-no-duplicate-main` / `landmark-main-is-top-level` — Multiple `<main>` landmarks on Homepage; at least one is nested inside another landmark.
- `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` — `<aside>` elements nested inside other landmarks on Homepage and Search.
- `landmark-unique` — Duplicate landmark roles with no distinguishing labels (Homepage, Search).
- `region` — Content exists outside any landmark on Homepage and Search.
- `landmark-one-main` — View Cart has no `<main>` at all.
- `page-has-heading-one` — View Cart has no `<h1>`.

**Minor (2)**
- `aria-allowed-role` — Elements using `role` values that are not permitted for that element type (Homepage, Search).

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` — JS-calculated viewport height unit (window.innerHeight / 100) is not being rounded before being written to the CSS custom property. The floating-point remainder causes subpixel layout rounding inconsistencies across components that use `calc(var(--vh) * N)`. Should be `Math.round()` or fixed to 2 decimal places.
- WebFont Loader (`webfontloader.js`) is loaded `async` and applies font-active classes to `<html>` after load. Until fonts resolve, fallback font renders, causing layout shifts (a likely CLS contributor that the missing CLS data would confirm).
- The Snapchat pixel (`tr.snapchat.com/config/...`) appears only on the Men category page HTML, not on Homepage or Login — inconsistent pixel placement across the funnel.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada bot-detection fingerprint endpoint (5 errors)**
```
/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
The path structure (`x-kpsdk-v`) is Kasada's client-side proof-of-work fingerprinting SDK. Automated test sessions exceed the challenge rate limit, triggering 429s. These errors will not appear in normal user sessions unless the user's browser is misconfigured, but they contaminate synthetic monitoring results and can block cart operations for flagged sessions.

**429 — Cart page**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart page itself returned 429 during the test. This is a Kasada challenge gate on the cart route — high-value bot target. If triggered for real users (e.g., after rapid product adds), it would silently fail the cart load with no user-visible error message, which is a conversion risk.

**CORS block — Stylitics stylesheet**
```
https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=48eb0a00f5
```
Blocked by CORS policy: missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for `https://www.skechers.com`. The Stylitics outfit-grid widget will render unstyled or not at all. This is a Stylitics CDN configuration issue — the vendor needs to add `skechers.com` to their CORS allowlist for this asset, or the site should proxy the stylesheet through its own domain.

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — View Cart document template (critical)**
The cart page is missing `<title>` and `lang`. Find the cart page template in SFCC (`cartPage.isml` or equivalent) and ensure it extends the base template that sets these. Validate that all modal-style overlays that render full documents also inherit the base template.

**Priority 2 — Fix aria-required-parent on Homepage (critical ARIA)**
Audit the homepage carousel/slider or tab component for ARIA role children (`role="option"`, `role="row"`, `role="tab"`, etc.) that are being rendered outside their required parent containers. Common cause: JS-rendered components that inject children before the parent container is in the DOM.

**Priority 3 — Stylitics CORS**
File a support ticket with Stylitics to add `https://www.skechers.com` to the CORS allowlist for `web-assets.stylitics.com`. As a short-term workaround, proxy `style.css` through a Skechers-controlled endpoint (e.g., `/on/demandware.static/...`).

**Priority 4 — Capture LCP and CLS**
The audit has no LCP or CLS data. Add real-user monitoring (RUM) for Core Web Vitals on PDP and PLP pages specifically. Quantum Metric (already present) can be configured to report these. Without LCP data, you cannot assess Google Search ranking impact.

**Priority 5 — Round `--vh` value**
In the JS that sets `document.documentElement.style.setProperty('--vh', ...)`, wrap the value in `parseFloat((window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(2))` to eliminate subpixel drift.

**Priority 6 — Landmark structure on Homepage**
Consolidate duplicate `<main>` elements — the page should have exactly one. Move `<aside>` elements to top-level (siblings of `<main>`, not children of `<header>` or `<section>`). Ensure all content is wrapped in a landmark region.

**Priority 7 — Third-party script audit**
15+ ad/analytics vendors are injected unconditionally on every page. Tag Manager should gate non-critical pixels (Taboola, Reddit, Snapchat, Pinterest, TikTok) behind a consent state check and defer loading until after LCP. Monetate is loaded twice from two different script paths — investigate whether both are necessary.
