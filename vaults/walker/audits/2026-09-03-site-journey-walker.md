# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-03**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I made it from the homepage all the way to a search results page, and along the way I found a genuinely nice product page and a decent shoe finder — but the two steps that actually matter for buying anything, Log In and Add to Cart, both failed to do their job. Logging in did nothing I could see. Adding my shoes to the cart got blocked by a size/width warning I never got to clear, and then the cart page itself loaded as a blank white screen. I closed the tab more confused than when I'd started, with an empty cart the whole way through.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** — up slightly from yesterday's 2/10, but still failing at the finish line. The browsing experience (category menu, shoe listing, product page, search) is solid enough to earn a few points back. The cart being a blank page and the login step doing nothing visible are the kind of failures that lose a sale outright, not just annoy a shopper.

## 3. What's Working

- **Product detail page (Step 6)** is clean and readable: big price, star rating with review count, a clear "Members Get 20% Off" callout, and a "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge right where I'm looking. That's the kind of comfort-forward signal I actually care about.
- **Men's Shoes listing (Step 5)** leads with exactly my categories — "Hands Free Slip-Ins," "Walking Shoes," "Athletic Sneakers" — as big photo tiles before I even scroll to the grid. That's smart for someone who doesn't want to wade through filters.
- **Category flyout menu (Step 4)** is well organized — Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections all listed with generous tap targets and a clear Back/X at top.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" (Step 9)** actually returned comfort-relevant shoes — slip-ins and a "Max Cushioning" model — with prices and a sale price called out in red. That's a real improvement over a search that returns junk.
- **Persistent top banner** (20% off / Labor Day sale) stayed consistent and readable across every page, so at least I always knew the deal was still on.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Log In did nothing.** Step 3's screenshot is pixel-identical to the homepage. No form, no modal, no "welcome back" — nothing to indicate the site registered my tap.
- **Dismiss Popups had nothing to dismiss** and the page was unchanged, so it's hard to know if that step worked or just had no popup to begin with.
- **Add to Cart failed silently on my behalf.** Step 7 shows red warnings — "Please select a width," "Please select a size" — meaning the size boxes shown were empty until I supposedly hit Add to Cart. Cart badge stayed at 0.
- **Cart page (Step 8) is a blank white screen.** Nothing rendered — no header, no banner, no "your cart is empty" message, nothing. For someone my age, a blank screen reads as "the site is broken," and I'd likely just leave.
- **Cart badge stuck at 0 through the entire rest of the journey** (Steps 8 and 9), confirming nothing ever actually got added, so the whole point of the trip — buying shoes — never happened.
- **No visible personalization anywhere.** Even setting aside that login didn't visibly work, there's no "Welcome back, Walker" or tailored recommendation strip once I'm supposedly signed in.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page rendering** — a blank screen at checkout is the single most damaging thing on this list; it needs to show cart contents or an empty-cart message, always.
2. **Make Add to Cart guide me, not block me** — if size/width are required, scroll to them or highlight them immediately when I tap Add to Cart, instead of leaving a silent validation error above the fold.
3. **Give login a visible result** — a confirmation banner, avatar change, or redirect so a 62-year-old shopper isn't left wondering if the tap registered.
4. **Surface a "sign-in benefits" or personalized strip** post-login (recently viewed, saved sizes) to justify the account in the first place.
5. **Add a comfort/arch-support filter** to the Men's Shoes and search results grids so I can narrow 737 results and 1,508 search results without scrolling forever.

## 6. Bottom Line

The pages I can *look at* — product detail, category browsing, search — are genuinely better than a lot of sites in this space and speak my language (slip-ins, cushioning, member discounts). But the pages I need to *act on* — log in, add to cart, view cart — either did nothing or broke outright. A comfort shopper like me will forgive a plain product grid; I won't forgive a blank cart page after I thought I bought something.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Audited 8 steps of a mobile journey through skechers.com (Homepage → Log In → Men category → Men > Shoes → Product detail → Add to Cart → View Cart → Search). The site is a Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware) storefront (`on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/...`) loaded with 20+ third-party tags (GTM, Monetate, TikTok, Snapchat, Reddit, Taboola, Quantum Metric, Pulse Insights, etc.) injected via GTM. Bot-detection/fingerprinting (Kasada `kpsdk`) is present and is actively rate-limiting the session (429s). One page state (View Cart) fails basic document requirements (`<title>`, `lang`). Post-category navigation shows TTFB dropping to ~25-50ms, consistent with client-side/SPA-style routing rather than full document reloads for later steps.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations across 3 of the 8 audited states:

**Homepage (9 violations)**
- `[critical] aria-required-parent` — ARIA role(s) used without the required parent role/context.
- `[serious] link-name` — one or more links have no discernible accessible name (screen reader users cannot identify the link's purpose).
- `[moderate] heading-order` — heading levels skip/are out of sequence.
- `[moderate] landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique` — multiple/nested landmark regions (likely from third-party widget markup duplicating `<main>`/`<aside>` roles).
- `[moderate] region` — content exists outside any landmark region.
- `[minor] aria-allowed-role` — an element uses a `role` value not permitted for its tag.

**View Cart (4 violations)**
- `[serious] document-title` — page has no non-empty `<title>`.
- `[serious] html-has-lang` — `<html>` missing `lang` attribute on this page state.
- `[moderate] landmark-one-main` — no `<main>` landmark present.
- `[moderate] page-has-heading-one` — no `<h1>` on the page.

**Search results (4 violations)**
- `[minor] aria-allowed-role`, `[moderate] landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` — same landmark/role pattern as homepage, indicating a shared layout/widget component reused across templates.

The cluster of landmark violations on Homepage and Search (identical violation set) points to a shared header/sidebar component rather than independent bugs.

## SEO

- **View Cart is missing `<title>` and `lang`** — this page state is effectively non-indexable/non-descriptive if crawled or shared, and screen readers/translation tools cannot determine document language.
- Homepage/Log In/Men category HTML samples correctly set `<html lang="en">` and load fonts via `webfontloader.js`, gating render on `wf-*-active` classes — this is a render-blocking dependency pattern that can affect indexation of above-the-fold text if fonts fail to load in time.
- No canonical, meta description, or structured data were visible in the truncated HTML samples provided — cannot confirm presence/absence; flagged as needing full `<head>` capture to verify.

## Mobile Optimization

- `<html>` carries an inline `style="--vh: 6.64px..."` — a custom-property viewport-height polyfill, typically added via JS to work around mobile browser chrome/URL-bar resizing (`100vh` bug workaround). This runs on every page load and depends on a resize/orientation JS listener; if that script fails or races, layout relying on `--vh` will be wrong until recalculated.
- Font loading is gated behind Web Font Loader (`wf-mulish-n3-active` … `wf-active` classes appended post-load) — on slower mobile connections this can produce a flash of invisible/unstyled text (FOIT/FOUT) since these classes are added asynchronously.
- No viewport meta tag was visible in the truncated samples — should be verified directly, as its absence/misconfiguration is a common mobile-rendering defect.

## Console & Network Errors

**Console (14 total, 2 distinct issues repeating):**
- 4× `429` resource load failures (see Network below).
- 4× `Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified...` — the page is invoking the browser's built-in Summarizer API (Chrome AI) with an unsupported language, and the call is aborted every time it fires. This is dead/misconfigured code executing repeatedly.
- 1× CORS block: `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` blocked from `www.skechers.com` origin — the Stylitics stylesheet is missing appropriate CORS headers, so the resource fails to apply.
- 1× `net::ERR_FAILED` (correlates with the blocked Stylitics stylesheet).

**Network (6 total):**
- 5× `429` on `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.728` — Kasada bot-detection fingerprint endpoint being rate-limited during a normal single-session crawl.
- 1× `429` on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` — the cart page itself returned 429 at least once during the journey, which is more serious: legitimate user traffic is being throttled by the anti-bot layer on a core commerce path.

## Recommendations

1. **Fix View Cart document metadata**: add a non-empty `<title>` and `lang` attribute to the cart page state — currently fails both SEO and a11y baseline checks.
2. **Investigate Kasada 429s on `/cart/`**: bot-detection is throttling a core transactional endpoint during normal browsing; tune fingerprint-endpoint or cart rate limits to avoid false positives on real users.
3. **Remove or gate the Summarizer API call**: it fires with an unsupported language on every relevant page and is aborted each time — dead code path, should be removed or corrected to specify a supported language.
4. **Fix Stylitics CORS configuration**: `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` needs proper `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` headers, or should be self-hosted/proxied, so the stylesheet actually loads.
5. **Resolve landmark/role structure** shared across Homepage and Search templates: eliminate duplicate `<main>`/`<aside>` roles and ensure all content sits inside a valid landmark — likely a single shared component fix.
6. **Add accessible names to unlabeled links** (Homepage `link-name` violation) and fix heading order.
7. **Re-run performance capture with LCP/CLS instrumentation enabled** — current data only covers TTFB; without LCP/CLS, Core Web Vitals compliance cannot be assessed.
8. **Confirm whether steps after "Men category" are full page loads or client-side transitions** — if SPA-routed, TTFB is not a meaningful metric for those steps and should be replaced with route-change timing.
