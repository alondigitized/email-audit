# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- **Skechers | "Your Items Are Waiting!" | 2026-05-01**
- Abandoned cart email — and they got the hero product right. That grey-and-blue Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro Slip-in sitting front and center is exactly what I was looking at. The "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US" headline is a little cute, but I don't mind it — at least they're acknowledging I've been around. The 20% off banner below the fold is the real hook. Problem is, by the time I scroll to it, I've already had to wade through a set of "recommended" products that look like they include women's shoes, a category nav that puts WOMEN before MEN, and a Mother's Day sale mention at the very bottom — none of which is me. Targeted where it counts, generic where it shouldn't be.

## What's working

- **Hero product is spot on.** Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro — Lareo. That's my shoe. Showing me exactly what I was looking at is the right call. I don't have to hunt for it.
- **"COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is direct.** No clever copy games. Just a clear button. I appreciate that.
- **20% off a single pair.** That's a real discount. Skechers aren't cheap and as a Plus member I'm always watching for savings. The banner is bold enough I noticed it even on a small screen.
- **Slip-ins are actually the star.** Unlike a lot of blast emails, this one isn't dumping running shoes or dress shoes on me. The product family feels right.

## What's weak

- **"We Recommended" products below the hero include what looks like women's styles.** I can see smaller product images under "HERE'S SOME MORE GREAT OPTIONS WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE" — and at least one appears to be a women's shoe. I'm 62 and shopping for myself. Stop sending me over to the women's aisle.
- **WOMEN listed before MEN in the category nav.** Small thing, but it's a signal I'm not the primary audience they designed this for.
- **"WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US…" is a bit too clever.** It's not offensive, but it made me pause — slightly reads like the store is watching me. For my generation, that feels a touch invasive, even if I logically know how cart recovery works.
- **20% off is below the fold.** On my phone, I'd have to scroll to see it. That's a miss. Lead with the offer.
- **Mother's Day sale callout at the very bottom.** I'm not buying Mother's Day gifts at Skechers. Fine print distracts from what should be a clean finish.
- **Fine print at the bottom is tiny.** I'd need to pinch-zoom to read the legal copy. Not ideal on a phone screen.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 20% off banner above the fold**, ideally right below the hero product and before any secondary recommendations. I should see the offer before I see the filler.
- 2. **Filter the "You May Also Like" module to men's only.** I have a gender on file. Use it.
- 3. **Flip the category nav order for this segment** — MEN before WOMEN when emailing a male customer.
- 4. **Drop the Mother's Day footnote** from this send. It's noise in an otherwise focused cart-recovery email.
- 5. **Soften "We noticed you noticing us"** — something like "You left something behind" is warmer and less surveillance-y for Boomer shoppers.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Your Items Are Waiting!" (2026-05-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary
Standard Attentive-powered cart-abandonment email using table-based layout with responsive CSS. Multiple HTTP-over-HTTPS violations and a malformed charset declaration are the most actionable infrastructure issues.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP instead of HTTPS on all tracked links — confirmed risk.**
Every `href` and `src` in the visible source uses `http://`, not `https://`:
- Click-tracking host: `http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...`
- Image CDN: `http://image.emails.skechers.com/…`

Plain-HTTP tracking URLs fail modern spam-filter reputation checks, trigger "not secure" warnings in some clients, and are vulnerable to MITM redirect stripping. Attentive supports HTTPS click tracking — this is a configuration flag in the sending account, not a code change.

**Tracking domain:** `skechers.attentivemail.com` — Attentive-managed subdomain; expected for this ESP.

**UTM parameters:** Not directly visible in the raw `href` values (they are encoded inside the `upn=` payload). Cannot confirm UTM presence or correctness without decoding; see §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Empty `<title>` element** | `<title></title>` — some clients (Apple Mail, Outlook on macOS) render this in the window/tab bar, producing a blank label |
| **Malformed charset `<meta>` tag** | `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` is missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` or `<meta charset="utf-8">`. Missing attribute causes charset declaration to be silently ignored in strict parsers. |
| **Link underlines suppressed globally** | `#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }` removes all visual link indicators — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 violation for users who rely on underlines to distinguish links from body text |
| **Layout tables missing `role="presentation"`** | All `<table>` elements used for layout (`.nl-container`, `.row`, `.row-content`) lack `role="presentation"`, causing screen readers to announce spurious table structure |
| **Image `alt` attributes** | `<img` tag is truncated — unable to confirm. Must verify all product images carry descriptive `alt` text and decorative images use `alt=""` |

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe)

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are **not present in the visible HTML fragment.** CAN-SPAM §5 requires both in every commercial message. These elements are almost certainly in the footer (truncated), but cannot be confirmed from the provided source.

**Email authentication headers not provided.** Cannot verify SPF, DKIM, or DMARC alignment. For a brand sending from `msgs.skechers.com`, DMARC alignment against the `skechers.com` organizational domain should be confirmed with the ESP.

List-Unsubscribe header (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) not supplied — required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (≥5k/day threshold, effective Feb 2024). Confirm this is set at the ESP level.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are encoded inside the Attentive `upn=` tracking payload, not appended as visible query strings. The actual destination URLs cannot be decoded from the source alone. **Required verification:** decode one `upn=` value and confirm:
- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present and non-empty
- `utm_content` or `utm_term` distinguishes CTA position (logo vs. product image vs. "Shop Now" button)
- Landing page matches the abandoned cart session (product page, not homepage)
