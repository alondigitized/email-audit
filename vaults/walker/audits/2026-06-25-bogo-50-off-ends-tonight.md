## Executive summary

- DSW is in my sender column, thumb moved, opened. At this point that's just reflex — I've opened every one of their sends this week. But when it loaded, I stopped and stared at the screen for a second. It's sandals. Wedges. Strappy flats. A wall of women's shoes from top to bottom. Same deal I've been looking at since Monday, just with "ENDS TONIGHT" slapped on the front instead of "LAST CALL." Yesterday's email said last call too, so my credibility meter ticked down a notch.
- Here's the thing though — BOGO 50% off is the kind of math I actually do in a shoe store. I can hold that number in my head, and it still works. The problem is the entire email is pointing me at things I have no use for. Wedges. Strappy sandals. "Mid Week Mood Boost." That's not me. That's not even close to me. I scrolled all the way to the bottom looking for a men's section or a walking shoe or something with a cushioned footbed and I came up empty.

- **BOGO 50% OFF is immediately visible** — no hunting, no decoding; the hero headline lands the deal in under two seconds.
- **"ENDS TONIGHT" creates real urgency** — it's a specific window, and it actually made me consider whether I had time to look for men's shoes before the deal expired.
- **Sandals starting at $39.99 in the preview** — a concrete price anchor that's easy to evaluate; I appreciated knowing the floor before I opened.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — everything loaded, nothing overlapped; a small thing but some brands can't even get this right.

## What's weak

- **The entire email is women's merchandise** — wedges, strappy sandals, heeled mules; I'm 62 and shopping for walking comfort, and there isn't a single men's shoe in sight.
- **"Mid Week Mood Boost" speaks to nobody I know** — that headline is aimed at a younger woman, not a guy checking his email after lunch who just wants cushioned slip-ons.
- **Credibility took a hit** — yesterday's email was "last call." Today's is "ends tonight." If last call keeps happening, the urgency stops meaning anything.
- **No comfort details anywhere** — not one word about cushioning, arch support, or fit. That's literally my deciding factor and DSW knows I'm shopping comfort brands; they just didn't act like it.
- **Preview text leaks spacer junk** — the "*AHEM* Sandals Starting at $39.99" is fine, but after it the inbox shows a long string of invisible padding characters; looks like the email template hiccuped.

- 1. **Show me men's content** — if DSW's list knows I've been clicking their men's walking and casual sections, segment this. Lead with one men's comfort product, even a single shoe, and the BOGO logic pays off immediately.
- 2. **Retire "last call" / "ends tonight" after day one** — running both in consecutive sends kills the believability. Pick the deadline, announce it once, and hold it.
- 3. **Drop "Mid Week Mood Boost"** — it reads as a social media caption for a 27-year-old. Replace it with something practical: "The deal's almost gone. Grab your size."
- 4. **Add a comfort signal** — one word in the sub-headline ("cushioned," "walking-ready," "arch-support") would make me feel like DSW remembered who I am.
- 5. **Fix the preheader spacer bleed** — the real copy ("Sandals Starting at $39.99") is solid; trim the spacers so inbox clients don't show the invisible padding trail.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@font-face` uses invalid `Src:` (capital S) — all four Futura PT and six HelveticaNow declarations**

```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/.../original.woff") format('woff');
```

`Src` is not a valid CSS property. The correct property is lowercase `src:`. Some CSS parsers are case-insensitive and will handle this silently; others (particularly WebKit strict mode) will fail to load the font and fall back to system fonts. This affects all 10 `@font-face` blocks.

**HelveticaNow served as `.ttf` only**

Futura PT is served as `.woff` (good email client support). HelveticaNow is served exclusively as `.ttf`, which has weaker support in email clients vs. `.woff`. Clients that don't render `.ttf` will fall back to the system font stack.

**Duplicate conflicting `-ms-text-size-adjust` values in the `*` selector**

```css
*{
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* set first */
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;
  ...
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides to none */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

The second declaration wins, setting `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` on all elements in Outlook/IE. This contradicts the intent of the `100%` value and can cause text scaling issues on Windows. The `none` value should be removed or the duplicate eliminated.

**Missing `http-equiv` on two meta tags**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```

Both are missing their `http-equiv` attribute, making them effectively no-ops:
- Should be: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`
- Should be: `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />`

Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, charset declaration relies solely on the MIME type in mail headers. Without `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, the IE rendering hint is ignored entirely.
