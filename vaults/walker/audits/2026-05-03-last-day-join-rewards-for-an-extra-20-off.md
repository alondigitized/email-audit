# LAST DAY ⏰ Join Rewards for an extra 20% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's not from Skechers, so right off the bat I'm already half-checked out. Famous Footwear's rewards pitch landed in my inbox with a "LAST DAY" hook and a 20% off offer — I can see that much in the hero and I understand what they're selling. But the preview text in my inbox was a garbled tracking link, which looked like the email was broken before I even opened it. Inside, the layout is clean enough and the offer is real, but the Nike banner pushes "Shop Women" and "Shop Kids" without a men's equivalent anywhere in sight. The product thumbnails are a mixed bag of athletic and casual styles, nothing I'd associate with comfort-first, slip-on ease. As a guy who's loyal to Skechers and my Skechers Plus account, Famous Footwear asking me to "join rewards" feels like a stranger asking me to switch teams. Not impossible, but the email didn't do the work to earn that.

## What's working

- The 20% off hero is clear and immediate — I didn't have to hunt for the offer. The "SHOP MEN" button is there, so at minimum they acknowledged I exist as a male shopper. The email renders crisply on what I assume is a mobile view; text is readable without squinting. The "Save more on styles you'll love" product grid gives me options to browse without being too overwhelming. The Nike 30% off section adds a second reason to engage, and the price angle on a well-known brand has universal pull.

## What's weak

- That preview text. In my inbox, all I saw was a raw URL — a long, ugly tracking link. That looks like spam or a broken email before I ever opened it. That alone might have stopped me cold. The Nike banner below the fold shows "SHOP WOMEN" and "SHOP KIDS" but as best I can see, there's no "SHOP MEN" equivalent for the Nike deal — which means the one brand that might have pulled me in is pointed at someone else. The rewards "join" ask feels cold; I already have a loyalty program with Skechers, and nothing here tells me why Famous Footwear's program is worth my time. No comfort-tech language, no slip-on callout, nothing that says "hey, older guy with wide feet who doesn't want to fiddle with laces — we've got something for you."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** A raw tracking URL as preview text looks like phishing. That's an embarrassing miss that hurts open rates before the email even has a chance.
- 2. **Add a men's CTA to the Nike section** — if the deal is sitewide, show me a path into it. I shouldn't have to figure out where the men's Nike shoes are.
- 3. **Lead with one comfort-focused product for men** — even one hero shoe in the product grid that signals "easy on, supportive sole, no break-in" would speak to guys like me who are shopping for relief, not fashion.
- 4. **Give me a reason to leave Skechers Plus.** If you want me to join your rewards program, show me what the comp looks like — points per dollar, member exclusives, something. Don't just wave 20% off and hope I bite.
- 5. **Tone the subject down one notch.** ALL CAPS "LAST DAY" plus an emoji reads a little breathless. A calmer "Last day: 20% off when you join Rewards" lands more credibly with my generation.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HIGH — Mixed content (HTTP image sources):**
Two LiveContent dynamic images load over HTTP, not HTTPS:
- `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NjV9...` (image `920205575250`)
- `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4NjV9...` (image `920155575454`)

Modern email clients that enforce HTTPS (Apple Mail, Outlook.com, Gmail on Chrome) will block these silently, breaking whatever dynamic/live content these serve.

**HIGH — Systemic missing alt text:**
30 of ~32 images have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Confirmed by QA. This includes:
- Logo (`3b6a7dc1...png`) — no alt
- All hero/product images (`ac216ca0`, `52828420`, `ad0cb890`, etc.)
- All 8 iGoDigital recommendation images (`recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/...`)
- Footer/social icons (`850f4f2d`, `252df0eb`)

Images-off rendering (default in many B2B clients, Outlook Desktop) will produce a blank email with no fallback text.

**LOW — `<meta charset>` tag is malformed:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is missing. The correct form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without it, the charset declaration has no effect; most clients will infer UTF-8 correctly but this is technically non-conformant.

**LOW — `user-scalable=0` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disabling user zoom is an accessibility anti-pattern (WCAG 1.4.4). Some mobile clients ignore this, but it should be removed.
