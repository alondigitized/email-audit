# Shop the New Mexico Third Jersey
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Oh, come on. I opened the adidas app two weeks ago looking at their Ultraboost — browsing sale shoes like I actually do — and THIS is what they sent me. A Mexico soccer jersey. Again. I've been keeping a mental tally on these FIFA emails since the summer started, and this one is pure continuation of that run. There is not a single shoe in this email. Not a sandal. Not a slipper. Nothing that has anything to do with why I'm on this mailing list.
- The photo is dark and atmospheric, the copy is all "pride" and "heritage" and "symbol" — I understand what they're going for, but I'm a 62-year-old guy from the Midwest who needs comfortable shoes for walking the golf course. This is not for me. At all.
- I'm marking this as read and moving on. I would not click. I would not forward it to anybody I know. The only reason I haven't unsubscribed from adidas yet is that one good "save $30 on orders $100+" email back on June 19th reminded me they CAN send something useful. But my patience for this FIFA stretch is running thin.

## What's working

- The sender name is clearly adidas — I know who sent it, no confusion there.
- Layout renders cleanly, no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing weird going on visually.
- It's a real product with a real name — they're not hiding what they're selling, I'll give them that.

## What's weak

- This is a soccer jersey email sent to a man who shops adidas for walking shoes and comfort sneakers — completely wrong product for my segment.
- There is no price, no discount, no offer of any kind. Nothing to do the math on.
- "Shop online or at your local adidas store" is not a call to action — it's a shrug. No button, no urgency, nothing to click on.
- The hero copy ("pride rooted in heritage, projected to the world") is brand manifesto language, not shopping language. It tells me nothing about what I'd be buying or why today.
- This is the third or fourth FIFA-adjacent email in two weeks. After the June 16th adiClub FIFA email and the World Cup content before it, I'm in FIFA fatigue territory with this brand right now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this to comfort/footwear buyers.** The single biggest fix isn't even in the email — it's the send list. If I've never clicked on a jersey, don't send me jersey emails. Use my browse and click history.
- 2. **Add any offer.** Even "Members save 15% on jerseys" gives me a reason to stop and read. Right now there is no purchase trigger whatsoever.
- 3. **Replace the soft text CTA with a real button.** "Shop Now" in a high-contrast button above the fold would at least give me a clear action if I happened to be interested.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Mexico Third Jersey — Members Save 15% Today Only`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `New: Mexico Third Jersey + free shipping on orders $75+`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Heritage style meets World Cup energy — shop before it sells out`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited run — Mexico Third Jersey now in stock`

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken destination link and a missing plain-text fallback are confirmed hard failures. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image assets and missing alt attributes are confirmed warnings that degrade rendering in strict clients and accessibility.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken destination link — unknown URL scheme**

The QA probe detected an unresolvable internal redirect:

```
unknown url type: '/g/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6?txn=34361580-706a-11f1-80a6-aea38baf8e0c'
```

The click-redirect chain resolves to:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6?cp_tp=v3...
```

The `/o/` → `/g/` internal redirect returns an invalid relative-path URL. Recipients clicking this link land on a broken page. This is the single highest-severity issue.

**[WARN] 28 tracking links not probed**

All 28 click-redirect links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` / `dv.adidas.com` — correct for ESP tracking, but the broken link above confirms at least one destination is misconfigured behind the redirect layer.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 6× HTTP image src — same asset repeated**

The arrow icon loads over HTTP, not HTTPS, in six places:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Clients with mixed-content blocking (Gmail, Outlook 2019+, Apple Mail with strict settings) will suppress this image silently. Fix: upgrade to `https://`.

**[WARN] 1× HTTP open-pixel / tracking image**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ee7ee317c-.../VXPyb5DLp86q1U3mz...
```
Same mixed-content risk as above; also affects open-tracking accuracy if blocked.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text**

- `10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6` (dynamic content image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `VXPyb5DLp86q1U3mzMLtEOQi-...` (tracking pixel — `alt=""` is acceptable here; a non-empty alt on a pixel is incorrect, so this warning may be a false positive)

The first (content image) should have descriptive alt text for image-off and screen-reader rendering.

**`@font-face` blocks are duplicated**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` declarations appear in two separate `<style>` blocks with identical rules. No functional breakage, but it adds ~1 KB of unnecessary CSS payload.

**`<meta content="...">` on `Content-Type` is malformed**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Browsers tolerate this, but it's technically invalid for HTML 4.01 Transitional (the declared DOCTYPE).

CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its absence triggers spam filters in SpamAssassin-based pipelines and is a hard fail for ISPs that score multipart/alternative absence. ### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
