# Move fast for Mother’s Day
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-05**
## Executive summary

- **From:** HOKA® | **Subject:** Move fast for Mother's Day
- Look, I'll be straight. This landed in my inbox about five days before Mother's Day and I can see exactly what they're doing — buy matching running shoes for Mom and kid before the May 6 cutoff and HOKA throws in free expedited shipping on orders over $200. Fine enough concept. Problem is, I'm a Skechers guy. I don't own a single pair of HOKAs. Every product I can see in this email is pointed squarely at women — feminine colorways, a female model in athletic wear, matching "mom and kid" shoe sets. There's not a single men's item, not a comfort technology callout, not a price break that makes this feel accessible. The only thing that even halfway catches my attention is that gift card section at the bottom, and that's a backup option, not a reason to open the email in the first place. Technically the email is clean and readable. Strategically, it's shot at the wrong deer.

## What's working

- The timing is sharp. Emailing on May 5 with a May 6 order cutoff creates real urgency without feeling manufactured. "Move fast" in the subject connects cleanly to the free shipping deadline — I get it immediately. The email itself is easy to read on my phone. Text is a decent size, buttons are wide enough to tap without squinting, and the layout doesn't ask me to zoom in or pinch around. The gift card module at the bottom is the smartest piece of the whole email — it's the only thing that's genuinely low-friction for a guy like me who doesn't know his wife's shoe size in whatever HOKA sizing system they use.

## What's weak

- **Everything is for women.** Every shoe I see is in a light blue or lavender colorway. The one model I can see is a woman in athletic wear. The headline is "Match with Mom." There's no men's product, no mention of comfort technology, no wide-width options, nothing about fit or feel that I'd care about. As a price-conscious shopper, the $200 minimum for free shipping also stings — that's a significant commitment to a brand I've never bought from. And HOKA doesn't mean anything to me the way Skechers does. I have no loyalty context, no prior purchase history, no reason to trust their sizing or comfort claims. I'm coming in cold.
- The "Top Gifts" grid shows four items but they're small on mobile — I can barely tell what two of them are. One looks like a dark jacket or bag. Another might be sandals. Without being able to read labels or prices at a glance, I'm not going to figure it out. That module does more to confuse than convert.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a men's gifting angle.** Even a secondary block — "Get Dad ready for summer too" or a men's shoe callout — would give me a reason to keep scrolling instead of backing out.
- 2. **Show a specific product with a price.** "Free shipping on $200+" means nothing until I know what $200 buys me. One hero shoe with a price tag changes the math immediately.
- 3. **Lower the gift card friction.** Put the gift card option higher in the email, not at the bottom. For a guy buying a gift for someone in a brand he doesn't know, that's the safe bet. Lead with it or at least surface it in the first scroll.
- 4. **Clarify the "Top Gifts" grid.** Add product names and prices under each image. Four mystery thumbnails don't help me decide.
- 5. **Acknowledge the audience.** If this email hit my inbox, HOKA knows something about me. Use it. Even a "Treating someone special this Mother's Day?" feels more targeted than a generic hero that assumes I'm a mom buying for myself.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed defects:**

- **Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tags** (lines 5–6). Two viewport declarations with conflicting `user-scalable` values (`maximum-scale=1` implies no scaling; second tag adds `user-scalable=yes`). Some clients parse only the first; behavior is undefined. Remove the second.

- **Missing `http-equiv` on charset `<meta>`** — `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, this declaration is non-functional as a content-type hint in older Outlook versions.

- **Broken CSS values in `@media (max-width:480px)` block:**
  ```css
  .nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
  .nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
  ```
  Both rules contain empty numeric values (`px` with no number, `solid` with no color). These declarations are invalid and will be silently dropped by all parsers. If these spacer/border classes are actually used in the mobile layout, the mobile rendering will be broken on affected elements.

- **`font-display: swap` in `@font-face`** — Not supported in any email client. The property is harmlessly ignored, but it adds unnecessary bytes and signals that the CSS was ported from a web stylesheet without email-specific review.

- **`@font-face` custom fonts** — `TheFutureHOKA` and `TheFutureHOKAMono` will not render in Gmail (any client), Outlook desktop, or Samsung Mail. The source does not show a fallback `font-family` stack in the body/table styles (truncation prevents full verification). Confirm that inline `font-family` declarations include a web-safe fallback (e.g., `"TheFutureHOKA", Arial, sans-serif`).

- **Global `* { border: none }` reset** — Aggressive; removes borders from all elements including any that may rely on border for visual structure (e.g., `<hr>`, button borders). Low risk for table-based email but can cause unexpected rendering in hybrid layouts.
