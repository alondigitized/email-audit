**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one's different, I'll give it that — no soccer jerseys, no teenagers on stone steps for once. But it's also about the emptiest email I've gotten from adidas yet. There's a marbled gray background with "ADIDAS COLLABORATIONS ARCHIVE" stamped on it, "up to 30% off," and then... nothing. No shoe. Not one photo of a sneaker anywhere in this whole thing.
- "Collaborations Archive" — I don't even know what that means. Archive of what? Old designer team-ups I've never heard of? At my age I want to know: is it a walking shoe, does it have arch support, what's the price. This tells me none of that. It's all mood, no product.
- Not clicking this one, not forwarding it. Straight to "read and forgot."

- The 30% off is stated plainly at the very top, so at least I know there's a real discount before I even open it.
- Clean layout, nothing broken or overlapping — easy enough to read on my phone.
- One clear "SHOP NOW" button up top, easy to tap.

## What's weak

- Zero product photography — no shoe, no model, nothing to actually look at and decide "is this for me."
- "Collaborations Archive" is jargon I don't understand — sounds like a sneakerhead thing, not a comfort-shoe thing.
- No mention of men's, no mention of walking or casual styles — could be anything, could be nothing I'd wear.
- Huge stretch of blank white space between the description and the footer — feels unfinished, like half the email didn't load.
- No price, no specific product, nothing that tells me if this even applies to something I'd wear to walk the dog.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual shoe in this email. A photo of a comfortable men's sneaker with the collab detail called out would do more than any amount of marble-background mood art.
- 2. Cut "Collaborations Archive" or explain it in plain English — "limited-edition styles you won't see again" — because right now it means nothing to a guy my age.
- 3. Add a specific callout for men's casual or walking styles so I know this isn't just kids' colorways or soccer gear in disguise.
- 4. Fill that dead white space with a second product row or at least a "shop men's" link.
