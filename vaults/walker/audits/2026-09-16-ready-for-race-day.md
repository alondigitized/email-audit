# Ready for race day?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Well, "Marathon Pack" — right off the bat I know this one ain't for me. I haven't run a marathon since Jimmy Carter was president, and that blurry action shot of some fella's legs mid-stride doesn't do a thing for me. Then there's the Ora Recovery Slide on that eye-searing yellow-green background — now THAT catches my eye, that's the kind of thing I'd actually put on my feet after mowing the lawn.
- The trouble is the whole email is built around racing and training, with the one product I'd actually consider buried two-thirds of the way down. I'd scroll right past the marathon stuff, pause on the slide, then hit that Strava challenge nonsense and lose interest entirely — running 26.2 miles "at my own pace" is still 26.2 miles more than I'm running.
- I'd skim it, maybe click the slide if I was already thinking about new house shoes, but I wouldn't forward this to anybody. Nobody in my circle is training for a marathon.

## What's working

- The Ora Recovery Slide section is genuinely appealing to me — "foot-cradling comfort," recovery, downtime — that's my language.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — one thing per section, easy to read on my phone without squinting.
- The four-shoe grid is laid out plain and simple, easy to tap.
- Type is big and bold, no tiny print I need my readers for (except that fine print wall at the bottom, obviously).

## What's weak

- The whole top half is marathon-and-race-day gear, which has nothing to do with how I shop — I'm not chasing personal records.
- That blurry runner photo is more artsy than useful — I can't even tell what the shoe looks like.
- The neon Strava "You + 26.2" challenge feels like it's aimed at my grandkids, not me.
- No mention of arch support, cushioning for all-day wear, or slip-on ease anywhere except buried in the slide copy.
- Nothing here acknowledges I might be a repeat customer — no loyalty points, no "welcome back."

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Recovery Slide, not the Marathon Pack — that's the actual crossover product for a comfort-first shopper like me, and it's the strongest asset in the whole email.
- 2. Add a plain, well-lit product shot of at least one shoe up top — I want to see the shoe, not an artistic blur.
- 3. Cut or shrink the Strava marathon challenge section, or at least segment it out to actual race customers.
- 4. Call out comfort features in plain words — "all-day cushioning," "easy on-and-off" — for those of us not racing.
- 5. Give returning customers something — a loyalty nod, a small discount — since this reads like a cold blast to everyone on the list.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Recover in the Ora Slide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Foot-cradling cushion for every day, not just race day`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Comfort that doesn't quit when the run does`
