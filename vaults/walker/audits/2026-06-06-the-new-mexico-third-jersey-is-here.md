# The New Mexico Third Jersey is Here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- I opened this and immediately thought, "Not again." Mexico Third Jersey. adidas is really leaning hard into this whole 2026 World Cup thing, and I get it — it's a big deal for them — but I am a sixty-two year old guy from the suburbs who buys shoes for his bad arches and the occasional walk around the neighborhood. I don't own a soccer jersey. I've never wanted to own a soccer jersey. The email is clean enough, and the big bold headline tells me exactly what I'm looking at, which I appreciate — but "exactly what I'm looking at" is something I have absolutely no use for.
- The body copy goes poetic on me: "It's the ultimate expression of pride, rooted in heritage and projected to the world." That's nice writing if you care about Mexican soccer identity, but I'm not the guy. There's a dark, moody hero image below the text that I could barely make out — looked like people in jerseys in a crowd setting, lots of shadow. No price. No offer. Just "shop online or at your local adidas store." That's it.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it — unless I happened to know someone who's into El Tri, in which case I'd forward it as a "hey, thought of you." But for me personally? Zero pull.

## What's working

- **adidas is a name I trust** — I know the brand, I'm apparently subscribed, so the sender alone clears the first hurdle.
- **The headline is big, bold, and immediate** — "MEXICO THIRD JERSEY" in all caps tells me exactly what this is in half a second. I didn't have to hunt.
- **No spam-feeling tricks** — No all-caps subject line, no fake urgency countdown, no "LIMITED TIME ONLY!!!!" shrieking. It's straightforward about what it's selling.
- **Layout is clean and uncluttered** — One column, big text, easy to read on a phone without zooming. That part I genuinely appreciate at my age.

## What's weak

- **Nothing here is for me** — Soccer jersey, zero cushioning mention, zero footwear, zero comfort technology. Completely outside my shopping lane.
- **No price, no deal, no incentive** — The only "CTA" is plain-text "Shop online or at your local adidas store." No button, no percentage off, nothing that makes me feel like there's a reason to act today.
- **The hero image is too dark** — I could barely make out what was in that photo. Moody and artistic is fine for a fashion brand, but I'm a practical shopper. Show me the thing you're selling, clearly lit.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — This is somewhere around my seventh or eighth adidas email in the past week or two. Soccer jerseys, running shoes I'll never buy, Arsenal winning some league. I'm starting to feel like adidas doesn't know I exist as an individual.
- **Poetic body copy misses the mark** — "Pride becomes a symbol" is beautiful language for someone emotionally invested in this jersey. For me it just sounds like noise before the "shop" link.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment before you send.** If adidas can see I've never clicked on a jersey, a soccer shoe, or anything sports-performance related, this email should never reach my inbox. A shoe recommendation email — even a generic "comfort walking shoes, now on sale" — would get my attention every single time.
- 2. **Add a price or an offer.** "The jersey is here" with no number attached is a press release, not a marketing email. Even a "starting at $X" or "members save 20%" would give me a reason to click.
- 3. **Light up the hero image.** If the product is beautiful, show it in actual light. The dark moody shot loses the design details completely on a small phone screen.
- 4. **Plain-text CTA is not enough.** Give me a button. Something with a color behind it that says "Shop the Mexico Jersey" — not buried text at the bottom of a paragraph.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Iconic design, World Cup energy — shop now before it sells out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Heritage on your back. Free shipping on orders over $50.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "New Mexico Third Jersey" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken redirect destination URL and a completely absent plain-text fallback. Six instances of the same HTTP image asset are additional deliverability risks. The rest of the send infrastructure (DKIM envelope, subscriber tokens) appears intact.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Malformed redirect destination**
The QA prober flagged `unknown url type: '/g/8b63a7a5-337b-4859-82f7-3a8b9184d0e8'`. The outer click-wrap URL is valid:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/8b63a7a5-337b-4859-82f7-3a8b9184d0e8?cp_tp=v3...
```

But the resolved inner destination is a bare path (`/g/<uuid>`) rather than an absolute URL. The dynamic content platform (Movable Ink or equivalent) did not substitute a full destination URL before send — clicking this link will land on a broken page or error.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links unverifiable**
All click-redirect domains were skipped by the prober. No broken links were confirmed among them, but they cannot be cleared.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] Six HTTP image requests — same asset**
The arrow icon is loaded over HTTP six times:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP images by default. The arrow will render as a broken image in most environments. All six `<img src>` references need to be changed to `https://`.

**[FAIL] Open-tracking pixel over HTTP**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e9b886cce-...
```

Same mixed-content problem — this pixel will be blocked, suppressing open-tracking data for a significant share of recipients.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- Hero/dynamic image served via `https://dv.adidas.com/o/8b63a7a5-...`
- Open-tracking pixel at `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`

Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled. The pixel intentionally has no visual content, but the hero image should carry descriptive alt text.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are each declared twice across two separate `<style>` blocks with inconsistent fallback stacks (`Calibri` / `Sans Serif` in block 1; `Arial` / `Helvetica` only in block 2). Redundant declarations add parse weight and the fallback inconsistency could surface in clients that process only one block.

**[INFO] Malformed charset meta tag**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Some older clients may not honor the charset declaration, risking character-encoding errors in non-ASCII content.

---

- `mi_cid=6739ca39099b72c7` (contact ID)

No visibly unfilled `{{token}}` or `[FNAME]`-style placeholders detected in the truncated source. Full source review required to clear this category.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative is 0 characters**
QA confirmed `Text version is 0 chars`. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly mandate a plain-text part, but its absence:
- Raises spam-filter scores on Postfix/SpamAssassin and Gmail's classifier
- Breaks accessibility for text-only clients and screen-reader-only workflows
- Violates best-practice guidance from most ESPs and the M3AAWG sender framework

A minimal plain-text mirror of the HTML body (product name, CTA URL, unsubscribe URL, physical address) must be present.

**[INFO] Unsubscribe and physical address** — cannot be confirmed from truncated source. Full source must include both a one-click (or clearly labeled) unsubscribe link and a physical mailing address to satisfy CAN-SPAM §5.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No standard UTM parameters present** in the resolvable destination URL. All tracking is encoded in proprietary params (`cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`). If the adidas analytics stack reads these params server-side and maps them to GA4 / Adobe, this is acceptable — but it should be verified that the broken `/g/<uuid>` destination (issue 2 above) is not the only UTM-carrying link in the email. If it is, campaign attribution will be dark for all clicks routed through that CTA.

The 29 unprobed tracking links cannot be assessed for UTM completeness.
