# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Full review
Here's my full review as Walker Miles.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers.com is a mixed bag on mobile. The navigation bones are decent — I found my way to Hands Free Slip-ins without getting totally lost, and the product page had the right information once I got there. But two things stopped me cold: the cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen (Step 8), and the search results for "comfort shoes" served me Women's shoes when I'm a logged-in man. Those aren't annoyances — they're broken pipes. I couldn't buy even if I wanted to.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. The navigation micro-improvement in the Men's menu doesn't offset a dead cart page and a misfiring search. No improvement. Possibly a regression on cart (blank screen is worse than a broken cart that at least renders something).

---

## 3. What's Working

**Promo banner is hard to miss.** "SAVE 25% OFF $100+ Code: SAVINGS" sits right at the very top in blue. I noticed it immediately. As someone who watches prices, that caught my eye on first load.

**Men's menu is organized and readable.** The slide-out menu (Step 4) is clean — large tap targets, clear labels, good type size. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" are all listed right there as quick-access shortcuts at the bottom. That's exactly the language I'm looking for. No hunting required.

**Product category page delivers.** 318 results for Hands Free Slip-ins (Step 5) with fit filters (Wide, Extra Wide, Medium) front and center as large blue buttons. Easy to tap. The "Free pickup at Set Location" toggle is a nice touch.

**Product detail page has the essentials.** Arch Fit 3.0 at $115 (Step 6) shows the Hands-Free Slip-ins badge, the star rating, the Members discount reminder. Size grid (Step 7) is generous with well-spaced boxes — my thumbs had no trouble picking a size. "Add to Cart" is a big blue full-width button, exactly right.

**The "Please select a size" error** (Step 7) is visible and properly warns me before I proceed. That's a small win for clarity.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is completely blank (Step 8). This is a critical failure.** After everything — finding the shoe, picking the size, tapping Add to Cart — the cart loads as a pure white screen. Nothing. No item, no price, no checkout button. I'd think the site crashed. I'd close the browser and go to DSW. This is the worst thing I saw today.

**Login doesn't appear to have worked (Step 3).** Steps 1, 2, and 3 all look identical. The cart icon shows "0" all the way through. There's no "Welcome back, Walker" or name in the header. The login step seems to have gone nowhere — the page just shows the same homepage. If I'm not recognized, none of the personalization matters.

**Search for "comfort shoes" returns Women's results (Step 9).** I typed "comfort shoes." I got 1,457 results — fine. But the first two products shown are both labeled "Women's." Contour Foam Cozy Fit, Relaxed Fit Glide-Step — both Women's. I'm a man. If I'm logged in (or even if I'm not), serving me Women's shoes first on a generic search is a trust-breaker. It tells me the site doesn't know me or doesn't care.

**Hero image speaks to athletes, not me (Steps 1-2).** "Skechers Running — Comfort That Performs" with a young basketball player. I'm 62 and I want to walk to the grocery store without my feet hurting. This hero doesn't speak to me at all. Hoka's homepage shows active older adults. New Balance shows real walkers. This feels like it's aimed at someone 30 years younger.

**No pop-up was actually dismissed (Step 2).** The "Dismiss Popups" step just shows the same page with a different rotating banner (Care Bears → Britto). If there was a pop-up, I never saw it get dismissed — or it never appeared in the first place. Either way, the step name doesn't match what I see.

**Only 1 review on the Arch Fit 3.0 (Step 6).** At $115, I want to know what other people think. One review gives me almost nothing to go on. Hoka and Brooks pages show hundreds of reviews. This makes me hesitant.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** A blank cart is a zero-revenue page. This is P0. Every visitor who gets this bounces and doesn't come back.

2. **Fix login persistence.** After logging in, the header should show my name or at minimum a filled-in account icon. The cart should reflect any saved items. Right now login feels like it silently fails.

3. **Filter search results by logged-in gender profile.** If I'm a male account holder, "comfort shoes" should default to Men's results, or at minimum show a mix — not open with two Women's shoes.

4. **Swap the hero or add a second hero for 50+ comfort shoppers.** "Comfort That Performs" for a basketball player is fine for one segment. But a walker on a boardwalk, or someone heading to the farmers market, would make me feel seen.

5. **Surface reviews aggressively.** A $115 shoe with 1 review looks abandoned. Pull in reviews from other colorways of the same model, or prompt recent buyers to leave feedback.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers knows what I want — Hands Free Slip-ins, Arch Fit, Wide Fit shortcuts are all right there in the nav. The product page has the right information. But the execution breaks down at the moment of truth: the cart is blank, login appears broken, and search doesn't know I'm a man. I found the right shoe and then hit a wall. That's not a UX problem, that's a plumbing problem, and until the cart works I'm not buying anything here. Score holds at **4/10** — same as yesterday, no improvement.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Eight-step mobile journey on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). Core TTFB is healthy for most pages but two pages spike sharply. The most severe technical failures are: (1) Kasada bot-protection rate-limiting the cart endpoint itself, and (2) the View Cart page rendering without a `<title>` or `lang` attribute, indicating a broken or non-standard page template. LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, leaving render performance unquantified.

---

## Accessibility

**17 violations total. Two are critical, two are serious.**

| Severity | Rule | Page | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` | Homepage | ARIA role used outside its required parent context — AT will misrepresent or skip the element |
| Critical | `button-name` | Homepage | One or more `<button>` elements have no accessible name — screen readers announce nothing |
| Serious | `document-title` | View Cart | `<title>` element is absent or empty — the page is invisible to AT page-list navigation and browser tab |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` | View Cart | `<html>` lacks `lang` attribute — AT cannot select the correct pronunciation engine |
| Moderate | `heading-order` | Homepage | Heading hierarchy is non-sequential |
| Moderate | `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-one-main` | Homepage, View Cart | Multiple conflicting `<main>` landmark issues across two pages |
| Moderate | `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, `region` | Homepage, Search | `<aside>` and other landmarks not at the correct nesting level; content outside landmark regions |

The two serious View Cart failures (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`) are especially notable: they suggest the cart is being rendered from a shell template that is missing standard `<head>` content, possibly a lightweight AJAX-hydrated view or an iframe overlay that bypasses the main Demandware template.

The duplicate `<main>` landmark and non-sequential headings on the Homepage reduce semantic clarity for search crawlers. No `hreflang` or structured data issues are visible from the provided source fragments.

The 386ms TTFB on Log In and Men category is the main server-side outlier. Both steps likely involve session initialization or heavier Demandware controller processing. This is the single largest latency issue on the path.

**LCP and CLS were not captured for any step.** Without these, the render performance half of Core Web Vitals is unquantified. This is a gap in the audit data, not a site absence.

**Third-party script density is high.** Across the three sampled pages, 20+ distinct third-party domains are loading scripts: Google (GTM, GA4, DV360, SA360), TikTok, Pinterest, Bing, Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Taboola, NextDoor, Monetate (×2 entry points), Bloomreach, Quantum Metric, Impact, Tune/Go2SDK, Stylitics, Pulse Insights, TrustArc, WebFontLoader. Many use `async` but they still consume network bandwidth and main-thread time at parse/execute.

**Duplicate script loads detected:**

- `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` — loaded twice on both Homepage and Log In

These are almost certainly GTM tag misconfiguration artifacts.

## Mobile Optimization

**Viewport height computed in JS:** `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` on `<html>` indicates a JavaScript workaround for mobile browser chrome (Safari's dynamic toolbar). This pattern is functional but means layout depending on `--vh` requires JS before it stabilizes — a potential CLS source that can't be confirmed without CLS data.

**WebFontLoader** (`webfontloader.js` from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`) manages the Mulish font family with 6 weight/style variants. The `wf-active` class fires after all fonts are ready, which can trigger a FOUT-style repaint on mobile if the CSS is keyed on that class. Loading the font file via `@font-face` with `font-display: swap` would be more mobile-appropriate.

**No `viewport` meta tag visible** in the truncated source samples, though this may be present further in the `<head>`. Needs verification.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada bot-protection rate-limiting (6× 429):**

The endpoint `https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522` is the Kasada SDK fingerprinting call. Five of the six 429s hit this path. In a real-browser session these would not 429; they are failing here because the test browser lacks a valid Kasada challenge token. This is an audit environment artifact, not a production user issue — but it confirms the site is Kasada-protected and automated testing will require Kasada bypass to get clean network traces.

**Cart 429 (`https://www.skechers.com/cart/`) is more serious.** If this 429 occurred during the actual View Cart step (not just a background retry), it means Kasada blocked the cart page load entirely for this session. This would directly cause the View Cart HTML anomalies (missing `<title>`, missing `lang`) if the response was a Kasada challenge page rather than the real cart template.

**Stylitics CORS block:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' 
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics "Complete the Look" widget stylesheet is failing to load. This breaks the widget's visual styling. Root cause is on Stylitics' CDN — their stylesheet is not being served with appropriate CORS headers for cross-origin `<link>` requests. The fix requires Stylitics to configure `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on their CDN, or to load the stylesheet differently (e.g., via a same-origin proxy or injected `<style>` block).

## Recommendations

**P0 — Confirm cart 429 is not user-facing.** Verify whether real mobile sessions ever receive a 429 on `/cart/`. If Kasada is misfiring on legitimate users (e.g., after a session timeout or on first load), the View Cart HTML anomalies and cart 429 are symptoms of a production availability issue, not just an audit artifact.

**P1 — Fix View Cart template.** Whether caused by Kasada or not, the View Cart page must always emit `<html lang="en">` and a non-empty `<title>`. If the cart has a lightweight/AJAX render path, that path needs the same `<head>` scaffolding as the full template.

**P1 — Fix the two critical ARIA violations on Homepage.** `aria-required-parent` and `button-name` are critical-severity failures affecting screen reader users on the highest-traffic page.

**P2 — Escalate Stylitics CORS to vendor.** File a ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` to their stylesheet CDN response. Workaround: proxy the CSS through Skechers' own CDN edge.

**P2 — Audit GTM for duplicate tags.** `creativecdn` and `ytc.js` firing twice per page indicates a GTM container misconfiguration. Each duplicate fires the associated analytics/ad event twice, corrupting conversion data.

**P3 — Capture LCP and CLS.** Rerun the audit with Lighthouse or a real-browser WebVitals observer to get the render half of Core Web Vitals. TTFB alone is insufficient for a complete performance picture.

**P3 — Investigate the 386ms TTFB on Log In and Men category.** Profile the Demandware controller response time for those two routes specifically. Session initialization or controller-level A/B test resolution (Monetate) are the most likely causes given the pattern.
