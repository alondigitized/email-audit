# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Right out of the gate, Skechers is leading with their Hands Free Slip-ins front and center — that product name alone tells me they know what a certain kind of shopper needs. There's a BOGO 50% off deal in the banner at the very top, which slows my scroll. The brand looks focused and promotional right now, pushing a specific product category rather than scattering my attention.

## What's working

- **"HANDS FREE Slip-ins GLIDE-STEP" hero headline** — those two words, "Hands Free," are doing real work. I don't need to be sold on the concept when bending down to wrestle with a lace is genuinely inconvenient. They led with the right product.
- **BOGO 50% OFF banner at the top** — hard to miss, clearly stated. A deal this visible on a shoe I'm already interested in makes me want to read further.
- **"Men" tab directly below the hero** — one tap and I'm looking at men's shoes. That's sensible mobile design; I don't want to fumble through nested menus just to find my section.
- **"Recently Viewed" tab** — if I was here two days ago and didn't pull the trigger, this tells me I can pick back up without starting from scratch. That matters to someone like me who browses a few times before buying.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no pop-ups, nothing jumping around, text is large enough to read without squinting. I'll stay on a page that behaves itself.

## What's weak

- **Young models** — The man in the hero looks to be maybe 30. The woman is even younger. I know the Slip-ins are popular with all ages, but if I saw someone who actually looked like they'd been on their feet for six decades, I'd feel a lot more like this shoe was made with me in mind.
- **"SHOP NOW" is too vague** — I don't know if tapping that lands me on women's Slip-ins, men's, or a general campaign page. "Shop Men's Slip-ins" would tell me where I'm going.
- **No free shipping mention** — Shipping cost is one of the details that decides whether I actually finish a purchase. It's not visible anywhere above the fold, which means I have to go hunting for it later.
- **No loyalty callout** — I've bought from Skechers before. I probably have points sitting somewhere. Not seeing any mention of my rewards or member pricing above the fold is a missed pull for a returning customer.
- **25% OFF Apparel banner** — I came here for shoes. Apparel is not why I opened this page, and this banner takes up premium real estate that could be pointing me toward a footwear deal.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the apparel promo for a footwear-specific offer** — something like "BOGO 50% OFF All Men's Slip-ins & Comfort Styles" keeps my eyes in the right product lane.
- **Change "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Men's Slip-ins"** — give me a destination, not just a nudge. Specificity at the CTA level costs nothing and converts better.
- **Add "Free shipping on orders over $X" near the CTA button** — one line of text right there does real conversion work. I shouldn't have to scroll to the footer to find out what shipping costs me.
- **Include one model who is visibly 55 or older** — doesn't need to own the page. One guy in the hero who looks like he's retired and active tells me the shoe was designed for my life, not just borrowed from the twenty-something running ads.
