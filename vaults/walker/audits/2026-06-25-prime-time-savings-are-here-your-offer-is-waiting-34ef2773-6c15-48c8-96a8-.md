# Prime Time Savings Are Here ☀️ Your Offer is waiting
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Here we go — Skechers Plus in my inbox, and before I've even tapped through, that same wall of raw JSON code is sitting in the preview where the actual pitch should be. By my count, every single Skechers email I've gotten this month has the same broken preview. I've stopped expecting it to be fixed.
- Once I get past that and actually look at the email, there's real money on the table: Buy One, Get One 50% off for Skechers+ members. That's a legitimate deal, and "Sun's Out, Deals Out" is clean summer energy — I get it immediately. The layout holds together on my phone, the offer is in the hero, and there's a SHOP MEN'S section I can see without scrolling.
- What bothers me is that Skechers forgot why I'm here. Nobody mentioned cushioning, arch support, Memory Foam, Slip-Ins — none of it. This reads like a sale email from any shoe company, not the brand I trust specifically because of how their walking shoes feel on my feet. I'd probably tap through because the BOGO deal is worth chasing, but I wouldn't forward this to a friend. Not until somebody at Skechers fixes that preview.

## What's working

- The Skechers+ BOGO 50% offer is in the hero, front and center — no hunting, no squinting, I saw it immediately
- The two-tier offer (members get BOGO vs. 20% off for everyone else) makes my loyalty card feel like it actually does something
- "Sun's Out Deals Out" lands clean for a summer sale — no overthinking required
- Layout is solid on mobile — no broken images, no jumbled text, everything lines up the way it should
- SHOP MEN'S is visible without scrolling, so I know my section is in here

## What's weak

- Zero comfort language anywhere in this email — no cushioning, no arch support, no Slip-Ins, no Memory Foam — for a brand I chose specifically for comfort, that's a miss
- The hero model is a young woman in trendy casual clothes, not someone who looks like me at 62 — the men's content is secondary placement
- The preview text is broken JSON schema garbage again — this is now at least the eleventh straight Skechers email this month with this same defect, and at this point it reads like nobody's watching the store
- The apparel section is filler for me — I'm not coming to Skechers for polo shirts
- "Prime Time Savings" in the subject doesn't tell me what the actual deal is until I open it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put comfort language in the hero.** "Air-Cooled Memory Foam" or a Slip-Ins callout costs nothing and reminds me exactly why I'm choosing Skechers over New Balance this week.
- 2. **Feature a men's hero or a product shot.** One specific shoe, named, with a comfort callout — not just "SHOP MEN'S" pointing at a general landing page.
- 3. **Fix the preview text.** This is broken in every single email I've gotten this month. Someone needs to look at this today, not next send.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "this weekend only" makes the BOGO feel urgent. "Your Offer is waiting" doesn't.
- 5. **Trim and sharpen the subject.** Drop "Prime Time" — it's generic radio-ad language. Lead with the actual deal.

### 1. Technical Summary

All links resolve and the email renders across breakpoints, but the entire asset infrastructure uses plain HTTP — a systemic issue that will cause image blocking in any modern mail client with mixed-content enforcement. One third-party pixel also loads over HTTP with no alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-tracking links** route through `click.emails.skechers.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). No broken destinations detected, but all were skipped in HTTP probing — destination URL health is unverified.

**Third-party pixels present:**
- `beacon.krxd.net` — Salesforce DMP user-match pixel (2 calls), ad impression pixel (1 call)
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net` — inbox deliverability monitoring
- `ink1000.com` — secondary open-tracking pixel

The `ink1000.com` pixel loads over **HTTP** (see §3), meaning it fires an unencrypted request that also fails to set alt text. This is the only open-tracking pixel with a mixed-content risk; the primary `click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx` pixel is implicit in the click domain and not separately flagged.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTTP image sources (13 images):**

Every image hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com` uses `http://`, not `https://`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy/block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected images include:

- Brand logo: `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`
- App Store / Google Play badges
- Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store icons
- Instagram, Facebook, YouTube social icons
- One unlabeled content image (`49468f73-...`)
- Open-tracking pixel: `http://www.ink1000.com/...`

**Alt text missing (2 images):**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel) — low severity (tracking pixel), but triggers accessibility linters
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt; recipients using screen readers or images-off rendering get no fallback text

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two separate `@media` blocks cover `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px` with identical rules — redundant but harmless. A third block at `max-width: 480px` in a second `<style>` tag re-declares `.show` and `.mobile-hidden`, duplicating earlier rules.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — suppresses user zoom on mobile. Minor accessibility concern (WCAG 1.4.4), non-blocking.
