# Deal hunting?🔎Your search is over
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Well, they got me to open it — "deal hunting" is pretty much my Saturday morning hobby and the subject line is punchy enough. But the second I see that preview text in my inbox, I nearly delete it on the spot. It's a raw web address — a long, ugly tracking link — sitting right where the preview copy should be. On my phone that fills the whole preview line. That looks like phishing, not Famous Footwear.
- Inside the email the BOGO hero is familiar — too familiar. I got this same BOGO offer two days ago, on the 16th. I almost wonder if I accidentally opened an old email. When I scroll down I get Birkenstock (okay, you have my attention again — I've been wearing those Arizonas around the house for six summers), a Brooks section, and then a chunk of women's sandals and kids' sandals that has nothing to do with me.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read and move on. They've sent me seven or eight emails this week and at this point the BOGO is like that neighbor who keeps showing up with the same covered dish — eventually you stop answering the door.

## What's working

- **Birkenstock section is relevant to me** — limited-time price drops on the exact style I already own and buy again is the right message; I noticed it immediately when I scrolled.
- **Brooks gets a featured spot** — that brand is on my short list and seeing it in the email body earns a scroll-past, even if the CTA is generic.
- **BOGO is a real, legible offer** — the number is big, the hero is clean, no guessing what the deal is.
- **No layout breakage in the body** — renders clean on my phone, text doesn't overlap, images load.
- **Seasonal relevance is there** — sandals in July, that's timely.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — this is a serious trust problem; it reads like spam before the email is even opened.
- **BOGO is a repeat from July 16th** — I remember that email; showing me the same hero two days later tells me the offer didn't move enough product or the marketing calendar is on autopilot.
- **Women's sandals dominate the bottom half** — a 60% off women's section is great for someone else; for me it's dead weight that makes the email feel like a broadcast to everyone, not me.
- **Kids' sandals section is completely irrelevant** — I don't have young kids at home and this just adds scrolling to find anything I'd actually wear.
- **"SHOP NOW" tells me nothing** — I'm 62, I want to know *what* I'm shopping for before I tap a button.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is the single most urgent change.** The raw URL makes it look like a phishing attempt in the inbox. A real preheader teasing the Birkenstock or Brooks offer would have lifted the open rate immediately.
- 2. **Retire the BOGO creative or give it a refresh angle** — if the BOGO is still running, say "BOGO extended" or show new arrivals in the deal. Repeating the same hero two days later trains subscribers to tune it out.
- 3. **Lead the body with men's comfort** — put a Birkenstock men's sandal or a Brooks walking shoe in the first scroll position, above the generic BOGO block, for a segment like me.
- 4. **Cut the kids' section for non-parent segments** — that real estate is wasted on me and muddies the hierarchy.
- 5. **Make the CTA specific** — "Shop Men's Birkenstocks" or "See Brooks Styles" beats "SHOP NOW" every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO extended + Birkenstock sale — this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Walker, Birkenstock prices just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 30% off slides and Brooks walkers — comfort picks inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited-time Birkenstock drops + BOGO still on. See what fits.`

## Technical Audit — Famous Footwear "Deal Hunting" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-layout HTML mailer with functional responsive breakpoints and no broken infrastructure. Primary issues are a systemic missing-alt-text problem across all content images and a plain-text part that is overwhelmingly URL content, both of which create deliverability and accessibility exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **All 33 CTAs route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`** — standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracking. Redirects were not probed (skipped by QA), so broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed or ruled out.
- **Open pixel present:** `https://click.email.famousfootwear.com/open.aspx?CZW5EN3RD4NE3FKHSIPO3PSQ4I.70249&d=70249&bmt=0` — single-pixel 1×1 GIF, expected for SFMC.
- No raw destination URLs are visible in the truncated source, so UTM parameter verification is deferred to Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — systemic missing alt text:**
QA flagged **26 images with empty or absent `alt` attributes**, including the logo (`3b6a7dc1...png`), the hero GIF (`721856e1...gif`), and all product/promotional images. With images blocked (default in Outlook, Gmail app), the email renders as a blank white column — no content is legible.

- Logo link: `<img src="...3b6a7dc1-6955-49f8-868a-bf62a15122ee.png" alt="" ...>` — alt is present but empty string; correct for decorative-only use, but the logo is a navigational element and should carry `alt="Famous Footwear"`.
- The 2px rule image (`4ccff4ad...jpg`) appears **4 times** in QA output — likely a reused spacer/divider; `alt=""` is appropriate there, but the repetition suggests the template is stamping the same asset multiple times rather than using a CSS border.

**Other rendering notes:**
- `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag disables pinch-zoom. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and fails on iOS 10+ anyway (ignored by Safari).
- `content="text/html; charset=UTF-8"` on the `<meta>` tag is missing the `http-equiv` attribute: should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Without it, the charset declaration is inert in some clients.
- CSS uses `lineapacing` (typo, line 1 of the inline style block in the header `<td>`) — has no effect but indicates a copy-paste error.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens visible in the truncated HTML (no `%%`, `{{`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`-style tokens). Cannot confirm whether suppression list tokens or dynamic content blocks are present deeper in the template. No issues found in the visible source.

### 5. Compliance

- **Unsubscribe link:** Not visible in the truncated source. CAN-SPAM and CASL require a functioning unsubscribe mechanism; cannot confirm presence — needs verification against the full HTML.
- **Physical mailing address:** Not visible in truncated source. CAN-SPAM §5 requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. Needs verification.
- **Preheader present:** `<div class="preheader" style="...display:none !important">Famous has the BEST deals.</div>` — correctly hidden visually, functions as preview text.
- **Sender domain:** `famousfootwear@email.famousfootwear.com` — subdomain `email.famousfootwear.com` is consistent with SFMC ESP sending infrastructure. DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment cannot be verified from HTML alone; should be confirmed against received headers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTAs pass through the SFMC click-redirect (`click.email.famousfootwear.com/?qs=...`), which encodes the destination URL opaquely. The destination URLs and any UTM parameters they carry are not inspectable from the HTML source. QA skipped probing all 33 tracking links.

**Risk:** If the ESP is not appending UTM parameters before the final redirect, attribution will be lost and all sessions will appear as direct traffic in analytics. This requires a live link-probe pass against the decoded destinations to confirm.
