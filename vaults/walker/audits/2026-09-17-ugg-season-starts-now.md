# UGG season starts now
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a nice change of pace — first email all week from these folks that isn't screaming "60% off clearance" at me. UGG season, fine, I get the seasonal logic, but there's a "SHOP MEN'S UGG" button buried in there so at least I'm not invisible this time. Still, let's be honest — a young woman cuddling a stack of boots and sherpa-lined boots piled up like a photo shoot isn't exactly speaking to a 62-year-old guy looking for something he can slip on to walk the dog. UGGs also aren't really my world — I want arch support and cushioning, not fuzzy boots.
- The "boot math: pay for them once + wear them always = basically free" line made me chuckle, I'll give them that, it's a cute hook. But there's no actual price anywhere in this email — no dollar figure, no percent off, just a free tote if I spend $99 and become a VIP. That's not really an offer, that's a loyalty pitch dressed up as one.
- Would I click? Maybe out of curiosity to see if men's UGG has anything with real support, but I'm not holding my breath. Wouldn't forward it to anyone — nothing here is urgent or personal enough. I'd skim it, maybe tap "Shop Men's UGG" out of boredom, then move on.

## What's working

- Actually broke the "60% off clearance" streak I've seen from DSW all week — feels different, not just another discount blast.
- There's a men's UGG button, so I'm at least acknowledged as a shopper, not just an afterthought.
- Clean layout, big bold "HAPPY UGG SEASON" banner is easy to read without squinting.
- Size grid and "shop wide width" buttons are a nice touch since my feet aren't exactly narrow anymore.

## What's weak

- No actual discount or price anywhere — "free tote with $99 purchase + VIP" isn't a deal I care about, I'm not chasing a tote.
- Every model shown is young and female — nothing here says "this is for a guy my age."
- UGGs aren't a comfort-first, arch-support shoe in my mind — this doesn't touch the cushioning/support stuff I actually shop for.
- "Boot math" wordplay is cute but there's no real number backing it up — feels like copy trying to distract from the lack of an offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual price or percent off on men's UGG styles — "boot math" needs a number to back it up, not just wordplay.
- 2. Swap in a photo of a guy trying on the men's styles, or at least show men's product shots up top instead of burying "Shop Men's UGG" as one of four small buttons.
- 3. Tie this to comfort/support language if any of these UGG styles have real arch support or memory foam — that's the hook that gets me to click, not "fuzzy."
- 4. Drop or shrink the VIP tote pitch — it's crowding out the actual product story at the very top of the email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `UGG season: comfort you can slip into`
