## Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date: May 30, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 3/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers made one small navigation improvement today — the Men's menu is clean, well-spaced, and surfaces "Hands Free Slip-ins" and "Comfort Technologies" exactly where I need them. But from there the experience falls apart. Login shows no personalization. The soccer prize giveaway still dominates the homepage. And the cart page — the single most critical step between browsing and buying — is a **completely blank white screen**. I couldn't see my items, let alone check out. That's not a friction point, that's a door with no handle.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: **3/10**

Same score as yesterday. The navigation improvement nudges things up slightly, but the blank cart is a hard regression that cancels any gains. A shopper who can't see their cart doesn't complete a purchase.

## 3. What's Working

**BOGO deal is impossible to miss.** "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" is the very first thing on screen — blue banner, bold text, full width. For someone doing price-vs-quality math in their head, that's the right opener.

**Men's navigation menu is the best screen in the whole journey.** Full-width touch targets, large readable text, no clutter. "Shoes," "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" — all right there. No hunting. This is what the rest of the site should feel like.

**Hands Free Slip-ins category page works.** 289 results, filter buttons for Wide/Extra Wide/Medium Fit at the top, clean sort bar. I can start narrowing immediately without digging through sub-menus.

**Product detail page is legible.** "$88.00" in plain view, deal reminder right below it, product name in large bold text. No zooming required.

**Add to Cart button is big and obvious.** Full-width blue bar pinned to the bottom of the screen. Hard to miss. Size grid tiles are large enough to tap without squinting.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is a blank white screen.** Step 8 rendered nothing — no items, no totals, no proceed-to-checkout. Completely empty. This is a critical failure. If this is what a real customer sees after adding a shoe, the sale is dead. No amount of good browsing experience recovers a broken cart.

**Login shows zero personalization.** After signing in, the homepage looks identical to the logged-out version. No "Welcome back, Walker." No "Based on your last order." No men's content pushed front-and-center. I could be anyone.

**Soccer prize giveaway is wasted real estate.** "Win the Ultimate Soccer Fan Prize Package" is the hero content on the homepage — above the fold, large, taking up more screen space than any shoe. I'm 62, shopping for comfortable walking shoes. This does nothing for me. It's not even subtle — it's the centerpiece.

**Search results show women's shoes to a logged-in male user.** Searching "comfort shoes" returns 1,452 results. The first two products I can see? Both labeled "Women's." If the site knows who I am after login, it shouldn't be surfacing women's styles first on a generic comfort search.

**No reviews on the featured product.** The Contour Foam - Cozy Fit Ohtro shows "No Reviews" right under the name. That's a trust killer for me. I'm cross-shopping Hoka and New Balance — those pages are packed with reviews. Zero reviews on a product makes me wonder if anyone's actually worn it.

**The "Dismiss Popups" step had no popup.** The page between homepage and login shows no modal, no popup, just a banner change ("Get Dad a Gift He'll Love"). Either there was no popup to dismiss or the automation handled it invisibly. Minor, but worth noting — if that step is capturing a real email capture popup, it's a missed opportunity to log the friction.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is priority zero. A blank cart is a conversion killer. Whatever is causing the white screen on `/cart/` needs a same-day fix.

2. **Personalize the post-login homepage.** Even one data point — name in the header, a "For You" rail with men's comfort styles — signals that logging in was worth it.

3. **Swap the homepage hero.** Soccer sweepstakes doesn't speak to the 55+ comfort buyer. Put a Hands Free Slip-ins lifestyle shot up there with a clear comfort message.

4. **Filter search results by the user's gender.** A logged-in male user searching "comfort shoes" should see men's products first by default, not women's.

5. **Surface social proof earlier.** Even two or three reviews on a product page changes the calculation. If Contour Foam is genuinely well-liked, get some reviews attached to it or promote a similar style that has them.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware). The primary systemic issue is Kasada bot protection (`x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.430`) blocking the automated session with repeated 429s, causing a cascade: the cart page returned malformed HTML, which explains several of the "serious" accessibility violations. Separately, a Stylitics third-party CORS misconfiguration breaks a widget integration. Third-party script payload is heavy but loaded async.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): An element with an ARIA role that requires a parent container role is orphaned. Likely a widget or carousel component rendered outside its expected DOM wrapper.

**Serious — both caused by the Kasada 429 on `/cart/`**
- `document-title`: View Cart returned a truncated/error HTML document with no `<title>`. Not a template bug — the page never fully rendered.
- `html-has-lang`: Same root cause. The `<html>` tag on the blocked cart response lacks a `lang` attribute, unlike all other pages which correctly carry `lang="en"`.

**Moderate (structural)**
- Homepage has duplicate `<main>` landmarks (`landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`) and an `<aside>` not at top level. Suggests nested layout components competing for landmark roles — a common SFCC cartridge composition issue.
- `heading-order` violation on Homepage: heading levels skip (e.g., H1 → H3), likely from independently-developed marketing components with hardcoded heading levels.
- `region` violations on Homepage and Search: content exists outside any landmark, meaning screen readers can't navigate to it via landmark shortcuts.

---

## SEO

- The `<title>` missing on View Cart is an SEO issue if Googlebot crawls a rate-limited session and receives the same truncated response. However, the 429 on `/cart/` is consistent with intentional bot-blocking, so Googlebot should receive a proper response with a valid crawl token — this requires verification.
- Protocol-relative URL on a Monetate script (`//se.monetate.net/...`) is an outdated pattern; not a direct SEO issue but signals legacy code.
- All other crawled pages have `lang="en"` and functional title elements in the source.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` CSS custom property set inline on `<html>` — this is a standard JS-computed 1vh fix for mobile browser chrome (664px viewport → 6.64px/unit). Correct approach, though the floating-point precision is cosmetically odd.
- No evidence of `viewport` meta tag in the truncated HTML samples, but absence may be due to truncation. Should be verified against full source.
- Font loading via WebFontLoader is synchronous-ish on mobile networks; native `@font-face` with `preload` would be faster.

---

## Console & Network Errors

**Root cause: Kasada bot protection rate-limiting the automated session**

The path `/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.430` is the Kasada KPSDK fingerprint endpoint. The UUID-style path segments are per-session tokens. Six 429s across the journey indicate Kasada classified the automated browser session as suspicious and began blocking it, eventually including the `/cart/` page itself.

**Consequence:** The cart page 429 returned malformed HTML, producing the View Cart accessibility failures (`document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main`, `page-has-heading-one`) — these are artifacts of the error response, not bugs in the cart template.

**Separate: CORS block on Stylitics**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=hotspots&theme=skechers...'
has been blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
This CSS is being fetched programmatically (via JS `fetch()` or `XMLHttpRequest`) rather than as a `<link>` element — `<link>` tags are not subject to CORS. The Stylitics widget JS is attempting a cross-origin fetch of its own stylesheet, and Stylitics' CDN is not returning the required `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header. This is a Stylitics-side misconfiguration or a version mismatch between the theme bundle and CDN config. The `skechers` theme and version `d3dca01827` should be verified with Stylitics support.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Kasada + audit tooling**: Run future automated audits with a Kasada-exempt session token or against a staging environment that bypasses bot protection. The current data has poisoned View Cart metrics and a11y results.

2. **Cart template resilience**: Verify the actual cart template includes `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>`, and `<h1>` — these should be in the SFCC template, not injected by JS. If they're template-present but missing in audit, it's solely the 429 issue.

3. **ARIA landmark audit**: The duplicate `<main>` and orphaned `<aside>` on Homepage need a DOM inspection to identify which SFCC cartridge is emitting the extra landmark. Fix at the cartridge template level, not with CSS `display:none`.

4. **Stylitics CORS**: File a bug with Stylitics referencing theme `skechers`, version `d3dca01827`, and the missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` response header on `web-assets.stylitics.com`. If the widget JS owns the fetch, it should switch to a `<link>` injection instead.

5. **Deduplicate CreativeCDN script**: Remove the duplicate `<script src="https://tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js">` in the Men category page — likely a GTM tag firing condition bug.

6. **Capture LCP/CLS**: The absence of LCP and CLS data for all 8 steps is the largest gap in this audit. These are the Core Web Vitals that most directly affect search ranking. Re-run with a real-user session (or a non-bot-detected headless session) and capture full Web Vitals.

7. **WebFont modernization**: Replace WebFontLoader + cdnjs with native `@font-face` + `<link rel="preload" as="font">` for the critical Mulish weights. Reduces a third-party CDN dependency and eliminates FOUT.
