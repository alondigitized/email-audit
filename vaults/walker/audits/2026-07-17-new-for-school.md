**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, HOKA pulled a hard left turn on me today. Yesterday they were introducing the Clifton 11 like it was the shoe event of the season — now they're sending me a back-to-school shopping guide. I'm 62. My school days ended before disco did. The hero is a young woman with her feet up on a chair, the main content grid is four children's shoes in sizes that wouldn't fit my big toe, and what they're calling "Top picks for grown ups, too" turns out to be the Women's Clifton 11 and a Women's Bondi 9. I can't wear those.
- The one thing that almost hooks me: the hero copy mentions wide sizes are available. I clock that immediately. Fit and width are the first things I want to know about a shoe. But that detail is buried inside two sentences of "back to class adventures" language that has nothing to do with my life, and then I'm looking at Transport Freedom for Little Kids.
- I would not click this. I would not forward it to a friend. I'd mark it read and keep scrolling. If I had grandkids who needed HOKA shoes maybe I'd come back — but even then I'd just go straight to the website.

- HOKA in the from line is a name I know, and I've been opening their emails for a couple weeks now — that brand trust is doing real work here
- "Wide sizes, too" tucked into the hero copy is the right kind of detail — sizing and fit are exactly what I want to see upfront
- The Clifton 11 name shows up in the adult section, which gives a bit of continuity with yesterday's dedicated launch email
- The layout is clean — nothing is broken, no images are missing, "Shop Back to School" button is obvious and tappable

## What's weak

- The entire primary content block is kids' shoes: Clifton 10 Big Kids, Clifton 10 Little Kids, Transport Freedom Big Kids, Transport Freedom Little Kids — none of that is for me
- The "grown ups" picks are both labeled Women's — Women's Clifton 11, Women's Bondi 9 — no men's product anywhere in the email
- The ID.me student and teacher discount is a complete dead end for me; I'm neither, and I don't know any 62-year-old who is, so that whole block is wasted real estate
- "New for school" as a subject line is basically a "skip" flag for anyone past their forties — it tells me before I even open it that this content probably isn't mine
- Seven HOKA emails in roughly ten days is starting to feel like a lot, even when the content is worth reading

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one men's wide-fit shoe in the "grown ups" section.** The Men's Clifton 11 Wide was literally the subject of yesterday's email — lead the adult block with it instead of two women's styles. I might actually click that.
- 2. **Segment this email.** Send the kids' shoe content to households with school-age children; send a "New Season Comfort" version to people like me. Forcing a 62-year-old to scroll past four children's products to reach anything adult is a quick way to train unsubscribes.
- 3. **Lead the adult section with comfort copy, not just product names.** A caption like "cushioned support for long days on your feet" would get me tapping. A product photo and a label won't.
- 4. **Swap the ID.me block for a loyalty or seasonal offer** for subscribers who aren't students or teachers — something I can actually use.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate `<meta name="viewport">` tag**
Two conflicting viewport declarations exist in `<head>`:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```
Second tag contradicts the first (`maximum-scale=1` vs `user-scalable=yes`). Remove the duplicate; keep only one.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Apple Mail, Samsung) render this as the email title in notification previews. Should contain a meaningful string (e.g., the subject line) or be omitted.

**Broken CSS property values in `@media (max-width: 480px)`**
Two rules have syntactically invalid values — the numeric component before `px` is missing:
```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```
Both rules will be silently dropped by the CSS parser in all clients. If these classes are applied to visible nav elements on mobile, layout will break at ≤480px viewport.

**`<meta http-equiv>` attribute missing**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without it, charset declaration has no effect in the MIME context.

**Web font support**
Five `@font-face` declarations load `TheFutureHOKA` variants from `hoka.com/on/demandware.static/...`. Gmail (webmail + Android app) and Outlook (Windows) do not support `@font-face` in email. Fallback font stack is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a safe stack (`sans-serif`) is defined on all text elements.
