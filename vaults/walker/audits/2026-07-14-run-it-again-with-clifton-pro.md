# Run it again with Clifton PRO
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Third time HOKA has put the Clifton PRO in front of me this week. July 9th was the launch email, July 11th it showed up again inside a color grid. Now here's "Run it again" — and honestly, that tagline lands like a nudge I didn't ask for. The hero is sharp: big neon shoe against a dark background, high-contrast yellow text, very clean layout. I can't argue with the production value.
- But I read three words in the body — "For serious runners" — and I mentally checked out. That is not me. I walk three miles every morning. I cross-shop Clifton because the cushioning reputation is solid, not because I'm stacking training miles. The whole email leans into performance running language: quick transitions, fast feel, edge when the daily miles stack. Nothing in here speaks to a guy who wants his feet to feel good on a long day of errands and a morning walk.
- I'd mark this read, I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it. HOKA had a really good send on July 7th — "Walk Stars," talked about cushioning and support, felt like it knew me. This one forgot I exist.

## What's working

- **Hero is visually strong.** The neon shoe against the dark background stops your thumb. Even on a phone screen it pops.
- **CTA appears twice and it's clear.** "Shop Clifton PRO" — I know exactly where that button goes. No mystery.
- **PROFLY™+ tech callout** is the one section that comes close to speaking my language — "rebound-boosting foam" and "smooth ride" could have been my hook if it wasn't buried below the fold in runner-speak.
- **No render bugs I can see.** Layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap. Solid execution technically.
- **Men's colorways in the product grid.** At least they included a men's section, even if the models and language aren't aimed at me.

## What's weak

- **"For serious runners" is the first line of body copy.** That's an immediate self-filter for me — I'm not a serious runner and HOKA knows it, or should.
- **Third Clifton PRO email in five days.** July 9, July 11 (tucked in a color grid), and now this. I've been patient but cadence fatigue is real.
- **Zero comfort language below the hero.** No arch support, no all-day wear, nothing about walking. The entire frame is speed and transitions.
- **No offer.** No price, no discount, no free shipping. Just "Shop." I'm not impulse-buying a $165 shoe without a reason to act today.
- **"Run it again" sign-off.** That closing image of runners sprinting is the final nail — confirms this whole email wasn't written for someone like me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with comfort, not performance.** "Meet Clifton PRO" could stay, but the subhead needs to replace "For serious runners" with something like "Built for miles that demand more from your feet." I'm not training. I'm living.
- 2. **Surface a price or a reason to act.** No discount, no urgency, no membership perk — there's nothing making me click today vs. next week vs. never.
- 3. **Segment the send.** Walkers and casual comfort buyers shouldn't get the same Clifton PRO email as marathon trainers. HOKA had my number on July 7th. They know who I am.
- 4. **Show the white/neutral colorway prominently.** The grid buried the calmer options under the neon. Men my age aren't reaching for lime green first.
- 5. **Cut one of these Clifton PRO emails.** Three in five days is too many. Either consolidate or change the angle — this one could have been the comfort walker cut, not another runner cut.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Duplicate, conflicting `viewport` meta tags**

```html
<!-- Tag 1 -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
<!-- Tag 2 -->
<meta content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=yes" name="viewport" />
```

Tag 1 caps zoom at 1× (blocks accessibility zoom). Tag 2 re-enables `user-scalable=yes`. The two declarations conflict; browser behavior on duplicate metas is undefined. Remove Tag 1; keep Tag 2 (or consolidate into one tag with `user-scalable=yes`).

**Issue 2 — Malformed CSS values in `@media (max-width: 480px)` block**

```css
.nav_padding { padding-top:px !important; padding-bottom:px !important; }
.nav_border  { border-top:px solid ; }
```

Both rules contain bare `px` with no numeric value and `border-top` is missing its color. These declarations are invalid and will be silently dropped by parsers — any nav spacing or border intended for mobile is absent. Fix: supply the numeric values (e.g., `padding-top: 0px`) or remove the dead rules.

**Issue 3 — Empty `<title>` tag**

```html
<title></title>
```

No content. Some clients (Samsung Mail, Outlook on macOS) surface the `<title>` as a fallback preview label. Not critical, but populate it with a meaningful value (e.g., `"Run it again with Clifton PRO — HOKA"`).

**Issue 4 — Missing `http-equiv` on charset declaration**

```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

The `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute is absent. Without it, some older Outlook/Lotus clients may not honor the charset declaration, which can corrupt special characters. Correct form:

```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```

**Web fonts — expected degradation, not a defect**

`TheFutureHOKA` and `TheFutureHOKAMono` are declared via `@font-face`. Gmail (web/Android), Outlook (Windows/Mac), and Yahoo strip or ignore `@font-face`. Fallback stacks are not visible in the truncated source — confirm system-font fallbacks are explicitly declared on `font-family` properties throughout the template.

**Alt text** — cannot audit from truncated source. Verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes (including spacer images, which should carry `alt=""`).
