# $10 OFF! You’re almost out of time…
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Same DSW email I've been getting all week, folks — I opened it because "you're almost out of time" plays on urgency, but the second I see six shoes up top and every single one is a women's heel, slingback, or bootie, I know this isn't for me. Skechers is buried in the fine print as a "qualifying brand," which is a joke — show me the shoe, don't make me hunt for the name in an offer-details paragraph.
- There is one bright spot: the "SHOP MEN" tab sits right there in the nav bar, so it's not like they forgot men exist entirely. But the whole visual real estate — hero banner, the six "so new so you" photos, even the size chart which stops at what looks like women's sizing — is aimed at somebody else's closet. I'm not clicking, and I'm sure not forwarding this to my buddy who wears a 10.5 wide.
- This is a mark-as-read-and-move-on email. The tiered $10/$20/$60 off deal is genuinely clear and I'll give them credit for that, but clarity on an offer I have no reason to use doesn't get me to the register.

## What's working

- The $10 off $49 / $20 off $99 / $60 off $199 tiers are laid out plainly — no squinting required to understand the math.
- The code COZYUP is visible right in the banner, not buried somewhere I'd have to scroll for.
- Free shipping for VIPs, in-store pickup, and the app download row are all easy comfort-brand-shopper signals — I like knowing pickup is an option.
- Nav bar has a "MEN" tab, so there's at least a path to my category even if the hero doesn't bother.

## What's weak

- Every single product photo — six of them — is a women's shoe: heels, slingbacks, booties. Not one sneaker, not one walking shoe, nothing for me.
- "So New, So You" as a headline rings hollow when "you" clearly isn't a 62-year-old guy looking for arch support.
- The size chart runs 5 through 11 in half sizes, which reads as women's sizing — no wide-width men's call-out until you hit the black button way down at "SHOP WIDE WIDTH," and even that's generic.
- Brooks, Skechers, Hoka-type comfort brands are only name-dropped in the tiny legal text at the bottom — nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere in the visuals.
- I've seen this same "$10/$20/$60 off" tiered structure and green banner in at least three emails this week (9/26 "$10 off ends TOMORROW," 9/27 "Last chance for $10 off") — nothing new here to make me open the next one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least half the hero product grid to men's comfort/walking shoes — if you're going to keep blasting me daily, at least rotate in something I'd actually wear.
- 2. Pull one comfort-brand callout (Skechers, Hoka, Brooks) out of the fine print and into an actual product tile with cushioning or arch-support language — that's the thing that gets a guy like me to click.
- 3. Vary the creative — this is the third near-identical green-banner tiered-discount email this week; even a good offer goes numb after this many repeats.
- 4. If DSW has purchase history showing I browse men's, segment me out of the head-to-toe women's hero entirely.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your size, your savings — up to $60 off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers, Brooks & more qualify — grab your size before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tiered savings on men's walking shoes, ends tonight`
