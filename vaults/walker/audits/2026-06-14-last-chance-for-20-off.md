# Last chance for 20% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Seventh DSW in a week — I've stopped counting on purpose because every time I count I feel like a lab rat. My thumb landed on this one before my brain even registered the sender, which is exactly the reflex habit DSW has built. The subject is cleaner than Thursday's 🚨🚨🚨 disaster, so I didn't feel pelted before I opened it — just a plain-English "last chance" and a number.
- But I opened to women's sandals. Again. "So New, So You" — a parade of strappy things in coral and beige that had nothing to do with me. Kept scrolling and found it: "Dad's Day Savings — Up to 25% Off Big-Deal Brands" with New Balance and Crocs logos sitting right there. Those are my brands. "Almost never on sale" is a line that actually lands for me. I sat up.
- I didn't click. But I came closer than I expected, given how much DSW goodwill I've burned through this week. The email buried the only part that mattered to me below content meant for somebody else's inbox.

## What's working

- New Balance and Crocs logos in the Dad's Day section are my exact cross-shop brands — the email knew what to put there, it just put it in the wrong spot.
- "Almost never on sale" under the Dad's Day headline is sharp copy. True, punchy, and it creates real urgency without emoji theatrics.
- "SHOP MORE DADS" is a no-nonsense button — I knew what it wanted from me.
- "Shop Wide Width" in the size block signals DSW understands its actual customer's feet, not just the model's feet.
- No emoji spam in the subject this time. After Thursday's 🚨🚨🚨 send, this reads like a real company.

## What's weak

- The hero opens on women's sandals — the "Dad's Day" email makes me scroll past strappy heels before I find anything for a man. That's backwards.
- Subject says 20% off; the Dad's Day section says 25% off. I know they're probably separate promotions layered together, but to my eye it reads like someone forgot to proofread before hitting send.
- The preheader padding — "Sale comes to an end *today*" is fine, but the string of invisible spacer characters trailing behind it looks broken in my inbox on the phone. Functional technique, ugly execution.
- Seven DSW emails in six days. I'm opening on muscle memory at this point, not genuine interest. That's a problem for them, not a compliment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the men's/Dad's Day content.** If the campaign is called "Dad's Day Savings," a 62-year-old male subscriber should not have to scroll past women's sandals to find it. Flip the layout — Dad's section first, everything else below.
- 2. **Reconcile the offer numbers.** Subject says 20%, email says 25%. Pick the bigger number for the subject — it's the better hook — and make sure they match.
- 3. **Name a brand in the subject or preheader.** "New Balance & Crocs — 25% off, ends today" would have tapped faster than a generic discount line. Brand specificity beats percentage abstraction for a comparison shopper like me.
- 4. **Clean up the preheader trailing characters.** The visible copy is fine. The padding technique is standard but the visual output on my phone looks like a glitch. A shorter, sharper preheader with no trailing noise would serve better.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** Seven emails in a week turns a subscribed fan into a reflex-tapper. One or two good sends beat seven forgettable ones.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's Day: 25% off New Balance, Crocs & more — ends today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your comfort brands are almost never on sale. Today they are.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Crocs, Nike — big-deal brands, rare discount, ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These brands are almost never this low. Last chance today.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — `Src:` (capital S) in all 10 `@font-face` blocks:**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...woff") format('woff');
```
The CSS property is `src` (lowercase). Capital `S` is invalid per spec. Apple Mail/WebKit tolerates it; Outlook and older clients will silently drop the font declaration, falling back to system fonts. This affects all Futura PT and HelveticaNow faces. Fix: lowercase all ten `Src:` occurrences.

**Bug — Contradictory `text-size-adjust` in the reset:**
```css
* { -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Both values appear in the same ruleset. `none` overrides `100%`, meaning Outlook/IE will disable text scaling entirely. The `100%` line should be removed or the duplicate eliminated.

**Bug — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This prevents users from zooming in on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or change to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Minor — `Margin: 0` (capital M) in heading reset:**
```css
h1, h2, h3, h4, h5, h6, p, ul, li { margin: 0 !important; Margin: 0 }
```
Duplicate with invalid casing. Browser-tolerant but redundant; remove the capital-M line.

**Web font coverage:** Futura PT (`.woff`) and HelveticaNow (`.ttf`) only render in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail. All other clients will fall back — confirm the fallback stack is explicitly declared in the body copy (not visible in truncated source).
