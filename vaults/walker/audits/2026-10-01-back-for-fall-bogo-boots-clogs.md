# Back for Fall: BOGO Boots & Clogs
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- I've seen this one before. Shoe Carnival sent me "BOGO Boots has your Fall Favs!" on the 27th, and the 15% off sitewide code FALL15 was the whole point of their September 16th email. Now both are stacked in one send. Four emails in five days, and this is the second one to push the same BOGO boots deal.
- The offer is easy to read. "BOGO FREE Boots and Clogs" is huge, and the 15% off banner above it is clear. But right under it, in small type, it says "Select Women's." Every product is a women's bootie or fuzzy clog. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for cushioning and arch support, so I'm done after about two seconds. There's no men's shoe and no comfort or walking talk anywhere. The only thing aimed at me is the "MENS" tab in the bottom nav, and I'd have to hunt for it.
- Would I click? No. Forward it? Only to my wife, if she wanted boots. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The BOGO headline is huge and the 15% off banner with code FALL15 is plain. I know the deal in one glance.
- The orange and yellow colors are bold and seasonal, and the "Fall" feel matches what's in the stores right now.
- The text is big enough to read on my phone without zooming, at least in the hero blocks.
- The "$10 off $74.98 / $30 off $149.98" step-up coupons are a straightforward way to save. The barcodes mean I could just show my phone at the register.
- The Shoe Perks "$10 reward" box at least acknowledges that I'm a member.

## What's weak

- "Select Women's" is small and sits under the headline. All six product pairs are women's boots and clogs, so this feels sent to somebody else.
- Two big offers compete at the top, the 15% sitewide and the BOGO. I don't know which one to look at first.
- The only links I can find are small underlined text, "Shop Now" and "Shop BOGO Free." They aren't big buttons, which is hard for thumbs on a phone.
- The fine print is a dense block with a long list of excluded brands. It excludes Hoka, Brooks, New Balance and Skechers, so for my usual shoes the 15% doesn't apply. That feels like bait.
- Nothing speaks to comfort, cushioning, arch support or slip-on convenience. The products show a name and no price.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by gender. Send men like me a men's version with a men's comfort shoe, such as the New Balance 603 they showed on the 28th, and cut the women's boots.
- 2. Pick one headline offer. Lead with BOGO or the 15% code, not both at the same size.
- 3. Put "Select Women's" in the headline itself so nobody feels tricked.
- 4. Make the buttons large with real verbs, like "Shop Women's BOGO Boots," and add prices under each product.
- 5. Space out the sends. Four in five days with recycled offers trains me to ignore them.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO boots & clogs are back — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy one pair, get one free: women's boots & clogs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 15% off sitewide with code FALL15, online today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cozy fall boots, step-up coupons and a $10 Perks reward inside.`

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed defect: an unresolved merge token (`[UNIQUE]`) in a tracking-pixel URL. The same pixel family also shows a malformed UTM query string and a stray zero-width character. The remaining findings are low-severity head-markup issues.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 42 links on `emltrk.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe, so none of their redirects, destinations or status codes are confirmed.
- **Malformed UTM on pixel 1:** `https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20261001_boots_clogs_AM`
  - `utm_campaign` is nested inside the value of the `t` parameter, not passed as its own query parameter. Most analytics tools will not parse it as campaign data.
  - The URL ends with a zero-width space (U+200B) after `_AM`. This can break exact-match campaign reporting or cause the pixel request to be rejected.
- **Unresolved token on pixel 2:** `https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` still contains the literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder. The per-recipient identifier was never substituted, so open or click attribution for this pixel is broken or collapses to a single anonymous ID.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** both images above have no `alt` attribute. If they are 1×1 tracking pixels, the fix is `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`). Their dimensions weren't visible in the truncated source.
- **Meta tags:** `<meta content="IE=edge" />` has no `http-equiv`, so it does nothing. `<meta content="text/html;" charset="utf-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The `charset` attribute is still honored by modern clients.
- **Doctype mismatch:** the doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional but `<html>` carries `xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"`. This is harmless in most clients but inconsistent.
- **Link styling:** the global rule `a, a:link, … { text-decoration:none !important; color:inherit !important; }` strips default link styling. Links that aren't styled as buttons will have no visual affordance, which hurts accessibility (WCAG 1.4.1). Whether this applies depends on the body markup, which wasn't visible.
- **Small mobile text:** `.responsive-text { font-size:12px !important; }` at `max-width:600px` sets mobile text below the typical 14px minimum.
- **Breakpoint overlap:** breakpoints at 450, 600 and 660px overlap. `.responsive-text` switches at 600px while `.container` switches at 660px. This can cause inconsistent layouts between 601 and 660px.
- **Positive:** `lang="en"` is set, and the viewport meta tag is present.
- **Not verifiable:** total HTML size (Gmail clips messages over about 102KB), the rest of the markup, and alt text on content images.
