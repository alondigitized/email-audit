# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date: 2026-05-29 | Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Today's run shows a site that hasn't improved its personalization or comfort-first messaging since yesterday — and it added one outright catastrophe: the cart page is completely blank. A guy who made it all the way to "Add to Cart" is now staring at a white screen with his wallet still in his pocket. That alone tanks the session. The Men's navigation menu remains the strongest part of the experience. Everything else is either flat or sliding backward.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** — Down from 4/10 yesterday. The blank cart is a conversion-killing regression. No login confirmation, no personalization, search surfacing women's shoes first. A customer like me doesn't retry. I close the tab and check DSW.

---

## 3. What's Working

- **Men's drawer navigation** is genuinely well-built. Large text, one item per row, full-width tap targets. "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" are surfaced right there without digging. That's exactly what I need to see.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins category page** lands cleanly. Clear heading, breadcrumbs, 291 results, width filters (Wide / Extra Wide / Medium) front and center. Comfortable to browse.
- **Product detail page** is solid. Price ($120) is large and easy to read. The BOGO offer ("Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart") is called out twice — in the banner and in red text on the product. No confusion about the deal.
- **ADD TO CART button** is big, blue, and sticky at the bottom of the screen. Any 62-year-old can find and tap that without squinting.
- **Size picker** boxes are adequately sized for finger tapping. The "Please select a size" validation catches the error clearly and in readable red text.
- **BOGO 50% OFF** is persistent in the top banner across every page — consistent and reassuring.

---

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank (Step 8).** White. Nothing. No product, no total, no checkout button. This is a full stop. I added a shoe, I expected to see it in my cart, and I got nothing. This is the single most damaging thing on this site today and it's new — it wasn't in yesterday's session.
- **Login step produced no visible result (Step 3).** The screenshot after "Log In" looks identical to the homepage. No welcome message, no name in the header, no loyalty points balance. I have no idea if I'm actually logged in. The account icon gives me no feedback.
- **The homepage is not for me.** A soccer prize giveaway. Young models walking urban streets. No older male, no comfort story, nothing that says "this brand gets your feet." I scroll past this fast.
- **No arch support language anywhere above the fold.** The word "cushioning" eventually appears on the product page, but "arch support" — the thing I care about most — is nowhere in my first 5 screens.
- **Search returns women's shoes first (Step 9).** I searched "comfort shoes" while supposedly logged into a male account and the top two results are both labeled "Women's." 1,454 results and they lead with the wrong gender. That's a personalization failure that would make me question whether this site knows me at all.
- **Popup dismissal left no trace (Step 2).** The banner swapped from the apparel sale to a "Get Dad a Gift He'll Love" message — which is at least relevant to the date — but I never saw an actual popup to dismiss. Either it didn't fire or it auto-resolved. Not necessarily a problem, but the Dad's Day message is generic and not directed at me as a customer.
- **Only 12 reviews on the Max Cushioning Premier 3.** At $120, I want social proof. Twelve reviews isn't enough to feel confident. Hoka and New Balance product pages I've browsed have hundreds.
- **Horizontal scroll nav on homepage** (Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs…) truncates off screen. I can't see what other categories exist without swiping, and there's no visual indicator that it scrolls.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is P0. A blank cart is an invisible wall between a customer and a purchase. Every day this is live is lost revenue.
2. **Confirm login with a visible signal.** Show my name or "Hi, Walker" somewhere in the header after login. Show my points balance. Make me feel like the site knows me.
3. **Filter search results by logged-in gender by default.** If I'm a male account, my first results for "comfort shoes" should be men's shoes. Full stop.
4. **Put "arch support" language on the homepage.** It's the #1 comfort attribute for men my age. It's a category in the menu — surface it in the hero banner or a featured module.
5. **Swap the soccer sweepstakes hero for a comfort-forward story.** Feature a man in his 60s walking with a dog or on a golf course. Speak to the customer who's actually buying Slip-ins.
6. **Increase social proof on the product page.** "12 Reviews" at $120 is thin. Surface the aggregate comfort/cushioning ratings or pull in community feedback.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The foundation is here — the Slip-ins category, the width filters, the deal communication, the ADD TO CART button all work. But I can't complete a purchase because the cart is blank, I'm not sure I'm even logged in, and the search treats me like I don't have a gender preference. Yesterday was a 4 because the experience was mediocre. Today is a 3 because it's mediocre plus broken. If I'm cross-shopping against New Balance's site or Hoka's, Skechers loses me at the cart.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Platform: Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware). All pages share the same server-rendered HTML shell with `lang="en"` and populated `<title>` — **except the View Cart page**, which is missing both. Third-party tag footprint is extremely heavy (20+ async scripts per page). A Kasada bot-detection loop generates repeated 429s that pollute the network waterfall. LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, leaving performance characterization incomplete.

---

## Accessibility

**Critical (1)**
- `aria-required-parent` (Homepage): One or more ARIA role elements lack required parent container roles. Likely a carousel or listbox widget with orphaned `role="option"` or `role="tab"` nodes. Assistive technologies will misinterpret the widget's structure.

**Serious (2) — both on View Cart**
- `document-title`: The cart page renders without a `<title>` element. Likely a client-side render path (modal overlay or SPA shell) that bypasses the standard `<head>` template. Screen readers announce the page as untitled.
- `html-has-lang`: Same cart render path omits `lang` on `<html>`. Screen readers default to OS locale, causing incorrect pronunciation for non-default locales.

**Moderate (12)**
- Homepage: `heading-order` violation (likely an `<h3>` or `<h4>` used where `<h2>` is expected, typical in promotional widgets); `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-no-duplicate-main` together indicate a `<main>` element nested inside another sectioning element, or two `<main>` elements present simultaneously; `landmark-complementary-is-top-level` is an `<aside>` nested inside `<main>` or a `<section>`; `landmark-unique` means two identical `role="banner"` or `role="navigation"` landmarks share no distinguishing `aria-label`; `region` means content exists outside any landmark.
- View Cart: `landmark-one-main` (no `<main>` at all) and `page-has-heading-one` (no `<h1>`) — consistent with a JS-rendered overlay that never sets document structure.
- Search: Same `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique`, and `region` pattern as Homepage, suggesting shared template defects.

---

## SEO

- View Cart missing `<title>`: While cart pages are typically `noindex`, its absence creates a crawl anomaly and breaks browser tab labeling.
- `html-has-lang` absent on View Cart: Language signals are used by Google for locale-targeting; missing it on a rendered URL is a soft signal gap.
- `heading-order` on Homepage: Misordered headings fragment content hierarchy for crawlers that use heading structure as an outline.
- No issues found with canonical tags, robots meta, or structured data based on available source samples.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` custom property**: The inline `style="--vh: 6.640000000000001px"` on `<html>` is a JS-calculated mobile viewport height workaround (common for 100vh bugs in iOS Safari). The raw floating-point value leaking into the attribute suggests the calculation runs on every resize without rounding. Not harmful, but the precision is unnecessary.
- **`vice-managed-src` attribute pattern**: Used for consent-gated scripts (Pinterest, Facebook, Yahoo, Nextdoor, Taboola). Correct pattern for consent management, but these scripts are fully deferred until consent fires — if the CMP script itself is slow, ad pixels and analytics will be silently absent from sessions.
- No evidence of `loading="lazy"` or responsive image patterns in the sampled HTML, but the samples are truncated so this cannot be confirmed.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 rate-limiting loop (6 console + 5 network hits):**
- All 429s target the same Kasada bot-detection fingerprinting endpoint: `/{uuid}/{uuid}/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.430`
- This is the Kasada KPSDK client making repeated fingerprint submissions that the server is throttling. In a real user session this loop resolves; in an automated audit session (Playwright/headless Chrome) Kasada's server-side signal scoring triggers rate limiting. The repeated retries from the client-side SDK then block the network waterfall.
- One additional 429 hits `/cart/` directly — the cart page itself is being rate-limited by Kasada, which explains the elevated TTFB (135ms) and the `document-title`/`html-has-lang` missing violations: if the cart request is being challenge-intercepted, a minimal error shell may be returned instead of the full page.

**CORS block on Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' 
blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics CDN is not returning CORS headers for `skechers.com` origin. This blocks the styling widget from loading. Root cause: either the Stylitics account configuration needs `skechers.com` added to the allowlist, or a version/cache-key mismatch (`version=62dd86ff7e`) is hitting an edge-cached response that predates the CORS configuration. This is a vendor integration issue.

**400 on Pinterest conversion endpoint (`ct.pinterest.com/v3/`):**
Malformed event payload or missing required field in the Pinterest CAPI call. Low severity but indicates a broken conversion signal for Pinterest ad attribution.

## Recommendations

**P0 — View Cart page structural integrity**
The missing `<title>` and `html[lang]` on View Cart, combined with the 429 from Kasada on `/cart/`, strongly suggests the cart page is returning a Kasada challenge/error shell instead of the real page in test environments. Verify that the cart page renders correctly for real users by checking server-side session logs, and ensure the full HTML template is used even when Kasada intercepts (challenge should happen at the network layer before HTML is served, not strip document metadata).

**P1 — Fix `aria-required-parent` on Homepage**
Audit all carousel/listbox/tab widget implementations for orphaned ARIA role children. This is a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.

**P1 — Fix Stylitics CORS configuration**
Contact Stylitics to add `https://www.skechers.com` to the `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` allowlist on their CDN. The integration is silently broken for all users.

**P2 — Remove duplicate script tags**
Deduplicate `ytc.js` (Yahoo) and `creativecdn` tag in GTM. Each double-fire sends duplicate conversion events and wastes bandwidth.

**P2 — Capture LCP and CLS**
Re-run the audit with Lighthouse or WebPageTest configured to report Core Web Vitals. TTFB alone is insufficient for performance assessment.

**P3 — Audit third-party script inventory**
20+ third-party origins per page is unusually high. Run a tag audit in GTM to identify stale or redundant pixels. Each origin adds a DNS lookup + TLS handshake to the critical path.

**P3 — Fix Pinterest 400 error**
Inspect the Pinterest pixel event payload being fired — likely a missing `event_id` or malformed `user_data` field in the CAPI integration.
