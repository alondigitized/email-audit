# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-20**
## Executive summary

- This is an abandoned cart email reminding me that I left something in my bag — and I appreciate that Skechers noticed. The hero product, the Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro Slip-ins, is squarely in my wheelhouse. The 20% off offer gets my attention too. But then things go sideways: the product thumbnails below the hero look mostly like women's shoes, the email turns into a catalog dump with navigation links and utility modules, and I start feeling like I'm being treated as a generic inbox subscriber rather than a loyal guy who came close to buying something. Good setup, weak follow-through.
- Solid trigger moment and relevant hero product, but diluted by clutter and gender mismatch below the fold.

## What's working

- **The hero is relevant to me.** Arch Fit Glide-Step Pro Slip-ins. That's exactly the kind of thing I'd have in my cart. Seeing it big and front-and-center makes me feel like the email knows what I was doing.
- **"COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is direct.** No games, no mystery. I know what they want me to do.
- **20% off a single pair.** That's a real offer. That's money. For a price-conscious member like me, that banner below the hero is a genuine hook.
- **Slip-ins are featured.** For me, that's the line. Seeing them prominently helps.

## What's weak

- **The product grid below the hero is mostly women's shoes.** I count several "Women's Slip-ins" variants. Why are you reminding me to complete MY order and then showing me women's footwear? That breaks the whole personalization illusion.
- **Too much clutter.** After the 20% off banner, I get: "Shop Additional Styles," a full category nav (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing, New Arrivals, Sale), a text-signup pitch, app download, curbside pickup, Shop Now Pay Later, Find a Store, Follow Us — all before the footer legal text. That's exhausting on a phone screen.
- **No mention of my Plus membership.** I'm a Skechers Plus member. Nothing in this email acknowledges that. No points reminder, no member-exclusive angle on the discount. I feel like a stranger.
- **"Let's Get Texting" module.** I already get emails from you. Mid-cart-recovery is not the moment to pitch another channel.
- **Small text in fine print.** The Spring Fling Sale terms at the bottom are tiny. I shouldn't have to squint to know if my 20% off is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the product grid to men's only** — or better, show the exact items I left in my cart if possible. If it can't be dynamic, cut the secondary products entirely.
- 2. **Acknowledge my Plus membership.** Even one line: "As a Skechers Plus member, this offer is waiting for you" changes the whole tone.
- 3. **Cut the utility modules for this send.** Curbside pickup, app download, text signup — those belong in a welcome email or a browse email, not a recovery email. Let the cart win.
- 4. **Move the 20% off banner higher** or fold it into the hero CTA area. Don't make me scroll past the hero to find the incentive.
- 5. **Increase body text size.** On a phone, the fine print is unreadable without zooming.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP click-tracking domain (confirmed)**

```

```
This includes both the "web version" link (row-1) and the logo/hero link (row-2). Plaintext HTTP tracking links can trigger browser/client security warnings and are flagged by spam filters. Attentive supports HTTPS tracking — this is a configuration issue on the sending account.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably older Outlook builds) display the document title in the window chrome. An empty title is not broken, but it is a missed fallback.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, older Outlook and Lotus Notes versions may not correctly declare encoding.

**Preheader padding uses mixed invisible-character techniques**
The preheader filler mixes two distinct Unicode strategies: `U+034F` (COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER, `͏`) followed by `U+00AD` (SOFT HYPHEN, `­`). Mixing techniques is redundant and can produce unexpected rendering in clients that partially handle one but not the other. A single consistent method (typically `&zwnj;` or `&#8203;`) is preferred.

**Image alt text unverifiable** — the `<img>` tags are in the truncated section. Must confirm all product/hero images have descriptive `alt` attributes; cart abandonment emails with missing alt text on product images render as blank in image-blocked clients, which critically breaks the cart recovery CTA.

**Responsive breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:620px)` is present and stack layout is declared. No issues found in visible CSS.
