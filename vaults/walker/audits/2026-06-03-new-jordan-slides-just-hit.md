# New Jordan slides just hit 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Famous Footwear dropped something in my inbox that's clearly not aimed at me — "Cool factor" and a young woman rocking Jordan slides is not how I shop. I've been buying from Famous for years, I know their prices, I know their stores. But Jordan? That's my grandson's territory. I'm looking for something I can slip on, walk six blocks in, and not feel it in my knees at dinner. This email is about looking fresh, not feeling good.
- The one thing that caught my eye mid-scroll was the Brooks Ghost 17 at $119.99 in the "Style of the week" block. That's a shoe I've looked at. But it's buried under slides for kids and a women's sandal sale — by the time I got there, I'd already mentally checked out. And yes, Famous Footwear pulled the same tracking-URL-as-preview trick again. I complained about this on the May 12th sends, and the May 16th send, and here it is a third time. At some point that stops being a mistake and starts being negligence.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't click. Wouldn't forward it — definitely not to my buddy Dave, who's as comfort-focused as I am.

## What's working

- **Famous Footwear is a trusted sender** — I open their emails on muscle memory, and this one got opened the same way.
- **The offer blocks are visible and readable** — "Up to 40% off" Teva and Nike, "Up to 50% off women's sandals," and the Brooks Ghost 17 price point are all legible without zooming. Text size is fine on mobile.
- **No render bugs** — email laid out clean, images loaded, nothing broken or overlapping.
- **Summer timing is right** — slides and sandals in early June makes sense; the seasonal logic is there even if the execution is off.
- **SHOP JORDAN button is clear** — whatever I think of the product, the CTA is unambiguous. One tap, I know where I'm going.

## What's weak

- **The preview is a raw tracking URL. Again.** I've flagged this from Famous Footwear on May 12th (twice), May 16th, and here it is June 3rd. This is the first thing I see in my inbox before I even open. It looks broken. It makes me wonder if I'm getting phished.
- **"Cool factor" is not my language** — I'm 62, not 22. That headline bounced right off me.
- **The model and the vibe are not me** — young woman, fashion posing, Jordan Jumpman logo. I don't have anything against Jordan shoes, but this whole email is styling for someone else.
- **The Kids section is irrelevant real estate** — colorful kids' slides take up a big visual chunk. I scrolled right past it, which means I almost missed the Brooks Ghost buried below.
- **Nothing about comfort, fit, or support** — not one word about cushioning, arch support, or whether these slides are actually walkable for someone my age with any sense of foot hygiene. That's the whole ballgame for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — permanently.** This is the single biggest drag on every Famous Footwear send I've seen this spring. Put real copy in there: a tease, a deal, anything. "New slides for summer — including Brooks Ghost 17 at $119" would've made me tap before I even opened.
- 2. **Segment the audience.** I should be getting the "men's comfort and walking" version of this email, not the fashion-forward young-woman version. Famous Footwear has my purchase history — they know I've bought Skechers and that I'm not buying Jordan.
- 3. **Lead with the buried deal.** The Brooks Ghost 17 at $119.99 is the most relevant thing in this email for me. It shouldn't be in a secondary scroll block — it should be the hero or at least a top-of-email call-out.
- 4. **Write one comfort signal into the hero.** Even if they keep the Jordan frame: "Slides built for all-day wear" or "Cushioned slides, now in stock" changes the conversation from fashion to function.
- 5. **Cut the kids section or move it to the bottom.** Men's casual shoppers don't need to scroll through kids' footwear to find their section.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cushioned, easy-on slides from Jordan, Brooks & Teva — shop the new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brooks Ghost 17 now $119.99 — plus up to 50% off summer slides`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

All 29 links route through `click.email.famousfootwear.com` (click-redirect). The QA probe correctly skipped these, meaning no destination URL — including UTM passthrough — could be validated. This is expected behavior for a click-tracking setup, but it leaves zero automated assurance that any link resolves.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv` attribute:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, some legacy clients may not recognize the charset declaration.

**20 images have empty or absent `alt` text** (confirmed by QA). Noteworthy cases:
- Logo (`3b6a7dc1-...png`): `alt=""` — should be `alt="Famous Footwear"`
- All product images: `alt=""` — renders as blank in image-blocked clients; screen readers get nothing

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile — violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**CSS typo — invalid property name:**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property; this rule is silently ignored. Intended value is likely `line-height` or `letter-spacing`.

**Invalid `max-height` value:**
```css
max-height:auto !important;
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: `none`, length, percentage). No visual effect in practice, but a correctness flag.

**Truncated inline style detected** in the source (`font-siz…`) — suggests the HTML was clipped during extraction. If this reflects the actual rendered source, an incomplete style attribute may cause parser warnings in strict clients.

**Preheader div is empty:**
```html
<div class="preheader" style="font-size:1px;display:none !important"></div>
```
No preheader text is set. Inbox preview text will pull from the first visible body content instead.

### 5. Compliance

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameter passthrough is unverifiable.** All click-tracking URLs are opaque redirects through `click.email.famousfootwear.com`. Without following the redirects, there is no way to confirm whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` parameters are appended to destination URLs.

The click-tracking query strings use encoded payloads (e.g., `?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ4OTZ9...`) rather than transparent UTM parameters at the redirect layer — UTM injection must be confirmed at the ESP configuration level, not from the HTML.
