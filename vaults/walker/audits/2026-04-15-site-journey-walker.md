# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-04-15**
## Full review
---

## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker Miles, 62, loyal Skechers Plus member, and I shop for one thing: comfortable Slip-ins for my feet. Today's visit had some real highs in the middle of the funnel — the Men's nav is clean, the Slip-ins product page was easy to read, and the BOGO deal was front and center. But the homepage ignored me completely (Mother's Day everywhere), the log-in step produced no visible login screen, the cart rendered as a **blank white page**, and search dumped Women's shoes on me first despite being logged in. The cart failure alone would've sent me to the phone to call customer service — or to a competitor.

## 3. What's Working

**Men's Category Nav (Step 4)** — This was actually the highlight of the whole trip. The MEN drawer is clean, text is large enough to read without squinting, and "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there as a quick link near the bottom. I didn't have to hunt. One tap and I was where I needed to be. Good job.

**Hands Free Slip-ins Category Page (Step 5)** — Landed exactly where I wanted. Big clear header "Hands Free Slip-ins," a description explaining the Heel Pillow technology (I like knowing that stuff), and width options (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) right up top. 265 results. The BOGO 50% OFF banner was still visible so I knew the deal was still on.

**Product Page (Step 6)** — The Larzo page was clean. Big product name, $110 price easy to read, and the red "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" badge was right below the price where I'd look. Product photo was clear. No clutter.

**Size Selection / Add to Cart (Step 7)** — Size grid was large, spaced out, easy to tap. The "Please select a size" error message was visible and clear. The blue ADD TO CART button at the bottom was full-width and easy to hit. Good button sizing for my thumbs.

**BOGO Deal Visibility** — That BOGO 50% OFF banner stayed pinned at the top through the whole journey. I never forgot about the deal. That's smart.

## 4. What's Weak

**Homepage — Completely Irrelevant to Me (Step 1)**
Full-screen Mother's Day campaign. Women, flowers, pink sneakers. I'm a 62-year-old guy shopping for my feet. There's nothing on that screen for me. Not even a hint of men's shoes. No comfort technology callout. If I landed here cold, I might assume Skechers doesn't make much for men. I'm a Skechers Plus member — why am I seeing the same generic campaign as a first-time visitor?

**Login — No Visible Confirmation (Step 3)**
The login screenshot looks exactly like the homepage. No login modal, no "Welcome back, Walker" message, no name in the header. I have no idea if I'm logged in. The account icon looks the same logged-in or not from what I can see. If I'm a Plus member, tell me. Show me something.

**Cart — Complete Failure (Step 8)**
Blank white screen. Nothing. After going through the whole journey — finding the right shoe, picking my size, hitting Add to Cart — I get a white page. That's catastrophic. I'd think my order didn't go through. I'd tap the button again. I'd double-order. Or I'd give up entirely. This is the single worst moment in the entire journey and it's at the finish line.

**Search Results Not Personalized (Step 9)**
I searched "slip in" while logged in as a male Skechers Plus member. The first two results are Women's shoes — "See Ya There" and "Cozy Fit," both labeled "Women's" right under the product names. 1,014 results but they're serving me women's content first. No Men's filter pre-applied. No acknowledgment of who I am. That's a miss.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately.** A blank cart page is a purchase blocker. This is priority one. Whatever is causing the white screen on `/cart/` needs to be diagnosed and resolved today. This likely cost real sales.

2. **Personalize the homepage for logged-in users.** I'm a Skechers Plus member. The hero banner should acknowledge that — "Welcome back, Walker. Check out what's new in Slip-ins." Even a secondary banner below the hero showing Men's Comfort Technology would help. Mother's Day is fine for unknown visitors; it's wrong for me.

3. **Confirm login visually.** Put my first name or "Plus Member" in the account icon area. A small "Hi, Walker" or a Plus badge goes a long way for trust and recognition.

4. **Filter search by gender for logged-in users.** If I'm a logged-in male, "slip in" search should default to Men's or at minimum not lead with Women's products. Apply my known gender preference as a soft default filter.

5. **Men's nav is good — surface it faster.** The Men's hamburger menu drawer works well. Consider adding a persistent "Men's" tab in the main nav bar so I don't need to open the full menu to start my journey.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

The middle of this funnel works — once I'm in the Men's Slip-ins category, the experience is genuinely good. But getting there requires ignoring a women-only homepage, hoping I'm logged in (no confirmation visible), and then at the very end hitting a blank cart page. For a 62-year-old who just wants to buy comfortable shoes without hassle, that blank cart is where I stop and go find my shoes somewhere else. Score stays at **4/10**. Fix the cart and confirm my login and it jumps to a 6 immediately.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set on `<html>` via inline style — this is a JS-computed `window.innerHeight / 100` workaround for the iOS Safari 100vh bug. The floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` vs `6.64`) is harmless but indicates the value is raw from `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding. Should be `Math.round` or `toFixed(2)` to keep the DOM clean.
- The approach itself is valid, but it creates a forced synchronous layout recalculation on viewport resize. On orientation change on mobile, this will trigger reflow.
- No evidence of responsive image `srcset` or `sizes` attributes in the truncated source, which is significant for mobile performance given the image-heavy product catalog.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada bot protection endpoint (5 hits):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308
```
The UUID-path structure and `x-kpsdk-v` parameter identify this as the **Kasada bot mitigation** fingerprinting beacon (`kpsdk` = Kasada SDK). The server is returning 429 to its own SDK's fingerprint calls, meaning the bot-detection infrastructure is rate-limiting itself — likely a misconfigured rate limit threshold, a Kasada config issue, or the test environment IP being flagged. This is a real operational error, not an artifact of automated testing.

**429 — Cart page:**
```
https://www.skechers.com/cart/
```
The cart itself is returning 429. Combined with the 140ms TTFB on View Cart, this suggests the cart endpoint is either under rate-limiting or the same Kasada misconfiguration is blocking the cart load.

**CORS — Stylitics outfit widget (2 errors):**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics' CDN is not returning an `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header for `https://www.skechers.com`. The stylesheet is loaded twice — once as a `<link>` (CORS preflight) and once via XHR, both blocked. The Stylitics hotspot/outfit widget will render without its styles, causing a broken visual state. This requires Stylitics to add `skechers.com` to their CORS allowlist, or the widget must be loaded differently (e.g., same-origin proxy).

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix immediately:**
1. **Kasada 429 loop**: Investigate why the Kasada SDK is rate-limiting its own fingerprint endpoint. Coordinate with Kasada support — misconfigured rate limits on `/fp` will degrade bot protection efficacy and create spurious errors across all pages.
2. **Cart 429**: The cart page returning 429 is a conversion-killing bug. Determine if this is Kasada, a CDN rule, or server-side rate limiting and resolve before it impacts production traffic.
3. **Critical ARIA violations on View Product** (`aria-required-children`, `aria-valid-attr-value`, `button-name`): These break screen reader access to the PDP. Audit the size/color selector widgets and any custom components for broken ARIA trees and unlabeled buttons.

**P1 — High priority:**
4. **Stylitics CORS**: Contact Stylitics to add the `skechers.com` origin to their CORS policy for `web-assets.stylitics.com`, or remove the duplicate XHR load path.
5. **Remove duplicate scripts**: Deduplicate `creativecdn`, Yahoo `ytc.js`, and consolidate Monetate to a single version. Each duplicate is wasted bandwidth and CPU on mobile.
6. **Capture LCP and CLS**: The audit is missing the two highest-weight CWV signals. Instrument via `PerformanceObserver` or a RUM provider (Quantum Metric is already installed — ensure LCP/CLS are being collected and reported).
7. **`link-in-text-block` on Men's Shoes**: Add underline or non-color visual differentiator to in-content links. This is a WCAG 2.1 §1.4.1 failure.

**P2 — Medium priority:**
8. **Unlabeled links** (`link-name`) on Homepage: Add `aria-label` or visible text to all anchor elements used as interactive controls.
9. **Landmark structure**: Resolve duplicate `<main>` and nested `<aside>`/complementary landmarks. The site appears to embed full page shells inside other shells — likely a template composition issue in the Salesforce Commerce Cloud setup.
10. **`--vh` precision**: Apply `parseFloat((window.innerHeight / 100).toFixed(2))` to avoid floating-point noise in the inline style.
11. **Responsive images**: Audit product image markup for `srcset`/`sizes` — critical for LCP on mobile given the image-heavy catalog.
