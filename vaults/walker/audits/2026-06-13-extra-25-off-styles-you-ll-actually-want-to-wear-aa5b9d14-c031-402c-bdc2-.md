# Extra 25% off styles you’ll actually want to wear
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — when I saw "Crocs" in my inbox this morning, my thumb almost twitched toward delete before my eyes even caught the subject line. I've been counting these things and I think I've had four Crocs emails since Father's Day alone. But "Extra 25% off" held me. A stacked discount — 25% on top of styles already up to 50% off — is real money, and I'm not too proud to click when the math works.
- Then I opened it, and the first thing I see is a hot-pink clog surrounded by neon lime-green "sale" boxes. Okay. That's... vivid. I'm 62 and I wear orthotics. That shoe, in that color, is not for me. The whole email reads like it was designed for a teenage girl's Instagram story, not a guy who just wants a comfortable slip-on for the backyard or the grocery run.
- There's no clear shop button, no men's styles, no comfort copy — just the stacked discount and a Jibbitz charms section I couldn't care less about. I'd note the price and go look for it myself on the Crocs site, but I wouldn't click anything in this email. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **The offer math is genuinely compelling** — extra 25% on already-reduced sale styles is a real discount, and it's stated plainly up top without burying it.
- **Subject line earned the open** — "styles you'll actually want to wear" has a tiny bit of cheek that made me curious instead of annoyed.
- **"Extra Extra!" at the top is punchy** — two-word hook that signals this is better than a normal sale.
- **No broken images or layout issues** — the email renders cleanly; I can see everything they intended me to see.

## What's weak

- **Hot pink clog is the wrong demo signal entirely** — no male shopper, no comfort messaging, nothing that says "this is for you, Walker."
- **The preheader is pure garbage** — whatever was supposed to show next to the subject in my inbox was blank. Wasted prime real estate.
- **No real CTA button anywhere prominent** — the only tappable things I see are App Store / Google Play downloads. I'm not downloading an app to shop a sale.
- **Jibbitz charms take up half the email** — I am not a 9-year-old. Charms are not why I'm here.
- **Tenth Crocs email in three weeks** — the cadence fatigue is real. After "Time's almost up!" from June 7th, and the Father's Day send on June 9th, this feels like the brand just has the fire hose on full blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show men's styles in the hero** — even one classic clog in black, navy, or grey alongside the 25% off headline would have doubled the relevance for me. Pink clog signals women's department immediately.
- 2. **Add a real "Shop Men's Sale" CTA button** — something I can tap with my thumb without hunting for it. Not an app download link.
- 3. **Ditch the Jibbitz section or move it to the footer** — if you're running a stacked discount email, let the discount breathe. Charms are a separate send.
- 4. **Write a real preheader** — even "Already up to 50% off — now take an extra 25%" would have made the inbox preview actually work.
- 5. **Fix the cadence** — three sends in a week dilutes everything. That "Time's almost up!" email June 7th was supposed to be a closer; this one arriving six days later undercuts the urgency of everything they've sent.
