# FINAL DAY: $20 off $99.98+
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Ten days, going on ten emails. By now I can picture the red-and-yellow "Inflation Relief" banner before the thing even loads. Today's subject at least does something right — "FINAL DAY" with a dollar figure makes me hesitate before I swipe left. I've been around long enough to know a real deadline from a manufactured one, and Shoe Carnival has been crying wolf long enough that I'm starting to wonder. But a $20 off is a $20 off.
- Here's the problem: once you get past that first coupon banner, the entire email is women's sandals and kids' shoes. Jellies. Pastel slides. A "Big Style for Littles" section. I'm a 62-year-old man looking for something cushioned to wear walking the neighborhood or running errands — and this email has nothing to show me. The discount is real and I'd use it. But the email itself gave me zero reason to go shopping.
- I'd copy the coupon code and close the tab. Maybe forward it to my wife because honestly, half this send was written for her anyway.

## What's working

- The $20 off hero is clean, prominent, and the code (MAY20) is big enough to read without zooming on my phone — that's the one thing that earns the open.
- "FINAL DAY" paired with "LIMITED TIME" is the right urgency language; it doesn't feel fake when it actually is the last day of the sale.
- The Shoe Perks $10 reward reminder at the bottom tells me my loyalty balance has value — I appreciate the nudge.
- Email renders clean — no broken images, no garbled text, everything loads correctly on mobile.

## What's weak

- After the hero offer, every product shown is women's slides, girls' Nikes, or kids' shoes — nothing for a man who wants a cushioned walking shoe.
- My inbox preview text was a raw tracking URL — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/..." — which looks like a spam mistake and nearly cost them the open.
- "Online Only" adds friction when I might be standing in the store next Saturday with my phone; making me remember a code and navigate a website is a step I might skip.
- This is the tenth email in about two weeks on the same red-and-yellow template — I referenced the 5/27 "Get Some Inflation Relief" send just yesterday, and before that the Memorial Day flood. The cadence is starting to train me to ignore them.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me one men's comfort shoe above the fold.** A single Skechers Go Walk or New Balance 877 right next to that $20 off banner turns this from a women's mailer I'm forwarding to my wife into something targeted at me.
- 2. **Fix the preview text immediately.** A raw tracking URL in the preheader is the single worst inbox signal in this email — it reads as broken and edges into spam territory. Even "Online only · Code MAY20 · expires tonight" is an enormous upgrade.
- 3. **Give the coupon a clear path to men's comfort styles.** Put a "Men's Walking Shoes" shortcut right below the Get Coupon button so I know exactly where I'm going when I click.
- 4. **Dial back the send cadence after the sale ends.** After ten emails in two weeks, the next one needs to earn its spot — a thematic reset, a new product drop, something that justifies another send.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with standard ESP scaffolding, but carries six actionable technical defects: an unresolved merge token in a tracking pixel, five content images lacking alt text, a malformed meta tag, and no dark-mode color-scheme support. The QA pipeline summary line incorrectly reports "0 warnings" despite emitting seven `[WARN]` entries — the summary counter appears to be miscounting warnings as non-issues.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unresolved merge token — HIGH**
Tracking pixel URL contains a literal `[UNIQUE]` placeholder:
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```
If this token was not substituted at send time, individual open tracking is broken for all recipients. Confirm whether the ESP substituted this before delivery; if not, open-rate data for this send is invalid.

**28 click-redirect links unprobed**
All 28 CTA/product links resolved through a click-redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP prober. Destination URLs and UTM parameter consistency cannot be confirmed from this audit. Manual spot-check of 3–5 links is recommended.

**UTM campaign parameter embedded in open-pixel URL**
```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260528_sandal_blowout_AM
```
The `t=` parameter is passing a full UTM string as a tracker value rather than a campaign ID. This is likely a custom field the ESP uses internally, but verify it is not being appended to destination URLs as a malformed double-encoded parameter.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on 5 content images — WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1**

| Image | src (truncated) |
|---|---|
| Product 1 | `.../120420_268965_SET/1?fmt=auto&$webPdpProduct$` |
| Product 2 | `.../110902_226118_SET/1?fmt=auto&$webPdpProduct$` |
| Product 3 | `.../114229_235197_SET/1?fmt=auto&$webPdpProduct$` |
| Product 4 | `.../186576_415732_SET/1?fmt=auto&$webPdpProduct$` |
| Hero/content | `...d06be110-6c0c-4df3-aeee-e04bf8e18969.jpg` |

All five render as invisible content to screen readers and in image-blocked clients (Gmail image-off, Outlook cached mode). Product images should carry descriptive alt text (`alt="Nike Air Max in White, sizes 7–12"`); decorative spacers should carry `alt=""`.

**Tracking pixels also missing alt=""**
The two emltrk.com open-pixel `<img>` tags lack `alt=""`. While functionally invisible, the absence causes some validators and strict mail clients to flag them. Add `alt=""` role="presentation"` to both.

**Malformed IE compatibility meta tag**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing required `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag is ignored by all clients and provides no IE rendering benefit.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light" />
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light" />
```
Explicitly disables dark mode. Apple Mail and iOS Mail will force-invert colors rather than applying the email's own dark-mode rules, which can invert images and break light-colored text on white backgrounds. Consider adding a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block or at minimum removing the `light`-only restriction.
