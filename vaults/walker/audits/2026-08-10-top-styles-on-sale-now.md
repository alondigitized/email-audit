# Top styles on sale now
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — this is basically the same "Top styles on sale now" email I got on August 7th, just re-run with a new date stamp. Same eight-shoe grid, same "Up to 30% off" pitch, same code. If I'm getting this twice in a week, that tells me New Balance isn't tracking who already opened the last one, which doesn't inspire confidence.
- That said, credit where it's due — this is still one of their better-built emails. Real prices, real percentages off, a proper grid I can scan in five seconds. The 574 Core at $74.99 and the 740 at $91.99 caught my eye because those are actual comfort-casual price points, not $200 running shoes I'll never lace up.
- Would I click? Maybe, just to see the 574 or 740 up close. Would I forward it? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, and honestly the repeat send makes me a little less likely to bother opening the next one.

## What's working

- The eight-shoe grid with name, list price, and sale price is exactly the kind of side-by-side comparison I want before I click anywhere.
- BTS26 for 30% off is a real, specific offer — not vague "save now" language.
- The 574 Core and 740 are priced in my comfort-casual range, which is rare for these emails.
- Clean black-and-white layout, big enough text, no clutter — easy to scan on my phone.

## What's weak

- Hero model is a young woman in cropped pants and ankle socks — that's not who's shopping for arch support and cushioning.
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere — just "top styles," which tells me nothing about fit.
- This is a near-identical repeat of the August 7th "Top styles on sale now" send — same format, same offer type, just days apart.
- No loyalty pricing or member perk called out, even though I've opened a dozen of these by now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop resending the same email format within a week — space these out or at least swap the hero shot so it doesn't feel like a glitch in my inbox.
- 2. Swap the hero image for a men's casual shoe — the 574 or 740 — shown on someone closer to my age, since those are the shoes actually priced for me.
- 3. Add one line of comfort language near the top: "cushioned, arch-supported, all-day comfort" — that's the hook that gets me to tap.
- 4. Surface loyalty/member pricing if I'm signed in — I've earned that by now given how many of these I open.

- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off comfort styles, ends Aug 20`
