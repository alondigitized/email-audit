- The one saving grace is that below the fake-cart shoe there's an actual real product grid — a men's slip-in arch fit, some men's Delson slip-ins, even a basketball shoe — plus a "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" button and a 40% off summer styles banner. - The men's product tiles further down — Arch Fit Arcade slip-ins, Delson 3.0 Roth Shoes — are actually in my wheelhouse, comfort-first slip-on stuff.
- "UP TO 40% OFF SUMMER STYLES" is a clear, specific offer with a real button and a dated window (8/14-8/19) in the fine print.
- Big blue "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" button is easy to spot and unambiguous.
- Layout is clean, no glitches or overlapping text that I can see.

## What's weak

- The hero is, once again, that pink floral D'Lites shoe I never touched — same one from at least four other emails this week, just relabeled.
- "You liked these, and you weren't wrong" is a flat-out lie for me. I never liked it, there's no cart, this isn't a real reminder of anything.
- The "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button implies I have an order in progress. I don't. That's the kind of pressure tactic that makes me trust a brand less, not more.
- No sizing, arch support, or cushioning detail anywhere near the hero product — the stuff I actually care about is buried in the small grid further down.

- 2. Lead with a men's comfort shoe I'd actually consider, like the Arch Fit Arcade slip-ins already in this email, instead of a floral women's shoe with no bearing on me.
- 3. Put the 40% off summer sale banner at the top, not buried two-thirds down — that's the one legitimately compelling thing in here.
- 4. Add one line of real product detail (cushioning, arch support, hands-free) right under the hero image.
