# Hurry! 30% Off + Free Shipping Won't Last
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, here's Crocs again with the same 30% off and free shipping I've now had on 10/2, 10/3, 10/4 and today. This one is the plainest of the bunch. Big black letters on pink and one black button. I got the point in two seconds. That's four days of the same offer, though, and "Don't Forget" and "The clock is ticking" start to feel like nagging.
- The picture is one gray clog sitting under a giant "SALE." It's a Baya-style clog. That's not what I shop for, and nothing tells me whether it's cushioned or has arch support. Nothing is slip-on friendly or men's, and there's no walking angle either. The offer is clear, but nothing in it speaks to my feet.
- Would I click? Probably not. If I needed a comfy clog I might, but the "Exclusions Apply" line makes me wonder what's left at 30% off. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The headline is huge and easy to read on my phone without zooming. 30% off plus free shipping is right there.
- There's one black "Shop the Sale" button. It's big enough to tap and I can't miss it.
- The deadline is in the fine print: Wednesday, October 7th at 11:59pm MT. That at least gives me a real date.
- The green Crocs logo at the top means I know who it's from right away.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, which is how I like it.

## What's weak

- I've seen this same offer four days running. By now "The clock is ticking" and "Hurry" don't feel urgent to me.
- The only shoe is one gray clog. I can't see whether it's cushioned or supportive, and no price or name goes with it.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the button, and the real terms ("select styles only") are in tiny type at the bottom. That feels like fine print hiding the catch.
- There's nothing for a 62-year-old man: no men's styles, no walking or slip-on comfort, and no reviews.
- The date is buried at the bottom, so the "ends soon" claim up top feels vague.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the real deadline in the headline and drop the "don't forget" nagging. Something like "Ends tomorrow, 11:59pm MT" would do. Then I'd believe it.
- 2. Show three or four actual comfort shoes with names and sale prices. A men's clog, a slip-on and a cushioned walker would suit me. Add a line on cushioning and arch support.
- 3. Say which styles are included, instead of "Exclusions Apply."
- 4. Space out the sends. Four in a row makes me tune the brand out.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tomorrow: 30% off + free shipping`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: 30% off comfy clogs and slip-ons`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Offer ends Wed 10/7 at 11:59pm MT. Men's and women's styles included.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our softest, easiest slip-ons are part of the sale. Free shipping on qualified orders.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is structurally sound: it has `lang="en"`, a viewport meta, a preheader, a mobile media query block and an Outlook-safe reset. The one confirmed defect is eight images served over plain `http://`. The source is truncated, so the body links, footer and headers could not be audited.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 20 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe because they sit on tracking/click-redirect domains (e.g. `click.crocs-email.com`). Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified.
- **Insecure image sources:** 8 images load from `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/…`:
  - `9c36c0ef-…png` (App Store badge)
  - `13246762-…png` (Google Play badge)

  - `e18949ff-…png` (Instagram)
  - `80f68cd8-…png` (Facebook)
  - `51d8144e-…png` (TikTok)
  - `1187ef58-…png` (YouTube)

  The QA report lists 7 of these 8 entries, but its text says "8 images". Only 7 are itemized, so the count is 7. The same host serves HTTPS elsewhere in the email, so the footer assets look like a template-level oversight.
- **Open pixel:** `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?I53HYEB6QCYEHGEICPVDMGGDNE.10199&d=10199&bmt=0` is HTTPS and well-formed.
- **Third-party beacon:** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=…` is a second tracking pixel from a different domain (Adobe Audience Manager). It is hidden with `visibility:hidden;display:none`, so it fires only in clients that load display:none images.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Mixed content:** The 7 `http://` images above may be blocked or rewritten by some clients and webmail proxies. These are the app-download and social icons, so they could silently disappear.
- **Missing alt on the demdex pixel:** The `<img>` has no `alt` attribute at all. It is hidden, so there is no screen-reader impact, but it is invalid markup. The first open pixel correctly uses `alt=""`.
- **Charset declaration:** In the source as supplied, `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither declaration is effective. This is worth checking against the raw source, because the pipeline may have stripped the attribute. If it is real, the UTF-8 charset relies on the `Content-Type` header alone.
- **Contradictory QA summary:** The report says "100% pass rate, 0 issue(s), 0 warning(s)" but lists 9 `[WARN]` entries. The summary counter appears not to include INFO-level warnings.
- **Fine as-is:**
  - `lang="en"` is present.
  - The viewport meta is present.
  - The preheader is hidden correctly.
  - The `-webkit-text-size-adjust` reset is present.
  - Web fonts use `font-display: swap`.
  - Alt text is present on the 7 flagged images.
