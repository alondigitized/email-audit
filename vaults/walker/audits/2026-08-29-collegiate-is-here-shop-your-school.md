# Collegiate is here! 📚 Shop your school.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- College team logos slapped on a clog — that's the whole pitch, and it's not for me. I haven't set foot on a college campus since I dropped my daughter off at hers, and unless one of these had a Nebraska Cornhuskers logo on it I wouldn't even glance twice. The model in the corner looks like she's about twenty-two, sitting sideways on a metal folding chair — nothing about this says "comfortable shoe for a 62-year-old guy," it says "dorm room decor."
- There's no price, no percentage off, nothing. Just "Find yours!" and four round buttons. I'd scroll past this in about two seconds and mark it read. Not forwarding it — well, actually, my buddy Dave went to Alabama, he'd probably get a kick out of the Crimson Tide clog, so maybe I'd shoot him a quick "look at this" text. But I'm not clicking through myself.

## What's working

- The stacked-clog product shot is clean and easy to read — six team logos lined up so you know exactly what the collection is.
- "Campus Cool" and the subhead are short and don't waste my time scrolling to figure out what's being sold.
- Four clearly labeled round buttons (Shop Clogs, Jibbitz Charms, Sale, Sandals) — big enough to tap without missing.

## What's weak

- Zero mention of cushioning, arch support, or comfort — the stuff I actually care about isn't anywhere in this email.
- No price or discount at all, so there's nothing pulling me to click versus just admiring the picture.
- The model and styling (twenty-something on a diner chair) reads college-kid, not a guy my age — I don't see myself in this one bit.
- This is another logo/theme send, same playbook as the Pokémon and NFL Crocs emails this week — team logos instead of NFL logos, same format.
- Huge blank space between the buttons and the app-store badges — feels like the email just runs out of things to say halfway through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the lead visual for a comfort angle — show the sole, call out the cushioning, and use a shopper who looks like an actual campus visitor (parent, alum, tailgater), not a runway model on a chair.
- 2. Put a real number in the subject or hero — "20% off Collegiate clogs" beats "Shop your school" every time for me.
- 3. Trim the dead space in the middle of the email — it makes the email feel unfinished and gives me nothing to do but keep scrolling.
- 4. If Crocs wants boomers in the door, run a "Big Ten alum sale" angle with actual comfort/fit copy alongside the team logos — pure novelty won't get my click.

- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off team-logo Crocs, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six schools, one comfortable clog — shop the lineup.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New team charms just dropped for game day.`
