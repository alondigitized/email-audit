# PR unlocked in adidas AdiZero Pacer 🔓 🏃
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- I've been getting Famous Footwear emails every day this week — at this point I recognize the sender before I read the subject. But this one stopped me, and not in a good way. "PR unlocked" — I had to read it twice before I figured out they mean Personal Record, like a running time. I'm 62, I walk for exercise, I'm not training for a 5K. The AdiZero Pacer is a performance running shoe, the kind of thing you'd see on someone at a track meet. That's not me. The preview text was a raw web link — I thought my phone was glitching.
- Inside the email I got a "Do real things" headline over a guy who looks like he's finishing a sprint, a grid of adidas fashion sneakers that looked like the Y2K stuff from yesterday's email, and then — buried below the fold — a 25% off clearance box that's actually useful. The 3X points app banner is recycled from a send I already got this week. I've seen that banner three times now.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it. If they had leaned into the clearance offer or pointed me toward something cushioned and walkable, I might have bitten. Instead I got a shoe built for people who track their pace.

## What's working

- The 25% off clearance banner is visible and the offer is real — that's the one hook I'd respond to.
- Famous Footwear is a brand I know and trust, so I opened it despite the garbage preheader.
- SHOP CROCS is down in the navigation row — smart to surface it, even if it's buried.

## What's weak

- The preheader is a raw tracking URL. On my phone it looked like spam. That's a near-dealbreaker before I even open it.
- "PR unlocked in adidas AdiZero Pacer" — a performance running shoe with running-PR messaging is not my aisle. I'm shopping for comfort and cushioning, not split times.
- The 3X points app banner is the same creative I saw in yesterday's email — at eleven sends in seven days, recycling creative feels lazy.
- "Do real things" is a vague brand slogan that tells me nothing about fit, feel, or price.
- The clearance offer is halfway down the email; on mobile I'd have to scroll to find the thing that might actually get me to buy.

- 2. **Lead with the clearance offer, not the performance shoe hero.** Move the 25% off box above the fold. That's what gets a walker like me to stay.
- 3. **Reframe the shoe copy around comfort, not performance.** Even if the AdiZero Pacer has cushioning, I see nothing about that here. "Built for your walk, not just the race" does more for me than "Do real things."
- 4. **Retire the 3X points app banner until there's something new to say.** Three sends with the same creative trains me to ignore it.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed charset meta tag (bug)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. Without that attribute the charset declaration is ignored by parsers. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Risk: character encoding falls back to client default; non-ASCII content (e.g., accented characters, emoji in subject line `🔓 🏃`) may render as mojibake in older Outlook versions.

**CSS property typo**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
`lineapacing` is not a valid CSS property (likely intended `line-height` or `letter-spacing`). All renderers will silently ignore it. The affected `<td>` loses its intended spacing.

**20 images missing alt text**
QA flagged 20 images with no `alt` attribute (distinct from `alt=""`). Full list includes product shots, divider GIF (`f7fd3d23...gif`), and promotional images. Screen readers will read out raw filenames or skip the element entirely depending on client. Affected sources (sample):
- `c9220297-b6ad-4211-8af3-8aa050fd985a.jpg`
- `f7fd3d23-0d7f-4739-9b26-32e89ade8dc4.gif`
- `1d721066-09a8-437b-8ad7-c4337048a15c.jpg`
- _(+17 more — see full QA list)_

**`user-scalable=0` (minor)**
Viewport uses `user-scalable=0` (integer). Spec value is `yes`/`no`. Most mobile browsers treat `0` as `no`, so functional impact is low, but it's non-compliant syntax.
