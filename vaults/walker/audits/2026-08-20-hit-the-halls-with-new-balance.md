# Hit the Halls With New Balance
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- New Balance in the subject line got my attention for half a second — that's a brand I actually wear — but then I open it and it's a young woman in a pink "Player's Club" sweatshirt sitting on the floor next to a shoe I can't even identify by name, just "408 SNEAKERS." No idea what that means or if it's a men's shoe. Scroll down and it's the exact same back-to-school playbook Shoe Carnival's been running all week — teenage girl in school hallway for the Nike sale, "Family Sale," kids' pricing called out bigger than adult pricing.
- There's a New Balance 515 in the "Trending" strip near the bottom, and that's honestly the only thing in this whole email aimed at a guy like me — small, buried, no price, no arch support or comfort language anywhere.
- I'm not clicking. This is the same email I saw on the 15th and the 8th with a different headline pasted on top, and I said as much then. Not forwarding it either — nothing here is a deal I'd want to tell a friend about.

## What's working

- New Balance in the subject line and a New Balance logo up top — that's a brand I trust and actually shop.
- The rewards banner (2-3X points, spend $100 get $10) is at least clear and not buried in fine print.
- Coupon codes ($20 off $99.98+, $10 off $74.98+) are legible and usable in-store, which matters to a guy who still likes to try shoes on.
- The New Balance 515 shown near the bottom is a shoe I'd actually consider — clean, simple, no loud colorway.

## What's weak

- The hero model is a young woman, and the shoe is labeled by a product number ("408 SNEAKERS") instead of a name — means nothing to me.
- Nike "Family Sale" section is a teenager in a school hallway again — that's three back-to-school sends in a row with the same visual, I'm not the parent buying these.
- "Athletics Sale for the Family" — Jordan/adidas/Converse — again nothing men's-casual or comfort-focused, just kids and team sports gear.
- Not one word about cushioning, arch support, or slip-on ease anywhere in this email — the stuff that actually decides whether I buy a shoe.
- Zero personalization — no "Walker," no nod to what I've bought before, just a mass blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with an actual men's New Balance walking or comfort shoe on a man my age, not a young woman in a sweatshirt — give the shoe a real name, not a SKU number.
- 2. Cut the third "family/back-to-school" themed send in a row — segment the list so guys like me get a comfort/casual-focused email instead of the school lookbook.
- 3. Call out arch support, cushioning, or "all-day comfort" in the copy near the hero shoe — that's the actual purchase driver for me.
- 4. Move the New Balance 515 (or a men's equivalent) up near the top instead of burying it after two unrelated sales blocks.
- 5. Use the coupon codes as the headline hook, not an afterthought at the bottom — "$20 off $99.98+" is a real reason to open, "Hit the Halls" isn't.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **4 images missing `alt` text**: two content images (`9521013e-7444-4804-9257-f83e6db7b8bc.jpg`, `a97db5d3-cfac-4b76-923a-ca38510bf3a6.jpg`) and two tracking-pixel images (the `emltrk.com` pixels noted above).
