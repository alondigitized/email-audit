# Starting today: up to 25% off select shoes. 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. New Balance has been flogging this same 25% off select shoes sale since at least May 11 — I've opened four versions of this email now, and this one lands on a Wednesday with "Starting today" plastered on it. That freshness signal got my attention at the inbox level, and the preview "Offer ends Wednesday" at least gives me a hard stop date, which is more than I got from the May 21 version. So I did open it.
- Then I scroll in and I'm looking at the same stack of shoe boxes and shopping bag from the May 18 members send. Still not a shoe in sight in the hero — just packaging. Below it, the copy does its job: "Up to 25% off select shoes, and up to 30% off select clothing." That's clear enough. But what actually stopped my thumb was the "More to explore" section — there's a Fresh Foam Arishi v4 with Bungee Lace staring at me. Bungee lace. That's hands-free. That's exactly my speed. That should be the hero, not a paper bag and some cardboard boxes.
- Would I click? Maybe, for the Arishi — but I'd have to scroll past a generic "Shop now" button and navigate to Men on my own to even find it. I wouldn't forward this. My buddy Frank doesn't need to see New Balance's sixth promotional email about a sale that never seems to end.

## What's working

- **"Starting today" + "Offer ends Wednesday" combination** — at the inbox level this reads like a fresh, time-limited window, not a stale carryover. That's a meaningful upgrade from the May 21 "Take up to 25% off" subject that had no urgency framing.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — the email is easy to scan on a phone. Big text, clear sections, nothing fighting for attention. I didn't have to squint.
- **Men / Women / Kids shop tabs** — I can immediately hit "Men" and get to my section without hunting through a homepage.
- **Fresh Foam Arishi v4 Bungee Lace with Top Strap showing up in the lower grid** — bungee lace means I don't have to bend over to put them on. That detail is buried but it's the most relevant product New Balance could have shown me.
- **No broken images or layout issues** — renders clean start to finish, which I notice because I've seen some messy ones from other brands this week.

## What's weak

- **Hero image is shoe boxes and a shopping bag — again** — I flagged this exact image on the May 18 members email. I'm looking at packaging, not shoes. Show me a shoe on a foot, ideally on someone who looks like they're walking a trail, not modeling a lookbook.
- **"Select shoes" is doing a lot of vague work** — I never know if the shoes I actually want are included or excluded until I click through and feel the disappointment. That fine-print loop has burned me before.
- **The Arishi with Bungee Lace is buried in a secondary grid below the fold** — the one product that speaks directly to what I care about (hands-free, slip-on, no bending) is treated as an afterthought.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment** — I'm a subscriber, presumably a member. The May 18 send explicitly called me out as a member with a special offer. This one treats me like a cold prospect off the street.
- **Sale fatigue is real** — this is the fourth time I've seen this exact sale framework since May 11. "Starting today" helps, but the goodwill is wearing thin.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the Arishi Bungee Lace the hero** — lead with the product that speaks directly to low-effort wearability. "No-bend fit, cushioned comfort — now 25% off" is a better headline than "Up to 25% off select shoes." A man my age wants to know immediately that this shoe is built for him.
- 2. **Retire the shoe boxes hero image** — any shoe photo is better than packaging. A real person walking in the shoe on concrete or a trail would close more sales.
- 3. **Include a member callout in the body** — even a single line like "As a New Balance member, your discount applies automatically" would make me feel like the email knows who I am.
- 4. **Name a specific deadline date, not just "Wednesday"** — "Offer ends Wednesday, June 3" lands harder than a day name, especially when I'm opening this Thursday evening and have to do calendar math.
- 5. **Rotate the sale framing** — four emails with "25% off select shoes" trains me to ignore them. Package it differently: a countdown, a "last day" narrative, or a comfort-feature angle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bungee Lace, Fresh Foam — 25% off through Wednesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 25% off window closes Wednesday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh Foam Arishi slip-ons and more — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Men's comfort styles included — ends midnight Wednesday`

## Technical Audit — New Balance "Up to 25% Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard HTML 4.01 Transitional template with dark mode support. Two confirmed code-level defects are present (a duplicate CSS class collision and unfilled template variable), and several structural issues reduce rendering reliability across clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML source is truncated before body links appear. The following can be confirmed from the `<head>`:

- **Mixed-content risk**: `.bgwhite` references a background image over `http://` (plain HTTP): `background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png')`. If the email is opened in a client that enforces HTTPS, this image will be blocked as mixed content.
- Full click-tracking and redirect-chain audit is not possible from the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS class collision — `.bg1` defined twice:**
```css
/* Definition 1 (line ~early) */
.bg1 {background-color: #ffffff;}

/* Definition 2 (line ~later, overrides) */
.bg1 {background:#58546d;}
```
Any element using `.bg1` will render with a dark purple-gray background (`#58546d`) rather than white. This is almost certainly unintended and will affect visual layout in all clients.

**`@font-face` declarations are empty shells — no `src:` property:**
```css
@font-face { font-family: proxima-nova,sans-serif; font-weight: 700; font-style: normal; }
```
None of the nine `@font-face` blocks include a `src:` URL. These declarations are inert — they register nothing. If proxima-nova and linotype-didot load at all, it is via a separate CDN `<link>` tag not visible in the truncated source. If that tag is absent, all type falls back to Arial/Times New Roman.

**Malformed IE compatibility meta tag:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`. Inconsequential for modern clients but technically invalid.

**Dark mode support is present** — `color-scheme: light dark` in both `<html>` and `<meta>`, and a `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present (truncated). This is correct practice.

**Alt text audit** — not possible from truncated source.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unfilled template variable confirmed:**
```css
.bgwhite { background-image: url('http://image.XXXXX.png'); }
```
`XXXXX` is a placeholder that was never substituted. If `.bgwhite` is applied to any container, it will attempt to load a broken URL and fail silently (background simply won't render). This should be a pre-send QA gate failure.

No subscriber-level merge tokens (`{{first_name}}`, `%%EMAIL%%`, etc.) are visible in the truncated `<head>`. Cannot confirm presence or absence in the body.
