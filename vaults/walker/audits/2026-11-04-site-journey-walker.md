# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-11-04**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through most of the browsing, but I never got a shoe into the cart. At step 7 I pressed Add to Cart without picking a size, and the page told me "Please select a size." The cart badge stayed at 0. Step 8, the cart page, was a completely blank white screen, so I couldn't tell whether the cart worked at all. The login step also showed nothing, and I never looked logged in. The browsing part is decent, with big readable menus and a "Hands Free Slip-ins" category up front. A shopper who can't see his cart, or whether he's signed in, will go buy from Hoka or Brooks instead.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** That's the same as yesterday, so no improvement and no regression in the score.

- Browsing, search and the product page work well enough to earn some credit.
- The cart is a blank page and the login never visibly worked, so the checkout path is unverified and probably broken.

I only have yesterday's score, not its details. I can't say which specific issues changed.

## 3. What's Working

- **Menu (step 4):** The Men menu is easy on a 62-year-old. The text is large, the rows are tall and well spaced, and the arrows are clear. "Comfort Technologies" is in the list, which is what I'm shopping for.
- **Men's Shoes page (step 5):** The row of tiles at the top starts with "Hands Free Slip-Ins," then "Walking Shoes," then Boots. That's close to my wish list. The Filter, Sort by, Size and Width buttons are easy to tap.
- **Size picker (step 6):** The size buttons are big and well spaced. Sold-out sizes are crossed out with a bell for restock alerts, which is a nice touch.
- **Product page price and rating:** The $165 price, the 4.5 stars and the 184 reviews are all up top. The "From $15/month" line is easy to read.
- **Search (step 9):** Searching "comfort shoes" gave 1,656 results, and the first ones are real comfort shoes: "Slip-ins: Contour Fit – Cozy Fit Luxe Comfort" and "Relaxed Fit: Parallel Lite." The product names include comfort terms.
- **Top nav:** The row with Slip-ins, Wide Fit and Arch Fit speaks directly to me.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Blank cart (step 8):** The page is white with no header, no items and no message. I'd assume the site is broken and leave.
- **Add to Cart failed (step 7):** The red "Please select a size" message is clear enough. But I stayed on the same screen, the badge still showed 0, and nothing led me back to the sizes. No size is pre-selected, so a first tap on the big blue button fails.
- **Login not visible (step 3):** The screenshot looks identical to the homepage. I didn't see a sign-in form or a "Hi, Walker" greeting. The search results still say "20% OFF or Log In for 25% OFF!", so I was clearly still logged out. I saw nothing personalized anywhere.
- **Popups (step 2):** The only thing I can say is that none got in the way. The banner copy switched from "Work & Safety" to "Apparel & Bags," so the top promo rotates. It's also not what I'm shopping for.
- **Homepage hero (step 1):** The first screen is a loud pop-art Britto collab with a woman in leggings. It has nothing for a man who wants cushioning and arch support, and "Shop" is a tiny link.
- **Small text:** The top banners, the "Details" links and the red promo line in search are around 11–12 px. I'd have to squint or zoom.
- **Cut-off nav:** The top row ends at "Max Cus…", so I don't know there are more options to swipe to.
- **Search results skew women's:** The first two results are labeled Women's, and the men's filter isn't on by default.
- **Product page:** The page says "Excluded from promotions," while the banner says "Members Save 25% OFF." That's confusing, and it makes me trust the site less. I also saw no comparison with Hoka, Brooks or New Balance, and no price-vs-quality math.
- **Men's Shoes grid:** The first shoes shown are the colorful Britto collab sneakers, not comfort walkers.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** It should always show a header, the items, a total and a checkout button, or at least an "empty cart" message. A blank screen should never happen.
2. **Make Add to Cart work or guide me.** Scroll to the size row and highlight it when I tap without a size, or pre-select my usual size for returning shoppers. Update the cart badge right away.
3. **Make login obvious.** After signing in, greet me by name, show my member price, and change the "Log In for 25% OFF" lines. Drop the contradictory "Excluded from promotions" label, or explain it.
4. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Lead with Slip-ins, Arch Fit, Wide Fit and walking shoes in a men's slot, not a fashion collab.
5. **Make text bigger.** Aim for at least 14–16 px on banners and promo lines. Show a "scroll for more" cue on the nav row.
6. **Respect what I've picked.** Search should honor Men's if I've been browsing Men's.
7. **Add trust content.** A short "why Arch Fit," cushioning ratings and a price-per-mile line would help me compare against Hoka and Brooks.

## 6. Bottom Line

I like the menus, the hands-free category and the readable size buttons, and search found real comfort shoes. But I couldn't buy anything: the cart was blank and the site never showed me I was signed in. Until the cart works and login does something, I'm buying elsewhere. Same 3/10 as yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The data is partial, and several signals suggest the crawl was degraded by bot mitigation. It supports a few firm findings and many "could not verify" items.

- **Bot protection throttled the session.** Six of the seven network errors are HTTP 429 on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.973`), and the seventh is a 429 on `/cart/`. The View Cart step probably received a challenge or error shell rather than the real cart (see Accessibility).
- **Navigation likely did not occur.** The Log In and Men category samples report URL `https://www.skechers.com/`, and their HTML is identical to the homepage's. Their TTFB (341ms) is also identical to the homepage's. These steps probably re-measured the homepage, so their per-step results are unreliable.
- **A GTM preview container is loaded on what looks like production.** The script URL is `gtm.js?id=GTM-TDRBQZ&gtm_auth=…&gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_cookies_win=x`. That is the GTM environment-snapshot form, so the page is serving a non-live container version.
- **LCP and CLS are missing for all 8 steps.** Core Web Vitals could not be assessed.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations across 3 steps.

**Homepage (9)**
- **Critical, `button-name`:** one or more buttons have no accessible name.
- **Critical, `aria-required-parent`:** child roles (for example `listitem` or `tab`) sit outside their required parent role.
- **Serious, `link-name`:** one or more links have no discernible text.
- **Landmark structure:** `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region` all fail. The likely cause is more than one `<main>`, with `<main>` and `<aside>` nested inside other landmarks. The page also has content outside landmarks.
- **Minor, `aria-allowed-role`:** a role is set on an element that doesn't allow it.

**View Cart (4)**
- `document-title`, `html-has-lang`, `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` all fail.
- Together these indicate a bare document with no title, `lang`, `<main>` or `<h1>`. That matches a 429 or challenge response rather than a real cart template. The homepage does set `<html lang="en">`.
- Treat this as an artifact of the throttled `/cart/` request, not a confirmed cart-page defect. It needs a re-run to verify.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4)**
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor), `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`.
- These repeat the homepage landmark problems, which points to a shared header or footer template.

**Not covered:** Log In, Men category, Men > Shoes, Product detail and Add to Cart report no violations. Given the identical HTML for the first two, I wouldn't read that as clean.

- The sample shows no `<meta name="viewport">`, but the `<head>` is truncated, so this is not verified.
- Touch-target size, tap delay and responsive images were not captured.
- Unnamed buttons and links (`button-name`, `link-name`) hit mobile screen-reader users hardest, because icon-only controls are common in mobile navigation.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (7):** six are 429 on the Kasada fingerprint endpoint. The seventh is 429 on `https://www.skechers.com/cart/`. The cart 429 is the most important item. It either breaks the checkout path for automated clients, or it is a rate limit our own repeated requests triggered.

**Console (27 total, 9 shown, repeating patterns):**
- **429 resource failures:** these match the network errors above.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document`:** a script registers an `unload` handler under a Permissions-Policy that disallows it. This is typical of third-party tags. It also makes the page ineligible for the back/forward cache (bfcache), which hurts back-navigation performance on mobile.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…`:** a script calls Chrome's built-in Summarizer API without a supported language. The source is unidentified. It could be a tag or a browser feature, and it does no harm beyond console noise.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the bot challenge handled.** Pace requests, use a warmed profile, or have Skechers allowlist the monitor. Then re-test `/cart/`, because the current View Cart findings are likely artifacts.
2. **Fix journey navigation capture.** Log In and Men category should record their own URLs and HTML. Add an assertion that the URL changed.
3. **Fix Web Vitals capture.** Start the `PerformanceObserver` with `buffered: true` (via an init script), and report the missing LCP and CLS values instead of a placeholder `?`.
4. **Site fixes (priority order):**
   - Give every icon-only button and link an accessible name (critical `button-name` and serious `link-name`).
   - Fix the ARIA parent/child roles behind `aria-required-parent`.
   - Use a single top-level `<main>`, and keep `<aside>` at the top level.
   - Give duplicate landmarks unique labels.
   - Put page content inside landmarks.
5. **Check the GTM environment.** Confirm that `gtm_preview=env-1` is meant for production. If not, publish the live container.
6. **Remove `unload` handlers** from third-party tags, or switch to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`, to restore bfcache eligibility.
7. **Trim third-party tags.** Audit the pixels for overlap and defer them until after interaction or consent. Add SRI where the vendor supports it.
