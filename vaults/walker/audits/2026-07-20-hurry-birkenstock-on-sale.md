# Hurry! Birkenstock on sale 🤯
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Well, "Birkenstock" in the subject line is usually enough to get me to tap — and I did. I've had a pair of their Arizona sandals for six summers, and just five days ago Famous Footwear sent me one called "Are you stocked on Birkenstock?" that I actually enjoyed opening. So I came in with good faith. But before I even opened this one, whatever my inbox showed me instead of a preview line was a jumble of letters and a web address. Not a sentence. Not a deal. A URL. That's a cold bucket of water before the first click.
- Once I'm inside, the email isn't bad. There's a real product — the Birkenstock sandal grid about midway down looks exactly like what I browse on a Sunday afternoon — and "Limited time price drops" is language I respond to. The BOGO banner at the very top confused me for a second because it felt like leftover creative from the last four emails I've gotten this week, not a fresh Birkenstock-specific pitch.
- I'd mark this one read and probably tap "Shop Birkenstock" — but I'd do it despite the execution, not because of it. I wouldn't forward it; I'd just quietly go look at the Arizonas myself.

## What's working

- **Birkenstock is in the subject** — that brand name alone earns the open from me, especially since I already know I like their sandals and I'm in the market.
- **The sandal product grid is clean and readable** — I can actually see the styles (Arizona silhouettes, the Boston clog) without squinting; the layout works on a phone screen.
- **"Earn 6x points" is visible above the Birkenstock CTA** — I'm a Rewards member and that's a real hook when I'm already planning to buy.
- **"Limited time price drops" is clear enough** — it tells me the price is actually reduced, not just featured.
- **"Shop Birkenstock" button is easy to tap** — big enough, not buried, no guesswork about where it goes.

## What's weak

- **The preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL** — worst first impression possible; for a second I thought the email was spam or broken.
- **BOGO is the hero above the fold, but this is supposed to be a Birkenstock email** — the brand story gets buried under a repeated campaign banner I've now seen from Famous Footwear at least three times this week.
- **No end date on "limited time"** — I've been burned before clicking "limited time" deals only to find the same price a week later; a real date would pull me in faster.
- **No men's-specific callout** — the Arizona comes in men's widths and I'd love to see that called out; right now I'm staring at generic sandal photography without knowing if my size/width is part of this drop.
- **The 🤯 emoji in the subject** — I'm not twenty-five; that reads as noise to me, not excitement.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — whatever broke the preview text (it's showing a click-tracking URL in my inbox) needs to be corrected before the next send. A line like *"Classic styles, limited-time prices — shop now"* would do the job.
- 2. **Lead with Birkenstock, not BOGO** — if the subject is Birkenstock, the hero image should be a Birkenstock Arizona or Boston, not the generic BOGO badge. The mismatch feels like I was bait-switched.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 22" would make "limited time" actually mean something and get me to act today instead of bookmarking it.
- 4. **Call out men's sizing and widths** — a line like "Available in men's wide widths" is worth more to me than any emoji.
- 5. **Rest the BOGO banner or integrate it** — I've seen that exact creative four times this week from Famous Footwear. Give me something fresh or tie it directly to Birkenstock pricing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Birkenstock price drops — limited time`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale: Birkenstock Arizonas + more, ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Classic sandals, real markdowns — shop men's styles now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited-time drops on Birkenstock — including wide widths`

**UTM visibility** — UTM parameters are not readable in the encoded `?qs=` payloads without decoding. Cannot confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` are being passed through. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — 31 images missing meaningful alt text.** Every product and decorative image carries `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. With images blocked (default in Outlook, Gmail Android), the email is a blank column of white space. Affected files include the hero GIF (`721856e1`), all product shots, and CTA badges. The logo image (`3b6a7dc1`) is wrapped in a link with `alt=""` — it renders as an anonymous clickable region when images are off.

**Invalid charset declaration.**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="Content-Type"`. The charset meta is not recognized by clients that enforce strict parsing. Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />
```

**Non-standard viewport value.**
```html
maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0
```
`user-scalable=0` is non-standard; the valid values are `yes`/`no`. Some parsers ignore `0`, leaving pinch-zoom unlocked unexpectedly.

**CSS typo — invalid property.**
```html
style="...lineapacing:20px..."
```
`lineapacing` does not exist; this declaration is silently dropped. Likely intended `line-height` or `letter-spacing`.

**No `<title>` element** in `<head>`. Minor, but some clients display it in tab/window titles.
