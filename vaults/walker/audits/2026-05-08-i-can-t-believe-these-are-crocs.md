# “I can’t believe these are Crocs”.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- Crocs isn't my brand — I'm a Skechers Slip-ins guy, full stop — but I'll give them a fair shake. The 15% off offer jumps right out at the top of the email, and I appreciate that they led with something concrete. The lifestyle photo looks like a real family enjoying the outdoors, and I can spot some folks closer to my age in there, which is refreshing. But a lot of this email doesn't feel like it was aimed at me specifically. The Jibbitz Charms section? That's for teenagers decorating their shoes — not my concern. The preview text in my inbox was completely broken, just a bunch of gibberish characters, which almost made me not open this at all. Overall it's a decent promotional email for a brand I recognize, but it never makes me feel like they know who I am or what I actually want in a shoe.

## What's working

- The **15% off** headline is clean and immediately readable — no hunting around for the deal. I like that it's right at the top. The **Recovery Slide** showing up in "Top Trending Styles" is the one product that actually speaks to my world; that's the kind of easy, comfortable footwear I'm open to. The **star rating** on the best-selling Classic is a nice touch — I trust customer reviews more than marketing copy. The Afterpay module is thoughtfully placed for someone like me who watches spending; paying over time on a smaller shoe purchase is actually reasonable.

## What's weak

- The **preview text** was a disaster. All I saw in my inbox was "Crocs" and then a wall of jumbled nonsense characters. That almost cost them the open. For a 62-year-old looking at a small phone screen, that reads as broken or suspicious.
- The **Jibbitz Charms section** — I had to stop and think about what I was even looking at. Shoe charms for adults? That's not me. It dilutes the email and pushes comfort content further down.
- The email **doesn't feel like it knows I'm a man** shopping for comfort. The lifestyle photo has women, kids, younger folks front and center. I see some older adults in there, but it reads family/female-forward, not like they pulled my record and said "hey Walker, here's the men's comfort lineup."
- There's also **no urgency on the 15% offer** — "your next order" doesn't tell me when it expires. I might bookmark this in my head and then forget it by the time I go to their site.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately.** Those spacer characters are leaking and it looks broken. Use real copy — even something like "Comfort that surprises people — 15% off inside" would work.
- 2. **Segment the men's module more prominently.** If I'm browsing Men's, show me men's shoes first, with men's lifestyle imagery. Swap out the family photo hero for something featuring a man my age being active and comfortable.
- 3. **Put a deadline on that 15% offer.** "Expires Sunday" or "Valid through this weekend" would push me to act instead of tabling it.
- 4. **Lead with the Recovery Slide or Mega Crush for a comfort-focused audience.** That's the product that might actually convert a non-Crocs guy like me.
- 5. **Drop the Jibbitz section** for this send, or move it to a separate email targeting younger buyers. It's noise for me.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is structurally sound with standard ESP click-wrapping and Adobe/Salesforce Marketing Cloud personalization infrastructure. Primary issues are mixed HTTP/HTTPS image assets and a plain-text body that is nearly all redirect URLs, which will trigger spam filters.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken destination links detected** (36 click-redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing — expected for wrapped tracking domains on `click.crocs-email.com`).

**Issue — Plain-text version is ~80% raw URLs:**

This is expected behavior, not a bug, but the fallback stack should be confirmed as acceptable.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Issue — Raw email address exposed in image request URLs:**

The iGoDigital recommendation slots use the literal subscriber email in the URL path:
```
https://10745993.recs.igodigital.com/rr/v2/65555c8fe5857c003bf95ed1/i/1/WALKER@AGENTMAIL.TO?category=clog
```

`WALKER@AGENTMAIL.TO` is the resolved value here (this is the test/seed address). In production this pattern means the recipient's email address is transmitted in plaintext in the image request URL, which is logged by any proxy, CDN, or mail client image proxy (e.g., Google Image Proxy, Apple MPP). This is a PII-in-URL anti-pattern — a hashed or opaque subscriber ID should be used instead.

No unresolved merge token syntax (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`, etc.) was detected in the truncated source.
