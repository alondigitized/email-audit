# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- Well, "we noticed you noticing us" — cute, but I didn't put anything in a cart that I remember, so my first thought is "did I really, or is this just a template?" That white and black Slip-in up top, the Contour Foam one, is actually a decent-looking shoe — clean lines, not one of those neon monstrosities I keep getting stuck with. But then it's the same grab-bag I've come to expect from this brand: kids' Bunnyhop basketball shoes in electric blue, women's floral D'Lites, more slip-ons scattered around like they're hoping something sticks.
- The "here's more you'll love" section is where it falls apart for me — I don't love five of those six shoes, I just happen to shop the same brand as people who do. And burying a 25% off KIDS sale near the bottom, after a men's cart-recovery pitch, tells me this thing was assembled by committee, not personalized for me specifically.
- I'd click on the "Complete Your Order" button if I actually recognized that shoe as something I put in a cart — but since I don't recall doing that, it feels more like guesswork than a real reminder. Not forwarding this to anybody. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The hero shoe — Skechers Slip-ins Contour Foam — is a legitimate men's-looking casual shoe with cushioning I can actually picture on my foot.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button is big, blue, easy to tap, no squinting required.
- Two of the six "more options" shoes (the Delson 3.0 Roth and the Max Cushioning Arch Fit Azusah) are actually men's styles with real soles, not the usual kids/women's clutter.
- Layout is clean and scannable — headers are big, sections are separated with dividers so I'm not lost scrolling.

## What's weak

- I don't remember putting anything in a cart, so "your cart is still waiting" feels like a stretch, not a real nudge.
- Half the "recommended" shoes are women's and kids' styles that have nothing to do with me — the floral D'Lites, the neon basketball shoe, none of that is my lane.
- The 25% off Back-to-School kids sale is buried near the bottom after a men's-adjacent pitch — if that's the real offer, lead with it or leave it for a separate email.
- No price shown anywhere on the actual cart item — if this is supposed to close a sale, tell me what I'd be paying.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show the actual price (and any savings) on the abandoned-cart shoe — that's the whole point of an abandoned-cart email, and it's missing.
- 2. Cut the "more options" grid down to men's styles only when the customer's cart item is a men's shoe — mixing in kids' and women's items just proves the algorithm isn't paying attention.
- 3. Either drop the kids sale into its own email or move it up top if that's actually the intended hero offer — right now it's a footnote to its own promotion.
- 4. Add a returning-customer touch — "based on your last order" or similar — so it feels like they know me, not just a mail-merge.
