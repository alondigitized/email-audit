# STARTS NOW! $10 OFF >>>
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Now this one I can actually use. "$10 OFF $49" tiered up to "$60 off $199" is a real, tiered offer that doesn't ask me to do math — that's a change from the fuzzy "up to 40% off adidas" games DSW's been running all week. But then it drops me right into "So New, So You" — five pairs of women's heels, snakeskin slingbacks, and leopard booties, plus one kids' sneaker. Out of six product tiles, exactly zero are men's comfort shoes.
- I'll give them credit for the "shop by size" grid up top and the wide-width/wide-calf buttons — that's useful, practical stuff. But there's nothing here for a guy who wants a cushioned slip-on. I'm not clicking through a wall of stilettos to hunt for something in my size. This one gets deleted, not forwarded — the offer's good, the merchandising just isn't for me.

## What's working

- The tiered dollar-off offer ($10/$20/$60) is concrete and easy to understand at a glance — no percentage math required.
- "Shop by Size" and "Shop Wide Width / Wide Calf" buttons are genuinely useful — that's the kind of practical filter I actually want.
- Clean black-and-white layout with the mint green pop on the pricing — easy to read, nothing cramped.
- Top banner calls out Birkenstock savings clearly, so I know there's a specific brand deal even if it's not my thing.
- "SHOP MEN" link is right there in the nav bar — at least the option exists.

## What's weak

- Every single "So New, So You" product is women's — heels, slingbacks, leopard booties — nothing that looks like it's aimed at me.
- No men's comfort or walking shoe shown anywhere in the whole email, despite a "SHOP MEN" link existing.
- "Claim $10 OFF" line just sits there with no visible button under it — looks like a dead link in this rendering.
- The offer code BACKPACK and all the real terms are buried down in a wall of gray fine print at the bottom — takes real digging to confirm it applies to what I'd buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this by shopper — if DSW knows I've browsed men's comfort shoes before, swap at least half that product grid for men's Skechers, Hoka, or New Balance styles instead of an all-women's lineup.
- 2. Fix or clarify the "Claim $10 OFF" element so it's an actual visible button, not orphaned text.
- 3. Pull the promo code and key terms (minimum spend, dates) up near the offer instead of burying them in the footer legal block.
- 4. Add one comfort-focused product row — even just "men's slip-ons" or "arch support picks" — to make the offer feel relevant to more than one demographic.
