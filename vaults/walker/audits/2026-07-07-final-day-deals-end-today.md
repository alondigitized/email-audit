# Final Day Deals End TODAY!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Seven emails in seven days. I wasn't expecting to feel much when this one hit my inbox, but "Final Day Deals End TODAY!" at least told me something was ending — which is more clarity than that back-to-school pivot yesterday gave me. Then I opened it and the very first thing my eyes landed on was a countdown clock above "Women's & Junior MEGA SALE." I'm a 62-year-old guy. I don't have junior anything in my life, and I haven't bought women's shoes since Mother's Day sometime in the last decade.
- Scrolled down and there they were — Skechers Slip-ins at 25% off, Crocs sandals at $39.98. Now that's my language. Those two mid-email sections are the only reason I stayed past the hero. Add in the $10 off $75+ coupon near the bottom and there's actually something here for me — I just had to dig through a Women's sale page to find it. That's backwards.
- And the preview text in my inbox was a raw tracking URL. Looked like a tech glitch. Bad first impression before I even tapped to open. I'll mark this read and maybe circle back for the Slip-ins if my feet are grumbling — but I'm not forwarding this to a single soul.

## What's working

- Countdown timer at 12:59:59 creates real urgency — if it's genuinely the last day, I feel the clock
- Skechers Slip-ins 25% off is squarely my comfort zone; hands-free and discounted is exactly how I want to hear about shoes
- Crocs sandals at $39.98 is a specific, honest price point — I cross-shop Crocs and that number makes sense to me
- $10 off $75+ coupon with a scannable barcode near the bottom is a concrete, usable saving
- 2.50X points section connects to their running 250-year campaign — at least there's creative continuity across the week

## What's weak

- The entire hero is "Women's & Junior MEGA SALE" — I'm the last man on earth that offer was written for
- "IN STORE ONLY!" is prominently disclosed in the hero, but I do most of my browsing on my phone; the biggest offer in the email effectively doesn't apply to how I actually shop
- Preview text was a raw tracking URL in my inbox — sloppy, and it made me think the email was broken before I opened it
- Seventh straight day of Shoe Carnival; the cadence is starting to wear on me more than any pair of shoes would
- The hero model is a woman in a blue floral dress — even subconsciously, I don't see anyone like me above the fold

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with men's product** — even one row. "Men's Comfort & Slip-ons — Final Day, From $39.98" would have me clicking immediately instead of scrolling past the women's shoot.
- 2. **Fix the preheader** — a leaked tracking URL in the preview is an embarrassing technical failure that costs first impressions and requires exactly zero budget to repair.
- 3. **Clarify online vs. in-store scope** — if the 50%/60% applies online too, say so; if it doesn't, stop leading with it to a subscriber who clearly shops on his phone.
- 4. **Segment by gender before daily-blasting** — I've presumably bought men's shoes every transaction; this email should be leading with men's product, not tunneling me through a junior sale to get there.
- 5. **Give Skechers Slip-ins more real estate** — it's buried mid-email but it's the most relevant offer for someone like me; that section deserves hero treatment, not afterthought positioning.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last Chance: Men's Slip-ons & Sandals 25% Off Today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final Hours — Comfort Shoes Up to 60% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Slip-ins, Crocs sandals & $10 off your order — ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your final-day picks: Slip-ins 25% off + $10 off $75+. Today only.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The four Amplience product images are almost certainly linked CTAs — missing alt text means screen readers announce nothing and images-off clients show blank boxes with no fallback label.

The hero/banner image (`465aca37...png`) is the highest-impact miss: if images are blocked, recipients see no content above the fold.

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (explicitly empty) to suppress screen-reader noise; currently they have no `alt` attribute at all, which some clients will vocalize as the filename.

**CSS notes:**
- Three overlapping `@media` breakpoints (320px, 450px, 660px) with duplicated utility classes (`.pad20`, `.padBot*`, etc.) — not a rendering bug, but increases parse weight unnecessarily.
- `color-scheme: light` / `supported-color-schemes: light` opts out of dark mode entirely. Intentional, but means dark-mode clients invert or recolor without sender control.
