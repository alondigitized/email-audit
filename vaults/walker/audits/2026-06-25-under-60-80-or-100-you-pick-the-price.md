# Under $60, $80, or $100, You Pick the Price
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Same broken JSON garbage in the preview — I counted, this is now ten straight Skechers emails with that problem. At some point that stops being a glitch and starts being a signal that nobody's watching the store. I'm surprised I'm still opening these.
- Inside, though? This one actually makes sense to me. Three clean price buckets — $60, $80, $100 — laid out so I can just pick the row that matches what I want to spend and go. That's how I actually shop. I'm not hunting for a specific colorway or chasing a brand story; I want a comfortable shoe under a number I can live with, and this email gives me exactly that framework. The BOGO 50% off banner at the top sweetens it.
- The Skechers Plus gift card panel got my attention too — I'm a member, and seeing a $250 gift card offer with a "Claim Now" button feels like they actually know who I am. I'd click. I probably wouldn't forward this to a friend — the broken preview makes the brand look sloppy — but for my own use, I'm heading to the $80 tier.

## What's working

- **Price-tier structure is genuinely useful.** Three clear sections — $60, $80, $100 and under — match exactly how I think about shoe budgets. No hunting required.
- **BOGO 50% offer is front and center.** Concrete, immediately visible, easy to understand. Didn't have to scroll to find it.
- **Skechers Plus panel feels earned.** Seeing member perks called out separately makes me feel like they know I'm not a first-time visitor. The $250 gift card claim stands out.
- **No broken images or layout collapse.** After so many Skechers emails with chaos in the preview, the body itself rendered clean on my phone.
- **"Your Shoes. Your Budget."** — that's plain language that doesn't talk down to me. Short, friendly, gets out of the way.

## What's weak

- **Woman in the hero, again.** I'm a 62-year-old guy buying walking shoes. The hero model is a young woman. I don't see myself in that photo, and none of the three price-tier panels led with men's styles either.
- **Zero comfort language.** Arch support, cushioning, memory foam, Slip-Ins — nothing. The whole pitch is price, which is fine, but I need a reason to buy *these* shoes at that price. What makes an $80 Skechers better on my feet than an $80 New Balance?
- **No deadline anywhere.** "Buy One Get One" is great, but how long? Today? This weekend? Two weeks? I don't feel the push to act now, which means I might just bookmark it and never come back.
- **That preview text.** Ten in a row now. Raw JSON schema code dumped into the inbox preview. Every. Single. Time. I've accepted it at this point but it genuinely erodes my trust in the brand a little more each send. Someone at Skechers needs to fix this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** This is the single highest-leverage change and it requires zero creative resources — just a tech fix. Ten consecutive broken previews is an embarrassment and a trust leak that costs opens every week.
- 2. **Lead the hero with a men's model in a comfort-specific style.** Show me a guy around my age in a Slip-In or an arch fit shoe. One image swap could double the relevance for the male 55+ segment.
- 3. **Add one comfort hook per price tier.** Something like: *"$80 & Under — includes Arch Fit and Memory Foam styles."* Gives me a reason to land in that tier beyond price alone.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the BOGO.** "Ends Sunday" or "This week only" — something that makes me act today instead of saving it for later and forgetting.
- 5. **Surface a single men's product in the hero** with a direct link — not just the category page. Show me one specific shoe that makes a case for the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Walk All Day Under $80 — Pick Your Price`
- **Subject Alt B:** `BOGO 50% Off + $60/$80/$100 Styles Inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three price buckets. Hundreds of styles. Buy one, get the second half off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Arch Fit, Slip-Ins, Memory Foam — your price, your pick.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Under $60/$80/$100" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken links or missing compliance elements, but carries a systemic HTTP/non-HTTPS image loading problem across 13+ assets hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com`, plus two third-party tracking pixels with security implications.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel over HTTP (no SSL)**
- `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — plain HTTP pixel; will be blocked or flagged by Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail privacy relay. No `alt` attribute.
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=...` — missing `https://` scheme prefix entirely; the `src` value as written is a relative path, meaning the pixel will 404 in every client.

**Third-party user-match beacons**
- Two `beacon.krxd.net` usermatch GIFs fire with a hashed subscriber ID (`9153f33f...`) and a raw numeric ID (`121173391`). These cross-site identity signals may conflict with iOS Mail Privacy Protection disclosures and CCPA data-sharing obligations depending on Krux/Salesforce DMP contract scope.

**26 click-redirect links** — QA correctly skipped HTTP probing; no broken link evidence, but UTM coverage should be confirmed (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (13 confirmed)**

All production images are served over `http://image.emails.skechers.com`, not `https://`. Affected assets include:

| Asset | Impact |
|---|---|
| Skechers logo (`dde00662-...png`) | Brand image hidden in Gmail |
| Hero/promo image (`49468f73-...png`) | Missing alt; invisible when blocked |
| App Store badge | CTA invisible |
| Google Play badge | CTA invisible |
| Curbside Pickup icon | Feature invisible |
| AfterPay icon | Payment option invisible |
| Find A Store icon | Store-finder CTA invisible |
| Instagram, Facebook, YouTube icons | Social links have no fallback text |

Gmail strips HTTP images by default since 2023 proxy enforcement. Outlook 365 prompts users to "Download pictures." Apple Mail fetches via proxy (HTTPS-only). **Net result: the email likely renders as a large blank column for a material share of recipients.**

**Missing `alt` text**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel) — minor for a tracking pixel, but flagged.
- `49468f73-...png` — this appears to be a content image (likely hero/promo), not a pixel. Missing `alt` means screen readers get nothing and Gmail's blocked-image fallback shows no label.

**CSS `max-scale` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4). iOS 10+ ignores this for user-initiated zoom, but it remains a best-practice flag.

**Duplicate media query blocks** — `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` declare identical rule sets, and a separate `@media (max-width: 480px)` block re-declares `.show` and `.mobile-hidden`. No functional breakage, but dead CSS weight.

The campaign slug `MKG_US_L60D_U_MULTI_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_06222026` is present in pixel calls, suggesting UTM tagging is templated — but this should be spot-checked on 2–3 resolved destination URLs to confirm `utm_campaign`, `utm_source=email`, and `utm_medium=email` are appended to Skechers.com landing pages.

Subject line ("Under $60, $80, or $100, You Pick the Price") implies price-filtered category pages as destinations. If CTAs land on the unfiltered homepage or a generic sale page, there is a continuity gap — but this requires resolved URLs to confirm.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before send / fix in template system**

1. **Migrate all `image.emails.skechers.com` assets to HTTPS.** This is a single DNS/CDN change or ESP setting update. Until fixed, a significant percentage of recipients see a broken email.
2. **Fix the Returnpath pixel `src`** — prepend `https://` to `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=...`. As-is it's a dead relative URL.
3. **Fix the ink1000 pixel** — change `http://` to `https://` and add `alt=""` explicitly.

**P1 — Address in next template revision**

4. **Add `alt` text to the hero/promo image** (`49468f73-...png`) — even a short descriptive string recovers accessibility and Gmail's image-blocked fallback label.
5. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta to restore pinch-to-zoom accessibility.
6. **Spot-check 3 resolved click URLs** for UTM parameter presence before confirming tracking coverage.

**P2 — Process / legal review**

7. **Review Krux DMP data-sharing disclosure** against current privacy policy — confirm hashed email cross-device matching is listed as a disclosed data-sharing use case.
8. **Consolidate duplicate media query blocks** in the template master to reduce payload and maintenance surface.
