**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**

- First thing I notice when this lands in my inbox is that the preview line is a wall of garbled URL — something like "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." — and that alone almost made me swipe left. Looks like a phishing attempt. But the subject "Limited Time: Red, White & New Deals" is clean, short, and I recognize Shoe Carnival after all those Father's Day emails, so I gave it the tap.

## What's working

- **The $10 off coupon with code SUMMER10 at the top** is the best element in this email — it applies to any purchase, the minimum ($74.98) is reasonable, and the "Get Your Coupon" button is right there without scrolling.
- **Patriotic theme is timely** — sending Red/White/New Deals the week of July 4th is smart sequencing; at least they're not still flogging Father's Day like the last four sends were.
- **Visual hierarchy is clear and bold** — the big red/white/blue banner catches the eye immediately, and the price callouts ($14.98 starting price, BOGO) are easy to read without squinting.
- **SHOE PERKS barcode at the bottom** — I like that they remembered I'm a member and gave me the in-store coupon bar code. That's a trust signal.
- **No render bugs in the body** — everything lined up, images loaded, buttons are tappable. Clean execution in the email itself.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is a disaster** — a raw tracking URL in the inbox preview makes this look like spam before I even open it. That one technical failure probably costs them opens from anyone paying attention.
- **This is a women's email wearing a gender-neutral disguise** — Women's sandals BOGO, Women's Sandal Blowout, Kids' Spot. There is not one men's shoe or comfort-fit mention in the entire scroll. I felt like I wandered into the wrong aisle.
- **"Limited Time" with no actual deadline** — if it's limited, tell me when it ends. "Through July 4th" would've created real urgency. Right now it just feels like marketing language.
- **No reviews, no fit details, no comfort signals** — not a word about arch support, cushioning, or sizing. After the June 17 Crocs + $20 off email, which at least connected to something on my radar, this one feels like they forgot who I am.

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — don't let another send go out with a tracking URL in the preview slot. A one-line fix before the next send: replace it with real copy that complements the subject.
- 2. **Add one men's comfort row** — even a single "Men's Walking & Slip-Ons from $29.98" block would have made me scroll more and click more. Right now there's zero reason for me to tap past the coupon.
- 3. **Put a hard deadline in the copy** — "Through July 4th" or "Ends Sunday" in both the subject and the body banner. "Limited Time" is wasted urgency.
- 4. **Segment or at least split the hero** — I don't need to see Women's BOGO above the fold. If segmentation isn't possible, at least lead with the universal coupon (which you did — good) and put the men's category second, not buried after three women's sections.
- 5. **Put the discount amount in the subject** — "$10 off" in the subject line would lift open rate for deal-hunters like me.

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Limited Time: Red, White & New Deals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Three click/open tracking URLs contain a zero-width space character (U+200B) that makes them non-ASCII-safe and likely broken at click time. Additionally, a content image is missing alt text and a merge token may be unresolved in a secondary open-pixel URL.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in tracking URLs (3 links)**

All three failures share the same root cause: a U+200B character is appended to the `utm_campaign` parameter value, making the URL non-ASCII-encodable.

Affected URLs:
```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260624_A_bogo_sandals_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rl&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260624_A_bogo_sandals_AM​
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?p&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260624_A_bogo_sandals_AM​
```

The invisible character sits at byte position 77, after the trailing `M` in `bogo_sandals_AM`. The `rd` variant is a click redirect (broken CTA link), `p` is the open-tracking pixel, and `rl` is likely the unsubscribe redirect — all three are affected. This indicates the zero-width space was introduced when the campaign string was copied (likely from a rich-text source), then injected into URL templates without sanitization.

**INFO — 26 additional tracking links not probed**

Secondary click domains were skipped by the QA crawler. If the same campaign string is used across all links, the U+200B contamination may affect more than the three confirmed failures.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Content image missing `alt` text**

```
src: https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/465aca37-a584-4d52-a5a1-61002d176bb3.png
```

This is a hosted content image (not a pixel), identified by its full CDN path. Missing `alt` degrades screen-reader experience and breaks the email's message when images are blocked by default (common in Outlook/corporate clients).

**INFO — Tracking pixels missing `alt`**

Both `ieezzy5m` and `0di86k1v` pixel images lack `alt` attributes. Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""`, which is acceptable; the absence of the attribute itself is a minor standards gap but unlikely to cause user-facing issues.

**INFO — Responsive breakpoints**

Four media query breakpoints defined (320px, 450px, 600px, 660px). No overlapping or conflicting rules observed in the visible CSS. Color-scheme meta tags declare `light` only — no dark mode handling is present, which may cause rendering issues in dark-mode email clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail).

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**WARN — Possible unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token**

Open-pixel URL on secondary tracker domain:
```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

`[UNIQUE]` is a per-recipient substitution token. If this value is not resolved at send time by the ESP, every recipient's open event will be attributed to the same placeholder identity, breaking open-rate tracking. Confirm this token is substituted by the sending platform before deployment.

No other merge tokens visible in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Unsubscribe link** — The `rl` tracking URL (type `rl` = "remove link" in common ESP conventions) is one of the three broken URLs. If this is the physical unsubscribe redirect, clicking it will fail, creating a CAN-SPAM compliance risk: recipients who attempt to opt out may be unable to do so.

**Authentication headers** — Cannot be assessed from HTML source alone. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for `email.shoecarnival.com` should be verified via sending-domain DNS records and ESP delivery logs.

**Physical address / footer** — Not visible in the provided HTML truncation; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer presence. Recommend verifying the full template includes a postal address.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM campaign date offset** — The campaign string `SCA_20260624_A_bogo_sandals_AM` uses date `20260624` (June 24), while the send date is June 25. This is a one-day lag, likely from build-day naming convention. Not a broken link, but UTM campaign attribution in analytics will appear under the prior date.

**UTM structure** — Only `utm_campaign` is visible in the tracking parameter (`?t=utm_campaign=...`). The format passes the full campaign string as the value of `t`, which the ESP tracker decodes before forwarding. Confirm the ESP properly appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_content` to the final destination URL — these are not visible in the click-tracker parameters themselves and may or may not be injected downstream.
