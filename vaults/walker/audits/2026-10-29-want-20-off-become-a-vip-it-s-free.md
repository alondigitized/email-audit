# Want 20% off? Become a VIP (it’s free!)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- DSW again, and I've lost count. After the SOBRIGHT ladder and "Join VIP -> Get access to 20% off" earlier today, this is the same VIP/FABULOUS 20% pitch with the subject reworded. The giant "20" is hard to miss. I can read it on my phone without squinting, and I'll give them that. But I've now been told about this deal three times in a day, so it doesn't feel like news.
- Scrolling down, it's mostly women's shoes: Mary Janes, stilettos, ankle-strap heels. There's one black slip-on sneaker for a man like me, and no cushioning, arch support or "walk all day" talk anywhere. The wide width and wide calf buttons are the only things that speak to fit. The sizes stop at 11, so no 12 or 13 for the men.
- Would I click? Probably not. Signing up is free, but I already got this offer this week. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The huge "20% OFF" is clear and readable on a phone, and I know the deal in two seconds.
- "Become a VIP" is a plain black button with a plain label, so I know what I'm tapping.
- "Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf" are real fit options, and that's the kind of thing I care about.
- The "Free shipping for VIPs / In-store pickup" row tells me what I get for joining.
- The email looks like DSW, and the look matches what I'd see on their site.

## What's weak

- This is the ninth or tenth send in about four days. The same 20% VIP deal came in "Become a VIP & get 20% off!" and "Join VIP -> Get access to 20% off" earlier today.
- The shoes are almost all dress and fashion styles, mostly women's. There's one men's sneaker and nothing about cushioning, arch support or slip-ons.
- The "20%" comes with an asterisk, and the real terms are way down at the bottom: regular-price only, a brand list, "not valid on Final Sale," and one use per customer. That feels a bit buried.
- The size chart only goes up to 11, so it doesn't work for most men.
- The "Wonderland" ribbon crowds the "Warehouse" word in the logo, so the header looks a little messy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the cadence. Send one VIP-signup email, not four near-copies in a day. That would lift this more than any design change.
- 2. Show me comfort. Put in men's slip-on and walking shoes from Skechers, Hoka and Brooks, with words like "cushioned" and "arch support." That's what gets me to the product page.
- 3. Put the deadline (Oct 29–Nov 1) and the key exclusions next to the offer instead of in the fine print at the bottom.
- 4. Add men's sizes 12–14 to the size row.
- 5. Pick one clear path. Use "Become a VIP" as the main button and drop the four text links under it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `VIP is free: 20% off through Nov 1`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's comfort shoes, 20% off for VIPs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code FABULOUS works on regular-price Skechers, Clarks & more. Ends Nov 1.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free to join. Free shipping on every VIP order.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Broken CTA (confirmed, QA FAIL):** The "Opt-In to Texts Get 15% Off" link returns 400.
  - URL: `https://ablink.dsw.com/f/a/_nzwEsFAXwZ9S8m6uWOn4w~~/AAQRxRA~/RfTMKObN…`
  - This is a conversion CTA tied to a discount, so a dead link here is high impact.
  - A 400 on a tracking-redirect URL can also mean the token was rejected, for example by a bot or non-browser request. A manual click-through should confirm it.
- **Link-wrapping bloat (QA WARN):** 25,179 of 29,535 plain-text characters (about 85%) are URLs. Every link is wrapped in a long `ablink.dsw.com` redirect token, which risks spam-filter scoring and an unreadable text part.
- **Probe coverage:** Only 12 links were probed and 58 were skipped (footer, utility, social). Those 58 are unverified, not confirmed good.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA WARN, 2 images):**
  - `https://ablink.dsw.com/q/HF229PQjGDPeL0NI68_l5g~~/AAQRxRA~/-lcqE1Rk…`
  - `https://mi.dsw.com/p/up/e6f23fb024fdcb2cc855b9ed672710d4/o.gif?mi_u=…`
  - The `o.gif` image is a tracking pixel, so it should carry `alt=""` to be treated as decorative. The first image should be checked: if it is content, it needs real alt text. If it is a pixel, it needs `alt=""`.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on some clients, which is an accessibility problem.
- **Malformed meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so neither declaration takes effect. Charset still falls back to the header or client default.
- The rest of the HTML was truncated, so no further rendering checks were possible.

No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the available source or QA output. The tracking pixel carries `mi_u=225090073`, which looks like a rendered user ID.

- **Unsubscribe:** QA always probes the unsubscribe link and reported no failure for it. The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and the physical postal address were not reported or visible in the truncated source, so they are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters cannot be verified.** All links go through `ablink.dsw.com` redirects, so the final destination and any UTM parameters aren't visible in the source.
- **SMS offer path:** the 400 means the subscriber never reaches the opt-in landing page. This is the one confirmed continuity break.
- **Pixel campaign ID:** the `o.gif` URL carries `mi_ecmp=20261029_P261592_V1_WM-_Offer_Stan…`. That is a campaign identifier and does not show landing-page alignment.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix or replace the SMS opt-in link, which returns 400. First re-test it manually in a browser to rule out a bot-block false positive.
2. Add `alt=""` to the `o.gif` pixel. Give the `ablink.dsw.com/q/…` image meaningful alt text if it is visible content.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag. Add `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` to the two meta tags.
4. Shorten or rebalance the plain-text part. Consider a custom short-link domain or fewer wrapped links.
5. Re-run the audit against the raw message with full headers to confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC, `List-Unsubscribe`, and the postal address.
6. Resolve the redirect chains for the primary CTAs to confirm the final URLs carry the expected UTM parameters.
