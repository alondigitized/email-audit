# Go hard. Go Nike.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction scrolling through this one: it's not talking to me. This is a track-and-field, back-to-school Nike pitch — a kid sprinting on a red track, close-ups of performance sneakers, a red-and-black Air Max hero shot. That's a young athlete's inbox, not a 62-year-old's. I don't play court sports anymore, and "Train hard. Play hard." isn't how I think about my next pair of shoes — I think about whether my arches will thank me after a Costco run.
- Famous Footwear's been in my inbox nearly every day this summer — I flagged the same thing on the adidas send yesterday and the BOGO sends before that. This is at least the sixth or seventh nearly-identical BOGO-plus-points banner I've seen from them this month, just with the logo swapped out.
- Would I click? Maybe the "SHOP MEN" button out of habit, mostly to see if there's anything cushioned buried in there — but I'm not going near "SHOP NIKE" for performance runners. Wouldn't forward this to anybody. It's a scroll-and-close.

## What's working

- The BOGO ½ off banner up top is big, purple, and impossible to miss — I know the deal before I've even started reading.
- "Up to 30% off Nike" with the red Air Max shot is a clean, legible offer block — clear percentage, clear brand.
- The rewards messaging (2X points, $10 reward with store pickup) is visible and I'm a member, so that part at least acknowledges I've got skin in the game.
- Nothing's broken — no overlapping text, no missing images, loads clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- Every model in here is a teenager or twenty-something — the kid on the track, the runner in the driveway, nobody who looks like they've got a knee that talks back.
- Zero mention of comfort, cushioning, or arch support anywhere — it's all "go hard," which is the opposite of what gets me to click.
- No sizing, fit, or walking-shoe info — just glamour shots of performance sneakers I'd never actually wear.
- "Exclusions apply," "mix and match excluded" — the fine print at the bottom undercuts how clean that BOGO banner looked up top.
- Cadence is wearing thin — this is another same-week Famous Footwear send with the same purple BOGO block I've already seen twice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this one — send the Nike back-to-school push to parents/students, and send guys my age a comfort-brand roundup (Skechers, Hoka, New Balance) instead of forcing a performance pitch on everybody.
- 2. Add a cushioning/arch-support callout line near the top for the comfort-shopper segment — even one sentence would do it.
- 3. Cut the send frequency — I've gotten this same BOGO structure five-plus times in two weeks; it's starting to blur together and I skim past it now.
- 4. Fix the preview text — right now it's leaking a raw tracking URL instead of real preheader copy, which looks broken in the inbox.
