# Crocs at Work™: New Styles Out Now!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is the first Crocs email in a while that has a real offer in it. The tiered "Buy 2, Get 20% Off, Buy 3, 30%, Buy 4, 40%" block is big and I read it in two seconds. Compare that to the 10/14 fuzzy-lined clog email with no percentage at all, or the 10/5 Halloween charms one where I couldn't find a shoe. This one at least tells me what I'd save.
- The top half is aimed at someone who works a shift: a guy mopping a wet floor in black slip-resistant clogs. I'm 62 and retired from that kind of work, so it's only half aimed at me. A grip sole and "support through every hour" do matter to my feet, though. But the offer wants me to buy four pairs, and I'm not stocking up on clogs. I'd click once to see whether a plain black clog is in the 20% group, then probably leave.
- I'd skim it and not forward it. It's a decent email for a nurse or a line cook. For me it's fine but not mine.

## What's working

- The tiered discount block is huge, black on yellow, and I can read it without zooming.
- The man mopping in black clogs is believable and looks closer to my age than the usual chunky-sneaker model.
- "Closed-toe protection / Grip sole / Easy-clean material" tells me what the shoe does in plain words.
- The black "Shop Crocs At Work™" button is easy to see and big enough to tap with my thumb.
- The "Free shipping on qualified orders" bar at the top matches the 30% and free shipping Crocs sent me earlier this month.

## What's weak

- "Buy 4" is a lot to ask. It's a stocking-up offer, not a "try one pair" offer.
- "Select styles" and "Exclusions apply" are on the page, but I can't tell which shoes qualify. That feels like fine print.
- No shoe name, no price, and no sizing or arch-support talk. "Comfort and Traction" is a slogan, not a reason.
- The four pill buttons at the bottom (Clogs, Jibbitz Charms, Sale, Sandals) scatter my attention, and I don't want charms.
- The real deadline, October 19th at 11:59pm MT, is buried in the tiny print at the very bottom. It should be up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the end date up next to the discount, for example "Ends Sunday," so the urgency is something I can see.
- 2. Show one specific shoe with its name and price, linked straight to that shoe. It would help to add one line on cushioning or support for people on their feet all day.
- 3. Add a "Buy 1, Save 15%" entry tier. That would pull in someone like me who isn't buying four pairs.
- 4. Say which styles are included, or link "Select styles" to a list.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is well-formed, with a responsive media query, `lang="en"`, and an open pixel and preheader in place. The source is truncated, though, and 19 tracking links were not probed, so link, footer and compliance checks are mostly unverifiable. Two `<meta>` tags are malformed, and the automated QA flagged one image with no alt text and unknown authentication status.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified redirects:** QA skipped 19 links as tracking/click-redirect domains (HTTP probe skipped). Destination resolution, status codes and redirect chains are unconfirmed.
- **Open pixel:** `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?HENRW3I3ALTUZPBUCWKJADS7Z4.10199&d=10199&bmt=0` is correctly sized (1x1, `alt=""`) and sits inside a hidden container. No issue.
- **Third-party pixel:** `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f…` is an Adobe Audience Manager beacon. It carries a per-recipient ID (`d_dpuid`) and an open flag, and it is hidden with `display:none`. Many clients, including Apple Mail Privacy Protection, may prefetch it, which would inflate the `c_emailopened=Yes` signal. This is a data-quality and privacy-disclosure concern, not a break.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` have no `http-equiv` attribute, so both are no-ops. The charset declaration is not actually applied from the HTML. It relies on the `Content-Type` header, which is not visible in the supplied data.
- **Missing alt text (QA warning):** the demdex `<img>` has no `alt` attribute. It is `display:none` / `visibility:hidden`, so the real-world impact is low, but it still fails automated checks. Add `alt=""`.
- **Positive:** viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, the `-webkit-text-size-adjust` and `-ms-text-size-adjust` resets, and the Apple data-detector override are present.
- **Positive:** the `@font-face` rules use `font-display: swap`. Fallback stacks are not visible in the truncated source.
- **Unverified:** the `u + .email-body #new-gmail-hack` selector depends on an element with that id existing. It is not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear. The remainder of the body was truncated and could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Authentication:** QA reports no `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC status unknown). It attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it is an audit-environment limitation, not a confirmed sender defect.
- **Footer:** the unsubscribe link, physical postal address and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are not in the supplied data. They cannot be confirmed or flagged.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. Click-through URLs, UTM parameters and landing pages are hidden behind the 19 skipped tracking links, and the visible source contains no destination URLs.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the meta tags: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`. A `<meta charset="utf-8">` is the simpler option for the first.
2. Add `alt=""` to the demdex pixel `<img>`.
3. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Resolve the 19 tracking links through their redirects (or via `click.crocs-email.com` with a follow-redirects probe) to confirm destinations, UTM parameters and landing-page status.
4. Capture the raw headers from the original delivery path to verify SPF, DKIM, DMARC and `List-Unsubscribe`. The relay stripped them here.
5. Confirm that the `d_dpuid` identifier and open-flag pixel are covered by the privacy policy and consent handling, and don't rely on `c_emailopened` as a reliable open metric.
