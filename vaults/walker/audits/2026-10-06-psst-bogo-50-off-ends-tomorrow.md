# Psst...BOGO 50% off ends tomorrow 👀
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- DSW again. This is the fifth BOGO-flavored note I've had since "BOGO 50% off starts…NOW!" on the 5th, and the subject line says it "ends tomorrow." The orange banner at the top is easy to read, with "BOGO 50% OFF*" in big white letters and a teal SHOP NOW button. But the banner calls it a "Long Weekend, Big Deal," which doesn't fit a Tuesday email. The subject's "Psst... 👀" feels like it was written for somebody half my age.
- Then the body is nearly all women's boots, heels and leopard-print booties. I counted maybe four pairs of men's-looking sneakers, and those are fashion sneakers rather than walking shoes. There's nothing about cushioning, arch support or slip-ons. I'd have to hunt for the "Men" link in the nav bar. A man my age who cares about comfort would likely skim, see boots and heels, and swipe it away.
- Would I click? Probably not, unless I was already shopping for a pair. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and move on. The BOGO deal is real, but I've seen it all week, and it doesn't speak to me.

## What's working

- The big orange "BOGO 50% OFF*" banner is easy to read on my phone, and the teal SHOP NOW button stands out.
- The deal is simple: buy one pair, get half off a second pair.
- The "Shop by Size" block with a "Shop Wide Width" button is a nice touch for a guy who sometimes needs a roomier fit.
- Free shipping for VIPs, in-store pickup and Afterpay are all listed in the "Shopping Made Easy" strip.
- It looks like DSW and matches the same orange banner I saw on the 5th, so I trust it.

## What's weak

- The "Psst... 👀" subject is gimmicky and doesn't suit a 62-year-old.
- "Long Weekend" is stale copy on a weekday and doesn't match "ends tomorrow."
- The product grid is mostly women's boots and heels. For a man, only the sneaker section is even close.
- There are no comfort cues: no cushioning, no arch support, no slip-on styles. Nothing here tells me why a shoe is comfortable.
- The "SHOP MEN" link is a small underlined line under the banner. Those tap targets are tiny for my thumbs.
- The fine print is tiny and sits at the bottom. The asterisk on the offer sends me down there to decode it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Sort the content by who's reading. Send me a men's block of comfort walkers and slip-ons, not women's heels. That one change would lift this email the most.
- 2. Fix the deadline copy. Say "Ends tomorrow, Oct 7" in the banner instead of "Long Weekend."
- 3. Add real comfort language to the product blocks, like "cushioned" or "all-day arch support," plus prices or star ratings.
- 4. Make "SHOP MEN" a full-size button, not a tiny underlined link.
- 5. Cut the extra BOGO sends this week. Combine them into one reminder.

  - This is the SMS-acquisition CTA, and a 400 means the click fails before it reaches a landing page.

- **[WARN] Plain-text >70% URLs:** 26,290 of 31,189 characters (about 84%) are URLs. The long `ablink.dsw.com` tracking tokens inflate the plain-text part. This can raise spam-filter scoring and makes the text version hard to read.
