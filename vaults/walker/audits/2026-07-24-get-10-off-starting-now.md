# Get $10 off starting…NOW!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Eight birthday emails in a week — or close to it — and this is the one DSW sends on Thursday morning. "Get $10 off starting…NOW!" I'll admit the dollar amount stopped my thumb for half a second. Ten bucks off a $49 purchase is real money, especially compared to whatever the "$20 VIP" deal I've been ignoring all week actually requires me to sign up for. The tiered structure at the top — $10 off $49, $20 off $99, $60 off $199 — is refreshingly readable. That part I genuinely liked.
- But then the email keeps going, and somewhere in the middle it shows me a "Shop by Size" grid that tops out at size 7.5. I wear a men's 11. That size section doesn't exist for me. And the "Shop Wide Calf" button sealed it: this email clearly landed in my inbox by mistake. I'm not the target. The shoes in the "So New, So You" grid are a mixed bag — some loafers, some dress boots — nothing that screams "cushioned walking shoe for a guy who's on his feet all day."
- Would I click? Maybe the top offer, out of habit and because $10 off $49 is a low bar. Forward to a friend? No. That size grid would confuse anyone I know who's also in the men's department.

## What's working

- **Tiered offer is prominent and scannable** — $10/$20/$60 stacked vertically with the spend thresholds, big and readable without zooming, exactly what I want to see on a phone.
- **"CLAIM YOUR $20 OFF" sub-link** signals there's a member benefit baked in, which I notice as an existing DSW customer.
- **"Come to Our Bday Party" section** actually interests me — Saturday in-store event means I can try shoes on my feet, not just guess from a thumbnail. That's something I'd consider.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — everything rendered, nothing overlapping, text is large enough that I didn't need to pinch-zoom.
- **"FYI: THESE PRICES WON'T LAST"** banner is honest and direct rather than vague — it tells me this is time-sensitive without pretending there's a countdown clock.

## What's weak

- **The size grid is women's sizes only** (5 through 7.5, plus "Shop Wide Calf") — that's a hard demographic miss for me. If DSW has my purchase history, they know I'm not shopping a size 6.
- **Two separate offer structures in one email create confusion** — the tiered $10/$20/$60 at the top AND a flat $20 off section lower down. Which one applies? Do they stack? I'm not hunting through fine print to find out.
- **"So New, So You" product grid is generic** — no comfort branding, no cushioning callouts, no men's walking shoes I recognize. It could be any department store's filler grid.
- **Preview text is mostly garbage characters** — "Tomorrow: our bday party in stores!" is the only readable part; the rest is a string of invisible spacer characters that would fill my inbox preview with junk.
- **Birthday email number eight (or so) this week** — the cadence has fully desensitized me to this brand's urgency signals. "NOW!" doesn't feel urgent when I've been reading "Don't miss out" since Monday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the size grid by gender** — show men's sizes (8–14) to male shoppers, women's to female. This single fix would stop me from feeling like a wrong-list mistake.
- 2. **Pick one offer, not two** — the tiered structure is better. Drop the flat $20-off section below or merge it into the tier logic. Two separate deal structures in one email creates doubt about which applies.
- 3. **Add one comfort-specific product** to the hero grid — a Skechers Arch Fit, a New Balance walking shoe, anything with a cushioning callout for a guy my age. It costs nothing to surface one SKU I'd recognize.
- 4. **Fix the preheader** — strip the spacer characters so the only thing inbox clients show is the real copy about the in-store event.
- 5. **Highlight the store party more prominently** — that's genuinely the most interesting thing in this email for someone who wants to try before they buy.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-store bday party Saturday — plus try the new comfort arrivals`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Src:` (capital S) in all `@font-face` rules — invalid CSS**
Every `@font-face` block uses `Src:` instead of the spec-required `src:`. CSS property names are case-sensitive in some parsers. Affects all 10 declared font faces (Futura PT Regular/Medium/Demi/Bold, HelveticaNow Regular/Medium/MediumItalic/Bold/BoldItalic/Italic). Fonts will fail to load in strict parsers; fallback system fonts will render instead.

```css
/* Bug — present in all 10 @font-face blocks */
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/.../original.woff") format('woff');
/*  ^-- should be lowercase `src:` */
```

**Missing `http-equiv` attributes on two meta tags**
```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, these tags are no-ops. The charset declaration is particularly important — without it, Outlook and some webmail clients may misinterpret encoding.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` declarations in reset**
```css
* {
  -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%;   /* prevent auto-scaling */
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;   /* overrides the line above — wins in cascade */
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
The `none` value will prevent all user-agent text scaling on mobile Safari, which can cause readability issues on small screens.

**Inconsistent font formats**
- Futura PT: `.woff` only
- HelveticaNow: `.ttf` only (no `.woff`/`.woff2`)

`.ttf` files are significantly larger than `.woff2`. HelveticaNow will load slower where it's supported, and the inconsistency suggests copy-paste error in template assembly.
