# Happy Father's Day to the Best Dad Ever!🏅
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- I opened this and my first thought was, "Here we go again." Shoe Carnival has been hammering the Father's Day drum since mid-June, and today is June 25th — four days after Father's Day came and went. At this point I've gotten so many of these emails I could recite the creative from memory: "Best Dad Ever," Skechers up front, Nike/Jordan in the middle, Crocs somewhere down the scroll, and a promo code with the word DAD in it. They're not wrong that I'm interested in this stuff. They're just off-calendar in a way that makes me feel like the brand is on autopilot.
- That said — there's real meat here. Skechers at 30% off is front and center, Men's Sandals for $19.98 caught my eye as I scrolled down, and there's a $20 off code (FORDAD) that's visible right in the hero without hunting for fine print. Shoe Perks $10 reward is also showing up, which I appreciate when I'm about to spend a hundred bucks anyway. These are the signals that keep me reading even when the calendar wrapper is stale.
- I'd probably click through to the Skechers section and maybe the Men's Sandals. I wouldn't forward this — it feels like the fourth time I've seen it this month and my buddies got the same emails. Mark it as read after I check the Skechers deal.

## What's working

- **Skechers is the lead brand and takes up real visual space** — the older gentleman sitting outdoors in the hero shot matches my demographic, and "UP TO 30% OFF" is legible without squinting.
- **$20 off $99.98+ with code FORDAD is right in the hero** — no scrolling, no hunting, the offer is visible the moment the email loads.
- **Men's Sandals at $19.98 is a concrete, low-barrier entry point** — that's a legitimate value play and it's in the men's section, not a mislabeled women's promo like the June 15 BOGO disaster.
- **Shoe Perks free $10 reward is visible** — loyalty perks showing up in the body reminds me there's a reason I'm on this list.
- **Crocs have their own section** — I cross-shop Crocs, so seeing them get a dedicated block (not buried) is the right call.

## What's weak

- **Father's Day was June 21. Today is June 25. The wrapper is four days expired** — this is now the fifth or sixth Father's Day send in a row and the occasion has passed. The June 20 "Final Day" email literally said the gift would arrive in time for Father's Day. None of this creative was updated for post-holiday clearance framing.
- **"For Daddy-Daughter Days" section is completely irrelevant to me** — I'm not shopping for matching parent-child shoes. It breaks the comfort-focused flow and sends a signal the email wasn't built for a 62-year-old solo shopper.
- **The preview text is a raw tracking URL** — opening this email from my inbox, I saw a long click.email.shoecarnival.com link before I saw any copy. That's not a preheader, that's a technical accident.
- **"5 DAYS ONLY!" feels hollow post-Father's Day** — I don't believe that deadline means anything when the holiday it's tied to has already passed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the Father's Day wrapper and flip to a post-holiday clearance or summer framing** — something like "Dad picked his favorites. Here's what's left at sale price." That's honest and still relevant on June 25.
- 2. **Cut the Daddy-Daughter matching section entirely for this audience segment** — replace it with a second men's comfort category (walking shoes, sandals, orthotics).
- 3. **Fix the preheader — add real copy** — right now the inbox shows a tracking URL. Any plain-English preheader is better than that.
- 4. **Put "Men's Sandals $19.98" higher in the email** — it's the most specific, lowest-friction offer and it's buried in the lower third.
- 5. **Name a specific Skechers style in the CTA** — "Shop Skechers GO WALK" beats "Shop Skechers" every time for someone like me who knows what I'm looking for.

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival Father's Day Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Four tracking/utility links are broken due to a zero-width space (U+200B) embedded in the UTM parameter string, rendering those redirects non-functional. One tracking pixel contains an unresolved merge token (`[UNIQUE]`), indicating an ESP render failure for that recipient.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**CRITICAL — Zero-width space in 4 URLs**

All four failures share the same root cause: a U+200B character appended to the campaign name in the `t=` parameter, at byte position 82–83:

```
https://ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&i=72180589&t=utm_campaign=SCA_20260621_skechers_nike_jordan_AM​
                                                                                                           ↑ U+200B here
```

Affected link variants: `?rd` (likely unsubscribe/remove), `?rl` (view in browser), `?p` (print), `?f` (forward). If `?rd` is the unsubscribe endpoint, this is a CAN-SPAM compliance risk (see §5).

The U+200B is invisible in the HTML editor, suggesting it was introduced via copy-paste into the ESP campaign builder — likely when copying the campaign name from a doc or spreadsheet.

**INFO — 25 tracking links not probed**

Click-redirect domains (`ieezzy5m.emltrk.com`) were skipped. These are presumed functional but unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 4 images**

| Image | File |
|---|---|
| Content image | `951dfca3-078c-47e4-a9c6-b5630a845bf2.png` |
| Content image | `05239c62-9b67-4426-b112-0b55431bed66.png` |
| Tracking pixel | `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?i=72180589&t=...` |
| Tracking pixel | `0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]` |

Content images with missing `alt` degrade screen-reader experience and break rendering when images are blocked (common in Outlook). Tracking pixels should use `alt=""` explicitly.

**Minor — malformed meta tag**

```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```

Missing `http-equiv` attribute. Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />`. Without it, the IE compatibility directive has no effect.

**No dark mode declared**

`color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` suppress dark mode entirely. Not a bug, but a deliberate constraint — confirm this is intentional.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — Unresolved `[UNIQUE]` token**

```
src: https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

The `[UNIQUE]` placeholder was not substituted before send. This tracking pixel — likely an open-pixel or engagement tracker for a secondary ESP — will log hits under a literal string `[UNIQUE]` rather than a per-recipient ID, collapsing all open events into a single record and invalidating open-rate data for this send.

No other unresolved merge tokens detected.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe / Authentication)

**Risk — Unsubscribe link may be broken**

The `?rd` variant URL (unsubscribe/remove from list) is one of the four broken URLs. If `ieezzy5m.emltrk.com/v2/ieezzy5m?rd&...` routes to the unsubscribe flow, the U+200B makes it non-resolving. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism for 30 days post-send. **Verify whether `?rd` maps to unsubscribe before treating this as resolved.**

Sender domain (`email.shoecarnival.com`) and tracking domain (`ieezzy5m.emltrk.com`) were not probed for SPF/DKIM/DMARC in this audit — check ESP delivery logs for authentication pass/fail.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM)

Campaign tag `SCA_20260621_skechers_nike_jordan_AM` is present in click-redirect URLs but is corrupted by the U+200B in the four broken links. The 25 functioning tracking links presumably carry the same campaign string without the stray character — verify in the ESP link editor that the clean campaign name is consistent across all links.

No landing page UTM alignment can be confirmed without probing the redirect chain (skipped for tracking domains).
