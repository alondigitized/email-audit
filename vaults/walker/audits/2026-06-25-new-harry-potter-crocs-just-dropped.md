# New Harry Potter™|Crocs just dropped
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Another Crocs email. I've been getting these things since late May — I reviewed the "Extra 25% off" send back on June 13th and thought maybe they were finally figuring out how to talk to me. This one took a hard left turn. Harry Potter? I'm 62 years old. The Marauder's Map means very little to me. I know what Harry Potter is, sure, but I'm not the person who grew up reading those books at sleepovers — my kids were, maybe. My grandkids definitely are. This email feels like it got airdropped into my inbox from somebody else's subscriber list.
- What I see in the image is a product shot of a clog with a map printed on it, a weak headline that says "There's More to Explore," some body copy explaining the product, and four buttons — "Shop Clogs," "Shop Jibbitz™ Charms," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals." No price. No discount. No comfort story. Not one word about cushioning or arch support. Just a novelty shoe I'd never wear to the grocery store or my Tuesday morning walk.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward it — maybe I'd mention it to my daughter if she's into that stuff, but it's not a shoe I'm buying.

## What's working

- **Brand logo and layout are clean** — Crocs logo is right at the top, image loads fully, no broken elements. Easy to register who sent this on a quick glance.
- **Four CTA buttons are well-spaced** — I can tap "Shop Clogs" or "Shop Sale" without fat-fingering something else. The button sizing is reasonable on mobile.
- **"Shop Sale" tucked in there** — that's the one button that almost got my thumb. A sale is always relevant to me; too bad nothing in the email tells me what's actually on sale.
- **New product drop is clearly communicated** — if I were a Harry Potter fan, I'd immediately understand what this email was about. The product photo does that job.

## What's weak

- **Zero comfort messaging** — not a single word about cushioning, arch support, or how this shoe feels on a 10,000-step day. That's the only reason I'm in Crocs' subscriber list.
- **Harry Potter is the wrong target for me** — this is a millennial and Gen Z collab. Sending it to a 62-year-old comfort shopper with no segmentation is a miss that stings more because I've seen Crocs do better (the Father's Day email on June 9th actually tried to earn my attention).
- **No offer, no price, no discount** — "Shop Sale" is a button with no context. What's on sale? How much? That's the whole reason I'd click.
- **"There's More to Explore" is a nothing headline** — it doesn't tell me the value proposition, the savings, or anything about why this should matter to me today. Weak.
- **Preview text is completely wasted** — whatever was in that preheader field is invisible gobbledygook. On my phone, that preview line is often what tips me to open or skip. This one gave me nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send away from comfort-first shoppers** — HP fans and 62-year-old comfort walkers are different people. This email should not be in my inbox at all. If Crocs must send it broad, they need a comfort-angle hook: *"Comfort you can cast a spell on — the Harry Potter™ Clog is cushioned for all-day wear."* That at least gives me a reason.
- 2. **Lead with an offer number** — "There's More to Explore" should be "$XX | Harry Potter™ Clogs Are Here." Give me a price or a deal and suddenly I have a reason to click even on a novelty item.
- 3. **Add one comfort callout in the product copy** — mention Croslite foam. One line. I need that to justify the purchase to myself.
- 4. **Fix the preview text** — it's invisible character spam. Write something real.
- 5. **Reduce to one primary CTA** — four buttons of equal weight ("Shop Clogs," "Shop Jibbitz™ Charms," "Shop Sale," "Shop Sandals") split my attention. Pick one and commit.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `All-day comfort meets Hogwarts — see the limited collection`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Marauder's Map Clog drops today — plus what's on sale`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Subject line artifact** — The delivered subject is:
```
New Harry Potter™|Crocs just dropped
```
The bare `|` pipe between `™` and `Crocs` is not a standard punctuation choice for subject lines. This pattern commonly indicates an unresolved ESP conditional or fallback expression (e.g., AMPscript `%%IF%%...%%ELSE%%Crocs%%ENDIF%%` or a Handlebars `{{value|default}}` that rendered the literal delimiter). **Confirm against the template source** — if the pipe is hardcoded text, document the intent; if it's a template artifact, fix the fallback logic.

**Subscriber tokens in body** — `c_subscriberID` and `d_dpuid` in the pixel are resolved (numeric/hashed values, not raw `%%token%%` syntax). No unresolved merge tokens visible in the provided HTML excerpt.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / CASL** — Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism not present in the provided HTML excerpt (source is truncated). Assume these exist in the footer — verify they are present and the unsubscribe link is a one-click or single-confirmation mechanism.

**Preheader implementation** — Two preheader techniques are stacked (the `.preheader` div and a zero-width-space padding div). This is standard defensive practice across ESPs; no issue.
