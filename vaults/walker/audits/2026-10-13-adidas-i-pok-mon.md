## Executive summary

- Well, here's another adidas email, and this one is Pikachu. Big yellow Pikachu on a black background takes up the whole top of the screen. I'm 62. I know Pokémon from my grandkids, but it doesn't make me want to buy shoes for myself. The "Pokémon favorites" line in the tiny preview text at the very top is about the only thing that tells me what this is about.
- The second half is better. There's a real pair of shoes, olive and brown suede Handball Spezials with a gum sole, and the picture is nice. But these are flat lace-up sneakers. They aren't cushioned slip-ons with arch support. Nothing here mentions comfort, cushioning, or fit. No price, no percent off, no deadline.
- I wouldn't click. I might forward the Pokémon half to my daughter for the grandkids, but I wouldn't forward it to a friend my age. I'd mark it read and move on. It's the fourth or fifth adidas email I've had this week.

## What's working

- The lower photo of the olive Spezials shows a real shoe, big and clear. It's the only part that spoke to a shoe buyer.
- The text is large and easy to read on my phone, and the black "Shop Now" buttons are big enough to tap.
- The layout is simple. It's one column, two blocks, and nothing is cramped.
- It looks like adidas. I knew who it was right away, and the pictures match what I'd see on their site.
- The "Season of Originals" copy names Samba, Gazelle and Spezial, so I know which styles it means.

## What's weak

- Pikachu is the first thing I see. It's a cartoon ad, and I don't see a shoe or anything for a 62-year-old walker.
- There's no price, discount, free shipping, or deadline anywhere. "Shop Now" doesn't tell me what I'd get.
- The Pokémon photo doesn't show any product. I can't tell if it's shoes, shirts, or toys.
- Nothing mentions comfort, cushioning, arch support, or slip-on ease. I'm cross-shopping Hoka, Brooks and Skechers, and this doesn't help me compare.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" bar at the top adds a third Shop Now button. It sends me in too many directions.
- The "gifts for everyone on your list" copy repeats the gifting theme from the October 8 emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one shoe and one offer. If Pokémon is the hook, show the actual product on the Pokémon half. Right now it's only a cartoon.
- 2. Add a number: a price, a percent off, or a ship date. People my age want to know what a thing costs before tapping.
- 3. For the gift half, speak to people like me. Say something about all-day comfort, or a cushioned walking option, instead of only flat classics.
- 4. Cut the extra banner at the top so each section has one button.
- 5. Use button copy that names what's behind it, like "Shop Pokémon kids' styles" and "Shop Spezial."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed failure from the automated QA: no plain-text alternative (0 chars). It also has several non-HTTPS image sources and missing alt attributes. The HTML source was truncated, so the unsubscribe link, footer, headers and destination URLs could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 18 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations and status codes are unconfirmed.
- **Insecure tracking pixel:** The image `NrGxHu3Pes5OMqoPfcfUtmDH9XNEB59-qn5t8wV63AA=452` loads over `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a13308e9cd-…`. It looks like the open-tracking pixel. Clients that block mixed or non-HTTPS content may drop it, which would undercount opens.
- **Insecure image host:** `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` is used 4 times, all as the "arrow" image.
- **Referrer meta tag:** `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` has no effect in most email clients. It can also strip referrer data in clients that honor it.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/40442bdd-554c-4e50-a3d6-a66de231e76d?cp_tp=…`
  - The tracking pixel `NrGxHu3…=452`. This one should have `alt=""`.
- **Non-HTTPS images:** The 4 "arrow" images and the tracking pixel (see section 2) may be blocked by strict clients. The arrows are decorative, so the impact is limited.
- **Charset declaration:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />` has no `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, so it does not declare a charset. The subject and body contain "Pokémon" and rely on the MIME header charset instead.
- **Markup mismatch:** The doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional, but the tags use XHTML-style self-closing syntax (`<meta … />`). This is harmless in practice.
- **Duplicated CSS:** The `@font-face` and `@media` blocks are repeated across multiple `<style>` tags, which adds weight. The second set also has a `!importa…` fragment, but that is probably the truncation point.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part:** QA reports `[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing or too short: Text version is 0 chars`. A multipart/alternative message with a text part is the expected practice. Its absence can hurt deliverability and breaks accessibility for text-only clients.
- **Not verifiable:** The unsubscribe link, physical postal address, `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results were not in the supplied data. I'm not flagging them.
