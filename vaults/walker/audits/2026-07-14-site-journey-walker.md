# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review
**Date:** July 14, 2026 | **Yesterday's Score: 4/10**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

I went through Skechers.com on my phone today looking for comfort shoes — specifically the Slip-ins Arch Fit line I've been eyeing. The navigation to find the right section was actually pretty decent this time, and the Hands Free Slip-ins page landed me exactly where I needed to be. But here's the thing that stopped me cold: when I went to check my cart after trying to add a shoe, the page came up completely blank. White screen. Nothing. That's not a minor hiccup — that's me closing the app and going to look at Hoka instead. The search results also showed me women's shoes when I typed "comfort shoes," which tells me nobody's paying attention to who I am even after I logged in. Overall, not much better than yesterday.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 3/10

**Down 1 point from yesterday (4/10) — regression on the most critical step: cart.**

The blank cart screen is a conversion killer. I had a $120 shoe in my hand, ready to buy. They lost the sale at the finish line.

## 3. What's Working

**Men's navigation menu (Step 4)** — This was genuinely good. When I opened the MEN section, I saw a clean slide-out panel with large, well-spaced rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. And right below that, "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Extra Wide Fit" — all as direct taps. No hunting around. That's the right way to organize for someone like me who knows what he wants. Touch targets were big enough that I didn't have to fumble.

**Hands Free Slip-ins category page (Step 5)** — The fit filter buttons at the top (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) are large, blue, and obvious. 313 results is a lot, but the breadcrumb "Men > Shoes" told me I was in the right place. Readable without zooming.

**Product page headline (Step 6)** — "Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit Skech-Air - Recomplete" — the title is large and the key words (Slip-ins, Arch Fit) are right there. Price is clear at $120. The members discount "Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER" was a nice touch and made me feel like logging in was worth something.

**Add to Cart button (Step 7)** — The big blue "ADD TO CART" pinned to the bottom of the screen is well-designed. Size tiles are reasonably spaced and the "Please select a size" error in red is clear guidance. No confusion there.

**Promo visibility** — "20% OFF Regular Priced Items, Code: SUMMER" appears persistently in the top banner across every screen. I couldn't miss it.

## 4. What's Weak

**Cart page is completely blank (Step 8) — Critical failure.** White screen. No product. No total. No checkout button. Nothing. I don't know if my shoe is in the cart, if the page crashed, or if I did something wrong. This is the worst possible thing to show a customer who just decided to buy. At 62, I'm not going to debug a website. I'm going to close it and go somewhere else.

**No reviews on the product (Step 6).** "No Reviews" under five empty stars. That's a trust problem for me. When I'm spending $120, I want to know other people have worn them and their feet felt better. I don't buy based on a photo alone. I need someone like me to say "these are great for walking all day." Blank stars make me think this shoe is brand new or nobody's buying it — either way, I'm hesitant.

**Login steps 2 and 3 showed me the same homepage.** I can't tell if I was actually logged in or not. No "Welcome back, Walker" message, no name in the header. The account icon looks the same before and after. If I'm logged in, show me something that confirms it.

**Search for "comfort shoes" returned women's shoes first (Step 9).** 1,440 results is overwhelming, and the first two I see are both labeled "Women's." I'm a man. I logged in. Skechers should know that. Showing me women's products at the top of my search tells me they're not paying attention to who I am. I'd have to filter again just to see men's options — extra steps I shouldn't need.

**Homepage hero "GAME ON!" (Step 1)** — Colorful retro sneakers and a "GAME ON!" tagline feel like they're selling to someone 30 years younger than me. Nothing about comfort, cushioning, or arch support on the first screen I see. If I hadn't already known where to go, this homepage would've made me think this isn't my store.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart immediately.** Whatever caused Step 8 to render blank is an active revenue blocker. This is the single highest-priority fix. Even if everything else is perfect, a blank cart means zero sales.

2. **Surface review counts before launch or flag "Be the first to review."** "No Reviews" with empty stars signals risk. Either don't launch products without reviews or replace the empty stars with "New Arrival — Be the first to review." Don't show me five empty stars.

3. **Personalize search results by gender.** If I'm logged in as a male customer shopping Men's, my "comfort shoes" search should default-filter to Men's. Women's results at the top are a friction point that erodes my trust that this site knows me.

4. **Confirm login state visibly.** Put "Hi, Walker" or a filled-in account icon in the header when I'm logged in. I should never have to wonder if I'm actually signed in.

5. **Swap homepage hero messaging for comfort-first.** Lead with "All-Day Arch Support" or "Slip in. Walk all day." rather than "GAME ON!" My demographic is your bread and butter — speak to us on the first screen.

6. **Cap or pre-filter the 313 Slip-in results.** 313 options with no initial sort is overwhelming. Default to "Best Rated" or "Most Comfortable" so I'm not staring at an "Exclusive" badge shoe with no context.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware, confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/` paths). The primary infrastructure issues are: Kasada bot-protection SDK generating 429s during the test session (including one on `/cart/` itself, blocking checkout), a broken Stylitics CORS configuration, an extremely bloated third-party tag load, and a View Cart page that renders without a `<title>` or `lang` attribute. LCP and CLS are unmeasured across all steps, making a complete Web Vitals assessment impossible.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport height hack:** `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set via JavaScript on the `<html>` element — a well-known workaround for older iOS Safari's dynamic toolbar behavior. Modern iOS/Chrome support `svh`/`dvh`/`lvh` natively; continuing to JS-patch this adds a layout-dependent JS execution on every resize.
- **WebFontLoader (Mulish, 5 weights):** Loaded async, which prevents render-blocking but means fonts may swap in after paint, contributing to CLS. The `wf-active` class is set by JS — without `font-display: optional` or `font-display: swap` + preload hints, FOUT/CLS risk is elevated on mobile.
- **No `preconnect` hints** are visible for high-priority third-party origins (TikTok, Monetate). Only `dns-prefetch` for Monetate is present. On mobile networks, missing `preconnect` adds 1–2 RTTs per origin for TLS negotiation.
- No evidence of `sizes` or `srcset` responsive image handling in the available HTML fragments.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 — Kasada fingerprint endpoint (5 hits):**
```
https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```
This is the Kasada SDK bot-protection flow. The 429s are expected when running from a headless/automated context (the SDK fingerprints the browser environment and rejects non-human signals). In production these should not appear for real users. The repeated retries without backoff suggest the SDK's retry logic is looping.

**429 — `/cart/` (1 hit):**
The cart page itself returning 429 is a separate, higher-severity issue. This is not the Kasada fingerprint endpoint — it is the cart render URL. Possible causes: Kasada challenge blocked the cart request (bot-protection false positive on cart), session rate limiting, or SFCC cart endpoint throttling. If this reproduces for real users, it represents a checkout-blocking regression.

**CORS block — Stylitics:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
Stylitics' CDN is not returning CORS headers for cross-origin stylesheet fetches from `www.skechers.com`. This breaks the outfit widget styling. Root cause is on Stylitics' CDN configuration (`web-assets.stylitics.com`), but Skechers needs to raise it with the vendor. The `ERR_FAILED` is the cascading resource load failure from this blocked stylesheet.

## Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Functional regressions:**
1. **Investigate the 429 on `/cart/`** — reproduce outside headless context to confirm whether real users are hitting cart blocks. If Kasada challenge tokens are not being threaded through to the cart request, this is a checkout funnel break.
2. **Fix the View Cart document skeleton** — the page must emit a valid `<title>`, `<html lang>`, `<main>`, and `<h1>`. This is both an accessibility Level A failure and a template defect. Check whether the cart is a full-page render or an SPA shell injection missing document-level metadata.

**Priority 2 — Third-party hygiene:**
3. **Deduplicate tag firing** — `creativecdn`, `monetate entry.js`, and Yahoo YTC are firing twice on the Men category page. Audit GTM container triggers against hardcoded script tags and remove one source of truth.
4. **Escalate Stylitics CORS misconfiguration** — the vendor needs to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` (or a wildcard) on `web-assets.stylitics.com`.

**Priority 3 — Accessibility:**
5. Fix `aria-required-parent` (critical) and `link-name` (serious) on homepage — these are WCAG 2.1 Level A violations with AT functional impact.
6. Fix landmark structure on homepage (duplicate `<main>`, nested `<aside>`) — affects keyboard and screen-reader navigation.

**Priority 4 — Performance observability:**
7. **Instrument LCP and CLS** on all 8 steps — without these, Web Vitals compliance cannot be assessed and no baseline exists for regressions.
8. **Replace the JS `--vh` hack** with `dvh`/`svh` CSS units and remove the resize listener — iOS 15.4+ and Chrome 108+ support these natively.
9. **Add `preconnect` for high-priority third-party origins** (TikTok analytics, Monetate) — currently only `dns-prefetch` is present, leaving TLS handshake latency unaddressed on mobile.
