# Back to School Starts NOW!📚✏️🍎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Ten emails in ten days — and I'm supposed to feel excited about back-to-school again? I saw this same pitch on July 6th and again on July 8th. At least those opened cleanly. This one hit my inbox and the preview text was a wall of gibberish — some long tracking URL — which almost made me think my phone was glitching. Not a great start.
- When I did open it, my eye jumped straight past the hero because a bunch of kids in sneakers don't speak to me. I'm 62. My kids haven't had a first day of school in twenty years. But then I scrolled down and hit the Brooks section — blue running shoe, "$89.98 NOW" — and that's the first moment the email actually talked to me. I cross-shop Brooks all the time. The sandal blowout at $14.98 at the bottom also caught my attention, though by then I'd already done a lot of scrolling.
- I'd click the Brooks deal. I would not forward this to anyone, and if I got one more back-to-school email from Shoe Carnival this week I'd start reaching for the unsubscribe button.

## What's working

- **Brooks Price Break section** — a specific shoe, a real price ($89.98), a brand I actually check. That's the only part of this email that felt like it was written for me.
- **Multiple concrete offers in one scroll** — $20 off, 25% off family styles, $14.98 sandal floor. Layered savings, easy to see.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — the email rendered fine, no overlapping text or blank blocks. Points for a professional execution.
- **New Balance and Asics logos visible** — brands I recognize; at least the family sale section had names I trust.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is a raw tracking URL** — "Shoe Carnival https://click.email.shoecarnival.com/?qs=ABB7..." is the first thing I read in my inbox. Looks like spam or a tech glitch. Knocked my trust down immediately.
- **Hero is 100% back-to-school — again** — this is the third time this same theme showed up in nine days. July 6 was the pivot, July 8 doubled down, and now today. I've never bought a back-to-school shoe in my life.
- **Ten emails in ten days** — I said this out loud to myself. The cadence is making me numb to the brand. Every day feels like a clearance-rack fire sale.
- **Sandal Blowout buried below the fold** — the one section with truly mass appeal (slip-on comfort, summer shoes) is dead last. I had to scroll past kids' shoes to get there.
- **Kids section is irrelevant real estate** — bright colored kid shoes take up meaningful screen space that could be showing me men's walking or slip-on styles.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately** — the leaking tracking URL is the single most damaging thing in this send. Replace with copy like "Brooks deals, sandal markdowns & more inside" before anything else ships.
- 2. **Lead with the Brooks section for loyalty members who skew 50+** — if Shoe Carnival knows I've looked at Brooks and New Balance before, flip the block order so comfort/performance is the hero, not back-to-school.
- 3. **Sandal Blowout moves to second position** — $14.98 starting price is genuinely eye-catching for someone like me. Bury it below kids' shoes and it's invisible.
- 4. **Dial down the cadence** — one email every two or three days would feel like news. Ten in ten days feels like panic.
- 5. **Give back-to-school a specific end date** — "Starts NOW!" tells me nothing. "Ends Sunday" gives me a reason to act today instead of closing the tab.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Shoe Carnival "Back to School Starts NOW!" (2026-07-10)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with well-layered responsive CSS and standard ESP compatibility fixes. One critical bug was identified: an unresolved merge token (`[UNIQUE]`) is rendering literally in the open-tracking pixel URL, which will corrupt open-rate reporting for all recipients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Unresolved merge token in open-tracking pixel**

The open-pixel src contains a literal `[UNIQUE]` token that was never substituted at send time:

```
https://0di86k1v.emltrk.com/v2/0di86k1v?d=[UNIQUE]
```

Every recipient fires the same pixel URL. This collapses all open events to a single identity, making the `[UNIQUE]` deduplication logic on the tracking server useless. Open counts and unique open rates for this send are unreliable.

**Informational — 26 click-redirect links unverifiable**

All 26 CTA links pass through a click-tracking redirect domain and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URLs and redirect chain integrity could not be confirmed. No failure, but a risk surface if the ESP redirect layer is misconfigured.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 2 content images**

Both hosted content images have no `alt` attribute:

- `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/9521013e-7444-4804-9257-f83e6db7b8bc.jpg`
- `https://image.email.shoecarnival.com/lib/fe3311737164047d701077/m/1/a97db5d3-cfac-4b76-923a-ca38510bf3a6.jpg`

Recipients with images disabled (common in B2B/corporate clients) see blank space with no context. This also fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels**

Both tracking pixel `<img>` tags (`ieezzy5m`, `0di86k1v` domains) have no `alt=""`. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) so screen readers don't announce them. Minor but should be standardized.

**CSS note — overlapping breakpoints**

Four breakpoints are defined: `320px`, `450px`, `600px`, `660px`. The `600px` and `660px` blocks define some of the same class names (`.pad20`, `.noLeftRightPad`, etc.) with identical values. This is redundant but not broken; harmless in practice.

**CSS note — duplicate `color:inherit` declaration**

In the global link reset:
```css
color:inherit !important;
color:inherit;
```
Both lines appear sequentially. The second is dead code — the `!important` version always wins. No rendering impact.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical (see §2)** — `[UNIQUE]` in open-pixel query param is the only detected unresolved token in the visible source.

No other bare merge tokens (e.g., `[[FIRST_NAME]]`, `{%first_name%}`) are visible in the truncated HTML. Full source would need inspection to confirm no subscriber-facing tokens are unsubstituted.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe** — Not verifiable from truncated HTML. The footer containing the physical mailing address and unsubscribe link is not present in the provided source excerpt. Must be confirmed in the full source.

**Sending domain** — `shoecarnival@email.shoecarnival.com` uses a dedicated sending subdomain, which is correct practice (isolates deliverability reputation from the root domain). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from HTML alone; should be verified against received headers.

**Dark mode** — `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` and `supported-color-schemes: light` explicitly opt out of dark mode rendering. No technical issue, but recipients using forced dark mode in Outlook or Apple Mail may see color inversion artifacts on images with white backgrounds.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM tagging confirmed** — One tracking link in the QA data exposes the campaign parameter:

```
utm_campaign=SCA_20260710_LPG_athletics_brooks_sandals_AM
```

Pattern follows `SCA_{date}_{placement}_{category}_{vendor}_{segment}`. The `_AM` suffix suggests a morning-cohort send variant. Assuming consistent UTM application across all 26 links (unverifiable due to redirect skipping), attribution to GA4/analytics should be intact.

**Landing page alignment** — Cannot verify without resolving redirect chains. With 26 unprobed links, there is no confirmation that click destinations are live or correctly mapped to the campaign's advertised products (Brooks, sandals).
