# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 4/10 (prev 4/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-12**
## Full review
Here is my full walk-through review as Walker Miles.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Skechers.com gets me to the right product category faster than I expected — "Hands Free Slip-ins" is right there in the men's menu, the size picker is clean, and the ADD TO CART button is impossible to miss. But then the wheels come off. The cart page loaded as a completely blank white screen — I can't check out. Search for "comfort shoes" shows me women's shoes. And the whole site feels like it was designed for my 30-year-old nephew, not me. Good bones, broken at the moment of truth.

---

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

Same score as yesterday. The navigation improvements are real, but a blank cart page is a conversion-killer that cancels out any gains. No improvement.

---

## 3. What's Working

**Men's menu is actually good for me.** The drawer navigation (Step 4) has "Comfort Technologies," "Hands Free Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," and "Extra Wide Fit" all visible without digging. That's exactly what I need and I didn't have to hunt for it. Whoever organized that menu understood my type of shopper.

**Fit options front and center on category page.** Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit as large pill buttons right at the top of the Hands Free Slip-ins page (Step 5) — I can filter before I even see a single shoe. That's smart.

**The size picker is comfortable to use.** Step 7 shows a generous grid of size boxes — each one is big enough to tap without squinting or mis-tapping. "Don't See Your Size?" is a nice touch too.

**ADD TO CART button.** Full-width, bold blue, pinned to the bottom of the screen. I can't miss it even if my glasses are off.

**Product name does its job.** "Skechers Slip-ins: Arch Fit D'Lites 330" — right in the name, I can see it's Slip-ins AND Arch Fit. Two of my three priorities confirmed without reading fine print.

**Members discount surfaced on PDP.** The red "Members Get 20% OFF! Use Code: SUMMER" line on the product page (Step 6) makes me feel like logging in was worth it — or would, if the cart worked.

---

## 4. What's Weak

**The cart is broken. Full stop.** Step 8 is a completely blank white page. Nothing. No items, no total, no checkout button — just white. This is a catastrophic failure. I selected a shoe, I went to check out, and the site handed me nothing. At my age I don't have patience for "try again later." I'd close the tab and call the 800 number or drive to DSW.

**Search "comfort shoes" shows me women's shoes.** Step 9: 1,437 results, and the first two products I see are both labeled "Women's." I'm presumably logged in as a male account. If Skechers knows who I am, why is it showing me women's clogs? This is a personalization failure that tells me the site doesn't actually know me.

**Homepage hero is not for me.** Steps 1–2 show two young people in streetwear posing in front of a building. I'm 62. Nothing in that image says "this brand understands your feet." Hoka and New Balance both show active older adults in their hero images. This one doesn't.

**Login state is invisible.** Steps 1, 2, and 3 look identical — same homepage, same cart showing 0. I can't tell if I'm actually logged in. There's no "Welcome, Walker" or name anywhere. If I'm logged in, show me something that proves it.

**3 stars, 7 reviews.** The Arch Fit D'Lites 330 has a mediocre rating and almost no reviews (Step 6). That's thin social proof for a $130 shoe. I comparison-shop against Brooks and Hoka — those brands have hundreds of reviews per model. This makes me hesitate.

**313 results with no default sorting.** The Hands Free Slip-ins page dumps 313 products on me with no obvious curation. I don't know if I'm seeing best-sellers, new arrivals, or random inventory. Overwhelming.

**No cushioning language on the PDP.** The product page (Step 6) has the Slip-ins badge but I don't see words like "cushioning," "memory foam," or "arch support" in the visible area. That's my third priority and I have to scroll or read fine print to find it.

---

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page immediately.** This is a P0. A blank cart means zero conversions from anyone who reached that step.

2. **Honor gender in search results.** If I'm logged in as male, "comfort shoes" should return men's results first, or at minimum a 50/50 mix — not two women's products leading the page.

3. **Put my name in the header when logged in.** Even just "Hi, Walker" next to the account icon removes the uncertainty about whether my session is active.

4. **Add cushioning/arch support copy above the fold on PDP.** Two bullet points — "Arch Fit® insole" and "Air-cooled memory foam cushioning" — before the size picker would close the sale for someone like me.

5. **Default the category page to "Best Rated" or "Most Popular" sort.** 313 options is too many. Surface the winners.

6. **Swap the hero image seasonally for one that includes people 55+.** I'm not asking for a walker and orthopedic shoes — just someone my age being active. That small change builds trust.

---

## 6. Bottom Line

Skechers has built the right road signs — Hands Free Slip-ins in the menu, fit options upfront, clean size picker — but the bridge is out at the end. A blank cart page means I cannot buy today no matter how well the rest worked. The search personalization miss and the youth-skewed homepage add up to a site that talks past me even when it has exactly what I need. Fix the cart. Fix search defaults. I'll come back. Until then, Brooks.com still has a working checkout.

**Score: 4/10 — no change from yesterday.** Navigation improved; cart is broken; net zero.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The skechers.com mobile journey runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFCC/Demandware, confirmed by `/on/demandware.static/` asset paths). The site carries a very heavy third-party script payload, is actively being rate-limited by its own bot-protection SDK (Kasada), and has a cart page that appears to be a JavaScript-rendered overlay that strips document-level metadata. LCP and CLS were not captured, leaving the most consumer-impactful Web Vitals unknown.

---

## Accessibility

**17 axe violations across 3 pages.**

| Severity | Issue | Page(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | `aria-required-parent` — ARIA children exist outside their required parent role | Homepage |
| Serious | `link-name` — links with no discernible text (likely icon-only buttons) | Homepage |
| Serious | `document-title` — `<title>` is empty or absent | View Cart |
| Serious | `html-has-lang` — `<html>` has no `lang` attribute | View Cart |
| Moderate | Duplicate `<main>` landmark | Homepage |
| Moderate | `<aside>`/complementary not top-level | Homepage, Search |
| Moderate | Heading order skipped (e.g. h1→h3) | Homepage |
| Moderate | No `<main>` landmark at all | View Cart |
| Moderate | No `<h1>` on page | View Cart |
| Minor | `aria-allowed-role` on incorrect elements | Homepage, Search |

The View Cart cluster is the most severe: if the cart is rendered as a client-side overlay replacing the document, the entire `<html>` shell is being swapped without setting `lang` or `<title>`. Screen readers will announce an untitled, language-unknown document. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 (Language of Page) and 2.4.2 (Page Titled).

---

## SEO

- **View Cart missing `<title>`** — Any direct deep-link to `/cart/` will be indexed as an untitled page. More likely the 429 is preventing proper render (see below), but the structural gap is real.
- **Log In and Men category both show `https://www.skechers.com/` as the captured URL** — suggests these states are SPA modal overlays on the root URL rather than distinct routes. If Googlebot renders these the same way, category and auth pages share the homepage URL, meaning no separate indexable signal for those states.
- **No canonical or hreflang issues observable** from the provided HTML excerpts — but the truncation limits confidence.
- **Third-party script volume** (15+ distinct origins in `<head>`) can delay indexable content if Googlebot's render budget is hit before meaningful DOM is painted.

## Mobile Optimization

- **`--vh` CSS custom property** is set inline on `<html>` (`--vh: 6.640000000000001px`). This is a JavaScript workaround for the iOS Safari 100vh bug. It's functional but requires JS to execute before viewport-height-dependent layouts render correctly — a possible LCP contributor.
- **`vice-managed-src` attribute** on several third-party scripts (Pinterest, Impact, Facebook, Nextdoor, Taboola, Yahoo x2) indicates a consent management platform (likely TrustArc, confirmed by inline TrustArc styles) is deferring those scripts. This is correct behavior for GDPR/CCPA compliance, but the number of deferred scripts suggests the CMP config may be under-inclusive — some tracking scripts load unconditionally.
- **Protocol-relative URL** (`//se.monetate.net/js/2/.../entry.js`) — a legacy pattern that defaults to the page's protocol. Not a bug on HTTPS, but an outdated practice.
- No evidence of `<link rel="preload">` for LCP image candidates in the provided HTML samples.

## Console & Network Errors

**Kasada bot protection rate-limiting (429 — 6 occurrences):**

```
429 https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.522
```

This endpoint is the Kasada KPSDK fingerprint collection endpoint. Getting 429s here means the session was flagged as a bot and the SDK is being throttled. The session likely triggered Kasada's risk threshold (automated test environment, headless browser, or repeated rapid navigation patterns). **Critically, `/cart/` also returned 429** — meaning the cart page itself was blocked. A real user in this session state would see a broken cart.

**Stylitics CORS failure:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...' blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header
```
The Stylitics outfit-recommendation widget CSS is being blocked. The stylesheet is being loaded via `<link>` but Stylitics' CDN isn't sending the CORS header for the skechers.com origin. This is a configuration issue on the Stylitics CDN side — the widget will fail to render its layout on any page where this occurs.

**Root cause for 429 cluster:** Automated/headless browser fingerprint detected by Kasada → KPSDK enters a retry/refresh loop → loop itself hits rate limit → cascades to cart endpoint. In production this should only affect bot traffic, but it means this audit session was partially blocked and some observed states (especially View Cart) may reflect degraded/bot-blocked rendering rather than the real user experience.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Fix now:**
1. **View Cart: inject `<title>` and `<html lang="en">`** when the cart overlay/SPA state activates. If this is a full-page route (not overlay), the server must render these unconditionally. Failing this, the page fails WCAG and will be indexed as untitled.
2. **Kasada + cart 429:** Audit the rate-limit policy on `/cart/` — it should never 429 a real user session. Either the bot-score threshold is too aggressive or the KPSDK retry loop is causing the cart endpoint to see the session as an attack.

**P1 — High impact:**
3. **Remove duplicate script tags:** Yahoo `ytc.js` loads twice on all pages; Monetate loads twice; CreativeCDN loads twice on Men category. Each duplicate is a wasted network round-trip.
4. **Stylitics CORS:** Engage the Stylitics integration team to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to their CDN response headers for `web-assets.stylitics.com`.
5. **Fix critical ARIA violation:** `aria-required-parent` on Homepage is the only critical-severity axe violation — find the element(s) using ARIA roles that require a parent container role and wrap them correctly.
6. **Fix Homepage link-name violations:** Icon-only links need `aria-label` or visually-hidden text.

**P2 — Performance:**
7. **Capture LCP and CLS for all steps** — the most important Web Vitals are missing from this audit. Use `web-vitals` JS lib or CrUX field data.
8. **Replace WebFontLoader with CSS `font-display: swap` + `<link rel="preload">`** — eliminates FOUT-driven CLS and removes a third-party CDN dependency.
9. **Audit Tag Manager for duplicate and unnecessary tags** — 20+ third-party origins with confirmed duplicates suggests the GTM container has accumulated tags without regular cleanup. Each origin adds ~50-200ms on a cold mobile connection.
10. **Investigate 458ms TTFB on Login/Men category** — confirm whether these routes bypass CDN caching due to session cookies, and if so, evaluate whether partial caching (stale-while-revalidate on the shell) is viable.
