# Looks Good. Feels Good. Get Yours.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-04**
## Executive summary

- Well, here comes Crocs again with the same 30% off and free shipping. I got that on 10/2 and again on 10/3 with "Reminder" in front. This time it's dressed up with a big photo of a white chunky shoe and a black speech-bubble caption with emojis. The caption reads like a teenager wrote it. "Literally everything else" and a flame emoji aren't aimed at a 62-year-old.
- The one line that does speak to me is "built for all-day comfort," and it sits in the hero. But the Echo II looks like a lumpy sneaker-clog with brown swirls on the sole. I couldn't tell whether it's slip-on or has any arch support, and there's no price. The 30% offer is buried in the middle of the email, below the hero.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Shop the Sale" button, since 30% off plus free shipping is something I can use. I wouldn't click the Echo II. I'd mark it read and wouldn't forward it to anyone. It's the fourth near-identical Crocs promo this week.

## What's working

- The "Take 30% Off + Get Free Shipping!" block is big, black on pale blue, and easy to read on my phone.
- "Shop the Sale" is a clear button, and "Exclusions Apply" is stated right under it instead of hidden.
- The black "Free shipping on qualified orders" bar at the top tells me about the shipping before I scroll.
- The category buttons (Clogs, Sandals, Sale, Jibbitz) are large, easy to tap, and let me skip the hero.
- The deadline is spelled out at the bottom: Wednesday, October 7th at 11:59pm MT.

## What's weak

- The hero caption with the emojis and "literally everything else" feels written for kids, not for me.
- The 30% offer is below the fold. The first screen is a shoe photo with no deal.
- The Echo II has no price, no sizing and no mention of cushioning or arch support. "Unparalleled style" doesn't tell me how it feels on my feet.
- It's the same offer as 10/2 and 10/3, and I'm tired of seeing it.
- The fine print is tiny and hard to read, and the "Refer a Friend" and app banners add clutter.
- The shoe photo is cropped to a sole and a pair of striped pants, so I never see the whole shoe.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the 30% off + free shipping in the hero and move the Echo II below it. Offer first, shoe second.
- 2. Replace the slang caption with comfort facts, like "cushioned all day, slips on in seconds."
- 3. Show the whole shoe, add a price, and add a line about support and fit.
- 4. Vary the creative and spacing. Don't send the same promo three days running.
- 5. Make the fine print larger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% Off + Free Shipping Through Wednesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Echo II: All-Day Comfort, 30% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Slip-on comfort for your feet, plus free shipping. Ends 10/7.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cushioned all day. Take 30% off today.`

## Technical Audit

1. **Technical Summary**
The email's HTML is well-formed for responsive rendering (viewport meta, media queries, Outlook and Gmail hacks, preheader), but it has several `http://` image sources, a Demdex pixel with no alt text, and an unsubscribe or footer section I can't verify because the source was truncated. The automated QA reports a 100% pass rate, but it flags 8 warnings that are real technical issues.

2. **Link & Tracking Issues**
- 25 tracking links were skipped in the QA check (HTTP probe skipped for click-redirect domains), so I can't confirm click-through resolution or destination status. The QA summary doesn't treat this as a failure.
- The open pixel `https://click.crocs-email.com/open.aspx?LWKVIGUS53FEJKWZTLKVH5C3XA.10205&d=10205&bmt=0` uses a subscriber-specific token. This is expected for ESP tracking.
- A second 1x1 beacon, `https://demandwarecrocs.demdex.net/event?d_dpid=196107&c_emailopened=Yes&d_dpuid=9153f33f...`, is a third-party Adobe Audience Manager (Demdex) pixel. It is wrapped in `visibility:hidden;display:none`. Some clients won't load a `display:none` image, so this open signal will be under-counted. This is a data-collection caveat, not a defect.
- The QA summary reports `0 issue(s), 0 warning(s)`, but it also lists eight `[WARN]` lines. The summary counter doesn't reflect the INFO-level warnings.

3. **Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Mixed content / `http://` images (7 instances).** These are all on `http://image.crocs-email.com/lib/fe66157070650c797512/m/1/`:
  - `9c36c0ef-...png` ("Download on the App Store")
  - `13246762-...png` ("Get It On Google Play")
  - `c4df71ec-...png` ("Find Your Store")
  - `e18949ff-...png` (Instagram)
  - `80f68cd8-...png` (Facebook)
  - `51d8144e-...png` (TikTok)
  - `1187ef58-...png` (YouTube)

  Some clients and proxies block or rewrite non-HTTPS sources. The app-store badges and social icons may then show as broken, leaving only the alt text. The same host serves HTTPS elsewhere in the template (it is the ESP image CDN), so switching these to `https://` should be straightforward.
- **Missing alt text.** The Demdex `event` pixel has no `alt` attribute. The visible 1x1 open pixel does have `alt=""`. Because the Demdex pixel is hidden and decorative, the impact is low, but `alt=""` would keep it consistent.
- **Web fonts.** `TT Crocs` is loaded from `media.crocs.com` with `font-display: swap`. Only the woff2 and woff fallbacks are declared, and I can't see the fallback font stack because the source was truncated. I'm not flagging this as a defect.
- **`<meta>` tags.** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=edge" />` lack `http-equiv`, so they are not functional charset or compatibility declarations. Charset detection falls back to the HTTP or MIME header, which is normally fine for email.
- **`<title>`.** The title is the generic "Crocs". This is minor and has no rendering impact.
- Positives: `lang="en"` on `<html>`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`, the Gmail `u + .email-body` hack, and responsive utility classes are all present.

4. **Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible source shows no unresolved tokens such as `%%first_name%%`, `{{...}}` or `[FNAME]`. The open pixel and the Demdex pixel carry resolved subscriber identifiers. The source was truncated, so merge tokens further down can't be assessed.

5. **Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- The truncated HTML doesn't include the footer, so I can't confirm the unsubscribe link, the physical postal address, or `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers. These items are not assessable from the data provided. I'm not marking them as failures.
- No SPF, DKIM or DMARC header data was supplied. The From address `email@crocs-email.com` uses a dedicated sending domain, but its alignment can't be verified.

6. **Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All 25 tracked links were skipped, so I can't confirm that UTM parameters, the final destinations or the link-to-landing-page match (Echo II product page) are correct.
- The preheader says "The Echo II. It's a look that turns heads." The subject says "Looks Good. Feels Good. Get Yours." Their landing-page alignment can't be verified.

7. **Recommendations**
1. Change the seven `http://image.crocs-email.com/...` image URLs to `https://` (app-store badges, "Find Your Store" and the four social icons).
2. Add `alt=""` to the Demdex `event` pixel. Consider moving it off `display:none` if open-tracking fidelity matters, or accept the under-count.
3. Fix the two `<meta>` tags so they use `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"` (or drop them).
4. Resolve a sample of the 25 skipped tracking links in a real client or ESP preview to verify the destinations, UTM parameters and Echo II landing page.
5. Improve QA tooling. Make the summary counter include the `[WARN]` findings, and run the compliance checks (unsubscribe, address, `List-Unsubscribe`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC) on the full, untruncated message with headers.
