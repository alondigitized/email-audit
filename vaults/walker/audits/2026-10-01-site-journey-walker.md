# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-01**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I got through the browsing fine, but I never got a shoe into my cart. The homepage and menus are clear enough, and the Slip-ins page and search results show me the right kind of shoe. Then Add to Cart failed, and the cart page came up as a completely blank white screen. If I were really shopping, I'd have given up there and gone to Hoka, Brooks or New Balance.

Compared with yesterday's 3/10, this is about the same. Browsing and search look decent, but the checkout path is still broken. I only have yesterday's score, not its details. I can't name specific regressions, only what I saw today.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10.** It's unchanged from yesterday.

A shopper who can't buy produces no revenue, whatever the rest of the site looks like. The score isn't lower because the product and search pages did what I needed.

## 3. What's Working

- **The top of the homepage is easy to read on a phone.** The logo, search, account, cart and menu icons are big and well spaced. "MENU" is labeled in words under the icon, which helps someone my age.
- **The shortcut tabs match what I shop for.** "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit" and "Max Cushion" sit right under the header. I'd tap "Arch Fit" first, though the row cuts off at "Max Cus" and I didn't know it scrolls sideways.
- **The Men menu is clean.** It opens as a big list with a Back button and an X. The rows are tall enough to tap without mistakes. "Comfort Technologies" is in the list.
- **The Men's Shoes page has useful quick links.** "Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first things I see, with photos. That is how I'd want to shop.
- **The product page is a good fit.** It's a Slip-ins Contour Foam shoe at $88.00 with 49 reviews and 4 stars. The "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge is on the image, and the price and rating are easy to read.
- **Search worked.** "comfort shoes" returned 1,566 results, and the first ones were Slip-ins with "Arch Comfort" and "Contour Foam" in the names. Shoe photos with color swatches and prices were visible.
- **The size picker is a good size.** The size buttons are large and easy to tap, and sold-out sizes are crossed out (6.5).

## 4. What's Weak

- **The cart page was blank.** Step 8 showed nothing but white space. There was no cart, no message and no way to continue. This is the worst problem I saw.
- **Add to Cart didn't work, and the page didn't guide me.** The page showed "Please select a width" and "Please select a size," and the cart badge stayed at 0. Size 13.0 looked outlined, but the page still asked for a size. I couldn't tell whether my tap registered. The error messages are far from the top, so on a phone I'd easily miss them or get confused.
- **I couldn't log in.** The "Log In" step showed the same homepage as before, with no sign-in form. The page still said "Log In for 20% OFF," so I was never recognized. Nothing felt personal to me.
- **The popup step looked identical to the homepage.** I can't tell whether any popup showed up or was dismissed. If one did, I never saw it.
- **There's no comparison against other brands and no price-vs-quality math.** I didn't see any mention of how this shoe compares to Hoka or Brooks. The only price help was "15% OFF or Log In for 20% OFF!" on the product and search pages, which I'd read as a gimmick.
- **The comfort details aren't visible on the first screen of the product page.** I saw the price and photo, but nothing about arch support, cushioning or width right away. I'd have to scroll to find out.
- **The first search results leaned women's.** The first two were labeled "Women's." I'm shopping for men, and the search gave me no clear way to narrow that on the first screen.
- **The text is on the small side.** The gray note lines, the "Women's" labels and the fine print in the top banners would make me pinch to zoom. The two stacked promo bars crowd the top and say slightly different things ("Members Save 20% OFF" and "25% off Apparel & Bags").
- **The price shows a range.** "$59.99 - $88.00" in the search results doesn't tell me which shoe or size costs what.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page first.** A blank cart is a revenue bug. It should always show items, an empty-cart message, or an error with a retry button. Add monitoring so this alerts someone.
2. **Make Add to Cart clearer.** When width or size is missing, scroll to the missing choice and highlight it. Pre-select the common width, or show a short note like "Pick a width, then a size." Don't let the button look like it worked when it didn't.
3. **Show a small confirmation after adding an item.** Update the cart badge right away.
4. **Show comfort facts near the top of each product page.** Put arch support, cushioning, width options and Slip-ins/hands-free status in a short list under the price.
5. **Make the sign-in step obvious.** If the login screen fails to open, the site should say so. If login works, the header should greet me and the "Log In for 20%" prompts should go away.
6. **Add a "Compare" or "Why this shoe" block for comfort-first shoppers.** A short line on cushioning level and value would help people like me, who cross-shop other brands.
7. **Increase base text size and clean up the top banners.** Use one promo bar, not two.
8. **Let me filter search results by Men's up front.**

## 6. Bottom Line

The shoes and the menus are fine. The part where I actually pay is broken, because the cart came up blank. I wouldn't buy here today. I'd go back to Brooks, Hoka or New Balance. My score is still 3/10, and it won't go up until a customer can add a shoe and see it in the cart.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was heavily degraded by bot-protection rate limiting, so several findings below may reflect the blocked session more than the site's normal behavior.

- **Rate limiting:** Six requests returned HTTP 429. Five were the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.797`). One was the document request for `/cart/`. The Kasada hits show an anti-bot layer challenging the automated session.
- **Cart page not captured:** The cart document itself was throttled. The View Cart axe results (no `<title>`, no `lang`, no `<main>`, no `<h1>`) look like an empty or error response, not the real cart template. I treated them as unverified.
- **Navigation not confirmed:** The HTML samples for Log In and Men category both report the URL `https://www.skechers.com/` and match the homepage markup. Those steps may not have navigated, or may have opened overlays on the same document. Their TTFB values (440ms) are near-identical to the homepage (459ms).
- **Missing metrics:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step (`?`), so there is no Core Web Vitals evidence. Only TTFB is available.

## Accessibility

17 axe violations across three steps (2 critical, 2 serious on the homepage, 2 serious on View Cart):

**Homepage (9 rules)**
- `button-name` (critical): at least one button has no accessible name.
- `aria-required-parent` (critical): an element with an ARIA role is missing its required parent role, for example a `listitem` or `tab` outside its `list` or `tablist`.
- `link-name` (serious): at least one link has no discernible text.
- `aria-allowed-role` (minor): a role is applied to an element that doesn't allow it.
- Landmark structure problems (moderate):
  - `landmark-no-duplicate-main`: more than one `main` landmark.
  - `landmark-main-is-top-level` and `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: these landmarks are nested inside other landmarks.
  - `landmark-unique`: landmarks are not distinguishable from each other.
  - `region`: content sits outside any landmark.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4 rules)**
- This step repeats `aria-allowed-role`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` and `region`. These are likely from shared header and footer templates.

**View Cart (4 rules, treat as unverified)**
- `document-title` and `html-has-lang` (serious): the response has no `<title>` or `lang`.
- `landmark-one-main` and `page-has-heading-one` (moderate): the response has no `main` landmark or `h1`.
- Given the 429 on `/cart/`, this is probably a throttled or interstitial page.

The homepage root has `lang="en"`, so the missing `lang` is specific to the cart response.

## SEO

- **Cart and search:** `document-title` is missing on the View Cart response, and there is no `h1` there. This is only a concern if it's the real cart template. Cart pages are normally `noindex` anyway.
- **Homepage:** `lang="en"` is present. No other SEO evidence is available from the truncated HTML, so I can't assess canonicals, meta tags or structured data.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** Homepage 459ms, Log In 440ms, Men 440ms, View Cart 142ms. Men > Shoes, Product detail, Add to Cart and Search are 24–32ms.
  - The 24–32ms values are implausibly low for a network round trip. They suggest client-side route transitions, cache hits or stale navigation entries, not fresh document loads.
  - The ~440–459ms values are within a normal range for a dynamic origin response.
- **LCP and CLS:** Not captured, so no conclusion is possible.
- **Third-party load:** The homepage `<head>` already contains 20+ async third-party tags, and the Men step shows about 30. They include:
  - GTM and gtag (three IDs)
  - Monetate
  - Quantum Metric
  - TikTok, Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Meta, Nextdoor, Taboola and Yahoo (`ytc.js`) pixels
  - Bing, Impact, RTB House (`creativecdn`), Bloomreach (`brsrvr`), Pulse Insights and Tune (`go2sdk`)
- **Duplicate scripts:** `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js` is injected twice, and `tags.creativecdn.com/dhGobFICKSkk6J9nvQPw.js` is injected twice on the Men step. These are redundant downloads and executions.
- **Font loading:** `webfontloader.js` comes from `cdnjs.cloudflare.com`, which adds a third-party origin to the font critical path. The `wf-*-active` classes show Mulish loaded through JS.
- **Cross-origin script integrity:** `piBootStrapperV1_2-min.js` carries SRI (`integrity=sha384-…`). The other third-party scripts in the sample do not.

## Mobile Optimization

- **Viewport units:** `--vh: 6.64px` is set inline on `<html>`, a JS-computed viewport-height workaround. A value this small suggests it was computed at an unusually small window height, which could affect any layout that uses it. The value probably reflects the headless viewport, not a real device.
- **Third-party weight:** The tag volume above is heavier on constrained mobile CPUs and networks.
- **Not assessed:** Tap-target size, viewport meta and responsive images, because the HTML sample is truncated.

## Console & Network Errors

- **HTTP 429 (6 requests, 5 distinct occurrences in the console):**
  - Five are Kasada `/fp` fingerprint calls. They repeat on every step, so the challenge isn't resolving. Kasada blocks or throttles the headless session.
  - The sixth is `GET /cart/`. It leaves the cart step uncovered.
- **`Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed in this document` (5 times):** A script is registering an `unload` handler under a Permissions-Policy that disallows it. This is a Chrome deprecation. Unload handlers also block back/forward cache, so identifying the owning script is worthwhile. The source isn't shown, so I can't attribute it. Third-party tags are the likely candidates.
- **`Unsupported Summarizer API languages…` (2 times):** This comes from Chrome's built-in Summarizer API, called with an unsupported language. It likely originates from the browser, an extension or an injected script, not from first-party code. I can't confirm the source from this output.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the bot challenge handled.** Allow-list the audit client with the site owner or use an approved test environment, or slow the request cadence. Then recapture View Cart, since the current cart results aren't usable.
2. **Fix the capture harness:**
   - Verify that Log In and Men category actually change `location`.
   - Collect LCP and CLS with a `PerformanceObserver` injected before navigation.
   - Rerun, because eight steps with no LCP or CLS is a gap.
3. **Fix homepage accessibility, starting with the two critical issues:**
   - Add accessible names to icon-only buttons (`aria-label` or visible text).
   - Correct the ARIA parent/child roles.
   - Add text or `aria-label` to empty links.
   - Remove the nested or duplicate `main` and `aside` landmarks.
4. **Give repeated landmarks unique labels** (`aria-label` on multiple `nav` or `aside` elements) and place all content inside landmarks.
5. **Remove duplicate tag injections** (`ytc.js`, `creativecdn`) and audit the Tag Manager container for redundancy. Defer or consent-gate non-essential pixels.
6. **Find the source of the `unload` handlers** and migrate to `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`.
7. **Replace the cdnjs `webfontloader` dependency** with self-hosted fonts and `font-display: swap`, or `<link rel="preload">` for the critical weights.
8. **Add SRI** where third-party scripts are stable and versioned.
