# Lightweight Style for Warmer Days
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Seven or eight Skechers emails in the past week, and here's another one. Before I even opened it, my inbox was showing that same broken JSON schema nonsense in the preview — the same garbage code leak I've seen in four or five of their sends this month. At some point that stops being a technical glitch and starts being the brand's calling card, which is not a compliment. The subject "Lightweight Style for Warmer Days" is pleasant enough, doesn't set off any alarm bells, but it's not waving me down either.
- Once inside, the BOGO 50% off banner hit me immediately — no scrolling, no hunting — and that's the right move. Canvas Classics and Boat Shoes are on the menu. They're handsome summer shoes; I won't argue with that. But nobody here is talking about my feet. No cushioning mention, no arch support, no slip-on entry. This is a summer lookbook, and I am not the lookbook customer. Yesterday's email ("Hands-Free Convenience Meets Next-Level Comfort") knew who I was. This one is addressing someone twenty years younger who's headed to a dock.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it. If the BOGO is still running at the end of the week and I need a casual canvas pair, I might dig this out, but that's a lukewarm maybe.

## What's working

- **BOGO front and center** — "Buy One, Get One 50% Off" is the very first banner I see. No coupon hunting, no fine print obscuring the lead.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — Banner → canvas section → boat shoe section → category grid. My eye knew where to go at each step without any confusion.
- **Email body renders without issues** — No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together. Clean.
- **Loyalty reward is visible** — The Skechers Plus $5 reward note near the bottom is a quiet trust cue; tells me being a member matters here.
- **Seasonal timing lands** — Canvas and boat shoes in late May makes calendar sense. They're not pushing rain boots.

## What's weak

- **Broken preview — fifth time this month** — The JSON schema code leaking into my inbox preview is now a pattern, not a one-off. At this point I expect it from Skechers and that expectation lowers my trust before I even tap open.
- **Zero comfort vocabulary** — No cushioning, no arch support, no memory foam, no slip-on. The one word in the subject that gestures at comfort is "lightweight," and that's it. Yesterday's send spoke my language; this one doesn't.
- **No expiration on the BOGO** — A deal with no deadline is a deal I put off. "Ends May 25" would push me to act now.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — This many Skechers emails this week means they're starting to blur. Even when the content is fine, the volume makes me less attentive to each individual send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is the number-one priority across the entire program.** Five broken previews in a month is a systemic problem. Write two sentences of real hook copy and inject it at the top of every template. Everything downstream benefits.
- 2. **Add one comfort line to the canvas and boat shoe copy.** "Memory foam insole" or "cushioned footbed, slip-on entry" bridges lifestyle and comfort. That single phrase changes who reaches for their wallet.
- 3. **Put an end date on the BOGO.** "Offer ends May 25" or "3 days left" is free urgency. Without it, I file this away and forget it.
- 4. **Segment the canvas lookbook away from the comfort-first subscriber.** If I've been clicking hands-free and walking-shoe content, send me that. If someone else is clicking canvas sneakers, send them this. One list, two very different emails.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Lightweight Style for Warmer Days"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken-experience bug (empty personalization token) and a systemic HTTP image-serving issue across the full asset library. No structural rendering failures, but 13+ images risk being blocked by modern email clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Third-party DMP pixel — subscriber data exposure**
Two Krux (`beacon.krxd.net`) pixels pass subscriber identifiers to a third-party data platform:
- SHA-256 hashed email: `partner_uid=9153f33fe5cdf59baf088bb835d3b39ef90f188bd8540a4faba92899227ced00`
- Raw subscriber ID: `partner_uid=121173391`

These are intentional DMP syncs, but confirm data sharing with Salesforce/Krux is active on every send.

**Schema-less Return Path pixel**
```
src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499"
```
No `http://` or `https://` prefix. In email rendering contexts this typically defaults to HTTP, not HTTPS. Should be explicitly `https://`.

**27 click-redirect links** — all routed through `click.emails.skechers.com`. Not a bug, but means 100% of click tracking depends on that subdomain's uptime.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image CDN — systemic (13 affected images)**
All production assets are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com/`. Gmail, Outlook 2016+, and Apple Mail proxy images over HTTPS; HTTP-only origins can be blocked or fail to load.

Affected assets include the logo, all footer icons (App Store, Google Play, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, AfterPay, Curbside Pickup, Find A Store):
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  ← logo
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png  ← unnamed
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif  ← third-party open pixel
```
The CDN should be `https://`. This is likely a template-level configuration issue, not per-send.

**Missing alt text — 2 images**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 open pixel) — decorative/tracking, `alt=""` is acceptable but currently absent
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — unknown content image with no alt attribute

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
Set globally via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. This prevents iOS from scaling up small text, which is an accessibility regression for low-vision users. The standard value is `100%`, not `none`.

**`<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` missing `http-equiv`**
Should be `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without `http-equiv`, some Outlook versions may not respect the charset declaration.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[FAIL] Empty name field — broken greeting**
QA confirmed the name merge token resolved to blank, producing `"Hi ,"` (or equivalent) in the rendered email. This is a send-time data issue — the subscriber record either has no first name or the field mapping is incorrect in the ESP template.

Evidence: QA finding — `Greeting has empty name field (e.g. "Hi ,")`

This is a broken-experience bug. The template should include a fallback: `%%=IIF(EMPTY(FirstName), "there", FirstName)=%%` (SFMC syntax) or equivalent.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated before the footer; cannot confirm from source. The QA summary shows no compliance failures, suggesting the unsubscribe link and physical address are present.

**Authentication headers** — Not verifiable from HTML source alone (requires received-message headers). The sending domain `emails.skechers.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; DKIM/SPF/DMARC status should be verified at the ESP level.

**Data sharing disclosure** — Krux DMP syncs (SHA-256 hashed email + raw subscriber ID passed to `beacon.krxd.net`) should be covered in the privacy policy. No code-level issue, but worth confirming policy language covers third-party DMP sharing.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `MKG_US_NA_U_MULTI_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_05202026` is present in the Krux impression pixel and ink1000 open pixel, confirming campaign-level tracking is wired. UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the 27 click-redirect links (skipped by QA probe). This should be spot-checked manually on 2-3 primary CTAs to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended post-redirect.
