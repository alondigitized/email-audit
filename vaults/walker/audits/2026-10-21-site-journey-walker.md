# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 3/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-10-21**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I'm Walker, and I walked this on my phone. The front door was locked and I got nothing but a gray error page. The first two steps both showed "This site can't be reached" with a DNS error. After that the site did load, but I never got a shoe into my cart. The cart ended on a blank white screen and the bag icon still said 0. If a shop doesn't open, I go to Hoka or Brooks, and I don't come back to try again.

**Compared with yesterday (3/10):** Nothing improved, and one thing may have slipped. The comfort-first content is good once you're inside the site. But the first page failed, the "Add to Cart" step didn't produce a cart, and the "Log In" step shows a homepage with no sign I was signed in.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10, flat against yesterday.**

- **Points for:** the menu structure, the "Slip-ins / Wide Fit / Arch Fit" labels, and the shoe page itself.
- **Points against:** the site couldn't be reached at the start, the cart didn't work, and nothing felt personal to me.
- **The DNS failure:** I can't tell if it was Skechers' problem or a blip on the test machine. A customer would just see a dead website either way.

## 3. What's Working

- **Words I understand.** The row under the logo reads "Slip-ins," "Wide Fit," "Arch Fit," and "Max Cus…" That's what I'm shopping for, and I didn't have to hunt for it.
- **The menu is easy on my thumbs.** The Men menu (step 4) has big, bold, well-spaced rows: Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections. There's a clear Back and X. It's the easiest screen in the whole walk.
- **"Hands Free Slip-Ins" and "Walking Shoes" are the first picture tiles** on Men's Shoes. That's my shopping list.
- **The product page is clean.**
  - The name is large and the price is plain: "$70.00."
  - Stars and the review count sit right there, and "Add to Wishlist" is easy to find.
  - It's a slide with "Arch Fit" in the name, which suits me.
  - The red "Get 2 Pairs for $100! Applied at Cart" is a good deal I can follow.
- **The size buttons are large.** Each one is a wide box showing Men's and Women's sizes together. I can tap those without squinting.
- **The "Add to Cart" button is big, dark blue and full width.** Nobody could miss it.
- **The blue banner says "20% OFF Sitewide."** It's the same deal on every page, so I trust it more.

## 4. What's Weak

- **The homepage never loaded** (steps 1 and 2). The "Reload" button was the only thing offered, and it's a Chrome error page, not Skechers'. No brand and no help.
- **Step 3 isn't a login.** It's the homepage with a photo of young people on a brick wall. The account icon shows no name and no greeting, and the bag says 0. If I was signed in, nothing said so. There's no "Welcome back, Walker" and no recent shoes, so it didn't feel personal at all.
- **The cart didn't work.**
  - Step 7 shows a red "Please select a size" warning, and I never picked one. The button was pressed anyway.
  - Step 8 is a completely blank white screen with no cart, no message and no way back. The bag still read 0.
  - A man who is 62 would assume it's broken and leave.
- **Search for "comfort shoes" starts with women's shoes.** The first two results were a pink women's sneaker and a women's black wedge loafer, both marked "Women's." I'm a man, so that's the wrong start. There are 1,621 results and no obvious "Men's" filter near the top.
- **Small text.** The "Unisex" label, the red promo lines, the "Women's" tag, the Klarna/Afterpay lines and the chips ("Filter / Sort by / Size / Width") are small. I'd have to pinch to zoom. The "Width" chip is cut off at the right edge.
- **Big empty gray bands** show on the Men's Shoes and product pages. The product page has a big blank space where the picture should be, so I'm looking at an empty box next to the price.
- **Nothing compares brands or does price-vs-quality math.** I see "2 pairs for $100," but nothing on cushioning or arch support versus Hoka, Brooks or New Balance.
- **The banner says "20% OFF Sitewide, Applied at Cart"** while search results say "20% OFF for Members." That's two different stories, which reads as fine print.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix reliability first.** Check that www.skechers.com resolves and stays up, and add a branded fallback page. Without a working homepage nothing else matters.
2. **Make Add to Cart work and be clear.**
   - Pick a default size, or open the size picker when the button is pressed.
   - Always land on a visible cart or a "Added!" confirmation, never a blank screen.
   - Show the cart count update.
3. **Show me I'm logged in.** Add a first-name greeting, show my saved size and width, and surface "Recently viewed" or "Arch Fit for you" on the homepage.
4. **Honor "men" in search.** If I came through Men's, show men's results first or offer a one-tap "Men's" filter at the top.
5. **Make text bigger.**
   - Raise the promo, price and label text by about 2 points.
   - Fix the cut-off filter chips.
   - Load the product photos before the text.
6. **Put comfort first on the homepage.** Slip-ins, arch support and a simple "Skechers vs. the other guys" comfort and price guide would help me decide.
7. **Make the 20% deal match everywhere.** Say clearly whether it's for everyone or for members only.

## 6. Bottom Line

Once inside, Skechers speaks my language: slip-ins, arch fit and wide sizes, with big buttons and a clear menu. But I couldn't get in at first, the cart came up blank, and the site didn't know me. Today I would have walked out and tried another store. I'd give it 3 out of 10, the same as yesterday.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

The journey was partly invalidated by bot-mitigation rate limiting. Requests to the Kasada fingerprint endpoint (`/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.918`) returned 429 five times, and `/cart/` returned 429 once. The View Cart step is the clearest casualty. Its axe failures (missing `<title>`, `lang`, `<main>` and `<h1>`) match the signature of a bare error document, not the real cart template. Treat those four findings as likely artifacts of the 429, not defects in the cart page.

Other data-quality issues:
- **Web Vitals:** LCP and CLS were not captured for any step, so only TTFB is usable.
- **Men category:** the step URL is `https://www.skechers.com/`, the homepage. Navigation to the category either didn't happen or wasn't recorded, and the HTML sample is identical to the Log In step.
- **Log In:** this step also records the homepage URL, so no login page was audited.
- **Truncated data:** 22 console errors were reported but only 10 were listed.

## Accessibility

8 axe violations across 2 steps.

**View Cart (4, probably from the 429 response):**
- [serious] `document-title`: no non-empty `<title>`.
- [serious] `html-has-lang`: no `lang` attribute. The homepage and PLP HTML do set `lang="en"`, so this points to an error document.
- [moderate] `landmark-one-main`: no main landmark.
- [moderate] `page-has-heading-one`: no level-one heading.
- Re-test once the cart loads successfully. If these persist on a 200 response, they are real defects.

**Search "comfort shoes" (4, likely real):**
- [minor] `aria-allowed-role`: a `role` value is invalid for its element.
- [moderate] `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`: an `<aside>` or complementary landmark is nested inside another landmark.
- [moderate] `landmark-unique`: multiple landmarks share the same role and label.
- [moderate] `region`: content sits outside any landmark.
- The element selectors weren't provided, so the specific nodes can't be identified.

## SEO

- The homepage and PLP set `lang="en"`.
- The PLP exposes a clean GTM `pageType`, a category path (`Men/Shoes`) and a product count (790).
- The sample HTML is truncated before `<title>`, meta description, canonical and structured data. These can't be assessed from this data.
- If View Cart's missing title is real, it matters little because cart pages are normally `noindex`. Verify that `noindex` is present.

## Performance

- **TTFB:** 448 ms (Log In), 448 ms (Men category), 565 ms (Men > Shoes), 421 ms (Product detail), 421 ms (Add to Cart), 130 ms (View Cart), 26 ms (Search).
  - The Product detail and Add to Cart values are identical, and the Log In and Men category values are identical. This suggests the same cached navigation entry was read twice, not fresh measurements.
  - The 26 ms Search value is implausibly low for a server-rendered results page. It is likely a cache or bfcache hit, or a same-document navigation.
  - The 130 ms View Cart value is likely the 429 response, which is fast to return.
- **LCP and CLS:** not captured for any step. The Web Vitals observers probably weren't attached before navigation, or buffered entries weren't read.
- **Third-party load:** the head injects a large number of async third-party scripts before first content:
  - Ad and marketing pixels: Taboola (3 scripts), TikTok (3), Snapchat, Reddit, Facebook, Pinterest, Yahoo (2, duplicate `ytc.js`), Nextdoor, Impact, Tune/go2sdk, RTB House (`creativecdn`, loaded twice).
  - Analytics and personalization: Quantum Metric, Monetate (2), Bing, Bloomreach, Pulse Insights, GTM and multiple gtag destinations.
  - Several are loaded twice, notably `tags.creativecdn.com/…js` and `s.yimg.com/wi/ytc.js`.
- **Font loader:** `webfontloader.js` comes from cdnjs, a third-party origin, and `wf-*-active` classes are applied at runtime. This adds a render-blocking dependency chain, with the potential for FOUT or layout shift.
- **GTM environment:** the GTM URL includes `gtm_preview=env-1&gtm_auth=…`, which loads a non-default GTM environment. The dataLayer reports `site.environment: "production"`, so a staging or preview container may be live on production.

## Mobile Optimization

- The `--vh` CSS variable is set inline on `<html>` (`6.64px`, so a 664px viewport height). This is the JS `vh` workaround for mobile browser chrome. `dvh` or `svh` units would remove the JS dependency.
- `data-passkey-available="true"` shows passkey support is detected on mobile.
- The viewport meta tag, tap-target sizes and responsive images were not in the truncated source and can't be assessed.

## Console & Network Errors

**Network (6, all 429):**
- 5× `/149e9513-…/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.918` is the Kasada client fingerprint POST. Repeated 429s mean the automation session is being rate-limited or flagged as a bot. Kasada's script may retry, which compounds the load.
- 1× `/cart/` returned 429, so the main document failed. This explains the View Cart axe results.

**Console (22 reported, 10 shown):**
- "Failed to load resource: 429" appears 4×, matching the network errors.
- "Unsupported Summarizer API languages were specified…" appears 3×. This comes from a script calling Chrome's built-in Summarizer API without a supported `outputLanguage`. It is probably an on-page third-party script (possibly an AI or accessibility widget), though a browser extension can't be ruled out. Fix it by specifying a supported language (en, es, ja) or feature-detecting first.
- "Permissions policy violation: unload is not allowed" appears 3×. A script registers an `unload` handler. Chrome is deprecating `unload` and blocks it under a Permissions-Policy. Handlers like this also disable bfcache, which hurts back/forward navigation on mobile. Replace them with `pagehide` or `visibilitychange`. The source is probably a third-party tag.

## Recommendations

1. **Re-run the audit with the bot-detection issue resolved.** Use a warmed session or allowlisted test IP, add backoff after a 429, and don't treat axe results from a 429 page as findings.
2. **Fix instrumentation.** Attach `PerformanceObserver` for `largest-contentful-paint` and `layout-shift` with `buffered: true` before navigation. Assert that each step's URL changed, because Log In and Men category both recorded `/`.
3. **Re-test cart accessibility** on a 200 response. Then fix the real Search findings: landmark nesting, unique labels or `aria-label`s, invalid `role` values, and content outside landmarks.
4. **Remove `unload` listeners** (replace with `pagehide`) to restore bfcache eligibility, and trace which tag registers them.
5. **Deduplicate and defer third-party tags.** Load RTB House and Yahoo once, and move non-critical pixels behind consent or idle-time loading in GTM.
6. **Check the GTM `gtm_preview=env-1` parameter.** Confirm production isn't pointing at a non-live container.
7. **Guard the Summarizer API call** with feature detection and a supported language.
8. **Self-host the web font loader** or use `font-display: swap` with preload to remove the cdnjs dependency.
