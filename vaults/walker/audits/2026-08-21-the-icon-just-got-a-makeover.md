# The icon just got a makeover
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least there's an actual shoe right up top this time — first one in about a week from adidas. It's a Samba, blue suede, sitting on some wood planks, no soccer jerseys or teenagers on stone steps for once. But "Samba Goes Metallic" isn't a shoe I'd wear — that's a style call for somebody a third my age who cares about "expression" and a "sleek metallic look." I want cushioning and arch support, not a fashion statement on my feet.
- Then it pivots hard to a $50-gift-card-for-$40 pitch, which is really just "give us $40 now so you spend more later" — not a real discount on anything I'd buy today. No mention of comfort, no arch support, no walking shoe, nothing that speaks to how I actually shop.
- I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward it. Straight to "read and forgotten," same as most of what adidas has sent me this month — though I'll give it credit for at least leading with a shoe instead of a jersey.

## What's working

- A real shoe front and center — first time in six sends I've seen one before scrolling.
- The offer bar up top ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") is clear, high contrast, easy to read.
- Fine print at the bottom actually spells out the gift card terms instead of hiding them — I respect that even if I don't want the offer.

## What's weak

- "Metallic Samba" is a fashion-forward casual sneaker, not a comfort or walking shoe — doesn't speak to me at all.
- The $50-for-$40 gift card isn't a discount on anything real, it's a prepay gimmick, and it's buried under a soccer-jersey-looking dark banner that almost reads like another jersey ad at first glance.
- No mention of comfort, cushioning, arch support, or men's casual anywhere — nothing tailored to what I actually search for.
- No loyalty/adiClub pricing shown here despite the newsletter yesterday pushing that program hard.
- Text-heavy, lots of white space between blocks — feels like I'm scrolling forever on my phone for not much payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a comfort-forward shoe (Cloudfoam, Ultraboost walking styles) instead of a metallic fashion Samba — that's the one thing that would actually make me stop scrolling.
- 2. Make the gift card offer look like a discount, not a soccer jersey ad — right now the dark stripe banner makes me think "oh no, not jerseys again" before I even read it.
- 3. Add a line connecting the gift card to something practical — "use it toward men's walking shoes" — instead of leaving it totally open-ended.
- 4. Tie it back to adiClub since that's fresh from yesterday's newsletter — show me points or member pricing if I'm a member.
