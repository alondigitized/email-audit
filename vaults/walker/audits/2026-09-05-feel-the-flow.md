# Feel the flow
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, "Focused energy" — HOKA's back with number fourteen, and this time they actually put four shoes in a grid before I even had to scroll much, which I'll give them credit for. It's the Bondi 9 in men's and women's, Skyward X 2, and the Mach 7 — but three of those four are runners, and I'm still waiting for someone at this company to remember I'm not training for anything, I'm just trying to walk to the mailbox without my knees complaining.
- The $20 off $150 members deal is the one thing that actually pulled my eye, and the Strava marathon challenge at the bottom is a flat-out miss for a guy my age — I'm not running 26.2 miles for a head-to-toe kit, at self-selected pace or otherwise.
- I wouldn't click past the member discount banner. Not forwarding this one — nothing here is a "hey, check this out" moment for anybody I know.

## What's working

- The $20-off-$150 members banner is a real, specific offer and it's easy to spot in that bright blue block.
- Four actual shoes shown clearly on a plain background — better than last week's colored-blocks-with-no-shoes email.
- "Shop the Pack" and "Shop Now" buttons are big, black, easy to tap on a phone.
- No render bugs — everything loaded clean, text is crisp, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- Still zero recovery or slip-on shoes — after the ORA Recovery Slide showed up on 8/30 and that laceless recovery shoe on 8/28, this one's a full reset back to racing flats and marathon carbon-plate energy.
- The hero shoe up top looks like a race trainer, not something with the arch support and cushioning I actually shop for.
- The Strava "You + 26.2" marathon challenge is completely irrelevant to a comfort-first 62-year-old — that's for the 25-year-olds training for Boston.
- No mention of comfort, cushion, arch support, or "everyday walking" anywhere in the copy — it's all "lock in," "flow," "focused energy," gym-rat language.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a comfort/recovery shoe up top instead of a race trainer — that's the one thing that's moved my needle in this whole run of HOKA emails (the recovery slide, the slip-on).
- 2. Cut the Strava marathon block entirely for segments like mine, or replace it with something like a "casual mileage" walking challenge.
- 3. Put actual comfort language in the copy — cushioning, arch support, all-day wear — not "lock in" and "feel the flow."
- 4. Move the $20-off-$150 offer higher, maybe right under the hero, since that's the strongest real hook in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Get $20 off your next pair`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New comfort colors, plus $20 off $150`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members: your $20 kicks in automatically at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh cushioned styles just landed`
