## 1. Executive Summary

The site looks mostly the same as yesterday. Navigation is organized and the product page itself is solid — Max Cushioning, Hands-Free Slip-ins, a clear deal — exactly what I came for. But two things torpedo the whole trip: the cart page loaded completely blank (I couldn't even see what I put in there), and when I searched "comfort shoes," the first results shown were Women's shoes. I'm a 62-year-old man who just logged in — why is the site showing me women's sneakers? These aren't small bugs. They're the difference between a sale and a bounce.

## 2. Business Impact Score: 4/10

**No change from yesterday.** The navigation got me to the right product efficiently, but a blank cart and a gender-blind search are the same critical failures as before. Flat is not good when the cart is broken.

## 3. What's Working

- **BOGO deal is unmissable.** That blue banner at the top — "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" — is clear and stays persistent across every step. I noticed it every time. That's the kind of deal that makes me feel like I'm not getting ripped off.
- **Men's menu is actually well-organized.** When I opened the hamburger, the MEN section gave me exactly what I'd want: Shoes, Comfort Technologies, Hands Free Slip-ins, Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit — all with big tappable rows. No hunting. No small print. A 62-year-old can work with this.
- **The Hands-Free Slip-ins category page is clean.** 292 results, width filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit, Medium Fit) right at the top. I appreciated seeing width right away — that matters to me more than color.
- **The product page sells the shoe.** "Max Cushioning Premier 3" in the name, the Hands-Free Slip-Ins badge on the image, 4.5 stars from 13 reviews, and the BOGO deal reinforced at the item level. $120 felt justified the way they framed it.
- **ADD TO CART button is excellent.** Full-width, dark blue, bold white text. I can't miss it or accidentally miss-tap it. That button is the best touch target on the whole site.
- **Size grid is generous.** Each size tile is large enough to tap without squinting. The "Please select a size" error is visible and clear — I knew exactly what I needed to do.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Cart page is completely blank.** Step 8 — pure white screen. Nothing. If I'm a real shopper and that happens, I assume the site is broken and I leave. This is the single biggest failure on the entire site and it's the same as yesterday. That's a cart abandonment machine.
- **Search doesn't know I'm a man.** I searched "comfort shoes" while supposedly logged in and the first two results are Women's Arch Fit and Women's Contour Foam. I'm a male customer. Whether I'm logged in or not, "comfort shoes" with 1,458 results should surface men's products first when there's any account signal at all. This is a personalization miss that costs real conversions.
- **Login didn't visibly do anything.** Step 3 looks identical to Step 1. The "Login | details" loyalty banner never changed to show my name or points balance. If I successfully logged in, the site didn't acknowledge me — no "Welcome back, Walker," no points displayed. It just felt like nothing happened.
- **Homepage hero is wrong for me.** "GAME ON!" with bright red, green, and blue athletic shoes feels like it's aimed at someone 30 years younger or golfers specifically. I'm here for comfortable walking shoes. The homepage could have led with cushioning or Slip-ins messaging and immediately told me I'm in the right place.
- **Top nav horizontal scroll cuts off.** The quick-category strip shows "Slip-ins, Sandals, Arch Fit, BOBs, C..." — that trailing "C" means there's more I can't see without scrolling sideways. Easy to miss options.
- **No arch support or cushioning callout on the category page.** The Hands-Free Slip-ins listing page just shows product tiles. I'd love a one-liner under the shoe name saying "Memory foam," "Arch Fit," or "Max Cushion" so I can compare without clicking into each one.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page. Today.** A blank cart is a broken checkout. This is priority one above everything else.
2. **Apply gender to search ranking.** A logged-in male user searching "comfort shoes" should see men's results first. If no login, default to popular rather than women's. This is a simple re-rank that will lift conversion meaningfully.
3. **Show a logged-in state confirmation.** When I sign in, change the banner to "Welcome back, Walker — 2X Points active" and show my points. Right now login is invisible to me.
4. **Swap the homepage hero for comfort messaging.** The "GAME ON!" campaign is fine somewhere else, but the landing page should lead with what brings Boomer men in — slip-on ease, cushioning, arch support. Even rotating the hero to show a Slip-ins lifestyle shot would help.
5. **Add comfort tech labels to category tiles.** Even a small badge or subtitle on each product card in the Slip-ins grid — "Arch Fit," "Max Cushion," "Memory Foam" — lets me comparison-shop without clicking in and out of 15 PDPs.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

8-step mobile journey audit of skechers.com. Primary issues: Kasada bot-protection rate-limiting is bleeding into the cart page (429 on `/cart/`), the View Cart page renders without a proper HTML document shell (missing `<title>` and `lang`), a Stylitics CORS misconfiguration breaks the outfitting widget, and the page carries 20+ third-party scripts with duplicate Monetate and CreativeCDN entries. TTFB is consistently fast (25–44ms) except View Cart (136ms, likely correlated with the 429 retry cycle). LCP and CLS were not captured for any step.

---

## Accessibility

**15 violations across 3 pages.**

**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `aria-required-parent` [critical] — An ARIA role is used on an element outside its required parent container. Likely a misuse of `role="option"` or `role="listitem"` outside a `[role="listbox"]`/`[role="list"]`.
- `aria-allowed-role` [minor] — A native element has a redundant or invalid ARIA role applied.
- `heading-order` [moderate] — Heading levels skip (e.g., `h1` → `h3`). Common in CMS/component-assembled pages.
- `landmark-main-is-top-level`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-complementary-is-top-level`, `landmark-unique` [moderate] — Multiple `<main>` elements detected; `<aside>` nested inside another landmark. Suggests widgets or iframes injecting their own landmark structure into the host page.
- `region` [moderate] — Content exists outside any landmark region.

**View Cart (4 violations, 2 serious):**
- `document-title` [serious] — `<title>` element is empty or absent. This is likely a cart rendered as a client-side overlay or iframe fragment that omits the document shell. Screen readers announce the tab title; empty title breaks orientation.
- `html-has-lang` [serious] — No `lang` attribute on `<html>`. Same root cause as above — the cart DOM may be injected or iframed without a full HTML document.
- `landmark-one-main` [moderate] — No `<main>` landmark.
- `page-has-heading-one` [moderate] — No `<h1>`.

**Search results (3 violations):** Duplicate landmarks and out-of-landmark content — consistent with the same widget injection pattern as the homepage.

## SEO

- View Cart missing `<title>` would cause Googlebot to index a blank title for the cart URL if it were crawlable. Cart should be `noindex` — verify `X-Robots-Tag` or `<meta name="robots">` is present on that page.
- Heading order violations on the homepage mean crawlers see a broken document outline, which can weaken topical signals.
- Duplicate `<main>` landmarks and fragmented landmark structure do not directly harm rankings but indicate the DOM is not well-structured for entity extraction.
- The `--vh` inline style on `<html>` is cosmetic and irrelevant to SEO.

## Mobile Optimization

- `--vh: 6.640000000000001px` is set as an inline CSS custom property on `<html>` via JavaScript. This is a standard mobile viewport-height workaround (for iOS Safari's dynamic toolbar), but the floating-point imprecision (`6.640000000000001` instead of `6.64`) indicates the JS is using `window.innerHeight / 100` without rounding. This can produce sub-pixel rendering artifacts in elements sized with `calc(var(--vh) * N)`.
- `vice-managed-src` attributes are used by the TrustArc CMP to gate script execution on consent. This is correct GDPR/CCPA behavior — scripts like Facebook Pixel, Pinterest, Yahoo, Nextdoor, and Taboola are deferred until consent is granted.
- No `<meta name="viewport">` tag visible in the truncated HTML, but the presence of `--vh` JS and mobile-class rendering suggests it exists later in `<head>`. Should be verified.

## Console & Network Errors

**429 Rate Limiting (6 network errors, 6 console errors):**

All 429s target two endpoints:
1. `/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.430` — This is the **Kasada bot-protection fingerprinting endpoint**. The GUID path and `x-kpsdk-v` parameter are Kasada SDK identifiers. Repeated 429s on this endpoint mean the bot-detection challenge failed or was rate-capped, and the client is retrying. In an automated/headless session this is expected, but if it occurs in real user sessions it means Kasada is mis-classifying legitimate mobile traffic and blocking the fingerprint collection needed for subsequent requests.
2. `https://www.skechers.com/cart/` — **The cart page itself returned 429.** This is the most critical finding. Kasada is blocking the cart page load for sessions flagged as bots. Any real user session that trips the bot heuristic (VPN, unusual UA, cookie anomaly) will be unable to access their cart. This is a direct checkout abandonment risk.

**CORS Error:**
```
Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?base=grid&theme=skechers&version=d3dca01827'
from origin 'https://www.skechers.com' has been blocked by CORS policy:
No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header is present
```
Stylitics CDN is not returning `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` on its stylesheet. This breaks the Stylitics outfitting/styling widget (used for "Complete the Look" modules). The `ERR_FAILED` entry is the failed fetch that follows the CORS block. Root cause is on Stylitics' CDN configuration, but skechers.com should open a ticket with Stylitics — or load the stylesheet through a same-origin proxy to avoid CORS entirely.

## Recommendations

**P0 — Cart 429 (revenue impact):**
Work with Kasada support to review the bot-scoring thresholds for mobile sessions. The cart endpoint should have a higher tolerance threshold or a step-up challenge (CAPTCHA) rather than a hard 429. Audit session replay tools (Quantum Metric is already deployed) for evidence of real users hitting this wall.

**P0 — View Cart document shell:**
The cart page is missing `<title>`, `html[lang]`, `<main>`, and `<h1>`. Determine whether the cart is rendered in an iframe, injected as a fragment, or is a SPA route that doesn't update document metadata. Whichever it is, these four elements must be present.

**P1 — Stylitics CORS:**
Open a support ticket with Stylitics to add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com` to the `style.css` CDN response. As a stopgap, proxy the stylesheet through `skechers.com/static/stylitics-style.css` to sidestep CORS.

**P1 — LCP/CLS collection:**
Re-run the audit with LCP and CLS instrumentation. TTFB is fast everywhere but gives no signal about paint timing or layout stability. With 20+ third-party scripts, high CLS is likely on product listing and search pages.

**P2 — Remove duplicate scripts:**
- Remove either the Monotate v2 or v3 entry — audit GTM to find which tag is redundant.
- Remove the duplicate CreativeCDN tag from the Men category container in GTM.
- Remove the duplicate Yahoo `ytc.js` tag.

**P2 — Consolidate `dns-prefetch`/`preconnect`:**
Add `<link rel="preconnect">` for the 3–5 highest-priority third-party origins (GTM, Quantum Metric, Monetate, Bloomreach). For lower-priority ad pixels, `dns-prefetch` is sufficient. Currently only Monetate has any hint.

**P2 — Fix `--vh` rounding:**
Change the JS that sets `--vh` from `window.innerHeight / 100` to `Math.round(window.innerHeight / 100 * 1000) / 1000 + 'px'` or equivalent to avoid floating-point noise in layout calculations.

**P3 — Migrate off WebFont Loader:**
Self-host Mulish, add `<link rel="preload" as="font">` for the two or three most-used weights, and set `font-display: swap` in the `@font-face` declarations. Remove the webfontloader.js CDN dependency entirely.

**P3 — Fix Homepage ARIA:**
Audit the `aria-required-parent` critical violation. Inspect the DOM for any `role="option"`, `role="row"`, `role="cell"`, `role="gridcell"`, or `role="listitem"` elements that are not wrapped in their required parent role containers. This is the only critical severity violation and requires code-level remediation.
