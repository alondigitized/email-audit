# Daily Journey: Walker Miles on www.skechers.com
**Score:** 3/10 (prev 2/10) · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-09**
## Full review
## 1. Executive Summary

I only made it three thin steps into an actual purchase before Skechers' mobile site let me down. The category browsing and product page are genuinely good — clear comfort messaging, wide-fit filters right up front, the exact shoe I was looking for. But the moment it mattered — logging in, adding to my cart, and looking at what I'd added — the site either did nothing visible or handed me a blank white screen. Then the search bar just gave up entirely. For someone like me who wants to get in, find a comfortable shoe, and check out without a headache, this is still a frustrating, half-working experience.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**3/10** — a slight improvement over yesterday's 2/10, but not by much. The browse-and-product experience earns real credit this time (clear comfort categories, good filters, relevant product). But the cart is still fundamentally broken (blank page) and search failed outright, so the two things that actually convert a shopper into a sale — cart and search — are both non-functional. A shopper who gets this far and hits a blank cart page will assume the site crashed and leave.

## 3. What's Working

- **Comfort-tech navigation up front**: The homepage nav bar puts "Slip-ins," "Workout Walker," and "Arch Fit" right at the top — exactly the language I care about, no digging required.
- **The mobile hamburger menu is well-built**: large, stacked, thumb-friendly rows (Shoes, Shop by Activity, Comfort Technologies, Collections) plus direct shortcuts to Hands Free Slip-ins, Wide Fit, and Extra Wide Fit — genuinely useful for my foot needs.
- **The Hands Free Slip-ins category page** leads with Wide Fit / Extra Wide Fit / Medium Fit as big blue filter buttons before I even see a shoe — that's smart, since fit is my first filter, not price or color.
- **Product detail page** was clean and readable: model name, star rating, price in large text, a "Hands Free Slip-ins" badge on the image itself, and a distinct size grid with big tappable boxes.
- **Size selection guardrail**: the site correctly stopped me from adding to cart without picking a size and showed a clear red warning — better than silently failing.

## 4. What's Weak

- **Login is invisible.** Tapping the account icon produced no visible change — same homepage, no login form, no "Welcome back" banner. I have no idea if I'm logged in or not.
- **No personalization anywhere.** Cart badge stayed at "0" the entire session, no tailored recommendations, no loyalty point callout — nothing that says the site knows who I am.
- **Cart page is a blank white screen.** Not "your cart is empty," not an error message — just nothing. That reads as "the site is broken" to someone my age, not "try again."
- **Search completely failed** — timed out after 30 seconds trying to even load. I couldn't search "comfort shoes" at all.
- **Top promo banner is irrelevant to me** — "25% OFF Kids Styles" and a Britto art collab banner eat premium real estate on every single page, pushing past a 62-year-old with foot pain who doesn't have kids' shoes on his mind.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Fix the cart page render** — a blank white screen after "View Cart" is a checkout-killing bug, full stop. At minimum show a spinner or an empty-cart message.
2. **Fix or diagnose search** — a 30-second timeout on a basic search means the feature is effectively offline for mobile users.
3. **Make login state visible** — show a confirmation, name, or account menu change immediately after I tap the login icon, even if the actual login flow requires a modal.
4. **Swap the top banner logic** — don't show kids' promos to a logged-in (or even anonymous) shopper browsing men's comfort shoes; rotate it toward relevant deals.
5. **Once size is picked, show cart count update instantly** — right now there's no feedback loop confirming an item successfully landed in the cart.

## 6. Bottom Line

The bones of a good comfort-shopping experience are here — the category structure and product page know what I want and lead with it. But a site that can't reliably show me my own cart or let me search isn't one I'd trust with my credit card. Until those two things work every time, I'd bail and go check DSW instead.

## 7. Evidence

**Step 1 — Homepage**: Clean layout — Skechers logo, search/account/cart/menu icons up top, promo banner ("25% OFF Kids Styles"), then three comfort-tech shortcut tabs (Slip-ins, Workout Walker, Arch Fit) above a large lifestyle hero image. Easy to read, big tap targets on the icons. Friction: the kids' promo banner is irrelevant to me and takes prime real estate. No personalization visible — I'm not logged in yet and nothing suggests it knows my browsing history.

Below that: filter/sort bar, "327 results," and a product grid with clear thumbnails and quick-add bag icons. This step delighted me — it's exactly the comfort-first framing I want, no digging.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary
The mobile journey through skechers.com (Homepage → Log In → Men category → Men > Shoes → Product Detail → Add to Cart → View Cart) surfaces 12 accessibility violations, 14 console errors, and 10 failed network requests. The most severe findings are a `critical` ARIA violation on the homepage and two `serious` violations (missing `<title>` and missing `lang` attribute) on the View Cart page — the latter is unusual since the homepage HTML does declare `lang="en"`, suggesting the cart page is served through a different rendering path (likely a SPA/iframe cart fragment) that doesn't inherit the base document's `<head>`.

## Accessibility
**Homepage (8 violations):**
- `[critical]` aria-required-parent — ARIA roles present without their required parent role container
- `[minor]` aria-allowed-role — invalid role value on an element
- `[moderate]` heading-order — heading levels skip/out of sequence
- `[moderate]` landmark-main-is-top-level, landmark-complementary-is-top-level — `<main>`/`<aside>` nested inside another landmark instead of top-level
- `[moderate]` landmark-no-duplicate-main, landmark-unique — multiple `<main>` regions and duplicate landmark roles
- `[moderate]` region — content exists outside any landmark

**View Cart (4 violations):**
- `[serious]` document-title — empty or missing `<title>`
- `[serious]` html-has-lang — `<html>` missing `lang` attribute
- `[moderate]` landmark-one-main — no `<main>` landmark present
- `[moderate]` page-has-heading-one — no `<h1>` on the page

The homepage cluster (duplicate/nested landmarks, broken heading order) points to component-level markup issues, likely from the third-party widget/personalization scripts injecting their own landmark roles into the DOM. The View Cart violations indicate the cart view is missing baseline document metadata entirely, which will also degrade screen-reader page announcement and browser tab identification.

## SEO
- View Cart page lacks a `<title>` element and `lang` attribute — both are on-page ranking/indexing signals and will affect how search engines and social crawlers represent that URL if it's ever indexed.
- View Cart's missing `<h1>` removes a primary on-page content signal for that route.
- Homepage/category/PDP HTML samples show `lang="en"` correctly set at the document level.
- Meta description/canonical/structured-data tags were not visible in the truncated HTML samples — not confirmed present or absent; requires full-source review to assess.

## Mobile Optimization
- The homepage sets an inline `style="--vh: 6.64...px"` custom property on `<html>`, indicating JS-based viewport-height correction (a common workaround for mobile browser chrome resizing). This is a functional pattern but adds a layout-dependent JS execution on load; if it runs late it can contribute to CLS.
- Viewport `<meta>` tag was not visible in the truncated HTML sample — presence/correctness not confirmed.
- The bot-detection/fingerprinting endpoint (`.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=...`, Kasada) returned 429 on repeated calls during the session, which on constrained mobile networks/retriable clients can add latency or block page functionality tied to that check (e.g., cart, checkout gating).

## Console & Network Errors
**14 console errors / 10 network failures, grouped by cause:**
- **429 Too Many Requests (5 occurrences)** — all against `www.skechers.com/.../fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.616` (Kasada bot-detection fingerprint endpoint) plus one against `/cart/` itself. Repeated rate-limiting on a first-party cart URL is notable and should be root-caused — it suggests the anti-bot layer is throttling legitimate traffic during a normal shopping session.
- **403 Forbidden (3 occurrences)** — `survey.pulseinsights.com/serve` (called twice, once per page view) and `BazaarvoiceProductData-GetProduct?pid=test` (a literal `pid=test` placeholder value reaching production — looks like a hardcoded/debug parameter that was never swapped for the real product ID).
- **CORS block (1 occurrence)** — `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css` blocked by missing `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header, so that stylesheet fails to load cross-origin.
- **400 Bad Request** — `ct.pinterest.com/v3/` conversion tracking call.
- **ERR_SOCKET_NOT_CONNECTED / ERR_FAILED (2 occurrences)** — generic network-layer failures, consistent with requests dropped during the 429 rate-limiting bursts.

## Recommendations
1. Fix View Cart's missing `<title>`, `lang` attribute, `<main>` landmark, and `<h1>` — these are `serious`-severity a11y gaps and directly affect SEO metadata for that route; confirm whether the cart is rendered via a separate template/fragment that bypasses the base `<head>`.
2. Investigate the homepage's nested/duplicate landmark roles (`aria-required-parent`, `landmark-no-duplicate-main`, `landmark-unique`) — likely caused by a third-party widget injecting its own `<main>`/ARIA roles into the page.
3. Root-cause the repeated 429s from the Kasada fingerprint endpoint and the `/cart/` URL — this is throttling within a single legitimate session and warrants investigation into rate-limit thresholds or client-side retry storms.
4. Remove the hardcoded `pid=test` parameter in the Bazaarvoice product-data call — it's returning 403 and appears to be leftover debug/test code in production.
5. Add the correct `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header on `web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css`, or self-host/proxy it, to stop the blocked stylesheet load.
6. Re-run the audit with LCP/CLS capture enabled — Web Vitals data is currently missing for every step, and the homepage's ~30+ third-party script tags plus the JS-driven `--vh` viewport calculation are plausible contributors to both metrics that can't be confirmed without real measurements.
