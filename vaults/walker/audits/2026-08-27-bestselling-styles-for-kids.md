# Bestselling styles for kids
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- Kids' shoes. I don't have little ones at home anymore — my grandkids maybe, but nobody's sending New Balance emails to my inbox for that, this just landed here same as all the other adult stuff they send me. Straight off the bat this one's not for me, and that's before I even get into the content.
- Even setting aside the "wrong audience" problem, there's nothing here for a guy like me anyway — no arch support talk, no cushioning callout, no comfort angle at all, just a kid's foot up on a stool wearing some purple-pink running shoe and a price-tier picker. This is the seventh or eighth New Balance email this month and now they're not even trying to hit my category.
- Not clicking, not forwarding, this one's getting archived without a second look.

## What's working

- The hero shot is clean, no render bugs, image loaded fine.
- Free shipping banner at $75+ is at least a clear, honest offer up top.
- Price-tier buttons (Under $75, $75-99, Over $100) are a decent shopping shortcut if I actually needed kids' shoes.
- "Shop in store" section with a real storefront photo is a nice touch for someone who'd rather try shoes on in person, which is more my style anyway.

## What's weak

- I've got no kids at home needing back-to-school shoes — this whole send just isn't relevant to me.
- Zero comfort or arch-support language anywhere, so even if I ignore the "kids" angle there's nothing pulling me in as a comfort shopper.
- No specific percent-off or dollar offer beyond the shipping threshold — it's just a browse-by-price nudge, not a deal.
- Six little product tiles in a grid, no story about why any one of these is worth my time.
- "In case you missed it" is a weak, generic headline that doesn't tell me anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out of kids'-category sends entirely and put me back on men's comfort/walking shoes — that's the single biggest fix, this email shouldn't have reached me.
- 2. If they insist on cross-selling kids' stuff to parents/grandparents, lead with an actual offer (a percent off, not just free shipping threshold) to earn the open.
- 3. Swap "In case you missed it" for something concrete about back-to-school timing, since that's clearly the intent here.
- 4. Add real merchandising story instead of a flat 6-shoe grid — tell me why the 574 or the Rave Run is the pick, not just list them.
