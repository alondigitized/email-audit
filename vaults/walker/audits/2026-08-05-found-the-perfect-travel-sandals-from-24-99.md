# Found: the perfect travel sandals from $24.99!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least DSW dropped the birthday gimmick for a second straight day — that's twice now, following the "40% off rarely-on-sale brands" email from yesterday. But I opened this one thinking maybe I'd finally see something for my feet, and instead I got a woman with a big head of curls holding up jelly flip-flops over her shoulder. Polka-dot slides, checkerboard jellies, floral thongs — every single sandal in this email is built for a beach bag, not a guy walking the dog or catching a flight to see the grandkids.
- The top banner with the Nike, New Balance, Crocs, adidas, Birkenstock logos got a half-second of my attention — that's real brand overlap with what I actually shop — but then the whole email pivots hard into "vacay sandals" and heels and flats. Nothing hands-free, nothing with arch support called out, nothing that says "comfort" anywhere I could see.
- I'm not clicking this one. Straight to archive. Wouldn't forward it either — my wife might like the jellies, but that's her inbox, not mine.

## What's working

- The top banner is clean and specific — "Up to 40% off" plus real brand logos I recognize and shop (New Balance, Crocs) caught my eye before anything else.
- $24.99 is a real number, not some vague "starting at" nonsense — I'll give them credit for that.
- Layout's tidy, nothing broken or overlapping, images all loaded fine.
- The loyalty blurb at the bottom about 5% back in Rewards is at least visible, even if it's buried down low.

## What's weak

- Every product in this email is a women's sandal — polka dots, jellies, floral flip-flops. Not one shoe here is built for me.
- The model, the "beach getaway" copy, the whole "vacay sandals" framing — none of it reads like it's talking to a 62-year-old guy who wants something cushioned to walk to the mailbox in.
- "Shop by size" only goes up to 11 — that's a women's size run, so even the size grid told me this wasn't for me.
- Heels, flats, and skinny sneakers in the "more ways to savor summer" row — again, nothing hands-free or arch-supported.
- Back to school section shows a kid's boot getting laced up — fine for grandkids maybe, but no adult men's comfort angle anywhere in this send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this one — if DSW knows I've never clicked a heel or a jelly sandal in six months of emails, don't send me the women's vacay sandal blast. Send me the men's slip-on/comfort version of this same sale.
- 2. Swap the hero product for something from the brand banner that actually applies to me — a New Balance or Crocs slide, not a floral flip-flop.
- 3. Add one line of comfort-specific copy — "cushioned footbed," "arch support," "slip on and go" — instead of "seize the (PTO) day."
- 4. If there's a men's version of this sandal sale, cross-sell it right under the hero instead of routing straight into "Shop All Women."
