# Psst…that $10 is still here…
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- I've been opening DSW almost every single day lately — they've become one of those senders where the name alone makes my thumb move. So I opened this one. But once it loaded, I genuinely had to scroll around trying to figure out what the $10 in the subject was about. The hero is women's heels and strappy sandals — "So New, So You" — and I'm thinking, okay, not me, and kept scrolling. Lower down I see "Dad's Day Savings — Up to 25% Off Big-Deal Brands" with New Balance and Crocs logos right there, and that's the email I was actually looking for. But DSW made me hunt for it past content that had nothing to do with me.
- The size selector with "Shop Wide Width" and "Shop Wide Calf" buttons is honestly the most useful thing in this whole email for a guy like me. More of that, less of the stilettos up top.
- As for the $10? I still don't know where it is. I scrolled the whole thing. It's probably buried in the fine print at the very bottom in the world's smallest font. That feels like a trick, and I don't like it. I'd mark this read and come back to the 25% off brands only if I was already in the market this week. No forwarding this one to anyone.

## What's working

- **Dad's Day Savings hook is seasonally right** — I know it's late June but Father's Day sales running a few extra days is totally normal and I'll take it.
- **Brand logos I recognize** — New Balance, Crocs, Converse, and DUDE are right there in my comfort-shoe rotation. Seeing those logos made me pause.
- **Wide Width button** — "Shop Wide Width" speaks directly to me. That's the kind of fit detail I actually care about and DSW doesn't always surface it this high up.
- **No render problems** — Email loaded clean on mobile, text was legible, images showed up, nothing was broken or overlapping.
- **VIP perks reminder** — Nice to see the loyalty section without it being pushy.

## What's weak

- **Subject says $10, email shows 25% off** — I opened looking for a $10 credit or coupon and the email never delivered it visibly. That's a bait that didn't pay off; I felt misled.
- **Women's new arrivals are the hero** — "So New, So You" with heels and sandals is the first thing I see. Twelve consecutive DSW sends and still leading with women's content for a customer who only clicks men's comfort styles.
- **No clear CTA tied to the Dad's Day offer** — The 25% off brands section has zero button. I'm supposed to just click a logo? That's not obvious.
- **"Up to 25% off" with an asterisk** — "These are almost never on sale" plus fine print buried at the very bottom. I've been burned by "up to" offers before — the shoe I want is always the excluded one.
- **Preview text is junk padding** — After "Save more while you can" it's just whitespace characters. That's filler and it shows.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Dad's Day men's savings section, not women's new arrivals** — Flip the layout. Men's brands with a comfort/walking angle should be the hero for any customer whose click history skews male.
- 2. **Put the $10 front and center** — If the subject promises $10, I need to see a prominent coupon or reward banner the second the email opens. Don't make me dig for it.
- 3. **Add a button to the 25% off brands section** — "Shop Men's Sale Brands" with a clear button, not just logo tiles.
- 4. **Cut the asterisk or explain it immediately** — "Big-Deal Brands*" with fine print three screens down kills trust. Say it plainly: "Includes New Balance, Crocs, and more — see site for exclusions."
- 5. **Shorten the cadence or segment harder** — This is the seventh email in a week. I'm still opening them out of habit, but I'm noticing. A miss like this (wrong hero, missing $10) is the kind that starts the unsubscribe thought.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Balance, Crocs, DUDE and more — rarely this low`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — accessibility violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Many ESPs now flag this.

**Capitalized `Src:` in all 10 `@font-face` declarations — invalid CSS**
```css
Src: url("https://braze-images.com/appboy/...") format('woff');
```
`src` must be lowercase per CSS spec. Browsers are forgiving, but it will silently fail on strict parsers and some older email clients. All 10 `@font-face` blocks are affected.

**Contradictory `text-size-adjust` values in `*` selector**
```css
*{ -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; ... -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
`100%` and `none` appear in the same rule block. The second declaration wins, disabling text size adjustment globally in IE/old Edge. Likely intentional but worth confirming — `none` is aggressive.

**Mixed font formats, no WOFF2**
- Futura PT: WOFF only (no WOFF2, no TTF fallback)
- HelveticaNow: TTF only (no WOFF or WOFF2)

WOFF2 is ~30% smaller and supported everywhere. Not a rendering bug but a load-time inefficiency for the 10 web font loads.

**No issues found:** Dark mode implementation is correctly structured (`color-scheme` meta + `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` + explicit `.dm-*` class overrides). Gmail `#MessageViewBody`/`#MessageWebViewDiv` fix is present.
