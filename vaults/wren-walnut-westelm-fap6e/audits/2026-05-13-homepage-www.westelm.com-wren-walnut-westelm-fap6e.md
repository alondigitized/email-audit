# Homepage snapshot · www.westelm.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- West Elm is leaning hard into an outdoor living push right now — the whole above-the-fold is a sun-drenched patio sectional with a "Lounge. Dine. Unwind." headline, and there's an Emma Chamberlain collaboration banner running at the very top. As someone who comes here specifically to obsess over indoor furniture and has zero outdoor space in my apartment, I'm already one thumb-swipe from bouncing to the rugs section. The brand still looks gorgeous and premium, but the homepage isn't working for me today.

## What's working

- **"Lounge. Dine. Unwind." headline** — three words, clean serif, aspirational without being try-hard. The register is exactly right for who West Elm is talking to.
- **Hero photography** — the white outdoor sectional against warm plaster walls and French doors is genuinely beautiful. Even though it's not my category, the aesthetic credibility makes me trust the brand.
- **Emma Chamberlain × West Elm banner** — I actually want to tap that. Collabs like W×R are what I wait for; a Chamberlain drop could have interesting pieces that cross over into indoor styling.
- **Sub-brand nav strip** (west elm / west elm office / west elm kids / west elm business) — I appreciate that it's persistent. I know at a glance that this is the main shopping lane.
- **CTA button** — white pill button on the hero is unambiguous, not a ghost outline I'd miss.

## What's weak

- **Outdoor focus misses returning indoor customers like me** — May is a valid outdoor push, but there's zero acknowledgment that I might already own the Andes sectional and be here for rugs or brass accents.
- **No Key Rewards hook above the fold** — I have the loyalty account. Show me my points, a member-exclusive price, anything. The account icon in the nav does nothing to surface that.
- **No offer with teeth** — "DISCOVER THE NEW COLLECTION" is not an incentive. Where's the 20%-off-rugs event I've been waiting on? If it's running, it's invisible here.
- **No people in the hero** — The lifestyle scene has no human presence, which makes it feel more like a showroom render than a home. For a brand that markets to design-curious Millennials, that's a missed warmth signal.
- **Top nav is truncated** — "west elm busine..." is cut off on mobile. Sloppy.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a Key Rewards balance or member-offer strip** directly below the hero — even "You have 240 points. See member deals →" would pull returning shoppers like me into a conversion path immediately.
- **Run a second hero tile or swipeable banner** for indoor categories — one slide for outdoor, one for rugs/lighting/accent furniture. Don't assume everyone arrived for the seasonal push.
- **Add a credible offer to the Emma Chamberlain banner** — change "DISCOVER THE NEW COLLECTION →" to something like "New collab — shop now, free shipping on orders $100+" to give the banner actual conversion weight.
- **Swap the hero to a lifestyle shot with a person in it** — show someone in a real-ish apartment space (not a pristine stage set) to anchor the aspiration in human reality.
