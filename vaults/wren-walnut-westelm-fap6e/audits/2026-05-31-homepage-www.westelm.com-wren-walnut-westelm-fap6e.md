# Homepage snapshot · www.westelm.com · 2026-05-31
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- West Elm opened with a gate — an email/SMS capture modal that fills the entire viewport before I've seen a single product. The brand is clearly West Elm (logo is right there), and behind the lightbox I can just barely make out what looks like warm lifestyle imagery and a "New summer styles" banner strip at the top. The primary message this homepage is sending me right now is: give us your data first, shop second.

## What's working

- **Key Rewards callout in the modal:** "become a Key Member and earn 2% back across all our brands" — I'm already a member and this is the exact cross-brand benefit I actually use (I buy from Pottery Barn for inherited pieces). It's real value, not fluff.
- **Modal renders cleanly:** no layout breakage, legible body copy, clear "NO THANKS" escape — at least it's not a roach motel.
- **"New summer styles" banner:** barely visible at the top but it confirms the site is current and seasonal — not a stale winter clearance ghost town.
- **Brand mark placement:** the west elm wordmark is centered and clean at the top of the modal — I know exactly where I am.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself is the problem:** forcing an email/SMS gate before showing me a single product is the fastest way to make me hit "NO THANKS" without engaging. I came to browse rugs, not negotiate data access.
- **SMS fine print is prominent and alarming:** there's a dense legal paragraph about "automated marketing text messages," "Wireless Carriers are not liable," and "STOP to cancel" — it reads like a terms-of-service scroll, not a warm membership pitch. Kills the vibe immediately.
- **No value exchange specific to me:** "new arrivals, sales & more" is so generic it's meaningless. As a Key Member already in the rug-sale-waiting mode, there's nothing here that feels targeted — no "20% off rugs" hook, no member-exclusive offer.
- **The actual homepage is invisible:** I cannot see the hero, the product categories, the sale modules, or any lifestyle content. The entire visual merchandising effort is wasted on first load.
- **Mobile number field feels aggressive:** asking for a phone number on the very first interaction — before I've even seen a product — is a lot. The "(optional)" label doesn't make it feel less intrusive.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the modal trigger by 10–15 seconds or one scroll depth** — let me see the homepage first. I'm far more likely to sign up after I've confirmed the sale I came for is real.
- **Surface a member-specific hook instead of cold acquisition copy** — for logged-in or cookied Key Members, swap "Find out first" for "Welcome back — your 20%-off rugs event is live" with a direct shop CTA.
- **Strip the SMS legal block into a collapsed disclosure link** — the dense carrier-liability paragraph is doing more damage than good above the fold; move it to a smaller linked disclosure under the phone field.
- **Show the offer first, ask for the email second** — flip the order: hero image + concrete sale banner, then a slim footer bar with the email capture. The product has to earn the lead.
