# Memorial Day deals = Up to 45% OFF😲
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's second Memorial Day email in 24 hours — yesterday's send announced the deals were "live," and today's lead with "Up to 45% OFF" attempts an escalation. The hero landing is clean: seasonal headline, lifestyle image, one "Shop now" button. Then the email detonates. Below the fold you get a buy-more-save-more tier (10%/15%/20%), a free product section, two flat-percentage save tiles, dollar-amount product tiles, a Samsung appliance bundle, a military discount module, and a Deals of the Day acquisition ask. That is at least seven distinct value propositions in a single send.
- The 45% number in the subject is real and surfaced immediately — that's discipline. But it shares visual real estate with 25% off, $40 off, $50 off, and $100 off, which dilutes the headline claim into noise. The buy-more-save-more tier is the biggest misfire: it requires mental math in the middle of a scroll-and-tap experience where every other section just hands you a number.
- Net judgment: technically competent promotional inventory blast, but Lowe's is burning inbox equity fast. Yesterday was Memorial Day too. A subscriber who opened both will wonder what's actually new here.

## What's working

- Hero is clean and unambiguous — "Memorial Day" + lifestyle image + "Shop now" lands in one read
- 45% appears immediately below the hero, delivering on the subject's promise without a bait-and-switch
- Military discount module is a consistent, brand-appropriate trust signal that rewards the right segment
- Samsung appliance bundle is a smart cross-sell anchor for high-intent buyers already in appliance consideration
- "We Help. You Save." footer reinforces the value-positioning brand voice throughout

## What's weak

- Buy-more-save-more tier (10%/15%/20% at spend thresholds) requires math where every other section just gives you a number — wrong pattern for a scroll email
- Seven competing offer types means no single offer dominates; the email has no spine
- Back-to-back Memorial Day sends with no clear differentiation from yesterday's ("deals are live" → "up to 45% off" is a weak escalation signal)
- Deals of the Day sign-up is an acquisition ask buried inside a retention promotional email — wrong audience, wrong moment
- No explicit deadline visible anywhere — "Memorial Day" implies urgency but doesn't state "ends Monday, May 26"

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one hero offer and own it** — "Up to 45% off appliances through Monday" is a send. Pick the category with the highest conversion rate and build the email around it. Everything else becomes a secondary row, not a competing hero.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline in the body** — "Offer ends May 26" in the hero copy turns implied urgency into a real one. It also differentiates this send from yesterday's.
- 3. **Kill the Deals of the Day sign-up block** — this is a retention send; acquisition asks dilute the primary CTA and confuse the send's purpose.
- 4. **Pull the buy-more-save-more into a dedicated send** — it's a structurally different offer type that needs its own real estate and its own creative to land.
- 5. **Open with the escalation signal** — if today's deal is bigger than yesterday's, say so. "Even bigger Memorial Day savings: now up to 45% off" communicates something new.
- **Subject Alt A:** `45% off ends Memorial Day — shop the list`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Bigger Memorial Day deals, through Monday only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Appliances, outdoor, tools — your category on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Through May 26: up to 45% off across the store.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 failure):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero/product image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` |
| Supporting graphic | `image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` |

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `sli.lowes.com/imp`) missing alt text is lower severity — `alt=""` is the correct value for decorative/invisible elements, but the QA tool flagged them because the attribute is absent rather than empty.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints:**
```css
/* Query A */
@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)

/* Query B */
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
Devices 375–480px wide match **both** queries simultaneously. Query A is WebKit-only; Query B is universal. Any property set in both blocks for `.container`, `.drop`, or `.photo` will have unpredictable cascade order depending on stylesheet position.

**Font loading — woff only, no woff2:**
All six `@font-face` declarations provide only `.woff`. Modern clients support `woff2` (30% smaller). Not a rendering failure, but increases load time.

**Redundant `local()` hints:**
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url(...)
```
Each `@font-face` duplicates the `local()` hint. Harmless but indicates a template copy-paste artifact.

**DIN Next CDN version mismatch:**
- Regular weight: `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- Bold/Black weights: `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff`

Different scaffold versions for the same typeface family — risk of metric or rendering inconsistency between weights.

**Legacy Hotmail hacks present** (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`) — these target Hotmail/Windows Live, which no longer exists. They do not cause harm but represent dead code.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured by AgentMail relay.**
This is a relay visibility gap, not a confirmed absence. Lowe's at scale would fail inbox provider requirements (Gmail, Yahoo) without this header. Cannot confirm either way from the relay capture alone.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not captured.**
Same caveat. One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) since 2024. If absent on the actual send, this is a deliverability compliance failure.

**`Authentication-Results` — not found via relay.**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. Sending domain `e.lowes.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; alignment with `lowes.com` DMARC policy is unverified from this capture.

**Physical mailing address** — HTML is truncated; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) compliance (footer address required). Flag for manual verification.
