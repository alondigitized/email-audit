# 50% OFF! July 4th is tomorrow🎇
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's seventh July 4th-dedicated promotional send in nine days, and it's the sharpest of the batch. "50% OFF! July 4th is tomorrow🎇" finally does what yesterday's "Extra Big Deals are officially ON" email should have done: it names a hard number and attaches a deadline the recipient cannot misinterpret. The subject wins on pure scarcity mechanics — "tomorrow" is as close to a hard stop as you can print without naming midnight.
- The body is still a cluttered six-product scatter, which is Lowe's recurring sin in this campaign. The countdown timer — the most compelling element in the email — is buried halfway down, underneath a 2×2 product grid. That is the wrong order. A live countdown with a hard-stop deadline should be the second thing you see, not the fourth.
- The email is well-rendered, seasonally sharp, and has real price specificity. If this is your first Lowe's July 4th email, it works. If you've received the last six, it is exhausting — and Lowe's has not earned that much frequency without meaningfully escalating the offer each time. The "50% OFF" lead finally feels like escalation. Ship it higher in the hierarchy next time.

## What's working

- **"50% OFF!" in the subject is the clearest number this campaign has thrown** — after six sends that leaned on "big deals" and "savings," naming the percentage feels like new information.
- **The countdown timer is a genuinely strong urgency device** — live hours/minutes/seconds with "July 4th" labeled is impossible to misread.
- **Price specificity in the product grid is real** — $349, $398, $299 visible at a glance; these aren't vague "save big" tiles.
- **Hero image reads July 4th immediately** — American flag on the porch, outdoor/summer context is on-theme and season-correct.
- **"Definitely time for these deals" is clean, understated hero copy** — doesn't oversell; lets the prices do the work.

## What's weak

- **The countdown timer is buried mid-email** — placed after a 2×2 product grid, the most urgent element in the email is not visible on first open.
- **"50% OFF" in the subject isn't clearly anchored to one product in the body** — the 50% badge appears on a single product tile, but the subject implies a sitewide or broad promotion; the disconnect creates a mild bait-and-switch feeling.
- **Six products compete with no clear hero** — there is no single product that owns this email; attention fragments across grills, outdoor furniture, and two $299 items below the fold.
- **Eighth send in nine days** — the cadence is so compressed that even a solid subject starts to filter out; the "deals are on" signal has lost its spike value.
- **"Shop now" primary CTA is generic** — every Lowe's email has the same button copy; no product-specific pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the countdown timer to the hero position** — immediately below the header nav, before any product tiles. It's the one element that couldn't have appeared in any prior send; lead with it.
- 2. **Anchor "50% OFF" to a single named product in the subject and hero** — e.g., "50% off EGO mowers" rather than a sitewide implication that doesn't match the body. Pick one hero, make it the offer.
- 3. **Reduce product tiles from six to three** — the two $299 items below the fold are below the decision threshold for most recipients who've already seen the grill and patio section.
- 4. **Make "Buy now" CTA copy product-specific** — "Buy the 5-burner grill" outperforms "Buy now" even at lower click rates because the converter is already committed.
- 5. **Cut frequency to every other day for this final push** — the last 48 hours before a sale-end date warrant two sends max (eve + day-of morning), not a send every day since June 25.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tomorrow only: up to 50% off grills, mowers, and more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day — the July 4th sale ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Countdown is live — deals end when July 4 ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grills, pressure washers, outdoor furniture — final hours on these prices`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**21 click-redirect links unresolved.** All outbound links route through Lowe's click-tracking domain; the QA probe skipped them. UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain.

**Impression pixel PII exposure.** Six `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels and the open-tracking pixel encode the recipient email address in plain-text query parameters:

```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=732026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/ee216b65ce7c7220/o.gif?mi_u=410146703
```

The `e=` parameter is the raw recipient address logged in every server-side request. Depending on jurisdiction, this constitutes PII in server logs accessible to the ESP and any CDN intermediary.

**CDN font version mismatch.** Two different scaffold versions are referenced in the same email:

- `DIN Next Regular` — `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- `DIN Next Bold / Black` — `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff` / `din-next-black.woff`

If 1.4.4 drops the bold/black variants, fallback behavior will differ across clients.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):

| Image | URL pattern |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `image.e.lowes.com/…/c301e24e-…jpg` |
| Open-track pixel | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/…/o.gif` |
| 5× impression pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?…` |
| Secondary image | `image.e.lowes.com/…/ad569dc5-…png` |

Tracking/pixel `<img>` elements should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to satisfy screen reader pass-through. Substantive content images require descriptive alt text.

**`.woff`-only font stack.** All `@font-face` declarations load only `.woff`. `.woff2` (20–30% smaller, universally supported in modern clients) is absent. Minor bandwidth impact; no rendering failure.

**Breakpoint targeting hack.** The 375–600 px media query appends `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` to restrict styles to WebKit only. This is an intentional client-targeting technique but undocumented; it will silently no-op on non-WebKit mobile clients in that width range.

**Duplicate `@font-face` conditional blocks.** Fellix and DIN Next families are each wrapped in separate `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <!--<![endif]-->` conditional comment pairs. Functionally equivalent to one block; not a bug, but increases HEAD payload.
