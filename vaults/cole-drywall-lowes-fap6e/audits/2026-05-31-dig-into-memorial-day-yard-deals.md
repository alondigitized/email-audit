# Dig into Memorial Day yard deals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Six days after Memorial Day, Lowe's is still leading with "Memorial Day" in the subject line — and that timing is the email's central problem. The May 29 appliance send wisely dropped the holiday label from the subject; this yard send backtracks, which is a real step backward. The creative itself is solid: three specific yard products at hard prices ($599 mower, $149 blower, $349 pressure washer), a clean lifestyle hero, and a triple-points loyalty hook that adds genuine pull for members. The structure is confident and the visual hierarchy works. But "Dig into Memorial Day yard deals" arriving on May 31 reads like a liquidation email, not a seasonal send.
- The "Don't miss the final days" urgency banner tries to save the timing but undercuts itself — if the deals are in their final days on day six post-holiday, why weren't they promoted six days ago? - Three specific products with hard dollar prices at a glance — no hunting for the offer.
- Triple-points loyalty activation module is visible and has a dedicated CTA ("Activate now"), not buried in fine print.
- Hero copy ("The kind of yard that gets double-takes") is lifestyle-oriented and category-appropriate without being generic.
- Greenworks pressure washer branding is visible — brand specificity builds trust vs. unnamed generics.
- No render bugs; layout is clean across what appears to be a single-column mobile layout.

## What's weak

- "Memorial Day yard deals" in the subject on May 31 is stale — the holiday was six days ago and the May 29 send had already moved past the label.
- The hero section doesn't show a price or % off, so the above-fold promise is lifestyle-only; you have to scroll to find the actual offer.
- "Don't miss the final days of these savings" urgency banner appears below three product blocks — it's placed where urgency is least effective.
- No percentage-off framing anywhere — $599/$149/$349 are prices, but without a "was" price or % off, the value proposition is unclear.
- Cadence fatigue: this is the seventh promotional email in roughly two weeks; a subscriber's finger is already hovering over unsubscribe.

- 1. **Drop "Memorial Day" from the subject entirely** — the May 29 appliance send did this correctly. Position as an end-of-month yard event or just a straight product send. The holiday framing is actively working against you.
- 2. **Anchor the hero with a price or savings claim** — "The kind of yard that gets double-takes" is nice copy, but adding "From $149" or "Up to $X off" above the fold would prevent the scroll-required offer discovery problem.
- 3. **Move the urgency banner above the product grid**, not below it. "Final days" means nothing if a subscriber already scrolled past the buy buttons.
- 4. **Add a "was" price or % off beside each product** — $599 for a mower tells me nothing about the deal quality without a reference price.
- 5. **Lead the loyalty module earlier** — triple points is the strongest reason to act today; burying it below three product blocks loses its urgency.

The overlap between these two queries for viewports 375–480px means both fire simultaneously. **`.ExternalClass` Hotmail hacks present** — legacy but harmless; standard boilerplate for Outlook/Hotmail compatibility.
