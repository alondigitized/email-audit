# Happy 250th, America! Savings up to $1,500 inside 👇
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's day-of July 4th send, and the "America's 250th" angle is the single smartest creative decision in the entire nine-send July 4th run. Every other brand is saying "July 4th sale" — Lowe's is the only one tying to the sesquicentennial milestone, and that specificity actually earns the $1,500 headline. The patriotic blue hero lands well: "Happy July 4th / Celebrate America's 250th" is clean, bold, and seasonally differentiated.
- Below the hero, it falls apart in the same way it always does. Six competing sections — outdoor power equipment, outdoor furniture, appliances, a sweepstakes, a free-item offer, and a fence-post product — means there is no hierarchy and no single item a subscriber is meant to act on. The sweepstakes "Activate now" CTA directly competes with shopping CTAs; neither wins. Coming a day after the "50% OFF! July 4th is tomorrow" send (which scored 8/10 precisely because it was focused), this email feels like someone dumped the entire July 4th catalog into one email because it's the last shot before the holiday ends.
- Judgment: strong subject, strong hero, executional clutter below the fold. A solid mid-tier send that earns its opens but leaves clicks on the table.

## What's working

- **America's 250th framing** is genuinely fresh — no other brand in this inbox is using it; gives Lowe's a reason to exist in a crowded holiday inbox.
- **Hero visual** is clean — blue background with stars, large "Happy July 4th" headline, and the $1,500 savings figure land immediately without parsing effort.
- **Concrete offers are stacked** — 40% off outdoor furniture/equipment and 25% off (appliances/grills) are both visible as you scroll, with clear circle callouts.
- **Free-item offer** ("Extra Big Deal FREE" with string lights) echoes the June 29 free beach bucket send and adds a non-price hook.

## What's weak

- **Six competing sections with equal visual weight** — there is no editorial prioritization; every section looks the same, so none commands attention.
- **"Up to $1,500" is a ceiling, not a typical savings** — the actual offers visible (40%, 25%) don't obviously add up to that figure, making the headline feel like a stretch.
- **Sweepstakes "Activate now" CTA** competes directly with commerce CTAs; adding a separate action type fragments intent.
- **"Inside 👇" in the subject line** is a tired attention-baiting cliché that undercuts the premium "America's 250th" positioning right next to it.
- **Subscriber fatigue is real** — this is the 8th or 9th July 4th send since June 25; the cadence signal alone will suppress opens from anyone who already purchased or dismissed the earlier sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to three sections max**: hero + single strongest deal (the 40% outdoor furniture is the most July 4th-adjacent) + free-item hook. Cut the sweepstakes, the fence post section, and the appliances section for this send.
- 2. **Kill "inside 👇"** — replace with urgency copy tied to end-of-day cutoff: "Today only" or "Ends tonight."
- 3. **Pin a countdown to the hero** — a graphic clock reading "Sale ends tonight at midnight" would convert the implicit July 4th urgency into an explicit action trigger.
- 4. **Anchor the $1,500 figure** — show one specific product or bundle that represents realistic savings at that level, or drop the figure and use the real top deal (40% off) as the hero offer instead.
- 5. **Remove the sweepstakes from the main email flow** — it belongs in a standalone email or a bottom-footer mention, not a mid-scroll section with its own CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: Up to 40% off outdoor + free gift 🎆`
- **Subject Alt B:** `America's 250th sale — biggest outdoor deals of the year`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grills, furniture, power equipment — shop before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off outdoor + a free item, just for today`

## Technical Audit — Lowe's "Happy 250th, America!" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with custom web fonts, two overlapping responsive breakpoints, and several tracking pixels. The AgentMail relay failed to capture List-Unsubscribe and authentication headers, creating compliance gaps that cannot be resolved from the captured source alone.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **21 click-tracking links skipped** — all routed through Lowe's ESP redirect layer (`e.lowes.com`). Final destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity cannot be verified without resolving the redirect chain.
- **5 impression/pixel tracking URLs** from `sli.lowes.com` contain the persona email address in plaintext query params: `e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=742026&lctg=410146703`. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (8 confirmed violations):**

| Image | Type | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` | Content image | No `alt` attribute |
| `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` | Content image | No `alt` attribute |
| `o.gif` (mi.lowes.com) | Open pixel | No `alt=""` — should be empty string, not absent |
| 5× `imp` (sli.lowes.com) | Impression pixels | No `alt=""` — same issue |

For tracking pixels, omitting `alt` entirely vs. setting `alt=""` is technically distinct — some screen readers will announce the `src` filename if `alt` is absent. Pixels should use `alt=""` role="presentation".

**Overlapping media queries:** Two breakpoint blocks with conflicting ranges:
- Block 1: `min-width: 375px AND max-width: 600px` (with `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` Safari guard)
- Block 2: `max-width: 480px` (general)

Devices at 375–480px width satisfy both blocks simultaneously. Rule specificity will determine which wins, but this is fragile and can produce unintended behavior on mid-size Android devices.

**Font loading:** `@font-face` declarations for Fellix and DIN Next reference `.woff` only — no `.woff2` sources. No explicit system font fallback stack is visible in the provided CSS. If lowescdn.com fonts fail to load, rendering falls back to browser default (typically Times New Roman), which will break layout significantly.
