# July 4th Extra Big Deals end today 🚨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's ninth-plus July 4th-adjacent send since June 25, and it arrives on July 6 — two days after the actual holiday. "July 4th Extra Big Deals end today" is doing honest deadline work, but "July 4th" as a hook lands stale when the holiday is already in the rearview. The countdown timer in the appliances hero ("Clock's ticking on these savings") is the single strongest element — it gives the deadline visual proof, which is exactly what moves someone sitting on an appliance purchase.
- The structural problem is the same one that dragged down the July 1 send: too many competing categories in one scroll. Appliances, grills, patio furniture, lawn equipment, and then a Lowe's Blast Party Event teaser all fight for attention. Nothing gets owned. The July 12 Blast Party section at the bottom is smart timing — pivot the next campaign before the July 4th fatigue fully sets in — but it dilutes the urgency message this email is supposed to close on.
- Compared to the July 3 send ("50% OFF! July 4th is tomorrow🎇"), which was the tightest of the July 4th run, this feels like a mechanical "last chance" retargeting extension rather than a genuinely crafted closer. Functional, but not memorable.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer in the hero** is visible and credible — "Clock's ticking on these savings" plus actual digits makes the "end today" deadline feel real, not just copy.
- **Specific prices in every section** ($349 grill, $179, $159, $399) — no vague "up to X% off" vagueness in most blocks; shoppers know what they're looking at.
- **Logical section sequencing** — appliances → grills → patio → yard equipment follows a coherent home-improvement mental model; not random.
- **Blast Party Event teaser** smartly piggybacks next campaign (July 12 MyLowe's Money Days) onto an existing open — low friction next step.

## What's weak

- **"July 4th" branding on July 6** reads as stale; the holiday hook has expired and "end today" would be stronger standing alone.
- **Every CTA is "Shop Now"** — generic verb, no category specificity; a "Shop Grills" or "Shop Appliances" CTA would lift click rate on those sections.
- **Four competing hero categories** above the fold fragment the offer; the July 3 send focused the same energy into a cleaner lead.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is send nine-plus in eleven days from the same sender; a subscriber who opened the July 3 "tomorrow is the last day" email got lied to, which erodes trust on this send's urgency claim.
- **Loyalty/MyLowe's member pricing** not visible in any product tile — missed chance to add urgency with exclusive member stacking on top of the sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "July 4th" from the subject and body hero** — the holiday is over; "Extra Big Deals End Today" with the countdown is stronger on July 6 than clinging to a passed date. The July 3 send owned "tomorrow"; this one needs to own "right now."
- 2. **Collapse to two categories max** — pick appliances (high AOV, countdown drives action) and one impulse category (grills). Cut patio and yard to footer tiles or a separate send.
- 3. **Rewrite CTAs to be category-specific** — "Shop Appliances," "Build Your Outdoor Kitchen," "Finish the Yard" instead of four identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 4. **Surface MyLowe's member stacking** — even one line "Members: stack your MyLowe's Money before it expires" ties back to the June 30 Money Days teaser and adds a personalization layer.
- 5. **Move Blast Party teaser up** — currently buried; if it's meant to drive July 12 registrations, it needs to be one section higher or get its own send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra Big Deals end tonight — clock's ticking`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: appliance deals expire today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fridges, grills, patio — all on sale through midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your countdown to the best prices of the summer`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 tracking links skipped (not probed).** All click-through URLs route through Lowe's ESP redirect infrastructure (`e.lowes.com`). No broken links confirmed, but destination URL UTM coverage cannot be verified — see §6.

**Impression pixels expose persona address** in query params:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090834&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=762026&lctg=410146703
```

The `e=` parameter encodes the recipient address in plain text in a GET request to a third-party pixel host. This is standard ESP behavior but means Lowe's has a copy of the etell persona address tied to campaign `p=762026`.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` attribute entirely:**

| Type | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Content image | `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` |
| Content image | `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` |
| Tracking pixel | `mi.lowes.com/.../o.gif?mi_u=410146703` |
| Impression pixels (×5) | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=109083{4,4,5,7,8}...` |

The tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string, not omitted) — screen readers will read the filename or src URL when `alt` is absent entirely. Content images need descriptive alt text.

**Overlapping media query breakpoints:** Two separate query blocks target the 375–480px range simultaneously (`min-width:375px and max-width:600px` AND `max-width:480px`). Properties like `.container`, `.drop`, and `.mobile-hidden` are declared in both blocks with differing values. In a 375–480px viewport both blocks apply; cascade order resolves it, but it creates fragile override dependency.

**Font loading is correctly MSO-guarded.** Both `@font-face` blocks (Fellix, DIN Next) are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->`, so Outlook Word-renderer clients won't attempt CDN font fetches — correct pattern, no issue.
