# Ends tomorrow: Big can't-miss appliance deals‼️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's running the urgency close on the Closeout Event they introduced yesterday (July 17, 8/10) — category-focused, "Ends tomorrow," appliances as the hero. The strategic call is sound: the July 17 email opened the door, this one is supposed to push the subscriber through it. The appliances hero does its job — "Flash sale alert!" in a red badge, a lifestyle kitchen image, two CTAs.
- Then a Messi/MLS sports sponsorship module lands in the middle of the send with zero connective tissue. A subscriber scrolling through appliance deals hits "Tomorrow's the day. Get access to one-of-a-kind Messi exclusives / Join for FREE" and their purchase intent evaporates. This is clearly a media/sponsorship insertion that belongs in its own email — not wedged between a Closeout Event module and a Moen bathroom savings block.
- The deeper structural miss: the subject promises "big can't-miss appliance deals" but the only concrete dollar figure visible in the body is "$30 off" on Moen kitchen and bathroom products — not appliances. The appliance hero is all urgency, no number. That gap between subject promise and body delivery is a trust tax at scroll depth.

## What's working

- "Ends tomorrow" in the subject and repeated as a CTA label creates a credible, non-hyped countdown.
- The flash sale alert badge (red, all-caps, top of hero) gives the eye an immediate landing point.
- The Closeout Event module reinforces continuity for subscribers who saw yesterday's send — it rewards engaged readers.
- The Moen block surfaces "3x MyLowe's Rewards" in-body, not just the footer — loyalty benefit placed where it can actually influence a click.
- Five navigation tabs (APPLIANCES, DAILY DEALS, PATIO, OUTDOOR TOOLS) at the top give power-browsers an escape hatch to the category they care about.

## What's weak

- The Messi/MLS module has no home in an appliance urgency email — it breaks category scent and signals that this send is monetized ad inventory, not subscriber-first curation.
- The appliance hero contains no specific discount — "Savings you'll love" with no % or $ attached makes the "Big can't-miss deals" subject line feel like a bluff.
- Five modules (flash sale → closeout → Messi → Moen → Deals of the Day) with no visual priority hierarchy after the hero creates scroll fatigue.
- The Deals of the Day newsletter sign-up module burns the bottom of an urgency send on an acquisition pitch instead of a second conversion push.
- "Can't-miss" + "‼️" in the subject are mild hype signals that would be fully earned by a visible discount amount — without one, they register as noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or quarantine the Messi module** — send it standalone or as a co-marketing email the same day. In an appliance urgency send it actively destroys the purchase funnel for the primary objective.
- 2. **Put a real number in the appliance hero** — "Flash sale alert: Up to 30% off select appliances" (or whatever is true) unlocks multiple click signals and earns the subject's "Big" claim.
- 3. **Collapse to three modules** — hero appliances → Closeout Event reinforcement → Moen loyalty upsell. Strip the Deals of the Day acquisition pitch; urgency sends shouldn't ask subscribers to sign up for something new.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero CTA from "Shop now" to "Shop appliance deals"** — it's one word more and a measurable click-lift on category intent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tomorrow: Up to 30% off select appliances`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Appliance Closeout: last day for flash sale pricing`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kitchen and laundry deals closing out — grab yours before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Closeout Event appliance picks won't last another day.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**7 images missing `alt` text (confirmed by QA):**

| Image | Type | URL fragment |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-...jpg` | Content image | `image.e.lowes.com/lib/.../m/59/` |
| `ad569dc5-...png` | Content image | `image.e.lowes.com/lib/.../m/71/` |
| `imp` (×5) | Tracking pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` |

Content images without `alt` break screen readers and render blank in image-blocked clients (common in corporate environments). Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.

**Overlapping media queries create conflicting rules on 375–480px viewports.** The email defines two separate breakpoints:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px)` — sets `.container { width: 100% }`
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` — also sets `.container { width: 100%; max-width: 480px }`

On a 375–480px device both fire simultaneously. The second query's `max-width: 480px` on `.container` adds a constraint the first query doesn't have; cascade order determines which wins, but the overlap is unintentional and increases risk of layout bugs on that device range.

**Duplicate `local()` font hints** (cosmetic, no functional impact):
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url(...)
```
Every `@font-face` block duplicates the `local()` source. Harmless but indicates template sloppiness.

**`.sub_nav` receives `background-color: #15b5e5` at mobile; `.sub_nav_bottom` does not.** If these elements are intended to be visually paired, the missing background on `sub_nav_bottom` is a rendering gap.

Font loading is correctly gated with `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` conditionals — Outlook clients receive safe system-font fallbacks.
