# Want to win $3,000 in concert gift cards?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's is playing off-field here — a concert sweepstakes from a home improvement retailer. The creative execution is clean: dark concert-crowd hero, bold "$3,000 in concert gift cards" headline, and a single prominent "Enter to win" CTA. The MyLowe's Rewards positioning and the member-perks section (complimentary lawn chair rentals, two-for-one tickets) do the work of making this feel like a loyalty play rather than a random giveaway.
- The strategic concern is cadence. This is the second entertainment/sweepstakes send in two days — the Messi TikTok halftime event just ran July 19 (9/10). That prior email had a product anchor (the 10-ft Messi inflatable) and a same-day 10x points offer that tied directly to shopping behavior. This one has no shopping hook at all — no discount, no product, no urgency deadline. It's purely brand/loyalty engagement, and landing it the day after an already-strong event email risks feeling like filler.
- The perks section at the bottom partially saves it: lawn chair rentals and two-for-one tickets suggest a real venue partnership (almost certainly Lowe's Charlotte Motor Speedway), which grounds the sweepstakes in something tangible. But that story isn't surfaced in the hero — subscribers who don't scroll will miss the connection between the sweepstakes prize and the member benefits.

## What's working

- **Visual hierarchy is airtight** — the dark crowd + white type combination makes the $3,000 prize the undisputed focal point; nothing competes with it.
- **"Enter to win" CTA is clean and unambiguous** — single button, high contrast, correctly placed directly under the offer copy.
- **Member perks section adds real value** — two specific, tangible benefits (chair rentals, 2-for-1 tickets) reinforce the membership proposition beyond just the grand prize.
- **Subject line is specific** — "$3,000 in concert gift cards" with the dollar amount in the subject is a concrete anchor that beats vague "exclusive opportunity" language.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout is clean, images loaded, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **Zero shopping behavior tied to entry** — unlike the Messi 10x points send, there's no "spend $X to earn entries" mechanic visible; this drives no revenue even if opens are strong.
- **No deadline visible anywhere in the body** — sweepstakes without a close date feel less urgent and harder to act on.
- **The venue connection is buried** — lawn chair rentals and 2-for-1 tickets imply a specific venue partnership that would make the whole email more coherent, but it's never named in the hero.
- **Back-to-back sweepstakes fatigue** — following the Messi event (July 19) with another entertainment sweepstakes the very next day trains subscribers to treat these as filler, not action items.
- **"Ticket Cash" term unexplained** — appears in the preheader and body copy with an asterisk, but what it is and how to earn it is not visible above the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a shopping entry mechanic** — "Spend $50, earn a sweepstakes entry" or "Every purchase this week = one entry" connects the sweepstakes to a transaction and justifies its place in a retail calendar. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Name the venue and add a close date** — "Win $3,000 in Ticket Cash for [Venue] concerts — enter by July 31" turns a vague giveaway into a credible, time-bounded offer.
- 3. **Move the member perks above the fold** — "Your MyLowe's membership already includes 2-for-1 tickets and lawn chair rentals" is a more compelling reason to enter than the prize alone; it should precede the CTA, not follow it.
- 4. **Explain Ticket Cash in one sentence** — the asterisk creates doubt; a 6-word explainer ("Ticket Cash = credits for concert tickets") removes it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your MyLowe's perks: 2-for-1 tickets + $3K giveaway`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Win $3,000 in concert tickets — enter by July 31`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, members already get lawn chair rentals and 2-for-1 access.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $50 this week for an extra entry. Ends July 31.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (confirmed by QA):**
```
src: https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/1/a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg
```
Image renders as a blank rectangle in image-off contexts (Outlook default, some corporate environments) and is invisible to screen readers. This appears to be a hero or promotional image given the file path pattern.

**Malformed DOCTYPE — missing space:**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN"
```
Should be `PUBLIC "-//...` (space before the quoted string). Technically non-conformant; major clients parse it anyway, but it signals template hygiene debt.

**Duplicate `<!--[if !mso]>` font blocks:** Two separate conditional comment wrappers are used to load `@font-face` declarations — one for the Fellix family, one for DIN Next. These can be merged into a single block with no functional difference, reducing parse overhead.

**Font format — `.woff` only:** All six `@font-face` declarations reference only `.woff`, not `.woff2`. `.woff2` provides ~26% smaller file size where supported (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail). Low priority given limited email font support generally, but worth noting.

**CDN version inconsistency — DIN Next:**
- `DIN Next Regular`: `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- `DIN Next Bold` / `DIN Next Black`: `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/...`

Bold and Black variants are pinned to an older CDN path than Regular. If `1.2.0` is ever purged, those weights break silently.

**Legacy Gmail `.mobilehide` hack present** (`.mobilehide { display: none; color: #fff; }`). Functional but outdated — modern Gmail (2019+) supports `display:none` directly in `<style>` blocks. Not a bug, just legacy debt.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not found (WARN)**
Required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (enforced February 2024 for senders >5k/day). Absence risks increased spam folder placement. The QA note indicates this may be a relay capture gap rather than a genuine omission — confirm against raw SMTP headers via a seed inbox test (e.g., Mail-Tester, GlockApps).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header — not found (WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe. Gmail surfaces a native "Unsubscribe" button in the UI only when this header is present alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Its absence degrades the unsubscribe path for Gmail users and is now a bulk sender requirement.
