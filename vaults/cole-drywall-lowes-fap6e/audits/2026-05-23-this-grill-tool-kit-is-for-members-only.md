# This grill tool kit is for members only 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the right pivot for day seven of Lowe's Memorial Day blitz. After a week of broad "up to 60% off" ceiling-claims (starting May 17), narrowing to a specific member-exclusive grill kit two days before the holiday is exactly the segmentation move the cadence needed. The hero is clean, seasonally timed, and member-gated in a way that actually communicates value — the green "Member Only Access" badge and Silver status progress bar pull together into a coherent loyalty story.
- The big miss is that the value is named but never quantified. "Member Only Access" to a product you won't price is exclusivity theater. A subscriber who just got six Lowe's emails this week needs a number — even an MSRP comparison — to convert without clicking through to find out if the "exclusive" kit is $19 or $199. The 3x points module adds a secondary incentive but the "eligible purchases" qualifier softens it.
- This is the most competently targeted send of the Memorial Day run, but the no-price decision and seventh-email-in-seven-days fatigue cap its ceiling.

## What's working

- **Member framing is specific and visible.** The green "Member Only Access" badge + "New!" product label communicate both exclusivity and novelty above the fold.
- **Silver status bar is a smart retention mechanic.** "$500 away from Silver" converts a header into a personal loyalty gap — pulls the reader toward an action the rest of the email reinforces.
- **Grilling hero is perfectly timed.** "Every grill master needs their sidekick" two days before Memorial Day is textbook seasonal precision, especially after the brand spent a week narrowing from broad savings to specific outdoor use cases.
- **3x points module adds a distinct secondary CTA.** "Activate now" gives an engaged member something to do even if they're not ready to buy the kit.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no visual bugs visible anywhere in the email.

## What's weak

- **No price on the grill kit.** "Member Only Access" without a visible MSRP or discount amount means the exclusivity promise has no payoff the reader can evaluate in-body.
- **3x points qualifier undercuts itself.** "Earn points on an eligible purchase" (with the implied cap) reads like a catch. If there are exclusions, footnote them — don't put them in the hero copy of the module.
- **"Buy now" is generic.** For a member-exclusive product launch, this CTA does nothing to reinforce the privilege angle the rest of the email is selling.
- **Seventh email in seven days.** After May 17, 18, 19, 21 (twice), and 22, subscriber tolerance for another Lowe's send is threadbare. The member-specific framing is the only thing buying goodwill here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price in the hero** — even "Members: $X | Retail: $Y" — so the exclusivity claim has a dollar value attached. This is the single highest-leverage change; without it, "member only" is a label, not an offer.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to reinforce the exclusivity angle.** "Shop the member kit" or "Claim yours first" converts the button from a generic transaction prompt into an extension of the member privilege story.
- 3. **Move the 3x points qualifier to a footnote.** The module headline should read "Earn 3x points this weekend" — full stop. The eligible-purchase fine print belongs below, not inline.
- 4. **Add a soft deadline to the body.** The subject implies urgency; the body never delivers one. "Available through Memorial Day" or "Limited to member inventory" closes the loop.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your exclusive grill kit — 2 days before it's gone 🔥`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Member-only price inside — see your deal before the holiday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New kit. Members first. Offer ends Memorial Day.`

## Technical Audit — "This grill tool kit is for members only 🔥" (Lowe's)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally well-formed XHTML 1.0 Transitional with responsive CSS. The primary technical concerns are missing authentication headers, a malformed plain-text alternative, missing alt attributes on two images, and an inconsistent CDN version path for web fonts.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

| Finding | Evidence |

| Tracking pixel lacks empty alt | `https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/3e2de60a12f7f6f8/o.gif?mi_u=410146703` — no `alt=""` attribute |

The 24 unprobed links represent the highest-risk unverified area. If any redirect to a 404 or wrong landing page, it won't surface in automated checks.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text:**
- `a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg` (`image.e.lowes.com`) — missing `alt` entirely. Screen readers will announce the filename; promotional images should have descriptive alt or `alt=""` if decorative.
- `o.gif` (tracking pixel) — should have `alt=""` to suppress reader announcement.

**Font CDN version mismatch:**
- `DIN Next Regular` loads from `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/`
- `DIN Next Bold` and `DIN Next Black` load from `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/`

Inconsistent scaffold versions risk visual inconsistency if the 1.2.0 and 1.4.4 cuts differ in metrics or hinting.

**WOFF-only font declarations:** All `@font-face` blocks declare only `format('woff')` — no WOFF2 fallback. WOFF2 is supported in Apple Mail and modern iOS, where it loads ~30% faster.

**Overlapping media query breakpoints:** Two queries target overlapping ranges:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px)` (Fellix/DIN block)
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` (main responsive block)

Devices at 375–480px hit both queries simultaneously. Cascade order means the second block wins, but `.container`, `.drop`, and `.photo` rules in the first block may produce unexpected overrides on notched iPhones.

**Legacy Hotmail CSS:** `.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` hacks target Hotmail circa 2010. Modern Outlook.com ignores these; they add ~500 bytes of dead CSS.

No issues found. No unresolved `{{token}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`, or `%%variable%%` patterns are visible in the provided HTML source.

### 7. Recommendations

**High priority:**
1. **Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers** to the sending platform config — required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk compliance; confirm whether the AgentMail relay strips or passes these through.
2. **Rebuild the plain-text alternative** as human-readable content. Current version is a URL dump (82% URLs) and will trigger spam filters on content-aware MTAs.
3. **Confirm SPF/DKIM pass** through the `e.lowes.com` relay — obtain `Authentication-Results` from a test delivery to a Gmail inbox and verify `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass`.

**Medium priority:**
4. **Add `alt=""` to tracking pixel** (`o.gif`) and a descriptive `alt` to the hero image (`a22207c1...jpg`).
5. **Probe 3–5 CTA redirect URLs manually** to verify UTM parameters survive the `e.lowes.com` click-redirect.
6. **Align DIN Next CDN paths** — standardize Bold and Black to `scaffold/1.4.4` to match Regular.

**Low priority:**
7. Add `format('woff2')` variants before `format('woff')` in all `@font-face` declarations.
8. Audit the 375–480px breakpoint overlap to ensure `.container` and `.drop` rules resolve deterministically on iPhone SE / notched devices.
9. Remove dead `.ReadMsgBody` / `.ExternalClass` legacy Hotmail CSS.
