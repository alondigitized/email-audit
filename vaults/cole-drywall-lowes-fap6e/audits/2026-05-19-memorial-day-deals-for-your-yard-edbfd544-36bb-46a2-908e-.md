# Memorial Day deals for your yard🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's narrowing its Memorial Day lens from "everything in the store at UP TO 60% OFF" to a specific yard-and-outdoor play: cordless tools, outdoor power equipment, and 5-for-$10 lawn care. That pivot is the right call given the cadence — subscribers have absorbed six-plus broad Memorial Day blasts in eight days. The category specificity (CRAFTSMAN V20, EGO POWER+, pressure washer, Miracle-Gro) makes this email more scannable than its predecessors.
- What undercuts it is the lack of urgency mechanics and price anchoring. Memorial Day is six days out, and not a single module says "Ends Monday" or shows a crossed-out MSRP. The $99/$479/$299 prices float without context — a subscriber has no idea if $479 for an EGO POWER+ is a deal or list price. That's a structural miss for a promotional send at this stage of the calendar.
- Verdict: a competent category-pivot email that does the minimum needed to differentiate from the prior sends, but leaves the hardest conversion work undone. The same subscriber who deleted four Lowe's emails this week will probably skim this one and close.

## What's working

- **Category specificity lands** — "for your yard" in the subject is the clearest audience segmentation Lowe's has done in this entire Memorial Day run; the outdoor power equipment grid delivers on the promise.
- **Specific prices ($99, $479, $299)** are scannable and concrete; far better than the vague "up to 60% OFF" framing from the May 17 send.
- **Recognized product brands** (CRAFTSMAN, EGO POWER+) add credibility — shoppers comparison-shop these SKUs and will recognize the names immediately.
- **5 for $10 lawn care module** at the bottom is practical and impulsive — a genuinely useful offer that requires no mental math.
- **Navigation bar** ("Memorial Day | Tools | Daily Deals | Appliances") gives scanners a quick bypass to their preferred category without forcing a full read.

## What's weak

- **Zero urgency mechanics** — Memorial Day is six days away and not one CTA or module copy says "Ends Monday" or gives a deadline; the entire email reads like an evergreen catalog.
- **No price anchoring** — $479 for an EGO POWER+ mower is meaningless without a struck-through MSRP; the subscriber can't gauge the actual savings.
- **Hero copy doesn't match the subject** — "Deals this HOT are made for summer plans" is generic lifestyle filler; the subject said "yard," and the hero copy should echo it.
- **No MyLowe's member callout** — the May 15 loyalty send specifically made membership feel valuable; this email abandons that thread entirely and gives members no reason to feel seen.
- **Eighth email in eight days** — cadence fatigue is real, and this send doesn't do enough to justify its slot; a tighter "last weekend before Memorial Day" frame with hard deadlines would.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to every CTA** — replace all "Shop now" buttons with "Shop now — Ends Monday" or a visible countdown badge. This is the single highest-lift change available at this stage of the calendar.
- 2. **Anchor every featured price with a struck-through MSRP** — e.g., "~~$599~~ $479" on the EGO POWER+. Subscribers need to see the savings, not just the price.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero copy to mirror the subject** — swap "Deals this HOT are made for summer plans" for something like "Your yard's best weekend starts here" or "Outfit your yard before the long weekend."
- 4. **Resurface the MyLowe's member angle** — a single badge ("Members earn 2× points this weekend") would differentiate from Amazon and Home Depot without requiring a layout change.
- 5. **Add "ends [date]" or a red countdown badge** to the 5-for-$10 lawn care offer — the format begs for urgency and gets none.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Outdoor power deals — EGO, CRAFTSMAN, pressure washers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `CRAFTSMAN V20 at $99 · EGO POWER+ at $479 · 5 for $10 lawn mix — ends Monday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last week to save big on mowers, tools, and lawn care before Memorial Day`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Eight images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):

| Asset | Type | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-…99.jpg` | Content image | Screen reader gap; broken image fallback shows no description |
| `ad569dc5-…05.png` | Content image | Same |
| `o.gif` (mi.lowes.com) | Open-tracking pixel | Low — tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string), not omitted `alt` |
| 5× `sli.lowes.com/imp` | Impression pixels | Same as above — `alt=""` required, attribute absent |

The two content images are the meaningful failures. The tracking pixels need `alt=""` (empty, not missing) to suppress screen reader noise.

**Font loading — WOFF only, no WOFF2:**

```css
url('https://www.lowescdn.com/sffonts/Fellix-Regular.woff') format('woff')
```

All six `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` only. WOFF2 (30% smaller) is supported by every modern email client that supports web fonts. Minor bandwidth concern, not a rendering defect.

**Dual `<!--[if !mso]>` blocks** — the custom font CSS is split across two conditional comment blocks for no apparent reason. Functionally harmless but increases parse weight.

**Media query fragmentation** — two separate responsive breakpoints defined in distinct `<style>` blocks (`min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` + `max-width: 480px`). These overlap at 480px. No confirmed rendering defect, but the overlap is a maintenance risk.
