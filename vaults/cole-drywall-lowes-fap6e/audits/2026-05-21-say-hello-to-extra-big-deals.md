# Say hello to Extra. Big. Deals. 🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's Memorial Day endgame move — a countdown-timer email designed to convert fence-sitters before the holiday weekend closes. The countdown mechanic is the right call at this stage of the cadence; the brand has already exhausted "Up to 60% OFF" escalations, so switching to clock-pressure is a legitimate gear shift. The execution inside is solid: specific dollar prices, multiple product pods, and a clean hierarchy anchored to the timer.
- The fatal wound is the subject line. After seven emails that led with concrete discount numbers, "Say hello to Extra. Big. Deals." abandons the thing that was working. The fragmented period-separated phrasing reads as a copywriting stylization that swaps specificity for swagger — right when the send needs the inbox open rate most. The body earns it; the envelope doesn't.
- Net judgment: a strong email let down by the one thing subscribers see before they decide to open. The countdown is a genuine urgency device and the price anchors are credible, but this send will underperform its potential purely on subject-line weakness in a fatigued cadence.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is the right escalation.** "Ending In 04:13:27:24" is a concrete, non-repeatable urgency signal — the first time this cadence has deployed real scarcity mechanics after seven discount-led sends.
- **Specific price anchors.** $1,399 on appliances and $499 on the grill give subscribers something real to evaluate instead of a vague "up to X% off."
- **Multi-category pod structure.** Appliances → lawn/outdoor → grill → financing covers three distinct buyer intents without the layout becoming cluttered.
- **"Grab them while you still can" + "Shop now"** — the hero CTA copy closes the urgency loop opened by the countdown; no ambiguity about what action to take.
- **Deals of the Day signup at the bottom** is a smart retention play at the close of a major campaign window.

## What's weak

- **Subject line drops specificity at the worst moment.** "Extra. Big. Deals." is style without substance — no number, no category, no reason to open that the previous seven sends didn't already provide.
- **The countdown timer is invisible from the inbox.** The urgency that earns the click lives entirely in the body; none of it bleeds into subject or preheader where it would actually drive opens.
- **"We've got you." pod is vague.** The lawn/outdoor section headline is a brand-voice statement, not an offer. The "Save up to $150" is buried; it should lead.
- **Financing pod (12–18 months) placed mid-email** interrupts the product-price rhythm. It belongs at the bottom as a secondary message, not sandwiched between grill and Deals of the Day.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the countdown in the subject.** Ending-in copy is the single highest-leverage change. Something like "4 days left: Extra Big Deals end Monday" would be the first time this cadence gives subscribers a specific end-date in the envelope.
- 2. **Replace "We've got you." with the offer.** The headline for the outdoor/lawn pod should read "Save up to $150 on select riding mowers" — the specificity that "We've got you." buries.
- 3. **Move the financing pod to the footer.** It disrupts the discount-browsing flow mid-email and is a secondary message better suited as a PS for already-interested subscribers.
- 4. **Preheader should echo the clock.** If the body opens with "The countdown is on," the preheader should reinforce the specific deadline — not duplicate the subject's "Extra Big Deals" phrasing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4 days left — Extra Big Deals end Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Countdown: $499 grill + big appliance deals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday: $1,399 appliances, $499 grills, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The clock is running — deals this big don't last`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA scan:

| Image | URL Fragment | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Hero/promo image | `c301e24e...jpg` | Content image |
| Promo image | `ad569dc5...png` | Content image |
| Open pixel | `o.gif` | Tracking pixel |
| 5× impression pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` | Tracking pixels |

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `imp`) should have `alt=""` (empty string) to be invisible to screen readers. Content images (`*.jpg`, `*.png`) require descriptive `alt` text per WCAG 2.1 AA.

**Custom web fonts load via WOFF only** (`lowescdn.com/sffonts/Fellix-*.woff`). No WOFF2 format is declared, which is the preferred modern format. Clients that don't support the web font will fall back silently, but loading WOFF2 first would reduce payload size.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two media queries target `max-width: 480px` and `min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px`. The 375–480px range is covered by both queries simultaneously; confirm specificity ordering doesn't produce unintended overrides on that range.

**Legacy Hotmail/ExternalClass hacks present** (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`). These are harmless but target clients with <0.1% market share and add noise.

However, it cannot be confirmed that Salesforce Marketing Cloud is emitting this header on the original SMTP envelope. If absent on the true delivery path, this is a **CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance failure** (both require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders as of 2024).

**`Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM):** Not captured via AgentMail relay. Cannot verify DKIM signing on `e.lowes.com`. Lowe's is a major ESP-managed sender and almost certainly signs, but this should be confirmed with a direct SMTP header dump from a real inbox (e.g., Gmail "Show original").
