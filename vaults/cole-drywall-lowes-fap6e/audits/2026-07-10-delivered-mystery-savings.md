# Delivered: Mystery savings 🎁
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- "Delivered: Mystery savings 🎁" lands on Day 2 of Money Days (July 9–12) — and doesn't mention Money Days once. That's the buried lede. Yesterday's email launched the event with authority and scored 8/10; this one pivots to a gamified "Tap to reveal" envelope hero that, for the vast majority of subscribers in Gmail or Outlook, renders as a static blue card with half-visible text. The mystery never resolves. The hero that's supposed to hook the scroll is, in practice, a broken experience.
- Below the fold, there's legitimate substance: 40% off décor lighting, 30% member pricing on bath, a tiered spend-and-save mechanic. But three separate offer sections compete for attention with identical "Shop now" labels and no priority ranking. The email tries to be a teaser, a category sale, and a loyalty mechanic simultaneously — and nails none of them cleanly.
- Compared to the candle email (July 7, 7/10) which had a clear creative point of view, or Money Days proper (July 9, 8/10) which had event authority, this send is the weakest in recent memory. A clever subject line doing real work in the inbox can't compensate for an offer that refuses to surface.

## What's working

- **"Delivered:" subject framing** mimics delivery notifications — a genuine pattern interrupt in a crowded inbox.
- **Preheader is real copy** ("Uncover the surprise. This week see Lowe's exclusive brands, lighting ideas and more.") — builds on the mystery without repeating the subject verbatim.
- **Concrete discounts exist** (40% off lighting/ceiling fans, 30% member bath) for anyone who scrolls to them.
- **Member pricing is called out explicitly**, reinforcing loyalty value in a straightforward way.
- **Tiered spend-and-save** is a smart basket-builder mechanic appropriate for the home improvement category.

## What's weak

- **Interactive envelope hero is broken** for most email clients — "Tap to reveal" goes nowhere in Gmail/Outlook; subscribers see a static card with partial text ("r sav...") and no payoff.
- **Zero connection to Money Days** despite running through July 12 — squanders the momentum built by yesterday's 8/10 launch send.
- **Visual hierarchy is cluttered** — three competing offer blocks with identical "Shop now" CTAs, no dominant hero deal.
- **CTAs are generic** — "Shop now" attached to broad category names drives weaker click intent than specific product or use-case copy.
- **Spend-and-save tiers buried at bottom** — the most basket-building mechanic in the email is the last thing anyone sees.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the broken interactive hero or replace it with a static reveal.** Show the best offer directly — "Up to 40% off lighting, today only" — with a real CTA button. An interactive element that doesn't render is actively worse than a static offer.
- 2. **Tie this to Money Days.** "Day 2 of Money Days — your mystery deal is inside" bridges yesterday's creative investment and keeps the July 9–12 event alive in the subscriber's mind.
- 3. **Pick one hero offer.** 40% off lighting is the most visual and giftable — lead with a single hero product image, not three competing category blocks.
- 4. **Specify the CTAs.** "Shop ceiling fans" or "See bath member deals" outperforms "Shop now" because the destination is legible before the click.
- 5. **Move spend tiers above the fold.** A tiered mechanic is a powerful basket-builder — it should anchor the scroll, not close the email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 40% off lighting — plus your mystery deal inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `40% off lighting + member bath deals — see what's waiting`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Money Days are live — here's your exclusive offer`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (real issue):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero/content JPG | `https://image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-...jpg` |
| Content PNG | `https://image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-...png` |

These should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked rendering.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels (minor, but noisy):**
Seven tracking pixels (`o.gif` ×2, `imp` ×5) have no `alt` attribute at all. They should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen reader announcements. Absence of the attribute is technically non-conformant even for spacer/pixel images.

**Overlapping CSS media query breakpoints:**
Two breakpoint blocks apply simultaneously in the 375–480px range:
```css
/* Block 1 */
@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)

/* Block 2 */
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
Both fire on WebKit devices between 375–480px. Properties like `.container`, `.drop`, `.photo`, and `.mobile-only` are redeclared in both blocks with different constraints (`max-width: 480px` vs `width: 100%`). Cascade order resolves this, but it's a maintenance hazard and may produce inconsistent rendering across WebKit versions.

**Web font format:** All `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` only — no `.woff2`. Email client support for `woff2` is limited, so this is low risk, but the CDN paths suggest `.woff2` variants may exist on `lowescdn.com` and could reduce font payload size.
