# 10 Days of Deals start TODAY 🎉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 2 of a declared 10-day Father's Day Deals campaign, and the smart move here is the catch-up framing: the hero leads with "You missed Day 1 of 10 Days Of Father's Day Deals. But more deals are still rolling." That's a legitimate FOMO lever — it signals ongoing value without making the recipient feel punished for skipping yesterday. The execution after the hero, though, is where this email loses momentum: five or more stacked content modules fight for equal visual weight, and no single offer wins. The DeWalt "FREE" gift call-out is the most purchase-ready signal in the entire email, but it's buried mid-scroll under a vague headline and brand logo rather than being the hero.
- The "$99" price module and the "G.A.T. is almost gone" section both carry urgency signals, but "G.A.T." is an unexplained abbreviation — even with a blue mascot character nearby, it demands insider knowledge a casual subscriber won't have. That's a wasted urgency moment. The email also continues Lowe's's pattern from prior sends (June 5–9 had four distinct emails in five days) of running the full product-category stack in a single send rather than committing to one theme.
- Overall: competent scaffolding, a genuinely strong hero frame, but diluted by the same multi-module sprawl that plagues the brand's high-frequency stretches. It's a 6 in practice where the DeWalt offer alone could have been a 9.

## What's working

- **"You missed Day 1" hook** creates real FOMO without being punishing — it implicitly signals the campaign is worth tracking across 10 days.
- **DeWalt FREE offer** is the most specific purchase trigger in the email — brand trust + a free item is a proven combo, and it's visually distinct with the DeWalt logo and product shot.
- **$99 price anchor** is visible and concrete — a number in the body beats vague "great deals" language every time.
- **Blue hero section** is clean and visually dominant — the eye lands there first, which is the right call for a campaign-launcher email.
- **"Deals of the Day" sign-up footer** is a smart retention play to capture deal-seekers who aren't already on that list.

## What's weak

- **Five-plus content modules of nearly equal visual weight** after the hero — no single offer wins the scroll, and attention evaporates fast.
- **"G.A.T. is almost gone"** is brand jargon — the abbreviation is unexplained, the urgency evaporates if you don't know what G.A.T. stands for.
- **"Shop now" repeated identically across every CTA** — no differentiation between a $99 product, a free DeWalt battery kit, and a riding mower; each deserves distinct copy.
- **No visible end date for the 10-day campaign** — "10 Days" starting June 11 implies June 20, but the math is never shown, so the urgency is soft.
- **High send frequency** from prior history (4 emails in 5 days, now daily for 10) risks fatigue — the cadence is working against opens even when the content is solid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate to one hero offer** — the DeWalt FREE gift is the sharpest purchase trigger here; make it the full hero, not a mid-scroll module. Everything else can live below the fold as supporting content.
- 2. **Explain the G.A.T.** — spell it out ("The G.A.T. — Greatest All-Time Tool Kit") or drop the abbreviation entirely. Urgency only works when the shopper knows what they're losing.
- 3. **Rewrite every CTA to match its section** — "Claim your free battery kit," "Shop $99 and under," "Build the perfect gift box." "Shop now" four times in a row is noise.
- 4. **Show the campaign end date explicitly** — "10 Days of Deals end June 20 — Day 2 of 10" anchors urgency and gives a reason to re-open tomorrow.
- 5. **Preheader should tease the best offer** — if the DeWalt FREE deal is the hook, lead with it in the preheader, not a generic campaign line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Day 2: Free DeWalt battery kit when you buy`
- **Subject Alt B:** `10-Day Deal drops: Day 2 of 10 is live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `FREE DeWalt 20V battery kit with qualifying purchase — today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `9 deals left. Today's best: free battery kit + $99 tool picks`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 tracked links unverified**
All click links route through Lowe's redirect infrastructure. The QA probe skipped HTTP resolution for these, so final destination URLs, UTM parameters, and 404s cannot be confirmed from this capture.

**Recipient PII in impression-tracking URLs (5 instances)**
The `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels encode the recipient email address as a plaintext GET parameter:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=6112026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```
The `e=` value is an unencoded email address. This appears in 5 separate pixels across the email. Server access logs at `sli.lowes.com` and any CDN/proxy in the chain will record this as plaintext PII.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE syntax error**
Missing space between `PUBLIC` and the FPI string:
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN"
```
Should be `PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD`. Strict parsers may reject the document or fall back to quirks mode.

**8 images missing alt text (QA-confirmed)**
- Content image: `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` — hero or feature image, missing descriptive alt
- Content image: `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` — product image, missing alt
- Tracking pixels: `o.gif`, and 5× `sli.lowes.com/imp` — these should carry `alt=""` explicitly; absence allows some screen readers to announce the src filename

**Font CDN version mismatch**
DIN Next Regular loads from `scaffold/1.4.4`, while DIN Next Bold and Black load from `scaffold/1.2.0`:
```
https://www.lowescdn.com/scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff
https://www.lowescdn.com/scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff
https://www.lowescdn.com/scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-black.woff
```
Cross-version mixing can produce metric inconsistencies (different advance widths), causing layout shifts if both weights render.

**No WOFF2 font sources**
All eight `@font-face` declarations specify `.woff` only. WOFF2 offers ~30% smaller file size and is supported by all clients that support web fonts in email. Missing `format('woff2')` source means unnecessary bandwidth on modern clients.

**Overlapping mobile breakpoints**
Two media queries share overlapping coverage for 375–480px devices:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)`
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)`

Both apply on 375–480px WebKit. Rules like `.drop` and `.drop-center` appear in both blocks with the same declarations, which is redundant but harmless. Any divergence between the two blocks on overlapping selectors would produce unpredictable cascading.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed (QA WARN)**
Header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 requires this for bulk mail. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, the audit cannot confirm it; if it is genuinely absent, this is a compliance defect. Gmail and Yahoo Mail require `List-Unsubscribe` for senders above 5,000/day volume thresholds.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — unconfirmed (QA WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Google and Yahoo enforce this requirement for bulk senders. Absence will cause Gmail to surface a manual unsubscribe flow, increasing spam complaints.

**Authentication-Results — unconfirmed (QA WARN)**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture. The sending domain is `e.lowes.com` (ESP subdomain). If DMARC alignment is not configured for this subdomain, emails may be flagged despite DKIM signing by the ESP.

**Recipient email in GET parameters**
As noted in §2, the plaintext `e=` parameter in `sli.lowes.com/imp` URLs records PII in server logs and any intermediary proxy. This is a GDPR Article 5(1)(f) data-minimization concern and would surface in a CAN-SPAM/CCPA audit if logs are retained without access controls.
