# FINAL HOURS: up to 50% OFF⏰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a "final hours" email that doesn't act like one. The subject promises urgency and "up to 50% OFF." The second hero delivers that: a big white "Get up to 50% OFF" over "Thanksgiving Day deals end tonight." But it sits behind a generic Black Friday member banner. The first screen sells early access to Doorbusters with no offer, so the subject's promise is not the first thing the eye lands on.
- Below the hero, the email becomes a kitchen sink. It has an in-store sweepstakes, a "12 Days of Deals" holiday décor block, a dense appliance offer, a MyLowe's Rewards sweepstakes and a Deals of the Day signup. Each block is well made, but together they split attention. The appliance block is the only one with real substance: 50% off, 25% off bundles, free delivery, and free install and haul-away. It is also loaded with fine print. This is the same pattern as the 11/25 ladders send, where the subject led with one offer and the body opened with a banner.
- Judgement: a credible, on-brand send for a real deadline, but it diffuses its own message. The urgency is genuine, but the email never says what is ending.

## What's working

- The second hero matches the subject. Its huge "50% OFF" with "deals end tonight" is the strongest visual in the email.
- The black, white and red palette is consistent and unmistakably Lowe's.
- The appliance block has concrete, stacked value: 50% off, 25% off a bundle, free delivery, and free installation and haul-away.
- The "Ending tomorrow" holiday décor block adds a second real deadline.
- The MyLowe's Rewards block gives members a reason to engage.

## What's weak

- The first hero is a "Shop now" banner with no price or percentage, so the subject's offer is pushed down to the second block.
- The email says "ending tonight" in one block, "tomorrow" in another and "Two Days Only" in a third. The deadlines contradict each other and weaken the "final hours" claim.
- There are six or more CTAs, and most say "Shop now" or "Sign up." Only "Enter now" and "Explore more" differ, and none names a product.
- The "up to 50% off" hero doesn't say what it applies to.
- The footer carries several long blocks of dense legal text, which is not a render bug but does add to the clutter.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the "Get up to 50% OFF / Thanksgiving Day deals end tonight" hero first and cut the generic early-access banner. The subject's promise should be the first thing the reader sees.
- 2. Make the countdown consistent. Pick one deadline, such as "ends tonight at 11:59 PM," and repeat it.
- 3. Name the category in the hero CTA, for example "Shop appliance deals" or "Shop tools."
- 4. Cut the in-store sweepstakes and the Rewards sweepstakes to one. Two prize blocks dilute a deal-focused send.
- 5. Move the free delivery and install benefits up into the hero as a single line, and push the legal text lower.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: up to 50% OFF appliances + free delivery`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: Thanksgiving deals end at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tools, appliances and décor, plus early Doorbuster access tomorrow at 6 a.m.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free delivery and install on major appliances. Doors open 6 a.m. Friday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The source and QA data show no hard failures, but the email has two real issues: unhashed recipient email addresses in tracking-pixel URLs, and 18 images with no `alt` attribute. The 57% pass rate mostly reflects unverifiable checks. Headers were not captured, and 31 links were skipped. The HTML is also truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and UTM details could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **PII in pixel URLs:** every `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixel carries the plaintext recipient address, for example `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=127768300&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=531941&lctg=410146703&stpe=pixel`. The address is in the URL query string, so it can end up in proxy, CDN and ad-server logs and in Referer headers. A hashed identifier would avoid this.
- **Duplicate impression pixels:** these appear twice each:
  - `s=127768300` through `127768304`
  - `s=2019286`
  - `s=1090834`

  This is likely a mobile/desktop block pair, but it could double-count impressions in clients that load both.
- **Unverified links:** 31 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the probe, so destination status, redirect chains and link-level errors are unknown.
- No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on content images (2):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

  These show nothing meaningful to screen readers or with images blocked.
- **Missing `alt` on tracking pixels (16):** all `sli.lowes.com/imp` images. These should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them. The lack of an attribute is a low-severity compliance gap.
- **Malformed DOCTYPE:** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space after `PUBLIC`. Clients usually tolerate this, but it is invalid.
- **Wrong XHTML namespace:** `xmlns="https://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` uses https. The namespace URI is `http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml`. This is harmless in practice.
- **No `lang` attribute:** the `<html>` tag has none, which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Legacy CSS:** the `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` hacks are outdated but harmless.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unrendered merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the visible source or QA output. The recipient email is resolved correctly in the pixel URLs. That is a PII concern (see section 2), not a token failure.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** neither was found. The QA tool says AgentMail may not relay these headers, so this is **unconfirmed, not a confirmed violation**. The sender is `lowes@e.lowes.com`, a large ESP sender, and would normally include both. Check the raw message headers.
- **Authentication-Results:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. This is a relay limitation.
- **Footer content:** the physical address and unsubscribe link fall outside the truncated HTML, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
This cannot be assessed. The visible source has no `href` values, and all 31 tracking links were skipped. No UTM parameters, redirect targets or landing pages were checked.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Replace the plaintext `e=<email>` in `sli.lowes.com/imp` URLs with a hashed or opaque ID.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two content images (`c301e24e…jpg`, `ad569dc5…png`) and `alt=""` to all 16 pixel images.
3. Confirm whether the duplicate impression pixels are intentional, and deduplicate them if they are not.
4. Fix the DOCTYPE spacing, set `<html lang="en">`, and use the `http://` XHTML namespace.
5. Tooling: capture raw headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, Authentication-Results) and resolve the 31 skipped tracking links, so those checks stop appearing as warnings and the pass rate reflects real results. Feed the untruncated HTML to the audit so footer, unsubscribe and UTM checks can run.
