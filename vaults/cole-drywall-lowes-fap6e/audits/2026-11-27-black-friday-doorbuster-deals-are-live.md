# ⚡️Black Friday Doorbuster Deals are LIVE⚡
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a real Black Friday doorbuster email, and it is a better fit for a home-and-kitchen persona than Lowe's usual sends. The hero is clean: black background, "Black Friday Doorbuster Deals," and three priced products ($99 tree, $299 LG fridge, $99 Craftsman rolling tower). A buyer sees concrete prices in the first screen, not a generic banner with a "Shop now" button.
- The email then tries to be everything at once. It has five or six offers in sequence: poinsettias at $1, an in-store prize giveaway, DEWALT batteries at $129, the appliance block with 50% off and 25% off bundles, a credit card promo, and a Deals of the Day signup. The appliance block carries the kitchen-relevant value, but it sits roughly halfway down. Dense legal copy at the bottom and several stacked "Shop now" buttons dilute the focus.
- Judgement: a solid, well-timed send with credible prices, but it leads with generic doorbusters instead of the strongest offer. After the Nov 26 "FINAL HOURS: up to 50% OFF" email, it also repeats the same 50% beat with little new news.

## What's working

- The hero shows specific prices on three products, so the doorbuster claim is concrete.
- The "FREE same-day delivery" banner above the hero is a strong, low-friction hook.
- The appliance block is well built: "up to 50% off," the 25% bundle, free delivery, and free installation and haul-away.
- The black-and-red palette reads as Black Friday and stays consistent with Lowe's earlier sends this week.
- The "Doors open at 6 a.m. today" in-store block creates same-day urgency that is believable.

## What's weak

- The appliance offer, the most relevant one for a home or kitchen persona, is buried below poinsettias, a prize giveaway and power-tool batteries.
- There are too many offers and too many "Shop now" buttons, so no single CTA clearly wins.
- The in-store prize block ("First 150 members") is irrelevant to someone shopping online and takes a full screen of space.
- The appliance block mixes 50% off, 25% off, free delivery, installation and haul-away, and a $896 threshold, which is hard to parse. Dense fine print follows at the bottom.
- The "Last day" tag on the appliance block conflicts with the "one-day-only" doorbusters above it. Which deadline applies is unclear.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the ALL-IN appliance offer into the second slot, directly under the hero, and drop the in-store prize block for online-focused recipients.
- 2. Cut the offer count to three: doorbusters, appliances, DEWALT. Move the credit card and Deals of the Day modules to a slim footer strip.
- 3. Make each CTA specific: "Shop doorbuster fridges" or "Shop 50% off appliances" instead of repeated "Shop now."
- 4. Add one clear deadline line near the top, such as "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM," to settle the urgency conflict.
- 5. Simplify the appliance block into a two-line offer, with the $896 and fine-print details behind a link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fridges from $299 + free same-day delivery`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off appliances, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `LG fridge $299, DEWALT 4-pack $129, doors open at 6 a.m.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free delivery and installation on select appliances. Ends tonight.`

# Technical Audit: Lowe's "Black Friday Doorbuster Deals are LIVE"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML email with responsive media queries and hosted web fonts. It has a malformed DOCTYPE and several images without alt text. The recipient's email address is embedded in plaintext in the impression-pixel URLs. Compliance and continuity can't be fully verified because the source is truncated and the relay stripped headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 32 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status are unconfirmed.
- **Recipient email in pixel URLs:** The `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels carry the address in the `e=` parameter, for example `e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app`. This is unhashed PII in a URL that is logged by proxies and CDNs. It appears on at least 8 distinct pixel URLs, for example `s=127768300&...&stpe=pixel`.
- **Duplicate pixels:** Several `imp` URLs appear twice (`s=127768300` to `s=127768304`, `s=2019286`, `s=1090834`). This could double-count impressions. It may also be an artifact of the extraction (e.g., a desktop/mobile variant pair), so treat it as a possible issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed DOCTYPE:** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space between `PUBLIC` and the quoted identifier. Most clients tolerate this, but it is invalid and can trigger quirks mode.
- **Missing alt text on content images (2):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

  If these are visible content, they are blank to screen readers and to clients that block images.
- **Missing alt text on tracking pixels (about 17):** The `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` images have no alt attribute. These are 1×1 pixels, so the impact is low. Adding `alt=""` and `role="presentation"` would be the correct markup.
- **Font fallback:** The `@font-face` rules use `Fellix` and `DIN Next` from `lowescdn.com`. Gmail and Outlook ignore them, so fallback stacks matter. These can't be verified in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
