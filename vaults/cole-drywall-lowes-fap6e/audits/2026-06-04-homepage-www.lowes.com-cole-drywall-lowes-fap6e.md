# Homepage snapshot · www.lowes.com · 2026-06-04
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's is running a Father's Day push that's timed right — 17 days out is the sweet spot for a campaign, and I'm exactly who they should be talking to. Problem is, the first thing I see isn't a sale on Kobalt bits — it's a sign-in modal covering my whole screen pitching me a $5 new-member discount I don't need because I've already got the Advantage card. Good campaign timing, bad first impression for an existing customer.

## What's working

- **Father's Day timing**: Running this campaign 17 days out is exactly right. My wife and kids might use this, and I'll probably use "it's Father's Day" as justification to buy myself something from the tool aisle.
- **Sign-in modal with Silver Key callout**: "FREE shipping with Silver Key status" reminds me why I've got this card. If I'd forgotten, this would snap me back.
- **Category pills behind the modal**: Even half-covered, I can see "Electric Range," "Washing Machine," and "Bathro..." — those are the big-ticket appliances I default to Lowe's for, especially around sale events.
- **Lifestyle hero image**: Dad and kid playing with the dog in the backyard sprinklers. That's me on a Saturday when I come up from the basement for air.
- **"Shop Now" CTA**: Direct, no-nonsense copy. I know what to do.

## What's weak

- **Forced sign-in modal for existing members**: I've got a Lowe's Advantage card. Opening with a new-member acquisition popup before I can even see the site is flat-out annoying. That $5 deal is for someone who hasn't signed up yet — not me.
- **Zero above-the-fold hook for my actual project**: I'm mid-basement. Drywall, Allen + Roth panels, Kobalt bits — nothing about building materials or project supplies anywhere I can see. Lowe's should know by now I'm not here for a watering can.
- **"FREE Gifts" strip is cut off at the bottom**: I can see the top edge of what looks like a meaningful promo strip, and I can't read it. That's a tease with no payoff on this screen.
- **No price magnitude in the hero**: "Great gifts for the greatest dad ever" is warm but doesn't move me. "Up to 25% off tools this Father's Day" would have me tapping immediately.
- **$5 new-member offer leading the modal**: I'm a returning customer being served an acquisition pitch. That's the wrong message for the wrong audience.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the new-member modal for returning users**: If I've browsed or purchased before, swap the popup for a personalized header — "Welcome back, Cole — X points ready to use." That's the returning-shopper experience a loyalty program should deliver.
- **Add a tools/project sub-banner under the Father's Day hero**: "Shop Kobalt — exclusively at Lowe's" or "Up to 30% off power tools for the builder dad." Differentiate from Home Depot by leaning into the private-label tool brands.
- **Anchor the hero with a percent or dollar off**: The Father's Day headline needs a price hook. Even "Gift cards starting at $25" or "Tools up to 40% off" gives me a reason to tap instead of window-shop.
- **Pull the "FREE Gifts" strip above the fold**: Whatever that blue banner is promising, it's compelling enough to tease — give it room to breathe where I can actually read it.
