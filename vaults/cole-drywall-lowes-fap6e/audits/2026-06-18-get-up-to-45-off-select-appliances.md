# Get up to 45% OFF select appliances 🥳
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 7 or 8 of Lowe's declared "10 Days of Father's Day Deals" run, and the seams are showing. The subject line promises appliances at 45% off, delivers that in the hero — then immediately pivots to indoor products at 40%, then kitchen islands, then Lowe's Home Cards, then a "Build it, spin it!" sweepstakes-style registration block, then a Deals of the Day newsletter signup. Six separate content blocks, five distinct CTAs. The email has no through-line.
- The countdown timer in the hero is showing 00:00:00, which in a static screenshot is expected — but to any recipient who opens this on a platform that doesn't execute JavaScript, that timer looks expired before they've even read the offer. Contrast this with the June 14 "Ends TOMORROW" send (8/10), where the calendar callout felt credible precisely because it didn't rely on a live timer. Same campaign, worse execution here.
- The kitchen island lifestyle section is pure aspirational padding — no price, no offer, no urgency. It exists to fill space. The "Register now" block for "Build it, spin it!" asks for sign-up commitment mid-purchase funnel. The "Deals of the Day / Sign up" module at the footer is re-subscribing people who are already subscribed. All of this would be fine in separate sends; stacked together, the email reads like the content team ran out of a cohesive angle and padded with whatever was available.

## What's working

- **Subject line delivers immediately.** "45%" is visible in the hero within the first scroll — the promise from the subject line is kept.
- **Multiple discount depths.** 45% off appliances and 40% off indoor products give different buyer intents somewhere to land.
- **"10 Days" navigation bar.** The top nav with category shortcuts (TOOLS, DAILY DEALS, APPLIANCES) is a smart structural choice that lets skimmers self-direct.
- **Clean layout.** No broken images, no text overlap, no lorem ipsum — the render itself is technically clean.

## What's weak

- **Countdown timer reads as expired.** The 00:00:00 display in the hero undercuts urgency rather than building it; the June 14 calendar block approach worked better.
- **Six competing sections fracture the appliance hook.** After the hero, the email lurches into indoor, kitchen, gift cards, a spin-to-win, and a newsletter CTA — the appliance message from the subject line evaporates.
- **"Build it, spin it!" register block is funnel-hostile.** Asking for a registration mid-email pulls a shopper out of purchase intent into a different commitment. The $14 price and "Register now" copy suggest a workshop or prize entry — neither is the appliance deal you came for.
- **Kitchen island section has no offer.** Aspirational lifestyle copy with no price, no discount, no deadline — dead weight in a promotional email.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the sixth-plus send in eight days. The free spatula email on June 13 (9/10) set a high bar with a singular, loyalty-anchored hook that this email can't match.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip it to one hero, one offer.** If the appliance sale is the lead, own it. Pull the kitchen island, gift cards, and spin blocks into their own sends. One email, one job.
- 2. **Replace the live countdown with a hard-date callout.** "Offer ends Sunday, June 20" in bold text beats a JavaScript timer that renders at zero for half your recipients.
- 3. **Make "Build it, spin it!" its own email** — that mechanic is interesting enough (sweepstakes + $14 entry price point) to anchor a standalone send, but it's noise here.
- 4. **Add a specific product hero.** Pick one appliance — a washer/dryer set, a refrigerator, a dishwasher — show the struck-through price and the sale price. "Up to 45%" is a ceiling; a specific product at $399 (was $699) is a conversion.
- 5. **Kill the Deals of the Day signup module.** You're already in their inbox. Re-subscribing them mid-email is a trust-eroding non sequitur.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed):

| Image | URL (abbreviated) |
|---|---|
| Hero/content `.jpg` | `image.e.lowes.com/…/c301e24e.jpg` |
| Content `.png` | `image.e.lowes.com/…/ad569dc5.png` |
| Open pixel `o.gif` (×3) | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/…` |
| Impression pixel `imp` (×5) | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=…` |

Tracking pixels and beacons should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to satisfy screen reader and accessibility linting requirements. Content images need descriptive alt text.

**Duplicate `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional blocks.** The `@font-face` declarations are split across two separate MSO conditionals — one for Fellix variants, one for DIN Next. These could be consolidated into a single block without functional impact.

**Responsive breakpoint overlap.** Two media queries target overlapping ranges:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)` — WebKit/Blink only
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` — all agents

The first query's `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` qualifier excludes Firefox and Samsung Internet from the 375–480px mobile styles, potentially causing layout inconsistencies on those clients.

**`.mobilehide` color hack.** CSS rule `a.mobileshow, .mobilehide { display: none; color: #fff; }` uses `color: #fff` as a Gmail visibility workaround. Functional, but worth confirming against current Gmail rendering behavior.
