# Up to 50% OFF select appliances + FREE delivery 🥳
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a long, crowded Lowe's send with a strong appliance offer that gets buried. The subject promises "Up to 50% OFF select appliances + FREE delivery." The ALL-IN Appliance Offer block delivers it: a big "50% off" over a fridge, washer and range, with a "FREE Delivery" badge. But the first thing you see is a red "Black Friday: Get to the BIG savings ahead of schedule" banner with no number in it. The appliance offer sits second, so the email opens with its weakest hero.
- The rest dilutes it. Below the appliances there is a Klarna banner, then a bathroom block with an "ENDS TODAY" marquee, then vanities, lighting, a water heater, KitchenAid financing, DEWALT and toy picks, and a Deals of the Day signup. That is about nine competing messages. The fine print runs to a wall of text, and the ALL-IN offer needs a $896 spend, so "free delivery" has conditions. This is a decent appliance offer in a catalog-style layout. It is relevant to someone shopping for appliances now, and it is weak for anyone else.
- Against the brand history, this is the third Black Friday-adjacent send in about a week (10/29 "The wait ends…", 11/2 pre-lit trees, 11/3 Traeger). The creative is the same loud red-and-blue template each time. The bathroom "ENDS TODAY" block also competes with the appliance subject line.

## What's working

- The "Get up to 50% off select major appliances" block has a big number and a clear product photo of a fridge, washer and range.
- The subject line's offer (50% off, free delivery) matches the first real offer block.
- The "FREE Delivery" green badge and the "FREE same-day delivery" strip under the nav are easy to read.
- Each block has its own "Shop now" button, so the click path is clear.
- The Lowe's logo and the myLowe's Rewards bar make the sender easy to recognise.

## What's weak

- The first hero, "Black Friday… Get to the BIG savings," has no number, so the best offer starts below it.
- Too many offers compete: appliances, Klarna, bathroom, vanities, lighting, water heater, KitchenAid financing, DEWALT, toys and Deals of the Day.
- The bathroom block's "ENDS TODAY" banner carries an urgency the appliance offer lacks. The scrolling "ENDS TODAY" text also looks cropped.
- The conditions are small: "when you spend $896 or more" and "Save up to an additional $250" are tucked into the block, and a long legal wall closes the email.
- The "Curated with you in mind" row (DEWALT batteries, squishy toy, router) has no link to appliances or the bathroom theme and looks random.
- The sponsored Klarna banner interrupts the flow between the two main offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the appliance block the hero and drop the generic Black Friday banner. Lead with "Up to 50% off appliances + free delivery" and show the $896 threshold next to it.
- 2. Cut the email to three modules: appliances, bathroom and one financing offer. Remove the Curated row and the water-heater tile.
- 3. Move the Klarna ad below the main offers, or remove it from this send.
- 4. Pick one urgency message. Either the appliance deal has a deadline or the bathroom block does, but not both.
- 5. Put the free-delivery terms in one visible line, for example "Free delivery on orders $896+."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Appliances up to 50% off, delivery's free`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fridge or washer? Up to 50% off + free delivery`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $896+ on major appliances and delivery is on us. Offer ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus an extra $250 off select laundry. See the full list inside.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source and QA data show no hard failures. The pass rate is 57%, with 0 errors and 3 warnings. Several checks couldn't be completed: the HTML was truncated, the 40 tracking links were not probed, and the relay stripped the authentication headers. The confirmed issues are a malformed DOCTYPE, missing alt attributes on 17 images, and the recipient's address passed in cleartext in pixel URLs.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 40 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing, so none of their destinations or status codes were validated.
- **Cleartext address in pixel URLs:** All 15 `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?...` impression pixels carry `e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app`. For example: `imp?s=127768300&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=529272&lctg=410146703&stpe=pixel`.
- **Empty `li_coord=` parameter:** It is empty on the `stpe=default` pixels `s=2019286` and `s=1090834`. Either a value that should have been populated wasn't, or the parameter is vestigial.
- **Duplicate pixels:** `s=2019286` and `s=1090834` each appear twice with identical parameters. This risks double-counted impressions.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed DOCTYPE:** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space after `PUBLIC`. Strict parsers may fall back to quirks mode.
- **Non-standard namespace:** `xmlns="https://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` uses `https`. The canonical namespace URI is `http://`. This is harmless in practice but incorrect.
- **Missing alt on content images (2):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`
  
  If either carries content, such as an offer or a CTA, it is invisible to screen readers and to clients that block images.
- **Missing alt on tracking pixels (15):** The `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels have no `alt`. Decorative or tracking images should use `alt=""` explicitly so screen readers skip them.
- **Web fonts:** Fellix and DIN Next are loaded from `lowescdn.com` inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` blocks. This is a correct, guarded pattern and I found no issue with it.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens in the visible source.** The empty `li_coord=` values are the only possible leftovers, and I can't confirm they are tokens.
- **Cleartext address (see §2):** Putting the recipient's raw email address in third-party pixel query strings exposes PII in URLs. These URLs end up in proxy logs, CDN logs, and the ad server's logs.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found (WARN):** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture it. This is unconfirmed, not a verified defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found (WARN):** One-click unsubscribe is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect it.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (WARN):** The relay probably stripped it, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for `e.lowes.com` are unknown.
- **Footer not assessed:** The visible-body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address sit in the truncated portion of the HTML, so I can't confirm either.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No issues found in the visible source, but this can't be assessed. The destination URLs fall in the truncated section or behind the 40 un-probed tracking redirects. UTM parameters and landing-page alignment with the "Up to 50% OFF select appliances + FREE delivery" offer are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the DOCTYPE to `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` and use `http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml` for the namespace.
2. Add meaningful `alt` text to `c301e24e-...jpg` and `ad569dc5-...png`. Add `alt=""` to all `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels.
3. Stop sending the raw email address in `e=` on impression pixels. Use a hashed or opaque subscriber ID instead.
4. Check the empty `li_coord=` parameter, and remove the duplicate pixels for `s=2019286` and `s=1090834`.
5. Re-run the audit against raw `.eml` source or a direct-to-inbox copy. That confirms `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results`, and gives full HTML for the footer and UTM checks.
6. Resolve the 40 redirect links with a redirect-following probe, such as HEAD requests that follow redirects to the final landing URL. That covers destinations and UTM carry-through.
