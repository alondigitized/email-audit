# Up to 45% OFF + FREE delivery on appliances 🚨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's leading with the appliance hero again — same play as 9/10's "Final days: Labor Day appliance deals. Save up to $1,500," just re-skinned with a 45%-off framing and a fine-print asterisk instead of a hard dollar number. The "ALL-IN Appliance Offer" block is unblurred, legible, and stacks the discount, free delivery, free install/haul-away, and a $100 laundry add-on with no scroll needed — that's a genuinely strong hero.
- Where it falls apart is honesty and cadence. "Up to 45% OFF" next to a wall of asterisks and a footer disclosure that discounts vary "equal to actual basic install fee" reads like the offer is doing more work in copy than in reality. And running a near-identical "appliances end tonight" LAST CHANCE banner for the second time in a week (9/10 and now 9/16) is going to numb a subscriber who's seen this exact beat before.
- Solid execution, recycled promise, offer language that needs a subscriber to do a lot of squinting.

## What's working

- Hero is unmissable: bold "45% off" headline, product photo cluster (washer, fridge, range) all rendered cleanly.
- Free Delivery / Free Install / Free Haul-away are called out as separate green tags — clear stacked value.
- "Shop now" CTA button is high-contrast navy-on-white and sits right next to the offer.
- Below-fold product grid (microwave, dishwasher, AC unit) gives concrete SKUs with individual "Buy now" buttons, not just a generic banner.
- "LAST CHANCE" repeating ticker at top reinforces urgency without needing to scroll.

## What's weak

- "Up to 45% off" is vague compared to 9/10's clean "$1,500" — this reads like a step back in specificity for the same appliance category.
- Fine print under the hero ("Discount will be applied evenly across all items purchased... discount varies & equal to actual basic install fee") signals the "45%" isn't really 45% off the product — that's the exact "buried fine print" pattern the rubric flags.
- Second near-identical Labor-Day-appliance "ends tonight" send in six days (9/10 → 9/16) with no new offer structure — this is reheated urgency, not new information.
- The credit financing block and Deals-of-the-Day banner beneath it dilute the single appliance CTA into a kitchen-sink email, same critique as the 9/13 Sunday Deals send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace "Up to 45% off" with a concrete number tied to a real product (e.g., "Save $400 on this fridge") — vague "up to" plus asterisks is the single biggest trust-and-clarity leak here.
- 2. Cut the credit-financing and Deals-of-the-Day blocks from this send entirely; let the appliance offer own the whole email like 9/10 did.
- 3. If this really is "the deals end tonight" for the second time in a week, say what's different this time (lower price, new SKU, last inventory) — otherwise skip the urgency framing and it'll read as recycled.
- 4. Move the free-delivery/install/haul-away stack above or beside the percentage, since that's the more concrete, differentiated value versus the vague "up to 45%."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Appliances: up to $500 off + free delivery, tonight only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fridge, washer, range — up to 45% off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free delivery, install, and haul-away on qualifying purchases`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $999+ and get a 2-year protection plan free`
