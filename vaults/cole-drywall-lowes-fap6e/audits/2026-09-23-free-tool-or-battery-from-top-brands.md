# FREE tool or battery from top brands🎁
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's actual best structural play in the recent run: a real BOGO-style offer ("FREE select item when you buy a select tool or battery") stacked with a hard number ("Save up to $250 on select outdoor power equipment") and a second hard number ("Save up to $100 on select EGO outdoor power equipment"), all in one send. Compare to 9/21's "35% off LG appliances" (7/10) — this has the same virtue of a real number tied to a real category, but goes further by layering three distinct offers (free item, $250 off, $100 off EGO) instead of just one.
- The layout is dense but legible: hero banner, then a trending-products carousel with three named SKUs and "Buy now" buttons, then the $250 mower banner, then a rewards/fall-cleanup module, then the EGO sub-brand push. Nothing looks broken — clean crops, readable text, consistent navy/lime color language throughout.
- The weak spot is the subject/preview pairing undersells what's actually a strong, offer-dense email — "FREE tool or battery" reads like a giveaway gimmick, not the multi-tiered savings event the body actually delivers.

## What's working

- Hero banner leads with a concrete, legible offer: "FREE select item when you buy a select tool or battery" with a clear "Shop now" CTA.
- Three named, recognizable SKUs (DEWALT 20V MAX, DEWALT XR, Craftsman V20) each get their own "Buy now" button — high specificity for a DIY/tools persona.
- Second hero banner adds a real number ("Save up to $250") over a clean mower product shot.
- Rewards module ("3x Points," "Activate now") gives loyalty members a visible, distinct reason to engage beyond the core offer.
- No render bugs — text, buttons, and product photography are all crisp and non-overlapping.

## What's weak

- Subject undersells the email — "FREE tool or battery" implies a single freebie gimmick, not the $250 + $100 + free-item stack actually inside.
- The EGO block is labeled "Sponsored," which slightly undercuts trust in that specific offer as a Lowe's-endorsed deal.
- Heavy vertical scroll before reaching the fall-cleanup/rewards content — a mobile reader has to scroll past four screens to get there.
- The top preheader text ("Save up to $250 on select power equipment. Plus, gear up for fall projects.") is more compelling than the actual subject line, which is backwards.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the subject for something that surfaces the dollar figure, since $250 is a stronger hook than "free tool or battery" and matches what the preview text already promises.
- 2. Move the $250 mower banner up to directly follow the hero, ahead of the three-SKU carousel, so the two strongest offers aren't separated by a lower-intensity module.
- 3. Add a countdown or explicit end-date to the hero offer to match the EGO block's visible "Offer ends 10/28/26" — right now only one of three offers has urgency.
- 4. Consider dropping or relabeling "Sponsored" on the EGO block to something like "EGO Days" without the ad disclosure competing visually with the lime "Save up to $100" callout.
