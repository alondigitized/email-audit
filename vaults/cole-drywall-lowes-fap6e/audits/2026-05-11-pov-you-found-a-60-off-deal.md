# POV: You found a 60% OFF deal👀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, grid-style "Deals of the Day" batch email from Lowe's — six product tiles with hard-number discounts, a bold red hero, and a consistent "Shop now" arrow treatment. The execution is competent and render-clean, but the content is a spread-bet on whoever opens: one person might care about the leaf blower, another about the impact driver, nobody about all six simultaneously. That breadth is its main liability.
- The subject leans hard into Gen-Z framing ("POV: You found a 60% OFF deal👀") which is an interesting swing for a home improvement brand. It works as a pattern-interrupt in a Lowe's-heavy inbox, but it doesn't tell you *what* the deal is — making the 60% hook feel slightly bait-y when the in-email hero doesn't lead with the same figure.
- Overall: a reliable utility send, not a great targeted send. Gets the job done for someone already shopping Lowe's; does little for anyone who isn't.

## What's working

- **Hard-number discounts are everywhere** — "Save $120," "60% Off," "Save $100," "FREE" are all visible at a glance. No vagueness.
- **Visual hierarchy is solid** — The red "Deals of the Day" hero immediately commands the eye, product tiles flow naturally below.
- **Product names are specific** — CRAFTSMAN® V20™, Husqvarna, Paslode. These aren't generic; they signal real SKUs.
- **Clean render** — No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. The six-tile grid is tight.
- **"FREE Bosch Battery Kit With Purchase" tile** — smart inclusion; adds an aspirational value anchor to an otherwise discount-heavy grid.

## What's weak

- **Hero doesn't mirror the subject's 60% claim** — The subject says "60% OFF deal" but the hero just says "Deals of the Day." The 60% tile (CRAFTSMAN Impact Driver) is buried as one of six equal tiles.
- **"Shop now" is doing all the heavy lifting** — Every CTA across the email uses the same flat verb phrase. No tile says "Get the Impact Driver" or "Grab the leaf blower deal."
- **Zero social proof** — Not a single review count, rating, or bestseller badge anywhere in the visible render.
- **Category spread undercuts relevance** — A bathroom vanity, a leaf blower, a power tool, siding nails, and a free battery kit are all on the same card. A non-DIY persona has no obvious entry point.
- **No loyalty hook** — No MyLowe's member pricing or points reminder visible, which is a miss for members.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 60% hero the actual hero** — If the subject is promising 60% off, the Impact Driver should be the full-width hero image with a "Shop the deal" button, not tile #3 of six.
- 2. **Add one social proof signal per tile** — Even a "⭐ 4.7 (2,841 reviews)" line under each product would materially lift click confidence.
- 3. **Differentiate CTA copy per tile** — "Shop the Driver →", "Shop the Blower →" costs nothing and reduces friction.
- 4. **Add a countdown or explicit expiration** — "Ends tonight" or "Today only" next to "Deals of the Day" would convert the implied urgency into felt urgency.
- 5. **Surface MyLowe's pricing** — If there's a member-exclusive stack, show it — it rewards loyalty and creates FOMO for non-members.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off this CRAFTSMAN® Impact Driver — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your deal alert: 60% off + free Bosch battery kit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: save $120 on a Husqvarna leaf blower and more. Ends today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six deals. One day. The impact driver is the one you want.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based skeleton with functional mobile media queries, but three compliance headers are undetectable via the AgentMail relay, six images are missing `alt` attributes, and 24 click-tracking links cannot be verified for destination accuracy or UTM coverage.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**24 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** All outbound links are wrapped through a click-redirect domain (likely `e.lowes.com` or a third-party ESP redirect). This prevents automated verification of:
- Whether destination URLs are live and returning 200
- Whether UTM parameters are present and correctly formed
- Whether any links resolve to 404 or expired landing pages

No broken links were *confirmed*, but none could be *cleared* either. Manual spot-check of at least the primary CTA links is required before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom web fonts declared but will not render in most clients.**
Four `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` files from `lowescdn.com` (Fellix Regular/Medium/SemiBold/Bold, DIN Next/Bold/Black). These are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`  guards, which is correct Outlook exclusion, but Gmail (web and Android), most Outlook versions, and several Samsung clients strip or ignore `@font-face` entirely. No fallback font stack is visible in the truncated source — if inline styles or table cells rely solely on `font-family: 'Fellix Regular'` without a web-safe fallback, those elements will render in the browser/client default (typically Times New Roman or Arial depending on client), breaking typographic hierarchy.

**Six images are missing `alt` attributes (QA confirmed):**

| File | URL | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-…jpg` | `https://image.e.lowes.com/…/m/59/c301e24e-…jpg` | No alt — likely hero or product image; blind users get nothing |
| `o.gif` | `https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/20af9ac4a7b6f5a4/o.gif?mi_u=410146703` | Tracking pixel — needs `alt=""` (empty, not absent) |
| `spacer_20140709.gif` (×3) | `https://image.e.lowes.com/…/m/37/spacer_20140709.gif` | Spacer GIFs — need `alt=""` not omitted `alt`; also, filename dates to 2014 |
| `ad569dc5-…png` | `https://image.e.lowes.com/…/m/71/ad569dc5-…png` | No alt — likely promotional product image |

The tracking pixel (`o.gif`) and spacers must have `alt=""` (explicitly empty), not a missing attribute — some screen readers will read the filename aloud if `alt` is absent entirely. Content images need descriptive `alt` text.

**`spacer_20140709.gif` asset age.** The filename suffix indicates this asset has been in production since at least 2014. No functional issue confirmed, but it is a staleness flag worth noting to the template team.

**`.mobilehide { color: #fff }` Gmail hack.** This is intentional (forces Gmail to not hyperlink hidden text) — not a bug, but worth documenting as intentional technical debt.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected (CAN-SPAM / Gmail Sender Guidelines).**
Google's February 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate a `List-Unsubscribe` header for senders above 5,000/day. Lowe's volume almost certainly exceeds this threshold. The QA system could not confirm the header's presence — it may be stripped by the AgentMail relay or genuinely absent. Either way, this must be verified directly against raw message headers from a received copy.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders) is unconfirmed. If missing, Gmail will surface a "Report spam" prompt instead of a clean unsubscribe flow, which directly harms sender reputation.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication unconfirmed.**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. The sending domain `e.lowes.com` is an ESP subdomain — DKIM alignment should be verified at the raw header level. A failed or missing DKIM signature combined with absent `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is a deliverability double-hit for Gmail.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from truncated source. Must be confirmed present in the footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 24 outbound links route through a click-redirect (ESP tracking) layer, making UTM parameter inspection impossible without manual testing. Risks:

- UTM parameters may be appended at the redirect layer rather than in the href — if the redirect chain breaks or strips query strings, analytics attribution is lost
- "60% OFF" offer framing in subject line must resolve to a landing page that surfaces this discount prominently; cannot be confirmed without clicking through

No UTM parameters are visible in the raw `href` values in the HTML (they would normally appear as `?utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...` before the redirect wraps them). If UTMs are only applied at the redirect layer, any tracking outage silently drops attribution with no fallback.
