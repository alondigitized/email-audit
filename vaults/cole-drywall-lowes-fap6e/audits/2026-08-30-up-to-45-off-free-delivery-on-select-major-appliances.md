# Up to 45% OFF + FREE delivery on select major appliances 😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "Up to 45% OFF + FREE delivery on select major appliances," but the hero doesn't deliver that — it's a generic "Sunday Deals" leaf-and-blue banner with a vague "Big savings for your fall projects" line, and the actual appliance offer doesn't appear until the second module. That's a real mismatch between subject and lead visual, the same structural habit this brand keeps repeating (see 8/27's nine-module cascade).
- Once you get to the second block, the appliance offer itself is genuinely strong: "Get up to 45% off select appliances" plus "FREE Delivery" and a clean shot of a matching stainless suite (fridge, range, microwave, dishwasher). That's the best single module in the email. But then it immediately sprawls into EGO power equipment, Halloween animatronics, mulch, seasonal picks, and a "curated" grid of a storage bin, cleaner, and blinds — none of which have anything to do with appliances or Sunday Deals.
- Net: the one email that should have been a tight appliance-focused send (mirroring 8/29's clean lead) buries its own headline offer under a Sunday Deals banner and drowns it in six unrelated modules right after. Judgment: middling execution of a genuinely good offer.

## What's working

- The 45% off + FREE Delivery appliance module is clear, with a real product shot of a matching stainless kitchen suite.
- Perk icons (free base install, free haul away, 2-year protection plan) are visible and legible under the appliance module.
- No render bugs — no overlapping text, broken images, or lorem ipsum anywhere in the scroll.
- "Curated with you in mind" grid uses real, distinct product images with visible prices context and clear "Buy now" CTAs.

## What's weak

- Hero banner doesn't match the subject line at all — "Sunday Deals" with leaf iconography leads, not the 45%-off appliance offer that got the subject line and presumably drove the open.
- The email sprawls into six-plus unrelated categories (power equipment, Halloween decor, mulch, seasonal picks, storage/cleaning/blinds) after the appliance module, diluting the one offer worth clicking.
- Halloween animatronics module clashes tonally with a Labor Day/fall appliance sale — confusing seasonal signal-mixing in one send.
- "$999 or more" spend threshold for free delivery is only shown in small text under the appliance module, not surfaced anywhere near the top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the "Sunday Deals" hero with the appliance offer itself — lead with "Up to 45% off + FREE Delivery" and the stainless suite photo, since that's what earned the open. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Cut the email after the appliance + EGO/Halloween module; move mulch, seasonal picks, and the curated grid to a separate send — this brand's cleanest-scoring email (8/29, 7.00/10) worked specifically because it stayed narrow.
- 3. Move the "$999 or more" spend threshold up next to the "FREE Delivery" badge so it's not buried in fine print below the fold of that module.
- 4. Drop or reschedule the Halloween animatronics block — it undercuts the fall/appliance framing the subject line set up.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 45% off appliances — free delivery ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your stainless kitchen suite is up to 45% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free install, haul-away, and a 2-year protection plan.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $999+ on major appliances and delivery's on us.`

# Technical Audit — Lowe's "Up to 45% OFF Appliances" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Lowe's ESP (e.lowes.com / Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style) template with heavy legacy MSO/Outlook conditional CSS; automated QA passed 57% with 3 warning categories and no hard errors — issues cluster around missing accessibility attributes and unverifiable auth/compliance headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 34 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (domains not resolvable/probeable directly) — link destinations could not be validated for 200 status or redirect integrity. Recommend manual click-through QA on a sample of these before send.
- Multiple 1x1 tracking pixels present via `sli.lowes.com/imp` (e.g., `?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=8302026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static`) — note the recipient email address is embedded in plaintext in the pixel URL query string (`e=` param), which is a PII-in-URL exposure pattern worth flagging even though it's standard for this ESP's tracking implementation.
- No broken/malformed link syntax found in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 6 images missing `alt` text, confirmed by QA:
  - `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` (image.e.lowes.com)
  - `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` (image.e.lowes.com)
  - 4× `sli.lowes.com/imp` tracking pixel images (functionally decorative, but missing `alt=""` rather than a null/empty attribute means some screen readers may announce the raw URL or filename)
- Template uses extensive legacy support scaffolding: `mso` conditional comments, `ExternalClass` fixes for Hotmail/Outlook.com, `x-apple-data-detectors` overrides, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` — indicates broad multi-client compatibility targeting (Outlook desktop, Gmail, Yahoo, Apple Mail).
- Responsive breakpoints defined at both 375–600px and ≤480px via media queries (`.mobile-hidden`, `.mobile-only`, `.container`, `.photo`) — structurally sound responsive approach, but rendering was not visually verified (source-only review).
- Custom web fonts (Fellix, DIN Next family) loaded via `@font-face` from `lowescdn.com` with no fallback stack shown in the truncated excerpt — verify a system-font fallback exists in body text styles (not visible in truncated source).
