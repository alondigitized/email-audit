# Save up to $250 on select outdoor power equipment
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's finally delivering exactly what the subject promises: "Save up to $250 select outdoor power equipment and accessories" sits over a clean lawn-mower hero, unblurred, no scroll needed, with a legible "Shop now" CTA. It's a genuine improvement over the muddled kitchen-sink grids from 9/13 and the recycled Labor Day heroes running 8/31–9/7 — this one has a single clear focal offer up top before the kitchen sink kicks in below.
- But once you scroll past the hero, this reverts to the same sprawling format as 9/13: free-item promo, garden soil, patio furniture, Kobalt toolboxes, leaf blowers, a "Curated with you in mind" grid, a sponsored banner, and a Deals-of-the-Day cross-promo — nine distinct offers in one email. The hero is strong; everything after it is undifferentiated volume.
- Verdict: best hero-to-subject match of the week, but the email still overstays its welcome after the fold.

## What's working

- Hero image and headline directly match the subject line — "Save up to $250 Select outdoor power equipment and accessories" is the first thing visible, unblurred.
- "Shop now" CTA button is clearly visible and high-contrast against the photo.
- Product photography (mower, leaf blower, patio scene) is sharp with no render bugs or overlapping text.
- Section breaks (blue banner bars: "Fresh blooms," "Curated with you in mind," "A little love for your patio") give some visual rhythm to a long email.

## What's weak

- Nine separate promos crammed into one send dilutes the strong outdoor-power-equipment hook the subject sets up.
- The 50%-off patio banner competes directly with the hero offer instead of reinforcing it — two different "our best deal" claims in one email.
- "Sponsored" Kobalt Days banner near the bottom blurs paid placement into the regular promo flow without clear separation.
- Deals-of-the-Day sign-up block at the very bottom is a house-list ask tacked onto a promotional email, adding more friction before the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the outdoor power equipment / fall-cleanup theme (mower hero, leaf blower/rake section, Kobalt outdoor tools) and drop patio, garden soil, and toolbox tiles into a separate send — a focused email converts better than a nine-offer scroll.
- 2. Move the 50%-off patio banner out entirely or subordinate it visually so it doesn't compete with the $250 hero claim.
- 3. Cut the Deals-of-the-Day sign-up ask — it's misplaced friction in a promotional email that already has a clear primary CTA.
