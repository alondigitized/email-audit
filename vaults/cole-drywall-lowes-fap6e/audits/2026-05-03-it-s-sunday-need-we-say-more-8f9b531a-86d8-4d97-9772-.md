# It’s Sunday ... Need we say more? 😉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's "Sunday Deals" email is a kitchen-sink weekly blast — five distinct campaigns crammed into one scroll: a pressure washer deal, spring clearance, Mother's Day flowers, Mother's Day gift cards, and "Halfway to Halloween." The brand execution is clean and recognizable, the product-level pricing is concrete, and the member gift benefit adds value. But the lack of editorial focus dilutes everything. A shopper who came for a pressure washer has to wade past flower bouquets and Halloween mascots to find it again. The subject line leans on personality over information, which works if you already love Lowe's Sunday Deals as a ritual — but is opaque to anyone else.

## What's working

- **Price-anchored hero product.** The Greenworks 2200-PSI pressure washer at $199 with a "Limited Time" badge is a solid, specific deal. PSI rating is a real product spec that earns trust.
- **Member perk is surfaced.** "FREE Member gift" with an end date (May 5) gives loyalty members a reason to act now.
- **Clean mobile-first layout.** Single-column, stacked modules, no cluttered sidebars. Renders without friction.
- **Sunday ritual framing.** "Yeah, it's that time again!" builds on a habitual open cadence — subscribers who expect Sunday deals will recognize the signal.
- **Mother's Day is timely.** Two Mother's Day modules land appropriately in early May.

## What's weak

- **Five campaigns = zero campaigns.** Pressure washer → spring clearance → flowers → gift cards → Halloween. Each module competes with the next. There's no editorial thread connecting them.
- **"Halfway to Halloween" is deeply dissonant.** Placed immediately after two Mother's Day modules in early May, the Halloween mascots read as a layout accident rather than an intentional campaign. Even if it's a known Lowe's gimmick, it fractures the mood of the send.
- **Subject line is content-free.** "It's Sunday... Need we say more? 😉" works only for Pavlovian openers who associate Sunday with Lowe's deals. First-time or lapsed subscribers get zero information.
- **No price comparisons or percentage-off framing.** "$199" for a pressure washer is meaningful — but without a "was $249" anchor, it doesn't feel like a deal until you go look it up.
- **"Spring Into Deals, are almost out."** Grammatically broken. Should read "are almost over" or "deals almost gone."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one editorial anchor per send.** Sunday Deals should lead with the sharpest deal and stick to a coherent theme — DIY spring prep, Mother's Day gifting, or Halloween prep. Not all three at once.
- 2. **Give the hero product a before/after price.** "$199 (reg. $249)" doubles the perceived value of the same deal with no additional effort.
- 3. **Move Halloween to its own dedicated send** — or at least a standalone teaser, not sandwiched between flower bouquets.
- 4. **Fix the grammar in "Spring Into Deals, are almost out."**
- 5. **Subject test: replace the wink with a product hook.** "Sunday only: $199 pressure washer + free member gift" would outperform the rhetorical tease for any subscriber outside the habit loop.

**Missing `alt` text — 2 content images:**

**Missing `alt=""` on tracking pixels (6 images):**
- `o.gif` at `mi.lowes.com` and 5 `imp` pixels at `sli.lowes.com` lack even empty alt attributes. Best practice for decorative/tracking images is `alt=""` to suppress screen reader announcements.

**Custom font fallback gap:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: 'Fellix Regular';
  src: local('Fellix Regular'), url('https://www.lowescdn.com/sffonts/Fellix-Regular.woff') format('woff');
}
```
The `local()` references are non-standard names unlikely to resolve on any user device. Gmail strips all `@font-face` blocks entirely. No system-font fallback stack (e.g., `Arial, sans-serif`) is declared alongside these custom fonts, meaning Gmail and Android users render with the browser default rather than a controlled fallback.

**WOFF only, no WOFF2:** Font URLs reference `.woff` exclusively. WOFF2 offers ~30% better compression; supported by all clients that support `@font-face` in email (iOS Mail, Apple Mail, Samsung Mail).
