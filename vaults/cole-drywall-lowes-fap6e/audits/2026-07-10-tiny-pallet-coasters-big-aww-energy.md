# Tiny pallet coasters. Big aww energy ☺️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's doing a deliberate lifestyle pivot on Day 3 of Money Days (July 9–12) — exactly the move they pulled on July 7 with the new summer candle. The hero is clean and well-composed: a latte-art coffee cup sitting on a tiny wooden pallet coaster, styled like a lifestyle flat-lay you'd find on a home-goods Instagram account. The "just $6" price is the whole argument, and it makes a strong one.
- The problem is strategic context, not execution. A subscriber who just got two Money Days emails is now staring at a $6 novelty coaster email with no connection to the event they were told runs through July 12. No bridging copy, no "grab this while you shop Money Days," nothing. The soft-sell approach worked on July 7 because it broke nine consecutive deal emails — here it just feels like a scheduling miss.
- There is no visible CTA button anywhere in the body. The entire conversion path depends on the subscriber intuiting that the hero image is clickable. For a $6 impulse product that lives and dies by frictionless conversion, that's a real gap.

## What's working

- **$6 price is front and center** — the offer is unmissable and hard to argue with.
- **Hero photo earns its keep** — the coffee-and-pallet composition is genuinely charming; it sells the lifestyle before the product.
- **"Member Only Access" badge adds exclusivity** — visible in the header, reinforces the loyalty mechanic without being loud about it.
- **Subject line is distinctive** — "Big aww energy" is unusual enough in a Lowe's inbox to stop the thumb.
- **Zero render bugs** — clean, crisp, no broken images or layout weirdness.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button** — there is no visible action element in the body; the whole email relies on a hero-image click that most subscribers won't assume is there.
- **Money Days is completely absent** — Day 3 of a four-day event and not a single mention; this breaks the narrative for engaged members who read the last two emails.
- **Price is buried in body copy** — "$6" appears in a small caption line, not a visual callout; a price badge on the image would have done more work.
- **No urgency** — $6 impulse items need a stock or time nudge; this email gives none.
- **Subject/body dissonance** — the subject promises "aww energy" and delivers a clean product photo, but the body copy ("The cutest spot to park your coffee") doesn't escalate the charm — it's softer than the subject set up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a CTA button.** Even a "Shop Now — $6" button below the offer line would materially lift click-through. This is the single highest-ROI change.
- 2. **Tie it to Money Days.** One line of copy like "While you're shopping Money Days, grab these for your coffee station" keeps the event narrative alive and adds cross-sell momentum.
- 3. **Make the $6 a visual badge on the hero image.** Overlay the price in a contrasting pill — subscribers should see the offer before they read anything.
- 4. **Add a light stock or time nudge.** "These go fast at $6" or "Available through July 12" gives an honest reason to click now.

## Technical Audit — "Tiny pallet coasters. Big aww energy ☺️" (MyLowe's Rewards)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound XHTML 1.0 Transitional with standard multi-client CSS resets, but carries three compliance gaps (missing physical address, absent List-Unsubscribe headers) and a deliverability risk from a URL-dense plain-text part. SPF/DKIM authentication status is unverifiable via the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**QA flagged 29 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `e.lowes.com` click-redirect domain. HTTP probing was not performed, so broken-destination links cannot be ruled out.

**Plain-text part is 77% URLs** (3,039 / 3,945 chars). Spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY`) penalize plain-text parts that are predominantly redirect URLs rather than readable content. This is a deliverability risk at volume.

No issues found with the `lowescdn.com` static asset URLs visible in the source (fonts load over HTTPS).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text** on one image:
```
src: https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/1/a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg
```
All other images need verification (source is truncated), but this one is confirmed missing `alt`.

**Inconsistent CDN versioning across `@font-face` declarations:**
- `DIN Next` (regular) references `scaffold/1.4.4`
- `DIN Next Bold` and `DIN Next Black` reference `scaffold/1.2.0`

If `1.2.0` paths are removed from the CDN, bold/black weight fallback will silently degrade to system fonts in clients that support `@font-face`.

**Overlapping mobile breakpoints:**
- One query targets `min-width: 375px AND max-width: 600px`
- Another targets `max-width: 480px`

The 375–480px range has two conflicting rule sets. In practice, the later `max-width: 480px` block wins for most properties, but specificity conflicts here could cause layout inconsistencies on mid-range Android devices.

**`@font-face` blocks correctly gated** behind `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` — Outlook rendering is not affected.
