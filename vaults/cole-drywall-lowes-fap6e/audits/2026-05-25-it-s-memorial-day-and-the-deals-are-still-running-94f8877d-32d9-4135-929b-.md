# It's Memorial Day, and the deals are still running😎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's ninth consecutive Memorial Day email — arriving on the holiday itself, May 25 — and it does exactly what a holiday-close send should do structurally: countdown timer at the top, FREE item hooks in the middle, loyalty multiplier for members, and a percentage discount grid below. Compare to yesterday's "Sunday Deals + Memorial Day deals" send (scored 5/10 for creative exhaustion), and this one is measurably tighter. The hero is cleaner, the FREE items section is the right emotional anchor, and the 5x rewards module gives loyalty members a concrete same-day reason to act.
- The critical flaw is visible immediately: the hero body copy reads "The deals are still here, along the rest" — an incomplete phrase that reads as a truncated or broken line. That's the first sentence a recipient processes after the headline, and it signals carelessness on a high-traffic send day. Below the fold, there are also too many competing modules (FREE offers, rewards multiplier, three separate discount tiers, Deals of the Day) pulling attention in different directions when a tighter funnel would close harder.
- Judgment: the countdown mechanic and FREE section are the right tools for this moment in the cadence, but the broken hero copy and module sprawl prevent this from being the clean holiday closer it should have been.

## What's working

- The countdown timer at the top of the email creates genuine, visible urgency on the actual holiday — this is the right mechanic for day nine.
- "Love FREE? We do too." with three distinct free tiers (BONUS / REBATE / COUPON) is the strongest conversion hook in the email; free-item anchors outperform percentage-off for click behavior.
- My Lowe's Rewards 5x multiplier gives loyalty members a specific, time-gated reason to act today over tomorrow.
- "The savings aren't over yet..." framing correctly positions the email as a last-chance close without being panicked or desperate.
- Clean render overall — no broken images, layout collapse, or overlapping elements visible.

## What's weak

- Hero body copy is visibly broken: "The deals are still here, along the rest" is not a grammatically complete sentence — "along the rest" has no clear referent.
- Four competing module types (FREE, rewards, percentage discounts, Deals of the Day) above the scroll line dilute focus; there's no single offer the eye commits to.
- "Shop now" CTA is maximally generic — by send nine of the same campaign, something deadline-specific would pull meaningfully harder.
- No personalization token anywhere in the visible email; after nine sends to the same subscriber, even a first-name mention would differentiate from the noise.
- Cadence tax is real: a subscriber who has opened every email since May 17 has seen this deal architecture multiple times, and "still running" reads as "same thing we said yesterday."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the hero copy before the next send.** "The deals are still here, along the rest" must be resolved — likely should read "The deals are still here — don't miss the rest" or "The deals are still here, and so is the FREE stuff." This is the most damaging issue visible in the render.
- 2. **Collapse above-fold to one hero hook.** The FREE items section is the strongest offer in the email — make it the entire fold. Move the percentage grid below; it reads as filler when competing with FREE.
- 3. **Replace "Shop now" with a deadline-anchored CTA.** "Claim my FREE item" or "Shop before midnight" — anything that re-introduces the time boundary at the moment of the click decision.
- 4. **Repeat the countdown closer to the first CTA.** The timer lives at the very top of the email; by the time the eye reaches the "Shop now" button, the urgency signal is gone. Add "Ends tonight" as a micro-line directly above or below the button.
- 5. **Add a first-name token to the hero or rewards module.** The 5x rewards section is the logical place: "[Name], your points are multiplying today."
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Ends tonight: FREE items + up to 40% off"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Last chance — Memorial Day deals close tonight"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Your FREE bonus item is still waiting. Grab it before midnight."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"5x points today only — here's what's still live."`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 9 images:**

| Image | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-…-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` | Content | `image.e.lowes.com` |
| `ad569dc5-…-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` | Content | `image.e.lowes.com` |
| `o.gif` (×2) | Open pixel | `mi.lowes.com` |
| `imp` (×5) | Impression pixel | `sli.lowes.com` |

Content images need descriptive `alt` values. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) — omitting the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to read out the raw `src` URL.

**Overlapping media query breakpoints** — two separate responsive blocks are defined:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)` (webkit-only)
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` (all screens)

Devices at 375–480px wide on WebKit satisfy both queries simultaneously. The second block lacks `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0`, so rules from the first block can be partially overridden. Low severity given the `.drop`, `.container`, and `.photo` rules are largely consistent between both blocks, but the overlap is fragile.

**`@font-face` declarations load `.woff` only** — no `.woff2` variants are specified for Fellix or DIN Next families. `.woff2` is ~30% smaller and supported in all major email clients that support web fonts at all. No `font-display` descriptor is set, which defaults to `auto` — acceptable but `swap` or `optional` would be more predictable.

**`<!--[if !mso]><!-->` wrapping is correct** — custom font CSS is gated behind non-MSO conditionals; Outlook will fall back to system fonts as intended.
