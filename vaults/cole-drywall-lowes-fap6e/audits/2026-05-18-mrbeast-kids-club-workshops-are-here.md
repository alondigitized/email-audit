# MrBeast Kids Club Workshops are HERE 🚀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a kids-workshop activation email promoting the MrBeast Kids Club at Lowe's stores — essentially the old "Build and Grow" program rebranded with MrBeast's IP. The execution inside the email is competent: a clear $14.98 price, three specific workshop dates (May 23, June 14, June 25), and a digital badge gamification layer that would genuinely appeal to parents with kids. The MrBeast brand partnership is a smart hook for a younger demographic.
- The problem is everything before the open. This arrives as the eighth Lowe's email in eight days, hard on the heels of Memorial Day appliance deals and MyLowe's points activation. The audience for those sends is adult homeowners. Blasting the full rewards list with kids workshop content is a segmentation failure, full stop. If you don't have kids, this email has zero relevance — and no signal in the subject or preheader warns you that it's for a different sub-audience.
- Verdict: a well-crafted email for the wrong list. The creative team did their job; the audience team didn't.

## What's working

- **Clear price in the hero:** $14.98 is stated immediately, no hunt required.
- **Specific upcoming dates:** Three workshop dates with "Register now" buttons give the email real utility — it doubles as a calendar prompt.
- **MrBeast branding is a genuine draw** for parents shopping with kids; it lifts the program above generic craft-kit positioning.
- **Digital badge / app integration** is a smart gamification layer that extends engagement beyond the in-store visit.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout is clean, images load, CTA buttons are prominent.

## What's weak

- **Segmentation failure is the core miss:** this is a kids program sent to the Memorial Day deal list. No household-with-children signal in subject or preheader.
- **Preheader is weak and internally repetitive:** "Create exclusive fun with our kits—and just live the fun." uses "fun" twice and says nothing that earns a click.
- **Cadence fatigue:** eighth email in eight days from Lowe's or MyLowe's Rewards — even a good email suffers here.
- **No member-specific benefit surfaced:** sent from MyLowe's Rewards but no loyalty perk or member pricing is called out, wasting the sender context.
- **$14.98 carries an asterisk** but the footnote text is too small to read — adds a minor trust friction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list first.** This email should go to households that have previously engaged with kids-content emails, Kids Club signups, or toy/craft purchases — not the full Memorial Day audience. Deployment fix, not creative fix; highest possible lift.
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader** to surface a parent benefit or a date: "3 free workshops this summer — first one May 23" beats platitudes.
- 3. **Subject Alt A:** `MrBeast Kids Club workshops start May 23 🚀`
- 4. **Subject Alt B:** `Your kids + MrBeast + free workshops = this summer`
- 5. **Preheader Alt A:** `3 hands-on workshops. Digital badges. First date: May 23.`
- 6. **Preheader Alt B:** `Build MrBeast toys in-store — first workshop May 23, free to join`
- 7. **Surface a loyalty angle.** If MyLowe's members get early registration or a badge bonus, say it. The sender is "MyLowe's Rewards" — use that context.
- 8. **Drop the asterisk ambiguity** or resolve it inline ("$14.98 for the kit — workshop registration is free").
