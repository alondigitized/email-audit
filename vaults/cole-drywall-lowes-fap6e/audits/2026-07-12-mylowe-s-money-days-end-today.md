# 💰MyLowe's Money Days end TODAY💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the closing bell on Money Days (July 9–12) — and it does the basic job. "FINAL DAY" in the hero is unmistakable, the $10-on-$50 loyalty offer is visible above the fold, and the "Shop now" button is right there. For anyone who opened the last few Money Days emails and was on the fence, this is a decent nudge.
- The problem is executional sloppiness in the lower half. A "FREE same-day delivery?" section — complete with what looks like food and produce imagery — appears mid-scroll, which is genuinely confusing in a hardware and home improvement email. That one block undercuts the trust the top half builds. It doesn't look like Lowe's; it looks like a pulled-from-template section that wasn't QA'd for this send.
- The overall structure is also crowded: the loyalty earn, a "More deals" product grid with 20%-off callouts, the same-day delivery block, a "We'll send deals you'll love" acquisition section, and a Deals of the Day module are all competing. That's four distinct sub-campaigns below the hero. For a "last chance / close today" email, the energy should be singular. Compared to the Money Days launch email on July 9 — which had a clean blue hero and a focused mechanic — this closing send feels like a content dump.
- Verdict: a serviceable urgency close that punches below what Lowe's has demonstrated they can do with this campaign.

## What's working

- **"FINAL DAY" banner is visually dominant.** The urgency read is immediate — you know exactly what this email is about before reading a word.
- **$10 on $50+ offer is above the fold with a visible CTA.** No scrolling required to understand the value proposition and act on it.
- **Event dates and mechanic are restated below the hero.** Good for anyone who forgot what Money Days is — the in-store + online framing is explicit.
- **20% off product grid reinforces deal depth.** Appliances and tools imagery keeps this grounded in the right category.

## What's weak

- **The "FREE same-day delivery?" section shows food/grocery imagery.** This is the single worst element in the email. Food does not belong in a Lowe's send and will generate genuine inbox confusion or distrust.
- **The question mark on "FREE same-day delivery?"** feels unfinished — it reads like a testing artifact or incomplete copy, not confident benefit communication.
- **Four competing modules below the hero dilute the close.** A "last day" email should have one job: get the click on the loyalty earn. The acquisition/newsletter module especially has no business being here today.
- **No price anchoring or urgency on the product tiles.** "20% off" is visible but without a reference price, it's abstract — no pull.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the fifth Money Days email in four days, and the July 11 send already played the "TODAY & TOMORROW" urgency card. Today's is the honest closer, but the audience has been conditioned to tune these out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or fix the same-day delivery section entirely.** If it must stay, swap the food imagery for power tools or lumber delivery. The current version is a trust bomb.
- 2. **Strip the email to two sections:** hero (FINAL DAY + $10 on $50+ + Shop now) and a single product grid. Drop the newsletter acquisition module — wrong send, wrong moment.
- 3. **Make "FREE same-day delivery" a statement, not a question.** "Get it today — free same-day delivery on orders $45+" (with a real product photo) converts; a question mark does not.
- 4. **Add a countdown or end-time.** "Ends at midnight" is more credible and urgent than just "TODAY" — it closes the ambiguity of how much time is actually left.
- 5. **Give the CTA more specificity.** "Shop Money Days Deals" beats "Shop now" on a close.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: Earn $10 on $50+ — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `💰 Money Days close in hours — don't miss $10 back`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final hours to earn extra MyLowe's Money in-store or online.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$10 back on $50+ today only — shop before midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is a standard table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional build with no critical rendering failures. The primary actionable findings are missing alt text on content images, unverified RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance, and recipient PII exposed in impression pixel query strings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe** — all route through Lowe's ESP click-tracker (domain pattern consistent with `e.lowes.com` redirect infrastructure). Destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be validated.

**Recipient email address exposed in pixel URLs (plaintext):**
All five `sli.lowes.com` impression pixels embed the subscriber's email address as a query parameter with no encoding:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=7122026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```
`e=` carries the raw address in the GET request. This is logged in server access logs and any intermediate CDN/proxy. Standard practice is to use a hashed or tokenized subscriber ID instead. The `mi.lowes.com` open pixel similarly exposes subscriber ID via `mi_u=410146703`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (2):**
These appear to be product/promotional images, not tracking pixels. Missing `alt` fails WCAG 2.1 AA and will render blank in image-blocking clients (Outlook default, many corporate environments):
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels (acceptable, but flagged by QA):**
`o.gif` (open pixel) and five `sli.lowes.com` impression pixels have no `alt`. These should carry `alt=""` explicitly rather than omitting the attribute entirely — the distinction matters for some screen reader parsers.

**Font loading — `.woff` only, no `.woff2`:**
All six `@font-face` declarations (Fellix and DIN Next families) load only `.woff`. WOFF2 offers ~30% smaller file size and is supported by all clients that support custom fonts in email. Not a blocking issue, but adds unnecessary payload on supported clients.

**Obsolete client hacks present (non-breaking):**
`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` rules target legacy Hotmail/Windows Live (deprecated 2012). These add CSS weight but cause no harm.

**Media query targeting:**
The primary responsive breakpoint uses `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` as part of the selector — this intentionally restricts styles to WebKit/Blink only, which appears deliberate for the 375–600px range. Verify this matches intended targeting.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND**
QA notes this may be a relay capture issue (AgentMail), but if genuinely absent from the envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced February 2024 for senders >5k/day) and CAN-SPAM best practices. Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): NOT FOUND**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail for bulk senders. Without this header, Gmail will surface its own unsubscribe prompt which bypasses the sender's list management. Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`.

**Authentication-Results: NOT FOUND**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unverified due to AgentMail relay not forwarding this header. Confirm DKIM signing on `e.lowes.com` and SPF alignment are intact at the MTA level — cannot confirm from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source. Standard Lowe's emails include this in the footer — verify it is present in the full render.
