# We've cracked the code on Dad's gifts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 4 of Lowe's declared "10 Days of Deals" Father's Day campaign — a positioning that's been working well across the recent run — and the email holds the thread competently. The hero ("Gifts for Dad — he won't stop showing off") pairs cleanly with a workshop/tool-chest background, and the product selection (hand tools, outdoor power equipment, gift cards) is squarely on-brand for a Father's Day push.
- The single most damaging element in the render is the countdown timer displaying **00:00:00**. At the moment this screenshot was captured, the Day 4 deal timer reads as fully expired. For a recipient opening this a few hours late, that kills urgency dead — and raises the question of whether the deal is even still live.
- Everything else is competent but not inspired. The subject is playful rather than specific, the email runs long with four distinct content sections, and the G.O.A.T. section near the bottom is too small and cryptic to drive any meaningful click intent. Solid mid-campaign execution; doesn't break new ground.

## What's working

- **Hero framing is tight.** "Gifts for Dad — he won't stop showing off" is personality-forward and the workshop imagery earns the line.
- **Free item offer is visible and prominent.** "FREE" rendered in large type in the wishlist grid stops the eye immediately.
- **$19.98 price anchor is readable** at the product tier level — a concrete, low-friction entry point.
- **Gift card hedge is smart.** The HomeCentr gift card section ("Choose from $25–$1,000") catches gift-givers who don't know what Dad actually wants — good safety net at the bottom of the funnel.
- **Campaign continuity is maintained.** "10 Days of Deals, Day 4" keeps the cadence coherent with the June 11 launch send.

## What's weak

- **Countdown reads 00:00:00 — the deal looks expired.** This is the worst possible signal to send mid-campaign. Whether it's a rendering timing issue or a genuine expiry, the recipient sees a dead clock.
- **Subject is clever but vague.** "We've cracked the code on Dad's gifts" tells you nothing about what's actually on offer — no price, no category, no urgency.
- **G.O.A.T. section is illegible.** "The G.O.A.T. is almost a.o.a." is too small and cryptic to parse quickly on mobile. The copy reads like an inside joke without a punchline.
- **Four distinct content sections create fatigue.** Wishlist picks → Free item → Gift card → GOAT deal is too many gear changes for one send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the countdown logic before sending.** A dead 00:00:00 timer actively destroys the urgency the campaign has built. If the deal window rolls daily, the timer must reset on open or be suppressed when expired.
- 2. **Make the subject line earn the open with a concrete hook.** The "cracked the code" framing is forgettable; lead with the deal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Day 4 Deal: FREE [product] + tools up to $X off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dad's wishlist, solved — free item inside`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to amplify, not repeat.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a $19.98 pick he'll actually use — today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `10 Days of Deals continues — grab today's free gift.`
- 4. **Cut the GOAT section or make it legible.** If it's meant to drive urgency ("almost out of stock"), say that plainly or drop it. Cryptic copy earns zero clicks.
- 5. **Consolidate to two content blocks.** Hero + Day 4 deal, then the gift card fallback. The free item and the GOAT deal can be one of those blocks, not two separate sections.
