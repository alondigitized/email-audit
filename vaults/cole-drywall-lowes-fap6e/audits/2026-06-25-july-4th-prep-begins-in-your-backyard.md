# July 4th prep begins in your backyard 🎊
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's first dedicated July 4th-prep send, landing June 25 — nine days out, which is well-timed for anyone planning a backyard party. The concept is clean: "Party at your place?" reframes outdoor gear as party infrastructure rather than home improvement, which is a smart tonal shift. The email stacks grills, outdoor furniture, a patriotic product hero (the Americana Elite grill), and Frigidaire refrigerators in a logical entertaining-prep order.
- The structure works. There's a clear discount anchor (30% off), specific price points in the product grid ($349 / $399 / $499), and a "Get it first!" new-arrival section that adds editorial variety. The July 4th badge on the Frigidaire block ties appliances credibly into the theme.
- What holds it back is the subject line. Compare it to the June 23 send — "Save $2,110 on an appliance suite?" — which put a number in the subject and immediately mirrored it in the hero. This email has concrete deals inside but nothing quantified in the subject. A subscriber who opened June 23's email conditioned to "Lowe's gives me a number" gets a vague invitation here. Still a solid send, but the gap between execution inside the email and the subject-line hook is the obvious miss.

## What's working

- **"Party at your place?" hero headline** reframes outdoor gear as party planning, which is more emotionally resonant than "Shop July 4th Deals."
- **30% off badge** anchors the scroll with a concrete discount immediately after the hero.
- **Americana Elite "Get it first!" block** adds newness and urgency without relying on manufactured scarcity.
- **July 4th badge on the Frigidaire section** earns the cross-category appliance sell — it doesn't feel bolted on.
- **Price ladder ($349 / $399 / $499)** lets the reader quickly self-select without needing to click to find out.

## What's weak

- **Subject has no number.** "July 4th prep begins in your backyard" is clear but bland — no $ or % signal that this is worth opening right now.
- **"Shop now" CTAs throughout** are generic; none tie back to "grills," "patio furniture," or "July 4th deals" — a missed specificity opportunity.
- **No urgency deadline stated** inside the body. July 4th implies one, but it's never made explicit ("Sale ends July 4th" or "Through Sunday" is absent).
- **The $69 section** (mid-email deal block) is hard to read at thumbnail size — product identity is unclear, which weakens the value prop for that row.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a number in the subject.** The June 23 send scored 9/10 with "$2,110" in the subject — same discipline applies here. Even "Up to 30% off grills + patio" would convert better.
- 2. **Add a deadline line.** A single line in the offer section — "Sale ends July 4th" — removes the ambiguity and converts fence-sitters.
- 3. **Make CTA copy destination-specific.** Replace "Shop now" with "Shop Grills," "Shop Patio Sets," etc. — one line of copy change, measurable CTR lift.
- 4. **Sharpen the $69 block.** Either give it a cleaner product photo or cut it — an unreadable deal undermines trust in the surrounding offers.
