# GO time for Memorial Day ✨ Save up to $1,700
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's urgency-close email for Memorial Day weekend, sent Thursday the 22nd — three days before the holiday. After a week-long cadence that included a countdown timer on May 21 ("Say hello to Extra. Big. Deals."), this send pivots from urgency mechanics back to offer stacking: appliances up to $1,700 off, patio furniture at $100 off, a 3x rewards Triple-up activation, a $9.99 priced item, and a grill play. That's five distinct value props fighting for real estate in a single scroll.
- The hero is the best part. "Long weekend loading... score these deals quick." is punchy, seasonally timed, and the blue Memorial Day creative stays consistent with the prior sends. Below the fold, though, the email becomes a catalog — multiple product rows, a loyalty activation block, a Deals of the Day module, and the $9.99 price point all compete without a clear hierarchy.
- The $1,700 savings number works hard in the subject line, but it's a ceiling across all appliances — not a single hero deal. The cumulative effect by send eight in ten days is diminishing returns. A subscriber who didn't convert on the May 21 countdown email needs a different hook, not more product rows.

## What's working

- **Hero copy is sharp.** "Long weekend loading... score these deals quick." is contemporary, mobile-native, and avoids Lowe's usual exclamation-mark pile-up.
- **$1,700 subject-to-body coherence.** The subject's headline savings number anchors immediately in the hero-adjacent product tile — no bait-and-switch.
- **Triple up 3x rewards is a meaningful differentiator.** Loyalty activation mid-email breaks the pure discount cadence and gives members a concrete reason to click beyond the sale.
- **Grill / "sizzling hit" block is seasonally on-point.** Memorial Day = grilling. This is the most intuitive product-moment pairing in the email.
- **No visible render bugs.** Clean layout, no broken images, consistent brand color palette top-to-bottom.

## What's weak

- **Too many parallel offers, no singular pull.** Appliances, patio, lawn equipment, grills, $9.99 item, Deals of the Day — the email has no single persuasive spine once you pass the hero.
- **"Shop now" is the only CTA verb used, repeatedly.** Every button says "Shop now." At seven or eight instances, it becomes invisible friction rather than a directional signal.
- **Cadence fatigue is real here.** This is the eighth-ish Memorial Day email in ten days. The May 21 countdown-timer email ("Say hello to Extra. Big. Deals.") already used urgency as its hook — this email needs a materially different angle and doesn't have one.
- **The $9.99 price point is stranded.** A specific dollar price creates a concrete mental image, but from the render it's unclear what product it belongs to or what the comparison value is.
- **Loyalty Triple up requires activation.** "Activate now" is a friction-bearing CTA mid-email. Subscribers who haven't already enrolled will skip past it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero deal and commit.** Either lead with appliances ($1,700) OR lead with the grill play ("A sizzling hit") — not both plus patio plus lawn. One deal with a countdown clock and a specific deadline outperforms five deals with a vague "GO time" frame.
- 2. **Differentiate CTA copy per block.** "Shop Appliances," "Shop Outdoor," "Activate Rewards" — each button should confirm where the tap lands, not echo the same "Shop now" across seven rows.
- 3. **Reframe the loyalty module.** "Triple up before it expires" with an explicit expiry date is more compelling than "Activate now." The urgency is there; surface it.
- 4. **Drop the Deals of the Day module from this send.** It dilutes the Memorial Day frame and conflicts with the main CTA. Deals of the Day belongs in a standalone email or the footer only.
- 5. **Surface a single $-off dollar threshold.** "Spend $299, save $30" or "Spend $499, save $75" — a tier that works across categories gives fence-sitters a concrete action. "$1,700" is aspirational; a lower-tier threshold converts the mid-range shopper.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Appliances, grills, patio — all marked down before the weekend's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 3x rewards window closes with the holiday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text missing on 8 images:**

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-...jpg` |
| Content image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-...png` |
| Open pixel | `mi.lowes.com/.../o.gif` |
| 5× impression pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090834/35/37/38` |

The hero and content images are the actionable ones — screen readers will announce the filename or skip them entirely. The tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `imp`) conventionally use `alt=""` (empty string) to silence screen readers; the current state of no `alt` attribute at all is technically invalid HTML.

**Font loading:** Four `@font-face` declarations (Fellix family) load `.woff` only — no `.woff2` fallback. This is a minor optimization gap, not a breakage. Fallback stack not visible in the truncated source; verify a system font fallback exists.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two overlapping media queries — `min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` and `max-width: 480px`. The 375–480px range matches both queries simultaneously. This is a known pattern in older ESP templates but can produce specificity conflicts; no confirmed breakage without full render test.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%FNAME%`) visible in the HTML source. No issues found.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA relay did not surface this header. Lowe's is a high-volume sender and almost certainly includes it at the MTA level; this is likely a relay capture gap, not a true absence. However, it cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — One-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5k/day) since February 2024. If this header is genuinely absent at the MTA, Lowe's is out of compliance with Google and Yahoo postmaster requirements. Again, likely a relay gap — but must be verified against raw SMTP headers.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown from this capture. Sending domain `e.lowes.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; authentication is expected to be in place but unverifiable here.

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are presumed present (standard Lowe's footer); HTML was truncated before footer — cannot confirm from source alone.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 20 click links pass through the Lowe's click-redirect layer before destination — UTM parameter presence on final landing URLs cannot be verified without resolving the redirects. The redirect chain itself is expected behavior for ESP click tracking.

**Recipient PII in pixel URLs:** `e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app` appears in plaintext in 5 impression pixel query strings. This is standard ESP behavior for per-subscriber render tracking, but the etell.app persona email is now in Lowe's pixel server logs and any intermediate proxy logs. No action needed operationally, but worth noting for persona exposure surface.
