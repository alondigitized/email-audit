# 💰Are you ready for MyLowe's Money Days?💰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is the execution email for MyLowe's Money Days — the reminder send that follows the June 30 teaser (scored 9/10), arriving three days before the July 9–12 event window. Where the teaser got credit for novelty ("first ever," "coming soon" framing), this send has to convert attention into shopping intent, and it mostly delivers: the $30/$200 offer is front and center, the dates are unmissable, and the design is clean and focused. One email, one offer, zero clutter.
- The failure is the subject line. "Are you ready?" is the most overused inbox question in promotional email — it generates no new information and buries the urgency behind a rhetorical shrug. With the event three days out, this send should be driving calendar intent, not asking rhetorical questions. A subscriber who missed the June 30 teaser gets zero urgency signal from the subject alone.
- The body copy also leans on "for the first time ever" — which was the marquee claim in the teaser. In a reminder email, that feels like reruns rather than a fresh reason to act. What's missing: any suggestion of *what to buy* to earn the $30. Category tiles or a single hero product would have transformed this from awareness into a pre-shopping prompt.

## What's working

- **Offer is specific and scannable**: "$30 in extra MyLowe's Money with a purchase of $200 or more" gives a clear spend/earn ratio in one sentence.
- **Dates are impossible to miss**: "July 9 – 12" is set in the hero in large type — any subscriber who opens knows exactly when to shop.
- **Single-offer focus**: No competing promotions, no cluttered tiles — the entire email exists to drive one action.
- **Loyalty exclusivity is maintained**: The framing keeps this as a member-only benefit, reinforcing the value of the rewards program.
- **Timing is right**: Three days before the event is the correct reminder window; not too early, not too late.

## What's weak

- **Subject undersells the urgency**: "Are you ready?" is a cliché that does zero informational work — no dollar amount, no dates, no deadline in the subject line itself.
- **"First time ever" is reruns**: This was the headline claim in the June 30 teaser. Repeating it in the reminder signals no new news, which is the wrong energy for an execution send.
- **No category or product signal**: The email tells you *that* you can earn $30 but gives you nothing to click on to start planning — no featured categories, no hero SKU, nothing to spark "I need that."
- **CTA button copy is not legible at render resolution**: Whatever the button says, it doesn't register distinctly from the body — it blends into the layout rather than demanding action.
- **Preview text unverifiable at screenshot resolution**: If the preheader repeats subject-line language, it's a missed slot for adding the date or the $30 hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the deadline and reward**: The event starts in three days — say that. Drop the rhetorical question entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `MyLowe's Money Days start Thursday — earn up to $30`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 days: earn $30 extra in MyLowe's Money (July 9–12)`
- 2. **Replace "are you ready?" preheader with spend-trigger copy** — something that converts the reader from "interesting" to "I should buy that thing this week."
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $200+, get up to $30 extra. Starts Thursday — plan your list now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your MyLowe's Money Days reminder: July 9–12, up to $30 extra. What's on your list?`
- 3. **Retire "for the first time ever"**: It was the novelty hook in the teaser. In the reminder, replace it with action-oriented copy: "Three days to stock up on tools, appliances, or flooring — and earn extra rewards doing it."
- 4. **Add 2–3 category tiles with entry-point prices**: Appliances, outdoor, flooring — each with a minimum eligible item so the subscriber can already be thinking "I need a new mower" before they click. Pre-shopping intent dramatically increases conversion once the event opens.
- 5. **Make the CTA specific**: "Start your list" or "See what qualifies" is more compelling than a generic shop button for a dates-bounded loyalty event.
