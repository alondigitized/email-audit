# FREE gifts on Dad's wishlist🥳
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's second Father's Day email on June 5 — the first was "Gifts for Dad Starting Under $50" (scored 8/10 today). The play here shifts to a gift-with-purchase mechanic: buy a select battery kit from DeWalt, Craftsman, or Kobalt, get a free tool. The three-brand structure gives the email real substance, and the battery kit specs are the right level of detail for a power tool buyer.
- The central problem is the subject and hero working against each other. "FREE gifts on Dad's wishlist" implies zero-cost gifts. What you find inside is a GWP offer requiring a battery kit purchase for every single item. The hero image — an artfully styled empty wooden shelving unit — compounds the confusion by showing no products whatsoever. It's an abstract lifestyle image in an email that needed a product shot.
- Two Father's Day emails from Lowe's in a single day is aggressive. A subscriber who already opened this morning's send is fatigued before they even see the subject line.

## What's working

- Three-brand structure (DeWalt, Craftsman, Kobalt) gives the reader a choice and makes the email feel curated rather than one-size-fits-all
- Battery specs (4.0–6.0Ah, 4.0/2.0Ah, 24V 2-Ah) are visible — the right detail level for someone actually shopping power tools
- Subject line is 31 characters, clean on mobile, no exclamation stacking
- Preheader "You shop and we'll add a FREE item on select purchases" explains the GWP mechanic accurately before the open

## What's weak

- Hero image of empty shelves shows no tools — fails to preview the actual offer and wastes the most valuable visual real estate in the email
- "FREE gifts" in both subject and hero implies no purchase necessary; every offer requires buying a battery kit — misleading at headline level
- Kobalt block is the weakest: "FREE Kobalt 24V 2-Ah battery kit when you buy a select Kobalt 24V battery kit" is BOGO batteries, not a tool gift — badly miscast for a Father's Day send
- No urgency anywhere: Father's Day is June 21 and nothing says "order by X" or "while supplies last"
- This is the second Father's Day email of the day from Lowe's — cadence fatigue from the same segment is a real cost

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the empty-shelves hero with a product shot** — show the actual DeWalt circular saw or Craftsman blower prominently. One sharp tool image instantly clarifies what "FREE gifts" means and validates the offer before scrolling.
- 2. **Reframe the hero copy to match the GWP mechanic** — "Buy Dad's battery kit. We'll throw in a free tool." converts better than headline copy that over-promises a no-cost gift. The offer is solid; the framing isn't.
- 3. **Add a delivery deadline** — "Order by June 17 to arrive before Father's Day" turns the entire email from evergreen to urgent. One line in the hero banner is all it takes.
- 4. **Upgrade or replace the Kobalt offer** — BOGO batteries is not a gift; replace the free item with a Kobalt-branded tool, or drop Kobalt entirely and add a third strong-brand power tool block.
- 5. **Stagger the two sends** — the morning email owned the gifting angle under $50; this GWP mechanic email should have dropped tomorrow as its own moment, targeting a different time-of-day or segment.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Lowe's second Father's Day email on June 5 — the first was "Gifts for Dad Starting Under $50" (scored 8/10 today). The play here shifts to a gift-with-purchase mechanic: buy a select battery kit from DeWalt, Craftsman, or Kobalt, get a free tool. The three-brand structure gives the email real substance, and the battery kit specs are the right level of detail for a power tool buyer.

The central problem is the subject and hero working against each other. "FREE gifts on Dad's wishlist" implies zero-cost gifts. What you find inside is a GWP offer requiring a battery kit purchase for every single item. The hero image — an artfully styled empty wooden shelving unit — compounds the confusion by showing no products whatsoever. It's an abstract lifestyle image in an email that needed a product shot.

Two Father's Day emails from Lowe's in a single day is aggressive. A subscriber who already opened this morning's send is fatigued before they even see the subject line.

## 2. What worked

- Three-brand structure (DeWalt, Craftsman, Kobalt) gives the reader a choice and makes the email feel curated rather than one-size-fits-all
- Battery specs (4.0–6.0Ah, 4.0/2.0Ah, 24V 2-Ah) are visible — the right detail level for someone actually shopping power tools
- Subject line is 31 characters, clean on mobile, no exclamation stacking
- Preheader "You shop and we'll add a FREE item on select purchases" explains the GWP mechanic accurately before the open

## 3. What didn't

- Hero image of empty shelves shows no tools — fails to preview the actual offer and wastes the most valuable visual real estate in the email
- "FREE gifts" in both subject and hero implies no purchase necessary; every offer requires buying a battery kit — misleading at headline level
- Kobalt block is the weakest: "FREE Kobalt 24V 2-Ah battery kit when you buy a select Kobalt 24V battery kit" is BOGO batteries, not a tool gift — badly miscast for a Father's Day send
- No urgency anywhere: Father's Day is June 21 and nothing says "order by X" or "while supplies last"
- This is the second Father's Day email of the day from Lowe's — cadence fatigue from the same segment is a real cost

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Replace the empty-shelves hero with a product shot** — show the actual DeWalt circular saw or Craftsman blower prominently. One sharp tool image instantly clarifies what "FREE gifts" means and validates the offer before scrolling.

2. **Reframe the hero copy to match the GWP mechanic** — "Buy Dad's battery kit. We'll throw in a free tool." converts better than headline copy that over-promises a no-cost gift. The offer is solid; the framing isn't.

3. **Add a delivery deadline** — "Order by June 17 to arrive before Father's Day" turns the entire email from evergreen to urgent. One line in the hero banner is all it takes.

4. **Upgrade or replace the Kobalt offer** — BOGO batteries is not a gift; replace the free item with a Kobalt-branded tool, or drop Kobalt entirely and add a third strong-brand power tool block.

5. **Stagger the two sends** — the morning email owned the gifting angle under $50; this GWP mechanic email should have dropped tomorrow as its own moment, targeting a different time-of-day or segment.

### 1. Technical Summary

Primary issues are missing alt attributes on tracking/impression pixels, and authentication/compliance headers not visible to the QA relay — which may reflect relay capture gaps rather than sender defects, but cannot be confirmed from available data.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-redirect links skipped** by HTTP probe — all routed through `e.lowes.com` redirect infrastructure (expected for a Salesforce Marketing Cloud sender). No broken links detected among probed URLs.

**Impression pixels confirmed present** via `sli.lowes.com/imp` — five distinct pixels with `s=` campaign IDs (1090834, 1090835, 1090837, 1090838), all correctly parameterized with `e=`, `p=`, `lctg=`, and `stpe=` values. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on 10 images** — confirmed by QA:

| Image | Type | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-...jpg` (`image.e.lowes.com`) | Content image | Screen readers silent; images-off rendering broken |
| `ad569dc5-...png` (`image.e.lowes.com`) | Content image | Same |
| `8323e445c38bde24.png` (`mi.lowes.com`) | Tracking pixel | `alt=""` preferred over absent attr |
| `o.gif` (`mi.lowes.com`) | Open pixel | Same |
| 5× `imp` pixels (`sli.lowes.com`) | Impression pixels | Same |

The two content images at `image.e.lowes.com` are the actionable accessibility gaps — blank alt on tracking pixels is tolerable but technically non-conformant (WCAG 2.1 requires `alt=""`  explicitly, not absent).

**Font loading**: Custom fonts (`Fellix`, `DIN Next`) load via `lowescdn.com` — `.woff` only, no `.woff2`. Not a rendering failure but suboptimal for clients that support woff2.

**`DIN Next Bold` and `DIN Next Black`** reference scaffold version `1.2.0` while `DIN Next` (regular) references `1.4.4` — version inconsistency in font CDN paths. No load failure expected but worth flagging for cache/CDN drift.

**Media queries**: Two overlapping breakpoint blocks (`min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` and `max-width: 480px`) could produce ambiguous behavior on 480px–600px viewports in WebKit clients. Not a breakage but a maintainability flag.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured** by QA relay. This is a WARN, not a confirmed absence: Salesforce Marketing Cloud reliably injects this header, and the AgentMail relay may not forward raw SMTP headers. Cannot confirm either way without direct header inspection.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) — not captured** — same caveat. Gmail and Apple Mail require this for the native unsubscribe button; absence would be a compliance gap with bulk sender requirements (Google's 2024 mandate for >5k/day senders).

**`Authentication-Results` — not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unverifiable. Sender domain `e.lowes.com` is a known SFMC subdomain with published DKIM; likely passing but not confirmed in this relay path.

**Action required**: Verify raw headers via direct SMTP capture or mail header analyzer tool (`mxtoolbox`, `mail-tester`) to confirm all three are present before treating these as sender defects.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

All 20 click links route through `e.lowes.com` redirect — UTM parameters, if any, are appended post-redirect and not inspectable from HTML source alone. The QA probe skipped these links, so landing page UTM alignment is **unverified**.

**Recommended follow-up**: Manually resolve one or two `e.lowes.com` redirects and confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on the final destination URL. Father's Day campaign traffic attribution depends on this.
