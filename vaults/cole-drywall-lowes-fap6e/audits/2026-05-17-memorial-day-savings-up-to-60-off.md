# Memorial Day savings⭐ Up to 60% OFF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's seventh email in eight days, and the subject-to-body tension is the loudest problem: "Up to 60% OFF" appears in the subject but the hero still leads with 45% off appliances — the same offer that headlined the May 15 send. If 60% is real, it's buried mid-scroll, which makes the subject read as an escalation tactic rather than a genuine news headline. That erodes trust, especially with a subscriber who's already opened six consecutive Lowe's emails this week.
- The email itself is competently built — clean render, Memorial Day framing carried through multiple sections, a personalized tools insert, and stacked offers (appliances, fans, tile, flooring, outdoor, patriotic) that cover a wide surface area. But wide coverage is also the problem: with eight distinct product sections and no single anchor, the email asks the reader to do too much work.
- The cadence is unsustainable at this point. By email seven, the urgency signals have fully burned out. A subscriber hitting delete before opening is not a render failure — it's a fatigue failure.

## What's working

- **"Curated with you in mind" tools section** adds a real personalization signal that separates this from a generic blast.
- **Memorial Day framing is coherent end-to-end** — hero, section headers, and the "red, white and blue" lifestyle block all reinforce the season, not just the subject line.
- **"Buy more, save more" + FREE tier** stacks incentive beyond a flat percent-off and rewards bigger basket sizes.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout gaps visible anywhere.
- **60% headline is a genuine escalation** from May 15's 45% promise; subscribers who track discounts will notice.

## What's weak

- **Subject claims 60% OFF but the hero still shows 45%** — the premium number is buried mid-email, creating a subject-to-hero mismatch that reads as bait.
- **Eight competing product sections** (appliances, outdoor, tools, fans, tile, flooring, patriotic, Deals of the Day) dilute focus; nothing gets the featured treatment it needs to convert.
- **No Memorial Day deadline date visible anywhere** — "Memorial Day savings" implies urgency but without a hard close date ("ends May 26"), the deadline is vague.
- **No MyLowe's Rewards pricing or loyalty badge** — a glaring miss two days after the May 15 loyalty-activation email explicitly told members to expect payoff deals.
- **"Shop now" repeated verbatim across every section** — no CTA differentiation between appliances and tile loses segmentation signal entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 60% offer to the hero.** If it's the lead in the subject, it must be the lead in the body. Swap the 45% appliance hero for whatever category has the 60% — or restructure so the hero banner explicitly says "Up to 60% OFF — find your deal below." Otherwise cut 60% from the subject entirely and stay honest at 45%.
- 2. **Surface a Memorial Day hard close date.** A small deadline bar — "Sale ends Monday, May 26" — anywhere in the upper third converts ambiguous "Memorial Day" urgency into a real ticking clock.
- 3. **Add a MyLowe's Rewards layer.** After the May 15 activation send primed members to expect perks, this email should deliver. A badge on the appliances section saying "Members earn 2× points" or "Member price: $X" closes the loop and rewards engagement.
- 4. **Collapse to three sections max.** Appliances (60% hero), tools/outdoor (the personalized insert), and one wildcard. The Deals of the Day grid at the bottom can do the long-tail work. Eight sections is a landing-page layout, not an email.
- 5. **Differentiate the CTAs by category.** "Shop appliances," "Shop tools," "Shop tile" instead of eight identical "Shop now" labels. Lower-effort for the marketer; meaningfully clearer for the scanner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"60% OFF this weekend — appliances, tools, tile + more"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Your biggest Lowe's discount of the year ends Monday"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Sale ends May 26 — here's where to find the 60% deals inside."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Members: stack your rewards points on top of Memorial Day pricing."`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound XHTML 1.0 Transitional with functional responsive breakpoints, but has two compliance gaps (missing List-Unsubscribe headers, SPF/DKIM status unknown via relay) and eight images lacking alt text — five of which are tracking pixels that expose the recipient address in plaintext URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 tracking links skipped** — all click URLs route through a redirect domain (consistent with ESP click-wrapping). This is expected behavior; no broken links detected via probe.

**Tracking pixels expose PII in URLs (HIGH):**
Five `sli.lowes.com/imp` beacon URLs include the recipient email address as a plaintext query parameter:

```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=5172026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```

The `&e=` parameter is unencoded. Any proxy, CDN, or mail security appliance that logs image requests will capture the email address in access logs. This is a data hygiene issue regardless of whether this recipient is a test persona.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text:**

| Image | URL (truncated) |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `.../c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` |
| Open-pixel | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/.../o.gif?mi_u=410146703` |
| 5× impression beacons | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=...` |
| Promo image | `.../ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` |

The hero and promo images need descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients. The tracking pixels and open pixel should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress screen-reader announcements.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two overlapping media queries defined — `min-width: 375px AND max-width: 600px` (webkit-only) and `max-width: 480px`. The upper bound mismatch (600px vs 480px) means devices between 480–600px get only the webkit-scoped styles, not the full mobile ruleset. This may cause layout issues on tablets in portrait mode on non-WebKit engines.

**Font loading:** Four `@font-face` blocks load `.woff` only — no `.woff2` fallback. Modern clients prefer woff2; woff-only increases load time marginally but will render correctly via system fallback.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — NOT FOUND (WARN)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024 for senders >5K/day). If it exists at the ESP layer and was stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — NOT FOUND (WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders. Absence triggers demotion to spam folder for qualifying sends.

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are expected in the email body (not visible in the truncated source). Cannot confirm from available data — flag for manual body check.

**Authentication-Results header — NOT FOUND (WARN)**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown because the AgentMail relay did not forward or synthesize `Authentication-Results`. Sending domain `e.lowes.com` is an ESP subdomain; DMARC alignment depends on whether the ESP's DKIM signature aligns with `lowes.com`. Cannot confirm from available data.
