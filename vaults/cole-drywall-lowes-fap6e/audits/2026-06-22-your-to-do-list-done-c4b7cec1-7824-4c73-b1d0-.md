# Your to-do list? Done.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's subscription product launch for HomeCare+ — a $99/year home maintenance plan — landing June 22, one day after the last Father's Day send. The timing is a clean, necessary pivot off a 9-day holiday run (the June 21 "Dad's day is today. No pressure 😉" email was the bow on that campaign), and the creative execution is professional: clear hero, three well-organized feature tiles, no render issues. But the strategy has a real problem — this is a subscription sell with zero urgency, zero introductory pricing, and the weakest possible CTA ("Learn more" twice) anchoring a $99 annual commitment from a cold start. You don't launch a subscription product with "Learn more."
- The subject "Your to-do list? Done." is clever wordplay but deliberately hides the actual pitch — a paid subscription service. A subscriber skimming their inbox post-Father's Day weekend has no idea this is a product launch. The email looks like a branded content piece more than a conversion send. The $99/year price appears in the hero but there's no comparison point (vs. what?) and no trial hook to reduce friction on a new category for Lowe's.
- Net judgment: a competent email that undersells an interesting new product by refusing to close.

## What's working

- **Clean creative break from the Father's Day run.** No holiday hangover — the pivot to HomeCare+ is visually and tonally fresh.
- **$99/year price is prominent.** The badge treatment in the hero makes the annual price legible at a glance.
- **Three-tile feature breakdown is readable.** 2 in-home visits / 5% off service items / automatic renewal is a scannable value proposition.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text or broken sections.
- **"New!" badge on the HomeCare+ logo.** Signals this is a launch, not a repeat promo.

## What's weak

- **"Learn more" used twice.** For a subscription product asking you to spend $99/year, this is an abandonment-rate CTA. "Start for $99" or "See what's included" converts better.
- **No urgency or introductory offer.** No deadline, no launch pricing, no "first month free" — nothing to act on today vs. tomorrow vs. never.
- **Subject hides the product entirely.** "Your to-do list? Done." could mean a summer sale, a service reminder, or an app feature — the HomeCare+ launch is buried behind cute wordplay.
- **No social proof.** A subscription product launch without a testimonial, member count, or satisfaction stat is a missed trust signal.
- **Partner brand at bottom-right of hero (looks like "fi" — possibly Frontdoor) is too small to be legible.** If there's a credible service partner powering these visits, that's a trust lever that's being wasted at thumbnail size.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace both "Learn more" CTAs with commitment-reducing language.** "See what HomeCare+ includes" or "Get HomeCare+ — $99/yr" eliminates the ambiguity. "Learn more" is what you use when you don't want to oversell; this product needs selling.
- 2. **Add a launch incentive or time-bound hook.** Even a soft one: "Founding member pricing" or "First 1,000 members lock in $99" makes the June 22 email feel like a reason to act now, not a brochure.
- 3. **Surface the service partner name and logo at readable size.** If Frontdoor or a comparable brand is backing the in-home visits, that partner's credibility is a major conversion signal.
- 4. **Add one testimonial or "X members enrolled" social proof.** Even a placeholder stat in the hero tile would help.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to telegraph the product.** The wordplay is fine but the email needs opens from people who actually want home maintenance, not people who think they're getting a sale summary.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: HomeCare+ for $99/yr — chores handled`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$99/yr gets you 2 in-home visits + 5% off maintenance items`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `2 visits, 7 services, automatic upkeep — all year for less than $10/month`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New from MyLowe's: a home maintenance subscription that does the to-do list for you`

This image has no `alt` attribute. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate mail) will show a broken placeholder with no context. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 failure.

**`@font-face` loaded conditionally (`<!--[if !mso]><!-->`)**

**Duplicate `local()` hints in `@font-face`:** Each font declaration repeats the `local()` hint twice (e.g., `local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular')`). Redundant but harmless; minor code hygiene issue.

**Media queries:** Two overlapping breakpoints exist (`min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` + `max-width: 480px`). The `480px` query will override the `375–600px` query on most phones. Behavior is probably intentional but should be verified against target device matrix.
