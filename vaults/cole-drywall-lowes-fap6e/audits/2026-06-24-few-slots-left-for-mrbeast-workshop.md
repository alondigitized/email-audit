# Few slots left for MrBeast Workshop 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Lowe's Kids Club doing a MrBeast co-branded in-store workshop — Saturday June 27, $14 a kid — and the subject line does the heavy lifting. "Few slots left for MrBeast Workshop" is the best subject in the recent batch: it's specific, short, and manufactures real scarcity without resorting to ALL CAPS or emoji spam. The hero follows through cleanly with a clear price, a date, and a "Register now" button above the fold.
- The problem is this email is sending to the full MyLowe's Rewards list, not just the parents-with-kids segment. A subscriber who doesn't have school-age children has literally no use for it. The MrBeast social proof is powerful for that demographic, but zero if the household has no kids. That targeting gap is the only structural flaw — the email itself is well-produced.
- Compared to the June 23 appliance suite send ($2,110 savings hero that scored 9/10), this is a smarter subject but a narrower audience. Clean cadence pivot — the Father's Day run is completely out of frame, and this feels like a fresh campaign rather than the eighth variation of the same deal.

## What's working

- **Subject is the best in recent memory** — "Few slots left" creates genuine FOMO without resorting to shouting; MrBeast brand name does the curiosity work.
- **$14 price is immediately visible in the hero** — no scroll required, no ambiguity.
- **MrBeast co-branding is legitimate social proof** — the #1 YouTube creator is a strong trust signal for any parent of a 6–12-year-old.
- **Two-module layout is clean** — hero → event registration, then app/badge upsell. No clutter, no layout breaks visible.
- **Date (Saturday, June 27) earns its own line** — urgency is date-anchored, not manufactured.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is broken or awkward** — the top-of-email text reads something close to "Register your free before the sale runs out," which is grammatically incomplete and uses "sale" for what's actually an event registration. It undercuts the otherwise clean subject.
- **No list segmentation visible** — this is clearly a Kids Club send going to a broader Rewards list; any subscriber without kids reads this and feels like a wrong number.
- **Badges module is burying the lead** — the "collect exclusive MrBeast digital badges" panel is compelling for the right audience but is secondary content that competes for attention below the fold.
- **Checklist copy under the hero is too small to read** — the three benefit bullets require a zoom; a lot of the "what you get" value proof is lost.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or rewrite the preheader immediately** — the current copy appears mangled. Correct version should be something like: "Lowe's Kids Club · Sat June 27 · Just $14 per child."
- 2. **Enlarge the workshop benefit bullets** — "Build a spin toy, take it home, get exclusive sweets" is good value; make it readable at first glance.
- 3. **Suppress to non-parent households** — even rough RFM segmentation (never bought from Kids or Toy categories, no prior Kids Club engagement) would dramatically lift relevance and protect sender reputation.
- 4. **Move the MrBeast badge graphic up** — the phone mockup showing badges is visually compelling; at minimum, push it above the fold or merge it into the hero module.

## Technical Audit — "Few slots left for MrBeast Workshop" (lowes@e.lowes.com)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based build with no critical rendering failures, but five QA warnings indicate compliance gaps (missing physical address, unsubscribe headers not captured) and deliverability risk (plain-text body is URL-dominated, authentication headers absent from relay).

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**31 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, meaning no destination URL validation was performed. Broken or expired landing pages would go undetected.

**Plain-text alternative is 77% URLs** (3,187 of 4,099 chars). This is a common spam signal for plain-text parts that are auto-generated from tracked URLs rather than human-readable content. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) penalize this pattern.

No broken links were confirmed, but link destination validation is effectively blind due to redirect wrapping.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text** on at least one image:
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/1/a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg` — no `alt` attribute present.

Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate clients) will show a blank gap with no context. QA flagged one instance; a full image audit should be run across all `<img>` tags.

**Font loading** — four custom `@font-face` declarations (Fellix family, DIN Next family) via `lowescdn.com`. Both `@font-face` blocks are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` conditionals, which correctly suppresses them in Outlook MSO but they will attempt to load in Gmail, Apple Mail, and mobile clients. This is expected behavior, but the `.woff`-only format (`format('woff')`) means no `.woff2` fallback, adding minor load overhead.

**Media query gap** — two breakpoints are defined: `min-width: 375px` to `max-width: 600px` (webkit-only) and `max-width: 480px`. The 481–599px range on non-webkit clients falls into a dead zone with no responsive rules applied.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Physical mailing address | **WARN** | Not detected in QA scan — required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) |
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay; may be present upstream but not verified |

The physical address absence is the highest-risk item — if the footer truly omits it, this is a CAN-SPAM violation. The truncated source prevents confirmation; the full HTML footer must be checked.

Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click unsubscribe. If this header is missing upstream (not just undetected by the relay), deliverability to Gmail and Yahoo inboxes is at risk.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params)

31 tracked links use a click-redirect domain (consistent with `e.lowes.com` ESP redirects). UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without probing through the redirect chain. If the ESP redirect strips or fails to append UTMs before the final destination, campaign attribution in GA4/Adobe Analytics will be lost.

No direct evidence of UTM parameters in the raw HTML source (they would typically appear in the pre-redirect href values or be injected by the ESP at click time). This should be verified by manually clicking a tracked link and inspecting the final URL.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Verify physical address in footer** — pull the complete HTML source and confirm a postal address is present. If missing, this must be added before the next send.
2. **Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers upstream** — check the raw headers at the ESP (not via AgentMail relay). If absent, enable one-click unsubscribe at the ESP level to comply with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy.
3. **Fix plain-text alternative** — regenerate the plain-text part as human-readable content with tracked URLs replaced by descriptive anchor text. The 77% URL ratio is a deliverability liability.
4. **Add `alt` text to all images** — at minimum the flagged `.jpg`; run a full `img[alt=""]` or `img:not([alt])` selector pass across the complete template.
5. **Validate UTM continuity** — manually trace one tracked link through the redirect to confirm UTM params survive to the final `lowes.com` landing page.
6. **Resolve the 481–599px responsive gap** — either extend the upper breakpoint to 600px without the webkit restriction, or consolidate the two media queries into one.
7. **Obtain authentication confirmation** — confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status directly from ESP send logs; do not rely on relay-captured headers for this signal.
