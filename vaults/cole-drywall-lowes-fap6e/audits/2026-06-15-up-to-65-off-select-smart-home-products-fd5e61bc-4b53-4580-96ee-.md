# Up to 65% OFF select smart home products🤩
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 5 of Lowe's "10 Days of Father's Day Deals" campaign, detoured into a smart home flash sale — Alexa, Ring, and Blink products at up to 65% off. The hero lands cleanly: the discount is prominent, the brands are named, and the CTA is obvious. That's the good part.
- The problem is everything below the fold. The Day 5 countdown module shows **00:00:00:00** — deal has ended, per the label — and that dead clock sits mid-email while the hero above is still asking you to shop. It creates a trust gap: if today's deal is over, am I buying into a stale offer? Below that sits a Kids Club "Swann Spinner Workshop" registration prompt ($14.99, June 21) that has nothing to do with smart home discounts or Father's Day gifts. The email is doing four jobs badly instead of one well.
- The subject over-delivers relative to the body: "up to 65% OFF" is a strong hook, but the email doesn't back it up with specific products, pricing anchors, or urgency other than a clock that reads zero.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is clear and above-fold** — 65% off, named brands (Alexa, Ring, Blink), blue "Shop now" button all visible immediately.
- **Subject line is direct** — specific percentage, specific category, under 45 characters. Earns the open.
- **Visual hierarchy is disciplined at the top** — the eye goes offer → brands → button without friction.
- **Father's Day campaign thread is maintained** — the "great gifts for the greatest dad" section keeps the seasonal context alive.

## What's weak

- **Dead countdown clock (00:00:00:00)** — "Day 5 deal has ended" sits in the middle of the email. Sends a mixed signal about whether the 65% off offer is still live.
- **Kids Club / Swann Spinner Workshop module** — completely off-topic for a smart home deal email sent to adults; feels like a list-management mistake.
- **No specific products shown with prices** — "up to 65% off" without a $29 Echo Dot or a Ring doorbell at a named price leaves money on the table.
- **Preview text pivots away from the subject** — "Plus: don't miss Day 5 of Father's Day Deals" is Father's Day, not smart home. Mismatched preamble undercuts the subject's smart home hook.
- **Three separate CTAs compete** — smart home shop, Father's Day shop, Kids Club register, Deals of the Day sign-up. Too many asks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove the expired countdown block entirely** — a dead clock mid-email undermines the offer above it. If Day 5 ended, cut the module; the email was sent on June 15 and that deal has visibly lapsed.
- 2. **Show two or three specific products with struck-through prices** — a Ring Video Doorbell at $49 (was $99) converts harder than "up to 65% off select products."
- 3. **Cut the Kids Club module** — mismatched audience. Either route it to a segmented send or drop it.
- 4. **Align the preheader to the smart home angle** — the current preheader (Father's Day Day 5) fights the subject line.
- 5. **Consolidate CTAs** — one email, one goal: the smart home sale. Move Deals of the Day sign-up to the footer or a separate send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA:

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-...jpg` |
| Open-pixel gif | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/.../o.gif?mi_u=410146703` |

| Product/promo image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-...png` |

The hero image (`c301e24e...jpg`) and promo image (`ad569dc5...png`) are content-carrying — missing `alt` breaks screen reader access and renders blank in image-off clients (Gmail default for unknown senders).

The tracking/beacon `<img>` tags should have `alt=""` (empty string) — omitting `alt` entirely is a WCAG failure even for decorative/functional pixels.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two overlapping media queries (`min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` and `max-width: 480px`) with conflicting `.container` width rules. On 481–600px viewports both queries are partially active, which can produce layout inconsistencies. Low severity.

**Font loading:** Custom fonts (`Fellix`, `DIN Next`) are WOFF-only — no WOFF2 fallback. Most modern clients support WOFF2 and would benefit from it; WOFF-only is not a bug but is suboptimal. Gmail and Outlook will fall back to system fonts regardless.

### 5. Compliance
