# The hottest drops are here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the same bait-and-switch playbook Sephora has run repeatedly this brand history — a top banner promising "50% off Select Beauty, Today Only" that gives way almost immediately to a long, undifferentiated new-arrivals catalog ("JUST DROPPED": Byredo, Dior, rhode, OUAI, Jung Saem Mool, Kayali, Touchland, amika). The subject line, "The hottest drops are here," doesn't even mention the 50% off deal that's sitting right at the top of the render — a missed opportunity, since that's the strongest hook in the email.
- Once you scroll past the hero banner, this reads exactly like the 8/23 and 8/30 "new arrivals" catalog sends — a vertical scroll of product tiles with almost no narrative or urgency tying them together, until a second, smaller "Get 15% off" + Charlotte Tilbury tile shows up near the bottom, buried after a dozen product cards. Two discount offers in one email, both undersold relative to the wall of "NEW" product launches between them.
- Visually the render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text — but the structure actively works against conversion: the actual discount, which is the highest-intent CTA, gets one screen at the top and one small tile nine sections later, while "new arrivals" (lowest urgency, hardest to act on) eats the entire middle of the email.

## What's working

- Hero banner is bold and legible — "50% off Select Beauty," "TODAY ONLY," and a clear "SHOP NOW" button all visible without ambiguity.
- Product photography is consistent and clean throughout — no render bugs, no lorem ipsum, no layout breaks.
- "NEW," "LIMITED EDITION," and "ONLY AT SEPHORA" badges are used consistently to signal exclusivity per product.
- "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" and "PLANET AWARE" certification badges appear near relevant products, reinforcing values-based buyers' trust.
- The Insider tier bar (INSIDER / VIB / ROUGE) at the footer nods to loyalty segmentation.

## What's weak

- Subject line doesn't reference the 50% off deal at all, even though it's the strongest asset in the email — a wasted hook.
- The 50% off banner and the "Get 15% off" / Charlotte Tilbury banner are disconnected offers with no unifying story — reads like two campaigns stitched together.
- The middle of the email is an undifferentiated product wall (8+ brand tiles) with no clear single hero product or "must click here" moment.
- Both discount CTAs get visually deprioritized relative to the sheer volume of "JUST DROPPED" tiles — the eye has to work hard to find the actual deal again after scrolling.
- No visible urgency/expiration detail on the 15% off Charlotte Tilbury offer (no "today only" or date), undercutting its own credibility next to the "TODAY ONLY" banner above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to lead with the discount, since it's the only concrete, actionable offer in the send — "the hottest drops" undersells what's actually the strongest asset.
- 2. Cut the product wall by at least half — pick 3-4 hero launches instead of 8+, and let the 50% off and 15% off banners bookend a tighter middle section instead of getting buried by it.
- 3. Add urgency language (a countdown or explicit end time) to the 15% off / Charlotte Tilbury banner to match the credibility of the "TODAY ONLY" hero.
- 4. Move the Charlotte Tilbury before/after or a similar social-proof element higher up — it's the only proof point in the whole email and it's stranded near the bottom.
- 5. Consider a single repeated sticky-style CTA ("Shop the Sale") between product sections so the actual discount stays reachable during the long scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off select beauty — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just dropped: Byredo, Dior + 50% off beauty`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your favorites, half off — plus first look at Byredo and Dior`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New from Byredo, Dior, and OUAI. Today only: 50% off select beauty.`
