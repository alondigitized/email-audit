# These picks are everywhere
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's second editorial-mode send since the Stacking Weekend arc ended — following the July 8 "Summer's Hottest Beauty" reset. The creative premise is social proof: "HOT ON SOCIAL, you've seen these picks everywhere." That's a smart narrative pivot after seven days of heavy discounting, but the execution is a kitchen-sink email. A "Get a Mini" top banner, a Patrick Starrr One Size brand spotlight, a multi-product grid, a 4X Points loyalty block, a Bundled Beauty section, and a Rhode launch all compete within a single scroll. No single story wins.
- The Patrick Starrr One Size hero is the clearest editorial anchor — a new brand with recognizable creator talent that actually justifies the "everywhere" premise. Rhode at the bottom is a second strong hook, but it's buried. These two could each carry a standalone send.
- Net judgment: a serviceable discovery email with an identity problem. The social-proof reset framing is right; the multi-section sprawl kills the landing.

## What's working

- **"HOT ON SOCIAL" hero framing** is the right post-promo reset — positions trend discovery over discounting after the Stacking Weekend blitz.
- **Patrick Starrr One Size spotlight** is a genuine editorial anchor; creator-tied brand launches give subscribers an actual reason to click beyond price.
- **4X Points block** surfaces loyalty value without requiring a sale — a smart bridge between the promotional and editorial eras.
- **Rhode section** — Hailey Bieber's brand carries massive cultural pull and will harvest curiosity-clicks from anyone tracking beauty social.
- **No render issues** — layout loads cleanly, no broken images or overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "These picks are everywhere"** carries no offer, no urgency, no category signal — there's no reason to open it cold from an inbox preview.
- **Too many competing sections** — Get a Mini / Hot on Social / Patrick Starrr / Product Grid / 4X Points / Bundled Beauty / Rhode — the eye has no single place to land after the hero.
- **Generic CTA copy throughout** — "SHOP NOW!" appears repeatedly with zero specificity to the brand or product being featured at that moment.
- **Rhode is buried at the bottom** — the most culturally potent brand in the email gets the least visual real estate and will miss most skimmers.
- **No visible deadline on 4X Points** — the loyalty offer is present but toothless; no end date shown means no conversion pressure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** Patrick Starrr One Size is one story. Rhode launch is another. Both deserve a full hero, not shared real-estate in a scrollable listicle.
- 2. **Add an expiry to the 4X Points offer.** "Earn 4X Points — ends Sunday" converts; "Earn 4X Points" is wallpaper.
- 3. **Make every CTA brand-specific.** "Shop One Size," "Discover Rhode," "Claim Your Mini" — verb + noun with context beats three identical "SHOP NOW!" buttons stacked down the page.
- 4. **Promote Rhode or lead with it in the next send** while the brand moment is at cultural peak. Don't bury Hailey Bieber.
- 5. **Write a real preheader** that previews the Patrick Starrr or Rhode story — right now the visible sub-copy echoes the subject almost word-for-word.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Pinch-zoom blocked (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming on iOS. Remove this attribute; Apple's Mail and Safari will handle scaling correctly without it.

**Issue 2 — Universal `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to the entire element set, this suppresses OS-level font scaling for users who have set larger system text sizes (common for low-vision users). Restrict to `table, td` only, which is the legitimate Outlook fix target.

**Issue 3 — Malformed robots meta (minor)**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Value should be `noindex` (no space). Inconsequential for email delivery but indicates a template copy-paste defect.
