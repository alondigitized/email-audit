# Something special awaits...This is what your hair wants.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a two-headline email wearing one subject line. "This is what your hair wants" promises a hair-focused send, but the first thing you actually see is a pink "up to 50% off MAKE UP FOR EVER" lip sale tied to National Lip Day — nothing to do with hair. The dae Dreamriser Volumizing Hair Mousse launch, which is presumably what the subject is about, doesn't show up until you've scrolled past the lip sale hero and a generic "New & Clean at Sephora" skincare before/after block.
- Once you get past that mismatch, the production quality is classic strong Sephora: clean grid layout, "ONLY AT SEPHORA" and "NEW" badges doing consistent merchandising work, a loyalty stack (4X Clinique points, 100-point rewards, Beauty Insider tier bar) at the bottom, and zero render bugs. It's the same reliable template that's carried this brand's last several sends.
- Judgement: solid craft, wrong sequencing. If the goal was to sell the hair mousse, it's buried under an unrelated lip promo; if the goal was the lip sale, the subject line is false advertising. Pick one story and lead with it.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum across a very long scroll.
- Clear loyalty ladder at the bottom: 4X points on Clinique, new 100-point rewards tier, Beauty Insider account bar — gives repeat shoppers a reason to stay engaged even after the main offers.
- "Today only!" on the lip sale gives real, credible urgency, and the hero CTA ("SHOP NOW") is impossible to miss.
- Consistent "NEW / ONLY AT SEPHORA / CLEAN" badge system across the Trending Picks grid makes scanning fast even in a long email.

## What's weak

- Subject promises hair, hero delivers lip sale — the Dreamriser Hair Mousse the subject is clearly teasing doesn't appear until several scrolls in, after an unrelated MUFE promo and a skincare before/after block.
- No discount or urgency on the actual hair product — the Dreamriser Mousse is presented at full price ($30) with no offer, while the unrelated lip sale gets the "50% off, today only" treatment.
- "Trending Picks for You" reads as a generic catch-all (lip liner, sunscreen, body mist, mascara, perfume) rather than a curated hair follow-through, diluting the hair narrative further.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the email despite a very long product scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Re-sequence the hero: if the subject is about hair, lead with the Dreamriser Mousse launch and give it the promo treatment (intro discount, "first to try it" urgency) instead of burying it under an unrelated lip sale.
- 2. Either drop the lip sale from this send or rewrite the subject to reflect it — don't promise hair and open with lips.
- 3. Add one review/rating snippet or "X,XXX loves this" proof point near the Dreamriser Mousse to build trust in a brand-new product.
- 4. Tighten "Trending Picks for You" to genuinely hair-adjacent or complementary items if the send is meant to be hair-focused, rather than a full-category grab bag.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your hair's new best friend just dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `NEW: 48-hour volume, only at Sephora`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the Dreamriser Mousse — plus today's lip sale, up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The volumizing mousse everyone's asking about (and a lip deal you'll want too)`
