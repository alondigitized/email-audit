# Save up to 50% on select lip must-haves ⭐
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic bait-and-switch send. The subject line promises "up to 50% on select lip must-haves" (plural — implying a broad lip sale), but the only discount anywhere in the render is a single "30% off select Charlotte Tilbury" banner tied to National Lip Day — a 20-point gap between promised and delivered savings, and a single-brand offer standing in for "must-haves." Everything below that top banner is an unrelated new-arrivals showcase spanning foundation, bronzer, blush, contour, and highlighter, with only two of the eight gridded products (Hourglass lip balm, Givenchy lip gloss) even being lip items — and none of them discounted.
- This is the second consecutive send with this exact structural flaw: the 7/25 "This is what your hair wants" email scored 8/10 for the same problem — a subject-promised theme that gets abandoned two sections in for a generic showcase. Doing it twice in two days with an even bigger promised-vs-delivered gap (50%→30%) is a pattern worth flagging to the team, not a one-off.
- The execution itself is clean — no render bugs, consistent branding, clear CTAs, loyalty tiers visible — so this isn't a production problem. It's a copy/offer-alignment problem that will erode trust with repeat opens if subscribers keep clicking in expecting the promised deal and finding something smaller and less relevant.

## What's working

- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent Sephora branding.
- The 30% Charlotte Tilbury banner is the first thing seen, with a clear "Today only!" deadline that creates real urgency.
- Loyalty/rewards signals are prominent — Insider points counter up top, full Beauty Insider tier chart (Insider/VIB/Rouge) in the footer.
- "NEW," "ONLY AT SEPHORA," and "CLEAN" badges give the product grid useful, scannable context.
- Prices are shown on every gridded product, which helps browsing intent even without a discount.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "50%" off; the actual visible offer is 30% off — a direct numeric mismatch a reader will notice within seconds.
- Subject promises "lip must-haves" (plural, sale-wide); only 2 of 8 featured new-arrival products are lip products, and none of them are discounted.
- The hero section ("You Deserve to Treat Yourself") completely drops the lip framing and pivots to a generic multi-category new-arrivals showcase.
- The email drifts further off-theme by the bottom third (hair care, skincare, Dyson styling tools) with zero connective tissue back to the subject's lip-sale promise.
- No social proof (star ratings, review counts) anywhere in the render to support the "must-haves" framing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject match the actual hero content — either lead with the real offer ("30% off Charlotte Tilbury") or rebuild the top banner into an actual multi-brand lip sale that justifies "50% on select lip must-haves."
- 2. Cut the "You Deserve to Treat Yourself" hero's disconnect — if the send is themed around lip products, keep the hero and grid lip-focused instead of pivoting into foundation/bronzer/contour.
- 3. Swap the generic "SHOP NOW" CTA copy on the top banner for something offer-specific, e.g. "Shop 30% Off Lip."
- 4. Add at least one lip-specific price/comparison callout (e.g., strike-through pricing) so the "50%" claim has a visible anchor somewhere in the body.
- 5. Replace the boilerplate shipping-perks preview text with a line that actually names the lip offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off Charlotte Tilbury — today only, for National Lip Day`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your lip kit, refreshed: new drops + 30% off Tilbury`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Markdowns on your fave lip picks — plus new arrivals across the store`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `National Lip Day deals end tonight. Here's what's new too.`
