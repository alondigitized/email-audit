# New beauty to obsess over 😍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure product-launch grid dressed up as "New beauty to obsess over" — and it delivers exactly what it promises, which is rare in this run. A top banner leads with "GET AK POINTS® ON ALL KOREAN BEAUTY" plus a "50% off Hair Faves" callout, then the hero drops into "HOT & NEW BEAUTY" and a nine-item vertical stack of new launches (Libre Vanille fragrance, Obart primer, Dior tinted moisturizer, Innisfree, M.A.C, Oribe, Lys Beauty, Sol de Janeiro, Unite Hair). It's essentially a scrollable catalog page, not a single offer.
- The subject sets expectations correctly — this is a "new arrivals" email, not a discount chase — and the layout mostly delivers, though it buries two real discounts (Korean beauty points, 50% off hair) above a wall of new-product tiles with no urgency or differentiation between them. Compared to 2026-09-23's "Hair Daily Deals" send, which had one clean, coherent offer, this one trades coherence for volume: nine products with no hierarchy telling you which one actually matters.
- Verdict: honest and on-brand, but it's a scroll-fest with two buried offers and zero standout hero — solid mid-tier send, not a top performer.

## What's working

- Subject and hero both say "new beauty" and the layout backs it up immediately with "HOT & NEW BEAUTY."
- Each product tile is clean — image, name, brand badge (NEW / ONLY AT SEPHORA / LIMITED EDITION), one-line description, and a "SHOP NOW" CTA, no overlapping text or broken renders.
- "Clean at Sephora" badges are visibly placed on qualifying products (Obart, Innisfree, Lys Beauty), giving ingredient-conscious shoppers a real filter.
- Two concrete offers are visible at the very top: Korean beauty points event and 50% off hair, both with clear CTAs.

## What's weak

- Nine near-equal product tiles with no visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye which is the "must-click" item.
- The two actual discounts (points event, 50% off hair) are crammed into a single top banner and get overshadowed almost instantly by the new-arrivals grid.
- No prices are shown for several items until deep in the tile copy (e.g., Libre Vanille "from $155"), making price comparison a scroll-and-hunt exercise.
- No social proof, ratings, or "bestseller" signal anywhere to help differentiate nine unranked new items.
- Generic subject/hero combo ("New beauty to obsess over" / "Hot & New Beauty") repeats language Sephora uses constantly for arrivals sends — no specific hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero product (e.g., Libre Vanille — it's the most premium/novel item) and give it a dedicated full-width hero block with price and a distinct CTA, then move the rest into a secondary "also new" grid — right now nothing wins the eye.
- 2. Split the top banner into two separate, sequential offer blocks (Korean beauty points, then hair sale) so each gets its own visual moment instead of sharing one banner.
- 3. Add price to every tile up front, not buried mid-copy — shoppers scan tiles fast and price is often the deciding factor.
- 4. Add a "bestseller" or "editor's pick" tag to at least one item in the grid to create a hierarchy signal among nine flat entries.
