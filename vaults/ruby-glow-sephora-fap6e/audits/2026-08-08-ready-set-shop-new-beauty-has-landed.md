# Ready, set, shop—new beauty has landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora running a straight new-arrivals catalog — "Just Dropped & Only Here" up top, then six brand sections back to back (Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez, Josie Maran, Patrick Ta, Innisfree, Marc Jacobs Beauty, Burberry) before closing with a points promo, a skincare teaser, and a lip roundup. Production is clean: consistent badges, real prices, "Clean at Sephora" / "Planet Aware" trust marks. But there's no organizing narrative here — unlike the 8/6 body-care send (9.00), which grouped products into a routine (Wash, Hydrate, Stay Fresh, Exfoliate), this is just brand-after-brand in a flat list, closer in caliber to the 8/4 "So hot right now" and 7/31 "Hot on social" roundups (both 8.00).
- The subject and every CTA are generic ("new beauty has landed," "Shop Now" x6), and there isn't a single model or demographic cue anywhere — it's all flat-lay product photography. That's fine for a browse-heavy shopper but does nothing to sharpen targeting or urgency.
- Solid, on-brand, well-organized — but undifferentiated versus this brand's stronger recent sends. A good execution of a middling email concept.

## What's working

- Hero "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" with a clear "SHOP NOW" button is visible immediately, no scroll required.
- Each brand section is self-contained — hero shot, product name, price, "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" badge — easy to scan brand by brand.
- Clean/sustainability badges (Planet Aware, Clean at Sephora) are placed right where relevant, reinforcing values this audience cares about.
- Real, specific pricing shown throughout ($16–$36, $30–$78, etc.) so shoppers know cost before clicking.
- Secondary modules (4X points on Pureology with dated window, Just-Dropped Skincare, Lip's Latest Lineup) add variety at the bottom without crowding the main new-arrivals block.

## What's weak

- Zero models or demographic signals anywhere in the email — every image is a flat-lay product shot, so there's no read on who this is targeted at.
- Subject and hero copy are generic ("new beauty has landed," "Meet your next haul") with no named product or brand to hook a scanning reader.
- Every single CTA button reads "Shop Now" — six identical buttons with no section-specific copy.
- No reviews, star ratings, or social proof anywhere, unlike the trending-picks sends this brand has run well before.
- Six-plus brand sections stacked vertically get repetitive by the bottom — the email is long before it reaches the points/skincare/lip modules.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the standout drop in the subject line — Rare Beauty x Selena Gomez is the most newsworthy "only at Sephora" launch here; lead with it instead of generic "new beauty."
- 2. Vary CTA copy per section ("Shop Rare Beauty," "Shop Josie Maran") instead of repeating "Shop Now" six times.
- 3. Rewrite the preview text to tease the actual new-arrival lineup instead of the standard shipping/delivery disclaimer, which reads as boilerplate.
- 4. Trim to the top 3–4 drops with a "See all new arrivals" link at the end, rather than a full six-brand wall — cuts scroll fatigue.
- 5. Add a star rating or review count to at least the hero product for social proof, which this send has none of.
