# Beauty’s most wanted just landed 🤩
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This one buries its own subject line. "Beauty's most wanted just landed 🤩" promises a new-arrival moment, but the actual hero at the top is the same "50% off Select Beauty" pink-gradient banner this brand has run all week (2026-09-12, 2026-09-14, 2026-09-15). The "Beauty's Biggest Hits" section with the Charlotte Tilbury bronzer duo is buried second, and the genuinely new item — the Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Blur Loose Setting Powder — doesn't show up until a third scroll, in a section that's visually quiet compared to the discount banner above it.
- Once you get there, the Charlotte Tilbury launch block is actually well done: clear "NEW FROM" framing, one hero product, one price, one CTA. But it's competing with a discount promo, a "hits" teaser, an 8-item grid dump, a haircare subscribe offer, a points multiplier, and a Kérastase holiday callout — six distinct offers in one email. This is a catalog dressed as an announcement.
- Judgement: mediocre execution of a fine idea. The launch narrative is real but it's the fourth priority in the layout, not the first.

## What's working

- The Charlotte Tilbury setting powder block has a clean single-product hero, a specific claim ("long-lasting, airbrushed, blurred look"), and one price ($50) with one CTA.
- No render bugs — images load, text is legible, no overlapping elements.
- The 8-item product grid is well-organized with consistent name/price formatting.
- "TODAY ONLY" and "24 HOURS ONLY" urgency badges are visually distinct (pink and black stamps) and easy to spot at a glance.

## What's weak

- Subject says "just landed" but the actual hero banner at the top is the recycled 50% off Select Beauty promo, not the new-arrival content — the same bait-and-switch pattern flagged in the 2026-09-14 K18 email.
- Six competing offers (50% off beauty, "Biggest Hits," CT launch, 8-item grid, 15% off haircare, 5X points, Kérastase sets) with no visual hierarchy telling the eye where to land first.
- "Beauty's Biggest Hits — They've earned their spot" is vague filler copy that doesn't say what's actually in the section.
- The named "most wanted" hero product (setting powder) is three scrolls deep, well below the fold on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject: lead with the Charlotte Tilbury setting powder as the actual "most wanted" arrival, not the recycled 50% off banner — this is the single highest-impact fix given the brand's pattern of subject/hero mismatch.
- 2. Cut the email to two sections max: the launch hero + the product grid. Move the 50% off banner, haircare subscribe offer, points multiplier, and Kérastase sets into a separate send — this is a catalog, not an announcement.
- 3. Replace "They've earned their spot" with copy naming what's in the grid (e.g., "This week's most-added items").
- 4. Give the product grid a "most wanted" framing tied to actual signal (bestseller badges, review counts) rather than a generic name.
