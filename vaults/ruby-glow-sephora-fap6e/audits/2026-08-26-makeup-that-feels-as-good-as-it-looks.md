# Makeup that feels as good as it looks 🌱
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "Clean at Sephora" catalog send — another App Fest banner stacked on top of a long vertical grid of "clean" beauty products with almost no narrative thread connecting them. The "Clean at Sephora" hero with the sky/cloud background and green "clean" seal badge is a nice visual break from the flat catalog grids this brand history keeps repeating (see 8/23's "Psst, something new just arrived" at 5/10), but it collapses into the exact same problem by the second screen: a scroll of 12+ individual products (Kosas, Milk, Aora Mexico, Westman Atelier, Lawless, Kaja, Caliray, Fenty...) each with a tiny "SHOP NOW" or no CTA at all, no single hero product to click.
- The subject line "Makeup that feels as good as it looks 🌱" is a genuinely good, differentiated line for this brand — clean-beauty positioning is a real category and the leaf emoji signals it without shouting. But the email itself buries that promise under an App Fest ad above the fold and then a wall of undifferentiated SKUs below. There's no single clear CTA a subscriber lands on.
- Compared to 8/21's KJH.brand launch (7/10, single hero product with a clean narrative), this is a step back toward the catalog-dump pattern. Judgement: functional but unfocused — 5/10 territory.

## What's working

- The "Clean at Sephora" hero section has real visual identity — sky background, green clean-seal badge, clear "SHOP NOW" button.
- Subject line ties directly to a real content pillar (clean ingredients) rather than another generic "new arrivals" grab-bag.
- Two NEW/EXCLUSIVE lip products (M.PH by Mary Phillips, Haus Labs by Lady Gaga) are called out with prices right under the hero, giving at least one clear entry point.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, prices and badges are legible throughout.

## What's weak

- App Fest promo banner sits above the "Clean at Sephora" hero, competing with and diluting the subject line's promise before the reader even gets to it.
- After the hero, the email reverts to a dense 2-column grid of 10+ products with no organizing narrative — no way to tell why these particular items were chosen together beyond "clean."
- Most grid items have no visible CTA button at all, just a product photo, name, and price — weak, ambiguous click paths.
- "Spot it. Shop it." section and "New from Summer Fridays" tacked on at the bottom feel like separate emails glued together, further diluting the clean-beauty theme.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the App Fest banner from this send entirely (it's competing content, not complementary — it already gets its own dedicated sends per the 8/17 and 8/24 history) and let "Clean at Sephora" own the top of the email.
- 2. Reduce the product grid from 10+ SKUs to a curated 4-6 "must-haves," each with a visible "Shop Now" CTA — the current wall-of-products format has been repeated too often (8/23) and consistently underperforms.
- 3. Give the two NEW/EXCLUSIVE lip launches (Mary Phillips, Haus Labs) their own mini hero block instead of burying them as text under the main image — they're the most differentiated, clickable items in the email.
- 4. Drop or relocate "Spot it. Shop it." and "Summer Fridays" to a separate send — they break the clean-beauty theme and read as filler.
