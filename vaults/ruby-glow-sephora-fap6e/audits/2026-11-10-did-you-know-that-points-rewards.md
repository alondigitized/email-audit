# Did you know that points = rewards?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the monthly Beauty Insider Rewards Bazaar refresh, and it works as a catalog, not as a campaign. The hero says "November rewards are here!" over a dark teal background with product shots and a SHOP NOW button. Below it, a Summer Fridays brand spotlight and a "750+ Point Rewards" grid show point costs and remaining stock ("40 coming soon", "32 available now", "16 available now").
- The scarcity counts are the best part, and the point-cost labels are clear. But the email has no money offer and no deadline, and it never says how many points the reader has. The subject, "Did you know that points = rewards?", is a lesson in how the program works. Anyone subscribed to Sephora already knows that. The hero copy ("Don't wait, they go fast!") is generic.
- The send is also long and dense. It runs from the hero through a brand spotlight, a nine-item grid, a gift-guide strip, a row of promo bars and a wall of fine print. That is a lot to scroll through for a "shop now" button that appears once, at the top. On Nov 6 Sephora sent the same Bazaar concept as "Use your 0 points on a 250-point bundle" (6/10). This send repeats it and swaps in a weaker, more generic subject. I'd put it at a middling 5.

## What's working

- Each reward shows a point cost and remaining stock (e.g. "24 available now", "500 available now"), which creates credible scarcity.
- The Summer Fridays spotlight leads with a brand people recognize and shows tote-bag bundles at 2,000 to 7,000 points.
- The "750+ Point Rewards" grid has a consistent layout with product name, description and cost under each item, so it is easy to scan.
- The hero sets a seasonal, current "November" frame, and the SHOP NOW button is clear and sits at the top.
- The bottom has useful member utilities, such as the credit card, the app and free same-day delivery.

## What's weak

- There is no monetary offer, such as a % off or free shipping, and no deadline. The reader gets no reason to act today.
- The subject is a rhetorical question that explains the program to people already enrolled.
- The email is very long. A single CTA at the top, plus dense small-type descriptions, leaves most of the content without a clear button.
- The reader's own points balance isn't visible, so they can't tell what they can afford.
- The hero body copy is small and pale on the dark background, and the text legal block under the button is tiny and hard to read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Personalize the hero with the reader's balance and what it buys: "You have X points: here's what they unlock." Show 3 to 4 rewards they can already afford.
- 2. Add a real deadline to the rewards ("Bazaar rewards end 11/30" or "while supplies last, 6 left"), and show the same urgency in the subject.
- 3. Cut the grid to 4 to 6 items and give each its own "Redeem" button. Move the legal text out of the hero.
- 4. Lead with the strongest-value reward, such as a high-value set at 750 points, instead of a long tote-bag spotlight.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your November rewards are live (some have 6 left)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend your points: Summer Fridays from 2,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Redeem for Summer Fridays, Too Faced and more. Stock is limited.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `750-point rewards start at just 24 left. See what you can claim.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **[WARN] Missing alt text** on the `app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF…` image. This is the `display:none` tracking pixel, so the real accessibility impact is negligible. Adding `alt=""` would still clear the flag.
- **Fixed-width layout vs. mobile:** the container is `<table width="700" … style="min-width:700px; width:700px">`, and the `@media (max-width:480px)` block only adjusts font and image sizes. The `min-width:700px` may force horizontal scroll or zoom-out on narrow clients that honor it. The viewport meta also sets `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks pinch-zoom (an accessibility concern).
- **Malformed CSS:** an HTML comment `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size {…} <![endif]-->` sits inside the `@media` block in `<style>`. Conditional comments don't work there, so the MSO-specific sizing rule is likely ignored and could break parsing of the rules after it in some clients.
- **Invalid robots value:** `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` should be `noindex`. This is low impact for email.
- **Nested hidden preheader divs:** the nested `display:none` div contains a long `&zwnj;&nbsp;` padding string. This is a standard technique and is fine.
