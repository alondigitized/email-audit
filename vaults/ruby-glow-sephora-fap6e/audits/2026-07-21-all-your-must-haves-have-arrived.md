# All your must-haves have arrived
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals editorial email — Sephora's "just dropped & only here" format stacked with six or more brand vignettes. The hero headline lands with exclusivity ("JUST DROPPED / & ONLY HERE"), and the 4X points banner at top maintains loyalty continuity from the July 19 send. But the subject — "All your must-haves have arrived" — is the weakest link: a generic claim that makes no promise and names no brand. The email lives and dies by whether the reader already loves one of the featured names.
- The body is a brand parade: Id Beauty, Jo Malone London, A Crown Affair, Touchland, what appears to be a Nars/Phillips collab, and more. Each section has its own CTA, but there's no single anchoring offer — no discount, no deadline, no clear reason to act today rather than tomorrow. The star ratings visible under the hero product are the only social proof. For Sephora's cadence (this is at least the fifth send in six days), a pure discovery send without a deal hook is a harder sell.
- Compared to the July 19 "4X Points on ALL Hair Care" send, which put a code and deadline front and center, this one asks readers to be excited about newness alone. That works for a highly engaged loyalist segment; it doesn't move the needle for the casual subscriber.

## What's working

- **Exclusivity hook is strong** — "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" sets a first-to-market tone that Sephora owns well.
- **4X points banner at top** — maintains the loyalty thread from July 19 without requiring a separate email.
- **Star ratings on the hero product** — the only social proof in the email, and it's placed where the eye lands first.
- **Brand diversity** — fragrance, hair, makeup, skincare covered in one scroll, useful for multi-category shoppers.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is inert** — "All your must-haves have arrived" could be from any retailer, any category, any day; it earns no open on its own.
- **No concrete offer** — no % off, no GWP, no BOGO; 4X points is a passive benefit, not an action trigger.
- **Six-plus brand blocks with no hierarchy** — the stack gives equal weight to every brand; nothing compels the reader to stop scrolling.
- **Zero urgency** — "just dropped" implies availability, not scarcity; nothing creates a reason to click now.
- **Brand names are hard to read at email width** — the Id Beauty / "RU CGANNA" block in particular is small and cramped.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name a brand or product** — "Jo Malone just dropped at Sephora" or "New: A Crown Affair, Id Beauty + more" gives the reader something to latch onto before they even open.
- 2. **Add a scarcity or access hook** — "First 48 hours: early access for Insiders" converts a discovery email into an event.
- 3. **Cut the brand stack to 3, give each more room** — deeper storytelling on fewer drops beats a listicle of thumbnails; let Jo Malone and one other hero breathe with a product close-up and one differentiating detail.
- 4. **Anchor the 4X points to new arrivals specifically** — "Earn 4X on everything that just dropped" ties the existing loyalty offer to this email's creative, making the points feel earned rather than ambient.
- 5. **Preheader should name a brand** — whatever the preheader currently says, it should tease the most recognizable name in the drop.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** applied to a broad selector list including `body`, `div`, `a`, `span`, `td`. This disables OS-level font scaling for accessibility users on WebKit.
