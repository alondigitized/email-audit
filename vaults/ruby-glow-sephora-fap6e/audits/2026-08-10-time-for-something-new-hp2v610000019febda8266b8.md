# Time for something new 🛍️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "new arrivals" grid again — the same beat as 8/8's "Ready, set, shop—new beauty has landed," just with a different hero treatment. The top offers a $10 bonus card with a $75+ purchase and a trial-size mascara GWP, then the "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" hero drops into a flat product grid of eight NEW-tagged items across totally unrelated categories (mascara, lip tint, eyeshadow, jelly wax cream, body butter, hair mousse, perfume, moisturizer).
- Compared to the 8/8 send, which organized new arrivals by brand section, this one skips straight to an undifferentiated product wall — no organizing principle beyond "new." It reads more like a catalog dump than a curated story, closer to the weaker end of Sephora's recent output than to the body-care (9.00) or Beauty Insider Celebration (9.00) sends that scored highest.
- Functionally sound, on-brand, no render bugs — but this is a mid-tier send. The subject and hero are vague ("something new") next to sharper recent subject lines like "PSA 📣 Get 4X points" or "Discover the art of body care."

## What's working

- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks across a long scroll.
- $10 bonus card offer and mascara trial-size GWP are both clearly stated with concrete terms up top.
- Each grid item has a visible "NEW" tag plus differentiators (Clean, Only at Sephora, Limited Edition, Online Only) that add useful shopping context.
- Bottom modules (App Early Access, Try It Love It, Fragrance Worth the Hype) add variety instead of repeating the same grid format.
- Beauty Insider tier bar at the footer reinforces loyalty status visibility.

## What's weak

- Hero copy "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" is generic and doesn't name a single specific product or category — weaker than the brand's own recent subject lines.
- The eight-item product grid has no organizing logic (skincare, makeup, hair, fragrance all mixed together) — it reads as a catalog dump rather than a curated pick, a step down from 8/8's brand-by-brand structure.
- Product photography is inconsistent in framing/lighting from tile to tile, giving the grid a slightly assembled-not-designed look.
- This is a near-repeat of the 8/8 "new arrivals" concept only 2 days later — cadence risk for a subscriber who already saw that format.
- No pricing hierarchy or bestseller/social proof signals anywhere in the grid to help prioritize which "new" item matters most.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" hero with one hero product story (like the K18 or body-care sends) instead of an 8-item flat grid — pick the strongest launch and lead with it.
- 2. Group the grid by category (Makeup / Skincare / Hair / Fragrance) instead of interleaving, so shoppers can jump to their focus area.
- 3. Space this out from the 8/8 new-arrivals send — back-to-back near-identical formats risk fatigue; alternate with loyalty or single-brand takeovers as recent sends have done.
- 4. Add a bestseller or review-count badge to at least the top 2-3 items to create prioritization cues.
