# K18 hair care FTW 🏆🧬
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a single-brand takeover for K18 — a step up from a flat discount grid, built around before/after proof shots and a real hero launch (FutureIQ Hair Longevity Serum, "up to 19,000 more hairs and 70% fewer grays"). It reads like the 8/6 body-care send (9.00) in structure — routine-style storytelling instead of coupon-stacking — and the free trial-size mask with promo code up top is a genuinely concrete offer, not vague "shop now" filler.
- Where it stumbles is stacking: free trial-size GWP, then a full-price $120 serum section, then a $75 mask section, then a 10%-off-with-subscribe banner, then a totally unrelated Clarins/BaubleBar dual-promo block, then a "Beauty Must-Haves" grab bag — five different offers in one email with no single clear hierarchy. The before/after photography does real work (especially the FutureIQ scalp shots), but the email tries to be a K18 launch, a loyalty event, and a general merch push all at once.
- Solid execution on individual blocks, muddled overall structure. Call it a 7/10 send — better than an average discount blast, held back by too many competing CTAs.

## What's working

- Clear, credible before/after scalp photography for the FutureIQ serum with a specific clinical claim ("19,000 more hairs and 70% fewer grays" from a 12-week study) — strong, non-generic social proof.
- Free K18 Trial-Size Mask GWP with a visible promo code (K18SERUM) and a clean "$40+ purchase" threshold is a concrete, honest-feeling offer right at the top.
- "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" badges and BEFORE/AFTER hair-mask visuals (four-minute repair claim) reinforce a credible ingredient/efficacy story, not just a discount pitch.
- Consistent K18 brand color and biotech visual language (holographic bubbles, clinical typography) makes the hero section feel premium and on-brand.

## What's weak

- Too many offers competing for attention: free GWP, $120 serum, $75 mask, 10%-off-subscribe, 4X points on Clarins, and a BaubleBar mystery reward all in one scroll — no single dominant CTA.
- The Clarins/BaubleBar "Spot it. Shop it." block is a jarring category break in the middle of what's otherwise a cohesive K18 story — it dilutes the hair-care narrative.
- Both hero products ($120 and $75) are full-price with no launch discount, which undercuts the urgency of the free-mask offer sitting right above them.
- "Beauty Must-Haves" footer block (NARS, LYS, REFY) feels bolted on and has no connection to the hair-care theme — a generic filler section other Sephora sends in this history have avoided.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half: keep the K18 story (GWP + FutureIQ + Mask) as its own focused send, and move Clarins 4X points / BaubleBar reward / Beauty Must-Haves into a separate multi-brand email — this is the single biggest lift since it removes the competing CTAs.
- 2. Lead with the free trial-size GWP as the hero, since it's the only single actionable offer, and push the $120/$75 full-price sections below it with the 10%-subscribe incentive folded into each, not as a separate banner.
- 3. Add a "why now" hook to the subject/hero (e.g., new launch, limited GWP quantity) — right now nothing signals urgency beyond "while supplies last" buried lower.
- 4. Tighten to one CTA color/style per section so the eye has a clear path instead of five equally-weighted "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: FutureIQ hair serum — 19,000 more hairs, clinically shown`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free K18 Trial Mask + our new hair-growth serum`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 4X points on Clarins, ends 8/12`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grayer, thinner hair? This 12-week study says otherwise.`
