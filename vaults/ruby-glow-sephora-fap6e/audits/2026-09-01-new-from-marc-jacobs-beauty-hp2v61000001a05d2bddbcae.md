# New from Marc Jacobs Beauty 💌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a single-brand launch email done reasonably well — a "Marc Jacobs Beauty is now online AND in store" announcement with a free-gift hook up top ("Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift — stock up... to snag this puffy makeup bag"), followed by a clean, well-organized grid of the new product line (concealer, eyeshadow, lipstick, bronzer, blush stick, eyeliner, mascara). Unlike several recent Sephora sends in this brand history (8/29, 8/31 bait-and-switch 50%-off subjects), the subject here matches the body almost exactly — "New from Marc Jacobs Beauty" and the hero delivers exactly that. That's a rare moment of subject/body honesty for this sender.
- Where it falls down is the back half: after a full scroll of MJ Beauty products, it pivots hard into unrelated cross-sell (Ole Henriksen app-early-access, Chanel fragrance, a 50% off "Select Beauty Today Only" banner, a hair tool). That tail dilutes an otherwise focused single-brand story and buries a real, time-bound offer (the 50% off) as an afterthought rather than a headline.
- Verdict: a solid, honest brand-launch email let down by a bolted-on generic promo tail — worth sending, but the free-gift CTA and the 50%-off banner are both stronger hooks than what's leading the subject/preview.

## What's working

- Subject and hero are aligned — "New from Marc Jacobs Beauty" delivers exactly the MJ Beauty launch shown, unlike the bait-and-switch pattern seen on 8/29 and 8/31.
- Free-gift offer is concrete and visible immediately below the utility bar ("Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift," "MJGIFT" code visible on the badge, "SHOP TO REDEEM" button).
- Clean, legible product grid — each of the 7 items has a distinct name, a one-line benefit description, and a matching product photo with no overlapping text or broken images.
- One product is called out with real specificity: "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA — Nude Awakening Hydrating Medium Coverage Concealer, $36" — actual price and exclusivity, not vague copy.

## What's weak

- The email is very long and loses its single-brand focus in the back third — Ole Henriksen, Chanel fragrance, and a generic "50% off Select Beauty" banner all compete for attention after 7 MJ product tiles.
- Only one of seven MJ products (the concealer) gets a price; the rest ("Born Star Eyeshadow," "Heart on Lipstick," etc.) have no price shown, so there's nothing to click toward as a specific purchase decision.
- The 50% off banner — the single strongest offer in the email — is visually the smallest, least prominent block, positioned last, right before the footer.
- No social proof or reviews anywhere in the render, despite this being framed as a new-launch discovery email where trust signals matter most.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the cross-sell tail (Ole Henriksen / Chanel / 50% banner) or move it to a separate send — a "new brand launch" email performs better as a focused story, and diluting it with unrelated promos undercuts the MJ narrative this email otherwise builds well.
- 2. Add prices to all seven MJ product tiles, not just the concealer — shoppers can't decide to click without knowing the price point.
- 3. If the 50% off Select Beauty offer stays in this email, promote it into the hero or a secondary banner near the top — right now the strongest discount in the email is also the most buried.
- 4. Add at least one review/rating snippet under the hero product (concealer) to build trust for a brand-new line shoppers haven't tried.
