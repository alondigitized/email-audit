# Obsessed? Same.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- "Obsessed? Same." is Sephora's third editorial-mode send in five days — following the "These picks are everywhere" social-proof send on July 11 and the "Summer's Hottest Beauty" reset on July 8. The creative throughline is clear: viral/trending products, HOT ON SOCIAL framing, editorial voice instead of promo urgency. The "Going Viral" hero is a natural extension of the arc and the casual subject line lands well as a conversational beat.
- The problem is structural: there's no hard offer driving the click. The only promotional hook in the whole email is a free AESTURA trial size at the top banner (a "Shop to Redeem" loyalty mechanic), which is easy to skip past. The body is a content grid — "You May Also Like," split-column "Hair's Latest Lineup / Score a Sephora Lip," and a Harlem Perfume Co. spotlight — with no price reduction or time-bounded incentive anchoring the scroll. Editorial sends work when they're interspersed with offer sends; three in a row blunts the pull.
- Bottom line: strong voice, consistent creative arc, but thin conversion mechanics. This is a brand-building send, not a revenue-driving one.

## What's working

- **"Going Viral" hero copy** is a clean continuation of the social-proof arc — feels timely and consistent with the persona Sephora has built this week.
- **HOT ON SOCIAL badge** adds a credible third-party signal right on the hero product, not buried below the fold.
- **AESTURA free trial promo banner** sits at the top of the email and gives loyalty members a tangible reason to click early.
- **Split-section layout** (Hair / Lip) breaks the scroll usefully, letting different intent signals find their own entry point.
- **Beauty Insider footer** reinforces member status and brand trust on close.

## What's weak

- **Subject "Obsessed? Same." gives zero product or category context** — cute voice but completely non-specific; someone glancing at their inbox has no idea what they'd be obsessed with.
- **No time-bounded offer in the body** — the only promo hook is the thin trial-size banner; no discount, no deadline, nothing to force the click.
- **Hero product identity is unclear at a glance** — the "Going Viral" headline is large but the actual featured product gets visually buried under the text treatment before the "You May Also Like" grid overwhelms it.
- **"You May Also Like" grid diffuses attention** — five or more products at similar visual weight with no ranked editorial hierarchy makes nothing feel urgent or special.
- **Three editorial sends in a row** with no hard offer creates cadence fatigue; there's nothing separating this from July 11 and July 8 in terms of ask.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer to the body** — even "Members get early access" or a product bundle with a point-multiplier would give the click a reason. Three editorial sends with no offer is starting to train subscribers that Sephora emails are browsing, not buying.
- 2. **Name the hero product in the subject** — "Obsessed? Same. (RHODE just went viral again)" or similar collapses subject vagueness without losing the voice.
- 3. **Elevate one hero product above the grid** — pick the single #1 viral item, give it a full-bleed moment before scrolling into the grid. Right now the grid undercuts whatever product the hero was meant to feature.
- 4. **Tighten the "You May Also Like" section** — 3 products with one-line callouts beats 5 products with identical visual weight.
- 5. **Move Beauty Insider messaging earlier** — right now it's footer-only; a mid-email "Earn 2X points on these picks this weekend" line would turn this into a functional member send.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `HOT ON SOCIAL: the one thing your routine is missing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what's trending + earn points on every pick this weekend`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disabling pinch-zoom on mobile fails WCAG SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied universally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Blanket suppression of OS-level text scaling overrides user accessibility preferences across all elements. Standard practice is `100%` (allow scaling) rather than `none`.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value). Screen readers cannot infer document language for correct pronunciation.

**Duplicate CSS rules for `.show` and `.appear`**
```css
*[class=show], .show { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
```
`display: block!important` immediately overridden by `display: table!important` in the same rule. The `block` declarations are dead code. No rendering breakage, but indicates a copy-paste artifact.

**`robots` meta value uses a space**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Correct value is `noindex` (no space). Most crawlers handle both, but the space-separated form is non-standard and may not be honored by all parsers.

**Layout tables lack `role="presentation"`** *(inferred — body truncated)*
Table-based email layouts should carry `role="presentation"` so screen readers skip structural markup. Cannot confirm body table tags without full source.
