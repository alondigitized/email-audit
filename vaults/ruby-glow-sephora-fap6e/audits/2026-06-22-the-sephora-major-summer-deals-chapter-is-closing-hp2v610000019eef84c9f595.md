# The Sephora Major Summer Deals chapter is closing ⏲️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a closing-urgency send for Sephora's Major Summer Deals campaign, which launched June 11. The subject nails the narrative bookend — "chapter is closing" echoes the "STARTS TODAY" launch language — and the timer emoji earns its place. Open it, though, and the editorial coherence breaks: what was sold as a sale-deadline email pivots hard into an app-download push, with the "App Exclusive / 24-Hour Early Access" block consuming more visual real estate than the sale hero itself. This isn't subtle cross-sell placement — it's the dominant creative element, and the "DOWNLOAD NOW" button is the most prominent CTA on screen.
- The product grid exists but is tiny and low-resolution at email scale. Marc Jacobs Beauty Is Back lives three sections down with no setup, the BeautyINSIDER section feels bolted on, and the Same-Day Delivery + 500 Bonus Points split panel is another unconsolidated message. That's four distinct asks in one send. Compared to the June 20 "Your beauty wins are turning up the heat" digest (which earned a 5/10 for doing exactly this), this version has a stronger hook but the same structural problem.
- The app push is clearly intentional — sender name is "Sephora App Early Access" — but framing it as sale-urgency email and then leading with "download our app" is a mild bait-and-switch on subscribers who opened for the deal.

## What's working

- **"Chapter is closing" subject copy** is genuinely elevated — it respects the subscriber's narrative intelligence and avoids the generic "LAST CHANCE" that every other retailer defaults to.
- **Up to 50% off visible in the hero** without scrolling; the sale offer is right at the top.
- **App Exclusive block is visually clean** — the pink gradient, phone mockup, and "DOWNLOAD NOW" CTA are well-produced and render without bugs.
- **500 Bonus Points + Same-Day Delivery** are legitimately useful value props if you're already a member, even if buried.

## What's weak

- **Primary CTA "DOWNLOAD NOW" does not serve the opening hook.** The reader opened expecting a sale-closing push; the email's largest block asks them to download an app. These are different conversion goals.
- **The product grid is too small** — six thumbnails at thumbnail scale, with no featured hero product, undercuts any product-led click impulse.
- **Marc Jacobs Beauty Is Back is an orphaned module.** No subject-line setup, no narrative transition — it appears out of nowhere and competes for attention.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Six Sephora emails in 11 days; the fifth or sixth send in a series has to do something meaningfully different to earn the open, and this one doesn't clear that bar after the shampoo/conditioner double-send on June 21.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse this to one job.** Either close the sale (lead with a product grid hero, deadline countdown, and "Shop Now" CTA) OR push app download — not both. The split mission dilutes both conversion goals.
- 2. **Give the product grid a hero treatment.** Pick one deal — a specific featured product at a specific price — and make it the visual anchor. The June 11 launch email showed this is possible in their design system.
- 3. **Move the App Exclusive block below the product grid**, or retire it to a footer strip. The app upsell belongs after the subscriber has been reminded why they should want to shop at all.
- 4. **Cut Marc Jacobs entirely** from this send — it belongs in its own email with its own subject line. It's getting lost here and deserves better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends tonight — up to 50% off select beauty ⏲️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: Major Summer Deals close Jun 25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your shortlist won't stay this price much longer.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `App members get 24-hour early access before the sale ends.`

**Issue 1 — User zoom disabled (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html

```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom on iOS/Android. This is a Level AA WCAG failure. Fix: remove `maximum-scale=1` or set it to `5`.

**Issue 2 — Text scaling suppressed globally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;

}
```
`text-size-adjust: none` is applied to 10 element types including `body`. This overrides OS-level accessibility text scaling for low-vision users. The selector set is broader than necessary — limit `text-size-adjust` to layout containers only, not `body` or `span`.
