# Open for our bag edit
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- "Open for our bag edit" is Sephora's editorial content play dressed up with a gift-with-purchase hook — a Charlotte Tilbury feature anchored by a free mascara offer at the top banner. The wordplay is intentional: "open for our bag edit" means both open the email and look inside the bag, which is a nice double meaning that rewards the click. The execution is coherent — the hero concept ("What's In Our Bag?") maps cleanly to the subject, and the Charlotte Tilbury brand carries enough cultural weight to justify the organizing principle.
- The email is visually dense. After the strong Charlotte Tilbury hero block, the scroll reveals a product grid, then a "Spot It. Shop It." lip roundup, then a "Try It. Love It." section, then an oil-balancing skincare push — four distinct content beats that dilute rather than build. The strongest content is at the top; the bottom half feels like a catch-all add-on.
- Overall judgment: a solid editorial send that earns its send with a real hook and a recognized brand partnership. The main drag is scrolling fragmentation — if the CT free-mascara offer were the consistent thread woven through the whole email rather than just the banner, this would land harder.

## What's working

- **Top banner GWP is immediately legible** — "Free Charlotte Tilbury Mascara" + "SHOP TO REDEEM" is a clear value prop before the hero even loads.
- **Hero concept and subject line match** — "WHAT'S IN OUR BAG? / JUST IN FROM CHARLOTTE TILBURY" is a direct payoff on the "bag edit" subject. No bait-and-switch.
- **Charlotte Tilbury editorial imagery** — the hero model photo is glamour-forward and on-brand for CT's aesthetic; it anchors the email in a recognizable identity.
- **"BEST OF LIP" section is well-framed** — after yesterday's "4X Points on All Hair Care" loyalty push, pivoting to a curated lip roundup feels like the right editorial beat.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breaks, text is legible throughout.

## What's weak

- **Four separate content themes after the hero** — product grid → lip roundup → try-it → oil-balancing hydration is too many pivots; the email loses its Charlotte Tilbury throughline halfway down.
- **"Oil-Balancing Hydration" at the bottom feels orphaned** — no connection to the bag edit / Charlotte Tilbury concept; reads like a remnant module dropped in from another campaign.
- **No price or offer details visible on the product thumbnails** — the grid shows product names and brand names but prices are too small to read; it's browsing, not buying.
- **No urgency on the GWP** — the free mascara offer has no visible end date or "while supplies last" framing, which removes the reason to click now vs. later.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Extend the Charlotte Tilbury thread through the whole email.** Cut or reorder the orphaned "Oil-Balancing Hydration" block. Keep the scroll experience anchored in the bag-edit concept — every section should feel like another item pulled from the same bag.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the GWP banner.** Even "Today only" or "Ends Sunday" creates the pull that's currently missing. The 2026-07-16 send ("10% off your $100+ purchase") did this right.
- 3. **Make the CTA in the product grid more specific.** "SHOP NOW" is generic; "Shop Charlotte Tilbury" or "Get the Look" ties the click back to the hero concept.
- 4. **Surface one price in the hero** — even just "starting from $XX" on the CT products would give the conversion-minded subscriber a reason to click instead of just admire.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The CT edit everyone's shopping — plus a free mascara with purchase.`
