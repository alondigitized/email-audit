# Psst, something new just arrived 👀
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a "new arrivals" catalog send — a long vertical scroll of NEW-at-Sephora launches (Tower 28, Drunk Elephant, Chris McMillan, One/Size, Aestura, Dr. Groot, Dolce&Gabbana) stacked one after another with almost no differentiation between them. It's the same structural weakness as 8/16's "The Drop-Everything Edit" and 8/12's near-identical curated grid: a wall of product blocks with no single hero the eye is directed toward. The one genuinely strong idea — a before/after skin photo for Tower 28 foundation — is buried as block #2 instead of leading the email.
- The App Fest banner and 25%-off-select-brands strip up top compete with the "NEW AND ONLY HERE" header for attention, so the email opens with three different offers before you've even seen a product. By the time you reach Dolce&Gabbana seven scrolls down, fatigue has set in. This reads as inventory-dump, not curation — functional but not a strong open-to-click driver.

## What's working

- The before/after skin photo for Tower 28 Beauty Foundation is a genuinely strong, scroll-stopping visual — real proof, not a flat product shot.
- "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" badges are repeated consistently across every block, reinforcing exclusivity as the throughline.
- Prices are visible on every product, which reduces click friction for price-sensitive shoppers.
- The green "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" seal appears on relevant products, a real trust/differentiation signal for ingredient-conscious shoppers.
- Footer loyalty section ("Insider," tier logins, rewards) is present for member context.

## What's weak

- No single hero — eight nearly-equal product blocks in a row means nothing anchors the primary CTA.
- Top of email is cluttered: an App Fest banner, a 25%-off strip, and the "NEW AND ONLY HERE" header all stacked before any product appears.
- Every block uses the identical "SHOP NOW" CTA — zero copy variation, so nothing signals which product is the priority.
- The email is extremely long or a single stitched screenshot — a reader has to scroll through 7+ full product sections before reaching Dolce&Gabbana, well past the point most subscribers disengage.
- Subject ("something new just arrived") promises one surprise item but the body delivers eight unrelated launches — a mismatch between subject framing and actual content.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero — lead with Tower 28 + the before/after photo as the entire top-of-email story, then collapse the other seven launches into a smaller "also new this week" grid below the fold. This fixes the no-hierarchy problem directly.
- 2. Remove or shrink the App Fest banner and 25%-off strip at the very top — they're competing offers that delay the "new arrivals" payoff the subject line promised.
- 3. Vary CTA copy per product ("Shop the Foundation," "Get the Serum") instead of eight identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 4. Shorten the send — 8 full product blocks is catalog-dump territory; cut to 4-5 max and route the rest to a "See all new arrivals" link.

- **Subject Alt B:** `8 new launches, only at Sephora`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real skin, real results — plus 7 more new-in favorites`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From Tower 28 to D&G, this week's freshest drops`
