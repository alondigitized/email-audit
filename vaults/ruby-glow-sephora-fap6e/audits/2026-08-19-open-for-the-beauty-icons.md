# Open for the beauty icons
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "Beauty's Most Wanted" send — a hero-driven launch push for Chanel's Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu fragrance, wrapped in a big catalog grid underneath. It's a step up from the flat catalog sends this brand history keeps flagging (8/13's "All Day. All Night.," 8/15's "The beauty forecast") because there's an actual hero product, a real image, and a specific price — but the subject line, "Open for the beauty icons," undersells all of it with vague curiosity-bait copy that has zero connection to fragrance, Chanel, or the trial-size offer up top.
- The top-of-email trial-size promo (NARS lip balm or Fenty gloss, free with $30 purchase) is buried above a generic "Beauty's Most Wanted" hero banner that has no product in it at all — just moody stock-adjacent copy. The actual named, priced hero (Chanel Coco Mademoiselle) doesn't appear until well into the scroll, after "Shop Now" already sent the reader nowhere specific. Below that it reverts to the familiar 8-product bestseller grid, then App Fest and Beauty Insider event callouts crowd the bottom.
- Net: decent creative assets (real hero model, real product, real price) let down by weak sequencing and a subject line that promises nothing concrete. Middle-of-pack for this brand's history — better than the flat grids, worse than 8/14's rhode Spotwear single-product takeover.

## What's working

- Clear, high-quality hero image of a named perfume (Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu) with visible price range ($162–195).
- Free trial-size offer at the very top is a concrete, specific incentive (NARS or Fenty, free with $30 purchase).
- Product grid below the hero has full specificity — brand, product name, and price on every item (Oribe $49, Shiseido $67, Kérastase $62, etc.).
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images, consistent Sephora black/white/red brand voice throughout.
- "Only At Sephora" and star-rating badges on select grid items add light social proof and exclusivity signal.

## What's weak

- Subject line "Open for the beauty icons" has no connection to any content in the email — not fragrance, not the trial offer, not App Fest.
- "Beauty's Most Wanted" hero banner (the first big visual after the header) has no product shown — it's a text-only teaser that delays the actual payoff (Chanel fragrance) by a full scroll.
- "Shop Now" CTA under the hero banner is generic and points nowhere specific since no product is shown yet.
- Bottom third gets crowded — App Fest, Tower 28 Beauty dibs, Beauty Insider events, and "The Latest Lineup" (Touchland) all compete in a tight space with no clear priority.
- Cadence concern: this is at least the third grid-heavy layout in a week (8/13, 8/15, now 8/19), and the brand history already flags this pattern as the weak mode.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject line to lead with the actual hero — the Chanel fragrance launch is the strongest, most specific asset in this email and the subject should say so, not hide behind vague "icons" language.
- 2. Cut or merge the "Beauty's Most Wanted" placeholder banner into the Chanel hero section so the fragrance image appears immediately after the top trial-size promo — right now the strongest visual is buried two banners deep.
- 3. Make "Shop Now" specific — change it to point straight at the Chanel fragrance rather than a generic catalog landing.
- 4. Simplify the bottom third — pick one of App Fest / Tower 28 / Insider events as the secondary CTA and trim the rest, or this becomes visual noise after an already-long scroll.
- 5. Reduce grid-heavy cadence — vary structure from the last two sends by leaning harder into single-launch takeover format (like 8/14's rhode Spotwear send), which this brand's history rates consistently higher.
