# Just dropped: point of view.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the exact same "50% off Select Beauty, Today Only" banner running on top of an unrelated product story that Sephora has been repeating all week — 8/28, 8/29, 8/31, 9/2 all opened this way, and this send is no different. Below the top banner it pivots entirely to a "point of view." collection launch (PHILR, Urban Decay, The INKEY List don't even match — the banner names those three brands but the entire rest of the email showcases a completely different "founder's pov" prep/snatch/wear routine). The mismatch between banner brands and body brands is a new and worse variant of the bait-and-switch this brand history keeps flagging.
- The "point of view." creative itself is clean — nice black-and-white product grid, clear step-by-step structure (prep it / snatch it / wear it), real testimonial quotes — but it's fighting the banner for attention and the shopper has no idea what's actually on sale. By the time you scroll to "Spot it. Shop it." the email has become a generic catalog scroll (skincare sets, a cologne launch, Olive Young cross-promo) exactly like the 8/30 and 8/23 sends.
- Judgement: mediocre, same pattern as the rest of this week's Sephora cadence — a strong discount headline used purely as an open-driver, disconnected from the actual content, undermining trust in the "Today Only" urgency.

## What's working

- Clear, scannable step structure (prep it / snatch it / wear it) with black headers that break up the long scroll.
- Testimonial quotes ("Every time I use it, my skin looks visibly smoother, firmer, and way more snatched.") add authentic social proof.
- Consistent black-and-white product photography gives the "point of view." collection a premium, cohesive look.
- Individual product tiles have clear pricing and product names, no lorem-ipsum or broken images visible.

## What's weak

- The top banner ("50% off Select Beauty... PHILR, Urban Decay, and The INKEY List") has zero connection to the "point of view." products shown below — none of the featured products appear to be from those three brands, repeating (and worsening) the bait-and-switch pattern flagged on 8/28, 8/29, 8/31, and 9/2.
- Primary CTA is ambiguous: is the click destination the 50% off sale or the "point of view." launch? Two competing "SHOP NOW"/"SHOP IT" CTAs above the fold split intent.
- After the "wear it" section, the email collapses into an unrelated catalog scroll (skincare value sets, Kilian fragrance, Olive Young) — same low-focus pattern as 8/30's "Iconic Beauty" and 8/23's "Psst, something new just arrived."
- No visible pricing/urgency on the actual "point of view." hero product tiles beyond the individual SKUs — the collection itself has no clear single offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: a "50% off Select Beauty" deal email with the discount as the sole hero (matching the 8/28 send that scored best in this history), and a separate "point of view." launch email with its own subject/CTA. Combining them dilutes both.
- 2. If keeping combined, make the top banner's brand list match what's actually shown below it — currently PHILR/Urban Decay/INKEY List names appear nowhere in the "point of view." product grid, which reads as false advertising.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA for the top of the email; don't run "SHOP NOW" and "SHOP IT" side by side competing for the same click.
- 4. Trim the tail-end catalog scroll (skincare sets / Kilian / Olive Young) — it's the same generic filler pattern flagged repeatedly, and it dilutes the "point of view." launch story this email is ostensibly about.
