# 💘JUST YOUR TYPE 💘
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "Just Your Type" is Sephora's fragrance personality play — the concept promises a curated match, but the execution delivers a generic blast. The 💘 heart emojis anchoring the subject are doing Valentine's Day work in mid-July, and the hero copy retreats to "This is for YOU" instead of actually showing fragrance types or guiding the subscriber toward a pick. The top-banner mini deal (Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off) is the best commercial hook in the email, but it's buried in a small strip above a lifestyle hero that doesn't mention the offer at all.
- Following the July 13 "New products have arrived" send — which was a tight, editorial drop — this email scatters across fragrances, makeup (Patrick Starr / ONE/SIZE), and hair care (Leave-In Conditioners) without a coherent thread. The "Just Your Type" concept gets abandoned almost immediately once you scroll past the Jo Malone London hero.
- The structural bones are fine: clean layout, no render bugs, a recognizable sender, visible pricing and product details on the Jo Malone feature, and a loyalty signal (2X Points). But the concept never pays off. This reads like a fragrance campaign that lost its editorial nerve halfway through build.

## What's working

- **Top-banner mini deal is concrete** — "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" immediately telegraphs a transaction reason to scroll.
- **Jo Malone London hero product is well-featured** — size (3.4 oz) and price ($115) are both visible, plus star ratings below the item.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text or broken sections.
- **Loyalty program integration** — the 2X Points call-out and Beauty INSIDER badge at close reinforce subscriber value.
- **Subject line length is mobile-friendly** — "💘JUST YOUR TYPE 💘" is short; it won't truncate.

## What's weak

- **Heart emojis are seasonally off** — 💘 in mid-July reads Valentine's Day misfire, not summer fragrance discovery.
- **Hero doesn't carry the concept** — "This is for YOU" is generic ad copy; the "Just Your Type" personality-matching premise is immediately abandoned instead of demonstrated.
- **Mini deal is not in the hero** — the strongest offer is in a small-type top banner, not the focal image where it would drive scroll intent.
- **Email sprawls across categories** — fragrances → makeup (Patrick Starr) → hair care (Leave-In Conditioners) with no connective tissue; the "type" concept doesn't stretch to these sections.
- **Cadence is beginning to stack** — this is the seventh Sephora send since July 6; the frequency is reaching the point where even relevant subjects get skimmed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the mini deal into the hero.** "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" is a real offer. Put it in the headline — "Find Your Type. Minis Are 50% Off." — instead of a small banner. Every scroll after that is bonus revenue.
- 2. **Drop the hearts; reframe the subject around the fragrance concept.** The pun works but the 💘 execution frames it as a seasonal event when it isn't.
- 3. **Deliver on the "type" premise.** Show 3–4 fragrance personality types ("The Citrus Minimalist," "The Warm Amber," etc.) with one hero mini from each. That's a concept; the current email is a product grid.
- 4. **Trim the category sprawl.** If the Patrick Starr and Leave-In sections must appear, give them a dividing header that signals a new section — don't let them dilute the fragrance concept mid-scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your fragrance type is waiting — minis 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Find your type → minis are 50% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Take the scent match quiz — then grab two minis at half price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh picks from Jo Malone, Valentino, and more — any 2 minis, 50% off.`

**Broad `text-size-adjust: none` suppression**

```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;

}
```
Applied to virtually every element. This compounds the zoom issue above and can prevent OS-level accessibility font scaling from working in some clients.
