# Beauty you can take from day to night 💕
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora falling back into flat catalog mode, and after last week's genuinely strong sends it's a letdown. The "All Day. All Night." hero has zero product specificity — no hero item, no offer, no reason to click beyond a mood board of eyeshadow swatches. Below it, the email dissolves into an eight-item unrelated product grid (skincare, fragrance, haircare, hand sanitizer) with no organizing logic, which reads exactly like 8/8's and 8/10's "new arrivals" grids the brand has already run twice this month.
- The one genuine offer — a free Rare Beauty lip trial with $30 purchase — is buried at the very top above the fold, in small type, and gets no visual reinforcement anywhere else in the email. Makeup by Mario gets a real callout ("NEW, Only at Sephora," $25, blendable cream eyeshadow) but it's sandwiched between a vague hero and a grid dump, so it never gets to be the star.
- Compare to 8/9's K18 send (8/10) or 8/6's body-care routine send (9/10) — those had a single clear protagonist. This one has four competing offers (trial GWP, "All Day/All Night" hero, Makeup by Mario, the 8-item grid) and none of them wins. Judgment: a mid-tier catalog dump, roughly on par with 8/10's "Time for something new" (6/10), not a step forward.

## What's working

- The GWP banner at top is a real, specific offer: free Rare Beauty Soft Pinch Liquid Blush with $30 purchase — concrete and actionable.
- Makeup by Mario section has a clean callout: "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA," named product, and price ($25) all visible.
- Product grid renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, prices and brand names all legible.
- "Spot it. Shop it." mini-sections (Fragrance Minis, Moisturizers For You) give some thematic grouping versus a totally flat grid.
- Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider / VIB / Rouge) is visible near the footer for loyalty members.

## What's weak

- The hero "All Day. All Night." has no product, no price, no specific claim — just a mood shot of eyeshadow swatches and a generic "This beauty never quits" tagline.
- No single hero product or offer anchors the email; attention is split across a GWP banner, an abstract hero, a Mario callout, and an 8-item unrelated grid.
- The core product grid (Shiseido, Prada, Farmacy, Maison Margiela, Kérastase, ViktorRolf, Touchland, Sulwhasoo) has no unifying theme — skincare next to fragrance next to hand sanitizer reads like a random assortment, not a curated pick.
- This is the third grid-style send this month with the same "just in / new arrivals" shape (8/8, 8/10, now 8/13) — cadence fatigue is setting in.
- The one real discount-adjacent offer (GWP with purchase) is small, top-of-email only, and never repeated or reinforced visually further down.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero and build the whole email around it — either the Makeup by Mario launch (it's new, exclusive, and has a price) or the GWP offer, not both plus a mood-board hero. A single clear protagonist is what made 8/9 (K18) and 8/6 (body care) score well.
- 2. Move the GWP offer higher/bigger and repeat it near the bottom CTA — right now a reader has to catch it in the first 3 seconds or lose it entirely.
- 3. Replace the flat 8-item product grid with a themed grouping (e.g., "New This Week" vs. "Editor Picks") so the eye has a reason to keep scrolling instead of skimming past a random assortment.
- 4. Give "All Day. All Night." an actual product tie-in (a long-wear foundation or setting spray) instead of leaving it as an abstract mood statement with no SKU.
- 5. Vary the template — three grid-shaped sends in two weeks (8/8, 8/10, 8/13) is enough that a subscriber will start tuning these out; borrow the routine/story structure from 8/6's body care send instead.
