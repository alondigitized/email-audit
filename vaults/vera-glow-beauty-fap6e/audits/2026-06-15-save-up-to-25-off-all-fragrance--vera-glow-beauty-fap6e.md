# Save up to 25% off all fragrance
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Day five. I'm going to say that plainly: this is the fifth Glossier sale email in five days, and June 14's subject line was literally "Save up to 25% off all fragrance" — which is identically the subject line I'm looking at right now. Not a paraphrase. Not a remix. The same string of characters. When I open my inbox and see a subject I already opened yesterday, my first instinct is that my mail client is showing me a cached thread. It's not a great instinct to trigger in a subscriber you want clicking.
- The email itself is actually fine — the two-panel hero (sale on the left, new seasonal scent on the right) is classic Glossier restraint, the product photography is beautiful, and the $47 Glossier You bottles look exactly as elegant as they always do. There's even a 5-star customer quote mid-scroll that I appreciated. And the subscription upsell at the bottom — "lock in 20% off" — is legitimately smart for a fragrance loyalist. But Glossier is so deep in the cadence hole at this point that none of that craft is landing the way it would have on day one or even day three.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I already felt like I bought or passed on June 11 when the big orange ANNUAL SALE hero made my thumb stop. By day five with a carbon-copied subject, I'm marking this as read and moving on.

## What's working

- **Product photography is impeccable** — the You and You Dew bottles against the clean white background is the Glossier aesthetic at its best; I'd stop the scroll for that visual alone in a different context.
- **Customer quote with 5 stars mid-email** — social proof placed at natural scroll depth is a smart choice; it's the kind of thing Ulta buries or skips entirely.
- **Price is visible upfront** — $47 on the product cards means I'm not clicking just to find the number; I can do the math (25% off = ~$35) before I've even committed a tap.
- **Subscription upsell is genuinely relevant** — "lock in 20% off" for fragrance subscribers is a real offer that would make a fan pause, especially if the Friends sale is ending.
- **No render bugs** — clean, crisp, the image grids loaded, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Exact repeat subject line from June 14** — this is the single biggest problem; a subscriber who opened yesterday recognizes this instantly and feels spammed, not invited.
- **Split hero dilutes the message** — the sale (left) and "new season fragrance" (right) are asking me to do two different things at once; Sephora's STARTS TODAY send nailed the one-message hierarchy that this email flattens.
- **No end date visible anywhere I can see** — ILIA's HAIM arc at least signaled scarcity; here I don't know if the 25% off ends tonight or next Thursday, which kills urgency on day five.
- **"Shop now" appears on every single CTA** — six or seven times; there's no "Explore the new scent" vs. "Shop the sale" distinction, so the two stories blur into each other.
- **Subscription section at the bottom competes with the sale** — if you're still in a sale window, leading me to a recurring commitment is a conflicted ask; Charlotte Tilbury's June 14 send had the same kitchen-sink problem and it muddied the message there too.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Change the subject line — do not repeat yesterday's verbatim** — even a one-word swap ("your" fragrance, "all-new" fragrance, or leaning into the new scent angle) would reset the perceived freshness in-inbox.
- 2. **Pick one hero story** — either close out the sale with urgency (hours left, end date visible) or introduce the new fragrance as a separate send; trying both dilutes both.
- 3. **Add a countdown or explicit end date** — "Sale ends Sunday" on the hero tile would do more to drive clicks on day five than any copy change.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTAs** — "Shop the sale" vs. "Discover the new scent" so my eyes know where each story is going.
- 5. **Move the subscription pitch to a post-purchase or cart-abandon flow** — not competing with a sale send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 25% off Glossier fragrance ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the new scent — and save 25% while you're here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Glossier You, You Dew, and the new seasonal drop — all on sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The annual Friends sale is wrapping up. Here's what I'd grab.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issues found:**

- **Heavily obfuscated class names** — All CSS classes are minified to single/short tokens (`a`, `b`, `bl`, `bz`, `bw`, etc.). This is not a rendering bug, but it makes QA diffing and future debugging extremely difficult and is a maintainability risk if the template is ever hand-edited.

- **Rollover/hover effects** — `.rollover:hover .rollover-first / .rollover-second` swap pattern is present. This works in Apple Mail but silently no-ops in Gmail, Outlook, and most webmail clients. Confirm fallback state (`.rollover-first`) is fully readable on its own.

- **Conditional comment targeting is fragmented** — Three separate MSO blocks:
  ```html
  <!--[if (mso 16)]>  →  anchor text-decoration fix
  <!--[if gte mso 9]>  →  sup font-size fix
  <!--[if gte mso 9]>  →  AllowPNG / PixelsPerInch
  <!--[if mso]>        →  Word DontUseAdvancedTypography
  ```
  Splitting `gte mso 9` into two separate blocks is redundant; no functional defect, but merge them to reduce parse overhead in Outlook's Word renderer.

- **`lang="en"` present on `<html>`** — Correct. No issue.

- **`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`** — Present. No issue.

- **`format-detection telephone=no`** — Present. Verify whether any phone numbers in the body *should* be tappable; if so, this meta suppresses them globally.
