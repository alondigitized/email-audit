# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Let me be direct: this is at minimum my sixth Skechers email in three weeks, and "This style has limited availability!" is a subject line Skechers has already run in this very sequence — it was literally referenced in my July 16th "Your Items Are Waiting!" review as part of the cart-abandon rotation. So I'm not discovering urgency here, I'm watching the drip timer reset.
- The hero copy — "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." — is almost endearing in how self-aware it is, and honestly? The BOBS Misty Kiss Summer Stroll platform sandal is cute. I did browse it. The logic of the trigger is correct. But the email completely undercuts its own subject line: "limited availability!" fires as the hook, and then the body delivers zero scarcity signals. No "2 left in size 8," no inventory counter, nothing. Drunk Elephant pulls this off because when they say "going fast," they actually show it. Skechers just asserts it and moves on.
- The 20% off for Skechers Plus members is real and I appreciate it, but it's buried below a generic six-product recommendation grid. I'm not clicking a "SHOP NOW" button when the discount it's presumably attached to is three scrolls away. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Hero product is front-and-center** — BOBS Misty Kiss, Summer Stroll is the first thing I see; product image plus "SHOP NOW" button land without scrolling.
- **Skechers Plus discount is concrete** — 20% off with CODE: SUMMER is a real offer with a real code, which is more honest than vague "exclusive savings" language.
- **Demographic match is solid** — women's sandal hero, women's styles in the recommendation grid; the platform silhouette skews millennial-casual without trying too hard.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no ghost sections. Layout holds.
- **Seasonal relevance** — sandals + Summer Favorites Sale in mid-July is genuinely timed right.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body scarcity gap** — "Limited availability!" as the subject creates a specific promise. The body fulfills zero of it. No inventory count, no size-level availability, no timer. Sephora shows low-stock indicators inline on product tiles; this email doesn't even gesture at it.
- **Discount is buried below the recommendations** — the most actionable thing in the email (20% off, code in hand) sits below a six-tile product grid. The hero CTA says "SHOP NOW" with no mention of the offer. I have to scroll past content to find the reason to click.
- **"SMS-202605" is rendering above the Skechers logo** — whatever that campaign-tracking label is, it's visible at the top of the email as what appears to be a system artifact. Not a great first impression, and it's almost certainly leaking as the inbox preheader.
- **Recommendation grid is generic** — six products with no readable labels at thumbnail size, no connection to my actual browse history beyond the hero product. ILIA's editorial product selections feel intentional; these look auto-populated.
- **Cadence is cooked** — this is the sixth email in roughly three weeks, and this subject has already been referenced in the sequence before. The "second look" self-awareness would be clever once. At send six, it reads like a script.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to the offer** — "SHOP NOW" on the hero button should read "SHOP NOW — 20% OFF" or the hero copy block should include the discount before the recommendation grid. The hook and the incentive need to live together.
- 2. **Make the scarcity verifiable or cut it** — "This style has limited availability!" needs to cash the check in the body. Add a size-level inventory indicator ("Only 3 left in size 7.5") or swap the subject for something the email actually delivers on. Sephora does this right with inline stock signals; Skechers is borrowing urgency language without the product data to back it.
- 3. **Fix or suppress the top-of-email campaign label** — "SMS-202605" should not be visible above the Skechers logo. If it's a tracking hook it needs to be hidden; if it's leaking as the preheader, that's a separate (and worse) problem.
- 4. **Highlight the BOBS charity angle** — the hero product is from the BOBS line, which has a charitable giving component. That's a real differentiator and it goes completely unmentioned. One line under the product name would do it and would give me a reason to feel good about clicking.
- 5. **Trigger a cadence audit** — six cart-abandon emails in three weeks is the email equivalent of someone tapping my shoulder every four days. Drunk Elephant spaces their commercial sends with enough editorial content that I don't develop an ignore reflex. Skechers is pure drip right now.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Only a few left in your size — grab it now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus: your 20% off code is waiting inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer Stroll sandal + member discount — don't leave it twice`
