# Spotlight on: Fresh
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. I've been tracking these Lush emails like I track the Sephora Beauty Insider event countdown, and "Spotlight on: Fresh" is actually a concept I was ready to get behind. After yesterday's "Uh-huh honey" drop — cute, but nothing to do with my wallet — I opened this one hoping they'd finally give me something to act on. The hero is exactly what Lush is: hot pink, a little chaotic, undeniably them. The food-menu framing ("What's on the menu today?") with strawberry, coffee, pineapple named products is genuinely fun and on-brand.
- Then I kept scrolling. Four products competing for the same real estate, zero offer math to run, and — I am not kidding — a Halloween "Live-Scream" block in the middle of July. Pumpkins. Ghosts. It's July 13th. I actually screenshot that section and sent it to my beauty group chat with three question marks.
- This is a mark-as-read email. Beautiful brand energy, nothing actionable. I would not click, I would not forward it to a friend unless that forward was "look at this Halloween content in summer." The July 11 "Zero packaging. Double points." send is the version of Lush I want to open every time — this one spent the goodwill that earned.

## What's working

- The hot-pink hero is instant brand recognition — Lush before the logo even loads.
- "What's on the menu today?" is a clever food-inspired editorial frame that feels native to their ingredient-forward identity.
- The four product cards use photography and ingredient names (Strawberry, Coffee, Pineapple) to communicate what's in the formula without a single wall of text.
- The "Ask an Expert" video block earns credibility — it's the kind of educational touchpoint that Drunk Elephant does in email and Instagram to justify the price point without screaming "SALE."
- The models read young and diverse, which is right for Lush's actual customer base — not a department-store fragrance counter vibe.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. No %, no GWP, no loyalty event tease. After "Zero packaging. Double points." set the bar two days ago, a pure editorial send with no math to do feels like a step backward.
- The Halloween "Live-Scream" block is July content chaos — 110+ days of lead time is not a teaser, it's noise that breaks the fresh/summer visual mood the hero worked hard to set.
- Four spotlighted products means no product actually got spotlighted — this is a catalog page in editorial clothing. Glossier made the whole "Scent of the Summer" email about one thing, and I remembered the product. I won't remember any of these four.
- "Find Your Fresh" as the hero CTA is vague in a way that Sally Beauty would never let slide — "Summer hair starts with a freebie" tells me exactly where I'm going and why.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a loyalty hook immediately.** Even "earn double points on Fresh products this week" converts this email for a Rouge-tier or Ulta Diamond shopper from a browse into a buy. The July 11 double-points send proved this works — replicate the mechanic.
- 2. **Commit to one hero product.** Cup O' Coffee is a Lush legend. Lead the whole email with it. Give it skin, give it a model, give it one sentence of benefit copy. The other three become "shop the full Fresh lineup" links at the bottom.
- 3. **Cut the Halloween block entirely or separate it.** If you need to tease October, send a Halloween-specific teaser in late September. Don't break a summer-fresh visual story with jack-o-lanterns.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA.** "Shop Fresh Face Masks" or "Try the Fresh Edit" gives me a landing destination, not a vibe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your new fresh obsession is waiting 🍓`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Coffee, strawberry, pineapple — which fresh is yours?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `4 fresh formulas powered by real ingredients`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Strawberry picking > scrolling. Shop the Fresh lineup.`
