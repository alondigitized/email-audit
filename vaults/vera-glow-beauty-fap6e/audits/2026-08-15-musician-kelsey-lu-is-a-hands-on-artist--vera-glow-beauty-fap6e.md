# Musician Kelsey Lu is a hands-on artist
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a beautifully art-directed email and I get why ILIA keeps doing musician profiles, but I'm being honest — as a Sephora Rouge/Ulta Diamond shopper who's doing points math on every send, "4x VIP points" buried in tiny caps at the very top is the only concrete hook in this entire scroll, and everything after it is mood board, not shopping list. Kelsey Lu is genuinely a great pick — Brooklyn musician, harp, tactile artist angle tying into a waterproof eyeliner launch is a clever concept — but I had to scroll past four quotes and three moody portraits before I even saw a product. Compare that to Glossier's blush-look email from earlier this week, which also went editorial but kept product front and center in every "look"; this one makes me work for it.
- I'd read this one, not delete-on-sight, because the visuals stopped my thumb. But would I click? Only if I scroll all the way to "Shop Thru Line" near the bottom, and honestly most people bail before that. Forward to a friend? Maybe the artsy portrait shot, not the email itself — this reads more like a Kelsey Lu editorial than an ILIA sales email, and that's the exact opposite problem I had with ILIA's 8/13 educator email, which had zero visual polish. ILIA keeps swinging between "no branding at all" and "so much branding I forget what I'm buying" — I want the middle.

## What's working

- Kelsey Lu as the face is a strong, specific creative choice — not generic influencer stock, and it fits ILIA's artsy-clean-girl positioning.
- "4x VIP Points — new Thru Line" up top is genuinely relevant to me since I'm tracking points across every brand right now — I just wish it wasn't 8pt text I nearly skipped.
- The shade-matching quote ("it looks really great on my skin tone") is a real personalization beat that ties color back to a real face, better than ILIA's generic template sends.
- Two clear CTA buttons ("Step Into The Studio" and "Shop Thru Line") in matching navy give me an obvious tap target on mobile.
- Product flat-lay near the bottom (pencils, mascara, eye stylus) is clean and finally shows me what I'm actually being sold.

## What's weak

- The offer (4x points) is one line of tiny text at the very top and never resurfaces — no reminder near the CTA, no urgency, no "ends" date.
- Way too much scroll before any product appears — this is closer to a magazine spread than a shopping email, and on my phone that's a lot of thumb-flicking for zero conversion pressure.
- No price anywhere. I'm Rouge/Diamond, I'm doing the points-per-dollar math constantly, and I can't do that math with zero dollar figures in sight.
- Nothing here says "new" with any specificity beyond "new Thru Line" in the tiny header — no launch date, no "first 48 hours" urgency like Drunk Elephant or Sally Beauty lean on (even though those get repetitive fast).
- It's mood over merch — gorgeous, but I came here to shop, not to read a Kelsey Lu profile piece.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 4x VIP points offer into the hero as actual visible copy — not 8pt caps at the top — and repeat it right above the "Shop Thru Line" CTA so it's the last thing I see before I decide to click.
- 2. Cut the scroll in half — two quotes and two portraits max, then get to product. This concept doesn't need four separate photo/quote blocks to land.
- 3. Add a price or bundle callout near the flat-lay shot ("Eye Stylus + Thru Line + Limitless Lash, from $XX") so I can actually do my points math before clicking.
- 4. Give this a real deadline for the 4x points — "thru 8/17" or similar — urgency is the one lever this send doesn't pull at all.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4x points, new shade: Kelsey Lu's Thru Line pick`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your VIP points just went 4x — new eyeliner shade`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the shade Kelsey Lu says "looks great on my skin tone" — 4x points thru Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New waterproof Thru Line + 4x VIP points, while it lasts.`
