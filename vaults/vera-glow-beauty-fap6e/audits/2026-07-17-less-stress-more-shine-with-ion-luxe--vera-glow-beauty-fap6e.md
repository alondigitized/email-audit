# Less Stress, More Shine with ion Luxe ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, here we are at email thirty-five from Sally — and I'll actually give them partial credit for this one: "Less Stress, More Shine with ion Luxe" is doing something different from the summer-hair loop they've been stuck in. Yesterday was "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" (send thirty-four), which itself landed a week after another "summer must-haves" email. So when a proper collection launch drops instead of another seasonal roundup, I register it. Slightly.
- The ion Luxe Infrared Collection has real appeal for me. Pro-grade styling tools, rose gold aesthetic, infrared heat technology — this is actually interesting product news. The photography is gorgeous, the model imagery shows actual styled results on real hair textures, and the product lineup (Sensor Dryer, Flat Iron 1.5", Thermal Round Brush, Curling Iron 1.25", Blowout Brush 2.5") gives me the specificity I need to know if I care. I do. The Sensor Dryer especially — I don't know that SKU and I want to.
- Here's where it falls apart: there is a red "SALE" badge on that hero and not a single dollar amount or percentage anywhere I can see. Not "20% off." Not "up to 30% off." Just... *SALE.* As someone who has tracked every point multiplier across Sephora Beauty Insider and Ulta 21 Days of Beauty for the past three years, I am extremely familiar with what it means when the number isn't in the email. It means the number isn't flattering, or the offer has conditions, or they want the click before you can do the math. I'd still click to find out — the collection is interesting enough — but I'm annoyed that I have to. I'm not forwarding this until I know what "sale" actually means.

## What's working

- The hero immediately reads as a product launch — "NEW!" badge plus "ion LUXE INFRARED COLLECTION" sets a different tone than another summer sale email; this feels like a drop.
- Product photography is genuinely beautiful — rose gold tones are consistent throughout, and the aspirational "finished hair" imagery on the models sells the outcome before the spec sheet does.
- Naming the individual SKUs with dimensions (Flat Iron 1.5", Curling Iron 1.25", Blowout Brush 2.5") is exactly the kind of detail I need — it tells me this is a lineup, not a one-off product spotlight.
- Model diversity is present and it works — seeing different hair textures showing styled results makes the "works for hair like mine" signal land.
- Single-column layout is clean, no render bugs, legible at mobile scale — the hierarchy pulls my eye down naturally from hero to products.

## What's weak

- "SALE" badge with zero specifics — no percentage, no dollar threshold, no "prices starting at." This is the most avoidable miss in the email.
- No loyalty acknowledgment anywhere. I'm a Sally Beauty Club member and there's nothing here about points multipliers, member pricing, or bonus rewards. Sephora would have put "Rouge early access" or "4x points" right below the hero CTA. This email treats me like a stranger.
- The "Discover more from ion®" category grid at the bottom (Hair Color, Hair Care, Hair Styling Products, Styling Tools) dilutes the launch focus — it turns a collection email into a site nav email, which is a different job entirely.
- "Shop Now" appears as the CTA copy twice, maybe more. After e.l.f.'s "Invisi-Stick Glitz SPF is back" leading with a product-specific hook, "Shop Now" feels like a placeholder someone forgot to personalize.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the actual number in the hero.** "20% off the ion Luxe Collection" or "Up to 30% off — ends Sunday" converts. "SALE" does not. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Add a loyalty hook beneath the primary CTA** — "Earn 2x points through [date]" or "Sally Beauty Club members: exclusive pricing below" would immediately shift this from cold broadcast to member communication.
- 3. **Give at least one hero product a specific CTA** — "Shop the Sensor Dryer →" over the dryer image would outperform generic "Shop Now" for anyone who wants that SKU specifically. Ulta does this in their product spotlight sends and it earns the tap.
- 4. **Cut or demote the "Discover more from ion®" category grid** — this belongs in a retention or browse-abandonment email, not a launch. Let the collection breathe.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`img { white-space: pre; }` (line in `<style>`)** — `white-space: pre` applied globally to all `<img>` elements is non-standard. Images have no text content so this is typically a no-op, but it is unexpected and may interact with certain Outlook rendering paths. Should be removed.

**`'Founders Grotesk'` referenced but never declared** — The rule `a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` specifies Founders Grotesk as the primary typeface for all anchor elements, but no `@font-face` block for Founders Grotesk exists in the `<style>` section. All clients will silently fall through to Helvetica. If Founders Grotesk is intentional, an `@font-face` src pointing to a hosted `.woff2` must be added. If Helvetica is the intended fallback everywhere, remove the Founders Grotesk reference.

**Open Sans loaded at `v17`** — Google Fonts URLs reference `/opensans/v17/`. Current Open Sans is v29+. Google does serve legacy version paths via redirect, but this adds a latency hop and may eventually break if Google deprecates old version paths. Update URLs to omit the version pin or use the CSS API endpoint (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Open+Sans`).

**External font CDN calls to `fonts.gstatic.com`** — Gmail (desktop web), Outlook (Win/Mac), and Yahoo strip or block external stylesheets and `@font-face` references. All six `@font-face` declarations are invisible in those clients. Ensure fallback stack (Arial/Helvetica) renders acceptably at all intended sizes — visual QA in Gmail web required.

**`.filter-item:hover` CSS rule** — Hover pseudo-class is supported only in Apple Mail, Thunderbird, and Outlook for Mac. This is acceptable as progressive enhancement, but confirm it is not load-bearing for usability (i.e., the item must be clearly tappable/clickable without hover state).

**`<!---->`  empty comment before `<style>`** — Appears to be a stripped template variable or CMS/ESP artifact. Harmless but indicates a merge/template processing issue worth investigating upstream.

**`-webkit-font-smoothing`, `-moz-osx-font-smoothing`, `text-rendering: optimizeLegibility` on `body`** — These CSS properties are ignored in virtually all email clients (applied to the webmail page body, not the email frame). No harm, but dead code.
