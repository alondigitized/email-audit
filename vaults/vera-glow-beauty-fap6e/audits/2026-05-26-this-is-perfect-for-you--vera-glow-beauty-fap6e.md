# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Five Skechers emails in eighteen days. Three of them cart-abandon triggers in the last seven. I want to be explicit about that math because "This is perfect for you!" lands in my inbox right after "Did you forget something?" and "Your Items Are Waiting!" did their thing, and there is a finite amount of "we're personally thinking about you" energy a brand can spend before it starts feeling automated and slightly desperate. The subject line is doing the most — implying curation, personalization, maybe even a stylist made a decision — and then I open to a chunky gray sneaker called the Skechers Garage that reads decidedly unisex, flanked by a product rec grid that includes Men's styles. That gap between what the subject promises and what the email delivers is the single biggest problem here.
- That said, the 20% off band is real and clearly communicated, the layout is clean with zero broken images, and I do actually shop Skechers for that BOGO window energy occasionally. The Spring Sale expiration is buried in fine print, which kills the urgency this email desperately needs to have.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't click. I definitely wouldn't forward it to anyone — and if a friend asked me about Skechers sales I'd tell her to just go to the site and not trust the email to tell her what's "perfect for her."

## What's working

- **20% off a single pair** is visible, specific, and doesn't require mental math — clean offer execution compared to the tiered thresholds Ulta has been running ($10 off $50 / $20 off $100) where I'm constantly counting my cart total
- **"STILL THINKING ABOUT IT? / YOU'VE GOT GREAT TASTE!"** copy is warm and non-pushy for a retargeting email — not the guilt-trip energy of "Did you forget something?" from May 19
- **Clean layout with no render bugs** — product images load, buttons are properly aligned, no overlapping text; compared to Charlotte Tilbury's zeroed-out countdown timer debacle I audited recently, this at least renders correctly
- **Afterpay and Klarna** callout in the footer is a genuine win for anyone budget-splitting a shoe purchase

## What's weak

- **The subject line is a lie** — "This is perfect for you!" implies a curation signal, but the product rec grid shows both Men's and Women's Slip-Ins and the hero shoe (Skechers Garage) reads very neutral-to-masculine, not a 29-year-old woman's targeted pick
- **No loyalty program mention** — Skechers has a Rewards program and this email doesn't reference it once; e.l.f. consistently bakes "Beauty Squad" language into their sends and it makes the email feel like it knows who I am
- **The offer deadline is invisible** — 5/31/26 is in the fine print at the very bottom, so "20% off a single pair" floats without urgency; Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family emails that I've been tracking put the deadline in the 20% off band itself
- **Third cart-abandon in one week is one too many** — this is the Skechers cart-abandon trilogy and the brand hasn't changed the offer, creative, or urgency level across any of them; it reads like a drip sequence nobody audited for fatigue
- **"TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" CTA** is soft for a retargeting email — it doesn't name the product, doesn't say "Shop the Garage" or "Grab Your Size"; Sephora's last-chance sends use CTAs that tell you exactly what you're clicking toward

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the shoe in the CTA** — change "TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" to "Shop the Skechers Garage" or at minimum "See Your Size" — zero ambiguity about what happens when you click
- 2. **Put the deadline in the offer band** — "20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR · ENDS MAY 31" takes one line of copy and transforms a flat discount into an urgent one
- 3. **Surface Skechers Rewards** — even one line ("Rouge members, your points add up here too" if cross-program, or "Earn Skechers Rewards points on this purchase") makes the email feel targeted to a known customer vs. a list blast
- 4. **Gender-filter the product rec grid** — if you know I was browsing, you almost certainly know my browsing history skews Women's; showing Men's Slip-Ins in a "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE" grid breaks the personalization illusion the subject line built
- 5. **Rest the cart-abandon sequence** — after three sends in seven days, the fourth should offer something meaningfully different (higher discount, free shipping, bundle), not the same 20% off in a new wrapper
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Skechers cart: 20% off — ends May 31`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Garage is still yours — take 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spring Sale ends soon — grab your size before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off a single pair + Afterpay available — shop before 5/31`
