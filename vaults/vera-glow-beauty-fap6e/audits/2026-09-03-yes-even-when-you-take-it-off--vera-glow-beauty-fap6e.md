# Yes, even when you take it off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is ILIA back on Overglaze again, and I'm a little "again?" about it — I logged the Motif + Gallery new-shade drop on 9/1 just two days ago, and now it's a different creative push for the same product line with the same close-up-lips hero treatment. That said, the "Even when you take it off" tagline is genuinely a good hook — it's answering the actual objection I have with tinted lip oils (they fade fast, they don't survive a coffee), and the fenugreek/seaweed ingredient callouts give me something concrete instead of just vibes.
- The $75+ free mini eye stylus GWP is fine but not exciting — I've seen ILIA run tighter, more compelling GWP mechanics before (the Lip Sketch mini on 8/31 felt more generous relative to the ask). The "100% said it locks in moisture" stat is doing real work for credibility, though the asterisk fine print is basically unreadable at this size.
- Would I click? Probably, mildly — the swatch grid at the bottom is what would actually get me to tap "Shop Overglaze," not the hero. I wouldn't forward this to a friend; it's a solid but not surprising send. Compare to Sephora's "point of view" email this week that actually got me scrolling the whole thing for a genuine new-brand reveal — this is competent product marketing, not a scroll-stopper.

## What's working

- The ingredient breakout (fenugreek seed extract, seaweed extract) gives me actual substance to chew on, which most mass-prestige emails skip entirely.
- The swatch/shade array photo near the bottom is the strongest selling moment in the email — that's what makes me believe in the shade range.
- "24 hours of hydration—from first swipe to last call" is a smart, lifestyle-relevant copy line for someone like me who wears makeup out at night.
- Clean, single-product focus — no six-category grid competing for attention like some sends I've seen from other brands this week.

## What's weak

- This is the second Overglaze-focused send in three days (9/1 was "NEW Overglaze shades—Motif + Gallery"), and cadence-wise it's starting to feel repetitive even though the creative angle shifted.
- Zero mention of ILIA VIP points or any loyalty math — I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond, and I'm always doing the points math, so a DTC brand asking for a full-price click with no loyalty hook is a missed opportunity.
- The "100%" stat's fine print is basically illegible at this render size, which reads a little bit like a buried claim even if it's probably just standard clinical-study disclosure.
- No specific shade names or "new" flag on this send (unlike 9/1's explicit Motif + Gallery drop) — I can't tell if this is pushing the same shades again or something fresh.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a loyalty or VIP tie-in — even a small "double points this week" or similar would close the gap with Sephora/Ulta math I'm always running.
- 2. Differentiate this from the 9/1 email explicitly — either name a specific shade or tie it to a moment (date night, back-to-office) so it doesn't read as a rerun.
- 3. Make the "100%" claim's fine print legible or move it to a footnote link — right now it looks like it's hiding something even if it isn't.
- 4. Tighten the GWP threshold or make it feel more special — "free mini eye stylus w/ $75+" is a fine offer but doesn't build urgency the way "last chance" did on 8/31.
