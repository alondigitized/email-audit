# Something new is coming
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- After four weeks of Glossier's "ends tonight (psych)" sale circus — and I say that as someone who actually tracked the resurrection arc across at least three "final hours" emails — this one lands like a breath of fresh air. No countdown clock. No urgency theater. Just a hero shot of apples in a red mesh bag and the words "Take a bite." I'll give it this: it stopped my scroll.
- The G NY Bake Seltzer tease is exactly the kind of left-field, editorial, NYC-love-letter move that makes Glossier *Glossier*. The creative is crisp, the layout breathes, and "Get on the list" is so low-pressure as a CTA that I barely registered I was being asked to do anything. The specific launch timeline — July 10th in-store, July 14th everywhere — is genuinely useful information, not fake urgency. That's the difference between this email and everything they sent me in June.
- That said, I clicked the list mostly out of brand loyalty and FOMO, not because this email gave me a reason to. There's no price anchor, no loyalty hook, and "G NY Bake Seltzer" told me absolutely nothing about what category of product I'm waiting for. I'd forward this to exactly one friend who nerds out on Glossier NYC drops. Just one.

## What's working

- **The creative reset is earned.** After the June sale chaos, a no-offer, no-urgency teaser is genuinely refreshing. ILIA pulled off the same kind of pivot after their five-day Buy 3 Save 20% run — that "PSA — mini totes are back" email knew how to shift gears cleanly.
- **"Take a bite" is legitimately good copy.** Sensory, short, unexpected — the complete opposite of Drunk Elephant's "Beautiful, glowing skin" from earlier this week, which said absolutely nothing.
- **The apple mesh bag image is editorial and distinctive.** This is not a model in Glossier pink. It's a produce aisle fever dream and it works.
- **Specific launch dates are actionable.** July 10th NYC exclusive, July 14th stores and dot-com — I know exactly when to show up. That beats the vague "coming soon" teaser format.
- **"Get on the list" removes all commitment friction.** No cart, no checkout, no decision anxiety. Ideal for a launch I can't evaluate yet.

## What's weak

- **"Something new is coming" is one of Glossier's weakest subject lines.** Curiosity-bait works once; after weeks of sale spam, I needed a signal to open, not a shrug. Compare ILIA's "PSA — mini totes are back" — three words told me exactly what to be excited about.
- **Zero loyalty or points angle.** I always do the math. If this launches during a Sephora Beauty Insider event window or has early-access perks for Glossier members, that information changes whether I buy day-one or wait. It's nowhere here.
- **The product category is genuinely unclear.** "Bake Seltzer" read to me as: face mist? body fizz? something I drink? The copy is charming but I needed one more word — "scented," "body," "skin" — to know what I was getting on a list *for*.
- **No price signal at all.** Even "starting at $X" helps me know if this is an impulse-buy or a "save it for points event" situation. Without it, I'm making a blind waitlist commitment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put something specific in the subject.** It doesn't have to spoil the concept — "Apple drop. NYC first." tells me enough to care without killing the tease.
- 2. **Anchor the price or tie in loyalty.** A single line — "Earn X points on launch day" or "Glossier members get early access" — transforms this from a window into a reason to act.
- 3. **Clarify the product category in the first body line.** One adjective does it: "a limited-edition apple-scented *body* treat" removes all ambiguity.
- 4. **Put July 10th in the preheader, not just the body.** That date is the real urgency signal. Use it where it's seen before the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Apple drop. NYC first, July 10.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the NYC-only launch we've been sitting on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `G NY Bake Seltzer — get on the list before it sells out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In stores July 10. Everywhere else July 14.`
