# There’s A Reason It’s Called Unreal 👀✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Email number fifteen-ish from Charlotte Tilbury, and the whiplash is real. After the June 3rd "four free eyeshadow shades" send (which genuinely earned its 8/10 by leading with the gift), this one shows up as a full product-education scroll — benefits bullets, application time claims, a shade-match quiz tease — and then hides the actual offer (free mini gift, app-gated) in the basement. The contrast between the June 3rd gift-drop and this explainer send isn't a bad strategy by itself, but the execution buries the hook.
- The "15 Second Application" and "Match It In 60 Seconds" pairing is clever — the brand found a consistent time-based language for this launch and it actually works as a device. The model casting shows warm deeper skin tones prominently in the hero, which is more than CT usually does. But without a price angle, urgency signal, or any acknowledgment that I'm a known customer who bought the Exagger-Eyes, this feels like it landed in my inbox instead of at me.
- I'd open it on brand recognition alone, but I'd scroll past the shade-match CTA without clicking unless I was already shopping this range. Wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The paired time claims ("15 Second Application" / "Match It In 60 Seconds") give the email a unified launch voice — that's rare for CT, which usually leans on vague luxury-speak.
- Warm/deeper skin tone model in the hero is a meaningful inclusivity signal and makes the product feel relevant across a real range of customers, not just the pale editorial default.
- The shade-match CTA is the most specific button copy CT has sent me in weeks — action-oriented, product-tied, and gives me an actual thing to do.
- Layout is clean with zero render issues — no broken images, no overlapping type, which is more than I can say for some of the mid-June ILIA sends that had copy-stack problems on mobile.
- Benefit bullets above the fold do the job of differentiating before they ask me to scroll — that's respecting my attention.

## What's weak

- The "Free Mini Magic Gift When You Spend $1 on the App" is the only real conversion hook in this email, and it's buried near the footer — if you're using a gift to drive app installs, that's your hero module, not your afterthought.
- No urgency anywhere: no deadline on the mini gift, no "limited shades," nothing — Drunk Elephant at least pretends there's a clock running.
- Zero loyalty acknowledgment — I'm a Sephora Rouge customer who's bought CT multiple times, and this email treats me like a stranger who's never heard of the brand. ILIA's "Meet the Soft Focus family" send earlier this month managed to make me feel like a known quantity without even using my name.
- The subject does no product-specificity work — "There's A Reason It's Called Unreal" reads as fragrance launch energy as much as foundation energy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the free mini offer to the hero section.** "Spend $1 on the app, get a free mini" is actually a compelling reason to download the app *today* — that belongs in the first scroll, not the last. Give it a stated value ("worth $X") and a 48-hour window.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the shade-match CTA.** "Find your shade before it sells out in your color" or a limited-time app incentive would convert the shade quiz from exploration into urgency.
- 3. **Acknowledge purchase or tier history.** Even "Already love Airbrush Flawless? Here's what Unreal Skin does differently" would make me feel seen without a personalization token.
- 4. **Lock down the subject with product specificity** — curiosity hooks work better when paired with a noun.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Unreal Skin: match your shade in 60 seconds 👀`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 15-sec foundation formula. Here's the proof.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free mini magic gift when you spend $1 in the app — 48 hours only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New serum foundation. One shade quiz. Zero flat-finish excuses.`
