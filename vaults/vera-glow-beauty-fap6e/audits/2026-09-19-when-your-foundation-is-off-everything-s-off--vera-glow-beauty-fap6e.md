# When your foundation is off, everything’s off. ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, e.l.f. is doing the "here's your whole face routine" bundle send again, and I'll admit the step-by-step cover/conceal/blend breakdown is clean — it reads like a mini tutorial, not a dump. But there's zero price-per-value math for me here beyond the individual SKU prices ($8, $5, $7), no bundle discount, no points multiplier, nothing that says "hey Rouge member" or "hey Diamond member" — it's just... a routine. The "Unglammy Valley" video teaser at the bottom is a nice hook but it's buried after four full product blocks, so by the time I'd get there on my phone I've already scrolled past three "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- This is fine, not exciting. e.l.f. at $5-$8 a pop isn't hitting my Sephora Rouge / Ulta Diamond stockpiling instinct at all — this is more of a "add to Target run" email than a "log into my beauty app" email. Compare this to Drunk Elephant's Kamo Triple-Peptide Cover Drops launch — that one at least gave me a 25-shade range and a real "new hybrid category" hook. This is e.l.f. doing what e.l.f. does well (clean, one-idea sends, like their 9/10 lip oil stick send) but stretched into a three-step routine that dilutes the single hero moment. I wouldn't forward this to a friend, I wouldn't click past browsing — I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The step 1/2/3 labeling (Cover, Conceal, Blend) gives a clear visual hierarchy I can skim without reading paragraphs.
- Each product shot is a clean close-up with the applicator/brush shown mid-use, which reads premium for a mass-price line.
- The "Top Picks for You" row at least gestures at personalization, even if it's generic complementary products (primer, setting spray, lip swear) rather than shade-matched.
- No render bugs — everything's crisp, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere. No %, no BOGO, no free shipping threshold call-out until the tiny footer icon — that's a miss for a Millennial who checks for a deal before clicking.
- Zero shade/skin-tone personalization — I'm told nothing about undertone matching, which matters way more to me than a generic "buildable coverage" line.
- The "Top Picks for You" products don't visually match my skin tone or say why they're "for me" — feels like a static module, not real personalization.
- Subject line ("When your foundation is off, everything's off") promises a problem/solution hook but the email just launches into steps — no acknowledgment of shade-matching pain points, which is the actual foundation problem people have.
- The category nav strip (NEW/EYES/LIPS/FACE/SKINCARE/HAIR) is just gray bars with no thumbnails — feels like an afterthought compared to Glossier's more curated single-focus sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add an actual offer — even a small "20% off Soft Glam collection" would turn this from a lookbook into a purchase trigger. Right now there's nothing pulling me to buy today vs. next time I'm at Target.
- 2. Move the "Unglammy Valley" video teaser up near the top — it's the most original, scroll-stopping asset in the whole email and it's stuck below three product blocks.
- 3. Add shade-range or "find your match" messaging under the Foundation step — the subject line promised this and the body never delivers it.
- 4. Bundle the three-step routine into one CTA ("Shop the Routine, $18") instead of three separate SHOP NOW buttons that fragment the ask.
