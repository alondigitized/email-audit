# Get a skincare duo on us + FREE shipping
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so e.l.f. SKIN is sliding in as a sub-brand sender today — and honestly I want to give them a little credit, because at least it's *not* another lip bundle. After "blurreal" yesterday and the three consecutive lip emails before it, a skincare duo headline registered as a genuine pattern break for me. The "FREE DUO GIFT!" badge on the hero product shot is the first thing my eye lands on, and that's the right call — a gift-with-purchase mechanic reads as generous in a way that a flat percent-off doesn't.
- But then I read one line lower: $50+ order AND a promo code (BURST) required at checkout. The hero says "on us" and the fine print says "if you do two things first." That's not quite on us. Compare that to how Drunk Elephant's "20-minute hack" on July 8 — which I'll admit I opened entirely on habit — at least delivered on whatever it teased inside. Here the gap between headline promise and actual offer conditions is just wide enough to create a little friction before I've even tapped.
- The "Top Picks for You" grid tries to sell personalization but shows three generic hydration SKUs with no skin-type context, no texture callout, nothing that signals e.l.f. SKIN knows anything about me. This is the fifth e.l.f.-family email in eight days — even accounting for the sub-brand split, my inbox reads it as e.l.f. fatigue. I'd stop at the promo code, screenshot it for later, and mark as read. No forward.

## What's working

- **"FREE DUO GIFT!" badge on the hero product image** — that visual badge in the corner is the fastest-reading offer signal in the email and it works; eye goes there immediately
- **Promo code (BURST) is legible in the body** — I don't have to click through to find it; I can screenshot and shop later, which matches how I actually use promo emails
- **e.l.f. SKIN sub-brand framing** — the logo clearly reads as skincare, not makeup, which at least signals that someone segmented a skincare list; after the July 7 lip-combo send I appreciate the pivot
- **Clean layout, zero render bugs** — hero, product grid, nav strip, footer all render perfectly; hierarchy from top to bottom is logical
- **Summer-appropriate product positioning** — skincare in July tracks with the season and picks up logically from the July 4 Suntouchable SPF send

## What's weak

- **"On us" headline vs. $50+ threshold + code requirement** — "get The Hottest Drops Duo on us" is a strong promise; the fine print one line below quietly walks it back; that gap erodes trust right in the hero
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is the most generic button on the internet** — after ILIA's buy-3-save-20 email where the math was *in* the CTA, "SHOP NOW" does zero conversion work; something like "Claim My Free Duo" would pull harder
- **"Top Picks for You" is a placeholder, not personalization** — three hydration products at $6–$13 with no ingredient callouts, no skin-type signals, no reason to believe this grid was built for me versus anyone else on the list
- **The navigation block (NEW · EYES · LIPS · FACE · SKINCARE · HAIR) is a website header, not email content** — that real estate could be a two-line hero benefit strip or a "why this duo" callout; Ulta would have put a product countdown there
- **Fifth e.l.f.-family email in eight days** — even with the sub-brand distinction, inbox cadence is maxed and the goodwill from yesterday's "blurreal" send doesn't carry forward when another email lands 24 hours later

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the hero copy match the actual offer** — "Get the Hottest Drops Duo FREE when you spend $50 — use code BURST" is honest, still exciting, and removes the gap between headline and fine print; kill "on us" unless it's genuinely unconditional
- 2. **Replace the nav block with a 2-column benefit strip** — one line per product in the duo: what it does, key texture/ingredient, skin type it's for; that's the content doing the last mile of conversion work that "SHOP NOW" can't do alone
- 3. **Upgrade the CTA to match the mechanic** — "Claim My Free Duo" or "Add Both + Use BURST" tells me exactly what clicking does; Glossier's recent teaser email had a better CTA on a single line of copy than this whole email
- 4. **Give "Top Picks for You" actual signal** — if you know I'm in skincare, show me the Thick Burst Drops with a "based on your routine" nudge; Drunk Elephant's July 8 product drill-down is the bar this grid should be shooting for
- 5. **Enforce a 48-hour gap between e.l.f. and e.l.f. SKIN sends** — or make the SKIN branding loud enough in the subject line (e.g., "e.l.f. SKIN free duo") that my brain buckets them separately before the fatigue sets in
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free skincare duo is yours — $50 + code BURST`
- **Subject Alt B:** `e.l.f. SKIN: Hottest Drops Duo on us (really)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Add to cart, enter BURST at checkout. Free shipping included. Online only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Thick Burst Drops + a free duo — this is a good cart.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issues found:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** (`<title></title>`): Some preview panes (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) display the document title; leaving it blank produces "Untitled" in those contexts. Should be set to match the subject line or sender name.
- **Mobile h1 font-size of 9px:** The media query rule `.row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size: 9px !important }` reduces a heading to 9px on mobile — well below the WCAG 2.1 minimum legible threshold. Evidence: `@media (max-width:660px)` block in `<style>`.
- **Google Fonts `<link>` tags:** `fonts.googleapis.com` stylesheets are stripped by Gmail, Outlook, and most major clients. Open Sans and Jost will not load. Fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) is present in inline styles, so this is a degraded-experience issue rather than a breakage — but the Jost font has **no inline fallback** in the preheader div (`font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif`; Jost is referenced in the stylesheet only).
- **`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }`:** This Gmail-targeting rule suppresses all link color and underlines inside the message body. If body text is dark and no explicit link color is set inline, linked text will be visually indistinguishable from surrounding copy.

**No issues found:** `lang="en"` is set on `<html>`. All `<table>` elements carry `role="presentation"`. The logo `alt` attribute is present (`alt="e.l.f."`).
