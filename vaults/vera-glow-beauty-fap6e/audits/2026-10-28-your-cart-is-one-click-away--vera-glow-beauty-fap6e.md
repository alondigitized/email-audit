# Your cart is one click away!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- Ugh, it's the eighth fake-cart email from Skechers in about two weeks, and I still haven't put anything in a cart. "Let's make it official. Your cart is ready when you are" is a relationship metaphor for a pair of pink jogger sneakers I never picked. The best thing here is the 20% off Skechers Plus banner, and it's buried below the product grid.
- The layout is clean and I could read it all without zooming. But the first screen is a giant headline, a lot of white space, and one shoe with a navy "Complete your order" button. After the 10/26 "Your cart expires soon!" and 10/23 "Your Items Are Waiting!" sends, I know this beat by heart. Sephora would open with my points balance and a real reason to come back. Ulta would lead with the deal.
- Would I click? No. Forward it to a friend? No. It gets marked as read. The 20% for members is the only reason I'd even think about it, and "Hey, we miss you. Here's 20% off." already made that offer better.

## What's working

- The Skechers logo and the big navy headline make the sender obvious right away.
- The "Complete your order" button is a clear, high-contrast CTA.
- The 20% OFF block in the dark navy band is the strongest visual in the email, and it's easy to read.
- The six-shoe grid has product names, so I can tell what each one is.
- The category list (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing, New Arrivals, Sale) is big and easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- I never added anything to a cart, so "your cart is ready" is a fake setup and I don't trust it.
- The 20% offer sits under the product grid and the "Shop all best sellers" bar. I had to scroll through about two-thirds of the email to find it.
- The offer has a "No Tricks, Just Treats" Halloween theme, but it's only for Skechers Plus members, and the fine print says online only, select regular-priced items, valid until 11/2. A Rouge/Diamond person like me hates this kind of fine print. I'd want to know if I'm in, what it excludes, and what my points would be worth.
- The hero shoe is a pale pink women's jogger floating in a lot of empty space. There's no price, no sizing, no reviews, and no stock cue.
- There's nothing personal here. It doesn't mention a name, a tier, a points balance, or what I've browsed.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% Plus offer to the top as the hero, with an expiry date ("Ends 11/2"). If the email says "cart", show the real item with price and size.
- 2. If I never had a cart, drop the cart framing and send a browse-based "picked for you" email. Real cart emails should come once, not seven times.
- 3. Add social proof (star ratings) and a price to the hero shoe.
- 4. Show Skechers Plus status or savings math near the top, like "Plus members save 20% through 11/2."
- 5. Shorten the footer fine print and make the exclusions clear.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Plus members: 20% off ends 11/2`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your GO WALK picks, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus members save 20% online through 11/2. Auto-applied at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The shoes you looked at, plus five more we think you'll love.`
