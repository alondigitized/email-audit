# The hoodie that started it all
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- I've been in the Glossier inbox for over a week now — Pink Capsule launch, "Fetch a little joy," summer activity checklist — and this one finally pivots away from the Pink Capsule to the hoodie origin story. Credit where it's due: the editorial angle is a genuine change of pace. "Every cool girl has lore" is very Glossier and it works. The Embroidered Pink Hoodie reveal is new information, and the Grand Bag is technically a new product.
- That said, I'm a Sephora Rouge member who does the points math in her sleep. There is not a single price, discount, or member incentive anywhere in this email. No "first access" language, no "limited drop" urgency, no Rouge equivalent — just four "Shop now" buttons and brand mythology. Compare this to how ILIA teased "Coming 06.02 — our softest launch yet" on May 29: one line of copy, a date, and actual anticipation built. Glossier dropped two new products and I have no idea what they cost or when they sell out.
- I'd click through to see the Embroidered Hoodie because the product is genuinely new and I'm curious about the price. But I would not forward this — there's nothing to forward. No deal, no urgency, no "you need to see this." It reads more like a brand mood board than an email that respects my buying behavior.

## What's working

- **"Every cool girl has lore"** — the lead copy is sharp and distinctly Glossier in voice; it earns a second of attention
- **Embroidered Pink Hoodie is a real new product drop** — not just a repromote of the original, which earns a little goodwill after weeks of Pink Capsule recycling
- **Layout is clean and renders without breakage** — images load, text doesn't overlap, hierarchy is legible
- **"The post heard around the internet" section** — referencing organic virality as social proof is smart and on-brand; the Glossier Pink Hoodie internet moment is real
- **"Fans pics" CTA** — using UGC as an entry point into the brand story is a smart editorial choice I haven't seen in the prior run of sends

## What's weak

- **Zero pricing, zero offer, zero urgency** — for a buyer doing the points math, this email gives me nothing to act on; compare to Charlotte Tilbury's digest which at least bundled products with clear purchase paths
- **Four "Shop now" buttons** — generic and undifferentiated; by the fourth one I've forgotten what I'm shopping for
- **The Pink Hoodie has already lived in my inbox multiple times this week** — introducing an "Embroidered" variant without acknowledging the original's recent resurgence makes this feel like a stealth re-launch, not a new drop
- **No loyalty signal whatsoever** — Drunk Elephant's post-Friends-and-Family sends at least nod to member status; Glossier treats me like a stranger here
- **Grand Bag feels tacked on** — pairing the hoodie lore with a bag launch in the same send dilutes both stories; one product would have landed cleaner

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price or a hook to the Embroidered Hoodie reveal** — "New. $X. Ships now." is all it takes to convert curiosity into a click with intent
- 2. **Consolidate to one CTA** — pick the hero (Embroidered Hoodie as new news) and cut the Grand Bag to a footer mention or save it for its own send
- 3. **Acknowledge the return of the original** — "Back by overwhelming demand" or "You asked, we listened" would make the origin-story angle feel earned rather than manufactured
- 4. **Add member or early-access framing** — even "Glossier You members shop first" would make a Rouge-tier-minded person feel seen
- 5. **The Grand Bag needs its own moment** — "barely printed for your experience" copy is confusing; this product deserves a cleaner brief

### 1. Technical Summary

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[ISSUE] Misused `<span>` as tracking hook**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KT5CGJVGB1MRM563W2SAV1F9_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on a `<span>`. Browsers and email clients silently ignore them — no stylesheet is loaded, no request is fired via this element alone. Klaviyo likely relies on JavaScript or a server-side render to resolve this into an actual open-tracking pixel. In stripped-JS environments (most email clients), this element does nothing. **If this is the open-tracking pixel, it is likely not firing reliably.** A standard 1×1 `<img>` tracking pixel is the correct implementation.

**[INFO] Tracking domain: `ctrk.klclick.com`** — confirmed Klaviyo click-redirect domain. No raw destination URLs visible in the truncated source; UTM parameter coverage cannot be verified.
