# Friends + Family, final call for 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. That's where I am with ILIA. And today — May 28 — I've already opened "Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" and "20% off ends in a few hours" from them. This is the third one hitting my inbox in the same day-of window. By this point, "final call" is less an urgency signal and more a Pavlovian flinch.
- Here's what I have to give them though: the email itself is mechanically strong. That cool-toned product stacking in the hero, crossed-out prices ($46 → $38.40 for the Super Serum Skin Tint that's been sitting in my cart), the B Corp and Leaping Bunny badges doing quiet trust work in the footer — the ILIA brand discipline is still intact. The body copy even has a behavioral trigger in it: "We see you've been browsing our friends + family sale" is the closest thing to personalization I've seen from them all month. And there's a buried GWP that deserves way more real estate — a free mini with any order is actually a solid incentive that got pushed into paragraph two of body copy like an afterthought.
- The thing that almost kills this for me is the P.S.: "Sale ends at 11:59pm 5/28/26." If I'm reading this on May 29, the sale is over. Gone. I've been handed a coupon to a store that closed last night. Drunk Elephant did something similar on their Friends & Family run — email thirteen explicitly said "we extended the sale" after email twelve said it was ending — and it felt chaotic. This feels the same. I'd click on the Skin Tint out of sheer cart inertia. I would not forward this to anyone. I'd also finally start the unsubscribe conversation with myself.

## What's working

- **Crossed-out prices with exact sale amounts** — $46 → $38.40 does the 20% math for me instantly; no calculator needed, no "shop to see price" nonsense
- **Behavioral trigger in the body copy** — "We see you've been browsing" is the only segmentation signal I've gotten from ILIA in 18 emails and it lands; Charlotte Tilbury does this in their personalized retargeting and it works there too
- **GWP mention (free mini with any order)** — buried in paragraph two but it's real incremental value; that detail alone would make me reconsider if I was on the fence
- **VIP tier called out in the header banner** — "20% OFF FOR VIPS*" acknowledges my membership before I even hit the body; Sally Beauty consistently does this well and it signals you know who I am
- **B Corp + Leaping Bunny footer** — clean beauty credibility signals that a Sephora Rouge customer actively tracks; every send reinforcing this builds a cumulative trust stack

## What's weak

- **P.S. says sale ends 5/28/26** — if this email is a 5/29 send, the offer is expired by the time it hits my inbox; that's not an urgency problem, that's a zombie email problem, and it's worse than no urgency at all
- **"Hi there" is not a name** — email eighteen, behavioral browse data in the body, and still no first name in the salutation; e.l.f. manages segmentation on a much higher volume schedule and still gets the personalization basics right
- **GWP is buried** — a free gift with purchase is a stronger closer than "20% off" for anyone who was already going to spend; it should have its own section or at minimum a bolded line, not paragraph two of the body
- **Three day-of sends is one too many** — "Finishing touches," "ends in a few hours," and now "final call" in the same inbox session dilutes all three; Glossier's post-Memorial Day pivot to a launch email showed that knowing when to stop is a brand signal, not a weakness

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the P.S. date or pull the email entirely** — if this is a 5/29 send with a 5/28 end date, it should not have gone out; build a send-time check against the sale close time; a broken offer is actively trust-damaging
- 2. **Give the GWP its own visual section** — "free mini with any order" deserves a dedicated row with a product image above the SHOP NOW button; it changes the math from "20% off" to "20% off plus a gift," which is a different behavioral trigger entirely
- 3. **Collapse to one send on the last day** — pick the best angle (this one, with the behavioral trigger and the GWP) and send once; three in a day trains subscribers to ignore all three
- 4. **Personalize the salutation** — "Hi Vera" or even "Hi [first name]" is table stakes; having browse data but not a name in the opener is a segmentation contradiction
- 5. **Give the product CTAs verb-plus-noun copy** — "Shop the Skin Tint" or "Get the Blush" instead of four identical "shop now →" links; the grid is doing heavy lifting and the CTAs are coasting

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only whitespace. Screen readers announce this as the document title; some clients (Apple Mail tabs, Samsung Mail) surface it directly to users. Should contain a human-readable subject or brand name.

**Google Fonts `@import` blocked by Gmail**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules inside `<style>` blocks. Montserrat will not load for Gmail users (the majority of consumer email opens). The stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` correctly falls back, so rendering degrades gracefully, but the brand font is absent for most recipients.

**Klaviyo custom font import — external dependency**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same Gmail-strip issue applies. If this custom font is load-bearing for brand rendering, it will also silently fail in Gmail.

**Global link `text-decoration: none` on all states**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link { … text-decoration:none }
a:visited { … text-decoration:none }
a:hover { … text-decoration:none }
```
No visual affordance distinguishes links from body text in hover or visited states. WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires links to be distinguishable from non-link text by more than color alone when inline with body copy. If any CTAs rely on underline for context, this suppresses them globally.

**Link color contrast — unverified**
`#5f4b3c` on `#FAF7F4` (body background) yields a contrast ratio of approximately 5.0:1 — passes WCAG AA for normal text but should be spot-checked against any lighter section backgrounds used deeper in the email.

**Preheader Unicode padding — minor spam signal**
```html
<div style="display:none;…">This is the finishing touch ͏ ͏ ͏ ͏ …</div>
```
Uses Unicode combining characters (U+034F) for preview text padding. This is a standard Klaviyo pattern, but certain content filters (Barracuda, Proofpoint) flag high-density runs of non-printable Unicode. Not a critical issue; worth monitoring deliverability.
