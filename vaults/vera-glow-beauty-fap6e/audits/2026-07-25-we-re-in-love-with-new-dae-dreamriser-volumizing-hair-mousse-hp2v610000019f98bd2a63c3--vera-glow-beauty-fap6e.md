# ❤️ We’re in love with NEW dae Dreamriser Volumizing Hair Mousse❤️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, the subject line promised me dae Dreamriser Volumizing Hair Mousse with hearts on both ends, and the actual email opens with a pink banner for 50% off Make Up For Ever because apparently it's "National Lip Day." I have to scroll past a full "New & Clean at Sephora" before-and-after section before the mousse I was emailed about even shows up. That's a bait-and-switch structure I'd expect from a lower-effort brand, not from Sephora.
- Once I do get to the dae section, it's actually solid — clean badge, "New · Only at Sephora," a 72-hour heat protectant claim, and a tidy "More dae Bestsellers" carousel underneath. But by the time I scroll that far I've already been shown three other unrelated offers (lip sale, clean-beauty hero, before/after skin), which dilutes the one thing that got me to open the email in the first place.
- Would I click? Probably, but on the Clinique 4x points banner or the Dior bestsellers, not the mousse the subject line sold me. I'd mark this as read, maybe screenshot the dae mousse section for myself since I do care about volumizing formulas, but I wouldn't forward it — it's too scattered to be worth someone else's time. Sephora, this is the kitchen-sink template again; ILIA's "goes well with free gloss" email a few weeks back proved a tighter, single-focus send converts better than this everything-bagel approach.

## What's working

- The dae Dreamriser section itself is clean — clear "New," "Only at Sephora," and a real product benefit ("72-hour heat protectant" for "fuller-looking hair") instead of vague fluff.
- "Trending Picks for You" is legit useful — Kulfi, Summer Fridays SPF, Kosas, Farmacy, all tagged with the green "Clean at Sephora" badge, which I actually filter by.
- Loyalty is front and center — Insider/VIB/Rouge tier bar, the Clinique 4x points app-only offer, and "New 100-Point Rewards" all show up, so at least the points math is available to me without digging.
- Before/after hero photography is clean and the models read like actual customers, not stock-photo filler.

## What's weak

- The subject sold me hair mousse; the first thing I see is a Make Up For Ever lip sale. That mismatch is worse than a duplicate-subject problem — it's a broken promise before I even scroll.
- "Get up to 50% off Select" plus an asterisked "Exclusions/terms apply" is soft bait — I have no idea what's actually included until I click through.
- No urgency or real personalization in the subject itself — "We're in love with" is generic brand-voice filler, not something that reads like it's about me specifically.
- This email is packed with five distinct offers (lip sale, clean picks, dae mousse, Clinique points, Dior, rewards) — it's trying to be every campaign at once instead of committing to the mousse launch it was named for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject line — lead with the dae Dreamriser Mousse front and center, not a Make Up For Ever sale. If you want to cross-sell lips, do it in a secondary module, not the header.
- 2. Cut this down to two modules max: hero product + one complementary offer (loyalty points tied to that purchase). Right now it reads like five separate emails stapled together.
- 3. Add a real urgency hook to the mousse launch — "first 48 hours," limited GWP, something — since right now there's zero reason to act today versus next week.
- 4. Swap "up to 50% off Select" for a specific, honest number or category so I'm not guessing what's excluded.
