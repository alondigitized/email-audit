# 25% off the whole enchilada
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Day two, send two — and I want to be precise about that, because I already reviewed "Stock up at 25% off!" this morning. The F&F launch hit yesterday, scored a 9 from me, I was already in. And DE is now doing what I called out on ILIA earlier this week: two emails in a single day for the same promotion. The email itself is genuinely excellent — "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS: GO SHOP!" in the header banner made me laugh out loud, the GWP spend tiers are real incentive math I want to run (free Hydro Bright kit at $100+, full-size F-Balm if I build a mask order), and the product grid is my actual replenishment list. C-Firma and T.L.C. Framboos are essentially empty on my shelf.
- Would I click? Probably, but from this morning's email — I already placed the order. This send is talking to a customer who's already checked out, literally. The case for this email being in my inbox at all is weak, and DE is spending goodwill they earned with months of clever wordplay on a redundant nudge. I would not forward it to a friend; I sent them yesterday's launch email instead.

## What's working

- "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS: GO SHOP!" in the header bar is the funniest copy DE has written all week — fully on brand, and it made me forget for a second that I'd already seen this offer.
- "This way to the friend zone." as the preheader paired with "25% off the whole enchilada" in the subject is a one-two wordplay sequence that earns an open. That combo is genuinely chef's kiss tier.
- The GWP spend tiers are additive — the "$100+ get a free Hydro Bright kit (VALUE $100+)" and the "FULL SIZE GIFT" F-Balm badge are new incentives that give me a reason to re-engage even after the launch email.
- Named product grid (C-Firma, B-Hydra, T.L.C. Framboos, A-Passioni) is doing real work — I don't need copy to know what these do; seeing my exact SKUs with 25% off is all the persuasion I need.
- Clean render, no broken images, layout holds up with colorful product photography that's instantly recognizable as DE.

## What's weak

- Second email of the day for the same sale. Charlotte Tilbury sent three in a day this week and I publicly called it out — DE is heading toward that territory and it will cost them.
- Zero personalization. Sephora's Memorial Day email showed me specific bonus-point math tied to my tier. DE has no loyalty program to mirror that, but they could at minimum segment on known repeat purchasers and surface "your usual suspects."
- No visible sale end date. "Don't wait!" without an anchor date ("ends Sunday," "48 hours left") is vague urgency that doesn't create the calendar moment I need to prioritize checkout.
- The GWP terms — particularly the F-Balm free gift and the kit value — are buried in the fine print block at the bottom. If the F-Balm is genuinely full size, that should be hero-level messaging, not a footnote.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One email per day, max, during the active sale.** Yesterday's launch earned the 9; today's send should be one targeted reminder, not two. Each incremental send against the same offer dilutes the urgency that made yesterday convert.
- 2. **Name the end date.** "Sale ends Sunday at midnight" turns "don't wait" from a platitude into a real decision point. Glossier did this cleanly in their Memorial Day email this week.
- 3. **Make the GWP the hero of Day 2.** The 25% off is established — everybody who was going to open the launch email already did. Today's angle should be: "You're already spending, here's why to spend $100+." Build the email around the kit GWP value, not a repetition of the discount.
- 4. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for something product-specific.** Even "Shop C-Firma" on the C-Firma tile or "Shop your all-stars" on the grid header adds the noun that makes the tap feel intentional.
- 5. **Surface the GWP fine print into scannable copy.** Replace the asterisk-dense block with two clear bullets in the body: "✓ Spend $100+ → free Hydro Bright Kit ($100 value)" and "✓ Any mask order → free full-size F-Balm."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends Sunday — your 25% off window is closing`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $100+, get a $100 kit free. Do the math.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Hydro Bright Kit when you spend $100+ sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your T.L.C. Framboos restock moment is right now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Invalid CSS inside `<style>` block — HTML comment syntax in media query**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment delimiters (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS. These rules are silently dropped by all modern CSS parsers. Any layout rules inside those comments will not apply on mobile.

**[HIGH] `.appleLinks a` suppresses link affordance in Apple Mail**
```css
.appleLinks a {color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important;}
```
This forces all links to black with no underline in Apple Mail — making CTAs visually indistinguishable from body text. Apple Mail is typically the #1 or #2 client by share in beauty verticals.

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate font loading — 6 extra HTTP requests**
Each of the 3 `db.onlinewebfonts.com` fonts is loaded twice: once via `<link>` in `<head>` and again via `@import` inside `<style>`. This doubles round-trips for fonts that most email clients will ignore anyway. Remove the `<link>` tags; keep only `@font-face` declarations.

**[MEDIUM] Universal `line-height: 100%` override**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```
Applied after `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}`, this cascades into every element including `<td>`, `<p>`, and `<span>`, potentially collapsing multi-line text. Any per-element `line-height` set later via inline styles will still win (inline beats class), but element-level stylesheet rules will not.

**[MEDIUM] Split font CDN origins**
`BrownRegular` is served from `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (versioned, controlled). `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (third-party, unversioned). A CDN outage or rate-limit on `db.onlinewebfonts.com` silently degrades 3 of 4 weights to system fallback with no error.

**[LOW] `.sm { display:none; }` — no corresponding show rule visible**
This class hides elements globally with no visible media query re-showing them. If `.sm` is used for mobile-only content, it will be permanently hidden unless a `display:block !important` rule exists later in the stylesheet (not visible in truncated source).

**[LOW] `max-height:1000000px` on all elements**
```css
*{max-height:1000000px;}
```
This is a known Gmail clipping workaround (Gmail clips HTML emails exceeding ~102KB). Its presence suggests the email is at or near Gmail's clip threshold — clipped emails show a "View entire message" link and suppress the pixel/open tracking for that portion.
