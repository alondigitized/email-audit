# Set It Bright for the right price. 🤑
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, e.l.f. finally got out of the toner/serum loop — this is color/face, not skincare, and the hero copy actually made me laugh out loud: "$64 for a setting powder?! yeah bright." That's a real hook, comparing itself to prestige pricing (Charlotte Tilbury/Hourglass territory) to justify the $12 ask. It's a legit price-anchoring flex and it lands for someone like me who's doing points math on every $12-vs-$64 comparison anyway.
- That said, once I scroll past the hero it turns into the same e.l.f. grab-bag I've seen all week — a bronzing drops GWP tucked under a $50 threshold, then Top Picks, then six gray accordion category bars that add zero value in a screenshot. This brand runs "everything on sale, everything a GWP" so often that even a good hook gets buried under filler by the time I'd hit unsubscribe fatigue. I'd click the hero button, skip everything after the Top Picks row, and this is a "read, maybe click, don't forward" — it's not shareable, it's just a decent single-item promo.
- Compared to what's in my inbox this month: Sephora's Clean-at-Sephora send buried its offer under an App Fest banner, and this at least leads with the offer immediately — no scroll needed. But Sally Beauty's COLORfest send has run nine times this month with one mechanic, and e.l.f. is heading the same direction with GWP-everything; I'm losing the ability to tell one e.l.f. email from the next.

## What's working

- The $64-vs-$12 price anchor in the hero headline is a genuinely clever hook — most beauty emails don't do direct price comparisons like that.
- One clear white pill CTA ("SHOP NOW") sits right under the offer, no scrolling required on the hero.
- Product shot is a real, well-lit image of the actual quad — no stock/lifestyle filler, no lorem-ipsum, no broken renders.
- "Top Picks for You" section at least gestures at personalization with prices and shop-now buttons per item, even if it's generic bestsellers.

## What's weak

- Zero tier/loyalty content — no e.l.f. Beauty Squad points math, no mention of my status, nothing. Sephora and Ulta both work rewards math into nearly every send; this brand's own loyalty plug is just a generic "download the app" footer icon.
- The GWP threshold ($50+ for Bronzing Drops) is a completely separate offer from the $12 headline item — mixing two CTAs one screen apart splits my attention instead of reinforcing one action.
- Six identical gray "NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE / HAIR" bars are pure filler that add scroll fatigue without visual interest — this is where I'd stop reading.
- No shade range or skin-tone personalization anywhere, despite this being a face/complexion product — pretty relevant omission for a setting powder.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the six gray category accordion bars entirely — they add nothing in a static render and just push the footer further down; replace with a second product or a review/rating strip instead.
- 2. Pick ONE hero offer. Either lead with the $12 powder or the $50+ GWP, not both stacked back to back — right now they compete instead of building toward one action.
- 3. Add e.l.f. Beauty Squad points value next to the price ("$12 = X points") — Sephora and Ulta customers like me expect that math, and e.l.f. never gives it to me.
- 4. Show a shade/undertone swatch for the powder quad since setting/color-correcting powders live and die by matching skin tone.
- **Subject Alt A:** `A $12 powder that shames $64 ones`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Set It Bright: bake, blur, correct — $12`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New color-correcting setting powder, plus a free bronzer at $50+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Blur, bake & brighten for less than your latte budget`
