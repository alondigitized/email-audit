# Your Halloween look starts here 🎃
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- The hero photo is the best part of this email. A woman with curly black hair and bright red tips is laughing, with orange eyeshadow. That's a real Halloween-adjacent look, and it made me stop. Then I scrolled and the pretty picture fell apart. The headline promises "Halloween plans? Start here." Right underneath is a 15% off offer, but it only works if I buy online and pick up in store. The product grid below is four ion color-care items: a booster, a 20-volume developer, a color sealer packette and an after-color packette. None of them are Halloween products, and none have a price.
- I've been tracking Sally for a week, and it's been a cycle of offers. On 10/5 I got a clean 20% off with BIGDEALS20. On 10/6 that turned into four mini-deals. On 10/8 it was "No tricks here—just savings" with no savings in the hero. Now it's 15% off with a pickup condition. The offer got smaller and harder to use each time. Sally has also shown me the "Get $5 off $25" rewards strip in every send since 10/2.
- Would I click? Maybe, only to check whether 15% stacks with anything. I wouldn't forward it. If I'm shopping for a Halloween look, I'm at Ulta or Sephora, not buying developer.

## What's working

- The hero photo is bold and fun. The red curls and orange shadow look like a real Halloween look.
- The 15% off is stated in plain words in the hero, so I don't need a code.
- The "Shop now" button on the dark burgundy panel is high contrast and easy to tap.
- The row of benefit icons (free shipping $40+, 2-hour delivery, buy online pick up in store) tells me quickly how I can get my order.
- The rewards strip at the top is visible, so members and prospects both see it.

## What's weak

- The offer is buried in the subhead and carries a pickup condition. It would be much clearer as a big number.
- The product grid has nothing to do with Halloween. The hero shows hair color, but the products are color boosters and developer.
- None of the four products has a price, a sale tag or a review count. I do points math, and there's nothing here to calculate.
- The "Shop now" links under each product look identical, so I can't tell what each one is for.
- The "$5 off $25" strip is a stranger's pitch. I'm already enrolled in Rewards, and nothing in the email recognizes that.
- The fine print is a huge wall of text and it's why I distrust the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the headline the offer: "15% off + free pickup" in a big pill, in the hero.
- 2. Make the product grid match the hero. Show the red color kit or temporary Halloween color, plus nails, glitter and face makeup. Include prices and a "Sale" tag.
- 3. Add a deadline. Right now there's no urgency, and the fine print only tells me it expires 10/31.
- 4. Personalize the rewards strip. Show me a points balance instead of a sign-up pitch.
- 5. Cut the fine print to two lines and link out to the full terms.
- **Subject Alt A:** `15% off Halloween color + nails — pickup only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Red tips, orange shadow: get the look for less`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order online, pick up in store, save 15% on color, nails and more.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template. The QA run found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings (57% pass rate). The substantive problems are an unresolved `[CACHEBUSTER]` macro in a third-party pixel, a recipient email address in image URLs, a non-HTTPS pixel, and several images with no alt text. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unresolved macro:** the pixel at `https://t.myvisualiq.net/impression_pixel?r=[CACHEBUSTER]&et=i&ago=212&…` still has the literal `[CACHEBUSTER]` in `r=`. The ad-server macro was never substituted, so the pixel can be cached and under-count, or be rejected.
- **Mixed content:** `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2990&…` loads over plain HTTP. Clients and proxies may block it, so the tracking doesn't fire. It also leaks the request in cleartext.
- **Unverified links:** 50 tracking or click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and redirect chains are unverified. The QA data has no evidence either way on whether any are broken.
- **Pixel count:** the source shows at least three third-party pixels (`pxl.mon-trk.com`, `myvisualiq.net`, `dotomi.com`) on top of the Evergage images. Each adds a request that can slow or fail rendering.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, dynamic content:** four Evergage images, `…/campaign/rfFwW/172J1/{1,2,3,4}/summary.png`, have no alt. These are likely the personalized content blocks, so with images blocked those slots are blank and unreadable to screen readers.
- **Missing alt text, pixels:** the tracking pixels `003aZ00000rqS8GQAU` (mon-trk), `impression_pixel` and `UCMController` have no alt. The QA flags them, but 1×1 pixels should carry `alt=""` and be hidden from assistive tech.
- **Empty image source:** one `<img>` has an empty `src` and no alt (`Image missing alt text: : src:`). Some clients request the page URL for an empty `src`. Remove the tag or populate it.
- **Markup validity:** `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` is not self-closed under the XHTML doctype. This is minor and has no known rendering impact.
- The HTML was truncated, so I can't verify the rest of the body, including `lang`, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and link text.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **PII in URLs:** the Evergage image URLs contain `userId=rae-l@ete…`, which is the recipient's email address in plaintext. The QA output cuts it off, but the pattern is visible. It sits in query strings that can land in proxy, CDN and third-party logs. It also shows up in image-proxy and forwarding scenarios.
- **Identifier in pixel path:** the `pxl.mon-trk.com/…/2323102/003aZ00000rqS8GQAU` path appears to carry a Salesforce Contact ID (`003…` prefix). It is a stable per-user identifier exposed to a third party.
- **Unresolved macro:** `[CACHEBUSTER]` (see Section 2).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** neither header was found. The QA notes say the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed sender defect. Without them there is no one-click unsubscribe, which Gmail and Yahoo require of bulk senders.
- **SPF/DKIM/DMARC:** `Authentication-Results` was not captured, so these can't be verified from this data.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** both are outside the truncated source I have, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
UTM parameters and landing-page alignment couldn't be assessed. The 50 click-tracking links were not probed, and the truncated HTML doesn't show final destinations. No issues are confirmed here.

1. **Fix the `[CACHEBUSTER]` substitution** in the VisualIQ pixel, or remove the pixel.
2. **Change the Dotomi pixel** (`login.dotomi.com`) to HTTPS, or drop it if it isn't essential.
3. **Remove the plaintext email from the `userId=` parameter** on the Evergage URLs. Use an opaque ID instead.
4. **Add alt text** to the four Evergage `summary.png` images, using descriptive text or fallback copy. Set `alt=""` on the tracking pixels.
5. **Remove or populate the empty-`src` `<img>`.**
6. **Check List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post and the authentication headers** against the original message headers in the source mailbox, not the relay copy, before calling them defects.
