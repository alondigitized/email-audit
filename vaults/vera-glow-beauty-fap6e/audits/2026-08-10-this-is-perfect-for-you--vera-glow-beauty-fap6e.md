# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- It's Skechers, again, retargeting me on a men's slip-in walker I never so much as glanced at — same playbook as "This is perfect for you!" from 7/28, just swapping the workout walker in for the grey slip-in. "You've got great taste!" is doing a lot of unearned flattering of a men's Go Walk sneaker that has nothing to do with me, a 29-year-old woman who lives in Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond math. There's no beauty content here at all, obviously — it's a shoe brand, so I'm auditing this purely as an off-category intruder in my inbox — but even grading on that curve, this is a weaker send than Skechers' own 7/28 one, which at least made me laugh.
- Compare it to how e.l.f. SKIN hooked me with the "$17 vs $185" dupe framing — that felt like it knew my brain. This is the opposite: a generic "here's more of what you didn't want" grid with zero personalization signal beyond "you looked at a shoe once, so now every walker/slip-in/sneaker in a five-mile radius is yours forever." I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding, I'm archiving without a second thought.

## What's working

- The hero product shot is clean and clearly rendered — no broken images, no layout bugs.
- "TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" is at least an honest, low-pressure CTA instead of a fake urgency play.
- The 25% off kids back-to-school banner is the one genuinely concrete, time-bound offer in the whole email (valid 8/5–8/13).

## What's weak

- Every single product shown — hero plus six "more options" — is a men's shoe. I'm a woman. This is retargeting with zero gender filtering, which is a bigger miss than Skechers' 7/29 grey-slip-in send.
- "You've got great taste!" as a headline for a men's Go Walk sneaker I never clicked is just wrong — it reads like a merge-field mismatch, not a compliment.
- The kids' 25% off banner is buried below six unrelated product tiles and a wall of "SHOP ADDITIONAL STYLES" text links — Ulta wouldn't bury an active offer that deep.
- Nothing here is remotely beauty-adjacent, so as far as my actual shopping life goes this is 100% noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the gender targeting before anything else — if I've never engaged with men's product, stop serving me an entire grid of it.
- 2. Move the 25% off kids sale to the top if that's the real offer of the day — don't bury the one concrete discount under six irrelevant retargeted sneakers.
- 3. Kill "You've got great taste!" — it only works if the product shown actually matches something I looked at.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `25% off kids' styles, auto-applied at checkout — thru 8/13`
