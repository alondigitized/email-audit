# What makes us smell so good?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I'm sitting here with thirteen Lush emails in my inbox since mid-May, and I want to give this one the fair hearing it actually earns: the concept is smart. "What's inside counts" — mapping four key ingredients to four specific products — is real editorial work. Lavender Oil → Sleepy, Patchouli → Karma, Balsam Fir Resin → Shiny Daisy, Rose Absolute → Rose Jam. That's a through-line. It's more interesting than the fragrance-layering concept from #12 two days ago, and it tells me something I didn't already know, which is rare in promo email. The color-coded gradient blocks by product make it navigable at a skim without reading a single word.
- Here's my problem: it's entirely a content play. Not one offer anywhere. I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond — I am always doing the loyalty math — and this email treats me identically to someone who's never bought a Lush product. No tier callout, no "earn 2x points on fragrance this week," nothing. Compare that to ILIA's "Meet the Soft Focus family" roundup last week: same editorial-meets-catalog format, but with a clear buy moment built in. This reads like a very pretty blog post. And Father's Day — the one time-sensitive commercial hook they had — gets stapled to the bottom below four deep-dive modules like an afterthought.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe loop back if I was already planning a Karma purchase. I'm not clicking today.

## What's working

- "What's inside counts" is genuinely good wordplay — I'll give Lush that pun without qualification
- Ingredient-to-product mapping is a real differentiator; Charlotte Tilbury's beach bag email from yesterday was gorgeous and taught me nothing — this actually tells me why Karma smells the way it does
- Color-coded gradients per ingredient section make the email skimmable without reading; that's solid visual design doing real navigation work
- Model diversity across all four sections is excellent — multiple skin tones, hair textures, age-range representation
- "Shop Fragrance" hero CTA is clean, specific, and above the fold

## What's weak

- Every in-body CTA reads "Read More" — that's a reading invitation, not a purchase trigger; I am a shopper
- Zero offer anywhere: no percentage off, no points multiplier, no loyalty tier callout, no shipping threshold
- Father's Day module appears as an unconnected footer with no narrative bridge from the ingredient story — feels like two separate emails were stitched together at the last minute
- Cadence fatigue is real and compounding: thirteen emails in three weeks means even a genuinely strong concept gets discounted on arrival

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the ingredient narrative to a loyalty offer** — "Earn 2x points on fragrance through Sunday" would flip this from content to commerce without touching the editorial concept. Ulta ties a GWP or tier perk to almost every major send; Lush left the entire commercial layer blank.
- 2. **Move Father's Day to the second block**, right after the hero intro — it's the only time-sensitive hook in the email and it's losing all traffic below four explainer modules.
- 3. **Replace every "Read More" with "Shop [Product Name]"** — "Shop Sleepy," "Shop Karma," "Shop Rose Jam." Verb + product name. e.l.f. does this consistently and it's the standard for click attribution in beauty email.
- 4. **Hold the next send at least four days** — the layering email and this ingredient email are conceptually close enough to blur together; they're diluting each other.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Lavender, patchouli, rose — what they actually do`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We mapped every key scent note to the product it powers`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: last-call Father's Day fragrance gifts inside`
