# 20% off ends tomorrow
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. This is email number fifteen from ILIA since May 16, and I say that number not to be dramatic but because it shapes everything about how I'm experiencing this send. The last one — "Layers of free gifts" — actually impressed me. ILIA finally found a creative angle for this campaign, the tiered gift structure was smart, and the score reflected that. This email feels like the morning after that breakthrough, when the brand woke up and forgot the lesson.
- The subject "20% off ends tomorrow" is the most stripped-down version of this message yet — cleaner than "Shop 20% off sitewide" from email twelve, but still a step backward from the concept email that preceded it. The body actually continues the Cart Collage idea from "Layers of free gifts," which I appreciate — the tiered gift tiers (mascara on any order, bronzer/highlighter on $100+, butter cream on $175+) are still present. But then there's a whole second section — "Up to 40% off Sale*" — which is genuinely new information (and compelling), buried below the fold, with an asterisk pointing to "see site for details." Two offers, one email, no clear hierarchy.
- I'd click because the "ends tomorrow" urgency is real and I have been watching this campaign for nearly two weeks and have not bought yet. But I am not forwarding this. Glossier just sent me "30% off ends soon" yesterday with cleaner execution and one unambiguous CTA. ILIA is giving me more value but packaging it worse.

## What's working

- **"A Cart Collage" continuity** — the gift tier structure from email fourteen is still here, and it rewards people who read the last send; actual narrative continuity is rare in promotional email.
- **"Up to 40% off Sale*" is genuinely additive** — stacking F&F savings on already-reduced items is a meaningful offer that hasn't been telegraphed in every previous email; this is the clearest reason to click.
- **"Ends tomorrow" subject line hits the right urgency note** — no exclamation point stacking, no all-caps, just a credible deadline. Compare to Sephora's "ENDS TODAY‼️" hitting three times in one day — this is the more respectful version.
- **2X VIP points callout** — as someone always doing the loyalty math, seeing this explicitly called out in the Cart Collage section is a genuine push factor.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, text is legible, product photography holds up.

## What's weak

- **"SALE SALE SALE" hero is a creative regression** — we went from a conceptual "Layers of free gifts" headline to triple-stamped generic. Charlotte Tilbury's Met Gala email (email ten of their run) proved you can drop the discount language entirely and still convert; ILIA proves the opposite here.
- **Two competing offers with no clear hierarchy** — 20% sitewide AND up to 40% on sale items are both real, but the email treats them as two separate campaigns stapled together, and I had to scroll twice to understand what ILIA actually wanted me to do.
- **The asterisk on "Up to 40% off Sale*"** — "*see site for details" is a tiny-print punt. Drunk Elephant did "Adios, 25% off…" and the offer was completely unambiguous; ILIA's asterisk makes me suspicious before I click.
- **No personalization signal** — I'm a VIP. The top utility bar acknowledges "20% OFF FOR VIPs" but nothing in the body knows my name, my purchase history, or which product category I'm most likely to convert on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make "Up to 40% off" the hero, not the footnote** — this is the genuinely new offer in email fifteen of this campaign; bury the 20% sitewide in the footer where it lives as context, lead with the sale-on-sale angle as the actual headline.
- 2. **Collapse to one CTA** — "SHOP THE SALE >" and "SHOP NOW >" pointing to two different-ish value propositions splits my attention. Pick one, make it land harder.
- 3. **Drop the asterisk or explain the asterisk inline** — if select items are up to 40% off, name the categories in the email. Don't make me click to discover the fine print.
- 4. **Bring the gift tier to the subject line** — "Free butter cream with $175+ — ends tomorrow" tells me more than a fourth iteration of "20% off."
- 5. **Use my VIP status** — if ILIA's email platform knows I'm a VIP (the utility bar does), one line of personalized copy ("Your VIP perks apply, Vera") closes more gaps than any subject line tweak.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus your free mini on any order — final hours of Friends & Family`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Trio of gifts, 2X VIP points, sale-on-sale pricing — tie it up today`
