# Going... Going... Gone! 20% Off Ending Soon
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's where I am with Sally. I wrote about email twenty-three just yesterday — "Time's running out on up to 40% off" — and I even noted then that we'd hit the point of diminishing returns. Today the subject goes full auction-house with "Going... Going... Gone!" and the actual offer *shrinks*. Yesterday it was 40% off select. Today it's 20% sitewide. If I'm doing points math (and I always am), this email is objectively worse than the one I got 24 hours ago from the same sender. That's a weird sequence.
- The render is clean — no broken images, the hierarchy is legible — and the sub-deal grid (40% styling tools, 40% ion hair care, BOGO Bondbar) is organized. But the hero number is 20% and for Sally, 20% sitewide isn't a stop-the-scroll offer. Sephora's "Last chance to save up to 50% on select beauty" — the one I gave 8/10 — had a bigger number AND named the prestige category. Ulta Diamond status at least makes me feel like I'm getting something the general public isn't. This email has no tier signal at all; I'm just "one per customer."
- Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding — there's nothing here a friend couldn't find by going to sally.com themselves.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is immediately visible.** "20% off sitewide" renders as the dominant visual element above the fold; no hunting required.
- **Sub-deal grid is organized and scannable.** The four offers (40% styling, 40% ion, 20% fragrances, BOGO Bondbar) are laid out clearly with corresponding product imagery.
- **Hard deadline is stated twice.** "Ends June 26" appears in the hero copy AND the asterisk fine print below — that's the right move.
- **Free 2-hour delivery callout** is a genuine logistics differentiator for someone buying last-minute, and it's placed below the fold before the footer without cluttering the hero.
- **No render bugs.** Clean email, readable type, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- **The offer number went down.** Yesterday Sally sent 40% off select; today's hero is 20% sitewide. For anyone paying attention to this inbox, that reads as a downgrade, not urgency. e.l.f. ran their shipping urgency email (#17 in their streak) and at least held the same offer through the close — they didn't reduce the discount and call it a finale.
- **"Going... Going... Gone!" is theatrical for Sally.** This framing works for a luxury drop or a limited-edition Charlotte Tilbury shade. For a mass professional beauty retailer running its 24th promo in three weeks, it reads like a boy-who-cried-wolf moment.
- **No prestige brand names visible.** The sub-deals feature ion™ and Bondbar™ — Sally house brands. For a Sephora Rouge/Ulta Diamond cross-shopper, I want to know if any aspirational or recognized brand is included. The June 20 email that named It's a 10 got my attention precisely because it named a brand. This one doesn't.
- **Zero loyalty personalization.** "Earn more rewards" is vague. Ulta would at least gesture toward my Diamond status even in a generic blast. Sally doesn't acknowledge that I exist as a repeat customer.
- **Fragrance at Sally is a non-starter for me.** Including 20% off select fragrances as a sub-deal when the assortment skews mass-market drugstore feels like filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the best sub-deal, not the blended sitewide number.** "40% off styling tools + 20% sitewide" is a stronger hero than "20% off sitewide" because 40% leads. Charlotte Tilbury does this — "Over 50% Off!" leads the hierarchy even when not everything is 50%.
- 2. **Name a brand in the subject line.** The June 20 send that hit 9/10 worked because it called out It's a 10 by name. If Bondbar or ion or any brand with some awareness is in the deal, put it in the subject.
- 3. **Add a Rewards Points value prop to the hero copy.** "20% off + earn [X] points" makes the offer land differently for a loyalty member. Right now "earn more rewards" floats disconnected in a subhead.
- 4. **Kill the theatrical auction opener after 24 emails.** "Going... Going... Gone!" plays on send #1; by send #24 it's just noise. A factual close is more credible.
- 5. **Lock the preheader.** The all-caps banner at the top ("BIG DEALS DAYS ARE HERE | SHOP NOW") is leaking into inbox preview. That's wasted real estate where a specific dollar amount or named brand would actually move opens.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off styling tools ends tonight — plus 20% sitewide`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last 20% off ends June 26 — ion, Bondbar + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Styling tools, hair care, fragrances — biggest discounts of the sale close tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off ion + Bondbar BOGO — 20% sitewide through midnight`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based email with a responsive mobile breakpoint at 499px. The visible HTML is structurally sound, but a font-family mismatch and an anomalous `img` CSS rule are confirmed issues; several compliance and tracking checks are unresolvable due to source truncation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — tracking links (click-wrapped URLs) are not present in the truncated source.

- Sending domain `em.sallybeauty.com` is a standard ESP subdomain (pattern consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from HTML alone.
- `.EID a { color: #464646 !important }` — this blanket override on an EID class could suppress link underlines/colors on any tracked anchor placed inside that container, making links visually indistinct. Verify no CTA links fall inside `.EID`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — orphaned font reference:**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` for `Founders Grotesk` is declared anywhere in the `<style>` block. Only Open Sans variants are loaded. Every anchor in the email will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif. If Founders Grotesk is intentional brand typography, it must be loaded or the rule corrected to match the loaded stack.

**Confirmed issue — stale font version:**
All Open Sans `@font-face` sources reference `v17` on `fonts.gstatic.com`:
```
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...)
```
Current Google Fonts Open Sans is v23+. v17 URLs still resolve (Google preserves old paths), but this signals the template hasn't been updated since ~2020. No functional breakage today, but a latent risk if Google ever retires the path.

**Confirmed issue — anomalous `img` rule:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `img` is a no-op for replaced elements, but it is semantically incorrect and could interact unexpectedly with certain email client CSS parsers. The rule should be removed.

**Cannot assess** — `alt` text on images is not visible in the truncated source. This is the highest-likelihood accessibility gap in any marketing email; must be verified against full source.
