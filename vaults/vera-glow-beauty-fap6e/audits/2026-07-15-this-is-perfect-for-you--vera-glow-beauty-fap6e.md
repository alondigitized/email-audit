# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay. I need to say this upfront: I already reviewed "This is perfect for you!" — from Skechers — on June 29th. Same sender, same subject, and now I'm staring at it again on July 15th. That's not personalization, that's a CRM team that forgot to QA their template library. And here's the kicker: the other Skechers email that landed in my inbox today was "Did you forget something?" — which I flagged on July 1st as a verbatim repeat of the July 1st version. So Skechers has now recycled **two** different browse-abandon subjects on the same day. My inbox literally has a déjà vu of a déjà vu.
- Setting that aside and looking at what's actually in the email — the hero is a solid browse-abandon trigger. The BOBS Misty Kiss in that sage-and-raffia colorway is honestly cute, and "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" with the product shot front and center is a competent setup. The Skechers Plus 20% off with code SUMMER is real money and I appreciate seeing it without digging. But then the email immediately undercuts itself: the "HERE'S SOME MORE GREAT OPTIONS" grid is serving me Men's styles and what looks like Kids' shoes. I don't have kids. I didn't browse the men's section. Why is this here?
- I wouldn't click. I'd mark it read and move on — not because the offer is bad, but because Skechers has conditioned me to not believe anything in the subject line is actually new or different. Forward to a friend? Absolutely not. If she needs a sandal recommendation I'll text her, not this.

## What's working

- **Browse-abandon hero is on-brief.** "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" paired with the BOBS Misty Kiss image is clean — I know immediately why I got this email.
- **Skechers Plus 20% off with code SUMMER is prominently placed** and doesn't require fine-print hunting. That's the right call. I do the points math, and this one is visible.
- **Seasonal alignment is there.** Strappy espadrille sandal in July is exactly right. Didn't feel like a stale promo pulled from the wrong quarter.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Given how many emails I review that glitch on product grids, this is worth noting.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject from June 29.** This is the same "This is perfect for you!" that dropped 16 days ago. I gave that one a 7. This isn't a sequel, it's a photocopy.
- **Product grid shows Men's and Kids'.** I'm 29 and shopping solo. The "WE THINK YOU'LL LOVE" section then serves me styles labeled Women's, Men's, and what reads as kids' sizing — that's a filter failure, and it pushed the whole "personalized" frame straight off a cliff.
- **No price on the hero product.** ILIA showed me "NEW—brown mascara, made mini" with a price right in the body. e.l.f. Hair gave me restock urgency and a SKU. Skechers makes me click before I even know if the BOBS sandal is $60 or $120. That's friction where there shouldn't be any.
- **"TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" is soft CTA copy.** Verb + noun technically, but "look" is not an action — "Shop 20% Off" or "Grab It Before It's Gone" would have actual pull.
- **Zero deadline on the 20% off.** Code SUMMER, no expiration visible anywhere in the body. I'm a points-math person — I want to know if this expires tonight or in two weeks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this subject line permanently.** It's now run twice. Any A/B test with a fresh line will beat it — there's no data to protect anymore. The June 29 send already burned the novelty.
- 2. **Show the price in the hero.** Right under the product name: "BOBS Misty Kiss — Summer Stroll | **$XX** → **$XX** with code SUMMER." Done. I can do the math before I click, which means I'm more likely to click.
- 3. **Filter the rec grid to women's only.** If the trigger was a women's browse, the recs should be women's. Pull men's and kids' out entirely for this segment.
- 4. **Add an expiration to the code.** "Offer ends Sunday" or even "this week only" — anything credible. Right now the 20% off feels open-ended and that kills urgency.
- 5. **Swap "TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" for "Shop 20% Off Now."** CTA copy should name the value, not just the behavior.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOBS Misty Kiss is waiting — 20% off ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still in your cart. Now 20% off with SUMMER.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus code SUMMER takes it down. Offer ends [date].`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your sandal, your discount — don't leave it twice.`

## Technical Audit — SKECHERS "This is perfect for you!" (Browse Abandonment)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is sent via Attentive (`msgs.skechers.com` / `skechers.attentivemail.com`) with standard ESP click-wrapping. HTML is well-formed with MSO/VML Outlook conditionals present. Audit is limited by source truncation — body, footer, and image blocks are not fully visible.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All links are Attentive click-wrapped** via `skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...`. This is expected for the ESP, but:

- **UTM parameters on destination URLs cannot be confirmed** without decoding the encoded redirect payloads. If Attentive is not configured to append UTM params to the final destination, click attribution in GA4/analytics will be lost.
- The "web version" link in `row-1 column-2` resolves through the same click redirect — confirm it lands on a hosted mirror, not a 404, post-send.

**Action needed:** Decode one redirect URL and verify the destination contains `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` at minimum.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| `<title>` tag is empty | `<title></title>` — screen readers announce this as the document title; some email clients surface it in thread previews |
| `alt` text on images | Cannot confirm — image blocks are in truncated portion; must verify all `<img>` tags have non-empty `alt` attributes |
| Preheader padding method | Uses a mix of U+034F (combining grapheme joiner `͏`) and U+00AD (soft hyphen `­`) — functional but CGJ characters are stripped by some aggressive spam filters, which could cause preheader bleed into visible body |

MSO VML namespaces, `role="presentation"` on tables, and the 620px mobile breakpoint are all correctly implemented.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- Subject line `"This is perfect for you!"` and preheader `"Take another look before these items sell out."` contain **no recipient name token** — acceptable for browse abandonment, but common to include `{{first_name}}` at minimum.
- **Cannot audit product recommendation tokens** (SKU, price, image URL) in the body — source is truncated. These are critical for a browse abandonment send; unfired tokens (e.g., `{{product_name}}` rendering literally) would be a P0 issue. Verify in the full source or a seed send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Cannot fully audit** from truncated source. The following must be confirmed in the full HTML:

- [ ] Physical mailing address present in footer (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5))
- [ ] One-click unsubscribe link present and functional (`List-Unsubscribe` header ideally present at the sending infrastructure level)
- [ ] Sending domain `msgs.skechers.com` has valid SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records — verify with `dig TXT msgs.skechers.com` and `dig TXT _dmarc.skechers.com`

From address `hello@msgs.skechers.com` is a subdomain send — confirm DMARC alignment (`msgs.skechers.com` must be covered by `skechers.com` DMARC policy or have its own).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- Cannot confirm UTM params on CTA destinations without decoding Attentive redirect URLs (see §2).
- Sending domain `msgs.skechers.com` is distinct from `skechers.com` — links should resolve to `www.skechers.com` product pages. Confirm no redirect chains that drop UTM params mid-hop.
- Browse abandonment emails should deep-link to the specific browsed product(s), not the homepage. Verify in seed send.
