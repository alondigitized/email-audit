# *poof* the results are in
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. We are at twenty-three ILIA emails in June, and this one — a consumer-study proof send for the Soft Focus Finishing Powder — lands on the exact same day ILIA is clearly trying to close the loop on a product they've been building toward since May 29. And here's the honest truth: the execution is genuinely good. The "proof is in the powder" hero line earns the pun. The three percentage stats (95%, 95%, 93%) give me something real to grab onto after two straight emails that leaned more editorial. The "beaming reviews" section with the "held my makeup without budging for hours" testimonial is exactly the wear-time proof I need before clicking Add to Cart on a prestige finishing powder.
- But I've seen the Soft Focus compact in my inbox four times in five days. If I haven't bought yet, I'm either waiting for an offer, or I've decided no. This email doesn't move the needle for the "waiting for a deal" camp — there's no discount, no urgency, no points hook. It's a well-crafted social proof play that would work hard if it landed earlier in the launch sequence. At email four? It's preaching to the already-converted.
- I'd click — I'm genuinely a fence-sitter on this compact and the review pulled me. I would not forward it. This is a conversion email, not a conversation starter.

## What's working

- **"Proof is in the powder" hero line** — the wordplay is earned, not forced, and it pairs with the open compact shot in a way that feels like a creative decision, not a pun factory
- **Three consumer study stats** — 95%, 95%, 93% give me actual claims to evaluate; this is the beauty-science credibility I've been trained to look for after years of reading ingredient-forward brands
- **"SHOP SOFT FOCUS" CTA** — specific verb + noun, dark button, zero ambiguity; Charlotte Tilbury could learn from this (their CTAs are consistently buried in digest noise)
- **Model photography** — close-up of a woman with my approximate age and a natural glow, not a retouched aspirational shot that feels like a 45-year-old brand campaign
- **Visual hierarchy** — product → headline → claims → reviews → CTA; my eye never got lost

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero urgency** — four Soft Focus emails in and there's still no incentive; e.l.f. at $7–$12 price points can skip the discount, but a prestige powder needs a reason to buy now
- **Campaign fatigue is real** — the June 3 ingredient breakdown was genuinely interesting and I'd score it just as high, but ILIA has depleted the goodwill buffer; the "*poof*" subject is cute and still works, but it's running on fumes
- **No loyalty signal anywhere** — I'm a Sephora Rouge member; a single "Earn Beauty Insider points on ILIA" line in the top banner or footer would make me feel known; instead I'm getting the same email as someone who's never heard of the brand
- **The stats are tiny on mobile** — the three percentage blocks stack vertically and the explanatory text beneath each one is small enough that I'd have to zoom to read the qualifier language

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a loyalty hook in the banner** — something like "Earn 3× Insider points at Sephora through Sunday" is the single highest-leverage change; it turns a generic proof email into a timed conversion lever for the exact customer this is aimed at
- 2. **Layer in credible scarcity** — this is a new launch; "Shade X selling fast" or "Restock not guaranteed" is believable and would give the fence-sitters a reason to act today
- 3. **Consolidate the stats into an infographic panel** — three stacked percentage blocks with small copy underneath is hard to scan at 375px width; a single side-by-side trio would be more punchable on mobile
- 4. **Bring the review quote into the hero** — "held my makeup without budging for hours" is the strongest line in the entire email and it's buried below the fold; lead with social proof, then back it up with the study stats
- 5. **Personalize at minimum with a shade reference** — even "for your shade range" as a hook would be enough to signal that ILIA knows me
