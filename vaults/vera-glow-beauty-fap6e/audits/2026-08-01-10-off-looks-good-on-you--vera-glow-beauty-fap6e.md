# 10% off looks good on you
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is Drunk Elephant's subscription pitch, and my gut reaction is "fine, but I've seen this exact hero shot layout from them like three times now." "Running on empty? Not so much." into "all good things get Subscribe to Save" — cute copy, but it's doing a LOT of work to dress up what is, functionally, an autoship ad. Compare that to last week's "Our top shelf" catalog dump or "Lippe lock" — DE has been alternating between "here's our whole wall" and "please subscribe" for weeks now, and neither is really a sale.
- The real offer buried at the bottom — free 5-piece set ($21 value) with $110+ orders — is honestly the most compelling thing in this whole email, and it's shoved under a five-step tutorial on HOW to subscribe. I shouldn't need a numbered instructional list to understand a discount code. Sephora's Beauty Insider Celebration send (8/10 last week) leads with the offer in giant gold type; this leads with a philosophy lesson about your skin barrier.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the "SHOP NOW" for the free gift set, since $110 isn't a crazy threshold for me and free product is free product. Would I forward this to a friend? No — this reads like DE nudging existing subscribers, not a launch or event worth sharing.

## What's working

- The product lineup shot (Lala Retro, C-Firma, Protini, T.L.C.) is clean and recognizable — I know these SKUs on sight, which is a small trust win.
- "10% off and free shipping on every order" is at least a concrete, specific number, not vague "save big" language.
- The $21-value free gift with $110+ is a real, quantified incentive buried at the bottom — that's the one thing here I'd actually act on.
- Dates and exclusions are spelled out in the fine print (7/27–8/3, 7/20–8/21) instead of hiding behind an asterisk with no explanation.

## What's weak

- The whole email is pitching a subscription, not a sale — "10% off looks good on you" in the subject reads like a discount, but it's actually a recurring-order discount, which feels like a bait-and-switch the moment you open it.
- Five numbered steps to explain "subscribe to a website" is patronizing and pushes the actual value (10% off, free shipping) to step 4 instead of leading with it.
- The better offer — the free 5-piece gift set — is crammed at the very bottom after all the subscription instructions, when it should've been the hero.
- Zero personalization: no skin type, no "you bought Protini last time, refill it," nothing that acknowledges I'm Rouge/Diamond tier and probably already own half these products.
- No urgency on the subscribe offer itself (the gift set has dates, but "Subscribe to Save" reads evergreen, which kills any reason to act today vs. next month).

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the free 5-piece set ($21 value, $110+ orders) as the hero — that's the actual limited-time offer and it's currently an afterthought below the fold.
- 2. Cut the 5-step tutorial down to one line: "10% off + free shipping, every order, no games." Nobody needs a wizard to explain autoship.
- 3. Add a subscriber-specific hook — "reorder your Protini" or similar — since DE clearly has purchase history on Rouge/Diamond-tier repeat buyers like me.
- 4. Give the subscription offer its own deadline or incentive stack (e.g., "subscribe by 8/10 and get double points") so it doesn't read as a permanent, skippable footnote.
- 5. Trim the legal-heavy fine print block visually — it's currently as long as the actual offer copy, which makes the whole email feel more terms-and-conditions than gift.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $21 gift is hiding at $110`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Refill mode: 10% off, no thinking required`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 5-piece set ($21 value) on orders $110+, through 8/3`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Subscribe once, save every time — plus a gift while it lasts`
