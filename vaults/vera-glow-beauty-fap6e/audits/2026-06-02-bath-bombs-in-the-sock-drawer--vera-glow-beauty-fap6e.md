# Bath bombs in the sock drawer?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Ten. We are at ten Lush emails in roughly three weeks, and I want to give this one a fair shake because the concept — "No Gatekeeping Here," staff sharing personal product obsessions — is genuinely one of the better editorial ideas Lush has floated since the Karma deep-dive on May 22. The visual is clean, the structure is editorial not promotional, and there's a real launch buried in here (Super Fairy Perfume) that I actually wanted to know about.
- But "No Peeky" as the hero CTA broke me. I read it three times. What am I not peeking at? The drawer? The email itself? The products? Lush's quirky copy is usually a trust signal for me — it's part of why I keep opening these — but this one crossed from charming into actively confusing. And while the multi-section format shows range, there is no price anywhere, no offer, no loyalty hook, nothing that tells me why I should act today versus whenever the mood strikes.
- I would not click. I would clock the Super Fairy Perfume as "apparently a thing now" and go look it up directly, giving Lush zero conversion credit. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **"No Gatekeeping Here" is a strong editorial premise** — "staff favorites" is a format I trust more than a sale banner, and the personalized framing at least suggests a human curated this
- **Visual hierarchy is clean top-to-bottom** — each card-section is clearly delineated, no overlapping text, nothing broken or cropped
- **New Super Fairy Perfume section stands out visually** — the pink/vibrant color block breaks the neutral palette and signals something new, which is the closest thing to urgency in this email
- **Bath bomb work-drawer card is thematically clever** — the idea of normalizing bath products as desk accessories lands for me; it's a real lifestyle reframe, not just a product shot

## What's weak

- **"No Peeky" CTA is genuinely confusing** — as a primary button action, it fails the verb-noun test and made me stop reading to decode it instead of click
- **No offer, no price, no points — nothing to act on** — Charlotte Tilbury's recent digest format (scored 9/10) ran the same multi-product structure but included prices and per-item "add to bag"; Lush gave me vibes and a mystery button
- **Super Fairy Perfume is buried five sections deep** — if this is a launch, this should be the hero; ILIA's "New Soft Focus Finishing Powder" email (scored 8/10) showed exactly how to tease a drop with a hard date and then land it cleanly in one email
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the softest send in the last six, and it's arriving at the precise moment my tolerance for non-deal Lush content is lowest

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Super Fairy Perfume launch** — flip the email so the new scent is the hero with a clear "New Arrival" badge, a price, and a direct product link; the "staff favorites" content works as the second section, not the first
- 2. **Kill "No Peeky," replace with something actionable** — "Shop Their Picks," "Grab My Favorites," or even "See the Stash" — anything with a noun
- 3. **Add a single loyalty hook** — one line that says "Rouge members: X points on any order today" would do more for my click rate than three more editorial sections
- 4. **Consolidate the multi-section format** — five product categories in one email dilutes each one; pick three and give them room to breathe; the Bath Bomb drawer card is the strongest, build around it
- 5. **Move Super Fairy Perfume timing** — if this is a launch today, it earns a standalone send; if it's just "available," say so with a price and a link
- **Subject Alt A:** `New drop: Super Fairy Perfume is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Lush edit: the drawer, the melt, the new scent`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Staff obsessions + a fragrance launch you're going to want`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bath bomb at your desk, massage bar in your bag — and something new`
