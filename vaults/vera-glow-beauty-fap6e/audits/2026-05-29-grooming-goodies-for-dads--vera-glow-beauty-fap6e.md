# Grooming goodies for Dads
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails from Lush in seventeen days, and this is the one where I get a bearded man. I get it — Father's Day is Sunday, the timing is correct, and honestly the Karma email on May 22 had me genuinely excited to open the next one. But the pivot from "gorgeous botanical fragrances for you" to "grooming goodies for dads" hits different when I've been building a mental picture of Lush as my self-care brand. The email is clean, numbered product callouts are a nice touch, and the hero headline "Breeze Into Beard Care" is breezy enough. The problem is that I am not buying beard oil for myself and nobody has given me a reason to buy it for someone else — there's no discount, no bundle, no free-with-purchase hook, nothing. Just vibes and beard wash.
- The copy in the "Gifts for Dads" section has a visible grammar slip — "to make feel special on their special day" — which is the kind of thing that makes me trust a brand slightly less in the moment. Small thing, but I noticed it.
- Mark as read. Might revisit Saturday if I'm panic-gifting for a dad.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing is right.** Father's Day is days away and the hook is anchored to that — unlike the May 27 "Stop and smell the flowers" email which had zero calendar urgency.
- **Numbered product cards work.** The #1/#2/#3 structure makes it scannable; I immediately knew Kalamazoo is the wash, Cosmetic Lad is the moisturizer, and the oil section is a clear separate tier.
- **Ingredient specificity.** Each product card calls out a key ingredient — enzyme wash, aloe vera, argan/almond/jojoba, sandalwood/cedarwood. Lush knows their customer reads labels.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero → numbered list → two-up oil grid → gifting closer. No clutter, no competing offers.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Charlotte Tilbury's email this week gave me free minis with purchase. ILIA had Friends + Family at 20% off. Lush is asking me to spend money on Dad with zero incentive — not even a "free bath bomb with $50 spend" tier.
- **Grammar error visible in body copy.** "to make feel special on their special day" is missing "them." Feels like it slipped QA.
- **The demographic mismatch is loud.** A bearded man in a plaid shirt is the full visual experience. I know this is a gifting email, but there's no woman-as-gifter framing, no "treat him" copy angle — it reads like the email wandered into my inbox by accident.
- **No deadline or urgency signal.** Father's Day is six days away and the email doesn't say that. ILIA put a hard date in their teaser subject. Lush left it implied.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email eight in seventeen days. The May 27 flowers email already felt like filler, and this one doesn't reset anything — it just continues the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an offer.** Even a "free Dirty shower gel when you spend $40 on Dad" gives me a reason to click today vs. Sunday. The May 22 Karma email worked because Karma is a known SKU with a scent story — this one has no anchor.
- 2. **Put Father's Day date in the hero or subject.** "Father's Day is [date]" in small copy under the headline does a lot of work for procrastinators.
- 3. **Frame through the gifter.** One sentence — "Pick the scent he'll actually use" or "We tested these on real beards" — acknowledges that *I'm* the one shopping, not him.
- 4. **Fix the body copy grammar error** in the "Gifts for Dads" section.
- 5. **Consider a gift-set product call.** If there's a Father's Day bundle SKU, it should be the hero — not four separate items I have to mentally assemble.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your dad's face will thank you`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Beard oils, washes & more — Father's Day picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fine essential oils + enzymatic washes — all made for rad dads. Free shipping $80+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day is [date]. Here are the grooming hits he'll actually reach for.`
