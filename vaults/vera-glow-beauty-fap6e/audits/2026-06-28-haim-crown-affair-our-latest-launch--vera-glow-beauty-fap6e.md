# HAIM, Crown Affair + our latest launch
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Nine. I know it's nine because I've been keeping a mental tally the same way I track ILIA's limited-edition launch windows — with an unhealthy amount of attention. And the thing about email nine is that it forces a question the brand has been dodging: *what am I supposed to do with this information?* The email bundles a HAIM collab, a Crown Affair recap (which ILIA already announced on June 23 — five days ago — in what scored an 8/10 precisely because it was tightly focused), a new powder, and a GWP all into one text-heavy newsletter format that reads like a founder catch-up email more than a purchase trigger.
- I genuinely like ILIA. I own the Super Serum Skin Tint, I was the first in my group chat to recommend Crown Affair's Dry Shampoo. So the collab angle isn't lost on me. But when I'm on email nine in ten days and the lead message is "We're fans of summer because of farmers markets and lazy picnics" — I am not thinking about adding to cart. I'm thinking about whether to scroll all the way to Unsubscribe.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. I would not click. I would not forward this — if a friend asked me about the HAIM collab I'd just DM her the product name, which is the brand's real loss here.

## What's working

- **HAIM partnership is actually a good hook** — GRAMMY-nominated collab with recognizable artists is legit cultural currency, and I haven't seen this one before in my inbox.
- **Free sharpener GWP is a specific, low-friction incentive** — "buy the liner + lip gloss, get the sharpener" is a believable offer and I can picture exactly what I'm getting.
- **The women in the photo read authentically** — the visual demo feels like real people in their 20s-30s, not overly styled stock photography.
- **Summer tone lands without feeling stale** — the copy at least matches the calendar, which is more than Glossier managed during their June non-sale sale.
- **No render bugs or layout breakage** — clean read, images loaded, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **"Hi there" in 2026 is a crime** — I am a known ILIA customer with a purchase history and a preference profile. Charlotte Tilbury has been greeting me by first name on every send since I first ordered. This reads like I'm on a cold list.
- **Crown Affair feels like a recap, not a launch** — this same collab was the *entire* subject line of the June 23 email, which landed as a focused 8/10. Five days later it's buried as bullet four in a newsletter. That's a demotion, not a reminder.
- **No CTA button visible** — the email leans on text links or inline mentions rather than a clear "Shop HAIM x ILIA" button. e.l.f. would never; every single e.l.f. send I've reviewed this month had a high-contrast tap target front and center.
- **Newsletter format dilutes the HAIM story** — four separate announcements in one send means none of them get enough air. Ulta does this with their digest emails and it always feels like I'm being handed a menu instead of a recommendation.
- **Zero loyalty signal** — I am on the level where I expect ILIA to at least acknowledge I'm a repeat buyer. Points math, early access, something. Nothing here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate the sends** — the HAIM collab alone could carry its own email with a single hero image, a tight story, and one CTA. Crown Affair already had its moment. The Soft Focus Powder deserves a standalone skincare-mode send the way "Skincare Mode: Summer" tried to do. Bundling four launches into a newsletter-style email collapses all of them.
- 2. **Add a persistent CTA button per section** — at minimum, "Shop the HAIM Duo" as a button under the HAIM copy, not just inline text. Mobile tap targets matter and I am reading this on my phone in the school pickup line.
- 3. **Personalize the greeting or the offer** — even "Hi Vera" would shift the tone. Better: surface a product from my actual purchase history ("You love the Super Serum Tint — the new Soft Focus Powder was made to go on top of it").
- 4. **Retire Crown Affair from this send** — it was the hero on June 23. Let it breathe. If you need to drive it again, own it with a subject line and a fresh angle. Don't bury it as a fourth bullet.
- 5. **Add a deadline to the GWP** — "free sharpener while supplies last" is a clock-less offer. Give me a date and suddenly I have a reason to act today instead of next week (or never).

- **Subject Alt B:** `New: the duo HAIM actually uses`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `GRAMMY-nominated collab, clean formula, dropping now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: free sharpener when you grab the Lip Sketch duo.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — broken destination URLs (post-redirect resolution):**

| QA Finding | URL |
|---|---|
| 404 | `https://iliabeauty.com/pages/haim-x-ilia)` — note trailing `)` in URL (likely a copy/paste artifact from markdown) |

| 404 | `https://manage.kmail-lists.com/subscriptions/unsubscribe?a=3DSguDsz&=` — `a=3D` is un-decoded quoted-printable (`=3D` → `=`); the parameter value `SguDsz` is correct but the `=3D` encoding was not decoded before the link was tested or served |

**Root cause pattern:** The `=3D` occurrences in the unsubscribe URL and the trailing `),` in product URLs strongly suggest quoted-printable encoding was applied to the HTML body but not fully decoded before link extraction — either in the sending pipeline or the QA checker. However, the trailing `)` and `),` on the product URLs are a separate bug: those punctuation characters appear to have been included inside the `href` attribute value rather than outside it.

**Tracking pixel:** `https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01KW837P8XAASCZR1SE63YRV6Y` is present (open-tracking pixel). No issues with the tracker URL itself, but the `<img>` has no `alt=""` attribute (see §3).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Missing alt text on two images:**
  - `2aa0e67a-8e4a-49f9-900e-e2378e86b5d1.jpeg` — main content image, `alt=""` attribute is present in the tag but the value is empty string. For a decorative image this is technically acceptable, but if this image carries product/campaign content, it should have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocking clients.
  - Tracking pixel `trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01KW837P8XAASCZR1SE63YRV6Y` — tracking pixels should explicitly carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress broken-image icons in blocking clients. Verify the attribute is present, not just omitted.

- **Font import via `@import` inside `<style>`:** `@import url("https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css")` is wrapped in an MSO conditional comment (`<!--[if !mso]>`), which is correct practice. No issue.

- **Inline `<p>` inside `<li>`:** The HTML nests `<p>` elements directly inside `<li>` elements. This is valid HTML5 but can cause unexpected spacing in older email clients (Outlook 2016/2019 in particular adds extra margin). Low severity but worth noting.

- **Mixed font-size units:** Body copy uses `font-size: 15px` on `<span>` elements, while `<li>` elements declare `font-size: 11pt`. These are not equivalent (11pt ≈ 14.67px). This can produce inconsistent rendering across clients.
