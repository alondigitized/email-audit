# Curl the e.l.f. up with this eye-ssential duo 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so e.l.f. is now on day five of a daily send streak, and they've finally pivoted from complexion territory into eyes — after the skin tint push, the Halo Glow highlighter drop, the setting spray, the blush besties, here we are with a mascara duo. And honestly? The category switch is the thing I appreciate most. My attention was starting to drift after four consecutive face/complexion sends; landing in eyes with a "step 1 / step 2" tutorial format actually made me stop scrolling.
- The email itself is clean. The big close-up eye hero — "Danielle wears Black" credit, dramatic lashes, strong editorial energy — pulls me in immediately. The pricing is right there upfront ($6 curler, $9 mascara), no hunting required. The "peep the ultimate curl combo" headline has that lowercase-casual brand voice e.l.f. has committed to, and it works. What it does NOT have: any discount, any urgency, any loyalty signal, or any social proof. For the price points, that's less of a deal-breaker than it would be from, say, Charlotte Tilbury — but it does limit how hard this email can actually swing.
- I'd click on the mascara. I wouldn't forward this — there's nothing in here that made me go "oh I need to send this to the group chat" the way the June 11 Halo Glow send did. Mark as read after clicking the mascara link, probably.

## What's working

- **Editorial hero image lands immediately.** The close-up eye with a model credit ("Danielle wears Black") is beauty-mag energy — it's aspirational without being inaccessible. The lashes speak for themselves.
- **Step-by-step tutorial format is smart.** Framing the curler and mascara as a two-step routine (not just "buy two things") gives the email a reason to exist. I don't usually reach for an eyelash curler on my own — this gave me context.
- **Price transparency up front.** $6 for the curler, $9 for the mascara, right there on the product callouts. e.l.f. knows their affordable-prestige positioning is a feature, not a footnote — they lean into it correctly.
- **"Top Picks for You" section.** A small thing, but including a personalization-signaling product grid at the bottom makes this feel like e.l.f. knows I have a purchase history, not that I'm a cold address.
- **Zero render bugs.** Layout is airtight — no broken images, no overlapping text, clean column structure. Compared to Charlotte Tilbury's cluttered everything-at-once email from a few days ago, this reads like a single clear idea.

## What's weak

- **Both steps say "curl it."** Step 1 is "curl it: Pro Eyelash Curler" — fine. Step 2 is "curl it: Incredi-curl Mascara" — but a curling mascara doesn't really *curl*, it sets curl. The copy verb felt lazy and slightly confusing on second read.
- **No discount, no urgency, nothing that makes today feel different from tomorrow.** At $6 and $9 these products are a no-brainer, but e.l.f. still isn't giving me a reason to click *now*. Glossier's annual sale emails created urgency; even the Sephora "STARTS TODAY" send I saw recently had a clear window. This has none.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing all the CTA heavy lifting.** Three SHOP NOW buttons and that's it. No "Get the curl combo," no "Try the $6 curler," no verb that connects to the actual product benefit. ILIA's "Get ready w/ HAIM" had specific, action-branded CTAs — this is the lazy default.
- **No social proof anywhere.** Not a single review quote, star rating, or "X people love this." For a mascara especially, I want to know if it actually holds curl all day or flakes by noon.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add urgency to the "duo" offer.** Even a "grab both for $15 — restocks are unpredictable" nudge, or an exclusive "duo bundle ships free for 48 hours" would transform this from a product newsletter into a reason to act. Right now it reads like a catalog page.
- 2. **Fix the step copy.** Step 1 = "curl it," Step 2 = "set it." The mascara seals the curl — give it the credit it deserves and make the two-step story actually sequential in feel.
- 3. **Swap the CTA copy.** "SHOP NOW" is dead. Try "Get the $9 Mascara" and "Snag the $6 Curler" — specific, priced, irresistible for the e.l.f. value-shopper brain.
- 4. **Drop one star rating into each product callout.** Even "4.7 stars · 12K reviews" under the mascara does the click conversion work that no hero copy can do.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$6 curler + $9 mascara = your new morning ritual`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lock curl. Add volume. No wasted steps.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two products. One lash moment. Shop the combo.`

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **UTM chain is opaque** (see L1). The Braze redirect will resolve to a final `elfcosmetics.com` URL; confirm the redirect destination carries `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before send. A broken UTM at the redirect layer will drop all attribution for this campaign in GA4/analytics.
- **Image CDN domain is `braze-images.com`**, not `elfcosmetics.com`. If a recipient's security gateway blocks third-party image hosts, the header image will fail to load — no fallback background color is set on the containing `<td>`. Set a brand-color `background-color` on the image cell as a defensive fallback.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Audit full HTML** — the provided source is truncated. Confirm physical address, unsubscribe link, and all CTA links are present.
2. **Verify UTM parameters** by following at least one Braze redirect link to the final landing URL and confirming campaign parameters are present.
3. **Remove empty block-2/3/4 tables** — they add dead markup and potential spacing artifacts.
4. **Set `background-color` on the logo `<td>`** to the brand color as a fallback when `braze-images.com` images are blocked.
5. **Pull raw message headers** (From an inbox capture or Braze test send) to verify DKIM alignment and `List-Unsubscribe` header presence before evaluating deliverability risk.
6. **Consider removing `tabindex="-1"` from the logo link** if keyboard accessibility is in scope.
