# The It kits
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay so "The It kits" lands in my inbox the morning after I opened "20% off ends soon!" — and somehow, within 24 hours, we've jumped to 25% off and a completely different offer framing. I'm a Sephora Rouge doing points math in my head constantly, so the moment I see competing percentage claims in the same week I start questioning whether the sale from yesterday is still running or whether I missed a window. That mental friction is DE's fault, not mine.
- The email itself is actually pretty — the pastel kit photography is very much DE's thing, and the kit naming (TRUST THE PROS, CATCH THE LIGHT, TEST THE WATER) gives me an outcome-first hook instead of just a product dump. The 25% OFF badge is in the hero above fold, free shipping is bannered at the top, and the three kit sections each call out specific product pairings by name. I can work with that.
- But this is email five (at minimum) in under two weeks from DE, and it's the first one without any deadline. After "ends soon" yesterday, a dateless offer reads as either still-running-but-repackaged or already-expired-and-I-missed-it. I'd click through to do the kit math against what I already own, but I wouldn't forward it — this is a "let me calculate before I impulse buy" click, not an "oh my god look at this" forward.

## What's working

- **Kit naming with outcome framing** — TRUST THE PROS, CATCH THE LIGHT, TEST THE WATER each sell a *skin result*, not just a product list. That's DE doing what DE does, and it lands for me.
- **Product specificity in each section** — D-Luma + Protini, D-Bronzi + Lala Retro, B-Hydra + Lala Retro with ingredient callouts means I can instantly map these against gaps in my current routine.
- **25% OFF badge in the hero, above fold** — visible before I scroll. ILIA's "In your own skin (tint)" email did this better with cleaner typographic hierarchy, but DE's placement here is solid.
- **Free ground shipping, no minimum, top banner** — removes friction for a $100+ kit purchase immediately.
- **Clean render, no layout breakage** — the colorful product photography is crisp and consistent with DE's visual brand.

## What's weak

- **Subject is completely offer-blind** — "The It kits" is a fashion-mag headline that tells me nothing from inbox. There's a 25% discount, three new kits, free shipping, and the subject communicates none of it.
- **Two competing savings claims with zero explanation** — a 25% OFF badge in the hero AND "35% LESS" copy further down. Is 35% the kit-vs.-individual value savings, and 25% is a promo stacked on top? Or are they the same thing said twice? I have no idea and the fine print is tiny.
- **No deadline, no urgency** — yesterday's send said "ends soon." This one says nothing. Cadence-aware subscribers (hi, that's me) will wonder if the offer already lapsed.
- **Full cadence fatigue** — this is email five-plus in ~10 days. Sally Beauty spaced out "Last Chance: BOGO 50% Off" with clear beats between sends. DE is blasting without building. I'm a loyal customer, not an inexhaustible open rate.
- **Zero loyalty acknowledgment** — not a hint of Sephora Rouge or Ulta Diamond relevance, no points reminder, no "you're a valued member" signal. Glossier at least waves a "just for you" flag. This email treats me like a cold list address.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject** — the kit concept is actually strong but completely buried. Anything with a number beats a brand tagline in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New grab-and-go kits — 25% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 kits. 35% less. Which skin are you shopping?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `D-Luma, D-Bronzi, or B-Hydra — find your match and save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping, no minimum. Pick your kit, done.`
- 2. **Untangle the two savings claims** — one sentence in the hero solves this: "Kits are already 35% less than buying individually — plus, take an extra 25% off through [DATE]." Right now these two numbers are at war with each other.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — after "ends soon" yesterday, a dateless email reads as ambiguous or expired. Even "through July 4" closes the loop and re-earns the urgency I'm primed for.
- 4. **Give Rouge/Diamond members something** — even a single line: "Your Sephora points apply at checkout." Lush × Sanrio pulled off a 9/10 in a crowded cadence because every email felt like it *deserved* my attention. A loyalty hook does that.
- 5. **Compress the bottom section** — "35% LESS (IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING)" and "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." are two separate creative moments trying to live in one scroll zone. Pick one and make it the close.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CONFIRMED DEFECT — Dead CSS rules via HTML comment delimiters inside `<style>` block:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS. These two rules are silently ignored by all CSS parsers. If `.contenttable` or `.mobile` elements exist in the body, they will not reflow correctly on mobile viewports ≤600px. This is a layout regression risk on small screens.

**CONFIRMED DEFECT — `text-size-adjust` set to `none` instead of `100%`:**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```

`none` fully disables user text scaling on iOS/WebKit. The correct value is `100%` (prevents automatic font inflation while preserving user zoom). Using `none` is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation.

**Minor — Duplicate font loading:**
The same three Brown variants (`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, `BrownThin`) are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags and `@font-face` declarations pointing to the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` CDN. This fires redundant HTTP requests on load.

**Minor — Third-party font CDN dependency:**
Six of seven `@font-face` sources resolve through `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN outside Drunk Elephant's control. Font failure here degrades to system fallback silently. Only `BrownRegular` is self-hosted on `drunkelephant.com`.
