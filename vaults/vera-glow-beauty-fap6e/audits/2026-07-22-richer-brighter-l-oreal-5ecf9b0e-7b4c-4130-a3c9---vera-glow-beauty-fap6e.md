# Richer. Brighter. L'Oreal. ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-seven Sally Beauty emails and this one is the first real brand spotlight I've seen in the whole run. "Richer. Brighter. L'Oreal." actually caught me — not because the formula is revolutionary, but because it's *about something* instead of another summer-hair soup send. The hero does its job: warm sunset highlights on a smiling woman who looks like she actually used the products. "No salon required" is the right hook. I have absolutely bleached my own hair at midnight between Sephora hauls and I felt seen for a split second.
- Then the prestige part of my brain kicked in. I buy L'Oreal at Ulta during 21 Days of Beauty — stacked with points, member pricing, and sometimes a GWP. Sally's ask is one free 2-hour delivery with a $45 minimum. No discount, no points, no loyalty math at all. Compare that to Sephora's current 4X points on all hair care, which had me doing actual mental math about whether to stock up on Kérastase instead. This email gives me zero math to do. There's also a noticeable step backward from Sally's own 2026-07-20 send, which at least gave me a "buy two, get one free" structure to evaluate. This one just shows me products and says "shop now."
- Would I click? Only if I was in genuine need-it-today mode and didn't want to wait for Ulta shipping. Would I forward? Only to a friend who already shops Sally. Mark as read, save the shade names for later.

## What's working

- Hero image earns the scroll — warm honey-to-gold highlights on a young woman, photographed well enough to make me want that exact result
- Subject line breaks the recent loop — "Richer. Brighter. L'Oreal." is punchy and brand-specific versus the seven "Summer Must-Haves" riffs in the past two weeks
- "No salon required" is smart product framing — directly addresses the biggest barrier to at-home color
- Shade names in the product grid (Honey Blonde, Magenta, Red) let me visualize without having to click through
- Clean, bug-free render — clear hierarchy from banner to hero to CTA to product grid, nothing fighting for attention

## What's weak

- Zero loyalty integration — I do the points math on every beauty email, and this gives me nothing to calculate; when Sephora is actively running 4X hair care points, a free delivery perk feels like bringing chips to a Michelin dinner
- Product grid is a SKU dump — Magenta, Red, Honey Blonde, Quick Blue lightener, and Developer in one frame is five separate color directions at once, not a curated look
- "Shop now" as the only CTA is doing zero lift — no specificity, no urgency, no reason to choose Sally over Ulta where I can earn points on the same L'Oreal SKUs
- No reviews or social proof anywhere — for a box color I might try for the first time, even a single star rating or review count would move me
- Delivery offer has no deadline — "free 2-hour delivery with $45" with no end date reads like a permanent perk, not a reason to act today

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a loyalty hook or actual discount.** Free delivery doesn't win against top-tier shipping status I hold everywhere. "Earn double Beauty Club points on L'Oreal this weekend" or "20% off L'Oreal — this week only" gives me math to do. That math is how I justify the Sally trip instead of waiting for Ulta 21 Days.
- 2. **Curate around one editorial look, not five unrelated SKUs.** Show me "The Sunset Blonde Kit" — lightener, developer, Honey Blonde — with the model's exact result. One look, one story, one click path. The current grid asks me to self-assemble a shopping list, which I won't do.
- 3. **Give "Shop now" a noun.** "Shop Sunset Shades" or "Find Your L'Oreal Shade" takes two seconds and converts better. Glossier's GWP reveal emails always name the item right on the button; Sally should steal that move.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the offer.** "Free 2-hr delivery ends Sunday" or "Summer color event: this week only" creates the action signal that's completely missing right now.
- 5. **Add one social proof signal.** A star rating on the hero shade or a single user-result shot would legitimize the DIY purchase decision for first-timers.
