# Save up to 50% on select lip must-haves ⭐
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject promised me "up to 50% on select lip must-haves" and what I actually get at the top is 30% off Charlotte Tilbury for "National Lip Day" — which, didn't Sephora already run me through National Lip Day on July 25th with the dae Dreamriser email that opened with a 50% MUFE lip banner? Same manufactured lip "holiday," different brand slapped on top, smaller discount than the subject line advertised. That's not a great start.
- Then the email just... pivots. "You Deserve to Treat Yourself" hero drops me into YSL cushion foundation and Marc Jacobs blush stick, then a full new-launch grid (Sarah Creal, Fara Homidi, Armani Beauty, Westman Atelier, Hourglass, Dolce&Gabbana, Dior, Givenchy), then hair, then skincare, then a Dyson Airwrap ad. None of that is lips. This isn't a lip sale email wearing a trench coat — it's the generic "New & Clean" weekly roundup I've now seen recycled at least twice this week, with a lip-day subject line stapled on top because someone needed a hook.
- I'm not clicking the hero CTA because the CT offer feels like a bait-and-switch versus the subject, but I might scroll the new-launch grid out of habit since I do track new-at-Sephora drops for points math. Not forwarding this, not deleting it either — straight to "seen it, moving on."

## What's working

- The "you have 0 points" header at least gestures at account context, even if the number's a letdown.
- New-launch grid is genuinely well-curated — CLEAN and ONLINE ONLY / ONLY AT SEPHORA tags on Sarah Creal, Fara Homidi, and Westman Atelier give me real reasons to care, and prices are listed inline so I'm not guessing.
- Beauty Insider tier bar (INSIDER / VIB / ROUGE) at the footer is a clean visual reminder of where I sit relative to Rouge.
- "Today only!" on the CT banner is at least a real, specific deadline, not vague "limited time" filler.

## What's weak

- Subject says 50% off lip must-haves, hero delivers 30% off one brand — that gap is the first thing I clock and it costs trust immediately.
- The email has zero focus: lips → face → hair → skincare → hair tools, all in one send. Nobody opens a "lip must-haves" email hoping for a Dyson Airwrap pitch.
- "National Lip Day" as the hook is stale — Sephora already used this exact holiday as the excuse for a 50%-off MUFE banner in a July 25th send under a totally different subject. Recycling the calendar excuse within 24 hours is lazy.
- No shade range, ingredient callouts, or "for your skin tone" personalization anywhere — for a lip/color-heavy send that's the one thing that would've made it feel tailored to me instead of a catalog page.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject and hero match — if the real offer is 30% off Charlotte Tilbury, say that in the subject. Don't promise 50% and deliver 30%; that's the fastest way to train me to stop trusting your subject lines.
- 2. Pick one lane. Either this is a lip-focused sale email (CT hero + lip products only) or it's the weekly new-launch roundup — don't merge both into one scroll that ends at a hair dryer.
- 3. Cut "National Lip Day" as a repeat device — Ulta's "this event is over" TATCHA email proved that stale campaign framing reads as sloppy; don't let Sephora fall into the same trap two sends in a row.
- 4. Add real personalization — my Rouge tier, my points balance that isn't zero, or a "picked for your skin tone" line on the CT products would do more work than another generic "SHOP NOW."
- 5. Give the primary CTA a specific verb+noun ("Shop Lip Sets") instead of the generic "SHOP NOW" repeated on every single module.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `National Lip Day deals inside — plus this week's new-at-Sephora drops`
