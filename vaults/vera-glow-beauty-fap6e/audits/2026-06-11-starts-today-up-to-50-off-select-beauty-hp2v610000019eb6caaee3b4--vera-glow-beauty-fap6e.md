# STARTS TODAY: up to 50% off select beauty 🎀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, real talk: my Sephora inbox is still recovering from May 26 when I counted three back-to-back "ENDS TODAY" sends in a single afternoon — same subject line, different send times, complete cadence chaos. So walking into "STARTS TODAY" two weeks later, I'm carrying some scar tissue. That said, this actually earns a reset. A new-sale announcement is fundamentally different from a desperation closer, and "Major Summer Deals" with a 500-point bonus and a three-tier discount structure is the kind of email where I immediately start running the Rouge math.
- What's in here is a lot: the sale launch, a Lancôme Balance Mode feature, a "Hot Deal" row, a new 100-point rewards announcement, AND a full Beauty Insider tier explainer at the bottom. I'm Rouge. I do not need Sephora to tell me what BI Cash is. The loyalty tutorial at the bottom treats me like I just walked in off the street, and after years of stacking event purchases, that stings a little. The product grid in the middle — "Save on Must-Haves Like These" — feels like it was assembled by an algorithm at 2am. No editorial POV, no ingredient story, just a scatter of items with no connective tissue.
- I'd click the hero "Shop Now" button to lock in the Rouge tier before the sale gets crowded. I would not forward this. e.l.f.'s "Take Spray Into Your Day" from last week had a single product and a reason to want it right now — this email gives me a sale and a grocery list. Useful, but not delightful.

## What's working

- **500-point bonus in a big pink circle in the hero** — points currency gets me every single time; this is placed exactly where I'd want it, before the scroll.
- **Three-tier discount structure visible up front** — I can immediately identify my Rouge benefit without hunting through body copy. The points math is why I stay Rouge.
- **"STARTS TODAY" framing** — announcing a beginning is smarter than another end-of-sale panic push. After the May 26 triple-send, this feels like a genuine calendar event, not a fourth alarm.
- **Lancôme Balance Mode buried mid-email** — weaving a real new launch into a sale send gives me a reason to browse beyond my existing wishlist. That's smart merchandising even if it's underplayed.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks** — mobile hierarchy holds, images loaded, nothing overlapping. Charlotte Tilbury was pulling a fake-glitch creative trick last week to hide their product; Sephora just shows me what I'm getting.

## What's weak

- **Beauty Insider tier explainer at the bottom** — Lush USA's June sends skip the loyalty tutorial entirely because their list already knows the program. I've been Rouge for years; this block is scroll weight that should be segmented out.
- **No end date anywhere visible** — "STARTS TODAY" creates urgency around the start, but the body never resolves when it ends. Drunk Elephant's last email gave me a deadline in the first scroll; Sephora leaves me guessing, which makes me less likely to act now vs. "later" (a.k.a. never).
- **Product grid has no editorial voice** — six items under "Save on Must-Haves Like These" with no "why" copy. Ulta's Dolce&Gabbana send this week at least led with a named fragrance and created a moment around it. This grid reads like the homepage browse tab, not a curated recommendation.
- **Cadence hangover is real** — this is another sale email off a cycle that already hammered my inbox into the ground by May 26. Even with fresh copy, my trust in Sephora's urgency signals is lower than it should be for one of my favorite brands.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Feature one hero product in the top half** — Lancôme Balance Mode is already in the email; move it to the lead position. One product with a sentence about why it matters this summer beats six random SKUs every time.
- 2. **Add the sale end date to the hero** — "STARTS TODAY — ends June 17" gives me a real reason to act now and a mental bookmark to come back. The urgency needs a resolution.
- 3. **Cut the BI tier explainer for known customers** — segment this block for new subscribers only. Rouge and VIB members see a version without it.
- 4. **Give the product grid a theme** — "Top summer skin picks for humid weather" or "Launch-day grabs before they sell out" beats algorithm stew.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly (confirmed)**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Suppresses OS-level accessibility font scaling across all body elements. Should be scoped only to the outer wrapper table, not `body` and all inline elements.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` (confirmed)**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers use this to select the correct voice/language engine. Required for WCAG 3.1.1.

**`robots` meta value malformed (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Value is `"no index"` (space). Correct value is `"noindex"` (no space). While most email clients strip this meta, it is technically invalid and would fail if the HTML were ever rendered in a browser context.
