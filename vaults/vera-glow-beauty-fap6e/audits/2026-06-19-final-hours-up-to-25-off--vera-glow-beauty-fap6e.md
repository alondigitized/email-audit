# Final Hours: Up to 25% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Day thirteen. And I say that because I have been tracking this Glossier sale the way I track my Sephora Rouge status — obsessively and with spreadsheet energy. The "ends tomorrow" email hit on June 19. The "ends tonight!" email also hit on June 19. And now — same day, same offer — "Final Hours." Three urgency escalations in a single inbox day from the same brand, same 25% off, same everything. At some point "final hours" stops meaning anything because I've been in final hours since this morning.
- The email itself? Clean. The hero copy "Okay, this is really it" is actually charming — I like the casual, self-aware tone. The product grid is solid, prices are visible, and the hierarchy flows top to bottom without breaking. The subscription upsell at the bottom is where Glossier loses the thread, though: they're asking me to lock in 20% off via subscription during a 25% off sitewide sale. That math doesn't help me.
- I would not click. I already opened the two earlier sends this morning. I'd mark this as read and let the tab I already have open do the work.

## What's working

- **"Okay, this is really it"** — genuinely funny and self-aware hero copy; it knows the sale has been going on and leans into it instead of pretending this is the first touch
- **Prices visible in the product grid** ($27, $25 callouts) — I don't have to click through to know if I'm looking at a budget or a splurge
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — logo → offer → products → subscription; no confused layout, no render issues, eye lands on the hero first
- **25% is stated clearly** — no buried asterisks, no "up to" buried in fine print, the offer reads as real
- **Demographic fit** — products, models, and palette are squarely aimed at someone my age and aesthetic; no frumpy or off-brand signaling

## What's weak

- **Three sends, same offer, same day** — this is the Charlotte Tilbury "Unreal" problem all over again (six sends in eight days on the same launch); "Final Hours" stops meaning urgency when I've already survived two final hours this morning
- **Subscription upsell at 20% during a 25%-off sale is backwards math** — if you're going to push a subscription, do it *after* the sale ends, not when subscribing gets me worse pricing than just shopping today
- **"Shop Now" everywhere** — every CTA is the same generic button; e.l.f. at least ties their CTA copy to the specific product or occasion; here it's the same three words seven times
- **No loyalty mention** — I'm a known Glossier customer and there's nothing in here about my tier, history, or whether I've already bought during this sale; Sephora would at least acknowledge my Rouge status in the copy

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one of the June 19 sends** — seriously, the "ends tonight!" and this "Final Hours" are the same email in a different jacket; consolidating would have preserved urgency credibility
- 2. **Flip the subscription module** — move it to a post-purchase triggered email, not mid-sale; the 20% vs 25% math actively undercuts both the sale and the subscription value prop
- 3. **Vary the CTAs** — "Shop Lash Slick," "Grab the Balm Dotcom," anything more specific than "Shop Now" repeated ad nauseam; Lush does this well, even at 18 sends a month they give you something to click *toward*
- 4. **Add a "you've earned this" loyalty hook** — one line acknowledging I'm a repeat customer lands harder than nothing; ILIA did this better with their "BFF + SPF" send by making it feel like a friends-and-family moment
- **Subject Alt A:** `This is the last one, promise — 25% off ends tonight`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `We said tonight and we meant it. Here's what to grab.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up now — 25% off everything, subscription math explained below`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Gmail head CSS stripping (confirmed issue)**
All styles are declared in `<head>` `<style>` blocks. Gmail web strips `<head>` CSS entirely. The entire hover/rollover interaction system (`.rollover:hover .rollover-first`, `.bj:hover`, etc.) will silently fail in Gmail — a significant rendering gap given Gmail's ~27% market share. Only inline styles survive Gmail.

**Hover states are non-functional in most clients**
The CSS defines extensive `:hover` button states across ~25 class selectors (`.bj-9168:hover`, `.bj-6784:hover`, etc.). These work only in Apple Mail and a subset of desktop clients. Outlook, Gmail (web + Android), and most mobile clients ignore `:hover` entirely.

**Vendor comment leaks internal metadata**
```html

```
Minor: exposes vendor relationship in email source. Not a functional issue but visible to any recipient who inspects source.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot verify; HTML is truncated before `<img>` tags appear. Must check that all promotional images carry descriptive `alt` attributes for accessibility and image-blocked rendering.
