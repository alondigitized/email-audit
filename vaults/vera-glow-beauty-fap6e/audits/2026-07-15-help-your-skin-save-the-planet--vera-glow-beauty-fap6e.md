# Help your skin. Save the planet.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so DE lands in my inbox with "Help your skin. Save the planet." — and after three genuinely clever punchy subjects in a row (*"Damn, your skin!" / "Time to come clean" / "20-minute hack"*), this one immediately reads like a different copywriter wrote it. It's a dual concept — skincare benefit *plus* eco-angle — and it dilutes both. I still opened because DE has trained me well, but the subject left me cold compared to their recent streak.
- Once I'm inside, the "UM, HELLOOOO. THE OFFER ENDS IN..." countdown with the playful all-caps is pure DE voice and I love it — that brand personality is crack for me. The REPLENISH/REPAIR/REFILL hero is clean, the three moisturizer refills are products I actually use (Lala Retro, Bora Barrier, Protini — this is basically my whole top shelf), and the value callouts ($57, $65, $63) make me feel smart for shopping refills.
- The big miss: the 10% off is *buried at the bottom* beneath three product grids. Vera who lives for points math had to scroll to find the actual discount. And the sustainability pitch up top — "Save money (and Mother Earth)" — tries to carry two messages at once, and neither one lands fully. I'd click for the refills anyway because I know these SKUs, but I shouldn't have to work this hard to find the offer. I'd mark this as read without forwarding — the sustainability angle isn't something my group chat needs to see.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer with "UM, HELLOOOO."** — that irreverent, slightly frantic voice is exactly DE, and urgency at the very top of the email earns immediate attention.
- **Product-specific value callouts ($57, $65, $63 VALUE)** — showing MSRP value inline next to refill prices is smart; it does the math for me so I feel the savings without hunting.
- **Three core moisturizers, clearly named** — Lala Retro, Bora Barrier Repair, Protini are hero SKUs that DE loyalists know and repurchase; this is well-targeted assortment curation.
- **"REPLENISH, REPAIR, REFILL" hero copy** — the three-word alliterative triple is satisfying, clean, and communicates the refill concept at a glance.
- **Clean render, zero visual bugs** — images load, layout holds, text is readable on mobile without zooming. After seeing Ulta send me an email where the hero literally said "this event is over," a clean render is legitimately a win.

## What's weak

- **10% off buried below three product blocks** — I had to scroll past the entire hero and product grid to find the discount. That's the offer that should anchor the top, not hide at the bottom as an afterthought.
- **"Help your skin. Save the planet." is too split** — after "Damn, your skin!" earned an 8/10 three days ago with a single tight concept, this dual-focus subject feels like committee copy. ILIA just pulled off a focused mini mascara pitch with "NEW—brown mascara, made mini" in one tight frame; DE's eco-skincare mashup doesn't hit as cleanly.
- **No loyalty or points signal anywhere** — I'm a known DE customer who buys through Sephora Rouge. Any mention of BI points on refills or "stack with your Beauty Insider event" would've immediately upgraded my cart math. Nothing. Silence.
- **"Subscribe to save" CTA at the bottom is underexplained** — is this a subscribe-and-save model like Amazon? A mailing list? The button is there but what I'm subscribing to is unclear, and that ambiguity makes me not tap it.
- **Sustainability framing feels generic** — "Save money (and Mother Earth)" is a nice line, but by 2026 every brand is running this angle. Lush ran it with their Fresh spotlight, Glossier's sustainability beats show up constantly. DE needed to make this *specifically* theirs — what makes DE's refill program different from anyone else's?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 10% off into the hero block, not the bottom.** "REPLENISH, REPAIR, REFILL — and save 10%" turns the sustainability angle into a concrete action, not just a vibe.
- 2. **Collapse to one hero concept in the subject line** — either the saving angle or the planet angle, not both. The planet angle is the differentiator; lean in.
- 3. **Add a Sephora BI points callout** — even one line: "Earn Beauty Insider points on every refill" would make me feel like a known customer, not a cold prospect.
- 4. **Clarify "Subscribe to save"** — one sentence: "Subscribe for recurring delivery at 15% off." If it's genuinely a better deal, show the math.
- 5. **Lean into the refill-as-smart-shopper angle** — frame these as "the edit for people who know better," which is very DE brand and very my identity.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lala Retro, Bora Barrier, Protini — all in eco-refill form.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] HTML comments inside `<style>` block** — the `@media (max-width: 600px)` rule contains raw HTML comment syntax:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comments are invalid CSS. Gmail's CSS parser and several Outlook versions will drop the surrounding rule block entirely on encountering `<!--`, breaking the mobile layout.

**[HIGH] `text-size-adjust: none` instead of `100%`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
`none` fully disables user font scaling. The correct value is `100%`. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile email clients. Second WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**[MEDIUM] Apple Mail link color suppression**
```css
.appleLinks a {color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important;}
```
Makes auto-detected Apple Mail links (phone numbers, addresses, dates) visually identical to body text — black, no underline. Users cannot distinguish them as interactive.

**[LOW] Triple-redundant font loading** — BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each loaded three ways:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...)` in CSS
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...") }` in CSS

Gmail strips `<link>` tags and `@import` statements; only `@font-face` survives. The first two mechanisms generate HTTP requests with zero rendering benefit and add load time. Additionally, BrownRegular is served from the first-party domain `www.drunkelephant.com`, while the other three weights are served from the third-party CDN `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — an inconsistency that creates an external dependency risk if that CDN is rate-limited or blocked by corporate proxies.
