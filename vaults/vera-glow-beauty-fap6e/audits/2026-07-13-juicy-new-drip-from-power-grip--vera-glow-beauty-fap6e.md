# Juicy NEW drip from Power Grip 🌹
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I am a Power Grip loyalist — the OG primer is in my morning routine, has been for two years — so "new drip from power grip" in the subject actually made me sit up a little. But then I open and... it's a product parade. Five different SKUs stacked in a single scroll, each with its own "Shop Now" button, and the "Duo on us" offer I actually care about is buried at the bottom after I've already mentally checked out.
- This feels like e.l.f. panicking. I've now gotten five emails in eleven days — blurreal, the $20 lip combos, the e.l.f. SKIN duo, the e.l.f. Hair sub-brand detour — and this one should've been the payoff moment for Power Grip fans. Instead it reads like a product catalog drop. ILIA sent a single esthetician packing-list email last week with zero offer, and it still felt more intentional than this because it committed to one story. This email is trying to sell me everything at once.
- I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably click the OG primer out of habit muscle memory, but e.l.f. is burning goodwill fast with this cadence and I'm starting to scan-and-archive instead of actually engaging.

## What's working

- **"New drip from power grip" is a cute headline** — "drip" as beauty slang for a product line extension lands for me, and the rosy-teal visual palette is very on-brand and clean
- **Multiple Setting Spray variants are news to me** — Rock and Dewy as named sub-SKUs signal actual product innovation, not just a shade extension; that's worth a line
- **No render bugs at all** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap; e.l.f. is always technically solid
- **Diverse model representation in the hero** — skin tones and textures are shown across the primer demos, which is exactly what I want from a grip/finish product

## What's weak

- **Five products, zero hierarchy** — there's no "this is the launch hero, these are supporting cast" signal; every product card gets equal real estate and a generic "Shop Now" button, so my eye doesn't know where to land
- **"Duo on us" offer is buried** — the only concrete value prop is at the bottom of a long product stack; Sephora Insider consistently leads with the offer and supports with product, and e.l.f. has it backwards here
- **No price anchoring anywhere above the fold** — I can't see a dollar figure or percentage until I scroll past three product sections; for a mass-prestige brand whose whole pitch is "more for less," hiding the math is a miss
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is e.l.f. send five in eleven days; after the e.l.f. Hair sub-brand email on July 10, my trust in "NEW!" as a signal is wearing thin

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero SKU and make it the whole email** — the Rock Setting Spray is genuinely new; build the email around it with a close-up visual, the formula story, and the $price anchor. Push the rest of the line to a "complete the grip routine" secondary section.
- 2. **Lead with the "Duo on us" offer** — or at minimum surface the price in the hero copy. "New Grip Setting Sprays, starting at $X" does more work than "new drip."
- 3. **Replace generic "Shop Now" with product-specific CTAs** — "Get the Rock Spray," "Try the Dewy Formula" — these convert better and feel less like a catalog.
- 4. **Slow the send cadence** — five emails in eleven days dilutes every individual launch; if Power Grip had been the only email this week, I'd have opened with way more intent.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New from Power Grip: the setting spray drop you asked for`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rock or Dewy finish? Two new grips, one big launch.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The primer you love just got a whole new drip routine.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Mixed ESP asset hosting**
- Click tracking: Sailthru (`links.elfcosmetics.com`, `~~/AAQRxRA~/` subscriber hash pattern)
- Image CDN: `braze-images.com/appboy/…` (Braze)
- Risk: if either system's link-wrapping is misconfigured on send, click attribution breaks on one layer without affecting the other. Verify the final resolved URL from the Sailthru redirect actually lands on a page with matching UTM params.

**Opaque redirect — UTM not visible in source**
- The only visible href is `https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/EqM1RtpioxjhgdrUC8l98w~~/AAQRxRA~/…`
- UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are not visible in the raw encoded URL. They may be appended by Sailthru's redirect, but this cannot be confirmed from source alone — requires a live redirect trace (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably older Android Gmail) surface `<title>` in the tab/window label. An empty value is benign but adds no fallback context.

**`tabindex="-1"` on hero link**
```html
<a href="…" target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Removing the hero image link from keyboard tab order fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1 (Keyboard). Screen-reader users and keyboard-only users cannot activate the primary CTA. `outline:none` compounds this by removing focus visibility.

**Google Fonts load path**
Correctly gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`; Outlook falls back to Open Sans → Arial → Sans-serif stack as intended. No issue.

**Fixed 640 px column with no fluid override at root**
`row-content` is hard-capped at `width:640px`. The `@media (max-width:660px)` block sets `width:100%!important` on `.row-content`, which is correct. No issue in practice, but the 660 px breakpoint (not 640 px) means a 641–660 px viewport gets the desktop layout at partial clip — minor.

**`row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1` font size forced to 9 px on mobile**
```css
.row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size:9px!important }
```
9 px is below the WCAG 2.1 recommended minimum (~12 px / 16 px body) and will likely fail legibility checks. Verify whether this is intentional (e.g., a legal-copy row) or a build artifact from the email editor.
