# We’re *sheer* for NEW shades❣️😚
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. I need everyone to understand that this is e.l.f. email number fifteen since May 15th, and my initial reflex before I even opened it was exhaustion. But — and this is the part I hate to admit — the email itself is actually doing a lot of things right once you're inside it. The shade grid for Sheer For It Blush Tints is the kind of product-first layout that makes me stop scrolling: nine shades with actual names, a $5 price point that's genuinely good, and a hero model who looks like she actually uses the product. The pun is cute. I'm not immune.
- What kills the momentum is the ghost of email twelve. Three days ago e.l.f. sent me "6 HOURS ONLY! 💗 2 for $8 Sheer For It!" — which I clocked as $4 per unit. Now they're back with the same product pitched as new shades at $5 each. I did that math instantly, and it soured me a little. The Target receipt section in the middle of the email also just… sits there, disrupting the flow like an ad that got dropped into the wrong placement.
- If this had been email four instead of fifteen, I'd have clicked immediately and probably forwarded to my group chat. At fifteen, I mark as read and think about unsubscribing.

## What's working

- **The shade grid is genuinely excellent** — nine named shades (So Petal, Mauve Actually, Cherry Cola, etc.) laid out in a clean 3x3 gives me real purchase context, not just vibes.
- **$5 is a headline price** — visible in the hero body copy without hunting, and for a blush tint that's actually competitive.
- **Visual hierarchy holds up** — hero → offer text → SHOP NOW → shade swatches → "Top Picks For You" is a natural read path; my eye never got confused.
- **Model demographic lands** — berry lips, millennial-pink energy, the aesthetic is clearly targeting me, not my mom.
- **"Top Picks For You" is doing something** — whether the personalization is real or faked I can't confirm, but the section signals that this isn't a pure batch-and-blast.

## What's weak

- **Sheer For It déjà vu is real** — email twelve was "2 for $8" on this exact product, and now it's $5 for new shades. The math doesn't favor today's send.
- **The Target receipt upload block is a flow-killer** — a mid-email interruption pushing me to photograph receipts breaks the browse-to-buy momentum cold; this belongs in a dedicated Target-audience segment, not a general send.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible CTA** — at fifteen emails I need "Shop Sheer For It" or "Find Your Shade" — something that earns the tap.
- **No urgency mechanism** — Glossier's "30% off ends soon" run had a deadline doing the work. This email has no pull-forward signal; I can revisit it literally whenever, so I probably won't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Target receipt block from this send** — save it for a Target-specific segment or a standalone email; it's eating conversion momentum right when the shade grid is doing its job.
- 2. **Make the CTA specific** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Find My Shade" or "Shop New Shades" to match the hero.
- 3. **Add a lightweight urgency hook** — even "new shades sell out fast" or a low-stock nudge on a couple of shades would manufacture pull-forward without being annoying.
- 4. **Acknowledge the Sheer For It reprise** — lean into "the shade range just got bigger" framing more explicitly so it doesn't feel like the same email recycled. Charlotte Tilbury managed to make a full Met Gala pivot feel earned; e.l.f. can at least frame "nine shades now" as a genuine expansion event.
- 5. **Price anchor the math against email 12** — "more shades, still just $5" closes the loop for people who saw the 2-for-$8 offer and are now suspicious.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sheer For It just got 9 shades. Still $5.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Sheer For It shades dropped — only $5 each`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cherry Cola, Mauve Actually, Maple Latte — find your blush tint.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `9 standout shades for cheeks & lips. Long-lasting, only $5.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on the BEE/Beefree editor framework, delivered via Braze (image CDN) and tracked through Sailthru (`links.elfcosmetics.com`). The truncated source reveals several structural and accessibility defects; footer/compliance section is not visible in the provided excerpt.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Sailthru redirect chain — UTM visibility unknown**
The only confirmed link in the excerpt is the logo:
```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/7B8f1vRRmyoA-W2L7ehRvg~~/...
```
This is a Sailthru encoded redirect. UTM parameters (if any) are encoded inside the payload and cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect. If UTMs are absent or mis-keyed, all clicks will land in GA4 as `(direct)`.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo anchor**
```html
<a href="..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
The logo CTA is explicitly excluded from keyboard tab order. If the same pattern is applied to promotional CTAs lower in the email (not visible in this excerpt), those buttons will also be unreachable by keyboard.

**`outline:none` on all links**
Same selector above removes the focus ring. Paired with `tabindex="-1"` this is a double accessibility suppression.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients (iOS Mail, Gmail accessibility mode) announce the title. Empty title degrades the experience for AT users.

**`<style>` block placed inside a `<div>` (invalid)**
Block-3 injects a `<style type="text/css">` element directly inside a `<td><div>` wrapper — not in `<head>`. Many email clients strip or ignore `<style>` outside `<head>`, meaning the `.responsive-text` responsive font-size rules will silently fail on those clients.

**Nested `nl-container` — template copy error**
The outer `nl-container` table contains an inner `nl-container` with its own full `row > row-content > column` stack for the logo block. This is a Beefree copy-paste artifact. It adds ~400 bytes of redundant markup and can confuse MSO rendering.

**Empty `html_block block-2`**
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
A completely empty block between the logo and the offer bar. Harmless but indicative of a template cleanup gap.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` tag**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]>` so it is intentionally skipped in Outlook. However, it will also fail in privacy-hardened clients (Apple Mail with "Block Remote Content" enabled) and some Android clients. The inline font-stack fallbacks (`Arial, Sans-serif`) handle this correctly — confirm they are used consistently throughout, not just in the nav bar.

**Image hosted on `braze-images.com`**
```
https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91.../original.png
```
This is the Braze legacy CDN domain (`appboy`). Braze has been migrating assets to `cdn.braze.com`. If this asset was uploaded pre-migration it should still resolve, but worth verifying it hasn't been orphaned.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

**UTMs unconfirmable without redirect resolution**
All clicks route through `links.elfcosmetics.com` Sailthru redirects. The actual destination URL and UTM parameters (campaign, source, medium, content) are encoded in the redirect hash and cannot be inspected from static source. Must be verified by following each redirect:

- Confirm `utm_campaign` matches the campaign slug in Sailthru

Without per-link `utm_content` differentiation, click attribution is aggregated and click-to-conversion mapping is impossible.
