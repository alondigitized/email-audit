# Skincare with options
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- "Skincare with options" drops two days after "Golden hour every hour" — and that subject line whiplash is real. DE has trained me with four consecutive sends that scored 8 or higher, each with a punchy, specific hook that made me feel known. "Skincare with options" sounds like a Gmail auto-category label, not a brand that said "Damn, your skin!" three weeks ago. I'd still open it because DE has earned that reflex — but they're coasting on brand equity here, not copywriting.
- Inside, the email recovers. "WHO HAS TIME TO LAYER?" is a genuinely good question for a skincare girl who's got a 12-step shelf and 6 actual morning minutes. The copy — save time, save money, save brainpower — lands in DE's irreverent, practical voice, and I appreciate that they name-checked the D-BRONZI and Lala Retro GWP up top. But then the banner that holds that offer is tiny, all-caps, and separated from the hero completely. Compare that to Sephora Insider, who never buries a GWP — it's always the hero image. DE did the opposite.
- I'd click because Amino Baby is already in my mental Sephora cart and a D-BRONZI sample at $75 is exactly how I justify the order. But I wouldn't forward this. Lush asked me a direct scalp question and still scored higher this week. DE can do better — and they know it.

## What's working

- **"WHO HAS TIME TO LAYER?" is a real insight, not a feature dump.** Efficient application for lazy-routine girls is a distinct positioning angle and DE nails the framing — it's benefit-led before it's product-led.
- **"Sploosion" is doing quiet brand work.** DE's signature word in the subheadline ("Mix, apply, and sploosion!") is a loyalty signal — if you don't know what that means, you're not the target, and I love being the target.
- **The GWP is a real pull.** D-BRONZI and Lala Retro samples at $75+ is actual value — not a vague "deluxe gift." Those are two products I would use, not gimmick samples.
- **Email renders clean.** No broken images, no lorem ipsum, no layout weirdness — after watching Ulta send me a post-mortem email for an event that already ended, "it loads correctly" is genuinely notable.

## What's weak

- **"Skincare with options" is the weakest subject in DE's recent run.** Zero personality, zero hook, zero reason to open except habit. "Hot takes" earned a click; this earns a sigh.
- **The GWP offer is doing a lot of heavy lifting from a tiny banner.** The real click incentive — free samples with $75 — is crammed into the top in small all-caps text while the hero real estate goes to a concept. That's backwards.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is lazy.** The email just told me a whole story about mixing and saving brainpower, and the button says "SHOP NOW." ILIA manages to match CTA copy to the product concept every send. DE abandoned the bit at the last step.
- **No loyalty context.** I'm always doing Sephora points math. If this $75 order hits during bonus points or if Amino Baby is part of an event, that context would seal the deal. Silence on this leaves money on the table.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to match DE's established voice.** This is the single highest-leverage fix — four strong sends trained me to expect wit, and this breaks the pattern cold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `No layering, no waiting, no problem`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mix, apply, done.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free D-BRONZI + Lala Retro samples with $75+ — no layering required`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two free deluxe samples when you spend $75. The math is easy.`
- 2. **Pull the GWP into the hero image.** Show the two sample products front and center with the headline. The "who has time to layer?" quip can live as the supporting copy — right now the offer and the concept are in two separate emails that happen to be stacked.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA button to close the loop.** "Get My Samples" or "Mix + Save" picks up the creative thread the email already started. "SHOP NOW" doesn't.
- 4. **Add one sentence of loyalty math.** "Earn [X] points on your $75 order at Sephora" is all it takes to make a Rouge member feel seen — and to tip the conversion.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead CSS rules (silent breakage):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block is not valid CSS. Both `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive rules are completely ignored by all email clients. Any elements using these classes will not resize on mobile.

**Triple-redundant font loading:**
Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded three ways simultaneously — `<link>` tag, `@import`, and `@font-face`. This triples HTTP requests for fonts that most email clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail with images off) will block regardless. Effective fallback is system sans-serif; the triple load adds overhead with no upside.

**Dual `@font-face` sources for BrownBold:** The font is declared first via first-party CDN (`drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`) then again via third-party (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`). The second declaration shadows the first.

**`maximum-scale=1` viewport:** Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile — an accessibility regression for low-vision users. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) flags this.

**`.appleLinks a { color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important; }`** — Forces all Apple Mail link color to pure black with no underline. If any link sits on a dark or image background, it becomes invisible. Verify contrast ratio across all image/background contexts.

**`*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` on all elements** — Suppresses browser text-size accessibility settings globally, not just on the `body`. Overly broad; should be scoped to `body` only.

**Duplicate `line-height: 100%` declaration:**
```css
.ExternalClass p, ... { line-height: 100% }
/* ... */
* { line-height: 100% }
```
The `*` rule makes the `.ExternalClass` rule redundant. Not a breakage, but a maintenance noise flag.
