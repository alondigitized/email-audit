# Did You Miss This Magic? 💌
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Email thirteen from Charlotte Tilbury. I'm keeping the count because the brand clearly isn't. This one arrives as "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" — a digest format, which is marketing speak for "please look at all the things you may have ignored." After twelve emails in roughly two and a half weeks — the 20% off barrage, the Met Gala Exagger-Eyes drop, the countdown timer that hit zero, and the FREE MINIS offer that was literally in my inbox yesterday — asking me if I "missed this magic" is a little audacious.
- The email surfaces five separate product stories: Exagger-Eyes (again), a step routine guide (useful, actually), an "IT'S BACK DARLING!" return product (the one thing here that's genuinely new information), a "MEET: GLOW + GO!" bronzer launch, Summer Beauty Icons, and a pout perfection section. That's five competing narratives with five "SHOP NOW" buttons and zero urgency or discount tying them together. Glossier just ran a follow-up send on the same Pink Capsule collection and I called them out for it — CT is doing the same thing here but louder and at higher frequency.
- Would I click? Probably on "IT'S BACK DARLING!" because revival products are my kryptonite and that section is actually new. Would I forward this to a friend? No. This is a brand catching its breath and hoping I didn't notice.

## What's working

- **The 1-2-3 step guide is editorial gold.** Prime + Moisturize → Sculpt + Highlight → Volumize + Maximize with a clear "3 FREE MINIS" payoff is the kind of beauty education content I actually absorb. ILIA does this well with their launch briefs — it's good to see CT go beyond pure "buy now" mode.
- **"IT'S BACK DARLING!" is a genuine hook.** A returning product creates real scarcity logic that a new launch can't fake. This section earns its place in the digest.
- **FREE MINIS offer sits high and reads clearly.** Even as a rerun of yesterday's email #12, it's not buried. Sephora has a habit of stacking their best offer below two paragraphs of copy — CT keeps it front-loaded.
- **Clean render, no broken sections.** Five product stories in a stacked layout with no overlapping text or broken images is genuinely a technical win at this length.
- **CT's aesthetic is consistent and distinctly theirs.** The warm rose-gold palette, the glamour photography, the signature feel — every section reads as Charlotte Tilbury. I know who sent this.

## What's weak

- **"Did You Miss This Magic?" is a subtle diss after thirteen emails.** I didn't miss anything. I've been here the whole time. The framing works for a list of cold subscribers, not for someone who's been opening your emails since May 15th.
- **Five product stories is four too many.** e.l.f.'s "babe wake up, new e.l.f. just dropped" had exactly one story and it worked. Five sections with five SHOP NOW buttons means I have no clear action — my thumb hovers and gives up.
- **No discount after a multi-week 20% off campaign is whiplash.** Drunk Elephant ran thirteen F&F emails but always kept the offer front and center. CT went from "20% OFF ENDS TONIGHT" to "here's a highlights reel" with no bridge. My points math brain noticed immediately.
- **Generic "SHOP NOW" CTAs on every single section.** "Shop the Return," "Get My Free Minis," "Meet Glow + Go" — any of these would direct me somewhere specific. SHOP NOW sends me nowhere in my head.
- **No loyalty signal anywhere.** I'm Charlotte's Angels tier (implied, given thirteen emails). Not one line of "as one of our most loyal customers" or "your early access." CT treated me like a stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with "IT'S BACK DARLING!" as the singular hero — save the digest for a weekly roundup format.** The revival product is the only genuinely new information here. Make that the whole email.
- 2. **Write product-specific CTAs for every section.** "Get the Free Minis Kit," "Shop the Comeback," "Discover Glow + Go" — each should tell me where I land before I click.
- 3. **Add one loyalty line near the top.** Even "You've been with us through the whole Exagger-Eyes launch — here's what you haven't seen yet" signals that CT knows who I am.
- 4. **Bridge the transition off the 20% off campaign.** A single line — "The sale is over, but the magic isn't" — would acknowledge the shift and retain goodwill instead of pretending the promo never existed.
