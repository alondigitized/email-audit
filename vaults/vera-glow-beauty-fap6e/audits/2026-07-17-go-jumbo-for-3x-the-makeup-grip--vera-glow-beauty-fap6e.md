# Go Jumbo for 3X the makeup grip 💚 
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so e.l.f. has been in my inbox every single day this week, and I walked in here braced for another snooze. Then I see the drag queen serving full glam in the hero and I'm like — okay, at least you're trying. The "SIZE THE E.L.F. UP ON YOUR FAVE PRIMER" wordplay lands, the $34/$53 value story is genuinely the strongest dollar-per-product math e.l.f. has pitched me in this whole run, and the double "GO JUMBO" button placement tells me someone actually thought about scroll behavior. The green-on-green brand consistency is immaculate.
- But I cannot ignore that this is the second Power Grip-adjacent email in four days. July 13 was "Juicy NEW drip from Power Grip" — that one already made me think I was caught up. Now we're back with the Jumbo format and I'm doing the mental math: I probably already own the original, I'm buying it through Ulta for the Diamond points, and this email gives me zero reason to break that habit. No deadline. No "Beauty Squad members get X." No urgency at all.
- I'd mark it read, not delete — the offer is genuinely solid and the creative is fun. But I'm not clicking through to buy direct when I can drop it in my Ulta cart and count toward my next reward. I wouldn't forward this; the Jumbo format feels niche enough that my group chat would shrug.

## What's working

- **Value math is front and center.** "$34 ($53 value)" in the hero is the right move — e.l.f.'s entire brand equity is accessible luxury, and the Jumbo format makes the savings feel concrete, not manufactured.
- **The drag queen casting.** Bold, inclusive, unapologetically e.l.f. — it's the brand doing what it actually does well, not chasing a trend. This isn't ILIA's tasteful editorial aesthetic, and it's not trying to be. It knows its lane.
- **Double CTA placement.** "GO JUMBO" appears in the hero and again at the bottom of the "WHY YOU'LL LOVE IT" section — smart architecture for scrollers.
- **"23 different nicknames (all from you)"** is a charming community-sourced copy moment. It's light social proof that doesn't feel forced.
- **"Top Picks for You" grid** with "ADD TO BAG" functionality at the bottom is a genuinely useful browse layer that Sephora regularly whiffs on in their own product emails.

## What's weak

- **Power Grip twice in four days.** After "Juicy NEW drip from Power Grip 🌹" on July 13, coming back to the same franchise — same product, different size — feels like editorial fatigue. The brand history is working against this email.
- **Zero urgency.** No deadline, no "while supplies last," no countdown. Compare this to Sephora's "Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase" — that email had me moving. This one lets me defer indefinitely.
- **No loyalty integration in the offer.** The Beauty Squad badge lives in the footer like legal copy. For someone who is constantly points-optimizing, the silence on whether e.l.f. rewards stack here — or whether I'd do better waiting for Ulta's 21 Days of Beauty — is a real conversion killer.
- **"GO JUMBO" as a CTA is fun but slightly vague.** The first time it makes me smile; by the second instance I want it to say "Shop Jumbo Power Grip Primer" so I know exactly where I'm landing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline or quantity signal.** "Only X left at this price" or "Offer ends Sunday" would convert the fence-sitters like me. Without it, the great value story just evaporates into the queue.
- 2. **Surface a Beauty Squad tie-in.** Even one line — "Earn Beauty Squad points on every order" — would make the direct-purchase case against Ulta. Right now Ulta wins that argument by default.
- 3. **Vary the Power Grip cadence.** If July 13 was the new colorways and July 17 is the Jumbo format, space those out by at least a week or combine them into one "the full Power Grip lineup" moment.
- 4. **Give the CTA a product noun.** "Shop Jumbo Primer" > "GO JUMBO" — same energy, more intent clarity.
- 5. **Lean into the size comparison visual more.** The Jumbo vs. OG side-by-side below the fold is good; pulling that into the hero (think Drunk Elephant's stripped-down product-first framing) would make the "3X" claim land viscerally instead of just textually.

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics "Go Jumbo" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is sent via Braze (ESP), using `links.elfcosmetics.com` for click-tracking and `braze-images.com` for image hosting. The truncated source reveals several structural and accessibility issues; compliance elements (footer, unsubscribe) cannot be fully assessed due to truncation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Nested template artifact — duplicate `nl-container`**
Inside `html_block block-1`, there is a full second `<table class="nl-container">` wrapping a second `<table class="row row-1">`. This is a block-within-block nesting artifact, likely from embedding a full layout template inside a Beefree/Braze drag-and-drop block. The outer container already provides the row structure; the inner container is redundant and can break column stacking on mobile.

```html
<!-- Outer row -->
<table class="row row-1" ...>
  ...
    <div ...>
      <!-- Inner duplicate layout -->
      <table class="nl-container" ...>
        <table class="row row-1" ...>   ← duplicate
```

**Click-tracking links wrap destination URLs opaquely**
All hrefs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` with base64-encoded payloads. This is expected for Braze, but means UTM parameters on destination URLs are not inspectable from the email source alone. See Section 6.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo CTA link**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This removes the logo link from keyboard navigation order. If this link is intentional (e.g., branded homepage CTA), removing it from tab order is an accessibility regression. Only use `tabindex="-1"` for purely decorative/redundant links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (Apple Mail tabs, Samsung Mail) display the `<title>` value as a fallback label. An empty title is a missed slot; it should mirror the subject line or preheader.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` — blocked in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip external `<link>` tags. Open Sans will silently fall back to Arial/Sans-serif in the majority of opens. This is a known limitation; the fallback stack is correctly defined, but the Google Fonts call has no practical effect at scale.

**Empty HTML blocks (block-2, block-3, block-4)**
Three consecutive `html_block` elements contain only whitespace divs:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These render as invisible zero-content cells but add DOM weight and can produce unexpected spacing in Outlook due to `mso-table-lspace/rspace` interactions. Should be removed if they serve no structural purpose.

**Image `alt` text is minimal**
```html
<img ... alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f.">
```
Acceptable for a logo, but when images are blocked (common in B2B/Outlook environments), the above-fold experience degrades to just "e.l.f." with no product or offer context. Consider `alt="e.l.f. Cosmetics — Go Jumbo Power Grip Primer"` to preserve message intent in image-off rendering.
