# Uh-huh honey 🍯
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. I've been counting Lush emails the way I track Ulta's "ends tonight" cycle — and one day after "Zero packaging. Double points." dropped (the email I actually stopped for because I could do the math), they're back with a honey collection showcase. The "Uh-huh honey" subject made me smile for exactly one second — Beyoncé reference, well played — and then I opened it and realized there's nothing here for me to *do*. No launch, no sale, no event, no points multiplier. Just: here are our honey-scented things, would you like to shop?
- The creative is clean and cohesive — the yellow-on-black color story works, "Honey I Washed The Kids" is a genuine cult product, and the layout doesn't fall apart. But this is a catalog email dressed up in a punny subject line. Compare it to how Drunk Elephant ran "Time to come clean" — editorial voice, product category, and a clear reason to click in one subject. Or how e.l.f. put `$14` directly in their subject line and let the price do the heavy lifting. Lush gave me a vibe. Vibes don't convert.
- I'd scroll this in about four seconds and mark it read. I wouldn't click through and I definitely wouldn't forward it — there's no deal to DM, no limited drop energy, nothing that makes a friend need to see this right now.

## What's working

- **The honey aesthetic is genuinely cohesive** — yellow/black color story, the bee puns in the hero copy ("A Buzzy Bestseller"), and the actual honey products all lock together visually. This is Lush doing Lush well.
- **"Honey I Washed The Kids" as the hero product is the right call** — it's their Tatcha Water Cream equivalent, a cult item that gets mentioned organically. Anchoring on it is smart editorial.
- **Diverse models featured with intention** — Black women are prominently in the imagery, not tucked into the bottom grid. Real representation, not performative.
- **"More Points, Less Plastic" section ties the loyalty thread** — it connects back to the app and the sustainability brand promise. A light callback that Lush subscribers will recognize.
- **Layout reads clean on mobile dimensions** — the product grid doesn't break, text doesn't overlap, and the hierarchy flows logically from hero to product tiles to app CTA.

## What's weak

- **No actual offer** — free shipping at $15+ is table stakes, not a reason to open my wallet today. Sephora's Beauty Insider events and Ulta's 21 Days give me a specific dollar value to react to. This gives me... delivery logistics.
- **Cadence is eating itself** — this is email number twenty-eight, landing one day after the double-points push. Without a distinct reason to exist today, it reads like a frequency-target filler send.
- **"Shop Now" is doing zero work** — after e.l.f. gave me `$14` in the subject line and DE gave me a pun-loaded CTA, "Shop Now" feels like a template placeholder. Even "Shop the Hive" is more Lush-coded.
- **Nothing is new** — "Honey I Washed The Kids" is a bestseller, not a launch. If there's a new format, size, or formula in this drop, the email doesn't tell me. No launch energy means no urgency to act before it sells out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject line do actual work** — the pun is great, but puns plus zero stakes = zero clicks. If the honey collection is on sale or a new product is dropping, say so. The Beyoncé hook earns a read; it should deliver something on the other side.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Honey I Washed The Kids is 20% off — buzz won't last 🍯`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New honey body wash drop: the cult one, better`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your cult-fave bath bar + 3 honey products worth the cart`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping stacks with rewards — stock the hive while you can`
- 2. **Add a time frame** — "This week only" or "July collection" adds urgency without lying. This email has no reason to exist on July 12 versus August 12. That's a problem.
- 3. **Make the product cards clickable with specific CTAs** — if tapping "Honey I Washed The Kids" in the grid goes directly to that PDP, that's a conversion. "Shop Now" going to a collection page adds a click. Remove the friction.
- 4. **Surface the loyalty hook mid-email, not just at the bottom** — "More Points, Less Plastic" is buried in the footer. Yesterday's email led with the double-points incentive; today's should at least say "earn rewards on honey favorites" somewhere above the fold on mobile.
- 5. **Wait 48 hours** — sending a product showcase the morning after a loyalty push means neither email gets to breathe. One more day and this lands with more distinct identity.
