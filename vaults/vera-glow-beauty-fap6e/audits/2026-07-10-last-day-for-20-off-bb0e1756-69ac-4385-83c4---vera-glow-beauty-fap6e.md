# Last day for 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. That's where I am with Sally, and "Last day for 20% off" landing the morning after "Your summer must-haves" is... a lot. But I'll give credit where it's due — this email at least has a reason to exist. The LAST DAY hero banner is clean, the 20% off number is front and center, and the deadline is the actual date (July 10, today), which makes it feel credible rather than manufactured.
- Here's what stings, though: I remember the July 3rd "Celebrate with 25% off sitewide" email — that one was clean and confident, full sitewide discount, no asterisk in the hero. Today I'm being urgency-nudged into 20% off *almost* everything, hair color excluded. That's a step down in generosity dressed up as a countdown. The "(almost)" is doing heavy lifting and I notice it every time.
- The Sally Rewards multiplier section gets a genuine thumbs up — I do the points math, and seeing 4x on $50+ spelled out makes me open a calculator. I'll probably click. I won't forward it.

## What's working

- "LAST DAY" in the hero band is visually punchy — the hierarchy is clean, offer lands before I have to scroll anywhere
- 20% off is a specific number and the deadline is an actual date, not "limited time" vagueness — that's Drunk Elephant energy in terms of respecting the subscriber's intelligence
- Sally Rewards Buy More Earn More section breaks out the 2x/3x/4x tiers explicitly — loyalty math made visible is always a win for someone like me who plans purchases around multipliers
- Clean render, no broken elements, icons all loaded, no layout chaos

## What's weak

- "20% off (almost) everything" in the hero reads like a hedge — the July 3rd 25% off sitewide had no parenthetical doubt and scored better for a reason
- Hair color excluded is a real miss for a hair brand; I came to Sally for color treatments and now I need to mental-math what's actually in scope
- Back-to-back sends (yesterday was "Your summer must-haves," today is last day for 20% off) — Glossier went through a sale-resurrection arc that annoyed me but at least it had dramatic pacing; this is just email fatigue
- No lifestyle context, no models, no editorial moment — Lush's "The root of good hair" send on July 8 had an ingredient story; this is a price billboard on a pastel background

## Recommendations

- 1. Own the exclusion in the hero copy — "20% off styling tools, nails & hair care" is more honest than "(almost) everything" and sets better expectations before I land on a restricted cart
- 2. Drop one hero product image — even a single tool or nail kit gives me something to want before I click; e.l.f. SKIN's duo-focused email last week proved a specific product converts harder than a category sweep
- 3. Make the Rewards section tier-aware — I don't know if I'm at the 4x level yet; Sephora has conditioned me to expect messaging that speaks to my actual tier, not a generic ladder
- 4. Pull the Klarna widget — I'm not financing a $40 Sally haul in four payments, and it's taking up real estate that could show me a hero product
- 5. Tighten cadence — two emails in 24 hours at the tail end of a sale signals panic, not strategy

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `Founders Grotesk` declared but not loaded**
```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```
No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the `<style>` block. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. If this is an intentional brand font, the declaration is missing; if it is a leftover, the dead reference should be removed.

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on `img`**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` is not a valid or meaningful property for `<img>` elements. It is a no-op on images in most clients, but it can cause whitespace artifacts around inline images in legacy rendering engines (Outlook, older Yahoo). The rule should be removed.

**Issue — `lang` attribute missing from `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang` attribute present. Screen readers (JAWS, NVDA) use this to select the correct voice/language profile. CAN-SPAM does not require it, but WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 does. Add `lang="en"`.

**Minor — Open Sans pinned to v17**
Font URLs reference `opensans/v17/...` from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Google has released newer variable-font versions; v17 static woff2 files remain served but are on a legacy path. No confirmed 404s, but worth updating on the next template revision. More importantly, most major clients (Gmail, Outlook) block external `@font-face` loading entirely, so these declarations affect only Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail.
