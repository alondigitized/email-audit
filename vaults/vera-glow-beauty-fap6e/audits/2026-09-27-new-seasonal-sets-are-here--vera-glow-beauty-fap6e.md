## Executive summary

- Okay, "Perennial Beauty" is gorgeous — I'll give ILIA that. The dried orange blossom shot with the sky-blue backdrop feels genuinely editorial, not stock-photo beauty-brand template. But I'm four scrolls deep before I hit a single named product with a price or a shade name I can picture on my skin tone, and that's the problem. This is "new holiday sets are here" energy dressed up as a California nature diary, and while it's easily the prettiest ILIA send I've logged (better than the sepia "Fall Tones" grid from 9/26), it asks a lot of patience for a Sunday inbox skim.
- Would I click? Maybe, on "SHOP THE COLLECTION" purely because the palette photography got me. Would I forward it? No — there's nothing here specific enough to send a friend ("look at this shade" or "this is 30% off" is forward-bait; "look at this flower" is not). This reads like a brand deck, not a targeted email to a Rouge/Diamond member who's doing points math in her head.

## What's working

- The hero visual — orange blossoms against sky blue — actually stopped my scroll, more art direction than most beauty brands bother with.
- "New Shades" tag on the baked face palette section gives me a real reason to care — last year's sold-out product coming back is a genuine hook, not filler copy.
- Section structure (Palettes → Mascara → Lip → Eye) is at least organized like a real gift-guide, easier to scan than Sally Beauty's usual sprawl.
- "1% for the Planet" partnership section with named local orgs (One Tree Planted, Ascot Hills Park) feels authentic to their "Southern California roots" framing, not just greenwashing copy.

## What's weak

- Zero prices anywhere. I'm Rouge tier and Diamond — I'm doing the math on every purchase, and this email gives me nothing to calculate with.
- No mention of VIP points, member pricing, or any loyalty signal at all — treated like a cold subscriber, not someone who's bought from them before.
- The "$75+*" free mini stylus offer at the very top is the ONLY concrete offer in the whole email, and it's in tiny top-banner text I'd blow past on mobile.
- Product photography is moody and pretty but genuinely hard to shop — the lip set photo shows me rocks and a hand, not the actual shade swatches I need to decide.
- "Perennial Beauty" as a title card is a cute pun but tells me nothing — compare to Ulta's "20% off is calling," which at least leads with the number.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual offer or price range in the hero, not buried in 8pt banner text — ILIA sold me nothing concrete until I'd scrolled past four product sections.
- 2. Add shade-swatch or "shop by shade" thumbnails next to each set — moody lifestyle shots are gorgeous but I can't tell if the blush shades work for my undertone.
- 3. Surface VIP/loyalty status somewhere — even a line like "ILIA VIP members get early access" would make me feel like a known customer instead of a cold lead.
- 4. Move the 1% for the Planet section up or trim it — it's a nice brand story but it's pushing actual shoppable product further down a very long scroll.
