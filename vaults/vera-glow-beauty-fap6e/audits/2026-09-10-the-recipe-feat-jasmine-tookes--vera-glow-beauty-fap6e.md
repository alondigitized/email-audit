# The Recipe feat. Jasmine Tookes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "The Recipe feat. Jasmine Tookes" — this is a genuinely fresh format for ILIA, and it's the best thing they've sent me this stretch. Instead of another Overglaze/Lip Sketch shade-recap I've now seen three different ways this week (9/3, 9/5, 9/6), this is a celebrity/founder tutorial with an actual 3-step numbered routine — Paint, Sketch, Frame — each step tied to a specific product and shade name. Jasmine Tookes as "model, mother, moonlights as a makeup artist" is a nice hook, and the quote pull with her portrait breaks up the product shots so it doesn't feel like a catalog dump.
- What actually gets me is the free mini Eye Stylus Shadow Stick at $75+ — that's a real, specific loyalty-adjacent perk, not a vague "treat yourself" line, and it's framed clean at the bottom with its own CLAIM NOW button. Compared to the Sephora Daily Deals email I scrolled through forever for "50% off select brands" with zero specificity, this is a breath of fresh air — one narrative, one gift mechanic, done.
- I'd open this, I'd scroll the whole thing because the routine format actually held my attention, and I'd probably forward it to my friend who's into the monochromatic eye trend. I wouldn't say it's an instant-click for me personally since I don't own Brunel or need a full 3-step kit right now, but it's the kind of send that keeps me subscribed instead of annoyed.

## What's working

- The numbered Paint/Sketch/Frame routine gives me a reason to read past the hero instead of skimming to the CTA.
- Jasmine Tookes as a working model-mom-MUA feels more credible than a generic influencer slap, and her quote adds warmth without being cheesy.
- The $75+ free mini gift is a concrete, honest offer with a visible asterisk pointing to real terms, not buried fine print.
- Product shots under each step (Eye Stylus, Thru Line liner, Limitless Mascara) are named and shaded specifically — Relic, Adorn, Gesture, Before Dawn — which matters to me since I'm always checking exact shade names before I add to cart.
- Clean use of white space and the dusty-rose/cream palette keeps it feeling premium, unlike the cluttered grid emails I get from mass brands.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere in the whole email — not on the eye stylus, liner, or mascara — so if I'm price-comparing against Sephora points or my Ulta 21 Days window, I have to leave the email to do that math myself.
- Zero mention of ILIA VIP or any tier/loyalty status despite the icon footer literally saying "Join ILIA VIP" — as a points-math person, that's the one thing I actually want surfaced up top, not buried in the footer icon row.
- No urgency or deadline on the $75+ gift beyond "see site for details," so there's no reason for me to act today versus next week.
- This is ILIA's fourth distinct creative approach in eight days (employee spotlight 9/8, lip math 9/6, blush routine 9/5, Overglaze reprise 9/3) — genuinely varied, but the cadence itself is a lot for one brand in my inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "starting at $X" under at least the hero Eye Stylus Shadow Stick — I shouldn't have to leave the email to know if this fits my budget before the $75 threshold.
- 2. Surface ILIA VIP points/status language near the gift banner ("VIP members get double points on this order") instead of leaving it as a tiny footer icon — that's the section I actually read twice.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the free-mini offer ("through 9/14") so it isn't open-ended and forgettable.
- 4. Consider a "shop this shade for your undertone" personalization line since Jasmine's look is deep/warm — a quick nod to skin tone matching would tie into ILIA's "clean, personalized" brand DNA better than a generic routine.
