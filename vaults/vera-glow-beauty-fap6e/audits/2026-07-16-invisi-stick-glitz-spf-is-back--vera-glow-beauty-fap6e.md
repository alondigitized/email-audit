# Invisi-Stick Glitz SPF is back ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, first: e.l.f. has been in my inbox five times in six days, so the bar for "earning" an open is getting genuinely high. And then this lands — "Invisi-Stick Glitz SPF is back" — and I'll admit, I straightened up. A sold-out SPF restock with a glitz finish in mid-July is exactly the kind of send that still cuts through frequency fatigue. The hero is gorgeous: big golden product image, that warm summer-glow palette, and copy that reads "SPF that gets glitz done." — which is punchy enough to stop my thumb. "BUTTERBALL YES" as the CTA made me actually laugh out loud, and honestly that's the e.l.f. voice firing on all cylinders.
- That said — I'm clocking a missed opportunity here. The Gloss Mode restock email from 7/14 scored high in my brain specifically because "2nd restock, act fast!" told me exactly how limited the window was. This subject is softer: "is back" doesn't tell me if there are 200 units or 20,000. The restock urgency is implied, not activated. And the body breaks the conversion funnel almost immediately with a "find your skincare quiz" section wedged between me and checkout.
- Would I click? Yes — this product is seasonal, functional, and that no-white-cast callout is genuinely relevant to me. Would I forward it to a friend? Probably just screenshot the product panel and text it. The email itself is more a landing page than a share-worthy moment.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is doing the heavy lifting.** The golden glittery product visual against that warm background stops the scroll — way more editorial than e.l.f.'s typical layout, closer to the energy I associate with a Sephora Collection launch email.
- **"BUTTERBALL YES" is perfect.** That CTA copy is weird and specific and funny — it's e.l.f. voice at its best. I'm not going to forget it.
- **"Clear on all skin tones (no white cast)"** is called out explicitly in the feature list, and that signal matters to me as someone who cross-shops shade-range across brands. e.l.f. gets credit for making it a bullet, not a footnote.
- **Seasonally airtight.** While Lush just sent me a full Halloween email today (July 16th — I cannot), this SPF send is exactly on-calendar for summer peak. Zero cringe, zero confusion.
- **The feature list — SPF 30, glitz finish, glides smooth, summer festival season — is concise and gives me a reason to care.** It's not just "this product exists." It's "here's why this specific product for this specific moment."

## What's weak

- **No urgency mechanics.** "Is back" implies scarcity but commits to nothing. Compare to the 7/14 Gloss Mode email: "2nd restock, act fast!" told me this was a flash window. This subject feels like a restock that'll last all summer, which deflates FOMO entirely.
- **The quiz section kills the funnel.** Right after "SHOP NOW," I hit "find your skincare / GET STARTED" — a quiz CTA. That's a momentum reset when I was already warm. Drunk Elephant and ILIA never break the single-intent flow like this.
- **Zero loyalty hook.** I'm a regular e.l.f. Beautyscape member and there's no mention of points, member-early-access, or any signal that e.l.f. knows I'm a repeat buyer. Sephora's Beauty Insider emails always surface a "bonus points on this purchase" line — it's the closer. This email treats me like a stranger.
- **The "Top Picks for You" grid at the bottom feels algorithmic-filler.** At that render size I can barely read the product names. Either lean into the Glitz SPF hero all the way, or make the grid actually useful with legible copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the restock scarcity in the subject.** "2nd restock, act fast!" on 7/14 is proof e.l.f. knows how to do this. The subject should be doing the urgency work, not leaving it vague.
- 2. **Kill the quiz section or move it to the footer.** Nothing should interrupt the path from hero → CTA → checkout. The quiz belongs at the bottom of the email as an afterthought, not planted between two shop buttons.
- 3. **Add a loyalty callout.** One line: "Beautyscape members earn points on every Glitz SPF purchase." Costs nothing to add; closes the loop for loyal buyers.
- 4. **Replace "#sunroutchable" as preheader.** Right now it's a campaign hashtag sitting in the preview slot — it adds nothing at the inbox level. That's a wasted character budget.
- 5. **Legible product grid or no product grid.** At the size it renders, the "Top Picks" section is unusable on mobile. Lean all the way into the Invisi-Stick hero or cut it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your sold-out Glitz SPF is back ☀️ (for now)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Glitz SPF restock — SPF 30, no white cast, won't last`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited glitz finish. SPF 30. Clear on all skin tones. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sold out once already — grab it before round two disappears.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze-encoded redirect URLs obscure final destinations**
All CTAs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/…~~/AAQRxRA~/…` — Braze's click-tracking format. The actual destination URL is base64-encoded inside the token. UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without decoding. If UTMs are appended post-redirect at the Braze level, they will be invisible to link validators and QA tooling that don't follow the full redirect chain.

**Recommendation:** Decode a sample link and confirm UTM params are present on the final landing page URL.

**Image asset hosted on `braze-images.com`**
```
src="https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6537d6e7f775160058956f5a/original.png?1698158310"
```

The `appboy` path prefix is Braze's legacy CDN namespace (Appboy was Braze's prior name). This is functional but indicates the asset predates a potential CDN migration. The cache-busting query string `?1698158310` corresponds to a 2023 Unix timestamp — this image was uploaded in 2023 and may not be the current campaign asset if the file was reused.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
The document title is blank. Some email clients (notably Apple Mail on macOS) surface the title in thread previews or tab labels. This is a minor gap.

**Google Fonts `<link>` will fail in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Web font loading via `<link>` is blocked by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and most corporate clients. The stack fallback (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) means rendering degrades gracefully, but the font load is a dead HTTP request for the majority of recipients. This is inside `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <![endif]-->` so it is at least suppressed in Outlook — correct pattern.

**Three consecutive empty `html_block` elements**
```html
<table class="html_block block-2" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
<table class="html_block block-3" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
<table class="html_block block-4" …><tr><td class="pad"><div … > </div></td></tr></table>
```
Blocks 2, 3, and 4 contain only a non-breaking space. These appear to be leftover placeholder rows from the BEE editor. They add ~300 bytes of dead markup and create invisible 0-height rows that some clients may render with default cell padding.

**Preheader present and correct**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;mso-hide:all;…">Returning by e.l.f.ing demand!</div>
```
Properly hidden with belt-and-suspenders CSS and MSO hide. No issues.

**Logo alt text is minimal but present**
```html
alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f."
```
Technically sufficient; images-off rendering will show "e.l.f." No issues.
