# 🔔 Don’t miss 30% off ALL Sephora Collection, Sephora Shopper
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I've received four-plus Stacking Weekend emails this week — the July 1st launch was a genuine 9, I had a cart loaded before I finished reading it. The July 3rd reminder hit well. The second July 3rd send was already redundant. And now this one rolls in on July 5th with a subject promising "30% off ALL Sephora Collection" and the body opens on... "BEAUTY WITH A FOLLOWING." That's a social trending hero. Not the Collection sale. Not the specific products I should be grabbing before 7/7. An influencer-adjacent editorial that pivots immediately into Paula's Choice new launches, a product grid, a "Beauty FOMO" social section, and a new product drop. It is a kitchen sink email wearing a sale subject line as a costume.
- I know about this sale. I planned around this sale. I already bought on July 1st. If you're sending me email number five, it needs to give me a reason to go back — which items are running low, what's new in Collection this season, something I don't already know. This does none of that. It also hits me with "Sephora Shopper" as personalization. I am a Rouge member. They have my full purchase history. "Sephora Shopper" is the minimum viable acknowledgment that I exist, and it lands like a form letter.
- Mark as read. Not forwarding to anyone. The only useful thing in here is the barcode I can screenshot for the store.

## What's working

- The Stacking Weekend banner at the very top is clean and surfaces the "Ends 7/7" deadline — for anyone who missed the first four emails, this is the right urgency signal
- In-store barcodes at the bottom are genuinely practical; I always screenshot those before a Sephora run
- No render bugs — the email is structurally clean and images load without gaps
- Paula's Choice new launch content is actually interesting to me as someone who tracks prestige skincare debuts — it just doesn't belong in this send

## What's weak

- Subject says "30% off ALL Sephora Collection" and the hero says "BEAUTY WITH A FOLLOWING" — those are two completely different creative concepts and I had to scroll past the hero to find the offer the subject promised
- Fifth email in four days on the same campaign with zero new creative angle — compare this to how ILIA ran their Buy 3 Save 20% arc, where each day shifted the concept (gamified dice, rock-paper-scissors, utility close); Sephora is just restacking the same message with different subject wording
- "Sephora Shopper" is not personalization for a Rouge tier member; Drunk Elephant called out "BEACH, PLEASE." with a single voice line and felt more personal than this
- The email tries to do too many things: sale reminder, Paula's Choice editorial, social trending, new product launch, loyalty — none of them land because none get enough room
- The hero real estate is entirely wasted on a social/influencer concept when the only ask should be "here are six Collection products to grab before Monday"

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the hero match the subject.** Surface the top 6–8 Sephora Collection bestsellers with a "moving fast — grab before 7/7" framing. The subject promises a Collection sale; the body should deliver a Collection sale, not a trending content module. e.l.f. SKIN's July 4th SPF send was consistent from subject to hero to CTA — this email breaks that contract in the first scroll.
- 2. **Drop the Paula's Choice content.** Spin that off into a dedicated new launch email — it's actually good content, just wrong container.
- 3. **Use my Rouge tier.** "As a Rouge member, here's what's almost gone" is a sentence that would make me click. "Sephora Shopper" makes me feel like I'm on a cold list.
- 4. **Kill the send or make it earn its place.** If you've already sent four Stacking Weekend emails, email five needs a new angle — sell-out alerts, specific Collection products, "staff picks from Collection" UGC. A re-skin of the same offer doesn't justify the cadence.
- 5. **Tighten the CTA.** "SHOP NOW" is the most generic possible button. "Shop Sephora Collection → 30% off" is at least directionally specific.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rouge alert: 30% off Sephora Collection ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `48 hrs left — these Collection picks are almost gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `These 6 bestsellers are moving fast before 7/7 — grab yours`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `2X points + free same-day delivery stack until Monday midnight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — blocks user zoom on mobile:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Drop `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `user-scalable=yes`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied to broad selector set:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Using `none` instead of `100%` suppresses iOS text scaling for users who have font-size preferences set at the OS level. Change to `100%`.

**`robots` meta malformed:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`"no index"` (two words with a space) is not a valid directive; the recognized value is `"noindex"`. Low risk in email context (crawlers rarely index raw ESP-served HTML), but should be corrected.

**Title tag is generic:** `<title>Sephora</title>` — no functional impact on deliverability, but screen readers announce this in some clients.
