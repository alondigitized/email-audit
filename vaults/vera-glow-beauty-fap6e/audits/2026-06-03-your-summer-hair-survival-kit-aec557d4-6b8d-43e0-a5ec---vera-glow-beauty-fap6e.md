# Your Summer Hair Survival Kit ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Email fourteen from Sally Beauty in three weeks — and honestly, I opened it anyway, which says something about the subject line. "Your Summer Hair Survival Kit" is a solid hook. *Survival kit* implies curation, not just a random product dump, and the BOGO 50% off is right there in the hero before I even blink. I don't have to hunt. That's respect for my time, which I appreciate after the May cavalcade of "last call" sends that weren't actually last calls.
- That said, my goodwill is running thin. The Humidity-Defying Serum and heat protection lineup is genuinely what I need right now — I've been fighting June frizz since Memorial Day — and the Pride Month color section is a cute, on-calendar touch. But then I see "Ends June 30," and whatever urgency the BOGO created just evaporates. A full month isn't a deadline. It's a permanent markdown with a cosmetic label on it.
- I'd probably click through to verify what qualifies as "select hair care" — the math is too good to ignore — but I'm not forwarding this to anyone. It reads like a functional grocery list, not a discovery. I mark it as read after clicking one product link.

## What's working

- **Hero offer above the fold, no hunting** — BOGO 50% is the first thing my eye lands on; the hierarchy earns its place.
- **Summer problem-solution framing** — "Put moisture in & keep frizz out" and "Defend your hair from heat damage" are June-specific benefits, not recycled all-seasons copy.
- **Diverse hero model does real work** — the curly-hair woman of color isn't decorative; watching her frizz-free result while reading about humidity serums is actually persuasive casting.
- **Pride Month section lands in the right week** — it doesn't feel bolted on; the colorful product palette makes sense with the timing.
- **Clean, scannable grid** — sections are labeled, visual breathing room is there, no layout chaos.

## What's weak

- **June 30 deadline kills the urgency entirely** — a BOGO running for a full calendar month isn't a sale, it's the new regular price. Compare this to Charlotte Tilbury's "FOUR Dreamy Eyeshadow Shades... For FREE!" send, which had a countdown-adjacent urgency even in the subject; Sally gives me four weeks and expects me to move today.
- **"Shop now" x five is lazy CTA work** — after ILIA spent an entire send educating me on kelp and kelp derivatives and still got a click from me, "Shop now" repeated five times feels like Sally checked out at the finish line. Name the categories in the buttons.
- **No loyalty/points math anywhere visible** — I am the person who mentally calculates Rouge points during checkout. Sally has a Beauty Club program and this email doesn't surface it once in any scannable section. That's a real miss for a BOGO event where stacking rewards would tip the "buy now" decision.
- **Cadence damage is compounding** — the "Okay, Last Chance 😉" on May 25 and the literal-next-day "Last Call for Beauty Deals" on May 28 already burned credibility. I approached this email pre-skeptical.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Shorten the deadline to 5-7 days** — "BOGO 50% off — ends Sunday" does more work than "Ends June 30." Urgency is a feature, not a constraint.
- 2. **Rename the CTAs to match the category** — "Shop Humidity Serums" / "Shop Heat Protectants" / "Shop Dry Shampoo" is three seconds of work and doubles the click signal. e.l.f. does this consistently with their category links; Sally should steal it.
- 3. **Surface the Beauty Club angle** — one sentence: "Stack your points on BOGO orders" is all it takes. Sephora makes points math the *reason* to buy during event windows; Sally is leaving that psychology on the table.
- 4. **Pick one hero product and commit** — ILIA's kelp email built an entire narrative around one ingredient story and I was hooked. Six products in a grid diffuses attention. The Humidity-Defying Serum could carry a summer frizz story on its own.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off: beat the summer frizz — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your frizz is on notice. BOGO 50% off, this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Humidity serums, heat protectants & dry shampoo — buy one, get one half off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer hair kit is waiting. BOGO 50% — don't sleep on it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">` has no `lang="en"`. Screen readers default to browser/system language; required for WCAG 3.1.1 and Apple Mail accessibility.

- **`Founders Grotesk` declared but not loaded.** The rule `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a custom font with no corresponding `@font-face` block. All link text will silently fall back to Helvetica on every client. If this font is intentional, it must be added via `@font-face` or served from a hosted CDN.

- **Open Sans v17 URLs.** Six `@font-face` blocks pull from `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/`. Google has versioned these as deprecated paths; the current version is v40+. Gmail, Outlook, and most corporate clients block external fonts anyway, making these requests wasted round-trips for the subset of clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) that would actually load them.

- **`img { white-space: pre; }` is a no-op.** `white-space` does not apply to replaced elements. Harmless but indicates a stale or copy-pasted template stylesheet.

- **Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`.** The industry standard is 600px. Devices rendering at 500–599px viewport width receive the desktop layout, which may be too wide for those screens.

- **`role="presentation"` on layout tables** cannot be confirmed from the truncated source. If absent, screen readers will announce structural tables as data tables.
