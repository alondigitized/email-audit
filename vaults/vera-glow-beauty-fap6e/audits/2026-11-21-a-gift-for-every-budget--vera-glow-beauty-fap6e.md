# A gift for every budget
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-21**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is a clean gift guide with real prices, and I like it better than the 11/19 "Plump from the inside out" send. That one hid its number. This one puts "$29," "$49," and "$99" in white circles right next to each kit, so I can do my math in a second. The "Under $30 / $50 / $100" structure is how I actually shop gifts: I set a budget per person, then pick.
- It's also a long scroll. The hero is just "Gifts under $30, $50, and $100" with a single "Shop now," and there's no percent-off anywhere up top. Sephora's Cyber Week email led with "30% OFF Benefit" in giant type and I got it in half a second. The real deal, "50% off all kits," is buried in the Atelier block at the very bottom, and the asterisk fine print is tiny. Next to the 11/17 "$158 of skincare for $69?!" send, this undersells itself.
- Would I click? Yes, probably on the Bright on Trend kit ($49, $91 value, which is about 46% off). Would I forward it? Maybe to my sister, since the "for your sister (the nice one)" copy is fun. I wouldn't forward it as a deal.

## What's working

- Price circles on every product ($29, $28, $24, $49, $37, $89, $90, $99) are readable at a glance and make the budget tiers easy to scan.
- The "$91 VALUE" and "$158 VALUE" tags under the kit prices let me do my points math. Bright on Trend is $49 for $91, which is about 46% off.
- The recipient copy ("your office wife," "your actual spouse") is a funny, millennial voice that fits me.
- Each tier has its own "Shop gifts under $X" button, so I can go straight to my budget.
- The kits are named with their contents (C-Luma, Protini, B-Goldi, Lala Retro). I know the exact SKUs, which Ulta's mass-prestige gift sets rarely list.

## What's weak

- The hero has no offer. "Shop now" under a generic holiday banner doesn't tell me anything. Sephora puts the percentage first.
- "50% off all kits" is only in the Atelier box at the bottom, in small type. The asterisk says it's "reflected at checkout," so I can't tell whether the circle prices are already discounted.
- No Rouge or loyalty angle at all. Drunk Elephant knows I'm in the Sephora ecosystem, and there's no Beauty Insider points or tier mention.
- It's a lot of scrolling for a gift guide, and the alternating image and text rows make it feel repetitive. The Juju Bar at $28 gets the same space as the $99 kit.
- This is another near-daily DE send, after 11/19, 11/17, 11/15, and 11/13. I'm tired of the cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put "Holiday kits 50% off, all month" in the hero banner, with the tier prices as secondary copy. That one change would lift this the most.
- 2. Cut each tier to two products. Shorter is better on a phone.
- 3. Move the fine print above the fold and say plainly whether the circle prices are already discounted.
- 4. Add a line like "Sephora Rouge: earn points on these kits."
- 5. Add a "best value" badge to Bright on Trend, where the value is clearest.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Holiday kits are 50% off, all month 🎁`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gifts under $30, $50, $100 (kits up to $91 value)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bright on Trend: $91 of skincare for $49. Shop by budget.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every kit is half off through 11/30. Here's your cheat sheet.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with a mobile media query and third-party tracking. The automated QA found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings. The real defects are insecure (`http://`) social icons, a recipient email address in a tracking URL, and font `@import` rules that are invalid because of where they sit in the stylesheet. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the relay and can't be confirmed from this data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links not probed:** 14 links on click-redirect domains were skipped, so destination status, redirect chains and landing pages are unverified.
- **Recipient email in a tracking URL (PII):** `https://drunkelephantus.everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/?e=darcy-ferment-drunkelephant-fap6e@etell.app&c=us_de_broad…` puts the plaintext address in the query string. It can leak through referrer headers, proxy logs and forwarded mail.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://mi.drunkelephant.com/p/up/80acaf1c74f9e002/o.gif?mi_u=98161986&mi_ecmp=us_de_broadcast_promotion_giftsbypriceres` carries a user ID (`mi_u`). It is a standard open pixel, but it has no `alt` (see section 3).
- **Two tracking stacks:** the `mi.drunkelephant.com` and `everestengagement.com` pixels are both present, which adds a request and a second place where recipient data can leak.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure images:** three social icons load over `http://`: Facebook (`cc02d07d-…png`), Instagram (`9b452915-…png`) and TikTok (`29f975e1-…png`), all on `http://image.cheers.drunkelephant.com/lib/fe3211717064057c751c78/m/1/`. They may be blocked or trigger mixed-content warnings. The same host likely serves HTTPS, so switching the scheme should fix this.
- **Missing alt text:** `o.gif` and the `everestengagement.com/ea/dKhpn7WOgU/` image have no `alt`. For tracking pixels, `alt=""` is the correct fix.
- **Invalid `@import`:** in the `<style>` block, `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…)` comes after the `body {…}` rule. `@import` must come first, so these are ignored. The `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/…">` tags in `<head>` are also stripped by Gmail and several other clients.
- **Font loading:** Brown Bold, Light and Thin depend on a third-party host (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`). The `BrownRegular` font-face points at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/…/v1719565389232/…`, a versioned site-asset path that could break when the site is redeployed. No web-font fallback stack is visible in the truncated source.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Odd mobile CSS:** `.vouchertext4` and `.fsname` set `max-width:15px/12px !important` and `width:17px !important` in the media queries. That could clip text on small screens. This is a code-level suspicion that needs a render test.
- **Stray comments in CSS:** `<!-- *[class=contenttable] … -->` sits inside the `@media` block, which is invalid CSS inside `<style>`. It is harmless but sloppy.
- **Generic `<title>`:** it says "Drunk Elephant" and is not descriptive.
