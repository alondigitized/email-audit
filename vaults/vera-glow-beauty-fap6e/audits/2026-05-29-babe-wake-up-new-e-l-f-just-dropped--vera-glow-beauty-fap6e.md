# Babe wake up, new e.l.f. just dropped 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Email seventeen from e.l.f. since May 15th. I counted. I'm still counting. My thumb has developed its own muscle memory for this sender, and today the subject lands with a very Gen-Z-coded "babe wake up" hook that I both appreciate and am mildly tired of in the same breath. But I opened it — I always open it, which at this point feels like a personal failing — and what's actually inside is a legitimately solid launch roundup: Soft Glam Cream Blush and Bronzer at $7 each, something bright for lips, a Power Grip Brow Setting Spray, a full lip marker spread, and a set at $4. The "drop e.l.f.ything: new makeup is here!" hero wordplay still works on me and I resent that.
- The layout is clean and functional. Every product gets an image, a name, a price, and a button. No mystery, no buried offer, no six-paragraph intro about the brand's "journey." That's better than what Glossier did with the Pink Capsule Collection email that scored a 5 from me — they made me scroll forever before I understood what they were actually selling. e.l.f. gives it to me straight, which I respect.
- I'd click on the cream blush — $7 for a cream formula is still genuinely unhinged value, and I want to stack-test it against my Sephora Rouge wishlist options. I would not forward this to a friend. Not because the products are bad, but because I have already sent e.l.f. recommendations four times this month and my group chat has started replying with "ok but is it drugstore" which is not the response I want anymore.

## What's working

- **"drop e.l.f.ything"** in the hero is clever, on-brand, and still lands — brand voice consistency earns goodwill when the cadence is trying to spend it
- **Prices are front and center** under every product — $7, $7, $4 — e.l.f.'s price IS the offer, and they don't hide it
- **Model photography is warm and inclusive** — the bronzer/blush section uses a model with a warm medium complexion that actually shows how the formula wears, which is useful to me as a shopper
- **Zero clutter** — no pop-up overlay, no "don't miss out" countdown timer in the middle of the product grid, just product → price → button
- **Multiple new SKUs in one send** rather than one hero product padded with filler, which makes it feel like a real drop moment

## What's weak

- **Seventeen emails in fourteen days** — the cadence is the review, at this point; no single email survives that volume without a haircut on open enthusiasm
- **"Shop Now" buttons everywhere** — generic. Charlotte Tilbury used "Complete Your Look" and it pulled harder because it felt intentional; "Shop Now" reads like a template
- **No loyalty or member angle** — I'm not seeing anything that speaks to me as a return customer, no "be first" framing, no points reminder, just the same email a cold subscriber gets
- **Subject doesn't name what dropped** — "new e.l.f. just dropped" is all vibe and no product signal; if you told me "Soft Glam Cream Blush $7" in the subject I would have clicked before I even opened

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one product name or price in the subject** — the difference between "Babe wake up, new e.l.f. just dropped" and "Babe wake up — $7 Cream Blush just dropped" is the difference between a tease and a tap. Name the hero SKU.
- 2. **Swap every "Shop Now" for product-specific CTA copy** — "Try the $7 Blush," "Grab the Brow Spray," something with a noun so I know where I'm landing before I tap.
- 3. **Add a single social proof signal** — one review count, one UGC mention, anything. ILIA's launch emails lean on waitlist numbers for their upcoming drop; e.l.f. has the community to do the same.
- 4. **Loyalty hook in the header** — a single line like "Points members: you're seeing this first" goes a long way toward making me feel chosen rather than blasted.
- 5. **Pull back to 4-5 emails per week max** — this is a creative problem, not just a frequency problem; after 17 sends the subject line has to work twice as hard and "babe wake up" is doing the lifting alone.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is rendered via Braze (formerly Appboy), using a table-based layout with MSO/VML conditionals for Outlook. Several structural and accessibility issues are present; link tracking is fully opaque through Braze's redirect layer, making UTM verification impossible from source alone.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All links route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` (Braze click-tracking)**. Destination URLs are not visible in source — UTM parameters, if any, are appended post-redirect and cannot be confirmed from the HTML.

**Evidence:** Only link in truncated source:
```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/9A0tMrMAhooMv4S2WwIWig~~/AAQRxRA~/i9VXyN33Jet...
```
No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` parameters are embedded before the Braze redirect token. Verify in Braze dashboard that redirect appends UTMs.

**Logo image hosted on legacy Braze domain:**
```
https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/...
```
`appboy` path is functional but reflects pre-rebrand asset storage. No deliverability risk, but image load depends on Braze CDN uptime.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some preview pane renderers use the title; leaving it blank degrades accessibility.

**Logo anchor has `tabindex="-1"`:**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes the logo link from keyboard navigation. This is intentional in some email templates (to prevent tab-focus on decorative links) but should be confirmed as deliberate UX.

**`<style>` block embedded inside body content (block-3 `<div>`):**
```html
<div style="..."><style type="text/css"> ... </style>
```
Inline `<style>` inside a content `<div>` is invalid HTML. Gmail strips `<style>` from body in some contexts; the `.responsive-text` mobile breakpoint rule may be silently dropped, locking font size at 16px on all devices.

**Nested `nl-container` table duplication in block-1:** The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner `nl-container` with identical structure. This appears to be a template assembly artifact (two wrapper layers concatenated). It adds markup weight and could cause double-rendering of padding in some clients.

---

The ellipsis (`…`) suggests the code was cut off either in source truncation provided for this audit or in the actual rendered output. If the latter, recipients receive an incomplete code — confirm full render in Braze preview and a seed send.

If this is a welcome series email, confirm whether dynamic offer eligibility gating via Liquid is expected and present in the non-truncated source.

**Authentication headers** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) are not evaluable from HTML source. Sending domain `m.elfcosmetics.com` should have DKIM signed with a `d=elfcosmetics.com` alignment and a DMARC policy of at minimum `p=quarantine`. Verify with a seed-send header dump or MXToolbox.
