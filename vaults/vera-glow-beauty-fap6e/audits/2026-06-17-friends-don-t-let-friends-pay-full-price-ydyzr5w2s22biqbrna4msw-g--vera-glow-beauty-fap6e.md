# Friends don't let friends pay full price
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Day seven. I want to say that upfront because I cannot separate the email from the run it's part of, and this send is actually doing something genuinely different — the subject pivots from the blunt discount headlines ("Save up to 25% off all fragrance," twice on June 15 alone) into a social, peer-sharing angle that I did not expect. "Friends don't let friends pay full price" is the first time Glossier has reframed the sale as something I'd *tell a friend about* rather than a deal I'd silently scroll past. The orange "Friends of Glossier" smiley badge in the hero is distinctly Glossier — playful, instantly branded, not the generic discount-blast look.
- But then I see "STILL ON" in gray above the hero copy and "not for long!" in the supporting text, and the spell breaks. I have received this sale offer in some form every single day since June 11. "Not for long!" loses all meaning on the seventh rotation. It's the retail equivalent of crying wolf with a 25%-off sign.
- The best thing in this entire email is buried at the bottom: the subscription lock-in offer. Twenty-five percent off on your first *and all future* subscription orders is actually new, actually compelling, and actually a reason to act right now instead of tomorrow — and Glossier hid it under the fold. I'd have clicked on that. Mark as read on the "Shop now" buttons above it.

## What's working

- The "Friends of Glossier" circle badge is sharp — it communicates membership tier and adds a social proof layer the previous six emails didn't have
- Subject line is the first genuinely creative pivot in this sale run; "friends don't let friends pay full price" makes me think *forward this to someone* rather than *scroll past*
- "Believe The Hype" UGC section with real star ratings and customer quote excerpts does real persuasion work — Drunk Elephant does this well too and Glossier earns credit here
- Glossier You 150ml appearing in "Extra Large Savings" is smart curation — fragrance is a considered purchase and that size matters
- The subscription CTA at the bottom is the only genuinely new offer in this entire sale run; it's a reason to act now that hasn't appeared before

## What's weak

- "STILL ON" + "not for long!" is a credibility problem on day seven — Sephora at least rotates featured product categories to fake freshness; this just admits the sale is still limping along
- "Shop now" across every single CTA is lazy — Drunk Elephant's product-specific buttons ("Shop Protini," "Shop the trio") make me feel like I'm being directed somewhere; "Shop now" goes nowhere specific
- The subscription offer is the hero this email needed and it's below the fold, after two product sections and a review carousel
- The "Extra Large Savings" price callout shows me $87 for Glossier You 150ml but I can't clearly read the before/after savings — in a *sale* email, show me what I'm saving, not just what I'm paying
- The social/sharing angle from the subject isn't followed through in the body — there's no "send to a friend" mechanic, no referral link, no copy that operationalizes the peer-sharing premise

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the subscription 25% lock-in to the hero** — it's the only offer in this email that's genuinely new and has lasting value; it should be the opening line, not the last thing I see before the footer
- 2. **Put a hard end date on the urgency** — "Sale ends Sunday at midnight" beats "not for long!" on every dimension; it's credible, it's specific, it actually creates FOMO instead of begging for it
- 3. **Follow through on the social angle** — if your subject is about telling friends, put a "copy this deal link" or "send to a friend" button in the body; the premise earns a mechanic
- 4. **Show savings delta in Extra Large Savings** — "Was $110 → Now $87" reads as a 21% saving at a glance; "$87" alone is just a price
- 5. **Retire "Shop now" for at least one specific CTA** — "Shop Glossier You" or "Lock in 25% forever" are buttons I'd actually tap
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your friends are already saving 25% — just saying`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: lock in 25% on every future order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Subscribe now and never pay full price again — Glossier You, Balm Dotcom, everything.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends soon. Here's the one offer worth keeping forever: 25% off every subscription order.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking element in `<body>`:**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KV9E2S3FM8ZWZFEZ7FMAN8RA_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. No email client will fetch this as a stylesheet, and no standard rendering engine will treat it as a click beacon. If Klaviyo intends this as a stylesheet injection (e.g., web fonts), it will silently fail in all major clients. If it's a tracking ping, it will only fire in webmail environments where Klaviyo's own JavaScript is executing — which is non-standard and unreliable. This element should be a `<link>` in `<head>` if it's a stylesheet, or removed if it's vestigial.

**Tracking domain:** All outbound links route through `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo standard). Consistent — no mixed tracking domains detected in visible source.
