# 20% Off Is HERE!  ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Charlotte Tilbury email in five days. I'm clocking the escalation: May 16 was a 15% off with exclusion-clause gymnastics that made me do mental math, May 18 was the mini gifts send that I genuinely loved (that one earned it), May 19 was the "WORLD-FAMOUS" brand filler I almost scrolled past — and now we're at 20% off everything with capital-E "EVERYTHING!" doing a lot of heavy lifting. I'll give CT credit for the percentage bump: 15% → 20% in under a week is an actual escalation, not just resending the same email in a different outfit. The hierarchy is clean, the loyalty callout is specific and genuinely useful, and the Collagen Lip Bath in Pillow Talk Pink is exactly the kind of free gift that hooks me.
- But — and this is a big but — "20% OFF EVERYTHING!" coming from the same brand that served me a wall of asterisks and fine-print exclusions on May 16 means I'm reading that hero copy with one eyebrow up. I can see the fine print block at the very bottom of this email. CT has trained me to go straight there before I get excited about anything. That trust erosion is entirely self-inflicted at this point.
- I would click for the loyalty gift specifically. I would not forward this to a friend unless I'd already verified the exclusions weren't brutal.

## What's working

- **The 20% hero is immediate and unambiguous** — large, ornate, pink-tinted, no scroll required. The offer is the first thing my eye lands on and "SHOP WITH 20% OFF" confirms the action before I have to think.
- **Loyalty callout is the star of this email** — naming the Collagen Lip Bath, specifying Pillow Talk Pink, and anchoring the $33 value makes this feel like a real reward, not a vague bonus. Compare this to the Ulta Benefit send from May 20 that buried member perks in a wall of text below the fold — CT is doing it right here.
- **CT escalated from 15% to 20%** — that's actual movement in offer value across the week, not just reskin-and-resend. That's more than e.l.f. has done with their 8-email May sprint, which has basically been the same Survivor collab energy on repeat.
- **The product flat lay above the second CTA** has warm, editorial richness — it looks like a Charlotte Tilbury mood board, not an Amazon cart. Brand feel stays intact even in a discount email.

## What's weak

- **"EVERYTHING!" is going to fight with the fine print** — I can see the legal block at the bottom, and after the May 16 exclusion experience, "EVERYTHING!" feels like a setup. One asterisk in the hero would actually be more honest and build more trust than letting me discover the caveats mid-checkout.
- **Four emails in five days is audibly loud at this point.** Drunk Elephant is doing three-word wordplay campaigns with actual build and momentum — "Let's get something clear" after "When you're bright, you're bright" feels like a coherent arc. This CT run feels like finance department drove the calendar.
- **No end date for the offer** — "for a limited time only" isn't urgency, it's filler. Lush's Dream Cream send included a clear hero deadline. Even a "ends Sunday" would move me faster than vague scarcity.
- **The app/loyalty features section at the bottom is visual noise** — three columns about free shipping, shade finder, and beauty secrets after the core offer and GWP just dilutes the close. Cut it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a credible deadline in the hero** — "20% Off Ends Sunday" beats "EVERYTHING!" for click velocity because it gives me a reason to act today rather than tab-and-forget.
- 2. **Move the loyalty free gift above the product flat lay** — it's the most differentiated offer on this page. Lead with the exclusivity; save the sitewide discount as the supporting act, not the only act.
- 3. **Dial back "EVERYTHING!" or add transparency** — "20% Off Sitewide\*" with a visible, single-line exclusions note up top actually converts better than an all-caps promise that breaks at checkout. I've learned this the hard way with CT specifically.
- 4. **Cut the three-column features section entirely** — this email has two strong offers. That section is marketing infrastructure dressed up as content.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 20% Off expires soon, darling`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free Pillow Talk gift + 20% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Collagen Lip Bath in Pillow Talk Pink is yours free — here's how to claim it`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends soon: 20% off sitewide + a free $33 gift for loyalty members`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Renders as a blank string in Outlook's title bar and some preview pane environments. Should be populated (e.g., the subject line or brand name).

**External font stylesheet will not load in most clients:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
The MSO conditional correctly excludes Outlook, but Gmail (web and app), Apple Mail on iOS, and Yahoo all strip or block `<link>` tags loading external CSS. Custom typefaces from this sheet will silently fall back to system fonts in the majority of recipients' inboxes. If fallback font-stacks are not defined inline, the visual hierarchy may shift unexpectedly.

**Image alt text:** The logo `<img>` alt attribute is not visible in the truncated source. If omitted, image-off rendering (common in Outlook by default) will show a blank where the logo should be. This requires source verification beyond the truncation point.
