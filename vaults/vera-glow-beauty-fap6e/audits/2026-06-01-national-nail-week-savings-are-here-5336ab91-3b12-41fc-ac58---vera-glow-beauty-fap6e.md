# National Nail Week Savings Are Here 💅
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen emails in roughly three weeks, and Sally finally pulled out a mechanic I hadn't seen in the entire run: a nail-specific BOGO tied to an actual calendar occasion. After the "Last Call ⚡" that wasn't last call, and the May 29 "flash sale through May 31" that apparently just shape-shifted into a June promotion, I came into this one with scar tissue — and still, the hero landed cleaner than expected. BOGO 50% off nail lacquer with a June 14 deadline is a sentence I might actually say to a friend. That's more than the last four Sally emails earned.
- The miss is the same miss that would get this email buried in my Sephora comparison folder: I cannot tell which nail lacquer brands are in this BOGO. Those are just pretty bottles. Are we talking OPI? Essie? Orly? A Sally house brand? For me, that answer changes whether I click in the next ten seconds or wait to see if Ulta's 21 Days of Beauty hits the same brand first. Charlotte Tilbury named a hero product in the headline of their "Magic Highlights" digest — Sally gives me a lavender bottle and a red button. Those are not equivalent.
- The second half of this email is a completely different shopping trip: Wella Lightener Bundle, hair care BOGO, Brazilian Wax Kit. I didn't sign up for a body wax email. I'd save this one to check the brand list later, but I'm not forwarding — my nail-obsessed friends buy their polish at Sephora, not Sally, and I'd have to caveat too hard.

## What's working

- **Freshest offer in the entire run.** BOGO 50% off is a new mechanic after endless 20%-off flash sales — this one feels like a different shopping event, not a reshuffled banner.
- **National Nail Week hook is calendar-legitimate.** June 1 timing is earned; the peg is real, and Sephora and Ulta are making the same play right now, which validates the occasion rather than competing with it.
- **Visual hierarchy is actually clean.** The nail polish hero dominates the fold, the 50% off copy is large and legible, the red "Shop Now" button reads immediately — no render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- **Deadline is visible in the body.** "Through June 14" is right there in the hero — that's a credible, specific deadline, not manufactured flash-sale panic.
- **Fulfillment stack is surfaced.** Free 2-hour delivery on $35+, free shipping, BOPUS, and Klarna are all visible without scrolling — that's a legitimately customer-friendly info layer.

## What's weak

- **Zero brand callouts in the nail BOGO.** This is the biggest miss. I need to know if OPI or Essie is in the BOGO before I make a decision — generic polish bottles don't give me that. e.l.f. always names what's in the deal; Sally hides it behind a category.
- **Secondary offers shatter the nail focus.** Jumping from nail lacquer to a Wella Lightener Bundle and a $39.99 Brazilian Glitter Wax Kit in the same email is three different shopping trips. The "More ways to save all month long" header signals this is a clearinghouse, not a curated moment.
- **"Savings Are Here" subject line wastes the BOGO mechanic.** The actual offer — buy one get one half off — is more compelling than anything the subject communicates. Leading with the mechanic would have done real work; "Savings Are Here" is placeholder copy.
- **Cadence fatigue is a tax on every signal in this email.** Email thirteen in three weeks means I'm reading "BOGO 50% off" with one eye already on the unsubscribe link. Lush managed to reset my attention with a genuine scent editorial on their ninth email; Sally has not earned that goodwill.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the brands in the nail hero** — even "OPI, Essie & more" directly under the BOGO headline triples my click impulse. A beauty insider doing the points math needs that signal before deciding whether to wait for Ulta's 21 Days of Beauty to cover the same SKU.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the mechanic:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off nail lacquer — National Nail Week 💅`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy one, get one 50% off nail polish · ends June 14`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to reinforce the deadline and brand breadth:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `OPI, Essie & more — through June 14 only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `June 14 deadline. Stock up on your go-to nail lacquer.`
- 4. **Cut or separate the secondary offers.** The Wella Bundle and Wax Kit belong in a separate "more to save" digest email, not as visual noise diluting the one legitimately strong hook this send had.
- 5. **Give the CTA more verb energy.** "Shop Now" is the "Discover" of buttons. "Shop the BOGO" or "Browse Nail Lacquer" is specific enough to pull the finger forward — same advice I'd give any brand coming off the heels of a run where every button says the exact same thing.

The `a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` rule references a custom font that has no corresponding `@font-face` declaration anywhere in the `<style>` block. All six `@font-face` entries load Open Sans only. Every link in the email will render in Helvetica (or system sans-serif) regardless of intent.

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts version:**
All Open Sans `@font-face` URLs reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/` — Open Sans is currently at v34+. Google Fonts preserves legacy version URLs, so rendering is not broken today, but any upstream CDN change would silently drop the font. The template should be updated to the current version URL or switched to a `<link>` tag pointing to `fonts.googleapis.com`.

`img { white-space: pre; }` is a no-op on replaced elements and is harmless, but indicates CSS cruft that may have been carried from a misconfigured template iteration.
