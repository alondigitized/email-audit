# Yes, please! Grab up to 50% off.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "SURPRISE! 50% OFF" up top with an extra day tacked on is a decent hook, but then this email just... keeps going. And going. It's basically a full-catalog dump — Kosas, BioDance, Crown Affair, Make Up For Ever, InnBeauty, Urban Decay, OLEHENRIKSEN, YSL, Marc Jacobs — with almost zero organization beyond "new stuff exists." There's no single hero I'm meant to care about, which for a launch email is a miss.
- The bright spot buried near the bottom is the 4X Points on fragrance through 9/23, which I already flagged as the real one-two punch when Sephora sent me that exact combo on 9/17 with "Your FREE fragrance sample set awaits" — so seeing it recycled again three sends later, now shoved under a wall of product tiles, feels like a downgrade. Compare that to 9/16's "Beauty's Biggest Hits," which led with 50% off Fenty/First Aid Beauty/Sephora Collection right up top and scored an 8 from me — this email has a better headline offer (up to 50%) but way worse execution because it's not surfaced anywhere near clearly enough.
- I'd scroll past this one. Not forwarding it — there's no single "you need to see this" moment, just a scroll-fatigue product grid. Marking as read, maybe circling back for the Chris McMillan shampoo since I've been wanting to try it.

## What's working

- The "SURPRISE! 50% OFF... An EXTRA day" banner at the top is a real, specific offer with urgency baked in.
- Chris McMillan Detox Shampoo gets a genuine hero treatment — full product shot, price, and a "CLEAN at Sephora" badge, which matters to me when I'm deciding what goes near my hairline.
- The "NEW · LIMITED EDITION · ONLINE ONLY" and "ONLY AT SEPHORA" tags on the product grid do quietly speak to my curation instinct — I like knowing what I literally cannot get at Ulta.
- Rewards tier bar (Insider/VIB/Rouge) at the bottom is at least present, even if it's generic and not telling me where I personally sit.
- 4X Points on fragrance (9/17–9/23) is legitimately good math for a Rouge member like me — I'll stack that if I remember it's even in here.

## What's weak

- This is basically 10 products stacked in a grid with no hierarchy — my eye doesn't know where to land after the shampoo hero, it's just scroll, scroll, scroll.
- The 50% off banner never resolves into an actual list of "Select Beauty" items — I have no idea what's included, which is the kind of fine-print vagueness that makes me suspicious of the offer.
- Zero personalization — no "Vera," no Rouge-tier callout, no points balance. Ulta's "Marisol, ENDS TODAY!" send at least fakes personal even if the code is generic; this doesn't even try.
- The fragrance 4X points banner is the same mechanic I already saw and liked on 9/17, but here it's crammed into a small tile competing with the YSL ad right next to it — the best offer in the email is visually the least important thing on the page.
- No skin tone/type personalization anywhere, despite this being a huge new-launch grid where that would actually help me decide what to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero offer and lead with it clearly — either the 50% off Select Beauty (with the actual category named) or the 4X fragrance points, not both diluted across a product wall. Right now the best deal (4X points) is the worst-placed.
- 2. Name what's included in "Select Beauty" directly in the banner — "50% off skincare & makeup" beats vague "Select Beauty" every time; Sephora's own 9/16 send did this better with named brands (Fenty, First Aid Beauty).
- 3. Add my Rouge tier and points balance somewhere near the top — even a simple "Rouge members: 4X points" line would beat the current one-size-fits-all banner.
- 4. Cut the product grid roughly in half — 10 launches in one email is catalog fatigue, not curation; group them by category (hair, skin, makeup) instead of a flat grid.
- 5. Move the fragrance 4X points banner up near the top fold — it's genuinely the best mechanic in this email and it's currently sandwiched at the bottom.
