# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so. Another Skechers email. Another browse-abandon trigger. Another "SHOP NOW." I've lost count of how many of these I've logged at this point, but the subject line is the tell that broke me a little — "This style has limited availability!" — which is literally, word for word, the subject Skechers sent on May 27th. I wrote about that one. I gave it a 7. And now here it is again, five weeks later, dressed in the same clothes.
- The self-aware copy inside — "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." — is actually kind of charming on its own. There's a wink there. The problem is the email doesn't back that wink up with anything. No discount. No urgency with a clock on it. No loyalty reward for coming back. Just the same teal SKX Nexus and a generic "SHOP NOW" button, presented like I haven't already seen this exact creative treatment multiple times this month.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. I wouldn't click, and I'd never forward this — what would I even say to a friend? "Hey, look, Skechers sent me the same email again"? No.

## What's working

- **The hero shoe is actually cute.** The teal/aqua SKX Nexus is a clean, fashion-forward colorway — styling is on the right track for a Millennial woman who cares about aesthetics.
- **"SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO."** — self-aware retargeting copy that at least acknowledges the dynamic. Genuinely better than pretending it's the first time.
- **Layout is clean and loads fast.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, no lorem ipsum — the email renders correctly and the visual hierarchy (hero → recommendations grid → nav) is easy to follow.
- **Women's product grid is correctly targeted.** The rec tiles are all women's athletic shoes, so at minimum the segmentation isn't showing me men's hiking boots.
- **Afterpay and Klarna visible** — smart for a shoe impulse purchase, even if it's buried at the bottom.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject line is an immediate credibility hit.** "This style has limited availability!" was the May 27 subject. Running it again June 30 tells me either their calendar is broken or they think I don't remember. I do.
- **Zero offer.** Not one discount. No percentage off, no dollar amount, no "come back for 15% off." ILIA's June 22 send started a whole run and still found ways to bake in value. Sephora would never send a retargeting email without a hook. This is just... a picture of a shoe.
- **"Limited availability" isn't substantiated anywhere.** The subject says limited, the hero copy says nothing about stock. How limited? One color? One size? It reads like a false urgency flag with no receipt to show.
- **No loyalty integration.** I'm a known customer — where's my points balance? Where's the "earn double points today" nudge? Ulta Beauty buries the rewards hook in nearly every send, and even their most bloated emails remember to include it.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA they could have picked.** Sally Beauty ran "BOGO 50% Off Hair Care" with a specific button this week. Even e.l.f. uses "SHOP [PRODUCT NAME]" to make the button feel like a destination. "SHOP NOW" goes nowhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an actual offer.** Retargeting without a price incentive is just spam. Even 10% off, "free shipping on your first pair," or a double points event would give me a reason to click that I don't currently have.
- 2. **Kill the recycled subject line.** If you're going to send the exact same urgency signal five weeks later, at least rotate the phrasing. The repeat is the kind of thing that trains subscribers to ignore you.
- 3. **Make the limited availability claim real.** Show remaining stock ("Only 3 left in your size"), a specific size exclusion, or a hard deadline. Right now it's a claim floating in a vacuum.
- 4. **Upgrade the CTA.** "SHOP THE SKX NEXUS" or "GRAB IT BEFORE IT'S GONE" — anything with a noun.
- 5. **Surface loyalty status.** If I'm in their system, show me my points balance or a tier benefit. Make me feel like a known customer, not an IP address that once clicked a shoe.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Teal's moving fast. Grab yours before it sells out.`
