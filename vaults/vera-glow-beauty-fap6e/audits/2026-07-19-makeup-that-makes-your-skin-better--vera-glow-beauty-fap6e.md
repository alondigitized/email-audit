# Makeup that makes your skin better™
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- ILIA, I love your products, but we need to talk. This is day five of a daily send streak — July 14, 15, 17, 18, and now today — and the subject they chose was their literal brand tagline. "Makeup that makes your skin better™" is the line on the jar, not an email hook. After the brown mascara mini on the 14th earned a 9 from me because it had a *specific* idea to sell, and "Two gifts are better than one" on the 18th kept the momentum going, landing in my inbox today with a brand manifesto scroll feels like a gear-shift into content filler. Glossier did this exact thing mid-July — pivoted from product to vibe — and I flagged it as a mistake then too.
- The email itself is a long catalog scroll: "Made for All Types" positioning, multiple product-type sections, a social proof block, and then finally a mini tote offer buried at the very bottom. There's no single hero moment, no reason why *today* is the day to shop, no urgency. Aesthetically it's still ILIA — the editorial skin photography is beautiful — but the creative is doing all the lifting while the offer strategy takes the day off.
- I would mark this as read. I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it. If you asked me what this email was about ten minutes from now, I'd say "...ILIA? Something about their foundation?"

## What's working

- **"Made for All Types" is the right positioning message** — inclusive clean beauty still resonates, and it's a stronger brand beat than anything Glossier has managed in their last eight emails
- **The social proof section ("A Million")** is a smart credibility signal — Drunk Elephant's "Golden hour every hour" wove this in effectively and ILIA is doing similar work here
- **The mini tote offer at the bottom** is at least *something* — free gift with purchase is always going to get my attention eventually, even if I have to scroll to find it
- **No render bugs** — the email displays cleanly with no broken images or layout chaos
- **ILIA's editorial skin-tone photography** is still their strongest visual asset and it shows here

## What's weak

- **Their tagline as the subject line** is the single biggest miss — I should not be able to swap this subject onto any email they've sent in the last five years and have it make equal sense
- **The mini tote offer is buried five sections deep** — if that's the campaign hook, it needs to be in the hero or at least in the subject; Sephora would have led the entire email with that offer and made it impossible to miss
- **Visual hierarchy is scattered** — too many equal-weight sections fighting for scroll energy; no single element dominates the way the brown mascara mini did on July 14th
- **No loyalty or points language visible** — as a Sephora Rouge doing the math on every purchase, I need to know if buying ILIA here earns me anything or if I'm better off going through Sephora to stack points
- **Cadence tax is real** — email five in five days and there's no new information, no launch, no concrete offer up top; this is the send that gets the ILIA tab closed on my inbox filter

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the mini tote or cut it entirely** — "the mini tote is back" is click-worthy; it shouldn't be the reward for scrolling through five brand sections. Move it to the hero, make it the whole point of the email.
- 2. **Replace the subject with the actual hook** — if the tote is the offer, say so; if it's a new shade launch, say that; a tagline subject concedes all open-rate lift back to the inbox
- 3. **Cut the section count to three** — pick three products, give each a clear CTA, kill the catalog scroll; Drunk Elephant disciplines themselves to fewer decisions per email and it shows in their click performance
- 4. **Add a loyalty signal** — one line: "Earn Sephora Beauty Insider points on every ILIA purchase" or an ILIA-rewards callout; without it I'm doing math in my head and the math says buy through Sephora to be safe
- 5. **Add a credible deadline to the tote offer** — "while supplies last" buries the urgency; a specific date ("offer ends July 21") would have me adding to cart immediately

- **Preheader Alt B:** `All skin types covered. Shop your shade — mini tote with qualifying purchase.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `@import` font loading will fail in Gmail:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules at parse time. Montserrat and the custom Klaviyo font will not load for the majority of subscribers. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` (applied to `p, h1, h2, h3`) will kick in correctly, but verify this is visually acceptable.

**Issue — Links are visually indistinguishable from body text:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
```
All five link pseudo-states are identical — same color as the brand text, no underline, no hover change. If any inline links appear in body copy (not just styled buttons), they will be invisible as links. Sighted keyboard users also lose hover feedback entirely.

**Minor — empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) render the title in the browser tab. Not critical, but a missed touchpoint.

**Cannot audit**: Image `alt` attributes — not visible in truncated source. All `<img>` tags must have non-empty `alt` text for accessibility and images-off rendering.
