# Your Shortcut to Beach Waves 💞
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Sally Beauty in my inbox with a beach waves pitch — I see it, I get it, it's seasonal. The "Skip the salon" angle is actually a flex because that's exactly how I'm thinking right now. The hero is clean, the model is doing the thing, and I spot the B2G1 deal fast enough that I don't feel lied to. But here's my honest reaction: Sally isn't where I live. I'm stacking Rouge points at Sephora or using my Ulta Diamond discount on T3 or Dyson tools, not CHI. The moment I saw CHI as the featured tool, my prestige-trained brain started doing the math on whether this is worth my loyalty program switch-up.
- The B2G1 on select hair care actually made me stop — Shimmer Lights purple conditioner is a legit product and I do use it. But then I scrolled and saw Dark Brown Hair Building Fibers lumped in with the beach waves products, and I immediately questioned whether anyone was paying attention when they assembled this. That's a curated-brand-girl dealbreaker.
- I'd probably click the Shimmer Lights specifically and bounce. I would not forward this. I'd mark it as "maybe later" and then forget about it.

## What's working

- **"Skip the salon. Keep the beach waves."** — The hero headline actually slaps. Short, confident, talks to me like I'm already thinking about it.
- **B2G1 deal with a hard deadline (May 22)** — Concrete, time-bounded, I can do the math immediately. That's how you speak my language.
- **Model with natural-looking beach waves** — She looks like a real person, age feels right for me, hair goal is aspirational without being unattainable.
- **Email renders clean** — No broken images, layout holds up, the hierarchy from hero → product → offer reads in the right order.
- **Shimmer Lights inclusion** — That product has a real cult following and is legitimately good. Smart pick for the hair care section.

## What's weak

- **Dark Brown Hair Building Fibers under the beach waves promo** — This is a jarring non-sequitur. Feels like a product grid auto-populate with zero editorial curation. It breaks trust in the whole selection.
- **CHI as the hero tool** — For a Sephora/Ulta shopper, CHI reads as mid-tier. If Sally carried something higher-end or leaned into the pro-supply angle as its actual competitive edge, I'd respect it more.
- **"Shop now" CTA is lazy** — There are like four "Shop now" buttons and none of them tell me what I'm clicking into. "Shop curling irons" or "Grab the waver" would do actual work.
- **Loyalty reward mention is buried at the bottom in tiny text** — I'm a points person and I almost missed it. That's leaving money on the table with customers like me.
- **No personalization** — "Your" in the subject isn't personalization. No first name, no tier acknowledgment, nothing that says Sally knows me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Ditch the Dark Brown Hair Building Fibers from this email entirely** — Curate the B2G1 section to on-theme products only. One off-topic product blows the whole editorial credibility.
- 2. **Lead with the loyalty math above the fold** — Something like "B2G1 + earn [X] rewards points" hooks a points-person before they scroll away.
- 3. **Replace "Shop now" with action-specific CTAs** — "Shop the waver," "Get the iron," "Try Shimmer Lights" — each CTA should describe the destination.
- 4. **Upgrade the hero tool credibility** — Lean into Sally's pro-supply identity ("salon-grade, not salon-priced") rather than fighting prestige brands on their own terms.
- 5. **Surface the loyalty section mid-email** — Move the rewards callout between the product sections, not after the footer.

`white-space: pre` on a replaced element (`<img>`) has no defined effect in the CSS spec but is non-standard and can trigger unexpected whitespace gaps in Gecko-based renderers. Should be removed.

**Issue 2 — Missing `@font-face` for `Founders Grotesk`**

Six `@font-face` declarations load Open Sans variants, but `Founders Grotesk` is never declared. Every anchor tag silently falls back to Helvetica. Either add the `@font-face` rule or correct the font stack.
