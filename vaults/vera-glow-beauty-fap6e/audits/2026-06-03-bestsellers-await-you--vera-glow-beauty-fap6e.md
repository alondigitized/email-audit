# Bestsellers await you!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so DE just sent what might be the most anonymous subject line in their recent history. After surviving fifteen-ish emails that swung from clever ("Adios, 25% off...") to catastrophically over-extended (the final-hours-that-never-ended), DE comes back June 3rd with "Bestsellers await you!" — and honestly? It got past me in the inbox entirely on autopilot. I opened out of brand recognition, not because the subject earned it.
- Inside, though, the email actually holds up better than I expected. The problem/solution format — Dull skin, Dry, Uneven, Puffy — speaks directly to how I think about my routine. And "SUMMER SKIN, SORTED" as the seasonal close lands. Clean layout, products I recognize and have opinions about, no gross render issues. This is a solid editorial send.
- But here's the thing: two days off the F&F sale cycle, DE wants me to shop at full price with zero offer, zero urgency, and zero loyalty math to do. ILIA's "NEW Soft Focus Finishing Powder" drop (scored 8/10) gave me a specific reason to click — a new product, a launch moment. This has none of that. I'd skim, maybe tap the C-Firma out of habit, but I'm not buying today. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Problem/solution format is genuinely smart for a beauty-obsessed audience** — "Dull skin? Dry? Uneven? Puffy?" maps exactly onto how I talk about my routine and look up products.
- **Product selection is credible** — C-Firma, what looks like Protini, the Babyfacial, an eye serum — these are the actual bestsellers, not filler SKUs.
- **"SUMMER SKIN, SORTED" is a clean seasonal close** — timely, confident, not overworked.
- **Visual hierarchy is clear** — the hero "NAME AN ISSUE. WELL, A SKIN ISSUE." anchors above the fold and the eye flows naturally through each concern block.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no broken sections.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is deeply forgettable** — "Bestsellers await you!" could've come from any brand, any week, any quarter. After the creative highs of "Adios, 25% off..." this is a stepdown that cost me any inbox excitement.
- **Zero offer = hard sell right after a sale** — Coming two days after "Hydrated skin" which itself followed the F&F marathon, asking me to shop full-price with no incentive reads as tone-deaf. Sephora's sale emails had an exact percentage front-and-center; this has nothing.
- **No loyalty framing at all** — I'm a devoted DE buyer and this email treats me like a cold stranger. No Rouge/Beautylover reference, no "as a member" nudge, nothing that says DE knows me.
- **CTA copy "SHOP NOW" is the laziest option** — Charlotte Tilbury ran "SHOP NOW" into the ground during their 11-email Exagger-Eyes cycle, and at least they had a free-shade hook. Verb + noun specificity ("Find Your Fix," "Shop the Edit") would do more work here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name a specific concern or product** — "Dull skin? DE has your fix." or the alts below. This is the single biggest lift. A generic "bestsellers" subject gets scrolled past in a crowded inbox, especially at email #15 in a streak.
- 2. **Add a soft loyalty or welcome-back hook for returning customers** — Even one line above the hero: "As a Drunk Elephant fan, you know these." It costs nothing and changes the tone from cold broadcast to relationship.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTAs per concern block** — Instead of one global "SHOP NOW," give each product section its own action: "Fix Dullness," "Hydrate," "Even Out," "Depuff." This creates click intent at the concern level, not just the brand level.
- 4. **Consider a GWP or sample offer to bridge the post-sale gap** — Coming right off F&F, even a "free deluxe sample with purchase" closes the psychological distance between sale-mode and full-price mode.
- 5. **Preheader should do real work** — If the visible small text at the top is the preheader copy, it's not pulling weight. Use it to name a specific product or concern.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dull, dry, uneven? We've got the fix.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer skin routine, built by DE`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `C-Firma, Protini + more — skin sorted for summer`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `4 bestsellers. 4 skin concerns. Find yours.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — HTML comments inside `<style>` block (invalid CSS):**

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block is invalid CSS. It is a legacy HTML 3.2 artifact and is parsed correctly by virtually no modern email client. These two ruleset declarations are effectively dead — `.contenttable` and `.mobile` width overrides will not apply on any client that enforces CSS spec. This can break mobile layout for the two affected elements.

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — Duplicate font loading (6 redundant HTTP requests):**

BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each loaded **twice** — once via `<link rel="stylesheet">` and again via `@import` inside the `<style>` block, pointing to the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` URLs:

```html
<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold" rel="stylesheet">
```
```css
@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold);
```

That's 6 requests where 3 are required. Most email clients strip `<link>` tags in `<head>`, so the `@import` copies are correct to keep — the `<link>` tags are the redundant ones and should be removed.

**Issue — Third-party font CDN dependency:**

Both `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (BrownBold/Light/Thin) and `www.drunkelephant.com` demandware static (BrownRegular) are loaded at render time. `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is not operated by the brand; an outage or rate limit causes fallback font rendering. Self-hosting all four weights on a brand-controlled CDN is lower risk.

**Cannot verify:** `alt` attributes on images — source is truncated before `<img>` tags. Must confirm all product images carry descriptive `alt` text and decorative images use `alt=""`.
