# NEW Thru Line Eye Essentials set
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually pretty covetable — "Thru Line Eye Essentials" is a genuinely smart bundle play: eyeliner + dual-ended blend brush + a custom sharpener, shot as a full artist's kit with pencil shavings scattered around like it's a still-life. ILIA's product photography game continues to be the best in my inbox this week, better than Glossier's flat product shots and honestly better than the pencils-as-school-supplies gag from ILIA's own "Clean Beauty 101" send on 8/4. But three separate "SAVE 15%" CTAs stacked down one long product page starts to feel less like storytelling and more like padding once I hit the third one.
- What actually gets me is the free mini gift mechanic buried at the very bottom — "Choose a free mini Eye Stylus with $75+" — that's a real GWP with a real threshold, and it should've been fighting the 15% off for hero real estate, not sitting after four scroll-stops of brush-angle education I didn't ask for.
- I'd click the 15% CTA up top without much thought since eyeliner + sharpener combo is exactly my "restock the kit" purchase pattern, but I wouldn't forward this — it's a nice-to-have, not a friend-worthy find like the Rory Gevis story send from 8/3 that at least gave me something to talk about.

## What's working

- The hero shot — loose pencils, curled shavings, the sharpener open — sells "artist's toolkit" instantly, no copy needed to get the concept.
- "An artist is only as good as their tools" is a clean, un-cringe hook that ties the whole email together without trying too hard.
- Breaking the set into hold the line / one brush, infinite eye looks / get to the point gives each component its own mini use-case, which is more useful than ILIA's usual "buy the trio" dump.
- 10 shades called out for the waterproof formula is a real personalization signal — I want to know it comes in more than one shade before I click.
- The free mini gift at $75+ is legit member-shopper bait; I'm the type who'll add a $12 lip crayon just to clear a gift threshold.

## What's weak

- Three identical navy "SAVE 15%" buttons running down the page is repetitive — by button two I've already decided, I don't need a third nudge.
- No actual price shown anywhere, just "15% off" with no baseline — I'm doing points-and-percentage math in my head with zero dollar figure to anchor it, which Sephora's emails almost always give me even when it's a flat coupon.
- Nothing here acknowledges I'm Rouge/Diamond — no "your tier gets X," no bonus points callout, which stings more in a beauty email than a general retailer one.
- The gift-with-purchase, the actual news of this send, is stranded at the bottom after four separate product-education blocks — that's an Ulta-level burial, and Ulta's "20% off ends tonight" emails at least keep the offer in the first screen.
- "*See site for details" under the gift line is the exact vague fine-print dodge that costs trust points for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "free mini Eye Stylus w/ $75+" gift up near the hero — that's the actual news hook, not a footnote. Sephora's Beauty Insider events lead with the gift mechanic; ILIA should too.
- 2. Cut two of the three "SAVE 15%" CTAs — keep one after the hero, one after the last product block. The middle one is dead weight.
- 3. Add a real price or "was $X now $Y" next to at least one CTA — I shouldn't have to do math with no anchor number.
- 4. Drop in a one-line VIP/tier nod ("ILIA VIP members save an extra X" or points-earn callout) — even ILIA's own loyalty program mention at the bottom (Join ILIA VIP) is a static footer icon, not a personalized hook.
- 5. Add a swatch strip of the 10 shades — "sensitive eyes and waterline safe" is a great claim but I want to see shade range before I click through.
