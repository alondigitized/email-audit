# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Four Skechers emails in twelve days, and now we're getting the second cart abandon in back-to-back days — yesterday was "Did you forget something?" and today it's "Your Items Are Waiting!" The headline "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US…" is actually good: self-aware, playful, the right tone for a brand that knows it caught me mid-scroll. But then I look at my "cart" and one of the three featured products is a Mommy & Me slip-in set. I don't have kids. Either the segmentation is off, or something got added to my cart I have zero memory of wanting. Either way, it breaks the illusion this email knows who I am.
- The skeleton is textbook cart abandon: specific products shown, clear blue CTA, loyalty reward callout further down. If yesterday's email hadn't already fired — same three products, same trigger, basically the same message with a new subject — I might have clicked today. But we already did this dance. Charlotte Tilbury tried three emails in four days on me this week and I'm already mentally scanning past them; Skechers is on the same trajectory.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. If I was serious about those sneakers, yesterday already gave me every reason to act.

## What's working

- "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US…" lands — it acknowledges the surveillance mechanic in a way that's charming rather than creepy, and it's better subject-adjacent copy than most cart abandons I see
- Three specific cart items are shown with actual names and distinct product photos — doesn't just say "you left something behind," shows exactly what
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is the cleanest CTA copy in Skechers' recent rotation — verb + object, zero ambiguity
- The "$5 REWARD ON $75+" block has a dark background that makes it pop visually from the rest of the layout; Ulta would have buried this below three rows of category imagery
- No visible render issues — product photos load, layout holds, text is readable without zooming

## What's weak

- Mommy & Me: Glide-Step Pro - Rosy Glow is a strange hero cart product for a 29-year-old with no kids in her purchase history — if the behavioral data doesn't support it, suppress it
- Zero pricing on any of the three cart items — I can't do the $5 reward math without clicking, which is exactly the friction a cart abandon should eliminate
- This is the second consecutive-day cart abandon; Drunk Elephant spaced their wordplay streak with breathing room between sends, and DE's May 20 email still felt like a fresh conversation — this one doesn't
- The $5 reward sits in its own island section, visually disconnected from the cart — the incentive never gets tied to the action it's supposed to drive
- Category nav grid (Women, Men, Kids, Clothing, New Arrivals, Sale) below the fold scatters focus when the entire email's one job is to close a single cart

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the Mommy & Me product for accounts without child-browsing signal** — showing it in the primary cart hero actively breaks segmentation trust and is the single highest-impact fix
- 2. **Show prices on the cart items** — ILIA does this right in their VIP emails; knowing the total + how close it is to $75 gives the $5 reward concrete, actionable meaning
- 3. **Wire the $5 reward into the cart section** — move the dollar math next to the cart total so the incentive is spatially connected to the button
- 4. **Wait 48 hours minimum before the re-trigger, or add new information** — a low-stock flag, a credible deadline, or a free-shipping unlock would justify a second send; restating "your items are waiting" doesn't
- 5. **Cut the category nav** — six navigation links below a cart abandon email is noise; save them for the browse-trigger sends
- **Subject Alt A:** `Still thinking? Here's $5 to decide.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Glide-Step Pro might not wait much longer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members get $5 back on $75+ — your cart is almost there.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes are moving fast on the styles you saved.`
