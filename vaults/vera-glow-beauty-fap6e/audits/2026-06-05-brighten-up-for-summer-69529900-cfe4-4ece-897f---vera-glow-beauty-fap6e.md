# Brighten Up for Summer ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen sends in three weeks, and Sally greets this one with "Brighten Up for Summer" — which, fine, I get it, DIY bleach kits spike in June. But after the "Last Call" that wasn't (May 28) and the survival kit that actually landed pretty well on the 3rd, this one feels like a reset to factory settings. The $15.99 Wella bundle is a real offer, the hero copy is clean, and I appreciate that "no salon required" is at least a clear value prop. The Sally Rewards $5/$50 reminder is the one thing that would make me pause, because yes, I do the math.
- But here's my honest take: if I'm shopping for lightener, I'm at Sally anyway. This email doesn't give me a *reason* to go now instead of whenever I happen to run out. No deadline. No "this week only." No "your rewards expire." Just... shop now. And for the fifteenth time this month, that's not enough.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't forward it. If I happened to be planning a highlight refresh, maybe I click — but this email isn't *creating* that intent, it's just hoping to catch me mid-thought.

## What's working

- **The hero copy earns its place.** "Lighten up for summer, *no salon required*" is punchy. The italicized "no salon required" signals DIY confidence, not desperation — that's a positioning choice that actually flatters the buyer.
- **$15.99 bundle is front and center.** Price-in-hero is exactly what I want. No hunting. Competes well with how Ulta's CT GWP email led with the gift mechanic right in the hero — same instinct, executed here.
- **Bundle component breakdown is useful.** Powder Lightener + Cream Developer + Post Color & Lightening Treatment listed out gives me the full kit picture without making me click to verify what's in the box.
- **Sally Rewards callout.** The $5 reward when you spend $50 is visible mid-scroll. I do that math automatically — it's not a great return rate but it's acknowledged, which is better than pretending loyalty doesn't exist.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum disasters. The pink-and-white palette reads summery without being chaotic.

## What's weak

- **No urgency whatsoever.** Compared to the "Last Call" send on May 28 (which at least pretended to have a deadline), this email has zero time pressure. If the offer is evergreen, it doesn't feel like news.
- **"Shop now" three times in a row.** Each product in the grid gets its own "Shop now" link, and the hero CTA is also "Shop now." After e.l.f. SKIN's ingredient-forward CTAs ("Meet your skingredients") and Glossier's personality-driven copy, this is as generic as it gets.
- **No personalization.** No name, no "based on your past color purchases," no tier acknowledgment. I'm a known Sally customer at this point — they have my data. The Licensed Colorist OnDemand feature literally exists to personalize recommendations, but the email treats me like a cold stranger.
- **Cadence erosion is real.** This is email number fifteen in three weeks. The June 3rd survival kit email actually worked on me — good hook, structured kit logic. Today's is covering the same territory with less specificity. Drunk Elephant had the same problem with "Bestsellers await you!" feeling anonymous after a run of strong sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** Even "through Sunday" creates action. Right now there's nothing to trigger a buy decision today vs. next Tuesday.
- 2. **Surface a loyalty-specific hook in the hero.** "Earn $5 back — buy your summer lightener kit now" uses the rewards mechanic as a reason to act, not just a footnote.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copies.** Hero: "Get the $15.99 Kit" / Grid items: "Add Lightener" / "Add Developer" — verb + noun, specific.
- 4. **Invoke the Licensed Colorist OnDemand feature earlier.** It's buried below the fold. "Not sure which shade to lift to? Chat with a licensed colorist free." That's a genuine differentiator Sephora doesn't offer in the same way — lean into it.
- 5. **Thin the cadence.** Batch this with the next send or hold until there's a flash sale to anchor it. Fifteen emails in three weeks is burning the relationship.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Missing `@font-face` for 'Founders Grotesk'**
The global anchor rule declares:
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` block for Founders Grotesk exists anywhere in the stylesheet — only Open Sans variants are loaded. Founders Grotesk is not a system font. Every `<a>` element will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif in all clients.

**[LOW] `white-space: pre` on `img` elements**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and has no effect on replaced elements, but it is noise that could interact unexpectedly with inline-block image layouts in older Outlook versions. Should be removed.

**[LOW] Stale Google Fonts version**
All six `@font-face` declarations reference Open Sans `v17` (e.g., `opensans/v17/`). Current Google Fonts serves v36+. While functional, stale version pinning means missing hinting and subsetting improvements. Not a breakage risk but worth updating.
