# Your next summer flavor...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Seven. Seven Glossier emails in eleven days — and I've been tracking them all. After the "Something new is coming" tease on July 7, the fragrance reveal, "The wait is almost over," the actual I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom launch on July 14, the redundant "NEW!" re-send the next day, yesterday's merch drop — now here we are on July 17 with "Your next summer flavor..." delivered with an ellipsis like we haven't just survived an entire product launch arc together. The pivot from I ❤️ NY hype to "pick your flavor vibe" is actually smart product thinking. The Balm Dotcom grid laid over summer fruit — strawberries, figs, citrus, mango — looks genuinely appealing. And "Apply liberally. Lick lips accordingly." is exactly the Glossier voice I showed up for.
- But here's the thing: there's no offer. Not a percentage, not a bonus, not a gift-with-purchase. Just products I could have bought last week, this week, or any week. After the emotional build of that launch sequence, landing on a plain discovery email feels like the anti-climax of a runway show that ends with "anyway, here's our website." And the I ❤️ NY merch tee is still floating in the middle of this email like yesterday's newsletter accidentally got copy-pasted in. Confused hierarchy.
- I'd probably scroll through because I'm invested in the arc at this point — but I wouldn't click. This is a mark-as-read, maybe-later situation. No forwarding to anyone.

## What's working

- **"Apply liberally. Lick lips accordingly."** — Classic Glossier copy. Playful, sensory, brand-consistent. That line earns its place.
- **The fruit grid is genuinely summery.** Balm Dotcom tubes against strawberries, figs, citrus, mango — the styling makes the product feel edible and aspirational without trying too hard.
- **Product grid is clean and scannable.** Multiple flavors clearly displayed with individual shop CTAs. No confusion about what's for sale.
- **Warm color palette.** Reds, oranges, creamy whites — this reads as summer without screaming seasonal panic the way Lush's mid-July Halloween email did.
- **Brand voice is coherent.** After six emails I know what to expect, and the tone here doesn't break anything.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** After seven emails and a full launch sequence, there's nothing to reward my attention — no loyalty perk, no bundle deal, no "Rouge"-equivalent member pricing. Sephora would've at minimum dangled a points multiplier here; even e.l.f.'s recent SPF restock email had a concrete price anchor.
- **The I ❤️ NY merch tee in the middle reads as a leftover from yesterday's email.** I already saw that product. It breaks the "your next summer flavor" framing mid-scroll.
- **"Shop now" is as generic as it gets.** Three words that tell me nothing about flavor, feel, or why now. Drunk Elephant's recent CTAs have been product-specific ("Try the Bundle" / "Shop Lala Retro") — this doesn't even try.
- **Subject line is all ellipsis, no payoff.** "Your next summer flavor..." doesn't tell me if this is a new launch, a sale, a restocked shade, or just a playlist of things I already know exist.
- **Seven emails in eleven days is pushing it.** I opened all of them because the arc was compelling, but this one doesn't add momentum — it drifts.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hook.** Even a "free shipping on lip orders today only" or a "Rouge exclusive early access to the next flavor" gives me a reason to click NOW instead of whenever. Discovery emails without offers need a harder editorial angle to justify existing.
- 2. **Cut the I ❤️ NY merch module entirely.** This email is about flavor exploration — let it breathe. That tee had its own email yesterday.
- 3. **Make "Shop now" flavor-specific.** "Find your flavor" or "Pick yours" doubles the specificity for zero cost.
- 4. **Give the subject a hint of what "next" means.** Is this a new launch? A restocked shade? A staff pick? Tease something real.
- 5. **Dial back send frequency.** Two emails in 48 hours on the same product family with no offer is the definition of inbox fatigue.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Balm Dotcom flavors, one for every mood you're in right now.`
