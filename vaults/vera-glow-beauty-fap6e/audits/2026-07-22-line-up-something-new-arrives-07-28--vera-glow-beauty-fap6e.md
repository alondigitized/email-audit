# Line up—something new arrives 07.28
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Eight consecutive ILIA emails, and this one finally breaks the "gift" loop they've been riding since July 18 — so credit for that. The black-and-white lash close-up is genuinely editorial, and "coming thru 07.28" lands with just enough cool-girl mystery that I didn't immediately swipe. The body copy reveals it's a waitlist situation with a sweepstakes attached — win a limited-edition PR mailer plus one of everything ILIA at a $1,000 value — and that's legitimately good bait for someone like me who lives for a beauty haul.
- Here's the rub: I have zero idea what I'm lining up for. Not a category, not a product silhouette, not even a vague "your routine is about to change" tease. Glossier spent two weeks building to their GWP reveal with actual narrative beats — ILIA just pivoted cold from a week of "gift" emails into a mystery drop on day eight. The cumulative send fatigue is real, and the lack of any product signal means I'm clicking for the sweepstakes entry, not because I'm excited about a specific thing.
- I'd click the waitlist — $1,000 of ILIA free is $1,000 of ILIA free — but I wouldn't forward it. There's nothing to share when there's nothing to show.

## What's working

- **The lash close-up earns the open.** Black and white, editorial, no product flatlay chaos — it actually made me stop mid-scroll, which is hard to do on send eight of eight.
- **"Coming thru 07.28" is confident and on-brand.** The casual phrasing fits ILIA's voice; the specific date makes it feel intentional rather than evergreen.
- **The sweepstakes copy is the right incentive for a waitlist ask.** "$1,000 value" next to "win" is the number that makes beauty addicts like me read the fine print, not skip it.
- **"LINE UP →" is a clean, one-job CTA.** No "discover" or "explore" filler — the arrow implies momentum and the copy implies a queue, which is conceptually correct for a waitlist.
- **The layout is tight and bug-free.** Perk icons (Leaping Bunny, recycling, VIP) are organized at the base without fighting the hero. Nothing's broken, nothing's overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Zero product signal.** Not a shadow, not a category word, not a texture hint. Glossier's July 7 tease had more intrigue because they'd been seeding something for days. This just drops a mystery on me with no runway — and after eight consecutive emails, I need more than a closed eye to stay curious.
- **Eight emails in a row is doing real damage to the cadence.** The "gift" campaign ran through July 21; this pivot came with no breathing room. The open rate on this is going to carry the fatigue of every email I almost-deleted this week.
- **The VIP/loyalty row is basically invisible.** I clocked "1VIP — Join ILIA VIP" only because I was looking for it. A loyalty-tier obsessive like me wants to know if being a VIP member gets me early access — burying that in a tiny icon footer below the shipping note is a miss.
- **Free shipping over $50 isn't a hook.** Sephora's 4X points hair care email from this week made loyalty math the entire hero concept. "Free U.S. shipping over $50" in gray footnote text reads like mandatory boilerplate, not a value prop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give me one category breadcrumb in the hero.** Not the product name — just "something for your lashes" or "something for your complexion." Mystery teaser plus a category target is what makes it feel personal rather than blasted.
- 2. **Add early access as a second beat to the waitlist.** "Join the list → be first to shop 07.28 + enter to win" beats "enter to win" alone. Early access is what actually gets beauty people to move.
- 3. **Surface the VIP benefit in the body.** One line above the CTA: "ILIA VIP members shop first." That's the points math I care about and it doesn't cost a design line.
- 4. **Let the July 21 email breathe before sending this.** One day off between the "gift" campaign close and the launch teaser signals a new chapter; same-day pivots read like a content calendar accident.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Join the waitlist + enter to win a $1,000 ILIA PR mailer`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIP members get early access. Everyone can enter to win.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some clients (Samsung Mail, older Android) surface this as the email subject fallback. Set a meaningful value or omit entirely.

**Issue — `lang` attribute missing on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">
```
No `lang="en"` declared. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) cannot select the correct language model; WCAG 3.1.1 failure.

**Issue — Links visually indistinguishable from body text:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
All five link pseudo-states share the same color as body text with no underline. Any inline text links will be invisible to users who rely on visual differentiation. CTA buttons styled via `bgcolor` attributes are unaffected, but this rule applies globally.

**Issue — External `@import` font loads:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web), Apple Mail (privacy relay), and Outlook block external CSS imports. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica`. Not a breakage — fallback stack `"Montserrat","Helvetica",sans-serif` is present — but the import itself fires an external HTTP request that may leak open/render data in privacy-hardened clients. Consider inlining the font-face declarations if Montserrat fidelity matters.
