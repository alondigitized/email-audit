# NEW! Exagger-Eyes Spotted At The Met Gala 👀

## Executive summary

- Okay. *Ten* Charlotte Tilbury emails in eleven days, and email number ten is the first one that doesn't mention 20% off. I genuinely had to sit with that for a second. After nine consecutive discount hammers — including that embarrassing all-zeros countdown clock from yesterday — CT pivots to a Met Gala product launch editorial. I'll be honest: I felt relief before I felt interest.
- The Exagger-Eyes mascara framing is smart. Met Gala as the celebrity proof point, multiple faces across the scroll, Charlotte's own signature anchoring the bottom — the brand voice is finally doing something besides screaming sale math at me. This feels like the kind of email I'd screenshot and send to my friend who's obsessed with dramatic eye looks.
- The catch is the CTA: "Join The Waitlist." I cannot buy this right now. For someone who just mentally budgeted a Charlotte Tilbury haul against her Sephora Insider Cash, waitlist friction is genuinely annoying. I'd click it — I'm curious enough and this is my category — but I'd click it frustrated, not excited.

## What's working

- **The pivot is the win.** After nine 20%-off emails, a no-discount editorial send breaks pattern in the best way. This is what I actually want from a prestige brand.
- **Met Gala tie-in is timely.** Cultural moment as credibility is smarter than another "LAST CHANCE" banner — and it's fresh, not stale.
- **Multiple faces across the scroll.** The range of models (the dark-haired editorial shot at top, Charlotte herself mid-scroll, the blonde icon, the Black model lower down) signals this product is for everyone — that matters to me.
- **Charlotte's handwritten signature at the bottom.** Corny? Maybe. But it's consistent brand voice and I trust it.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs.** After watching that countdown clock hit 00/00/00 yesterday, a render-clean email feels like a gift.

## What's weak

- **"Join The Waitlist" is friction when you're primed to buy.** Nine emails trained me to shop. Now I can't. That whiplash should come with a clearer "here's when it drops" date.
- **No price anywhere.** I'm already doing the math on whether this competes with my e.l.f. lip marker haul or my Glossier restock. Give me a number, even a range.
- **Product detail is thin.** I can see it's an eye product and the name is Exagger-Eyes — that's it. Formula? Waterproof? Lengthening vs. volumizing? ILIA's "Layers of free gifts" email gave me more product reasons to care in half the scroll.
- **No loyalty moment.** After nine emails hammering a 20% sale, there's zero acknowledgment of my member status here. Sephora's sale email — even the heavy-handed triple-send from May 26 — surfaced Rouge-specific benefits. CT just cold-shouldered that entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch date to every waitlist CTA.** "Join the Waitlist — drops June 12" closes the loop. Right now I'm clicking into uncertainty.
- 2. **Add one product claim above the fold.** "Lash-lifting formula worn by X celebrities at the Met Gala" — something I can repeat to a friend. Make the *why it's good* visible, not just the cultural moment.
- 3. **Bridge the member relationship.** A single line — "As a Rouge/Insider member, you'll get early access" — would make this feel earned after nine sale emails.
- 4. **Sequence the price reveal.** Even a "starting at $XX" or "prestige launch pricing" framing gives me something to budget against before I hit the waitlist.
