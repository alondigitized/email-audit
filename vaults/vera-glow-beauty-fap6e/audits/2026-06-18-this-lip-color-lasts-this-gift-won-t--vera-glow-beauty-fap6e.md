# This lip color lasts. This gift won’t.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so let me set the context: this is ILIA email number five (at least) in about nine days. I had back-to-back sends on June 16 — "BFF + SPF" AND "Summer Skin Staples" — on the same day, which already had me side-eyeing the frequency. Now June 18 rolls in with a lip-focused GWP email, and I'm going to try to read it fresh, but that cadence is hanging over my shoulder like a hovering brand associate.
- And honestly? The content itself is solid. "Lip imitates art" is genuinely pretty copy, the model shot is beautiful, the swatch strips for the Mini Lip Sketch Duo let me self-select before I even click, and the hero image has that editorial-meets-clinical ILIA energy I actually like. But the CTA "Build Your Cart" is doing something strange — it sounds like a checkout workflow, not an invitation to shop. And the GWP specifics (what IS the gift?) are murky at first glance. If I'm already fatigued on ILIA and the gift isn't immediately obvious, I'm not urgently clicking.
- I'd mark this one as read without clicking, but I'd file the Lip Sketch Duo info for later. I would not forward — I'm not in a "here, you need this" headspace with a brand that's been in my inbox this aggressively. A friend who doesn't already know ILIA would get a better intro somewhere else in this send sequence.

## What's working

- **"Lip imitates art."** — This headline earns its real estate. Short, clever, elevated. Very ILIA.
- **Swatch strips in the Lip Sketch Duo section** — I can actually see shades before I click. That's the kind of product detail that moves me on a lip email.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — Hero → copy → CTA → editorial section → product grid. My eye moves through it without fighting.
- **No render bugs** — Fonts load, images load, layout holds. After seeing Ulta send that "Beauty Break / this event is over" banner on an active sale email, clean rendering is not something I take for granted.
- **"Latest + Greatest" product grid is useful** — It's not a core conversion driver, but as a Rouge member I appreciate product discovery within the send.

## What's weak

- **"Build Your Cart" is a weird CTA** — It reads like a UX pattern from a subscription configurator, not a beauty brand invite. Charlotte Tilbury runs "Shop Now" everywhere and it converts precisely because it's frictionless. This makes me pause to interpret.
- **The GWP gift details are vague** — "This gift won't" is doing urgency work in the subject line, but I can't clearly tell what the gift IS at a glance in the email body. The offer needs to front-load the prize, not the deadline.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — Fifth email from ILIA since June 9. "BFF + SPF" and "Summer Skin Staples" both hit June 16. I know ILIA's aesthetic now — I've learned it. Repeating "Latest + Greatest" as a section in back-to-back sends starts to feel like a catalog, not curation.
- **"Shop Now" under product tiles** — The bottom grid CTAs are generic. Drunk Elephant does tiered CTAs per product ("Save 20% → [product name]") and it feels intentional. "Shop Now" under six products in a row is wallpaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the gift in the first screen** — The GWP urgency only works if I know what I'm about to lose. A single line — "Free [Gift Name] with any lip purchase. Through [Day]." — placed directly under the hero image would double the urgency payoff of the subject line.
- 2. **Replace "Build Your Cart" with something actionable** — "Get the Look" (mirrors the section header below), "Claim Your Gift," or even "Shop Lip Color" would all outperform. Glossier uses playful specificity in their CTAs and it reads warm, not clinical.
- 3. **Cut "Latest + Greatest" or make it earn it** — Either editorialize the picks (why these six products, why now, what's the connection to the lip story?) or remove the section and let the lip focus breathe. Sally Beauty's "More Beauty, Less Spend" send was overstuffed in the same way and felt like a flyer.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency or differentiate harder** — June 16 double-send was already a lot. June 18 with a fourth distinct email arc needs to feel meaningfully different, not another variation on the same prestige palette.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<html>` missing `lang` attribute** — screen readers cannot determine language. Should be `<html lang="en" ...>`.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title> </title>` renders as a blank tab title in browser-based clients (Gmail, Yahoo). Use the subject line or a descriptive string.

**Google Fonts `@import` will silently fail in most desktop clients** — `@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap)` is blocked by Gmail (strips `<style>` blocks with `@import`), Outlook (ignores web fonts entirely), and Apple Mail on some configurations. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` in `p, h1, h2, h3` is correct. However, `h4` declares `"Helvetica Neue", Arial` without `Montserrat` — inconsistent fallback chain.

**Klaviyo custom font import also subject to blocking** — `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css)` carries the same risk. If custom fonts are non-critical, both imports should be moved to a `<link>` tag wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional, which survives more clients.

**All link states set to identical non-interactive style:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Hover state provides zero affordance. Not a blocker, but hover differentiation (underline or slight color shift) improves usability in webmail clients that support it.

**Image alt text** — not visible in truncated source. Confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes; decorative images should use `alt=""`.
