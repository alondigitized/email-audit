# Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I need to lead with this: I *already reviewed* a "Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase." from Sephora today. Same subject line, same sender, same date — this is the second send. After the double "💘JUST YOUR TYPE💘" yesterday and the duplicate "New products have arrived" on July 13th, Sephora now has a legitimate pattern of accidental re-sends, and my inbox fatigue is real. At this point I'm not asking myself "should I click?" — I'm asking "did their ESP malfunction again?"
- Inside the email itself, there's actually a lot going on — almost too much. The free hair sample set gets a solid hero treatment with warm product imagery and an "Shop To Redeem" CTA, and the 300+ point rewards section below is a genuine Rouge member hook. But Sephora is fighting against themselves by stacking the 10% off badge, the free hair sample hero, a product grid, a "Spot & Shop" module, a "Bundled Beauty" section, a Fragrances for Him block (for HIM — in MY inbox??), and the point rewards section all in one scroll. It's not an email, it's a homepage dump.
- Would I click? Grudgingly, yes — the 10% off with a $100 minimum is math I can do in my sleep, and I'm always three steps away from a restocking run. But I would not forward this to a friend. I'd probably check my Sent folder to confirm I didn't already redeem something from the first send.

## What's working

- **The free hair sample set hero is clean.** Warm orange-to-pink palette, products shown clearly, single "Shop To Redeem" CTA — that section works as a standalone.
- **"New 300+ Point Rewards Are Here!" is a genuine Rouge hook.** As someone who does points math on every transaction, this section earned a pause-and-read.
- **10% off on $100+ is dead simple.** No mystery, no "up to X%" nonsense — the exact percentage and minimum are right there, which is more honest than most Ulta GWP offers that bury the qualifying spend in the fine print.
- **No render bugs.** The email is visually clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Sephora's production quality is consistently solid.
- **The summer/July timing tracks.** Stocking up before August heat damage season is real; the hair care angle makes sense.

## What's weak

- **Duplicate send is a trust problem.** I already opened and evaluated this exact subject line from Sephora today. That's not a creative misstep — that's an operational failure that erodes list credibility.
- **"Fragrances for Him" has no business being in my inbox.** I'm a Rouge member with a documented purchase history that is 100% female beauty. ILIA and Drunk Elephant would never serve me a "For Him" fragrance hero; Sephora should know better.
- **Too many competing offers kill hierarchy.** 10% off badge *plus* free sample set *plus* bundled sets *plus* point rewards — I don't know what Sephora wants me to do first. Glossier's worst impulse is a wall of product links; Sephora just did the same thing.
- **The 10% off asterisks signal exclusions without explaining them.** "10%\*\*" with fine print below creates a hesitation beat that undercuts the "honest offer" feeling — especially after I've been burned by Sephora exclusions on prestige skincare before.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the re-send.** If this is a second blast, that's a process fix, not a creative one — but it's the single highest-impact change available.
- 2. **Pick one hero offer and commit.** Either lead with 10% off and let the free sample be a secondary callout, or lead with the sample set and treat the discount as the footer urgency driver. Not both as equal heroes.
- 3. **Pull the "Fragrances for Him" block** from female-skewing segments. Segment on purchase history; Rouge members have the data trail to support it.
- 4. **Add the specific sample set product names.** Showing the bottles is good; naming the brands in the set (is that Olaplex? Moroccanoil?) would convert browsers into clickers instantly.
- 5. **Tighten the "Shop To Redeem" copy.** It's vague — "Claim Your Free Hair Set" tells me exactly what happens when I tap.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rouge perk: Free hair set + 10% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `10% off $100 today — plus a free hair sample set`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Redeem your free hair sample set before midnight — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your Rouge points and save — offer ends tonight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Pinch-to-zoom disabled (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS Safari. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA) violation. Fix: remove `maximum-scale=1` or raise it to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue 2 — System text-size-adjust suppressed globally (confirmed)**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to nearly every element type. Overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling for users who rely on it. Standard practice is `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` — not `none` across all elements.

**Issue 3 — `<title>` is non-descriptive (confirmed)**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
When recipients open in a browser tab or forward the email, the tab title carries no context. Minor, but best practice is `<title>Sephora | Today only: 10% off $100+</title>`.
