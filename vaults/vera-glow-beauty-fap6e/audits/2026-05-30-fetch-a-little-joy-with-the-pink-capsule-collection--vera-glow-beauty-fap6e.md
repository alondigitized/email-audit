# Fetch a little joy with The Pink Capsule Collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Two days ago I got the "NEW! The Pink Capsule Collection" email, and now I'm getting the follow-up — same collection, different angle. I clocked that immediately. The "Fetch a little joy" subject got a small smile out of me (the pet accessories pun lands), but the smile wore off fast when I opened to find a hoodie, an errand bag, and some dog accessories. I subscribe to Glossier for skincare and makeup. This is merch. That disconnect is the whole story here.
- The email is pretty — pink, cohesive, young-woman energy — but it's selling me lifestyle products I didn't ask for, from a brand I trust for Boy Brow and Cloud Paint. The CTAs are scattered across four product tiles with four different button labels ("Find out how," "Shop us," "Get yours," "Go fetch"), so there's no single pull strong enough to make me click. If I wanted a Glossier hoodie, I would have bought it two days ago. This is a mark-as-read situation. I would not forward this.

## What's working

- The pink-on-pink visual identity is very on-brand and polished — no broken layouts, imagery renders clean
- The golden retriever photo is genuinely cute; the pet accessories angle is a differentiated move I haven't seen Glossier do before
- The "Go fetch" CTA copy is clever — it's the one moment the email sounds like it has a personality
- Female lifestyle models match my age range and aesthetic; it doesn't feel like it was shot for a different customer

## What's weak

- This is the second email about this exact collection in two days — Glossier already burned the launch moment on May 28; today reads like a weak echo
- No beauty product in sight above the fold — I'm a Sephora Rouge because of skincare, not because I'm in the market for embroidered hoodies
- Four CTAs with four different copy labels dilutes any purchase urgency; ILIA's "Coming 06.02" single-product focus is cleaner than this multi-tile scatter
- Zero offer, zero discount, zero loyalty reward — after a week-plus of Memorial Day sale emails, dropping a full-price merch email with no hook feels tone-deaf to where my brain is right now
- The "Errand Bag" section reads like an afterthought — brief copy, vague pitch, weaker photo energy than the hoodie tile

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the beauty angle.** If there are any actual Glossier beauty products tied to this capsule (a Pink Balm.com? a You-scented product? anything), put that above the fold. The merch can live below it.
- 2. **Collapse to one hero product.** Charlotte Tilbury's "Complete Your Exagger-Eyes Look" email from earlier this month earned a 9 because it built the whole email around one specific offer — this email is trying to sell me four different things and selling me none of them.
- 3. **Add an insider perk.** If I'm a Glossier subscriber, make me feel like it — early access, a bundle, a gift-with-purchase for Glossier You. Something that makes this feel curated for me, not a broadcast.
- 4. **Don't send the follow-up two days after launch.** If the May 28 send already existed, this needed at minimum five days of breathing room, or a meaningful new angle (e.g., "Customers are already styling it" + UGC).

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Abnormal open-tracking implementation**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KSV2GBH592WTWWC9B06HX871_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This construct does not load a stylesheet and will not fire an HTTP request in standards-compliant clients. Probable intent: open tracking pixel excluded from Outlook via `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional wrapper. However, WebKit-based clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) may request `href` values on inline elements — behavior is inconsistent across clients. If this is the sole open-tracking mechanism, open rates will be unreliable. A standard `<img width="1" height="1">` pixel is the correct implementation.
