# Spritz. Dry. Frizz goes byeeee 👋
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen-plus days into this e.l.f. Hair streak and this one's actually a keeper — a before/after with real frizz (not a filtered fantasy), a five-step tutorial that reads like an actual routine, and a $9 hero product I can picture myself needing in this August humidity. It's the most useful send this brand has run all month, more instructional than promotional, which is a nice change of pace from the GWP/BOGO merry-go-round I've been logging since "National Lipstick Day."
- The before/after up top is doing real work — that's not an ultra-glam model shot, that's "my hair in the elevator" energy, and the "e.l.f. filter" label is a cute touch that doesn't oversell. Where it loses me is the length: five numbered steps plus a treatment oil upsell plus a "Top Picks" carousel plus a six-item nav grid before I even hit the footer. I'd click Shop Now on the $9 spray, but I'm not scrolling through all of it on my phone during a commute.
- Compared to Sally Beauty's COLORfest launch (7/31, 7/10) which at least anchors a whole campaign moment, or ILIA's "Clean Beauty 101" back-to-school hook (8/10), this is a solid single-product story but it's buried under e.l.f.'s now-standard everything-and-the-kitchen-sink scroll. Would I forward it? To a curly-haired friend who complains about humidity every summer, yes — this is genuinely shareable. Would I read all five steps on my phone? No, I'd tap Shop Now on step one and bail.

## What's working

- The before/after hero is relatable, not aspirational — real frizz, real transformation, no airbrushing pretending
- $9 price point called out immediately under the headline, no hunting for it
- Numbered step-by-step tutorial format actually teaches me how to use the product, which builds trust I'll use it right
- The treatment oil cross-sell (step 5, $10) is a logical routine add-on, not a random tack-on
- Vegan/cruelty-free badge and "e.l.f. is for every eye, lip, and face" footer keeps the brand voice consistent

## What's weak

- Five full-bleed image sections plus three more product tiles plus a six-category nav grid is a lot of scroll for one $9 hero product — Drunk Elephant's "Something big..." teaser (8/9, scored 9) proves less is more
- No loyalty tie-in anywhere — I'm Rouge/Diamond tier elsewhere and this doesn't even nod at e.l.f.'s own rewards program
- Repeated "Shop Now" button five times in a row starts to feel like button fatigue rather than clear hierarchy
- "Top Picks for You" reads like generic merchandising, not actual personalization — no sizing/shade/hair-type signal behind it
- This is now day sixteen-plus of e.l.f. in my inbox; even a good send like this one is starting to blur into the frizz-care, lip gloss, bronzer parade from the last two weeks

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to hero + 2-3 steps max, move the full 5-step tutorial to a landing page — mobile readers won't scroll a wall of numbered instructions
- 2. Add a loyalty hook — "e.l.f. Beauty Squad members earn 2x points on Hair this week" would immediately lift relevance for tier-chasers like me
- 3. Replace "Top Picks for You" with an actual personalization signal (hair type quiz result, past purchase) or cut it — generic carousels don't earn trust
- 4. Consolidate the five "Shop Now" CTAs into two: one after the hero, one at the bottom
- 5. Ease off cadence — after sixteen straight days, even a strong send like this benefits from a day of silence to reset attention
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $9 fix for humidity hair`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 5-step frizz routine, done in 60 seconds`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Anti-Frizz Spray + Gloss Oil = your new humidity kit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shake, section, spray, dry, seal — here's how`
