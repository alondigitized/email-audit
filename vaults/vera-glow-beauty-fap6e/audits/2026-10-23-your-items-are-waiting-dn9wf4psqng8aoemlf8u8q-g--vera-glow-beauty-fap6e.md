# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth fake-cart email from Skechers in about a week, and I still haven't put anything in a cart. "We noticed you noticing us… your cart is still waiting" is the same beat as 10/22 "Don't leave these styles behind!" and "Did you forget something?". It's also the same beat as 10/18 "This is perfect for you!". At this point it reads as a cadence problem, not a personalized nudge.
- The one real thing in here is the 20% off members banner with a $5 reward at $75. It sits way below the fold, under a giant lilac sneaker and a six-shoe grid. I'm an Ulta Diamond and Sephora Rouge, so I do points math all day. A tier-style offer buried under a fake-cart hero feels like a stranger-blast.
- Would I click? No. I'd mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, because there's nothing to show a friend. The one saving grace is that the hero is at least a women's shoe, not the kids' toy-truck sneaker from 10/19.

## What's working

- The hero is a women's GO WALK Workout Walker in a soft lilac. It's clean, big, and finally an adult-women's product.
- "Complete Your Order" is a navy button with a clear verb and noun. It's unambiguous, even if the premise is false.
- The 20% off band is the highest-contrast block in the email, and it has the $5 reward math at $75 right there.
- The six-shoe grid of women's styles, like the pink Zinger Evo and the Topnotch, matches my demographic much better than the kids' sends.
- Afterpay and Klarna logos are a nice trust cue for a $100+ shoe.

## What's weak

- The cart premise is false. I never added anything, so the whole headline feels like a lie.
- The 20% off is buried below two product sections. Ulta would put the member coupon in the top banner and Sephora would lead with the Insider tier perk.
- "Members take 20% off" doesn't say what membership means. I'm not told whether I'm Skechers Plus or just a subscriber, so it doesn't treat me like a known customer.
- The fine print is a long wall of tiny text, with "select regular priced items" and "online only" exclusions I only learn about at the bottom. That hurts honesty.
- There's no price, no ratings and no reviews on any shoe. The grid shows names only, so I can't tell if I'd click for value.
- The nav stack of Women, Men, Kids and Clothing, plus the app, curbside and texting blocks, is a lot of footer clutter. It reads like a template.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% off and $5 reward banner to the top, directly under the logo. Make it the hero, with the cart shoe beneath it.
- 2. Only send cart emails to people who actually have a cart. For everyone else, send a browse-based "back in stock" or "new women's styles" email. I count five near-identical sends this week, so cap it.
- 3. Add prices and star ratings to the grid, plus a sale-price strike-through, so the 20% is visible on a real product.
- 4. Make the member status explicit, e.g. "Skechers Plus: 20% off through 10/26", and say it's auto-applied.
- 5. Shorten the fine print and put the key exclusions (sale items, online only) next to the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off through 10/26 for Skechers Plus`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Women's Slip-ins, 20% off + $5 reward`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Auto-applied at checkout. Earn a $5 reward on $75+ through 10/26.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Slip-ins pick plus five more women's styles, all eligible.`

Several compliance and deliverability checks could not be confirmed because the AgentMail relay did not capture headers.
