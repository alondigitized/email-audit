# Ending soon: 25% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Eight. I've now received eight emails from Drunk Elephant Friends & Family, and I want to open with this: technically, this is one of the better-executed urgency closers DE has sent in the whole run. The countdown timer at the top is real urgency, not manufactured drama, and the spend-threshold gifts — Hydra and Bright Boi free at $100+, a full-size F-Balm at $175+ — are the first genuinely new value-adds I've seen since the campaign launched. The kits at 35% off buried in the lower half? That's actually the freshest angle in the whole email and it's below the fold.
- The context I can't shake: I bought on launch day. The original F&F invite earned a 9 from me. Every send since has been addressing a customer who already converted, with zero behavioral segmentation to show for it. Charlotte Tilbury ran eight emails this same period with the same "20% OFF!!" subject dressed in panic, scored a 5 from me — DE is operating in a completely different tier creatively. But Glossier closed out their Memorial Day campaign in three clean emails. That's the comparison that stings here.
- Would I click? The $175 F-Balm math is doing something to my brain, so probably yes. Forward to a friend? Only if they texted me asking whether F&F was still live — which, after eight emails, they absolutely know it is.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer leading the header** — 00 days, 03 hours reads as an actual deadline, not a generic "hurry!" banner. My brain immediately starts doing the math.
- **Spend tiers are specific and readable** — "$100+ gets you Hydra and Bright Boi, $175+ gets you full-size F-Balm" is the kind of GWP stacking I'd screenshot and send in a group chat. ILIA is running a very similar layered-gifts play right now and it's earning them credit; DE holds the same lane here.
- **Kits at 35% off** — a better deal than the base 25% and the only genuinely new angle in the second half of this campaign. Smart to include it, even if it's too low on the page.
- **Subject line discipline** — "Ending soon: 25% off" at 21 characters is clean, urgency-forward, and has no exclamation-point panic. Compare to Charlotte Tilbury's "20% OFF!! Now Is The Time To Shop! 🛍️✨" and this feels like a different editorial standard entirely.
- **Visual flow is correct** — countdown → hero offer → spend tiers → kits. The hierarchy works and nothing is fighting for attention at the same level.

## What's weak

- **Email 8, same core offer, zero send-time segmentation** — I already bought. A customer who converted on day one is receiving the same blast as someone who's been ignoring this campaign. e.l.f. shows signs of cadence awareness; DE is not segmenting by purchase signal.
- **CTA button copy is garbled** — The pink button is visible but the copy reads as something like "GET YOU DO," which doesn't parse as a call to action. Playful DE voice is a brand asset; broken playful DE voice is a credibility problem.
- **Preheader is doing a different job than the email** — The subscribe-to-save "20% off your first subscription order" callout at the top is a separate conversion goal dropped inside a last-call sale email. It's muddying the urgency moment, not supporting it.
- **No specific product hero** — The offer is sitewide, which is fine, but there's no single product being spotlit as the "get this one thing before it sells out" anchor. Sephora's 50%-off email this week at least routes me toward a curated selection; this drops me into an open catalog.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment out converted buyers** — Anyone who purchased during this F&F window shouldn't be getting the urgency blast. Route them to "you bought — here's what's in your kit, here's what else to stock" instead. This is the single biggest lift available to DE right now.
- 2. **Fix the CTA button copy before it ships** — Whatever that button was supposed to say, it didn't survive render. Audit before send.
- 3. **Lead with the 35% kits angle** — Move it above the spend-tier section or make it the sub-headline under the countdown. "Kits are 35% off" is a new reason to buy for someone who looked at 25% and passed. It shouldn't be buried.
- 4. **Replace the preheader** — The subscribe-to-save pitch belongs in a post-purchase drip or an onboarding sequence, not in the last-call email of a Friends & Family campaign.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-size F-Balm is free at $175+. Clock is running.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bright Boi is free at $100+. Take what you need before midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Dead responsive CSS rules (HTML comments inside CSS block):**
Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules use HTML comment syntax instead of CSS comment syntax:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comment delimiters (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS. These rules will be silently ignored by all CSS parsers, meaning `.contenttable` and `.mobile` elements will not reflow at the 600px breakpoint. This is a confirmed rendering defect on mobile.

**Redundant font loading — 3x requests per font family:**
BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each loaded via three separate mechanisms:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` (head)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` (inline `<style>`)
3. `@font-face` src pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...`

This generates 6–9 redundant HTTP requests per render, adding latency before the email paints. De-duplicate to `@font-face` declarations only.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**
Brown font variants are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. This service is a community font host with no SLA. Downtime or rate-limiting causes silent fallback to system fonts, affecting layout fidelity. BrownRegular is served from `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` — a first-party path that is more reliable.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility defect (violates WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text, Level AA).

**Aggressive global `line-height: 100%`:**
`*{line-height: 100%}` overrides line-height on all elements including buttons and multi-line text, which can compress leading and cause overlapping text in certain clients (notably Samsung Mail and older Android WebView).
