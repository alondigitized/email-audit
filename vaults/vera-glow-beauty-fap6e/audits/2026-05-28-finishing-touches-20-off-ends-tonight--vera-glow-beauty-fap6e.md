# Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I'm going to lead with that because it's the only honest framing for this email. ILIA has been in my inbox every single day since May 16, sometimes twice, and "Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" is number sixteen in what has become the most prolonged Friends & Family campaign I've tracked this season. For reference, Drunk Elephant hit their "Final hours" wall at email thirteen, and by that point their "We extended the sale" email the night before had already burned their credibility. ILIA has somehow pushed three more sends past that.
- Here's the thing though: the email is actually well-made. The "Choose Your Mini" section is the first genuinely new offer hook since "Layers of free gifts" on May 25, and the free mini with no minimum is a concrete, action-worthy incentive that I hadn't seen foregrounded before in this campaign. The "A Cart Collage" section does real loyalty math — full-size bronzer at $33 value, barrier cream at $17, plus VIP stacking — and I actually stopped to calculate my cart total, which means the email earned a moment of my attention. That's not nothing at email sixteen.
- Would I click? Yes, but only because the free mini is a new data point and I happen to have the ILIA site in another tab already. Would I forward it? No. Would I screenshot the "Choose Your Mini" CTA and drop it in my beauty Discord? Absolutely. That's the actual utility this email delivers by this point in the cadence — a very narrow, very specific hook that one or two friends might act on. Not a brand moment. A clearance signal.

## What's working

- **"Choose Your Mini" is the only new thing in sixteen emails, and it lands.** Free mini, no minimum stated clearly — that's a real incentive that creates a decision I haven't made before in this campaign.
- **"A Cart Collage" makes the loyalty math legible.** Showing the full-size bronzer/highlighter at $33 value and barrier cream at $17 alongside the 2x VIP points and tier perks gives me actual numbers to work with. I did the math. That's the email doing its job.
- **Subject line is technically clean.** Thirty-eight characters, no emoji stack, no ALL CAPS — compared to the Sephora triple-send of "ENDS TODAY‼️Up to 50% off select beauty." hitting my inbox three times in one session, ILIA's restraint reads as brand confidence.
- **The VIP upsell is embedded naturally.** "Or 25% if you join our free VIP program" in the body copy rather than shoved in a flashing banner — I read it, I processed it, I wasn't annoyed by it.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds on mobile. Clean execution is table stakes but worth crediting when it's there.

## What's weak

- **"Finishing touches" as a concept is underworked.** The phrase sets up a genuine curation opportunity — show me the actual finishing-touch products: setting spray, mascara, a lip gloss, the things people forget to add. Instead the email uses the metaphor as a headline and then delivers the same generic "shop the sale" body copy as emails nine through fifteen.
- **Sixteen "last chances" have made "ends tonight" unreadable as urgency.** The cadence has trained me to treat every deadline as provisional. I believe "ends tonight" less at email sixteen than I would have at email three.
- **No social proof on the hero products.** Charlotte Tilbury on email eleven still anchored to specific product names with implied bestseller status. ILIA shows me "full-size bronzer or highlighter" without naming which one, how many people bought it, or why it's worth the slot — that's a missed close on a fence-sitter.
- **The VIP loyalty math has three overlapping tracks.** 25% off OR 2x points PLUS +10% off sale PLUS +40% on select items — which stacks? Which is the actual best-case scenario? Drunk Elephant's F&F emails handled this better by anchoring to a final price on a specific product rather than making me do the algebra.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the free mini above the fold.** By email sixteen, anyone who cares about the 20% off has already shopped or will shop anyway. The free mini with no minimum is the new, differentiating offer — it should be the hero, not the third section.
- 2. **Commit to the "finishing touches" concept with real curation.** Pick six SKUs that earn that label — the serum you layer under foundation, the setting product, the mascara — and build the grid around those specifically. Give the theme actual editorial weight instead of using it as a header for a generic sale page link.
- 3. **Collapse the VIP math into one number.** Choose the highest-value scenario for a VIP customer on one real product and show a before/after price. Let that one number do the work instead of three stacked percentages.
- 4. **Add one social proof signal to the product grid.** A units-sold count or a star rating on the bronzer or the barrier cream would give a first-time buyer something to latch onto. The Sephora sale emails always surface a "fan favorite" label — that framing does work.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your finishing touch: free mini + 20% off, ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No minimum needed. Choose your free mini before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIPs get 25%. Everyone gets a free mini. Offer ends tonight.`
