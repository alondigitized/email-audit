# Summer to go
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Drunk Elephant drops "Summer to go" in my inbox two days after the "Oh, my stars" send, which if I'm counting (I am) puts us at six DE emails in about ten days. And look, I opened "Oh, my stars" on brand loyalty alone, and I'm doing the same here — but the subject is working against itself. "Summer to go" is a pun, I get it, but it reads like a Chipotle order-ahead notification, not a skincare drop. Nothing in those three words tells me there's a curated five-product kit at a meaningful savings, which is genuinely the thing I care most about.
- Once you're inside the email, it lands better. The "Summer Better Than Others" hero is confidently DE — the flat-lay of the kit looks aspirational and summery, the five-product callout grid (D-BronzI, Lippe Balm, Beste No. 9, Lala Retro, B-Hydra) is a solid hit-parade of products I already have opinions on, and the "35% LESS IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING" angle speaks directly to my inner spreadsheet. I always do the kit math. I will absolutely calculate whether the kit beats my next Sephora Beauty Insider event pricing, and at least DE gives me the number to work with.
- The "MAKING IT RAIN" section at the bottom loses me though — I genuinely cannot tell what it's selling. And "GLOW FORTH" as a CTA is a vibe, not a destination. I'd click, but I'd click *despite* the email, not because of it. Mark as read but click through.

## What's working

- The five-product callout grid ("Glow with D-BronzI," "Plump with Lippe Balm," etc.) is doing real lifting — it tells me exactly what's in the kit and what each product *does*, which matters for my decision.
- "35% LESS IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING" is smart framing for someone who does the math; positioning it as savings vs. buying individually plays to my points-math instincts better than a flat badge.
- Visual hierarchy in the hero is clean — the summer kit reads immediately, the bold headline doesn't fight it, and the CTA button lands before the scroll.
- The seasonal timing is earned — summer travel/take-with-you framing in late June feels intentional, not recycled.
- The color palette and playful packaging feel cohesive and on-brand for the DE customer (including me).

## What's weak

- "GLOW FORTH" is not a CTA, it's a mood board caption. Glossier's "Final Hours" email from June 19 managed to feel excited *and* specific — DE could take notes.
- The "MAKING IT RAIN" section is a mystery product reveal with no setup. I can see a lavender pouch but I can't connect it to an offer, a name, or a reason to care.
- Cadence fatigue is real. Six emails in ten days from one brand is Charlotte Tilbury territory, and CT at least varied the offer type week to week. The DE drum is starting to blur.
- No loyalty hook anywhere — the Ulta MUGLER email at least gestured at member benefits; DE is treating me like a stranger after six consecutive emails.
- The "35% less toxins... or 35% off select kits" copy construction is slippery — I had to read it twice to understand whether the savings was on the kit or on individual products. Don't make me work for it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the kit and the savings number.** "Summer to go" throws away the best hook you have. A five-product curated kit at 35% savings is the whole email — put it in the subject.
- 2. Replace "GLOW FORTH" with "Shop the Summer Kit" — verb plus a specific noun, lands the destination.
- 3. Either give "MAKING IT RAIN" a product name and price up front, or cut it. Mystery drops work for collabs with hype buildup, not a pastel pouch below the fold.
- 4. Add a deadline to the 35% offer. Even "through June 30" converts savings math into urgency. Without one, I'll think "I'll check at my next Beauty Insider event" and close the tab.
- 5. A single member-acknowledgment line — "Rouge members, earn points on this kit" — would make me feel recognized after six emails.
- **Subject Alt A:** `This 5-product summer kit saves you 35%`
- **Subject Alt B:** `D-BronzI, Lala Retro, Beste No. 9 — in one bag`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A curated kit of your warm-weather favorites — at 35% off the individual price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer routine, ready to go — kit savings end soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — broken mobile CSS rules (HTML comments inside `<style>`):**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!--` and `-->` are HTML comment delimiters, not CSS syntax. Every compliant CSS parser discards these two rules entirely. Elements with `class="contenttable"` and `class="mobile"` will not receive their responsive widths on screens ≤600px, likely breaking the mobile layout.

**Accessibility — pinch-zoom disabled:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove the attribute or set `maximum-scale=5`.

**Accessibility — global text-size-adjust suppression:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

Applied to every element via the `*` selector. This blocks iOS's automatic text legibility adjustments across the entire email, not just the intended targets.

**Triple font loading — same weights loaded three ways:**

- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold">` (HTTP request at parse time)
- `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold)` inside `<style>` (second request, render-blocking)
- `@font-face { font-family: "BrownBold"; src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` (third declaration)

Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are all loaded this way. BrownRegular is the exception — hosted on `drunkelephant.com` via `@font-face` only (correct approach). The three duplicated weights generate 6 unnecessary HTTP round-trips per render.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**

Three of four font weights depend exclusively on `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. If that CDN is unavailable, Bold, Light, and Thin fall back to the system stack. Regular is self-hosted and unaffected.
