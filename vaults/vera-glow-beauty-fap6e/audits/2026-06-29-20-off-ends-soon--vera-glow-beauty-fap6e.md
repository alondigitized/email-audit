# 20% off ends soon!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay. I opened "20% off ends soon!" expecting a general sitewide sale — the kind of thing I'd quickly math against my Sephora Beauty Insider points before deciding whether to pull the trigger. What I got instead was a SheerID professional verification program. Teachers. First responders. Healthcare workers. Military. Students. That is genuinely a lovely thing for Drunk Elephant to do. It is also completely useless to me.
- The bait-and-switch between subject line and body is the entire problem here. "20% off ends soon!" reads as a countdown clock on a sale I could join. The actual email is a gated benefit I can't access unless I happen to work in one of five specific professions — which this email assumes without segmenting. If DE has my job title or knows I'm not a student, why is this landing in my inbox at all? I'm watching my friend get a gift at a party I wasn't invited to.
- The creative itself is fine — the model is on-brand, the layout is clean, the five category buttons are smartly designed. But I'm marking this as read and moving on. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it (unless I had a nurse friend who's into DE, which, honestly, I might text separately), and I'm now one step closer to hitting the unsubscribe button after five sends in barely two weeks.

## What's working

- **Brand aesthetic is still clean and consistent** — the minimalist white palette, the "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." header, and the model touching her skin are all very DE. No render chaos.
- **Offer amount is front and center** — 20% off is visible in the hero subtext and in the subject, so there's no confusion about the size of the discount itself.
- **The five category CTA buttons are smart UX** — breaking teacher/student/military/healthcare/first responder into individual tap targets instead of one generic "verify here" link shows some care for the experience once you're past the gatekeeping problem.
- **The upgrade from 15% to 20% is a genuinely good hook** — "up from 15% for a limited time only" is an incremental urgency signal that would land hard on someone who qualifies.
- **Three-step flow is clear** — the SheerID process is explained without jargon, which reduces anxiety about the verification ask.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a flat-out misdirect** — "20% off ends soon!" implies a general sale, and sending this to non-qualifying subscribers wastes their open and burns trust. Glossier got roasted in my inbox for less obvious bait-and-switches and they at least gave me the discount.
- **Zero segmentation signal** — if DE knows I'm not a teacher, first responder, or student, this email should never have hit my inbox. Sephora would have filtered this. The fact that it didn't tells me I'm in a blast list.
- **No product hook anywhere** — there's not a single DE SKU in this email. After five sends featuring Protini, T.L.C. Framboos, and their travel kit, pivoting to pure-process content with zero product mention feels jarring and hollow.
- **Cadence is making everything worse** — this is email number five-plus in about two weeks from DE. The send that burned me most was the "Summer to go" on June 21 — that was the one that made me feel like I was actually valued. This one makes me feel like I'm on a list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this send for non-eligible subscribers.** This is the single biggest lift. If DE has any behavioral or profile data (or even just doesn't know profession → send everyone), they should add a SheerID pre-check step so only people likely to qualify receive it. Sending a gated discount to the full list is just brand erosion.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to reflect what the email actually is.** The current subject sets up a bait-and-switch. If you must send broadly, the subject needs to tell the story.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Do you qualify for 20% off every month?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off for teachers, nurses & first responders`
- 3. **Lead the body with who qualifies**, not the percentage. The current layout buries the professional gate under a generic-feeling hero. Lead with the five professions.
- 4. **Add one product image.** Even a small grid of the hero SKUs they'd be 20% off would remind me why the discount matters. e.l.f. Cosmetics wired product images into every tier of their skin tint send and I clicked within ten seconds — DE could take notes.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Teachers, nurses, first responders & students: here's your exclusive 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Qualify in 3 steps. Keep saving every month — up from 15% off, limited time`
- 5. **Introduce a gift-a-discount mechanic** for non-qualifying subscribers. If I can't use this, let me forward a verified link to someone who can — that extends reach and turns the send into a goodwill moment for me instead of a dead end.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax breaks mobile CSS rules**
Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment delimiters:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` is invalid inside a `<style>` tag. CSS parsers discard these lines entirely, so `.contenttable` and `.mobile` width overrides never fire on narrow viewports. This is a live layout defect on mobile.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font loading — redundant HTTP requests**
Brown Bold, Light, and Thin are loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` in `<style>`
3. `@font-face` src blocks pointing to the same CDN

The `<link>` and `@import` are redundant for the same URL and add unnecessary round-trips. Most ESP environments and email clients (Gmail, Outlook) will block the external requests regardless.

**[MEDIUM] Third-party font CDN dependency**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a font aggregator. `BrownRegular` is served from first-party Demandware CDN (`drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/`). The CDN split is inconsistent and `db.onlinewebfonts.com` has no SLA guarantee. If the CDN is unavailable or rate-limits the request, all three weight variants fall back to system fonts, while Regular does not.

**[LOW] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Setting this to `none` (vs. `100%`) on the `*` selector suppresses iOS's automatic text scaling. This is an accessibility concern — users with low vision who rely on browser/OS zoom will have text scaling actively disabled.

**[LOW] Apple Mail link affordance removed**
```css
.appleLinks a { color: #000000 !important; text-decoration: none !important; }
```
Any link wrapped in `.appleLinks` renders identically to surrounding body text in Apple Mail — no color, no underline. If CTA buttons use this class, they will be visually indistinguishable from non-interactive text on macOS/iOS Mail.
