# Shop NEW! Unreal Highlighter On The App! 🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury is back — again. This is at least send number six since June 11 based on my inbox, and I say that upfront because cadence fatigue is real context for how I'm reading this one. The "exclusive early access on the app" hook is clever in theory: a new highlighter drop, early access framing, prestige positioning. But the moment I clock that "SHOP ON THE APP" is the primary CTA, I feel the hand reaching for my data rather than a genuine gift. This isn't "you're special, shop early" — this is "download our app, we need installs."
- The actual product story is interesting. "92% Skincare Ingredients. 100% UNREAL Glow" lands well, and the visual of glowing skin on the model is summertime-perfect for mid-June. But then the email just keeps going: dewy glow section, blurring effect section, free mini gifts section, app discovery banner. By the fourth beat I've forgotten what I was supposed to want. Compare this to how Glossier ran their sale (which I audited earlier this week scoring 9/10) — one offer, one scroll, done. Charlotte Tilbury is doing the opposite.
- I'd probably open this because the Unreal Highlighter is genuinely new and I track CT launches. But I'm not clicking through to download an app mid-commute just to shop a highlighter I could find on the site anyway. Mark as read, check the website later, maybe never come back to it.

## What's working

- **Product story is solid** — "92% Skincare Ingredients. 100% UNREAL Glow" is a real differentiator and speaks directly to my preference for hybrid skincare-makeup hybrids.
- **Visual is on-brand and seasonally right** — the glowing skin model fits summer 2026; the warm, dewy aesthetic matches CT's house aesthetic without feeling recycled.
- **The free minis section has genuine appeal** — "Unlock 2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts" is the kind of get-your-gifts hook I actually respond to and would look for at checkout.
- **Subject line names the product specifically** — "Unreal Highlighter" in the subject tells me exactly what the drop is; that's better than the vague "This Content Is TOO HOT To Load" game they played June 11.

## What's weak

- **The app gate kills the moment** — "SHOP ON THE APP" as the primary CTA immediately breaks the fantasy. This is a product launch email that turns into an app-install campaign. Those are different emails with different jobs.
- **Way too many modules competing** — hero / summer glow block / blurring effect block / free gift block / app discovery banner is five distinct stories in one scroll. Even ILIA managed to stretch a four-day campaign arc without cramming it all into one send.
- **No price anchor or deal** — there's no % off, no exclusive pricing, no early access discount. "Early access" without a tangible benefit is just inconvenience dressed up as privilege.
- **Cadence problem is real** — after the June 14 "Get Your Summer Glow To Go" send and the June 13 kitchen-sink email before that, this feels like the fourth slightly-overlapping concept in a row. The summer-glow aesthetic is getting repetitive.
- **CTA hierarchy is muddy** — I count at least two "SHOP ON THE APP" buttons and a separate "GET YOUR GIFTS" and "DISCOVER MORE" button. Which action do they actually want me to take?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate the app-launch email from the product-launch email.** If the goal is app installs, make that the whole email with a strong install incentive (10% off first app order, exclusive shade, etc.). If the goal is introducing Unreal Highlighter, let it live on the site too and lead with the product story.
- 2. **Cut to three modules max.** Hero with product story → free gift offer → single CTA. Everything else is noise.
- 3. **Add a concrete offer** — "Exclusive early access + 15% off your first Unreal order via the app" would make the app gate feel like a trade I'm willing to make, not a barrier.
- 4. **Add even one social proof signal** — a rating, a quote, a "wore this to X" moment. Drunk Elephant consistently does this and it's what makes me trust a new launch enough to buy day one.
- 5. **One CTA button, max.** Pick: shop the launch or claim the free gift. Not both, not three.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Drop: Unreal Highlighter is here — app-exclusive early access`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your early access to Unreal Highlighter starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `92% skincare ingredients. Grab yours before it goes wide.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus unlock 2 free glowing skin minis with your first order.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External font stylesheet via `<link>` tag (confirmed issue):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
This is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` to skip Outlook, but `<link>` stylesheets are stripped or ignored by Gmail (webmail and Android app), Yahoo Mail, and Samsung Mail. The custom typeface will silently fall back in all of those clients with no error — undetectable to the sender unless tested. Fonts should be inlined via `@font-face` inside a `<style>` block, or the fallback stack must be acceptable as the primary rendering.

**Link inventory and UTM coverage:** Cannot fully assess — source is truncated before CTA `<a>` tags appear.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) surface the `<title>` as a window/tab label or preview. An empty string results in a blank or client-default label. Should be populated to match the subject line or a descriptive internal label.

**Link underline suppression:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This blanket rule removes underline decoration from all links in the message body. Combined with `color:inherit`, links become visually indistinguishable from body text in any client that applies this selector, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.1 — use of color alone to convey information). A minimum fix is to remove `text-decoration:none` from this rule and scope text-decoration overrides only to styled CTA buttons.

**Preheader implementation:** Correctly hidden via `display:none !important; visibility:hidden; mso-hide:all` — no issue.

**MSO/VML namespaces and `<o:PixelsPerInch>` / `<o:AllowPNG>` declarations:** Present and correctly structured for Outlook compatibility — no issue.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present — no issue.
