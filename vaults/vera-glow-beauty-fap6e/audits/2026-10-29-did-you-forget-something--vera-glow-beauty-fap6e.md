# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the ninth fake-cart email from Skechers in about two weeks, and I still haven't put a single thing in a cart. "Ready to check out? Complete your order" in gold foil letters is a cute Halloween-gold hero, but it has nothing to do with me. After "Your cart is one click away!" on 10/28 and "Your cart expires soon!" on 10/26, I'm numb to it.
- The one useful thing is buried under six shoes and a black bar: 20% off for Skechers Plus members, valid through 11/2. That's the real reason to open this, and it's in the bottom third. Ulta would have put that percentage in the hero. I'd skim it, maybe tap the 20% banner, and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The 20% off Skechers Plus banner is big and white on navy, so the number is easy to find once I scroll to it.
- "Valid thru 11/2" gives me a real deadline I can plan around.
- The hero shoe (GO WALK Workout Walker) has a clean product photo and a name I can search.
- The six-shoe grid with women's styles first is the most useful part. It has slip-ins and the Aero running shoes, and at least the product names are readable.
- The footer has Afterpay and Klarna, so I can see payment options before I click.

## What's weak

- Nothing is in my cart, and the hero asks me to "complete your order." That's the same fake-abandonment beat as the 10/22, 10/23, 10/26 and 10/28 sends.
- The two "Complete your order" buttons (black and orange) promise a checkout, but they probably just go to a product page. I'd feel tricked.
- The 20% member deal is below the fold. Sephora's Beauty Insider emails open with the points or event math, and this one makes me hunt for it.
- The product grid has no prices, no reviews and no sale tags, so there's nothing to do math on. I can't tell if any of these shoes is actually discounted.
- The hero shoe is a men's-leaning olive sneaker. The women's grid is small, and the text under each shoe is tiny on my phone.
- The fine print is a dense block. It says "select regular priced items," which narrows the offer after it looked broad.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending cart-abandonment copy to people who haven't abandoned a cart. Lead with the 20% Plus member offer in the hero and use the shoes as support.
- 2. Make one hero and one CTA that match: "Shop 20% off" instead of two "Complete your order" buttons.
- 3. Add prices, sale marks and star ratings under each shoe so there's something concrete to react to.
- 4. Say what Plus is and whether I'm in it. Show me "You're a Plus member" or "Join free."
- 5. Cap the cadence. Nine near-identical sends in two weeks reads like a stalker, not a brand.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off for Plus members thru 11/2`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Slip-ins you looked at, now 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus members save 20% online only. Ends 11/2.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Best-selling slip-ins and Aero runners, all in one spot.`
