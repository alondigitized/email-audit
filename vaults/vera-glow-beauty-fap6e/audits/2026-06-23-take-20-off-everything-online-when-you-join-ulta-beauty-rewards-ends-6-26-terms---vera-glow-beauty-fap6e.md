# Take 20% OFF everything online⁺⁺ when you join Ulta Beauty Rewards® (ends 6.26) Terms & excl. apply
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- The second I clocked "when you join Ulta Beauty Rewards" I had to put my phone down for a second. I'm Diamond. I have been Diamond. I have done math I'm not proud of to stay Diamond. And Ulta is in my inbox trying to recruit me like I'm a skincare tourist who stumbled into the loyalty program for the first time. That's the whole problem with this email in four words: wrong audience, right offer.
- Setting that aside — because the design itself is actually doing a lot right — "summer deal days" with 20% off everything online is a genuinely good hook. The hero is clean, the seasonal framing is fresh, the urgency is real (ends 6.26, which is today as I'm reading this, so: aggressive). The product grid is dense but I can see prices clearly, which means I can actually do the math. That part I respect.
- But the "join" framing never goes away. It's the premise of the entire email. So I'm sitting here as a Diamond member watching Ulta pitch me their own program I've been in for years. Mark as read. Would not click, would not forward — though I might screenshot the 20% off and ask a friend who isn't a member to sign up so she can grab something for me.

## What's working

- **Hero is clear and above the fold.** "Summer deal days / 20% off" lands immediately — no scrolling required to get the offer, which is more than I can say for past Ulta sends.
- **Seasonal framing is fresh.** "Summer deal days" actually feels current — a real departure from the "Beauty Break / this event is over" zombie banner that haunted their last few emails (looking at you, June 17 MUGLER sends).
- **Price-before/after math is visible.** Product grid shows pricing clearly — as someone who always does the points math, I appreciate that I don't have to dig.
- **Time-bound urgency is credible.** "Ends 6.26" with a specific date reads as real, not fake FOMO. Glossier's "Final Hours" send on June 20 did the same thing well — this matches that energy.
- **Product breadth is there.** Makeup, skincare, hair — I can see range across categories, which tells me the 20% is genuinely sitewide.

## What's weak

- **"When you join" is the whole premise — and I'm already a member.** This is a new-member acquisition email blasted to Diamond tier. It's like Sephora sending me a "discover Rouge" email. The segmentation failure is fatal to relevance.
- **Subject line is 90+ characters.** On mobile, it truncates into gibberish before the urgency even registers. Charlotte Tilbury's "Early Access: Up To 45% Off Beauty Kits In The App! 🤩💫" from June 15 was long too, but the payoff was *front-loaded*. This one buries "ends 6.26" behind a wall of copy.
- **The "⁺⁺ Terms & excl. apply" in the subject is a trust tax.** It primes me to assume the 20% has a dozen carve-outs before I even open. Fine print in the subject line reads as legal CYA, not customer-first communication.
- **No personalization signal anywhere.** Drunk Elephant's "Summer to go" sent two days after "Oh, my stars" at least felt like a brand who knew I was a regular. Ulta is talking at me like I'm a stranger.
- **"100+ free gifts" section at the bottom:** I can see it but it feels like a buried afterthought — if GWP is the hook, it should be higher.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this email to non-members only.** The single highest-impact change. Diamond and Platinum members should never see "when you join" — they should see "your 20% starts now." Two versions of the same email, totally different subject lines, radically different performance.
- 2. **Front-load the discount, cut the legal copy from the subject.** Move "ends 6.26" to the front. Save "⁺⁺ Terms & excl. apply" for the body.
- 3. **Give Diamond/Platinum tier a loyalty callout in the hero.** Even a single "Diamond member exclusive" badge lifts relevance signal and rewards loyalty instead of punishing it.
- 4. **Elevate the GWP section.** "100+ free gifts" is buried. If that's a real differentiator vs. Sephora, it belongs in the hero or at minimum above the product grid.
- 5. **Add one hero product.** The grid is great for browsing but ILIA's "Skincare Mode: Summer" and e.l.f. Hair's launch email both showed that *one anchoring product* gives the eye somewhere to land before the scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off sitewide — today only, Diamond ✨`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer deal days: 20% off ends tonight (6.26)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop 100+ free gift options + everything ships at a discount. Ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 20% off everything online — plus 100+ GWP choices before midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Global Gmail link reset will break CTA styling**
```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
  font-size: inherit !important;
  ...
}
```
This selector targets Gmail's message wrapper and strips color and underline from every `<a>` tag in the email. Any CTA `<a>` without an inline `style="color: ...; text-decoration: ..."` attribute will render as unstyled body text. This is a known Gmail gotcha but the rule is present and must be countered with per-element inline styles on all CTAs.

**[MEDIUM] Google Fonts `<link>` tag will silently fail in most clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:...">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and most corporate webmail strip external `<link>` tags. `DM Sans` will silently fall back to Helvetica/Arial. Not a breakage, but font rendering across clients will be inconsistent unless a fallback stack is explicitly defined.

**[MEDIUM] Dead commented-out code inflates payload**
A large CSS block (~50+ lines) for a hamburger menu is commented out with the note `/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu ... */`. This dead code adds unnecessary payload weight. Gmail clips emails over 102KB; bloat from dead code counts against that limit.

**[LOW] Deprecated `@-ms-viewport` rule present**
```css
@-ms-viewport { width: device-width; }
```
No modern email client uses this. Safe to remove.

**[LOW] Subject line uses Unicode superscript characters**
`⁺⁺` (U+207A) in the subject: `"Take 20% OFF everything online⁺⁺"`. These render correctly in most clients but should be regression-tested in Outlook 2019/2021, which occasionally substitutes or drops uncommon Unicode code points.

**[LOW] `<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Ulta Beauty </title>
```
Minor QA signal — indicates the title wasn't deliberately set for this send.

**[INFO] `translate="no"` on `<html>`**
Prevents Gmail/Chrome auto-translation. Likely intentional for brand-name fidelity, but it blocks translation for non-English-speaking recipients.

**[INFO] Dark mode meta tags present**
