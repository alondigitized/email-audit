# The wait is almost over…
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so after the "Something new is coming" tease on July 7 that earned my trust back after the June "ends tonight (just kidding, again)" sale circus, and then the fragrance reveal on the 12th — this is now act three: the Balm Dotcom drop. And visually? The NYC skyline hero with the red tube and the apple is doing exactly what it needs to do. It tells the whole story in one image before I read a single word. I'm charmed.
- But here's my actual inbox experience: "The wait is almost over…" means nothing if you weren't tracking the July 7 tease. Drunk Elephant's "Damn, your skin!" names *you*. Lush's "Spotlight on: Fresh" names the *ingredient*. This subject names nothing — it's riding the coattails of a narrative that a lot of subscribers didn't follow. The preheader ("Trust us, you don't want to miss this one.") rescues it somewhat, but that's a close call.
- The truncated body copy — "newest, most-edition balm" — is the one thing that stopped my scroll mid-email. For a brand that sells effortless cool-girl precision, a visibly broken sentence is a trust nick. I'd absolutely hit "Sign up" because I am, objectively, a Balm Dotcom person and limited NYC editions are my kryptonite. But I'm not forwarding this to anyone who isn't already in the Glossier ecosystem — it doesn't recruit.

## What's working

- **The NYC visual concept earns every pixel** — skyline + Balm Dotcom tube overlaid with an apple makes "Take a bite out of the Big Apple" land before you've processed what you're reading. No over-explanation needed.
- **"COMING TOMORROW" is the right urgency level** — concrete, not hysterical, and a complete contrast to the "ends tonight / psych" energy from June.
- **"Sign up to be first" is a smart pre-launch mechanic** — gives me a low-stakes action that still signals I care, before I've committed money.
- **"Apple-flavored" is a satisfying product detail** — one adjective that ties the creative concept to an actual formulation decision. More brands should do this.
- **Brand voice holds end-to-end** — the spare Glossier wordmark, the cheeky "Trust us" opener, the two emoji icons at the footer sign-off. This reads unmistakably Glossier.

## What's weak

- **"The wait is almost over…" is context-dependent to the point of vagueness** — if I hadn't opened the July 7 tease, I'd have no frame for "the wait." DE's "Damn, your skin!" and Sephora's blunt "New products have arrived" both outperform this on cold-open clarity.
- **The body copy truncation is visible and distracting** — "newest, most-edition balm" is clearly missing a word and reads like a broken merge token or render fail, even if it isn't one.
- **No price anchor anywhere** — I'm doing the "is this worth it" math in my head with zero information. Even "same iconic price" would close the loop.
- **No tint vs. clear callout** — Balm Dotcom loyalists care about this deeply. One word under the product name would do it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the truncated copy before this send goes out again** — "most-edition" is the first thing a careful reader catches and the last thing Glossier wants associated with a limited drop.
- 2. **Put the product name in the subject** — the July 7 tease earned its vagueness by being first; this one is third in sequence and needs to deliver, not tease.
- 3. **Add a price or value signal under the CTA** — even "Launching at the classic $14 price point" closes the loop for the math-doers in the audience (hi, it's me).
- 4. **One-word tint callout under the product name** — "Clear" or "Tinted" or "Sheer" — pick one, put it in a small cap line under "I ♥ NY Balm Dotcom."

## Technical Audit — Glossier "The wait is almost over…"

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo (sending domain `eml.glossier.com`, click tracking via `ctrk.klclick.com`) using what appears to be a Stripo-compatible template with full Outlook MSO conditional block coverage. The HTML is structurally sound but contains one non-standard open-tracking implementation and several rendering concerns worth flagging.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard open-tracking implementation.**
The open pixel is injected as:
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXERY89H2C3W2NKBMNFWXGJF_0"></span>
```
`rel="stylesheet"` is not a valid attribute on `<span>` — browsers and email clients ignore it. This request will not fire in most email clients; it appears to be an attempt to load a "stylesheet" URL to trigger a server hit, but `<span>` elements do not fetch `href`. If this is intended as the open-tracking mechanism, it will under-report opens. Standard implementation uses an `<img>` with `height="1" width="1"` and `alt=""`. Klaviyo's own pixel SDK typically injects a `<img>` tag — this `<span>` construction is anomalous and likely a template error.

**Cannot audit destination URLs** — the HTML is truncated before body CTAs are visible. Verify all `ctrk.klclick.com` redirects resolve to live pages and carry UTM parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hover-based rollover images will fail silently in most clients.**
The template includes:
```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { max-height:0 !important; display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { max-height:none !important; display:inline-block !important }
```
CSS `:hover` pseudo-class is supported only in Apple Mail, Outlook.com (webmail), and a handful of others. In Gmail (Android/iOS), Outlook desktop, and Samsung Mail, only the `.rollover-first` image will ever display. If the rollover-second image carries the primary CTA or product shot, those recipients never see it. Confirm the first-state image is always the complete/meaningful content.

**Preheader padding technique is correct** — uses U+034F (combining grapheme joiner `͏`) and U+00AD (soft hyphen `­`) to prevent inbox clients from pulling body text into the preview. No issue, but worth noting for audit trail.

**Alt text on images** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt=""` attributes; images-off rendering in Outlook is common.

**`lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"`** are correctly set on the `<html>` element.

**MSO conditionals** — present and complete (`mso 16`, `gte mso 9`, `[if mso]` for VML background, Word typography suppression). Outlook rendering baseline is covered.
