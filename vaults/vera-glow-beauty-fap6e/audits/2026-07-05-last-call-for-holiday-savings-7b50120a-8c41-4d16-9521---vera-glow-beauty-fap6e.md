# Last Call for Holiday Savings
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. That's where I am with Sally, and I want to be clear: yesterday's email — the "Celebrate with 25% off sitewide" Fourth of July send — that one was actually good. The hero creative earned its moment, the code AMERICA25 was front and center, and I genuinely added a barrel brush to my cart. I gave it a 10. Then I woke up today and Sally sent me the same email with a different subject line. The code is still AMERICA25. The discount is still 25% sitewide. The hero is still that rose gold styling brush. The only creative delta is the headline changed from "Celebrate" to "Last Day to Save." That's not a last-call email. That's a find-replace.
- The email renders clean — I'll give it that. Red holiday background, code above the fold, "Shop now" button, 2-hour delivery badge. Visual hierarchy is not the problem. The problem is the product grid below the fold is doing Sally's reputation no favors: Salon Care Honey Almond Shampoo and Beauty Secrets Drip & Clog Proof Hair Glue. I am a Sephora Rouge member. I spend four figures a year at Ulta. I am not the target for a $6 shampoo and hair glue. ILIA ran a five-day campaign this week where every single day had a different creative hook — day two dice, day four lip-shade breakdown, day five "Ends Tonight" with actual product specificity. Sally is running the same creative for the third day and calling it a sequence.
- I am marking this read. I already shopped the code or I didn't. Either way, this email has no new information for me.

## What's working

- AMERICA25 is big, visible, and above the fold — zero hunting required
- Hero layout is clean: headline → discount amount → CTA, no competing elements
- 2-hour delivery surfaces in both the hero body copy and the fulfillment strip below — consistent reinforcement
- Klarna callout is legitimately useful for a higher-ticket tool purchase; the $35 threshold is low but the callout itself adds trust
- The rose gold styling brush image is aspirational and on-brand for a Millennial female shopper — right product for the hero slot

## What's weak

- AMERICA25 is the same code from yesterday's email — "Last Call" urgency dies the moment I check my inbox and see a near-identical send from 24 hours ago
- Product grid is generic mass-tier: Salon Care and Beauty Secrets are professional-supply staples, not the ion or Wella colorist picks that would actually move a prestige cross-shopper
- Zero loyalty callout — Sephora Stacking Weekend emails always surface my Rouge status; Sally treats me like a stranger
- "Shop now" appears four times in the product grid with no destination differentiation — ILIA's grid linked directly to product PDPs with shade names; this just dumps me to a category browse
- No social proof anywhere: no "bestseller" badge, no review count, nothing to create urgency beyond the redundant deadline copy

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give this email something yesterday's didn't have.** "Last Call" should mean: here are the three items that actually sold out or are running low. Surface real scarcity signals — "only 12 left of the ion 10-vol developer," something actionable. Right now the creative is copy-pasted with a clock tacked on.
- 2. **Replace the generic grid with role-based product slots.** If Sally's data shows I'm an ion buyer, lead with ion. If I'm a tool buyer (which the hero implies), link directly to the rose gold brush with shade/size info. Drunk Elephant's Fourth of July email at least kept the product story consistent from hero to grid.
- 3. **Give each CTA copy a job.** "Shop Hair Color," "Shop Styling Tools" — anything that tells me where I land when I click. Four "Shop now" links feel lazy.
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook.** Even "Sally Rewards members: your points apply at checkout" makes me feel like a known customer, not a cold email blast.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `AMERICA25 ends tonight — 3 things still in stock`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: 25% off ion, Wella & your cart`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code AMERICA25 — 2-hour delivery still on`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Low stock alert: these are almost gone at 25% off`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**`img { white-space: pre; }`** (line in `<style>` block)
`white-space: pre` is not a valid or meaningful property for replaced elements like `<img>`. While browsers ignore it on images, it is a malformed rule that indicates template hygiene issues and could behave unexpectedly in non-standard renderers.

**`'Founders Grotesk'` referenced but never loaded**
The global `a` selector sets `font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif`, but no `@font-face` declaration for Founders Grotesk exists — only Open Sans is loaded. Every link in the email falls back to Helvetica. If Founders Grotesk is a brand font, this is a rendering regression across all clients.

**Open Sans pinned to `v17`**
All six `@font-face` src URLs reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Current Open Sans on Google Fonts is v29+. Google typically preserves old version paths, but this is fragile and technically outdated.

**Web fonts in email — limited client support**
`@font-face` from an external URL renders only in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and a small number of clients. Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (Windows), and Samsung Mail all strip or ignore it. Fallback stacks must carry the full visual weight — verify the Helvetica/sans-serif fallback is acceptable at production.

**`-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic` on `img`**
IE-only property, unsupported in all modern email clients and browsers. Dead code; harmless but indicates template age.
