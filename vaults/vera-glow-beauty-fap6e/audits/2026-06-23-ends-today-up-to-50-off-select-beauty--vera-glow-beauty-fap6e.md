# ENDS TODAY: up to 50% off select beauty 🫣
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I opened this the second I saw "ENDS TODAY" from Sephora because I'm Rouge and that's just what we do — but let me be real about the timing. Yesterday I got "The Sephora Major Summer Deals chapter is closing ⏲️" and I gave it an 8 because the narrative landing felt earned. Today the chapter is still closing? That clock emoji has been ticking for 24 hours now. The urgency is starting to feel like a weekly reset rather than a hard wall.
- The email itself is a lot. Hero with the Major Summer Deals banner, a 50% / 25% off split tier, a Beauty Insiders section with bonus points and same-day delivery callouts, a product grid I can barely parse at a glance, a MERIT "Just Arrived" feature, and a TRY IT. LOVE IT. section that reads like a Costco end cap. I'm scrolling through half the beauty department and nothing is saying *this specific thing is 50% off, go get it right now.*
- Would I click? Yes — but only because I'm conditioned to treat any Sephora sale-end email as a prompt to open the app and start cart-building. I wouldn't forward this. There's nothing specific enough in here to screenshot and drop in the group chat. This is a "mark read, open app, spend $80 I didn't plan on" email. Which, honestly? That might be exactly what they want.

## What's working

- **The 50% / 25% tier split is immediately scannable** — knowing there are two discount depths before I even scroll tells me where my budget goes.
- **Beauty Insiders Get More section earns its place** — seeing the bonus points badge, same-day delivery, and the 15% benefit called out made me feel like a known customer rather than a broadcast recipient. That section does real work for Rouge members.
- **MERIT "Just Arrived" is the right brand for me** — clean, Millennial-coded, launch-day energy. Dedicating a named section to it instead of burying it in the product grid was the right call. That's the one thing I'd actually screenshot.
- **"ENDS TODAY" is credible in context** — this sale has been building across multiple sends, so the hard cutoff reads as real, not manufactured.
- **No render bugs visible** — clean layout, images loaded, no overlapping copy. Compared to that Ulta MUGLER email where the "Beauty Break / this event is over" banner sat right at the top breaking the experience, this is tight.

## What's weak

- **Yesterday's email already closed this chapter** — "The Sephora Major Summer Deals chapter is closing ⏲️" landed 24 hours ago with the same energy. Running the same urgency beat two days running dilutes both sends. Charlotte Tilbury did nine emails in two weeks and I started tuning out around send seven; Sephora is on a faster trajectory to the same fate.
- **"Select beauty" is doing zero lifting** — I still don't know if the Drunk Elephant SPF I've been eyeing is in the sale. Without that clarity I'm clicking out of FOMO, not intention, and FOMO clicks are the ones I regret.
- **The product grid is visually noisy** — multiple thumbnails, small format, hard to distinguish at a glance. Glossier's "Final Hours: Up to 25% off" from a few days ago nailed the opposite approach: one hero product, one clear story, one click destination.
- **"TRY IT. LOVE IT." copy is brand-voiceless** — it sounds like a QVC segment title, not Sephora. Even ILIA — who I clocked at seven emails in nine days with increasingly thin creative — maintains cleaner brand language than this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one can't-miss SKU at 50% off** — "Tatcha Dewy Skin Cream: 50% off, ends tonight" beats "up to 50% off select beauty" every single time. Give me the thing, then let me browse the rest.
- 2. **Use my Rouge purchase history to surface a personalized rec** — Ulta is targeting by brand at least (GWP by brand tier), but Sephora has years of my transaction data. Show me something in my actual repurchase cycle instead of a buffet.
- 3. **Either extend the chapter narrative or drop it** — if yesterday was "closing," today should be "final page" or "last chapter." Don't reset to a neutral "ENDS TODAY." The story arc was working; maintain it.
- 4. **Cap the product grid at four SKUs, full product names visible** — at current density I can't tell what I'm looking at without clicking through, which means I'm not pre-deciding what I want before I land in the app.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Beauty Insiders: bonus points stack on top of your sale price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The MERIT drop + everything 50% off. Both end today.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). This is a known Sephora template choice but is still a real accessibility defect.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied globally (confirmed)**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Suppresses OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Applying this to `body` and all block elements is overly broad — the intent (prevent iOS auto-scaling phone numbers) is achieved with `a[href^=tel]` alone.

**Alt text on images** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Must verify that all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes, especially hero and product images.

**`<title>Sephora</title>` (confirmed)** — generic; screen readers announce this as the document title in webmail. Low severity but could be more descriptive.

**`robots: no index` (confirmed)** — correct practice for emails with a hosted web version; not an issue.
