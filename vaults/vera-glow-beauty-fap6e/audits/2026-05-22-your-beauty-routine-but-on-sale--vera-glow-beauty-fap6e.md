# Your Beauty Routine, But on Sale ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Seven Sally Beauty emails in eleven days, and here we are on May 22 — the Thursday before Memorial Day — with another pass at the same "up to 50% off" messaging they already tried yesterday. I'm not even mad, I'm just tired. The subject line has a cute little "but on sale" twist that almost got me, but when I opened it I got seven different offer tiles fighting for attention like contestants on a game show. My brain went into deal-math mode immediately: 20% off sitewide *and* individual product discounts? Which stacks, which doesn't? The answer is almost definitely "nothing stacks and the 20% is probably just on full-price stuff."
- The hero is a Memorial Day styling tools sale, and I'll give them this — the model has beautiful hair and the $5 nail lacquer deal at the top caught my eye in a good way. But then the email just keeps going. And going. Volumizing powder, curl activator, a braiding kit, Shimmer Lights multi-buy, cordless flat irons… at some point I've scrolled past my impulse buy window and I'm just reading like a grocery circular.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe circle back if I'm actively shopping for a hot tool or Wella product. I would not forward it — Drunk Elephant ran a single-offer F&F email this week and that's the one I texted my friend about.

## What's working

- **The $5 nail lacquer deal is a scroll-stopper** — specific, cheap, low-commitment, and nail always catches my eye as a Sally strength
- **Model is a young woman of color with styled hair** — feels right for the brand, demographic signal matches me
- **20% off sitewide banner is front and center** — even if the terms are probably a minefield, at least I see it immediately without scrolling
- **Memorial Day timing is on-point** — sale is running before the weekend, not after, which is the right call
- **Ion cordless tools at 60% off is a real deal** — that's a legit number for a styling tool category they actually own

## What's weak

- **Seven offer blocks is too many** — Sephora's sale emails lead with *one* hero discount and let the page breathe; this feels like every markdown manager had a deadline
- **"Up to 50% off" in the hero is classic vague bait** — the best actual deal I can find by scrolling is the 60% on cordless tools, which should be the hero
- **"Shop Now" repeated on every tile is lazy CTA copy** — e.l.f. at least writes "Shop the Drop" or product-specific button text; "Shop Now" seven times is invisible
- **No loyalty angle whatsoever** — I'm doing points math in my head and Sally hasn't given me a single reward-based reason to buy today vs. waiting
- **This is the second consecutive Memorial Day email** — yesterday's "Up to 50% Off" already made the pitch; this one doesn't escalate the offer or add urgency, it just repeats

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one hero deal, cut four offer tiles** — the 60% off cordless tools is the strongest specific offer in this email; lead with that, ditch the braiding kit and multi-buy shimmer lights tiles entirely
- 2. **Put a deadline in the subject line** — "ends Monday" would unlock the urgency signal this email is missing entirely
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA buttons** — "Shop Styling Tools," "Grab the $5 Nails," "Score 60% Off" — anything with a noun
- 4. **Add a loyalty callout** — even one line: "Pro members get an extra 10%" would make me feel like a known customer, not a cold lead
- 5. **Don't send two Memorial Day emails with the same headline two days in a row** — if you're going to follow up, escalate: drop the price, add a door-buster, or send a "last chance" angle
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off cordless tools — Memorial Day ends Monday`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus $5 nail lacquer and 30% off Wella — sale ends May 26`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `@font-face` for 'Founders Grotesk':**
The global anchor rule `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a custom font with no corresponding `@font-face` declaration in the `<style>` block. Every link in the email silently falls back to Helvetica. If this font is intentional, it needs a hosted woff2 `@font-face` entry; otherwise the declaration is dead weight.

**Issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`:**
`white-space: pre` on `img` elements is a non-standard rule with no documented email rendering benefit. It does not affect block-level images but may cause unexpected spacing on inline images in some Outlook versions. Should be removed.

**Minor — Open Sans pinned to v17:**
`@font-face` URLs reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Current version is v36. Version-pinned paths are stable until Google deprecates them, but this is 5+ major versions behind. Low immediate risk; worth updating on next template revision.

**Minor — Mobile breakpoint at 499px:**
`@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)` is non-standard. Most ESP templates use 480px or 600px. Devices between 500–599px wide receive desktop layout. Confirm this matches QA device testing matrix.

**Cannot audit — alt text:**
Image tags are not visible in truncated source. All `<img>` elements require descriptive `alt` attributes; decorative images require `alt=""`.
