# Skincare Mode: Summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Seven. This is email number seven from ILIA in roughly nine days, and I had to double-check my count because "Skincare Mode: Summer" lands so similarly to "Summer Skin Staples" from June 16 that my brain filed them in the same mental drawer. That's the lens everything gets read through now — not "is this good?" but "did I just see this?"
- And here's the thing: the email itself is actually gorgeous. Clean editorial layout, a stunning Black woman model with skin so dewy I want to throw my wallet at ILIA immediately, and a routine-driven architecture — cleanser → SPF → shine control → definition — that maps to how I actually build a summer morning. If this were email two or three this month, I'd be bookmarking it. But at seven in nine days, hitting the SPF beat *again* (same core message as "Summer Skin Staples," same "wear SPF" energy, nearly identical section format) starts to feel less like brand consistency and more like creative autopilot.
- No loyalty signal, no offer in the hero, no urgency. The "stay sharp" section buried at the bottom hints at something deal-adjacent but it's so far below the fold it functionally doesn't exist on mobile. Charlotte Tilbury put 45% off in the subject line and still barely earned my click — ILIA is asking me to shop at full price, email seven, on content I've already seen a version of. I'll mark it read, maybe mentally file one product, and wait to see what lands next week.

## What's working

- Hero model is doing everything right — a Black woman with genuinely radiant, dewy skin is the aspirational signal I want from clean beauty. Demographic representation lands for me as a 29-year-old who notices who's in the campaign.
- Routine architecture is smart curation: Start With Skincare → Wear SPF → Soften Shine → Stay Sharp flows exactly like a real morning routine. That's useful, not just product dumping.
- "Wear SPF · Wear SPF · Wear SPF" is a fun typographic beat — cheeky and self-aware, and ILIA earns it because their SPF products are actually good.
- Production quality is spotless. Clean render, no broken images, crisp layout at every section. ILIA never makes me feel like their email was rushed.
- Subject line at 21 characters is mobile-friendly and doesn't yell at me.

## What's weak

- Email seven in nine days. The June 16 "Summer Skin Staples" already ran this exact format — editorial sections, routine steps, lifestyle copy. Sending it again on June 22 makes the prior send feel like a test draft.
- Zero price signal in the hero. Sephora's Major Summer Deals closing email this month was built around a specific urgency and offer — that's why it got two sends and still earned the opens. "SHOP NOW" with no discount or GWP attached is a shrug.
- The gift card hook from June 18 ("This lip color lasts. This gift won't.") and June 20 ("A gift for you") was played twice and then just... dropped. This email doesn't close the loop on it, doesn't escalate, doesn't acknowledge it. Makes those two sends feel like noise.
- "Stay sharp" with what looks like an offer is buried at the bottom of a long scroll. If there's a deal there, it belongs in the subject line. e.l.f. named the brand right in the subject ("50% Off It's a 10") — that's why it worked.
- "SHOP NOW" is the most generic CTA in the game. After building a whole aspirational routine, send me somewhere specific.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with whatever is in "stay sharp"** — if there's a sale or extra % off in that bottom section, that is the email. That belongs in the hero and the subject line. Bury the editorial below it, not the offer.
- 2. **Retire the summer skincare editorial until July** — three ILIA sends this month have essentially been "SPF + glow + summer routine." Give me a formulator deep-dive, a founder story, a skin-type targeting angle, anything that breaks the template.
- 3. **Make every section CTA specific** — "Shop My SPF Routine," "Find Shine Control," "Shop Lip Define" — name where I land, not just "Shop Now."
- 4. **Close the gift card loop** — you played that angle twice, either confirm it expired or convert me. Don't just move on like it never happened; it makes the brand feel disorganized.
- 5. **Dial the cadence back** — four to five days between sends resets my goodwill. Seven sends in nine days means the next email starts in a hole.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty:**
```html

```
Whitespace-only title. Some clients surface this in tab/window labels. Set to a meaningful value or omit.

**CSS `@import` for fonts will fail in most email clients:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Outlook, Gmail (webmail), and several mobile clients strip or block `@import` inside `<style>` blocks. Montserrat will silently fall back to the declared stack `"Helvetica", sans-serif` — confirm that's acceptable; if not, use a `<link>` tag instead (better client support).

**No hover-state differentiation on links:**
```css
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
