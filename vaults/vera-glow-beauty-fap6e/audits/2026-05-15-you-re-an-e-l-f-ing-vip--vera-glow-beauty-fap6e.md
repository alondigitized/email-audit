# You're an e.l.f.ing VIP!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Fourth e.l.f. email in under three days, and this time they went full loyalty-program pitch. I'll be honest — the subject got me curious for half a second because "VIP" implies I've already earned something, but the moment I opened it I got the bait-and-switch: it's a "join our Beauty Squad" recruitment email. I'm already Rouge tier at Sephora and Diamond at Ulta — my loyalty-program bar is high, and asking me to chase $5 off and a Venmo payout without an immediate reward for doing so is a tough sell. The Venmo payout perk is genuinely unexpected and I want to respect that differentiation, but it's buried in a six-icon grid where you have to stop and read every tile to even clock it.
- The layout is clean — no broken images, readable hierarchy, app section is tidy — but the whole email is structured around getting me to do work (join, download app) with a promise of future payoff. No urgency, no sale, no "here's the $5 off if you join today." Compare that to Ulta's mystery offer last week, which at least personalized the subject with a name and gave me something to open and claim. This email is asking me to commit to a new loyalty program cold, and the value proof is a sneak peek of icons, not a demo.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Wouldn't click the "JOIN NOW," probably wouldn't forward — unless I was texting a friend who had never heard of Beauty Squad and wanted the Venmo payout explained.

## What's working

- **The Venmo payout reward is a legit differentiator.** No other beauty loyalty program I'm in pays out to Venmo — that's more interesting than another free birthday product, and it deserves a headline spot, not a grid tile.
- **"HEY, PERK UP!" hero copy is punchy and on-brand.** The playful e.l.f. voice is consistent; the purple palette reads recognizably e.l.f. even without seeing the logo.
- **Rewards sneak peek is well-organized.** Six-tile grid is clean, icons are readable, I can parse all six perks at a glance — $5 off, free full-size, birthday gifts, Venmo payout, early access, gift cards.
- **"Top Picks for You" with prices attached.** Power Grip Primer at $11, Lip Liner at $3 — having real prices removes a click barrier. Drunk Elephant would never show you $3 product and that's honestly their loss.
- **No render bugs.** Layout held up cleanly — no overlapping text, no broken images.

## What's weak

- **Subject-body mismatch is the biggest miss.** "You're an e.l.f.ing VIP!" implies I've already earned status, but the very first CTA says "JOIN NOW FOR FREE" — that's jarring. Either I'm a VIP or I'm not. This feels like a segmentation error, or at minimum a copy contradiction.
- **Zero urgency, anywhere.** No deadline, no "join by Sunday," no limited-time bonus points for signing up. Sally Beauty's flash sale from yesterday at least told me the offer ended soon — this email reads like it could have been sent any day of the year.
- **The "Top Picks for You" section earns that name.** These feel algorithmically generated but presented without any actual "why I'd like them" framing. No shade recommendation, no "because you loved Holy Hydration" logic is visible. Sephora's personalization emails at least tell me why something's recommended.
- **Four e.l.f. emails in ~2.5 days.** I'm building the ignore reflex now. The May 13 halo skin tint was a 9/10 for me, the May 14 glass-skin double-tap was strong — but this fourth send is loyalty recruitment with no promotional hook. The cadence is starting to dilute the brand.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Venmo payout.** This is the only thing in this email that Sephora and Ulta literally can't offer. Put it in the subject, put it in the hero — "Join Beauty Squad. Yes, we Venmo you." That's a forward-worthy hook.
- 2. **Resolve the VIP contradiction.** Either I'm already a VIP (in which case, show me my current points balance and what tier I'm at) or I'm being invited to join (in which case, don't call me a VIP before I've done anything). Pick one lane.
- 3. **Add a join bonus with a deadline.** "Earn 100 bonus points when you join by Sunday" turns this from evergreen recruitment into a time-sensitive action. No deadline = no urgency = no click.
- 4. **Move the Venmo payout tile to position one in the rewards grid.** Right now it's fourth out of six — it'll get skipped. That's your strongest differentiator hidden in the middle of the row.
- 5. **Trim to one CTA.** "JOIN NOW FOR FREE" in the hero, then "DOWNLOAD THE APP" below it — two separate asks in the same email split my attention. Pick the one that matters more and own it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `We Venmo. Sephora doesn't. Join Beauty Squad.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Beauty Squad welcome gift is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn points, get free products — and yes, Venmo payouts are real.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Join free. Score $5 off, birthday gifts + a Venmo payout. No, really.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze click-tracking wrapping confirmed:**
```
href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/qlCQpa53yl1X7CkoP9xtGg~~/.../bSY3P9q..."
```
- This is expected Braze behavior — the destination URL and any UTM params are encoded inside the redirect payload, not visible in raw HTML source.
- **Issue:** Cannot confirm UTM params are appended without resolving the redirect. If Braze link tracking is disabled or the redirect chain breaks, all click attribution is lost with no fallback.
- **Issue:** `tabindex="-1"` is set on the hero image anchor. This removes the link from keyboard tab order entirely, making the primary CTA unreachable for keyboard-only users.

```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts external load — fails in major clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` so it's suppressed for Outlook, but Gmail (Android), Samsung Mail, and several corporate clients also block external stylesheets. Fallback chain `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is correctly defined, but the font load will silently fail in a significant share of recipients.

**Link color/decoration suppressed globally:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This strips visual affordances from all links in Gmail's web client. Any inline text links (if present in the truncated body) become visually indistinguishable from surrounding copy — an accessibility failure.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers use the document title for email context. Should be set to a meaningful string (e.g., the subject line).

**Alt text present on hero image:** `alt="e.l.f. beauty squad"` — no issue.

**VML conditionals present** for Outlook rendering — correct implementation.

**Nested `nl-container` tables:** The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner `nl-container` with identical attributes. This is redundant markup but not a rendering defect in practice.
