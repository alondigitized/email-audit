# We extended the sale…but this is it!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Email twelve. I need to just let that sit for a second — because yesterday's "Adios, 25% off..." was genuinely the cleverest subject line in this entire campaign, and I opened it specifically because it felt like a real farewell. Then this morning "Hi again, 25% off!" landed as a self-aware comeback. And now, later the same day, we're getting an extension email with "ENDS TONIGHT" bolded in the body. I read those two words and my brain immediately asked: okay, but what time tomorrow?
- The credibility on urgency is gone. Not damaged — gone. When a brand has said "last day" four times, said goodbye, said hi again, and is now extending, "this is not a drill, people" reads as comedy rather than copy. The fine print, if you squint, says valid through 5/28 at 2:59 AM ET, which is technically tonight — but after eleven prior sends, I have no reason to believe this deadline will hold.
- What saves this from the recycling bin is the offer itself, which is genuinely stacked: 25% sitewide, 35% on kits, and a tiered GWP structure with specific thresholds spelled out clearly. I've had my eye on the Hydra and Bright kit, and "free kit at $100 spent" is real math I can do. I'd probably click — but not because Anna convinced me this was the last chance. I'd click because the deal is legitimately good and I've already been doing the ROI math for eleven emails.
- Would I forward to a friend? I'd text a screenshot of the GWP tiers. The email? No.

## What's working

- The tiered GWP structure is the clearest offer communication in this entire campaign — three tiers, three specific dollar thresholds, specific products named, code FIRM called out explicitly at the $50 level. Most brands bury this.
- "This is not a drill, people" is charming; Anna's consistent voice is one of the only reasons this campaign hasn't fully collapsed under its own weight.
- Plain-text format scans instantly on mobile — the bulleted offer list is at the top with zero image-load delay or render friction.
- The phone number for personal recommendations is a genuine premium touch; almost no beauty brand does this in email and it signals prestige without screaming it.
- 25% sitewide + 35% on kits + F-Balm mask at $175 is actually a stacked offer that can compete with a Sephora Beauty Insider event day.

## What's weak

- "Hello there!" on email twelve from the head of marketing who has been writing to me personally for two weeks is a jarring credibility crack — if this is a letter from Anna, Anna knows my name.
- "ENDS TONIGHT" has no teeth. ILIA's "20% off ends tomorrow" worked on email fifteen because ILIA hadn't already said goodbye and brought the sale back from the dead. DE burned the urgency signal.
- Zero visual anchor — not one product image, no branded header, no lifestyle shot. At $175 GWP I want to *see* the full-size F-Balm mask I'm earning; a single inline image would do enormous conversion work here.
- Category-level CTAs ("essentials," "new," "moisturizers," "serums") are missed shots — no specific product call, no "shop the kit," no "get the F-Balm." Compare to how ILIA surfaced a specific hero product with a direct link; that's the gap.
- The fine print is a four-asterisk wall of dense text stacked after the close — date windows, exclusions, GWP terms, minimum spend layers. That complexity belongs on a landing page, not below the signature.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Personalize the greeting** — "Hello there!" on email twelve after eleven "personal notes from Anna" breaks the conceit completely. Use the subscriber's first name or cut the greeting entirely and lead with the hook.
- 2. **Own the extension honestly** — "We said goodbye, you crashed the site, we caved" is the version of this email that has personality *and* credibility. Pretending "ENDS TONIGHT" is fresh urgency insults the reader's intelligence at this stage of the campaign.
- 3. **Add one product image** — The F-Balm mask at $175 GWP is the aspirational tier. Show it. A single inline shot between the offer bullets and the body copy would give the eye somewhere to land and remind me what I'm actually shopping for.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTAs to specific actions** — "Earn the F-Balm free at $175 →" or "Shop the Bouncy Brightfacial kit →" beats "moisturizers" as a linked noun every time.
- 5. **Compress the fine print** — One sentence ("Terms and exclusions apply; see site for details.") plus a hyperlink covers the legal need without the four-footnote stack.

- **Subject Alt B:** `You asked, we caved. One more day, 25% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We said goodbye. You kept shopping. Earn the F-Balm mask free at $175 — ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% sitewide, 35% kits, and a free full-size F-Balm if you spend $175. This is the real last push.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — HTML comment syntax inside a `<style>` block (confirmed bug):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` is HTML comment syntax. Inside a `<style>` block it is not valid CSS. Most modern parsers ignore it silently, but strict CSS parsers (e.g., some Outlook and Samsung Mail versions) may parse the surrounding rules incorrectly. The commented-out rules serve no purpose and should be removed entirely.

**Issue 2 — Triple font loading for the same typefaces:**
- `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>` (3 tags)
- `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` in `<style>` (3 duplicates)
- `@font-face` blocks pointing to the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/` files

Result: up to 9 external font HTTP requests for 3 font weights. Clients that respect web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) will fire all of them. This adds latency with no rendering benefit.

**Issue 3 — Aggressive global `*` reset:**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly}
```
`line-height: 100%` applied to `*` overrides all descendant line-height values unless individually re-declared. This is a known cause of clipped or collapsed text in table cells across Yahoo Mail and older Outlook builds. The `max-height:1000000px` is a recognized Gmail clip-prevention hack but should be scoped rather than applied globally.

**Issue 4 — Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**
This is not a Drunk Elephant–controlled domain. If the CDN is unavailable or rate-limits the send volume, fonts silently fail across all rendering environments. Self-hosted fonts on `drunkelephant.com` or a controlled CDN are more reliable.
