# Ready, set, glow ✨ This TATCHA gift with select $60 online purchase** is a DEW! Terms & excl. apply 💜
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, first thing I need to clock: the hero banner says "Beauty Break / this event is over" — and if I'm reading that correctly, Ulta just sent me an email to tell me an event has ended. That is... a choice. I'm going to give Ulta the benefit of the doubt that something in the layout stacked oddly, but on first glance it reads like a tombstone for a sale I already missed, which is not the open-to-click pipeline you want from your hero.
- Past that confusion, the Tatcha GWP angle is genuinely interesting to me. A gift with a $60 purchase is the kind of offer I am always running mental math on — especially when the brand is Tatcha, which I would absolutely stock up on at the right threshold. The "is a DEW!" pun in the subject earned a small grin from me because yes, I know exactly what that's referencing. The email scrolls long though: multiple sections, multiple product categories, at least four distinct CTAs by the time I hit the rewards block at the bottom. This is one of those emails that tried to do too much and ends up doing nothing in particular very well.
- Would I click? Maybe once — and only on the Tatcha section. I would not forward this. I would not screenshot it for a friend. And I've been in Ulta's inbox enough in the last two weeks (the relentless 20%-off-to-join-Rewards campaign that ended last week) that I'm fatiguing on their volume before I even read the first word.

## What's working

- **Tatcha logo placement is strong.** Having the Tatcha branding bold and center after the hero signals this is a real prestige collab, not a generic sale email.
- **The "is a DEW!" pun in the subject is earned.** It's dorky in a way that actually fits the Tatcha Dewy Skin universe — I noticed it and it made sense immediately.
- **Skin-type personalization section is a nice touch.** The "Choose a cleanser and moisturizer duo targeted to your skin type" block is exactly the kind of content-meets-commerce framing I want from a skincare send.
- **Multiple brand sections create a real browse feel.** For someone already in shopping mode, the scrollable product groupings (Tatcha, then tarte-looking greens, then the hero moisturizer) feel like a curated edit, not a homepage dump.

## What's weak

- **The "Beauty Break / this event is over" hero is a disaster if I'm reading it right.** Whether it's a stale asset, a bad copy stack, or a genuine render issue, the first thing I see tells me the offer expired. I closed a Glossier email the other week (June 24) for exactly the same stale-urgency reason — Ulta cannot afford this.
- **The subject line is doing too much.** "Ready, set, glow ✨ This TATCHA gift with select $60 online purchase\*\* is a DEW! Terms & excl. apply 💜" — that's over 100 characters with two asterisks and two emoji. On my lock screen this truncates before I even get to "DEW." The "Terms & excl. apply" should never be in the subject line.
- **No loyalty tier acknowledgment.** I'm Diamond. Sephora's "Stacking Weekend" email I reviewed last week opened with explicit Rouge/VIB tier callouts. This email treats me like a walk-in off the street — generic $60 threshold, no Diamond bonus signal.
- **The rewards section is buried at the bottom.** If there's a points multiplier or Diamond perk attached to the Tatcha purchase, I can't find it until I've already scrolled past five sections.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or retire the "Beauty Break" hero copy immediately.** If the event is live, the hero should say so with a clear end date. If it ended, pull the email. ILIA's last send I reviewed had cleaner offer framing in a single hero sentence — Ulta should take notes.
- 2. **Cut the subject line in half and drop the fine print.** The asterisks and "Terms & excl. apply" belong in the footer, not the subject. Lead with the pun and the brand.
- 3. **Surface the Diamond/rewards angle early.** If Diamond members get bonus points on this purchase, say it in the first scroll. That's what differentiates Ulta from just buying Tatcha direct.
- 4. **Consolidate to one or two CTAs.** The multi-section scroll with separate CTAs per product dilutes the Tatcha GWP message that earned the open.
- 5. **Add a hard end date on the GWP offer.** Sally Beauty's "Last Chance: BOGO 50%" I reviewed recently had a clear deadline; Ulta's offer feels open-ended and therefore deferrable.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — External Google Fonts stylesheet will silently fail in most clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:ital,opsz,wght@0,9..40,100..1000;1,9..40,100..1000&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), Yahoo Mail, and Apple Mail on iOS strip or ignore `<link>` tags in `<head>`. DM Sans will not render for the majority of recipients. The declared fallback chain (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` in `.helvetica`) will activate silently — confirm visual layouts were QA'd against system fonts, not the web font.

**Issue — Aggressive link color suppression in Gmail web view:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
  font-size: inherit !important;
  font-family: inherit !important;
  font-weight: inherit !important;
  line-height: inherit !important;
}
```
This blanket override removes all visual link affordances inside Gmail's web client. CTA buttons styled with background-color survive, but any plain-text links (e.g., footer legal, unsubscribe) will be invisible as links. If the unsubscribe link is text-only, this rule suppresses its styling.

**Issue — Dead commented code still inflates payload:**
```css
/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu |BEGIN Hamburger Menu
   ... [~60 lines of CSS] ...
   END Hamburger Menu */
```
The commented block is retained verbatim in `<style>`. Estimated 2–3 KB of inert CSS. Additionally, the store-dropdown CSS (`#mobile-label-store`, `#menu-wrapper-store`, etc.) immediately follows and is **not** commented out — if the corresponding HTML elements are absent from the body, this is dead weight CSS that still parses.

**Minor — `<title>` trailing space:**
```html
<title>Ulta Beauty </title>
```
Some clients surface the `<title>` value in tab/preview UI. Cosmetic, but indicative of template hygiene gap.

**No issues found:** `lang="en-US"`, `translate="no"`, `charset=UTF-8`, MSO `AllowPNG`/`PixelsPerInch`, `viewport` meta, Gmail Android margin fix (`div[style*="margin: 16px 0"]`), and `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` suppression are all correctly implemented.
