# Bestie, if you haven’t shopped e.l.f. SKIN, wyd?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I actually stan e.l.f. — this isn't one of those emails I auto-delete. The "wyd?" energy in the subject is very on-brand for them and it made me smirk a little, which is more than I can say for 80% of my inbox. The hero "high-key results. low-key prices." flows straight into individual product callouts with actual dollar amounts, which tells me exactly what I'm looking at without having to scroll four screens.
- That said, the moment I hit the Target receipt-upload section, the whole vibe shifted. I shop e.l.f. on the e.l.f. site specifically because their loyalty experience is cleaner — getting redirected to photograph a Target receipt is a chore I associate with CPG couponing, not a beauty brand I have feelings about. And while the individual product prices are legit ($11–$16), there's no sale signal here. No percentage off, no event code, no "Beauty Squad exclusive." It just... reads as a product education email dressed up in sale clothing.
- I'd click through to look at the Supra Glow serum, honestly. But I wouldn't forward this — there's nothing scream-worthy about it.

## What's working

- **The subject line voice is pitch-perfect.** "Bestie... wyd?" is exactly how e.l.f. talks to their audience and it doesn't feel try-hard — it feels earned.
- **Hero copy is tight.** "High-key results. low-key prices. Don't sleep on high-perf skincare." — three punchy lines that actually tell me what this is about before I read a single product name.
- **Individual product prices are front and center.** $15, $16, $11 — seeing actual numbers without hunting for them is something Sephora's editorial emails almost never do.
- **Beauty Squad + Target rewards are both surfaced.** Two loyalty angles in one email; I appreciate that they acknowledged multiple shopping paths.
- **"Top Picks for You" section at the bottom.** Signals some personalization is happening, which makes me feel like they know I've browsed skincare vs. color.

## What's weak

- **No promotional hook.** There's no sale, no event, no limited window — just regular prices. The subject implies urgency ("wyd?!") but the body delivers catalog content. That's a bait-and-switch in energy if not offer.
- **The Target receipt-upload block kills the vibe.** After a tight brand-forward hero, suddenly I'm looking at a big yellow Target section asking me to photograph grocery receipts. It reads like a CPG brand, not a beauty brand.
- **No social proof visible anywhere.** e.l.f. has a rabid review base. Not a single star rating or "X sold" callout in sight — missed opportunity given the product spotlights.
- **Product detail is light.** I can see names and prices but nothing about texture, skin type, or even a one-line benefit claim per product. The Vitamin C serum is legitimately good — say something about it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer or event peg.** Even "Beauty Squad members get free shipping on orders $20+" counts. Right now it reads as a no-reason send. Add a hook or hold it for a sales event.
- 2. **Pull the Target block into a secondary email or footer mention.** The receipt-upload CTA is a separate experience with a different job-to-be-done — it undercuts the e.l.f.-owned brand vibe here.
- 3. **One-line benefit per product.** Under "Supra Glow 15% Vitamin C Glow Serum": "15% Vitamin C, no irritation, $15." Under "Holy Hydration Cleansing Balm": "Melts SPF and mascara clean." Make it scannable.
- 4. **Add star ratings or review counts under each product CTA.** e.l.f. has the reviews — show them.
- 5. **Sharpen the primary CTA.** "SHOP E.L.F. SKIN" is generic. "Build Your Routine for Under $50" or "Shop the Skin Drop" would give me more reason to click.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag (confirmed bug):**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Samsung Mail, Outlook Mobile) surface `<title>` content in notification previews. An empty title degrades deliverability UX in those clients.

**Empty primary font-family string (confirmed bug):**
```html
style="font-family:'',Verdana,Sans-serif"
```
The first value is an empty string `''`. The browser/client skips it and falls through to Verdana, but this is broken CSS — the intent was almost certainly `'Open Sans'`. Affects the desktop-hidden block specifically.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` tag:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (all variants), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags in `<head>`. Open Sans will not render for the majority of recipients. The fallback chain `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` handles this acceptably, but the font load is wasted bandwidth for ~80%+ of opens.

**Alt text:** Hero image has `alt="e.l.f. SKIN"` — present and non-empty. No issue.

**Role attributes:** All tables carry `role="presentation"` — correct.
