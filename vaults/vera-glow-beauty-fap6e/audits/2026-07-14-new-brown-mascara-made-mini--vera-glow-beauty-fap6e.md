## What's working

- **Brown mascara is a legit gap-fill.** The vast majority of mascara launches are jet-black; positioning this as a soft-glam alternative is differentiated and I immediately registered it as something I'd check out for no-makeup makeup days.
- **The benefit list is actually functional.** "Flake-resistant," "safe for sensitive eyes," "smudge-resistant," "up to 12-hour wear" — that's the checklist I run in my head every time. Seeing it laid out cleanly saves me a Google.
- **Social proof stat does real work.** "100% said it lifts, separates + defines lashes*" — even with the asterisk, that claim slows my scroll.
- **The wand and brush product shots are detailed.** I can see the actual applicator shape, which matters more to me than most people realize — brush geometry is how I predict what a mascara does to my lashes before I buy.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero urgency.** ILIA has now given me five sends in six days and none of them have a financial hook. I'm doing Sephora Beauty Insider event math in my head at all times, and right now there's nothing to calculate. Drunk Elephant's "Damn, your skin!" three days ago had the same no-discount energy but at least the subject line made me open before I noticed.
- **"SHOP NOW >" is doing the absolute minimum.** After building a tightly focused product story, the CTA just... exists generically. Glossier on their Balm Dotcom launch had a specific call to the product in the button copy — ILIA could do the same with "Shop double define →" and immediately feel more intentional.
- **The mini tote section dilutes the message.** The July 9 mini tote campaign was a full send on its own. Tacking it onto a mascara launch email feels like padding, not cross-sell strategy. It breaks the visual flow and makes the email feel like two ideas stapled together.
- **No price or shade context anywhere visible.** I don't know if this mini is $18 or $28, and I don't know what "brown" looks like on a deeper skin tone. That context affects whether I click now or add it to my "check the ILIA site when I have 20 minutes" list — and currently it's the latter.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch price anchor in the hero.** Even a simple "$XX for the mini" or "Under $25, full results" in the hero section gives me the math I need to convert. Right now I have to click just to find out if this fits my budget, and friction before purchase intent is the worst kind.
- 2. **Give the CTA button a name.** "Shop double define →" or "Try it mini →" — make the button copy mirror the product so it feels like the conclusion of the story, not a generic redirect.
- 3. **Cut the mini tote or move it to a footer PS.** One tight product message is more powerful than two diluted ones. If the tote has to stay, make it a three-line footer mention, not a full section.
- 4. **Add shade context or a "looks good on" signal.** Even a short "warm brown, wearable on all skin tones" in the body copy would address the purchase hesitation I'd have before clicking to the site.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `lang` attribute** — `<html>` has no `lang="en"`. Screen readers cannot determine the document language. Fix: `<html lang="en" ...>`.

- **Empty `<title>`** — `<title> </title>` (whitespace only). Some clients surface this as the tab/window title. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

- **`@import` font loading will silently fail in Gmail and Outlook** — Both:
  ```
  @import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
  @import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
  ```
  are stripped by Gmail (which ignores `<style>` blocks entirely) and blocked by Outlook. The fallback stack `"Helvetica", sans-serif` applies in those clients, which is functional but means the Montserrat brand font never renders for the majority of users. This is a known limitation of email, not a fixable bug without web font service workarounds, but it should be documented.

- **All link hover states are identical to default state** — `a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }` provides zero visual feedback on hover. Not a breaking issue but reduces usability on desktop clients that respect hover CSS.

- **Preheader padding uses Unicode filler characters** — The invisible `͏` (U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER) characters are a common preheader-stuffing technique, but some spam filters (SpamAssassin) penalize runs of invisible Unicode. Consider using `&zwnj;` (`&#8204;`) or `&nbsp;` instead, which are more broadly accepted.

- **Image `alt` attributes** — Cannot assess from truncated source. All product images must have descriptive `alt` text; hero/banner images should have non-empty `alt` rather than `alt=""`.
