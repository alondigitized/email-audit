# Beauty pre-check
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ILIA slides into my Thursday with "Beauty pre-check" and I will say this — the "now boarding" hero stopped my thumb for a second. The travel flat-lay is *very* ILIA: products arranged like they're clearing TSA, warm tones, clean editorial energy. The wordplay is genuinely cute and I clocked it immediately as a TSA Pre-Check riff. Points for concept execution.
- But here's the thing: this is email number five from ILIA this week. Five. And for the fifth time, there is not a single offer anywhere in this email. No percentage off, no GWP, no double-points event, no "Sephora Beauty Insider exclusive." Meanwhile Sephora sent me an actual "10% off $100+" with a countdown on the same date — that's the baseline my inbox is calibrated to. ILIA keeps dropping editorial content that reads more like a lookbook than a marketing email, and I'm starting to feel like I'm following their brand Instagram, not their subscriber list.
- The brown mascara mini is *also* in here — same product ILIA already dedicated a full email to on July 14 (their 9/10 send that week). Three days later it's getting a cameo as module four of an SPF-focused "pack your bag" curated edit. I love the mascara, but the repeat feels lazy. The tote bag at the bottom is a pivot I definitely didn't ask for. I'd mark this read, not click, not forward.

## What's working

- **The "now boarding" hero concept is genuinely clever** — travel editorial in July makes seasonal sense and the flat-lay execution is visually sharp and very on-brand.
- **Star ratings and review counts on each product module** are doing real work here — social proof stacked into every section makes the products feel validated without requiring a single word of copy.
- **Product module hierarchy is clean** — each section has a name, a descriptor, and a CTA button; nothing overlaps, nothing breaks, no mystery meat layout.
- **The Protect + Blur Duo pairing is strategically smart** — bundling a routine (sunscreen + color) rather than a single SKU gives me more reason to click than a lone product shot would.
- **Render is flawless** — no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no overlapping text; ILIA consistently delivers clean emails.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero incentive, fifth editorial email in a row** — I do points math; without a GWP, a Sephora Beauty Insider multiplier callout, or even a limited-time angle, there's nothing pulling me to act *now* versus scrolling past.
- **The brown mascara mini is a July 14 repeat** — three days after ILIA's best send of the week, re-featuring it as a middle module in a different email undercuts the launch momentum they'd built.
- **The Mini Tote is Back feels like a different email** — dropping a bag at the bottom of a sunscreen-and-mascara routine edit reads like a site merchandising dump, not a curated travel story.
- **No urgency mechanism at all** — not even a soft "limited quantities" line; compared to Drunk Elephant's "hot takes" urgency framing and Sally Beauty's event tie-in, this has no reason-to-click-today energy.
- **Multiple modules, all generic CTAs** — every button reads "SHOP NOW" (or equivalent), which means five editorial sections compete equally for my attention and nothing wins.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give me one offer** — even a modest GWP (mini cleanser with $65+ SPF purchase) turns this from a nice-looking pass into a reason to open my Sephora app and add to cart. The travel editorial angle practically writes the hook: "Pack light, earn heavy — free travel size with any $65 SPF purchase."
- 2. **Lead with the strongest single product, not five modules** — pick the Sunscreen + Color Routine as the hero SKU, give it one full editorial moment with ingredients/SPF number visible, and make everything else secondary. Glossier does this relentlessly; ILIA splits the frame.
- 3. **Retire the brown mascara mini from this email** — it had its moment on July 14. Let it breathe or run a targeted re-engagement for people who viewed but didn't buy.
- 4. **Cut the tote or give it its own email** — the bag pivot breaks the "airport beauty routine" story and reads like a clearance module.
- 5. **Add a loyalty callout in the CTA or header** — even "earn points on all SPF" with a Sephora Insider or Credo Beauty badge would signal that ILIA knows who I am.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Value is a single space. Some email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) surface the title; screen readers may announce it as blank. Should reflect the subject line or campaign name.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` — will not render in Gmail or Outlook**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules entirely. Outlook ignores them. Montserrat will **not render** in either client. The fallback stack (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is functional, but the intended brand font is effectively absent for the majority of recipients.

**All anchor states are identical — no hover feedback**
```css
a:not([name]), a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover {
  color: #5f4b3c; text-decoration: none;
}
```
`a:hover` is identical to resting state. Users get zero visual affordance when hovering over links. Accessibility concern (WCAG 2.4.4 – Link Purpose).

**Image alt text** — unverifiable from truncated source; must audit `<img>` tags in body.

**Preheader padding technique** — using Unicode U+034F (combining grapheme joiner) as invisible filler characters is functional but increases raw byte size of the preheader block. No bug, but worth noting.
