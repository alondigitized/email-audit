# Save on your faves: up to 25% off select shampoos and conditioners 🌟
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so walking into this one with full receipts: this is shampoo email number three in four days. June 19 sent the same "25% off shampoos and conditioners" promo twice in the same day — I covered both — and now here it is again on June 21 with a slightly different subject line dressed up with a "your faves" hook. I'm not mad at the offer, I'm mad at the execution. If Sephora had staggered this better — or actually changed what they were selling — I'd be leaning in. Instead I'm doing the mental math of "did I already shop this? yes. should I shop it again? no." The email itself is technically fine. The hero lands, the Beauty Insider section is present, SHOP NOW is findable. But the structure below the fold is a mess: Everyday Icons brushes compete with a hair promo, which competes with a Kérastase feature, which competes with a skincare grid. Nobody told this email what it was supposed to be about.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I'd mark it read and mentally add it to my "Sephora is really testing me this week" tally.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is legible fast.** "Up to 25% off Shampoos & Conditioners" reads in under a second — no mystery, no hunt.
- **Beauty Insider section is present.** I saw the rewards block toward the bottom; as a Rouge member, that's the bare minimum I expect Sephora to show me.
- **Kérastase callout is a nice category signal.** "New From Kérastase" is a prestige hair-care brand name that earns attention — that's the kind of specific product curation I want more of.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loaded, nothing broken, text isn't overlapping.

## What's weak

- **This is shampoo email three in four days.** The June 19 double-send already burned goodwill. Sending the same promo on June 21 with "save on your faves" instead of "woo-hoo" doesn't fool me — I see through the subject-line swap.
- **"Everyday Icons" brushes have zero business being in this email.** A #71 Pro Brush callout in the middle of a shampoo promo is brand-consistency chaos. Pick a lane.
- **The product grid below the fold is unreadable at mobile scale.** Multiple small product images with what I assume are names I cannot parse — that's not shopping, that's squinting.
- **"SHOP NOW" is lazy CTA copy.** Sally Beauty named a specific product in their subject line ("50% Off It's a 10") and still outperformed this. A "Shop Hair" or "Shop 25% Off" button would take two seconds to write and actually convert.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** Charlotte Tilbury's "Lock In For The Longest Day" gave me a real deadline tied to the solstice. This email feels like the sale is just... running forever.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the brush section or give it its own send.** An "Everyday Icons" brushes callout in a shampoo email splits attention and undercuts both promotions. Segment these.
- 2. **Make Kérastase the hero, not a section.** If you're leading with prestige hair care, commit to it. Hero a specific Kérastase product with a price callout — that's what converts Sephora Rouge members who are cross-shopping launches.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "through Sunday" gives me a reason to act today instead of tomorrow-that-never-comes.
- 4. **Fix the CTA copy.** "SHOP NOW" → "Shop 25% Off Hair" — it's six words and it works.
- 5. **Rest the shampoo offer for 48 hours.** The compounding fatigue from June 19's double-send is the single biggest conversion killer here.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kérastase is 25% off — yes, really`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your hair routine just got cheaper (ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `25% off select shampoos, conditioners, and prestige brands you actually use`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kérastase, plus your other faves — through this weekend only`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **User zoom blocked**: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

- **Text-size-adjust suppressed globally**: The rule `div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }` disables OS-level text scaling across virtually all elements. This overrides accessibility settings for users who have increased system font size. Scope this rule narrowly (e.g., to `td` or `.sup` only, where it's legitimately needed for Outlook control).

- **Generic `<title>`**: `<title>Sephora</title>` — some email clients (e.g., Apple Mail, Outlook on Mac) surface the `<title>` in tab/window previews. A descriptive value like `Sephora — Up to 25% Off Shampoos & Conditioners` is more useful, though impact is minor.

- **`robots` meta value has a space**: `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` — should be `noindex` (no space). Negligible in email context since crawlers rarely index email HTML, but technically malformed.

No issues found with DOCTYPE selection (XHTML 1.0 Transitional is correct for broad email client support), MSO VML namespace inclusion, or the Outlook/Gmail data-detector suppression rules (both are properly implemented).
