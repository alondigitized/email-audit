# Ready, Set, Getaway ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. I counted — this is the nineteenth Sally email in something like three weeks, and they opened with "Ready, Set, Getaway." No offer. No number. Just a pun and a sun emoji like I'm supposed to feel something. The June 15 "More Beauty, Less Spend" send was already number eighteen and at least led with a dollar signal in the subject. This one buried the BOGO in the hero body copy and expected me to do the work.
- Once I'm inside, it's actually not terrible — there's a real BOGO 50% off hair care ending June 20, a buy-one-get-one-free nail polish, a GiGi wax bundle at $39.99, and the hair color BOGO further down. That's a lot of live deals for one send. But then there's a "get up to 20% off select clippers and trimmers" section near the bottom, and I genuinely checked the sender name again. Am I on a barber email list? Sally has always blurred the line between pro supply and consumer beauty, but putting clippers in a summer-getaway packing list email aimed at a female audience is a swing-and-a-miss.
- Compare this to how Sephora structures a sale push — they lead with the offer tier, category, and deadline in the first two scrolls and stop there. This email keeps going and going. I'd click the hair care BOGO, probably skip everything else, and not forward this to anyone because the clippers section would confuse my group chat. Mark it as read, grab the deal, move on.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is specific and time-stamped.** "Buy one, get 50% off select hair care / Ends June 20" is unambiguous — I know exactly what I'm getting and when it dies.
- **Nail section is travel-coded.** The "nail must-haves to pop in your travel tote" framing earns the summer getaway theme more than the hero does.
- **Sally Rewards placement is smart.** Putting the "earn points on every purchase" block right after the hero reminds me my spend is stacking — that's the kind of loyalty-math nudge that works on me.
- **Multiple live offers in one send.** BOGO hair care, BOGO nail polish, and the $39.99 GiGi wax bundle all have visible CTAs. The value density is real even if the edit is loose.
- **Model matches the target demo.** The hero model — long, styled blonde hair, neutral background — is relevant to the hair care offer and reads young-ish female, which is right for me.

## What's weak

- **Clippers and trimmers at the bottom.** I cannot stress this enough: in an email titled "summer getaway" sent to a beauty subscriber, a "20% off clippers" section reads like a list-blast mistake. Ulta would have segmented this out.
- **Subject line offers nothing.** "Ready, Set, Getaway ☀️" tells me zero about what's inside. The Glossier "Up to 25% off ends tonight!" send I audited same week is annoying for other reasons, but at least I knew what I was clicking into.
- **Way too many sections.** Hair care → Rewards → Nail polish → Travel nail → Wax bundle → Hair color → Clippers. That's seven distinct beats. Drunk Elephant pulled a vague subject too, but at least their email had a single clear through-line once I opened.
- **No loyalty-tier personalization.** No mention of my Rewards status, no "as a member" framing, no tier unlock. Sephora's Beauty Insider sends feel like they know who I am. This feels like a mailer to "everyone."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the clippers section entirely from this audience segment.** Or create a separate send for pro/barber customers. Keeping it here actively confuses the summer beauty packing-list narrative and signals no segmentation is happening.
- 2. **Put the offer in the subject line.** "BOGO 50% hair care, ends June 20" tells me everything in seven words. "Ready, Set, Getaway" tells me nothing. Swap the pun for the deal — that's the single highest-lift change here.
- 3. **Trim to three sections max.** Hero hair care → Nail travel capsule → One wildcard (wax bundle OR hair color). The email is doing too many things and none of them feel curated.
- 4. **Give the Rewards block actual stakes.** "Earn points on every purchase" is table stakes copy. Tell me how many points I'd earn on the BOGO purchase, or what reward threshold I'm close to. That's the math I'd actually stop and read.
- 5. **Add a tight deadline counter or "ends tomorrow" flag** in the email body — June 20 is mentioned but visually easy to skip at scroll speed.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nail travel pack, GiGi wax bundle, and more — all ending soon`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: `Founders Grotesk` declared with no `@font-face` rule**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
Six `@font-face` blocks load Open Sans only. `Founders Grotesk` is never declared, so all anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. If Founders Grotesk is intentional, it requires a hosted `@font-face` declaration pointing to a CDN-hosted file (not Google Fonts, which doesn't carry it).

**Issue: Open Sans loaded at version `v17` (2019)**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Google Fonts currently serves v40+. v17 still resolves, but hardcoded version URLs bypass gstatic's automatic update path. If Google retires v17 assets, the font silently disappears. Prefer the Google Fonts CSS API URL or self-host.

**Issue: `font-display: swap` in email context**
All six `@font-face` blocks declare `font-display: swap`. Email clients do not implement the CSS Font Loading API — this property is ignored in every tested client and adds dead bytes.

**Issue: `img { white-space: pre; }`**
`white-space` is not a valid property on replaced elements (`<img>`). It has no rendering effect but is noise. Not harmful, but indicates template cruft.

**Positive signals:**
- `[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset present — prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers/dates inside anchor text.
- `u ~ div .pattern { min-width: 100vw !important; }` — correct Gmail clipping/dark-mode hack.
- VML namespaces (`xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`) present for Outlook VML background image support.
- `X-UA-Compatible: IE=edge` inside conditional comment — correct Outlook rendering target.

**Cannot assess from truncated source:** `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `alt` attributes on images, color contrast ratios.
