# The ultimate beauty besties 🤝🩷
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's where we are with e.l.f. since May 15th — and I have to give them this: they found a real calendar hook with National Best Friends Day, and the "BFe.l.F.s" wordplay actually made me smirk. The layout is clean, every duo has a price anchored right in the headline copy, and the savings badges give it a deal-board energy that e.l.f. has earned the right to lean into at these price points.
- But here's my problem: I just opened "Must-duos for your skin 👯‍♀️" *yesterday* and scored it 9/10 for basically the same concept — curated product pairings, same structure, same tone. Running a duos concept back-to-back is a creative traffic jam. The BFF Day hook is the only thing that separates these two emails, and if I'd been skimming I might not have even noticed they weren't the same send. Compare that to ILIA's "Meet the Soft Focus family," which landed as a purposeful family consolidation because it had a launch sequence behind it — this feels more like the calendar drove the brief.
- I'd mark it read, but I wouldn't click immediately. The duo I'm most interested in (smokeshow soulmates, $10) I already put on a mental list from yesterday's email. The *only* thing that might pull me to tap today is the BFF Day urgency — if this deal expires tonight, that's an actual reason to act. But nothing on screen tells me it does.

## What's working

- **"BFe.l.F.s" wordplay is genuinely clever** — feels on-brand and earns the BFF Day hook rather than just slapping a holiday sticker on a product grid.
- **Prices anchored in headlines** — "smokeshow soulmates, $10" puts the number before you even clock the product, exactly how I scan deal emails on my phone.
- **Savings badges ($5 off) add instant value signal** — not buried in fine print, sitting right on the product image where my eye lands.
- **Clean vertical stack, no render issues** — the three-duo structure loads correctly, hierarchy is obvious, nothing fights for attention.
- **National Best Friends Day is a real, timely hook** — unlike Charlotte Tilbury's "Beach Bag Glow-Up" from June 7 which was seasonal-generic, this email has a specific date tied to it.

## What's weak

- **Day-after-duos creative fatigue** — "Must-duos for your skin 👯‍♀️" was yesterday. Running the same conceptual territory back-to-back erodes the signal on both sends.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are completely generic** — at this point in the email, "Shop Smokeshow Duo" or "Get the $10 Pair" would do so much more work than a three-word button anyone ignores.
- **No loyalty angle anywhere** — e.l.f. has a rewards program; showing me points-on-purchase or a members-only early-access note would do something Ulta and Sephora do better in almost every send.
- **No expiration date** — if this is a BFF *Day* deal, tell me it ends at midnight. The urgency is implied by the holiday but never confirmed, which takes pressure off the click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the hero** — one line: "Offer ends tonight at midnight." National Best Friends Day urgency is built-in and credible; use it. This single change probably lifts click rate the most.
- 2. **Make CTAs duo-specific** — "Shop the $10 Smokeshow Duo" instead of "SHOP NOW." Each section already names the pair; the button should close the loop.
- 3. **Surface the rewards math** — even a line like "Earn X points on each duo" converts a browser-window shopper into a loyalty-stacker. This is the gap versus every strong Ulta send I've reviewed lately.
- 4. **On the creative calendar: don't run back-to-back duo concepts** — swap yesterday's "Must-duos for skin" to a skincare-specific audience segment and save the full BFF Day email as a one-off.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your beauty BFF just got cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three e.l.f. pairs worth sharing — today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Soulmate mascara. $10. Ends tonight.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics "Beauty Besties" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout built in Beefree/Braze with a 640px fixed container. A structural anomaly — a complete nested `nl-container` email tree inside an `html_block` div — is the most significant rendering risk; everything else is minor or unverifiable from the truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redirect chain wraps destination URLs completely.** All CTAs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` using Braze's encoded link-wrapping scheme. The final destination and any UTM parameters are not inspectable from source — only verifiable by following the redirect chain.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the hero anchor** (`<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">`): This explicitly removes the CTA from keyboard tab order. Not a deliverability issue, but see §3.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Nested full email structure inside `html_block`.** `block-1` contains a complete second `nl-container` → `row-1` → `row-content` → `column` → `image_block` tree rendered inside a `<div>` inside the outer table. This is a Beefree "HTML block" artifact where a sub-template was embedded wholesale. Some clients will re-parse the inner table structure independently, causing double-rendering or broken column widths.

**Empty spacer blocks.** `block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4` each contain only a `&nbsp;`-equivalent empty `<div>` with no height or padding attributes set. If spacing is intentional, explicit `height` or `padding` attributes on the `<td>` are more reliable across Outlook versions than empty divs.

**`<title></title>` is empty.** Some assistive technologies surface the `<title>` element; not a hard failure but easily filled.

**Google Fonts `<link>` tag** (`fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans`) is inside a `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` comment. Gmail strips `<link>` tags; Apple Mail and most modern clients will load it. The Arial fallback is present and correct.

**`#MessageViewBody a { text-decoration: none }` global rule** suppresses underlines on all links sitewide in Yahoo/AOL clients. For users relying on underline as a link affordance, this is an accessibility flag.

**`tabindex="-1"` on hero link** (repeated from §2): keyboard-only users cannot navigate to the primary CTA.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (`{{`, `*|...|*`, `<%=`) are visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence or absence of personalization in below-fold content.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe / Authentication)

**Source is truncated — footer not present.** CAN-SPAM mandates physical mailing address and a functional unsubscribe mechanism in every commercial email. These are typically in the footer; they cannot be confirmed or denied from what was provided. Flag for manual verification.

**Sender domain:** `makeup@m.elfcosmetics.com` (subdomain `m.` consistent with a dedicated sending domain). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML source alone — requires header inspection (e.g., `Authentication-Results` header from the received copy).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

Destination URLs are fully obfuscated inside Braze's encoded redirect strings. UTM parameter presence must be verified by tracing at least one link through the redirect chain (e.g., `curl -IL "https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/hN0Yjv7..."`) to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended before the final destination.

If UTMs are injected by the Braze redirect handler at click time rather than baked into the source URL, they will not appear in the HTML — this is a supported Braze pattern but should be confirmed against the campaign setup.

---
