# This could be your moment, friend
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is genuinely the most bare-bones send I've gotten from ILIA all week — no hero image, no product shot, not even a logo up top, just a wall of plain text that reads like a personal email from "Marissa." And this is the SECOND ILIA email in my inbox today, after the "makeup that makes your skin better" send I already logged this morning — so now I've got two ILIA emails same-day, plus the 9/13 and 9/12 VIP pushes. That's four VIP-flavored sends in three days.
- The offer itself is actually solid on paper — 20% off sitewide, a free Multi-Stick, a free Velvet Makeup Bag at $100+, up to 40% off lip and cheek, and a "20% off forever" subscription hook — but it's buried in bullet-point plain text with zero visual hierarchy, so my eye doesn't land anywhere. Compare this to Glossier's Boy Brow send, which I called out for having real product photography and reviews instead of screaming banners — this ILIA email goes the opposite direction of both the banner-scream AND the polish. It's just... text. Sephora's "Chat" emails at least have a header and a points callout; this doesn't even clear that bar.
- Would I click? Maybe, because the 20%-forever-subscription line is genuinely interesting to me as someone who does the points math obsessively. But I wouldn't forward this to a friend — there's nothing show-worthy here, and honestly this reads like it skipped the design step entirely.

## What's working

- The 20% off sitewide + two stacked free gifts (Multi-Stick, Velvet Makeup Bag at $100+) is a real, specific, generous offer — better than Ulta's usual one-item-only coupon mechanic.
- "20% off forever" via subscription is a clever loyalty hook that speaks directly to someone like me who's always doing the points/savings math.
- "VIPs also get redeemable points, free shipping every order, and early access to launches" directly hits my loyalty-program radar.
- Signed by "Marissa (from ILIA)" gives it a personal, human feel rather than a corporate blast.

## What's weak

- Zero visual design — no hero image, no logo, no product photography, no color. It looks like a text-only fallback, not a finished campaign email.
- No sense of scroll structure or hierarchy — the offer, the gifts, and the CTA all read at the same visual weight, so nothing pops.
- This is ILIA's fourth VIP-sale-flavored email in three days (9/12 "VIP Sale starts now," 9/13 "VIPs get the best + 20% off," 9/14 "makeup that makes your skin better," and now this) — the fatigue is real and I'm starting to tune it out.
- Subject "This could be your moment, friend" is vague filler — doesn't mention the sale, the discount, or the gifts, so it undersells everything in the body.
- No product imagery means I can't see shade range, texture, or anything relevant to actually deciding to buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add actual visual design — a hero banner with the Multi-Stick and Velvet Makeup Bag photographed, plus the 20%-off callout as a graphic, not just a text line. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Put the offer in the subject line instead of vague "your moment, friend" copy — I should know it's 20% off + free gifts before I even open.
- 3. Cut the send frequency — four VIP-themed emails in three days is how I start marking ILIA as read without opening, the same fatigue pattern I flagged on Sally Beauty's repeated Appiversary well.
- 4. Turn "20% off forever" subscription into its own visually distinct callout box — it's the most differentiated offer here and it's getting lost in a bullet list.
- 5. Add a single clear CTA button ("Shop the VIP Sale") instead of inline text links scattered through the copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off + 2 free gifts — VIP Sale is live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free Multi-Stick is waiting (VIP Sale)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free gifts, 20% off sitewide, and points that never expire.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two free gifts, one sale — here's how VIP math works out.`
