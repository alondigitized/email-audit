# Hi again, 25% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. I need everyone to sit with that number for a second. This is email eleven from Drunk Elephant Friends & Family, and yesterday they sent me both "Last day for 25% off!" AND "Adios, 25% off..." — the farewell AND the door-slam. And now today it's "Hi again." They literally said goodbye and came back. That's not a campaign arc, that's a hostage situation.
- The email itself is technically solid. Countdown timer at 12 minutes and change, clean hero, the "MASK ME ANYTHING" GWP hook is genuinely smart (spend $70+, get a full-size B-Balm), and the product grid at the bottom is well-organized. I will say: "TIME TO STOCK UP. Everything is 25% off!" is actually the right framing for a late-campaign send — it gives me permission to be transactional instead of pretending this is a discovery moment.
- But that countdown timer. It's showing under 13 minutes. This sale has sent me eleven emails over multiple days and extended at least once after the "Adios" send. If that timer is a rolling evergreen and not a real deadline, that's a dark pattern I won't forgive. I'm a Sephora Rouge member — I know how to read an urgency hook, and this one feels manufactured. I'd mark-as-read and maybe come back to restock Protini, but I'm not sprinting to a SHOP NOW button I've seen ten times already.

## What's working

- **The "MASK ME ANYTHING" GWP section is the freshest thing in this email.** A full-size B-Balm for $70+ spend is a concrete incentive I haven't seen framed this way in the campaign — this is the one thing I'd actually click through for.
- **The countdown timer creates urgency even if I'm suspicious of it.** Twelve minutes is visceral. Sephora does the same trick and it works on me every time.
- **"TIME TO STOCK UP" is exactly the right late-campaign positioning.** It drops the pretense that I'm discovering DE for the first time and treats me like the repeat buyer I am.
- **Product grid is clean and specific** — Protini Refillable, Lala Retro, Slaai, Marula Face Oil. These are the heroes, no filler.
- **No render bugs.** Layout reads fine top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **The "Hi again" energy after "Adios" is either very charming or deeply annoying.** It's the latter at email eleven. The self-awareness reads as smug, not endearing.
- **The subscribe-to-save upsell is buried in the subheader greeting.** "Sign up for Subscribe to Save and get 25% off your first subscription order" is a completely different offer from the F&F deal, and slipping it into the "HI FRIEND" text feels like a bait-and-switch wrapper on a loyalty pitch.
- **That countdown timer cannot possibly be real.** If the sale ended in 12 minutes of this email sending, it expired before most people opened it. After watching this campaign extend through "Adios" into "Hi again," I have zero faith in this deadline.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing too much heavy lifting.** Five different "SHOP NOW" buttons, none of them differentiated. Even e.l.f. — which sends just as frequently — bothers to write "Shop the Drop" or category-specific CTAs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the subscribe-to-save hook from the hero.** If DE wants to push subscriptions, that's a separate send with a real headline. Smuggling it into the F&F greeting undermines both offers.
- 2. **Make the countdown timer verifiable or drop it entirely.** A real deadline that proves out builds trust; a fake one that rolls over destroys it. Sally Beauty's "Okay, Last Chance 😉" worked because the self-awareness was honest — DE saying "Adios" then "Hi again" with a 12-minute clock just reads as manipulative.
- 3. **Give the "MASK ME ANYTHING" section top billing.** The GWP with spend threshold is genuinely new information in this campaign — it should be the hero, not buried below the sitewide 25% banner I've seen ten times.
- 4. **Make the SHOP NOW CTAs product-specific.** "Shop Protini," "Shop Marula Oil" — Sephora does category-level CTAs in their end-of-day sale emails and it drives more targeted clicks than a generic "SHOP NOW" grid.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a. Duplicate font loading — triple-request pattern for BrownBold/BrownLight/BrownThin**

Each of the three fonts is loaded via three separate mechanisms:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/{hash}?family=Brown+Bold">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/{hash}?family=Brown+Bold)` inside `<style>`
3. An explicit `@font-face` block inside `<style>` pointing to the same font files at `db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/{hash}.woff2`

The `<link>` and `@import` reference the same CSS stylesheet URL — that alone is a duplicate request. The redundant inline `@font-face` adds a third load path. This wastes round-trips and can cause render delays in clients that do honor web fonts (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS).

**b. HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block**

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- -->` is HTML comment syntax, not CSS. These appear to be dead/commented-out responsive rules. In strict CSS parsers these are silently ignored; in some email preprocessors they can leak into rendered output or cause parse errors. Dead code should be removed, not commented out.

**c. `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Disables user pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility violation — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of function.

**d. Global `line-height: 100%` reset**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applied to all elements. A 100% line-height computes to 1× the element's own font-size, which is tighter than the WCAG 1.4.12 minimum of 1.5× for body text. This overrides any web font's natural metrics and can cause descenders to clip.

**e. `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

Suppresses the user agent's automatic text scaling on mobile. Acceptable as a narrow reset on the `body` element (standard email practice); applying it via the `*` selector to all elements is over-broad.

**f. Third-party font CDN dependency**

`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — not controlled by Drunk Elephant. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`. The inconsistency means three of four font weights have an external single point of failure.
