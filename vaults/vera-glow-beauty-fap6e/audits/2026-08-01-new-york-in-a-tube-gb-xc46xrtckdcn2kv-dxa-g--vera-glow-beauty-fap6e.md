# New York in a tube
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so today Glossier's actually doing something other than reshuffling the same GWP bundle — this is a real launch, a new "Juicy Apple" flavor of Balm Dotcom dressed up as an ode to NYC, complete with yellow-cab hero shot and a fishnet bag of apples that's honestly a cute piece of creative. After thirteen-plus emails since July 7th where half of them were just "here's what's in the bag" flat lays, seeing an actual new SKU with its own "why you'll love it" section feels like a small relief.
- That said, I clocked the Red Mini Beauty Bag GWP still sitting at the bottom under "STILL ON" — same $75+ threshold that's been running since before 7/30's "Our go-to summer lip?" send, which I gave a 9 specifically because that one buried the GWP mid-scroll behind a tutorial. This one does the same thing structurally: new-launch energy up top, recycled GWP tacked on at the end like an afterthought. It's a long scroll too — hero, "why you'll love it," ingredient/flavor block, two-up merch grid, then the bag — which is the same fatigue I called out on Sephora's "Hot on Social" waterfall (6/10) this week.
- Would I click? Probably, just to see the Applebaby keychain up close — it's genuinely a fun limited-edition piece of merch. Would I forward it? No. Nothing here beats what I've already seen from Glossier, and there's zero rewards/loyalty math for me to chew on, which Ulta's "Lucky you" 20%-off send (9/10) absolutely nailed this week with a real barcode discount mechanic.

## What's working

- The yellow-cab hero with "Glossier ❤️ Spring for New York" script overlay is a genuinely fresh visual — first new hero concept I've seen from them in weeks of near-identical flat lays.
- "Why you'll love it" actually names the benefit (hydrating, addictive flavor, sheer clear tint) instead of just showing product on a table — more than I got from 7/27's "What's in a bag's balm" which had zero offer or copy depth.
- The fishnet bag of apples for the "juicy apple in a tube" section is a clever, on-theme prop shot that ties the flavor name to the visual without being cheesy.
- "Shop now" is repeated consistently on every section (five separate CTAs) so there's no ambiguity about what to tap no matter where I stop scrolling.
- The Applebaby Keychain being flagged "Limited edition" is a real urgency lever, more honest than manufactured discount countdowns.

## What's weak

- The Red Mini Beauty Bag GWP is the same offer that's been running since at least mid-July — "STILL ON" basically admits it's a rerun, and it's buried at the very bottom after five other CTAs.
- Zero loyalty or points language anywhere — no rewards tier callout, nothing acknowledging I'm a repeat customer, which stings when Ulta and Sephora both lean hard into member math in their sends.
- Subject line "New York in a tube" gives me no product category signal at all — I'd have no idea this is a lip balm launch until I open it.
- It's a long email — hero, ingredient block, flavor block, merch grid, GWP footer — and by the third "Shop now" I'm scroll-fatigued, same complaint I had about Sephora's waterfall structure this week.
- No mention of skin tone/lip tone shade-matching even though the tint is "universally flattering clear" — a throwaway claim, not real personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the subject line with the product category so it reads as a launch, not a mystery — right now it undersells the fact this is a NEW flavor drop.
- 2. Cut the GWP section entirely or move it up near the hero — burying a recycled offer at the bottom after five CTAs reads as an afterthought, not a incentive.
- 3. Add one loyalty-program line — even "Rewards members, this counts toward your next perk" would close the gap versus Sephora/Ulta's tier-math emails.
- 4. Trim the scroll — combine "why you'll love it" and "juicy apple in a tube" into one section; they're saying the same thing twice with different photos.
- 5. Show the tint on an actual lip swatch across a couple of skin tones instead of just claiming "universally flattering" — Drunk Elephant's "Lippe Lock" send got real mileage out of visible before/after lip shots.
