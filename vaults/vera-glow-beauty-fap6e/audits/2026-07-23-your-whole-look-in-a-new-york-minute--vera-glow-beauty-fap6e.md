# Your whole look, in a New York Minute
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Email eleven. Eleven emails in sixteen days, and Glossier is closing out the I ❤️ NY campaign with the "complete your look" move — the routine walkthrough that's supposed to make you feel like you need all five products to leave the house. Boy Brow, Cloud Paint, Lip Line, Balm Dotcom, a scent close. It's a classic beauty editorial funnel and Glossier executes it cleanly. The close-up skin photography is gorgeous, the layout stacks well on mobile, and "Choose your shade" on the lip section is the smartest CTA they've written all campaign.
- But here's my honest reaction: I've been tracking this campaign since July 7 and I am emotionally at the end of my rope with it. The GWP smiley bag reveal on July 21 was the high point — I gave it an 8, it felt like a real payoff. This feels like the hangover email. The "In a New York Minute Set" at the top is promising but I can't see any bundled price or savings spelled out, so Glossier is asking me to shop a routine with zero financial incentive. No deal. No points event. No urgency that's actually real. Just vibes.
- I'd scroll through, maybe tap "Choose your shade" on the Lip Line because I've been eyeing the formula, but I would not convert. Wouldn't forward it either — my friends already know about this campaign because I've been texting them about it for two weeks.

## What's working

- The close-up skin texture photography is still doing its job — dreamy and aspirational without feeling retouched into oblivion, which matches why I follow Glossier in the first place
- Stacking the five products as a sequential routine (brows → cheeks → lips → balm → scent) gives the email a logical through-line that makes the upsell feel editorial, not transactional
- "Choose your shade" on the lip step is specific and action-oriented — it knows I'm going to get stuck on shade selection and meets me there
- The "In a New York Minute Set" bundle placement at the hero is smart; leading with the set before showing the individual products primes me to see value in buying together
- Layout is clean, no render issues, text reads without zooming — compared to Ulta's July 18 debacle where I got an email announcing a Beauty Break that had already ended, this is technically competent

## What's weak

- No price, no deal, no percentage off anywhere I can see — for a campaign that's been running for 16 days, the "complete the look" email really needed a bundle discount or a loyalty incentive to close the loop, and this doesn't have one
- The smiley face + wave hand emoji at the very bottom feels like a brand tic that's lost meaning after 11 emails; compare ILIA's editorial black-and-white lash close-up from July 21 that landed fresh every time
- "New York Minute" as urgency is a wordplay that reads clever the first time but this is email 11 — the NYC theming is wallpaper at this point
- No social proof visible anywhere — reviews, ratings, "best-seller" tags — nothing to tip a fence-sitter into buying the set vs. picking one item
- Eleven emails in sixteen days is not right; even e.l.f.'s "6 HOURS ONLY" flash-send cadence felt more earned than this slow burn that never fully extinguishes

## Recommendations

- The single highest-lift change: **add a bundle price or limited-time discount on the "In a New York Minute Set."** If the set saves me even $10 vs. buying five items individually, say it in the hero. That's what converts a routine email into a purchase email. Everything else is polish.
- 1. Put "Save $X on the In a New York Minute Set" or a percentage in the hero block — the set concept is doing a lot of work but the math isn't visible
- 2. Add a "Limited quantities / ships before August 1" or similar credible deadline — not wordplay, a real date
- 3. Swap the bottom emoji sign-off for a single quote from a real reviewer on the Balm Dotcom since that's the hero product of this whole campaign
- 4. Pull back to 2x/week cadence at minimum — the Sephora 4X points hair care email from earlier this month knew when to be quiet between sends; Glossier doesn't

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid open-tracking span element**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KY63Y1XQS020CDPVRESND4Y1_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This is a non-standard Klaviyo open-tracking pattern that achieves nothing functionally (no image request, no CSS load) and will be silently ignored by browsers. It may trigger spam/security scanners that flag attribute misuse. Klaviyo's standard open pixel is a `<img>` tag — this variant is atypical.

**Click tracking domain**: All links route through `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo's click-tracking domain). No issues with the domain itself, but UTM parameter passthrough cannot be verified from the truncated source (see §6).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader padding technique — functional but noisy**
The preheader hidden span uses Unicode U+034F (COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER `͏`) repeated ~160 times to pad preview text and prevent body bleed-through. This is a known workaround, but some aggressive spam filters flag high densities of non-printable Unicode in hidden text.

**Minified class names** (`.a`, `.b`, `.bf`, `.q`, etc.) are aggressively obfuscated. Not a rendering issue, but complicates post-send debugging and QA diffs.
