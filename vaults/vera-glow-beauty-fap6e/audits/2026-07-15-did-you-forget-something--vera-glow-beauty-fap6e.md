# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Déjà vu, but not the fun kind. Skechers ran this exact subject line on July 1st — 14 days ago — and here it is again, word-for-word. My first thought opening the email body wasn't "oh, I forgot something," it was "wait, did I accidentally open my archive?" The automation is either not tracking what it's already fired, or it is and someone decided I forgot the email too.
- The creative itself is functional: the gold confetti hero is clean, three specific Slip-ins get surfaced with actual product names, and "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" runs as the primary CTA twice so you can't miss it. The 20% off with CODE: SUMMER for Skechers Plus members is the most actionable thing here. But it lives in a mid-email banner below the product photos — completely decoupled from the hero — so the incentive and the action never meet in the same scroll zone. And that "Mommy & Me: Glide-Step Pro" sitting right in the hero carousel? I did not browse a matching mom-daughter sneaker. That one placement tells me this isn't actually a triggered abandonment pull from my cart — it's a batch blast with the subject dressed up to feel personal.
- I'm not clicking today. I'd mark it read and wait for something I actually browsed. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- The gold/confetti hero is visually distinct — contrast is high, the eye lands immediately on "READY TO CHECK OUT?" without having to hunt
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA appears twice, hero and mid-email — structural repetition is actually right here
- Three named products with visible labels (Glide-Step Pro, BOBS Sport Solid, Mommy & Me) beats the "shop our favorites" homepage dump format
- CODE: SUMMER is right there in the offer banner — no mystery link needed
- afterpay and Klarna showing at the bottom are quiet signals I genuinely appreciate; installment options matter to me on $80+ footwear

## What's weak

- Identical subject line to July 1st — same campaign, 14-day cadence, no creative variation whatsoever; ILIA did three brown-mascara-related sends in under a month and every one felt like a distinct drop, this is the opposite
- "Mommy & Me" in the hero carousel when I didn't browse it blows the personalization premise the subject line promises
- The 20% off offer is buried below the fold on mobile, separated from the "complete your order" action — if the code is the hook, it belongs in the hero
- No deadline on CODE: SUMMER, so the urgency the subject attempts to manufacture has no structural support
- Six "best seller" thumbnails below the main product block are too small to read label text without zooming — they feel like fill

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the duplicate subject line** — automated sequences need a suppression check that stops resending the same subject within a rolling 30-day window; the July 1 send actually worked (scored an 8) because it switched from the "limited availability" template; recycling it two weeks later undercuts that win entirely
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Slip-ins are still waiting — 20% off inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer code, expiring soon 👟 check your cart`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus members: code SUMMER takes 20% off before the season ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three styles you looked at — plus a member deal worth using`
- 2. **Pull the offer into the hero** — "20% off with code SUMMER" should be a subhead or a callout within the gold hero banner, not four scrolls down in a separate segment
- 3. **Segment out the Mommy & Me** — if I didn't browse it, it shouldn't anchor the carousel; this category skews family-purchase intent and I'm not there
- 4. **Add a hard expiry to the code** — even "code valid through Sunday" would give this urgency without screaming countdown-timer spam
- 5. **Trim the best sellers grid or make it readable** — six thumbnails with micro-label text does nothing for tap conversions; two featured styles with readable names would convert better than six unreadable ones

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is blank. Some email clients (notably Gmail web) surface the `<title>` value in tab labels and accessibility trees. A descriptive title (e.g., `"Your Skechers cart is waiting"`) would improve screen reader context.

**Preheader padding technique** — The preheader div uses two distinct invisible-character types for length padding:
- U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER (`͏`) — 150 repetitions
- U+00AD SOFT HYPHEN (`­`) — ~200 repetitions

Soft hyphens (`­`) can render as visible hyphens in some obscure clients and webmail renderers. The safer, universally compatible approach is to use only `&zwnj;` (`&#8204;`) or `&nbsp;` sequences. Not a critical failure but worth standardizing.

**`#MessageViewBody a` rule** (`color:inherit; text-decoration:none`) — This CSS targets Outlook Web App's message container and suppresses all link underlines globally within that scope. If any links lack explicit color styling in their inline attributes, they will inherit the surrounding text color and become visually invisible as links in OWA. Verify that all CTA links have explicit `color` set inline.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule** — Present and correct. Prevents iOS Mail from auto-linking phone numbers and addresses found in body copy.

**MSO conditional comments** — Present and well-formed (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, `<!--<![endif]-->`). VML namespace and `PixelsPerInch` / `AllowPNG` Office document settings are included — correct for Outlook rendering fidelity.
