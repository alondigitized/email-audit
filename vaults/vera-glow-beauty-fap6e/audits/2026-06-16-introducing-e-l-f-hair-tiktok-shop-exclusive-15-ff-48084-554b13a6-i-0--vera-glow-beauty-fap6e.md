# Introducing e.l.f. Hair 💖 TikTok Shop Exclusive
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Day seven. I counted. Between the main e.l.f. account and e.l.f. SKIN that dropped June 14, this is the seventh consecutive day I've had an e.l.f. send in my inbox. And now there's a third entity — e.l.f. Hair. I want to give this a fair shake because a brand launching an entirely new category IS genuinely newsworthy, and "WHAT THE H.E.L.F.?" made me snort-laugh, which is more than Sephora's identical back-to-back "50% off select beauty" blasts managed this week.
- The hero does its job — big visual energy, punchy wordplay, wild voluminous hair that makes the category instantly legible. The bundle pricing ($29, $22, $22) is displayed front-and-center, which I appreciate. But the TikTok Shop Exclusive framing hits different when you're a Sephora Rouge member who does the points math before every checkout. I'm not dropping e.l.f. purchases on TikTok Shop when I could be stacking Sephora points or Ulta cash back. The whole email is essentially pointing me toward a platform that zeros out my loyalty equity.
- I'd skim this, bookmark it mentally, and not click today. Maybe when it lands at Ulta or Sephora proper. No forward — too niche for my group chat unless one of them is specifically a TikTok Shop person.

## What's working

- **"WHAT THE H.E.L.F.?" is a genuinely great headline.** It earns the hero real estate — wordplay, surprise, and category clarity in four words. Charlotte Tilbury and their nth "Shop NEW! Exclusive on the App!" subject line could take a lesson here.
- **Three bundles, three prices, no math required.** Frizz To Rizz Trio $29, 2-In-Wonder Trio $22, The Haze Gang $22 — I can evaluate the value prop in ten seconds flat without clicking anywhere.
- **Color palette is confident and category-coded.** Lime, lavender, teal, dark hero — it reads "hair energy" not "recycled skincare template." That's not a given; Glossier's sale emails this week all used the same pink-on-white regardless of category.
- **The "our new 10 in hair" section positions a real hero SKU.** One lead product, flagged as the anchor of the launch. That's editorial thinking, not just a product grid dump.
- **Model and aesthetic match the TikTok/Gen Z-adjacent aesthetic e.l.f. lives in.** The chaotic-hair energy is on-brand and speaks to the same crowd that made the setting spray go viral.

## What's weak

- **TikTok Shop Exclusive is doing the heaviest lifting in the subject line — and it's the biggest friction point.** I am a Sephora Rouge member. Every dollar I spend outside Sephora or Ulta is a dollar not earning points toward a birthday gift or a Beauty Insider event redemption. The word "exclusive" to a loyalty-tier shopper means "you'll pay full price with no stack."
- **"Top Picks for You" section still shows EYES, LIPS, FACE, SKINCARE before HAIR.** This is clearly a canned recommendation module that wasn't customized for the hair launch email. I'm here for the hair drop — why are you recommending me concealer?
- **Day seven is too many.** The e.l.f. SKIN email June 14 was already a second sender identity hitting me on the same day as main e.l.f. Now there's a third. The brand history of consecutive sends is starting to feel like spam regardless of how good the individual creative is.
- **No loyalty or points angle for non-TikTok-Shop shoppers.** ILIA's "BFF + SPF" email at least acknowledged its customer relationship implicitly through editorial voice. This email treats me like a TikTok user discovering e.l.f. for the first time, not a brand loyalist who's been in the ecosystem for years.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an "also available at Ulta / Sephora soon" or "join the waitlist" line.** The TikTok Shop exclusive is a valid marketing play, but you're burning your loyal customer base to do it. One line of relief copy — "not on TikTok? Get notified when it drops at Ulta" — keeps the loyalist in the funnel.
- 2. **Strip the EYES / LIPS / FACE / FACE / SKINCARE recommendation module from a hair launch email.** Replace it with a single "Complete the hair routine" cross-sell that stays in-category.
- 3. **Throttle the send cadence to 3x/week across all e.l.f. entities combined.** A new sub-brand hitting my inbox on day seven of consecutive sends is day seven — it doesn't matter that it's a different sender name.
- 4. **Give the "10 in hair" product a dedicated hero moment.** Right now it's a dark-background section below the fold. For what appears to be your launch hero SKU, it should be the email, not a scroll-past.
- 5. **Add a "Save $X when you bundle" callout to the bundle tiles.** The prices are there but the savings aren't. ILIA does this cleanly; Glossier made it feel editorial this week. The math is Vera's love language.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: Nested `nl-container` inside `html_block`**
`html_block block-1` wraps a full email skeleton — its own `nl-container → row-1 → row-content → column-1` tree — inside a `<div>` that is itself already inside the outer `nl-container`. This double-nesting is structurally anomalous and indicates the body content was pasted as a raw HTML block inside a Beefree/similar template rather than rendered natively. In strict clients (Outlook, Apple Mail) this can produce layout breaks.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several clients (Outlook desktop, Samsung Mail) display the `<title>` value in the preview pane or window chrome. Empty leaves a blank or falls back to the subject line inconsistently.

**Logo link is removed from keyboard tab order:**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Setting `tabindex="-1"` on the primary hero CTA removes it from keyboard navigation, failing WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1.

**Link underlines globally suppressed:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
In Gmail (which wraps content in `#MessageViewBody`), all links lose color and underline, making them visually indistinguishable from body text. This is a common but real accessibility and click-rate issue.

**Google Fonts will not load in Gmail or Outlook:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
```
Gmail strips external `<link>` tags; Outlook ignores them entirely. The declared fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` will render instead — acceptable, but the font load is a no-op for the majority of clients.
