# A winning Wimbledon routine
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- ILIA showed up with "shade. set. match" and I will not pretend that didn't make me smirk — that's genuinely good wordplay, and the Wimbledon timing is impeccable given the tournament is literally running right now. The complexion lineup they're showcasing (Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40, Skin Blur Concealer, Soft Focus Finishing Powder) makes complete sense for "five hours outside in the heat" territory, and that testimonial quote — "[my] base stayed fresh after 5hrs of watching tennis in the heat" — is the kind of social proof that actually lands because it maps to a real scenario I can picture myself in.
- Here's my issue: the comeback tote is *back again*. This is the third ILIA email in four days where the tote has made an appearance — "PSA—mini totes are back" on July 6, "A mini mentality" yesterday, and now the tote is doing a "comeback" encore today. I have tote fatigue. The tote is clearly their summer tentpole GWP, but showing it three times in four days flattens the excitement. It felt like a plot twist on July 6. Now it just feels like they ran out of ideas for the footer.
- I would click through to the Skin Tint. I would not forward this to a friend — yesterday's "A mini mentality" was a cleaner, more focused send. Mark as read after clicking one product link.

## What's working

- **"shade. set. match" is a genuine ear-worm** — the shade/set/match triple play on tennis scoring is the kind of copy that earns a pause-and-reread on a scroll.
- **Wimbledon timing is airtight** — the tournament is live, the heat-survival context is real, this doesn't feel manufactured.
- **That testimonial is doing real work** — "5hrs of watching tennis in the heat" is specific and experiential; this is what Drunk Elephant does well with their clean-girl social proof and ILIA is matching that energy here.
- **Product lineup is clear and contextually earned** — SPF 40 Skin Tint in an outdoor summer email isn't lazy, it's actually correct; Glossier would have done this with vaguer aspirational copy and no product anchors.
- **Visual hierarchy reads fast** — tennis model, hero copy, quote, products, tote; the eye knows where to go.

## What's weak

- **Tote fatigue is real** — three tote appearances in four days from the same sender is too many; the "comeback" framing no longer lands when it never actually left.
- **No discount or concrete offer anchor** — Sally Beauty was running "20% off, last day" pressure the same week; ILIA is asking me to spend without any price incentive, which is fine for a launch but harder when it's positioned as a "routine" (plural product) buy.
- **The Wimbledon hook doesn't extend to product copy** — once you scroll past the hero, the products are just... the products. No match-day use tips, no SPF reminder in the summer heat framing below the fold.
- **No loyalty signal** — I'm a known ILIA customer and there's zero Rouge/member tier nod (ILIA doesn't have a points program, but a loyalty acknowledgment like "your go-tos" or "your summer wardrobe" would differentiate from a cold subscriber send).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the tote for at least a week** — or make its third appearance earn it by tying it explicitly to a spend threshold visible in the hero ("free tote with $75+"), not just a footer section.
- 2. **Add a heat-survival tip layer to each product** — one line per product that extends the tennis narrative ("reapply SPF Skin Tint at halftime" type energy).
- 3. **CTA copy** — "SHOP GAME FACE" is fine but "Build Your Game Face →" is more active and implies curation, which this routine actually is.
- 4. **Introduce a soft urgency signal** — Wimbledon ends July 13; something like "before the final whistle" gives this a natural shelf life without fake countdown energy.

- **Subject Alt B:** `5 hours outside. Base still going.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `SPF 40 Skin Tint + the rest of your Wimbledon kit — shop the routine`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The complexion edit for outdoor summer heat — built for the long match`
