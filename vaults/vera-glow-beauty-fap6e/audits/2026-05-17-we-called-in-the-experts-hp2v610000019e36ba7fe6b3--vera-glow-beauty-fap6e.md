# WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS 🤝🏽
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- OK so this is the second "WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS 🤝🏽" send from Sephora I've encountered today — same subject line, same date — and whether that's a resend-to-non-openers situation or an A/B test variant, it's the exact thing that shouldn't happen to a Rouge member who opens Sephora emails immediately. Two editorial sends in one cycle with identical subject lines is a data hygiene issue, not a creative choice.
- The email itself is doing something I actually respect: it's a proper "the Chat" editorial drop with a slow-beauty routine breakdown from a featured insider and a makeup artist's three tips. Specific products get named, specific techniques get called out, and the diverse casting reads genuinely Millennial and not focus-grouped. I'm not mad at the content. But I came to this email — and to every Sephora email — with the quiet hope of a points event, an early-access drop, a Rouge moment. This is a magazine column. Drunk Elephant pulled off wordplay subject lines ("When you're bright, you're bright.") in a way that made me feel like the email was for me specifically. This one feels like it's for anyone who reads beauty content, which means it's for no one in particular.
- Would I click? Probably the makeup tips section — the "3 tips, 1 brilliant artist" structure is genuinely strong. Would I forward? No. This is a mark-as-read, wait-for-the-next-Beauty-Insider-event email.

## What's working

- **Specific product callouts in the routine section.** Real product names mentioned in actual routine steps — that's content that earns the scroll. Sephora didn't just say "shop skincare"; someone laid out their actual morning ritual.
- **"3 Makeup Tips, 1 Brilliant Artist" headline structure.** Number + payoff is a proven click hook and it works here. Feels editorial, not sales-floor.
- **Casting is right.** The women featured read diverse, age-appropriate, and authentic for a late-twenties Millennial beauty audience. Charlotte Tilbury sends me very pale, very '90s supermodel energy — Sephora's inclusion effort is visible and I notice it.
- **Beauty Insider tiers at the bottom.** Seeing the ROUGE / VIB / INSIDER BLUSH tier ladder acknowledged in the email body is a small loyalty acknowledgment. It's the floor of personalization, not the ceiling, but it's there.
- **Clean render.** No broken image gaps, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum bleeding through. CT's mobile grid occasionally breaks on my phone — this doesn't.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** I am Rouge tier. I stock up at the Beauty Insider event like it's a national holiday. An email with no points peg, no early-access window, no "featured products at Insider pricing" line is a missed conversion. ILIA's welcome email, which I just gave 9/10, landed during a live Friends + Family window. Sephora — a brand with infinitely more trust equity — sent me two expert content emails today with nothing to redeem. The math is wrong.
- **ALL CAPS subject line is mass-blast energy.** "WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS" reads like a press release, not a note to a loyal member. Drunk Elephant's quieter wordplay approach signals confidence. Screaming in the inbox signals volume-over-relevance.
- **The Running Point / Netflix basketball section is genuinely disorienting.** I had to look twice. A basketball TV show ICYMI block sandwiched between a makeup tutorial and my loyalty tiers? Even if the partnership is real and smart at a brand level, it kills the editorial momentum and reads like a different email got spliced in mid-send.
- **CTAs are text links, not buttons.** "BUILD YOUR ZEN ROUTINE +" and "SHE SPILLS MORE HERE +" work hard in all-caps, but on mobile I'm going to fat-finger around them. Ulta builds real tappable buttons. Sally Beauty's flash-sale sends have cleaner CTA button targets than this.
- **No cadence signal for "the Chat."** I don't know if this drops weekly, monthly, or whenever. Lush has trained me to expect their Saturday drops. ILIA uses the Welcome flow to set expectations. "The Chat" just... appears. That's not how you build a ritual open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach a soft commercial hook to the routine content.** Pull the exact named products from the expert's routine into a mini shop-the-story row with prices and Insider-tier callouts. Even "earn 2x points on these picks through Sunday" would turn this from content-only to content-plus-commerce without breaking the editorial feel.
- 2. **Lowercase the subject.** "We called in the experts 🤝🏽" reads warmer, more editorial, and exactly on-tone for what's inside. The ALL CAPS approach signals a mass blast, which undercuts a Rouge-tier send.
- 3. **Give "the Chat" a cadence hook in the masthead.** "the Chat | Every Tuesday" or "Issue 12" — something that frames this as a subscription rather than a one-off editorial.
- 4. **Pull the Running Point section into its own send or give it real estate.** A compressed ICYMI block is the worst of both worlds: it's not useful as content and it fragments the beauty focus.
- 5. **Upgrade CTAs to real buttons.** Bordered, tappable, with 44px minimum tap target on mobile.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The full morning-to-night ritual, named products and all — plus a makeup artist's best-kept secret`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks user-initiated pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of function. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element — confirmed issue**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value) is present. Screen readers cannot infer language for correct pronunciation. Add `lang="en"` (or `lang="en-US"` to match the `<!-- US shell snippet -->` comment).

**`<title>` is non-descriptive**
```html

```
If this email has a hosted web version, this title provides no context. Low severity, but a missed opportunity for screen readers and browser tabs.
