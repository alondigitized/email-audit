- This is Sephora's usual catalog dump with a cute subject line on top. "Moves fast" promises urgency, and the hero doesn't back it up. Nothing in it speaks to my skin tone or my past buys.
- Would I click? Yes, but only for the 4X moisturizer code, and only after I scrolled past the fragrance hero. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it and leave it as read.

## What's working

- The top banner stacks the Savings Event with the 30% and 10% tier offers right away. That's how I plan my stock-up.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" and "NEW • LIMITED EDITION" tags tell me what's exclusive without making me click.
- Prices are on almost every tile, so I can sort out what's worth it. That's cleaner than Ulta's "shop now" blocks that hide the price.
- The 4X points callout has a code (HYDRATE) and a deadline, 10/21. I can act on that. The barcode at the bottom is handy for in-store use.
- The Kuromi x Touchland tile at $20 is a fun, giftable find.

## What's weak

- The hero is a Valentino fragrance launch at $198 and $170. That's not an everyday purchase for me, and the "moves fast" subject doesn't connect to it.
- The best deal, 4X points on moisturizers, is buried below about nine product tiles. The Rouge tier math should come first.
- "Everyone's Talking About These" is a grab bag of unrelated products. Nothing groups by category, and nothing is personalized to me as a Rouge member.
- It's too long and the product text is tiny on mobile. The legal fine print at the bottom is a wall of text.
- Sephora keeps sending this same layout. Glossier's "STILL ON! Get 20% off best-sellers" put one offer up front, and Sephora opens less cleanly than that.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 4X points moisturizer deal to the top, above the Valentino hero. Make it the hero and put the code and end date right on it.
- 2. Cut the product grid in half, and group what's left by category (skincare, fragrance, makeup).
- 3. Show my tier status and a points-to-reward number near the top, like "You're X points from a reward."
- 4. Use one hero per send. e.l.f.'s setting spray email had three picks with a $11 price on each, and that was easier to shop.
