# LA wildfire resilience—an update
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Okay. I want to start by noting that this is email number... I've lost count, but it's a lot — and after nine, ten, eleven consecutive ILIA sends that were all product launches, collabs, and "don't miss it" urgency plays, this one just pivots hard into CSR territory. It's a wildfire resilience update. Tree planting. 1% for the Planet. Real talk: I respect it, because ILIA has always had this identity — it's not like they invented the cause angle to distract me — but this landing in my inbox right after the Crown Affair collab run and the Skin Tint launches feels like I was mid-sentence at the beauty counter and someone handed me a nonprofit annual report.
- The "Skin Tint Plants Trees" hook at the bottom is the only place where this email remembers that I'm a beauty consumer and not a PTA parent. It ties purchase behavior to the environmental impact, which is genuinely their best move in this send. But there's no offer. No points angle. No early access. No reason to click *right now* rather than feel vaguely warm about ILIA's values and move on.
- I'd mark this as read. I would not click. I would not forward it — unless a friend was specifically asking me "is ILIA actually about the clean beauty thing or is it just marketing?" in which case: yes, forwarded, evidence delivered.

## What's working

- **The "Skin Tint Plants Trees" section earns its place** — connecting a specific product (the Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40) to the tree-planting mission is the one moment where I'm thinking about buying, not just reading.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — the eye flows naturally from hero → cause story → product → CTA, no layout weirdness or broken images.
- **The 1% for the Planet logo at the top** is a trust signal I actually recognize and respect; it's not just ILIA saying "we care."
- **Tone is restrained and honest** — no over-the-top emotional manipulation, no manufactured urgency. For a cause email, that reads as genuinely credible. Drunk Elephant would have made it about a kit drop.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no hook for me specifically** — I'm a Sephora Rouge doing points math. "LEARN MORE >" after a tree-planting story isn't a CTA, it's a shrug. Glossier at least gave me 25% off when they wanted me to engage with their values content.
- **The subject line will get scrolled past** — "LA wildfire resilience—an update" is a press release headline, not an inbox opener. After "In your own skin (tint)" scored so well on June 29, this is a whiplash drop in subject line energy.
- **No loyalty or member framing** — if buying the Skin Tint literally plants trees, why isn't there a line about earning Rouge points *and* planting trees? Sally Beauty would have found a way to stack the offer.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is somewhere around email ten-plus in ten days and it's the most low-conversion one in the run. The timing makes it harder to land.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the product-mission tie earlier.** "Skin Tint Plants Trees" is buried. Move that hook to the top or at least above the fold — it's the only thing in this email that makes me think about my cart.
- 2. **Add a loyalty bridge.** Something like: "Every Skin Tint purchase plants a tree *and* earns your Sephora points." Give me two reasons to click, not zero.
- 3. **Reframe the CTA.** "LEARN MORE >" does nothing. Try "Shop Skin Tint, Plant a Tree" — that's a verb-noun CTA that actually tells me what happens when I click.
- 4. **Consider the cadence.** Drop a beat before this one — let the Crown Affair collab emails breathe, then send the values content as a standalone moment, not buried in a product blitz.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Skin Tint just planted a tree in LA`
- **Subject Alt B:** `After the wildfires, ILIA went back to plant`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `100% of Skin Tint purchases fund real wildfire recovery — here's what grew`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 million trees planted. Here's how your last order helped.`
