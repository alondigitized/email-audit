# 20% OFF Charlotte's Best-Selling Beauty Icons! 💫
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Six Charlotte Tilbury emails in seven days, and now we've hit two in a single day. I already opened "Peel To Reveal Your Pillow Talk Gift" this morning — that scratch-card gimmick was actually fun — and now here's a straight 20% off best-sellers send hitting my inbox a few hours later. Honestly? My first reaction was *didn't you just send me a 20% off email yesterday?* And yes, they did — "20% Off Is HERE!" landed May 21 and scored high because the launch moment felt real. Today's version feels like the same sale wearing different clothes.
- That said, the email itself is clean. The 20% OFF hero is big, the "SHOP WITH 20% OFF" button is right there above the fold, and the product grid with actual named products and prices is genuinely useful — I'm doing the icons math in my head already (Magic Serum at 20% off is real money). The loyalty section landing mid-scroll is a nice pull. This isn't a bad email. It's a redundant one.
- I'd probably click — not because this email convinced me, but because CT at 20% off is always worth a look and I have the tab open anyway from the morning send. Would I forward it to a friend? No. Yesterday I might have. Today it feels like I'd be the fifth person sending the same sale link.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is immediate.** "20% OFF BEST-SELLING BEAUTY ICONS" dominates the top — no ambiguity, no scroll required. Drunk Elephant's F&F send did the same thing and I praised them for it; CT earns the same credit here.
- **Actual products with prices in the grid.** Named icons with visible pricing means I can do the savings math without clicking — that's a real trust signal vs. Ulta's vague spend-and-save structure.
- **"EXCLUSIVE TO LOYALTY MEMBERS" section.** They remembered I'm not a stranger. This mid-email loyalty callout beats the cold treatment ILIA gave me in their base-layer send where no membership was acknowledged.
- **Rose-gold aesthetic is on-brand and aspirational without feeling exclusionary.** Models look like they're in my demographic range; nothing jarring.
- **App promo at the bottom doesn't interfere with the main offer.** It's there, it's tasteful, it stays in its lane.

## What's weak

- **This is the second CT email today and the second "20% off" email in two days.** The offer launched yesterday. Sending again today in a different wrapper reads as either desperation or a segmentation mistake — neither is a good look for a luxury brand.
- **No end date visible.** Drunk Elephant's F&F send was explicit about the deadline and it made me move. "20% off Icons" with no expiration is less urgent than it should be, and CT's past exclusion gymnastics (that May 16 send made me do mental math) mean I need credibility signals to feel safe clicking.
- **The icons selected feel safe/predictable.** Nothing here surprised me — Magic Serum, Pillow Talk products, highlighter. If I've been subscribed for a while, I already own half this list. A "new to you" or personalized icon recommendation would have elevated this.
- **Zero social proof.** ILIA dropped reviews into their email flow; Glossier used UGC; even e.l.f.'s lip launch leaned on community content. CT's prestige positioning doesn't exempt it from "real people love this" signals.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second same-day send or at minimum differentiate the angle hard.** The morning send was a fun Pillow Talk reveal. This one should either own a completely different product family or go out tomorrow. Two emails in one day from the same brand in the same sale window is the fastest way to get filtered.
- 2. **Add a visible sale end date in the hero.** "Ends Sunday midnight" does more work than the entire app-promo section below the fold. Put it directly under the 20% OFF headline.
- 3. **Swap the generic icons grid for a "built for you" edit** — even 2-3 products flagged as matching my known purchase history would make this feel targeted vs. mass-broadcast. ILIA's loyalty pitch landed because it referenced my tier; CT's loyalty section doesn't do that here.
- 4. **Trim or relocate the app promo.** It's a competing CTA that dilutes the sale focus. Move it to the footer or drop it entirely for a sale-window send.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Partially verifiable** — links are in the truncated section. From what is visible:

- No link `href` values or UTM strings are present in the visible head/CSS block, so click-tracking coverage cannot be confirmed.
- External font dependency at `https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css` is a live HTTP request from recipient clients. If that asset 404s or is slow, fallback fonts silently activate — not a tracking issue per se, but a deliverability-adjacent infrastructure dependency.

**Action required:** Verify all CTA buttons use tracked redirect URLs (ESP click-wrap or UTM-annotated), not bare `charlottetilbury.com` hrefs.

---

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** (`<title></title>`): Screen readers in some clients (VoiceOver + Apple Mail) fall back to reading the document title. An empty title leaves a blank announcement. Should be set to match the preheader or subject.
- **`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }`**: This Gmail-targeting rule strips underline and forces link color to match surrounding text on all anchors within Gmail's wrapper. Links become visually indistinguishable from body copy unless the template uses explicit button styling on every CTA. Inline color/underline on anchor elements needs to be confirmed for every text link.
- **Font loaded only for non-MSO clients** (`<!--[if !mso]><!-->…<!--<![endif]-->`): Outlook users receive system fallback fonts. This is expected and handled, but the fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a named web-safe fallback is declared in the `font-family` property, not just the Google/CT font name.
- **MSO VML declarations present and correct** — `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `<o:AllowPNG/>` are both set; Outlook rendering baseline is properly configured.
- **`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — correct.
- **Image `alt` text** — cannot be verified from the truncated source; must be audited in the full HTML. Product images without `alt` are a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.
