# A quick note from team ILIA
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- My first thought was that this is the fifth ILIA email in about three days, and this one has no math to do. After the 20/25% banner stack on "Skin Tint SPF 40" (11/25) and "All eyes: up to 25% off" (11/25), I opened "A quick note from team ILIA" braced for another asterisk. What I got was a Thanksgiving greeting: "We hope your plate is *full* today," a flat-lay on a silver platter, and a handwritten "Team ilia" sign-off. It's a nice-looking card, but it's a card and not a send.
- The *Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details* line in the footer is the only reference to a promo, and it points at an asterisk that doesn't exist anywhere above it. I can't tell whether the sale is still on, whether VIP pricing applies, or what to do next. On a day when I'm cross-shopping Sephora and Ulta, a brand that skips the offer loses me.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. It's pleasant, and after the earlier sends it feels like ILIA taking a breath without giving me a reason to come back.

## What's working

- The serif headline with the italic *full* is on brand and easy to read on a phone. It's the same typography that made the Barrier Build hero (11/24) feel premium.
- The flat-lay of a serum, a mascara, a lip product and a pink silk scrunchie on a platter is cute and tied to the holiday. It's shelf appeal without a hard sell.
- A gratitude-only send after four promo-heavy emails is a reasonable cadence reset. Sephora's six-in-six-days run didn't give me that.
- The footer trust row (Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, Pact recycling, shade match, auto-replenish) is clean and scannable.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no button and no product link anywhere. The whole email is a dead end, and Ulta's Black Friday tier ladder at least told me what to buy.
- "Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details" is a tiny footer link to an asterisk that appears nowhere in the body. It reads as a leftover template element, and it's confusing.
- It's still the fifth ILIA email in roughly three days. Even a nice note adds to the pile, and I'm already skimming past the sender.
- "Join ILIA VIP" is the only loyalty touchpoint, and it's a small footer tile. I'm already doing 20% vs. 25% VIP math, so I wanted a clear tier or points nudge.
- The flat-lay shows no models and no named products. I can't tell what's in the photo, so I can't add anything to my Sephora-versus-ILIA haul list.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one clear CTA beneath the sign-off, such as "Shop 25% off for VIPs," with a visible button. Thanksgiving is the one day people have time to browse, so a note with no path forward wastes it.
- 2. Make the platter shoppable. Tag or link the serum, the mascara and the lip product, so the flat-lay works as a mini gift edit like the "Double the gifts" send did.
- 3. Fix or drop the "Promotion Details" line. If a promo is running, put the offer and deadline above the fold. If not, remove the link.
- 4. Give the VIP tile a real job, for example "VIP members: your 25% is live through Cyber Monday."
- 5. Cut the frequency. Fold this note into the sale send instead of making it the fifth email in three days.
