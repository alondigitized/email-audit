# Obsessed? Same.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Ten emails in twelve days — I have been keeping count — and Sephora swings editorial again with "Obsessed? Same." after the Stacking Weekend percentage-sign marathon. I actually respect the pivot. Leading with Rhode in a "Going Viral" hero is the most self-aware thing this email could do: that peptide lip treatment has been all over my FYP for weeks and I have been watching it sell out drop by drop.
- Then I opened the body and the OUT OF STOCK label was sitting directly on the hero product. The thing I wanted. That is genuinely the fastest way to make me put my phone down. If you are going to build a "viral sellout" narrative, you cannot lead with the thing that already sold out — link me to a waitlist, an alternative, anything.
- And then I kept scrolling and found... five more content modules. Hair lineup. Clean lip. Fragrance recs. Harlem Perfume Co. That is not an editorial email, that is category chaos. I will mark it read, I will not forward it to a friend, and I will go directly to Rhode's website to check restock.

## What's working

- **Rhode in the hero** — exactly the right brand call; it is the most-discussed lip product in my social feed right now and Sephora placing it first shows someone read the cultural moment correctly.
- **"HOT ON SOCIAL" badge** — earns its placement given the editorial framing; it is the right trust signal for this content strategy.
- **AESTURA free trial at banner position** — I do the points math obsessively and a redemption offer at the top of the email catches my eye before I even hit the hero.
- **Harlem Perfume Co. feature** — a Black-owned indie brand getting editorial placement inside Sephora is exactly the kind of discovery content that makes me tap; that one actually has real click energy.
- **Brand voice coherence** — "Obsessed? Same." in the inbox to "Going Viral" in the body lands as one consistent thought, which Sephora does not always nail after a promo blitz.

## What's weak

- **OUT OF STOCK on the hero product** — this is a hard conversion kill; if Rhode is sold out, swap to Rhode adjacent stock or give me a restock notification CTA instead.
- **Five modules, no hierarchy** — hair, clean lip, fragrance, Harlem Perfume, "You May Also Like" is five pitches in one email; compare how Drunk Elephant's "Time to come clean" — which scored a 9 in my recent reads — built its entire email around one product family and never scattered the ask.
- **Product labels unreadable at mobile size** — the "You May Also Like" fragrance grid has names and tags that disappear at this scale; e.l.f.'s "Get on the cream team" put the $14 price point in big legible type right under the product, which is how you make a product grid scannable on a phone.
- **No points context for a Rouge member** — the AESTURA offer is a redemption, meaning I presumably need points to claim it, but there is no balance callout, no tier acknowledgment; Lush's "Zero packaging. Double points." opened with the exact math I needed to decide — that is what I want from a loyalty email.
- **No deadline on the AESTURA offer** — "while supplies last" with zero date is not urgency; it is ambient anxiety, and I will not act on it today.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the OOS hero or replace it** — show the Rhode Peptide Glazing Fluid or another in-stock Rhode SKU, or add a "notify me when it's back" button. Do not waste the cultural moment on a product I literally cannot buy.
- 2. **Cut to two modules** — lead with the Going Viral social moment (two or three products I can actually cart), close with the AESTURA redemption. Save hair and fragrance for their own sends.
- 3. **Surface Rouge status and points balance** — personalize the AESTURA banner to show my points total and what I can redeem; that is the single thing that would make me tap the CTA immediately.
- 4. **Add a concrete deadline to the offer** — "Free AESTURA trial, redeem by July 18" gives me a specific reason to act today.
- 5. **Increase type size on the product grid** — price or one key claim (VIRAL, NEW, EXCLUSIVE) needs to be legible at thumbnail scale.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` suppresses user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS for users with low vision. Drop `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to `body` and `span` this suppresses OS-level font scaling for visually impaired users on iOS/Android. Scope this to layout containers only (outer `<table>`, `<td>`), not presentational or inline elements.

**Issue — no `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers default to OS language; missing `lang` causes mispronunciation in multilingual ESPs. Add `lang="en"` (or appropriate locale).

**Observation — mobile breakpoint at 480px is narrow**
`@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` excludes 390–480px iPhones from the desktop layout without triggering mobile styles. Standard safe threshold is `600px`. Low risk for Sephora's likely ESP rendering environment, but worth flagging.

**Observation — very large explicit mobile paddings**
`padc6` sets `padding: 19px 0px 148px 0px` and `padc8` sets `padding: 15px 0px 159px 0px` on mobile. These are likely background-image positioning hacks. Confirm these don't produce blank scroll areas on devices where background images fail to load.
