# Buy 3, Save 20% starts now
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, email eleven — or twelve, honestly I've stopped keeping an exact count — and ILIA is back in sell mode after that brief detour into wildfire messaging. The pivot from "here's how we're thinking about LA resilience" to "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now" is *fast*. Like, 24-hour fast. I appreciate that ILIA isn't milking the CSR content for weeks, but the whiplash is real.
- That said? The mechanic is actually well-matched to how I shop. I already build mental ILIA trios in my head — Super Serum Skin Tint, the Limitless Lash mascara, something from the lip lineup — and 20% off three items on the brand's own site gets my attention immediately. The "power of YOUR sale" language is doing something smart: it's positioning this as personalized even though it's a sitewide bundle deal. The measuring tape in the hero is a cute visual metaphor for "tailored savings" that doesn't feel try-hard.
- But here's the thing I keep circling back to: I do all my ILIA buying at Sephora during double or triple points events. Buying direct at 20% off means I'm leaving Rouge points on the table. ILIA doesn't tell me what I'm *gaining* by going direct — no loyalty layer, no "earn on top of this," nothing. So I'd read it, I'd do the math, and I'd probably wait for the next Sephora event unless I'm already stocked out. Mark as read, revisit later.

## What's working

- **Offer clarity is immediate** — "Buy 3, Save 20%" is in the hero at a large enough size that I don't have to hunt for it. Competing against Sephora's Stacking Weekend (which made the same bet and nailed it), this holds up.
- **Three-step mechanic is clean** — "Build. Code. Save." takes up the body scroll nicely and eliminates any guesswork about how to redeem.
- **Code is prominent** — "BUNDLEUP" appears three times: the top banner, the body copy, and the button. I won't forget it.
- **The top announcement bar** earns its keep — rare for that strip to add information rather than repeat the subject, and here it does.
- **Product photography is on-brand** — the pink scissors + ILIA bottles on the soft blush background is clean, editorial, and very much ILIA's aesthetic. No cluttered SKU dump.

## What's weak

- **No end date anywhere** — "starts now" is great but "ends when?" is nowhere. Compare this to Sally Beauty's "Last Chance: BOGO 50% Off" which baked urgency into the subject *and* the body. ILIA gives me no deadline, so I file it in the "I'll do this later" pile, which is the kiss of death for email.
- **Asterisk on the core offer** — "*See note for details" appears right under the hero copy. I read every asterisk. If there are exclusions baked in, I need to see them above the fold, not in a footer footnote that requires scrolling.
- **Zero loyalty math** — no callout to Sephora, no note that this is direct-only savings, no VIP perk stacking. For someone doing the points calculation in real time, ILIA is asking me to make a blind purchase decision.
- **Pivot speed post-wildfire** — the June 30 resilience email asked for goodwill. This one, 24 hours later, asks for my wallet. The sequencing needed at least one more day of breathing room.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline, even if it's soft** — "ends July 7" in the hero copy would change my behavior immediately. Right now this has the urgency of "free shipping over $35," which is always true.
- 2. **Acknowledge the asterisk upfront** — if there are category exclusions (some brands carve out tinted SPFs, for example), put them in the hero subhead. "Valid on everything except X" is more trustworthy than a footnote.
- 3. **Give me a trio starter** — feature one curated trio (e.g., tint + mascara + lip) with a single "shop this trio" CTA. Drunk Elephant's "It kits" did exactly this and made the bundle mechanic tangible. Right now I'm doing all the curation work myself.
- 4. **Add a direct vs. Sephora value prop** — even one line like "20% off direct is more than you'll save with points alone" would short-circuit my hesitation.
- 5. **Space the sequence** — a 24-hour gap after a CSR/resilience send is too fast. The buying email needed 48–72 hours to land without feeling transactional.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in Gmail and Outlook**

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail strips `@import` from `<style>` blocks. Outlook ignores them entirely. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is correctly defined, so visual degradation is minimal — but custom brand fonts will not render in the two largest desktop/webmail clients.

**Google Fonts API endpoint is outdated**

`fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat` is the legacy v1 endpoint. The current endpoint is `css2` (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat:wght@400&display=swap`). The legacy endpoint still works but may be deprecated without notice.

**Hover state suppressed for all anchor elements**

```css
a:hover { color: #5f4b3c; text-decoration: none }
```

This disables any visual feedback on hover in the browser/web-view render of the email. Not a deliverability issue, but reduces interactivity signal for web-view and HTML preview panes.

**Empty `<title>` tag**

```html
<title> </title>
```

Only a whitespace character. Renders as a blank tab title in the web-view version. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.
