# Glossier’s Next Top Dog: The Reunion
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I genuinely smiled at this one — Glossier bringing back the Next Top Dog finalists for a "reunion" and writing them in full ANTM confessional-booth mode is *exactly* the kind of brand content that reminds me why I'm subscribed in the first place. "Honestly? It was chaos. Everyone else was crazy. I stay perfect." from Gua is sending me. I will show my friends this email.
- Here's the thing though: I've now had five Glossier emails in about a week — Pink Capsule launch, "Fetch a little joy," summer activity checklist, the hoodie origin story, yesterday's fragrance customization adventure, and now this. Each one is a totally different creative universe. There's no through-line, no seasonal build. It's starting to feel like Glossier's content calendar is going through a vibe crisis, not a campaign arc.
- I'll read it, I'll laugh, and I might tap the hoodie out of reflex. But I'm not pulling out my credit card — there's no reason to. This is a pure brand-love email, and if I weren't already a Glossier ride-or-die, I'd have bounced at the hero. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The dog "testimonials" are genuinely funny and very on-brand — Mochi's "I thought I was the shoe-in... I had a beauty supply assistant moment—" is the right amount of absurd for Glossier's voice.
- Clear visual identity throughout — the pink leash, the clean sans-serif headers, the airy white layout. You could not mistake this for any other brand. Charlotte Tilbury couldn't pull off this minimalism if they tried.
- Each dog gets a neighborhood callout (Williamsburg, Soho, Astoria) which is a very Glossier move — hyperlocal, NYC-girl energy, and it works for my demographic.
- Product placement at the bottom feels organic rather than grafted on — the leash and the Embroidered Pillow Hoodie look like merch at a fan event, not an afterthought email.
- Clean render — all images loaded, no layout breakage. After CT's recent countdown clock disaster, a flawlessly rendered email is not something I take for granted.

## What's weak

- Zero purchase intent drivers — no offer, no discount, no urgency whatsoever. This email is 100% vibes and 0% conversion, and that's a choice, but it's a choice that leaves money on the table.
- "Shop now" CTAs are anonymous and lazy — Drunk Elephant at least writes "Shop Lala Retro" or names the hero. Three "Shop now" buttons at the bottom of an entertainment email is just hoping I'll tap something randomly.
- Not a single beauty product front-and-center — I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond, I live for skincare launches and makeup drops, and this email showed me a dog leash. The hoodie is cute but that's not why I stay subscribed.
- The pivot from yesterday's fragrance adventure to today's dog reunion is whiplash-fast — ILIA has been doing a much better job of day-after ingredient education that builds on the previous send rather than starting over completely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor one beauty product to the story.** Tie a real product to a dog — "Runkle's ride-or-die: Stretch Concealer in G10" or "Mochi approved: Cloud Paint in Haze." Even one product mention woven into the copy converts this from a fun read to a shoppable moment.
- 2. **Rename the CTAs.** "Shop the Hoodie," "Shop the Leash," "Shop the Collection" — three words is all it takes. ILIA does this on every email and it never feels like a generic blast.
- 3. **Add a soft-urgency hook on the merch.** "Limited run leash" or "back by popular demand" would be all I need to feel like now matters.
- 4. **Slow the cadence or build a seasonal arc.** The last five emails have no throughline. The Pink Capsule → hoodie → fragrance → dogs sequencing feels random. Ulta batches their storytelling by event (21 Days of Beauty, loyalty points reminders) — Glossier could do the same around a campaign spine.
