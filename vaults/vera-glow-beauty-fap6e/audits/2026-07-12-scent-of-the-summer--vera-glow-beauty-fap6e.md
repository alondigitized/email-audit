# Scent of the summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay so Glossier delivered on the "Something new is coming" tease from July 7 — and honestly? The payoff is pretty. "Making waves" as the campaign name is doing exactly what a summer fragrance launch should feel: coastal, golden hour, effortless. The bottle-on-the-pebbled-beach imagery is legitimately gorgeous and the multi-review module is a smart move for a fragrance you literally cannot smell through a phone screen. Selling a scent via vibes and other people's words is the only lever you have, and they leaned into it.
- That said, I am standing here doing absolutely zero points math because there is no price anywhere in this email. None. Not even a "starting at $X." I'm a Sephora Rouge — I want to know if this is a $35 body mist or a $75 EDP before I click into anything. Drunk Elephant's "Time to come clean" last week put a product and a price in the hero. Sally Beauty gave me the exact percentage off in the subject line. Glossier is asking me to vibe my way to a purchase decision, and that's... a miss for someone who tracks spend closely.
- The "I ♥ NY Balm Dotcom" module at the bottom also feels bolted on. Is that a separate launch? A cross-sell? A limited collab? It muddies the story right when the fragrance narrative is getting good. I'd click to learn more about the scent — I would absolutely not forward this one because there's nothing actionable to share without a price.

## What's working

- **The teaser payoff timing is chef's kiss.** July 7 was "Something new is coming" (a 9/10 send that I genuinely liked), and July 12 is the reveal. That arc works. Glossier actually stuck the landing on a two-beat sequence for once instead of running four "ends tonight" emails in a row.
- **Multi-review module is smart fragrance strategy.** Five-star testimonials talking about "creamy," "fresh," "log door after a day at the beach" — that's sensory language that does the olfactory heavy lifting I can't do myself from a screen. More useful than a list of notes.
- **Editorial imagery is distinctly Glossier.** The bottle on the rocky shore, the golden hour palette, the net bag with red globes — it feels like a magazine shoot, not a Mailchimp template. Sephora's "Obsessed? Same." double-send last week had solid visual hierarchy but nowhere near this level of art direction.
- **"Making waves" hero copy is clean and seasonally on-point.** Short, declarative, summer-coded. No hashtag overload, no emoji stuffing.
- **No visible render bugs.** The layout holds across all the modules — nothing overlapping, no broken images.

## What's weak

- **Zero price point anywhere.** I'm a loyalty-tier collector who plans spend around events. Without knowing what this fragrance costs, I can't assess whether to buy now vs. wait for the next Beauty Insider event or Ulta 21 Days window. ILIA did the same price-free editorial thing recently and I liked the content but didn't click to purchase for the same reason.
- **"Shop now" × 4 is lazy CTA copy.** Every single module has "Shop now." No specificity on what I'm shopping — the fragrance? The full line? The Balm Dotcom? A bundle? e.l.f. puts the product name in the CTA copy; Glossier should too.
- **The "I ♥ NY Balm Dotcom" drop at the bottom breaks focus.** Is this a second product launch in the same email? A collab limited edition? It needed its own send or at least a clear headline that ties it to the fragrance story.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment whatsoever.** I've been a Glossier customer long enough to track their sale resurrection cycles intimately. There's not a single "member," "you," or tier signal in this entire email. Drunk Elephant's welcome-to-the-club energy hits totally different — this treats me like a stranger on their mailing list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price (or "from $XX") in the hero section.** A single number unlocks the purchase calculus for every loyalty-tier shopper in the list. It doesn't undercut the aesthetic — Drunk Elephant prices in-email and still feels premium.
- 2. **Make the CTAs product-specific.** Replace the four "Shop now" buttons with "Shop Making Waves," "Explore the scent," or even "Get yours" — anything that creates specificity.
- 3. **Give the Balm Dotcom section a connecting headline.** Something like "While you're here — a NY summer classic" so it reads as a curated add-on, not a second email glued to the bottom.
- 4. **Add a single loyalty signal.** Even "Shop before the next Glossier event" or a Sephora-channel callout would acknowledge that their buyer base is cross-shopping everything on points calendars.
- 5. **Lead the Balm Dotcom section with what "I ♥ NY" actually *is* — new shade? Limited collab? — before asking for a sign-up.**

## Technical Audit — Glossier "Scent of the summer"

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo (klclick.com), uses XHTML 1.0 Transitional with standard MSO conditional comment structure. One invalid tracking beacon implementation is confirmed; full link and compliance audit is blocked by the truncated HTML source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid open-tracking beacon element**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXCAPPQT6MKM7XV6967T2MV9_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This is semantically malformed HTML. Standard Klaviyo open tracking uses a 1×1 `<img>` pixel; this pattern will be silently ignored by strict parsers (e.g., some corporate gateways, newer Gmail rendering paths). Open-rate data may be undercounted.

**Fix:** Replace with a standard 1×1 tracking pixel:
```html
<img src="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/..." width="1" height="1" border="0" alt="" />
```

**Click-link audit:** Blocked — HTML is truncated before the body links appear. Cannot confirm whether all CTA hrefs resolve through Klaviyo redirect or direct, or verify UTM parameter presence.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**No confirmed rendering bugs** in the visible portion. Correct patterns are present:
- `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">` — screen-reader and bidi support
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` — prevents iOS auto-scaling
- `<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection">` — suppresses auto-linked phone numbers
