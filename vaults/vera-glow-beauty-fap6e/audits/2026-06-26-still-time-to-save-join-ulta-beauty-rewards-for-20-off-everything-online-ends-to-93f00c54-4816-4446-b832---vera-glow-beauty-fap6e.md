# Still time to save 💸️ Join Ulta Beauty Rewards® for 20% off everything online⁺⁺—ends tonight! Use it again (Terms & excl. apply)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- So. Here we are again. I already wrote about this email on June 23 — twice, actually, because Ulta sent it twice that day — and then I wrote about it *again* on June 26 with the "ends tonight" urgency layer added. This is now the fourth time I have sat with essentially this creative, and I want that on record before I say anything else.
- The email itself is fine — honestly it's better than fine. The 20% off hero is bold and immediately legible, the product grid is stacked with real prices (I clocked the before/afters pretty quickly), and the GWP pink bag offer is a genuinely nice sweetener. The layout doesn't break, there are no mystery lorem-ipsum fields, and the Rewards section at the bottom signals at least some attempt at personalization. If I were a stranger to Ulta's inbox, this would be a decent mid-tier promotional email.
- But I'm not a stranger. I am Diamond. I have been Diamond long enough that the phrase "Join Ulta Beauty Rewards" in my inbox feels like receiving a note that says "Have you considered eating food?" The targeting miss is still there, unchanged across four sends. I'd mark it read. I would not forward it. I would definitely not click "join" anything.

## What's working

- **The 20% hero is immediate and readable** — orange background, bold white type, no ambiguity about what the offer is. My eyes land there first.
- **"Stacks with 3,000+ products on sale!" is legitimately strong copy** — the stacking message is the actual differentiator and I'm glad it's in the second line of the hero rather than buried.
- **The product grid shows real before/after pricing** — I can do the math at a glance, which is how I actually shop. This beats e.l.f.'s launch emails that don't show price until you click.
- **Free 7-piece beauty bag section is visually distinct** — the pink banner breaks up the scroll and the GWP feels like a value-add, not a footnote.
- **Rewards section at the bottom** — even if the subject line disrespects my tier, there's at least a member-flavored module near the bottom. It's a start.

## What's weak

- **"Join Ulta Beauty Rewards" is still in the subject on the fourth send.** I am Diamond. This is the single biggest targeting failure in this campaign and no one has fixed it across four versions. Sephora's sale emails at least know I'm Rouge — they lean into the tier language even when the offer is the same for everyone.
- **The subject line is exhausting to parse** — it's doing too many jobs at once: urgency, program pitch, offer detail, deadline, repeat-send disclosure, and legal disclaimer. By the time I've processed all of that in my notification tray, I'm already tired.
- **No standout product story** — the grid has 8+ products but nothing is *the* hero item. Sally Beauty at least frames their discount sends around a category anchor. This is just "everything," which paradoxically makes it feel like nothing.
- **"Use it again" is interesting transparency but wrong placement** — putting the repeat-send acknowledgment in the subject (or preheader) is savvy in theory, but in practice it reads as "we know you've seen this and we're asking you to tolerate it one more time." That's not a great ask for a Diamond member.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the existing-member list and swap the subject.** Diamond and Platinum members should never see "join Ulta Beauty Rewards." Give them "Your 20% is back — stacks with 3,000+ sale items, ends tonight." One sentence, no program pitch, respects the relationship.
- 2. **Pick one product to carry the whole email.** Feature a trending SKU in the hero with a dedicated link — a Charlotte Tilbury pillow lip or a Drunk Elephant protini — then use the grid for supporting items. Gives me a reason to click instead of scroll past.
- 3. **Cut the subject down to ~50 characters.** Everything after "ends tonight" belongs in the preheader, not the subject.
- 4. **Move the GWP higher.** The free 7-piece beauty bag is the most exciting thing in this email and it's near the bottom. Lead with it alongside the 20% off to create a compound-value hook.
- 5. **Kill the "Use it again" framing.** If you're going to re-send, just re-send. Narrating the repeat send calls attention to the cadence problem without solving it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 20% stacks with 3,000+ sale items — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack your savings: 20% off + sale prices, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 7-piece bag when you spend $40 — use your discount before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `3,000+ products on sale and your 20% still applies — goes away tonight`
