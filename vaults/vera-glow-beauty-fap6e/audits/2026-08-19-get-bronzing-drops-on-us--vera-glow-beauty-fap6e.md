# Get Bronzing Drops on us ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, a free full-size GWP with a $50 order is a real offer, and the gold "Bronzing Drops" hero shot dripping product is genuinely pretty — that's a strong open. But "Get Bronzing Drops on us" is also just a threshold gift-with-purchase, dressed up like a gift, and I've now had e.l.f. run me through Bubble recycled hero shots (8/7, 8/11, 8/12, 8/13), then finally a real dupe comparison on 8/17, then real SKIN skincare on 8/16 — and now we're back to "spend $50, get a free thing," which is the oldest trick in the Sephora/Ulta playbook, not something distinctly e.l.f.
- Then it swerves hard into a grocery-cart photo of pickle jars for a lip balm launch that's not even out yet ("Dropping Soon") and I genuinely had to reread it to confirm I wasn't looking at a food brand's email by accident. That's a fun stunt for TikTok but it buries the actual GWP offer I might act on under a "notify me" for a product I can't buy today.
- I'd scroll past this one. Not deleting it, not forwarding it — my Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond boxes both have actual member-tier math sitting in them right now, and this doesn't out-hustle either.

## What's working

- The Bronzing Drops product shot with the drip is legit scroll-stopping and on-brand for the "bronze + nourish" pitch.
- "No code necessary at checkout" removes real friction — I don't have to dig for a promo code.
- "Online only" + the $50 threshold is at least an honest, non-buried offer — no fine print tricks.
- The pickle-jar Dill-lulu stunt is memorable and distinct from anything Sephora or Ulta would ever run — genuinely on-brand e.l.f. humor.

## What's weak

- The GWP shade is locked to "Pure Gold" — no shade-range or skin-tone personalization at all, which as someone who tracks ingredient/shade fit closely is a miss.
- The pickle photo for a product that's "dropping soon" is a bait-and-switch on my attention — I opened for a bronzer gift, got produce.
- Zero loyalty/Beauty Squad points math anywhere in this email — Ulta's version of this exact GWP mechanic at least name-drops the coupon code and tier language even when it's repetitive (see their 8/19 "20% off" send).
- "Top Picks for You" is clearly not "for me" — a mauve lip liner, a primer, and a setting spray with no purchase or browse history driving it reads like a generic grid, same complaint I had about e.l.f.'s 8/18 "weekly highlight reel" send.
- The six-category nav bar (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) at the bottom is dead weight — nobody's tapping a gray bar with no visual, that's real estate Glossier or ILIA would've used for a second product story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the whole email with the GWP threshold math as a badge on the hero image itself ("FREE Bronzing Drops with $50+") instead of making me read three lines of body copy to find out the mechanic — Drunk Elephant's flat product-line dump got a 5/10 partly for the same "make me work for the offer" issue.
- 2. Cut the pickle-jar teaser entirely or move it to its own send once Dill-lulu actually launches — teasing an unavailable product in the same email as an active offer splits my attention and neither one wins.
- 3. Add real personalization to "Top Picks for You" — even browsing-history-based logic would beat this generic three-pack.
- 4. Give Beauty Squad members a points callout on the $50 threshold — even Ulta's tired 20%-off-one-item send at least references the loyalty program by name.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shade Pure Gold, on us — plus a first look at Dill-lulu lip balms`

  - `/products/power-grip-primer?Size=Regular+0.8+oz+%2F+24+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount` ("Receipt scanning" link)
