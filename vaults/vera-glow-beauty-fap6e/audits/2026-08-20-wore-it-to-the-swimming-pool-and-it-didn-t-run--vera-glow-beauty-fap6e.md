- Okay, this is ILIA's best send of the week, hands down. And the body backs it up: 4x VIP points banner up top, then a clean stat-stacked proof cascade — 100% applies comfortably, 98% blendable, 96% wears all day, 90% doesn't skip/drag/tug — each paired with a real face shot, not a stock render. That's the exact "personalized skin type/performance proof" beat I keep saying this brand skips.
- What pulls it down from a 9 is that the swimming pool hook from the subject line never actually shows up in the email — no pool shot, no waterproof-in-water visual, nothing that pays off the exact promise that got me to open. - I'd click "Shop Thru Line" — the eyeliner itself looks genuinely good and the 24-hour/waterproof/sensitive-eye claims are concrete enough to convert me. I wouldn't forward it to a friend, but I'd screenshot the "no tugging and lasts all day" quote card if I were actually shopping eyeliner today.

## What's working

- The 4x VIP points banner at the very top is loyalty math I can actually do at a glance — better than ILIA's 8/17 send which buried points language.
- Real diverse face macro shots for every stat (not one token model) — this is the demographic/skin-tone signal I keep asking ILIA for and rarely get.
- Concrete numbers (100%, 98%, 96%, 90%) beat vague "customers love it" copy — specificity I don't get from Glossier's seasonal fluff.
- Two distinct CTAs ("Shop Thru Line" and "Shop the Eyeliner") both point to the actual product, not a homepage dump.
- The handwritten "no tugging and lasts all day" quote card is a nice tactile trust cue, similar energy to their 8/19 "3 Ways to Wear" send that I liked.

## What's weak

- The subject promises a swimming pool story and the email never shows water, a pool, or even a "swim-tested" visual — total disconnect between hook and payoff.
- Four near-identical stat blocks in a row (100%/98%/96%/90%) start to blur together by the third one — it's proof overload without variety in layout.
- The "art of the eye" section at the bottom (Eye Stylus, Thru Line, Limitless Lash bundle) feels bolted on and dilutes the single-product focus that made the top half strong.
- No specific % or $ off anywhere — just "free shipping over $50" in the footer, so if I'm doing points math, there's no discount to calculate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one pool or water-splash visual mid-email to actually deliver on the subject line's promise — right now it's false advertising on the hook.
- 2. Cut one of the four stat blocks (96% and 98% are redundant) and use that space for the swim/water proof instead.
- 3. Move "the art of the eye" bundle section to a separate send — it competes with the eyeliner's single-product clarity.
- 4. Add an actual dollar or percent offer, even a small one, since "free shipping over $50" isn't a real incentive for a loyalty shopper like me.
