# Memorial Day Glow-Up: Up to 50% Off ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Six Sally Beauty emails in ten days, and this one lands on Memorial Day with a 50% off headline — okay, fine, I see you Sally. But I'm immediately doing two mental calculations at once: there's a "20% off sitewide flash sale" banner *and* a "up to 50% off" hero competing for my attention in the first scroll. Which offer am I supposed to care about? The flash sale is literally layered on top of the Memorial Day sale like they copy-pasted two different campaigns into one send. That said — GHD cordless tools at 50% off is the kind of number that makes me stop and actually read. GHD retails high and never goes on deep sale at Sephora or Ulta, so finding that buried in a Sally email is actually a sleeper deal.
- The model is gorgeous and the textured-hair product curation underneath her is clearly intentional — braiding kit, curl activator, leave-in conditioner — but as someone who skews prestige-brand haul shopping over Sally's pro-supply vibe, most of that grid feels like it's for a different customer. The nail section is a total afterthought ($5 nail lacquer? I'm a Sephora Rouge), and the "2 for $15" section reads like a drugstore endcap, not a sale I'd screenshot and send to my group chat.
- I'd mark this as read, potentially click through specifically to the GHD section, but I would not forward this. It's too scattered to be shareable, and the dual-promo energy makes it feel disorganized rather than generous.

## What's working

- **GHD at 50% off is a genuine hook** — prestige styling tools on deep discount is rare and actually gives this email a reason to exist beyond "yet another Sally sale."
- **Hero hierarchy lands clean** — "Up to 50% off" reads immediately on the screen, the model is prominent, and the "Shop Now" CTA button is dark and unambiguous below the fold.
- **Memorial Day timing is on-point** — like Glossier's "Memorial Day Sale starts NOW" send I saw today, the seasonal peg gives the discount real-world context instead of floating promo language.
- **Textured-hair curation is deliberate** — the model, the braiding kit, the curl activator, the leave-in all tell a consistent product story for that customer segment.
- **No broken layouts or render bugs** — the grid renders cleanly, images are crisp, nothing overlaps.

## What's weak

- **Two competing offers, zero hierarchy between them** — the "20% off sitewide flash sale" banner sitting above the "up to 50% off" hero creates confusion. Am I supposed to use a code? Does the flash sale stack? Drunk Elephant's Friends & Family send handled a single-offer escalation beautifully; Sally is doing the opposite here.
- **No loyalty signal whatsoever** — I'm a Sally Beauty Club member and there's zero acknowledgment of my tier, my points, or any member-exclusive framing. Ulta would have personalized this with my name and a spend-tier offer by now.
- **The product grid is all over the place** — nails, hair care, braiding supplies, and GHD styling tools in the same scroll feels like a warehouse clearance, not a curated sale event. Charlotte Tilbury's recent 20% off send had one clear hero product family; this is seven.
- **No end date in the email** — "Memorial Day" implies Monday but I shouldn't have to infer a deadline from a calendar holiday. If this sale ends Sunday at midnight, say that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one of the offers** — either lead with the flash sale code OR the Memorial Day percentage off. Not both. The cognitive load of reconciling two simultaneous promotions is real and it's killing conversion on both.
- 2. **Isolate the GHD module and push it up** — that 50% off prestige tool deal should be in the top third of the email, not buried after braiding kits. Lead with the thing that differentiates you.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Monday 11:59 PM" in the hero text. Right now "Memorial Day" is doing all the deadline lifting and it's not enough.
- 4. **Drop a loyalty callout** — even one line: "Club members earn points on every dollar" converts me. I'm always doing that math.
- 5. **Slim the grid** — three product stories max. Pick: GHD tools, textured hair care, OR nail. Not all three.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day sale: GHD, hair care, more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cordless curling iron, leave-ins, and braiding kits — all on deep sale this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50% off GHD ends Monday. Plus up to 50% off sitewide.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`img { white-space: pre; }` (head `<style>`)** — Anomalous. `white-space` is not a valid property for `<img>` elements and has no effect, but its presence suggests a template copy-paste artifact. Low risk; should be removed.

**Missing `@font-face` for Founders Grotesk** — The global `a {}` rule declares `font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif`, but no `@font-face` block for Founders Grotesk exists in the source. Every link in the email silently falls back to Helvetica. If brand rendering of Founders Grotesk is intended, the webfont must be declared or the fallback stack changed intentionally.

**Open Sans loaded at version 17** (`fonts.gstatic.com/.../opensans/v17/`) — Current Google Fonts serves v35+. The v17 endpoint is still served by Google's CDN so it won't break, but the `local()` hints reference legacy PostScript names (`OpenSans-Italic`, `OpenSans-SemiBold`, etc.) that don't match system font names on macOS 13+ or Windows 11, defeating the local-cache optimization.

**External font CDN blocked by major clients** — Gmail (web), Gmail iOS, Outlook 2016–2024, and Samsung Mail all block `@font-face` from external URLs. Open Sans will not render in these clients; Helvetica/Arial fallback applies. This is expected email behavior but confirm the fallback stack is visually acceptable.

**`box-shadow` on `.boxShadow` class** — Not supported in Outlook, Gmail, or Yahoo Mail. Will silently drop. No functional issue, but the visual design relies on a shadow that 60–70% of recipients will not see.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`** — Non-standard. Most email frameworks use 600px. Devices with viewport widths between 500–599px (many Android phones in landscape) will render the desktop layout without mobile overrides.
