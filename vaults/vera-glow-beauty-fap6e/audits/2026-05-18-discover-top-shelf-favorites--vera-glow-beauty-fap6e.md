# Discover top shelf favorites
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Third ILIA email in three days, and honestly I feel it. The May 16 welcome was smart timing, May 17's award editorial was genuinely impressive, and now May 18 lands as a brand overview — essentially a "here's who we are" dump after I've already been welcomed and shown the awards shelf. The creative isn't bad, but the sequencing makes it feel like the ESP fired a journey step on autopilot rather than a human deciding this was the right send right now.
- What I'm looking at is a long vertical scroll — Cult Classics hero, then Super Serum Skin Tint feature, then mascara feature, then what looks like a social-proof milestone, then finally 20% off sitewide buried near the bottom. The offer is real and the content is solid, but burying a sitewide discount five sections deep is a choice I don't love. Compare this to Charlotte Tilbury's GWP email from recent days, where the gift was the hero — full stop. ILIA made me work for the discount.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. The 20% off is genuinely compelling for someone like me doing Ulta 21 Days math on everything, but I've already seen ILIA's greatest hits twice this week. I'm not forwarding this one.

## What's working

- **Super Serum Skin Tint social proof is stacked and specific** — "17,000+ five-star reviews," "30 flexible shades," "17+ industry awards" in one tight block earns real trust. That's e.l.f.-level stat-stacking done at prestige.
- **"The holy grail of mascara" pull quote lands** — Mascara section uses a consumer quote, not brand speak. That feels earned.
- **Diverse hero model** — The skin tint section features a deep-complexion model in great light. Shades 30 flexible = inclusive, and the visual proof is right there. That's doing the work for me.
- **20% off sitewide is a real offer** — Concrete, not "limited time savings" vagueness. I can do math with that number.
- **Email renders clean** — No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Clean mobile layout.

## What's weak

- **The offer is buried five sections deep** — Drunk Elephant leads with the hook; Charlotte Tilbury's GWP email put the gift above the fold. ILIA is making me scroll past a brand manifesto to find the reason to shop today. That's backwards.
- **Third ILIA email in three days, and this one re-explains the brand** — After "Welcome" and "Award Winners," I don't need "here are our cult classics." The journey sequencing feels like a drip template misfiring, not intentional curation.
- **"Discover top shelf favorites" subject is doing nothing** — No urgency, no product name, no offer signal. Drunk Elephant would've had a pun. e.l.f. would've put the price in the line. ILIA gave me a vague imperative.
- **No deadline on the 20% off** — I can't do urgency math without an end date. Is this the Friends + Family event? A flash sale? An evergreen code? The ambiguity kills my reason to click today vs. tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 20% off.** Make the hero section the offer, not the brand story. This is what Charlotte Tilbury does and it works — the offer IS the reason to open.
- 2. **Add an end date to the sitewide discount.** "20% off through Sunday" gives me a reason to stop doing Ulta math and act now.
- 3. **Collapse to one product hero per send.** After three emails, pick a lane — either the Super Serum Skin Tint or the mascara, not both. Tight = trustworthy. Drunk Elephant never stacks two hero products in one send.
- 4. **Rethink the journey sequencing** — day 3 should be conversion content, not brand re-intro. Show me the bestseller with reviews and a tight offer. That's all.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font calls will silently fail in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore `@import` rules. Montserrat will not load in these clients — fallback chain (`"Helvetica", sans-serif`) will render instead. This is survivable but should be noted if brand font consistency is required. Mitigation: inline critical font weights via `<link>` in `<head>` (partially respected by Apple Mail/iOS) or accept system font fallback.

**All anchor states neutralized:**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover state has no visual differentiation from static text. Not a blocker (email CTAs typically use button containers), but confirm all interactive links are wrapped in styled button elements rather than bare inline anchors.

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A whitespace-only title provides no context to screen readers that expose the document title. Should be `<title>ILIA – Discover top shelf favorites</title>` or similar.

**Alt text on images:** Cannot verify — `<img>` tags not present in truncated source. Require non-empty `alt` on all product images and decorative `alt=""` on spacers.

**Mobile breakpoint:** 480px threshold is correctly applied across all responsive rules. Column stacking logic (`div.kl-row.colstack`) is present and standard.

**MSO/VML conditionals:** Correctly structured for Outlook rendering. `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` block with `AllowPNG` and 96 DPI is present.
