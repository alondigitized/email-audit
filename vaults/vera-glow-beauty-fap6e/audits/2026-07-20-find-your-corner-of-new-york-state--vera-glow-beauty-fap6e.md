# Find your corner of New York State
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Nine. This is email nine in thirteen days from Glossier, and I am tracking every single one. After they peaked on July 18 with "Savor the Big Apple" — which I genuinely loved, gave it an 8, thought the campaign momentum was landing — they went vlog with "24 Hours with Glossier" yesterday and lost me. Today they're back to product, which should be a win. The concept is cute: each Balm Dotcom flavor maps to a different corner of New York State. Strawberry for upstate berry farms, Mango for the Caribbean-inflected neighborhoods, Black Cherry for wherever, I ❤️ NY for the city itself. Smart brief. Mediocre execution.
- The problem is the email opens with "Did you know?" — which is a hook that makes me go *know what?* and then I have to read body copy I can't parse at mobile size to find out. That's backwards. The whole point of the NY-corners concept is the payoff, and they buried it. What I see is a product catalog with a geographic metaphor that only lands if you're already into the campaign. The free Big Apple Sticker Pack is dangled at the very bottom with truncated copy. A GWP is exactly the move I want at this stage of a product launch — and they put it last.
- Mark as read. I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding. If my friend hadn't already been texting me about the I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom since the July 14 launch, this email would do nothing. It's a campaign afterthought.

## What's working

- **The NY-corners product map is conceptually strong.** Matching each Balm Dotcom flavor to a region of New York State is the kind of brand storytelling Glossier is good at — I can see the brief was smart.
- **Individual product cards are clean.** Each flavor gets its own image, short descriptor, and Shop now button — easy to scan on mobile.
- **No visual chaos.** The email is clean, well-spaced, and on-brand. No broken images, no overlapping text, Glossier red is consistent.
- **I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom placement as a card** (not just the hero) keeps the launch product visible without feeling like they're overselling it — a minor grace note after the July 14–18 push.
- **Sticker pack GWP is a smart loyalty-coded move** — even if it's buried, it's the right kind of Glossier incentive.

## What's weak

- **"Did you know?" is a terrible hook.** It's mystery-box copy that makes me do work. Drunk Elephant opened "Golden hour every hour" and I knew exactly what I was getting. This gives me nothing.
- **No offer with teeth.** Nine emails in, I need a reason to click beyond "here are the products again." No bundle pricing, no loyalty points, no shipping incentive — nothing that moves me.
- **Sticker pack GWP is buried and copy is truncated.** The one moment of actual incentive is at the bottom with cut-off text. e.l.f. would have led with this; Glossier hid it.
- **"Shop now" buttons are generic across every card.** After nine emails, "Shop Strawberry" or "Get the NY bundle" would feel intentional. "Shop now" on five consecutive cards feels like a template default.
- **Cadence is doing real damage here.** Ulta ran duplicate sends on hair care points recently and I flagged it as a credibility problem. Glossier isn't there yet — but nine in thirteen days, with yesterday's content pivot still fresh, and this feels like volume-filling, not storytelling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the geographic concept as actual copy, not "Did you know?"** Open with a headline like "Every corner of New York has a flavor. What's yours?" — let me understand the premise before I see the product grid.
- 2. **Surface the sticker pack GWP above the fold.** Make it the hero moment or at minimum a mid-email callout with visible conditions. "Free with the NY Bundle — limited time" does more work than a buried "Claim yours."
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs by product.** "Shop Black Cherry," "Try Mango," "Get the I ❤️ NY edition" — each should feel like a choice, not a copy-paste.
- 4. **Add a loyalty signal.** I'm a Rouge member at Sephora. Glossier doesn't have a tiered program but they could at minimum say "first to know = first to shop" to signal I'm getting something for being subscribed.
- 5. **Trim the cadence or make this send do more.** ILIA is on daily sends and I called them out for it. Glossier is close to that ceiling.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Which corner of NY are you?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Black Cherry, Strawberry, Mango, and the one you've been waiting for — with a free sticker pack inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Map your flavor. Plus: a free Big Apple Sticker Pack with the NY bundle.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard open-tracking element**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXYCKAM72P7E73P828XGC1FV_0">
```

A `<span>` does not support `rel` or `href` attributes — this will silently fail to load any stylesheet in any standards-compliant renderer. This appears to be a non-standard open-tracking mechanism targeting clients that fetch external CSS files but suppress traditional `<img>` tracking pixels. It is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-- -->` so Outlook will never fire it.
