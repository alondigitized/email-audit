# New at Sally: Summer's Brightest Finds ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. I counted. This is the twenty-first Sally email in roughly three weeks, and they titled this one "Summer's Brightest Finds" — which is essentially "Fresh Finds for Summer" from June 12 (that was number seventeen) with the words shuffled. I noticed immediately. My brain has pattern-matched this sender into the same mental bucket as every other "here's stuff" browse email that shows up when a brand runs out of actual offers to push.
- What's sitting at the very top is a "Big Deals are Coming!" banner — not a deal, a *promise* of a deal. That is bait. Charlotte Tilbury led with "Up To 45% OFF Starts NOW!" in her subject line two weeks ago and I felt it. Sally has a banner that says deals are coming and then shows me a "Shop Now" button for… what, exactly? New at Sally. Cool. I shop at Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond — I need a reason, not an announcement.
- The Pride Month section is at least timely — I'm reviewing this on June 22 and Pride content in June lands vs. feeling performative — and the nature-inspired color block lower down has a "SAVE" callout that's the closest thing to an actual offer in this email. Mark as read. Will not forward.

## What's working

- **Timing on Pride Month content** — colorful, on-calendar for June, the hair color tie-in makes category sense for Sally
- **Hero model is demographically on target** — woman with styled highlighted hair, relevant to the email's "bold color" angle
- **Clean layout, no visible render bugs** — easy to scan, product tiles in the trending section are legible
- **"Nature-inspired color" section has a savings callout** — first actual money signal in the whole email, even if it's buried below the fold
- **Sender ID is unambiguous** — Sally Beauty, recognizable logo, not a deliverability mystery

## What's weak

- **"Big Deals are Coming!" is a tease, not a deal** — that banner earns the scroll-past; Sephora's "Major Summer Deals chapter is closing" had urgency AND a real offer, this has neither
- **"Summer's Brightest Finds" is a recycled concept** — June 12's "Fresh Finds for Summer" was functionally this same email; at email twenty-one I am tracking the repeats
- **Zero offer in the hero** — "Keep summer bright and fresh / Shop Now" sends me nowhere specific; ILIA at least anchored "Skincare Mode: Summer" to a product family with a defined aesthetic
- **No loyalty signal anywhere** — I am a real customer who spends real money at Sally on professional haircolor; nothing in this email acknowledges that
- **"Shop Now" CTA copy on every button** — not one link says what I'm shopping for; the e.l.f. Hair launch email said "Hair it is!" and I clicked reflexively because it was specific

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the actual deal** — if "Big Deals are Coming" is real, launch date + percentage belongs in the hero, not a teaser banner that gets ignored; move the SAVE callout from the nature-inspired section to the top or cut the tease entirely
- 2. **Name a brand in the subject** — the June 20 "Don't Miss 50% Off It's a 10" send scored a pause from me specifically because it named a brand; do that again
- 3. **Retire "Shop Now"** — replace every button with the specific action: "Shop Trending Color," "Explore Pride Looks," "See the SAVE Deal"
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook** — one line acknowledging returning customers ("Stock up before prices change, Beauty Club members") lifts the relational tone without a full redesign
- 5. **Differentiate from the June 12 send** — if you're going to email me twice a week, the creative has to feel different; same vibe, new words is not a new email

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font reference with no @font-face declaration:**

```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```

`Founders Grotesk` is referenced as the primary typeface for all anchor elements, but there is no corresponding `@font-face` rule in the `<style>` block. Every linked element will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif in all clients.

**Stale Open Sans version pinned in woff2 URLs:**

All six `@font-face` blocks hard-code `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Current Open Sans is v40+. Google's CDN has historically served old version paths, but these are static asset URLs — not the version-agnostic Google Fonts API endpoint. If Google retires these paths, all Open Sans rendering silently breaks with no fallback warning.

Correct pattern:
```html
<!-- version-agnostic, always resolves to current -->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Open+Sans:wght@400;600;700;800&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```

**`white-space: pre` on `img` elements:**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```

`white-space: pre` is a text-node property and has no effect on replaced elements (`<img>`). It is harmless but is likely a copy/paste artifact from a text element rule. Remove to avoid confusion.

**Mobile breakpoint at 499px:**

The sole responsive breakpoint is `@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)`. The industry standard for email is 600px. Devices rendering at 500–600px (tablets in portrait, some large Android phones) will receive the desktop layout without column stacking. Verify this is intentional.

**Accessibility — cannot fully assess from truncated source.** Check that:
- All `<img>` tags carry meaningful `alt` attributes (or `alt=""` for decorative images)
- CTA buttons have readable text nodes, not image-only buttons
- Color contrast meets WCAG AA (especially `.EID a { color: #464646 }` against background)
