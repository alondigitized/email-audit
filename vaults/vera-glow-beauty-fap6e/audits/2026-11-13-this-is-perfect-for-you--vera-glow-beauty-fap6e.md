# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-13**
## Executive summary

- It's Skechers in my beauty inbox again, and the subject "This is perfect for you!" is a promise the email doesn't keep. It's the same browse-abandonment nudge I've had about nine times in two weeks. 11/12 was "Don't leave these styles behind!" and 11/7 was "This style has limited availability!" Now the hero says "Still thinking about it? You've got great taste!" and shows one Britto 1992 sneaker. I never put anything in a cart, so "still thinking" reads as fake familiarity.
- The Black Friday banner is the only thing I'd do math on. BOGO 50% off, or 20% off a single pair, is about 25% effective across two pairs. It sits below two screens of product grid. Sephora's Savings Event emails put the tier discount first, and this one puts a lone shoe first. I'm not clicking, and I wouldn't forward it. A friend who runs would care, but I'm a lipstick-and-serum girl.

## What's working

- The Britto 1992 hero is a colorful, clean product shot. The pink and teal sole is a fun moment.
- "BUY ONE, GET ONE 50% OFF OR 20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR" is a real offer I can calculate. "Discount auto-applied at cart" means no code to hunt for.
- The navy banner is the strongest contrast block and does stop my scroll.
- "Take another look" is a clear button, and the 3x2 grid of women's styles is easy to scan.

## What's weak

- The subject says "perfect for you" but I've never browsed Skechers sneakers. The hero's "Still thinking about it?" reads as an assumption, not personalization.
- The offer is buried. A Black Friday countdown appears only after the hero and six product tiles. Ulta would have led with the deal.
- The product captions are tiny and unreadable on a phone without zooming. There are no prices, reviews or sale tags, so nothing helps me decide.
- There's no loyalty or member angle at all, which is a miss for someone who does tier math on every send.
- The offer window is 11/12 to 11/17 but never appears as a countdown. A "countdown" with no clock isn't credible urgency, and the fine print excludes sale styles.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the BOGO banner above the hero and make the offer the headline. Keep the hero product under it.
- 2. Show real browse data with price and "was/now" on the hero, or drop the "still thinking" line for people who didn't browse.
- 3. Add one social-proof line (stars or review count) and bigger captions.
- 4. Add a real countdown or an "ends 11/17" badge near the deal.
- 5. Add a member-pricing line if there's a loyalty program.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off sneakers through 11/17`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Britto sneaker + Black Friday BOGO`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy one, get one 50% off, auto-applied at cart. Ends 11/17.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Britto 1992 is still here, and now it's part of BOGO 50% off.`
