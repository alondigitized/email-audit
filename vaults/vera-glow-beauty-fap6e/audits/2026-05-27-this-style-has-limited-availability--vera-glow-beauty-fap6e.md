# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Email seven from Skechers in under three weeks, and this one lands the morning after what was already a double-send day on the 26th — "Your cart expires soon!" plus "This is perfect for you!" in the same inbox session. At this point opening a Skechers email feels like a reflex, not a decision. The browse-abandon hook — "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." — is genuinely the best copy this brand has written in this entire run. It's self-aware, it's direct, and it almost made me smile. Almost.
- The problem is that the hero product is Skechers Garage, which is visually male-coded — chunky, graphic, sporty in a way that skews sneakerhead-boy-bedroom rather than anyone-who-shops-in-my-feed. If Skechers actually knows I browsed that shoe, cool, show it to me. But paired with a product grid of generic slip-in thumbnails and a 20% off banner buried below two scrolls, the whole email feels like it was assembled from parts rather than built for *me*. The scarcity hook in the subject — "limited availability" — is doing a lot of heavy lifting for a claim I have no reason to believe after six previous urgency pings.
- I would not click. I would definitely not forward this. I would do what I've been doing with Skechers all week: passive mark-as-read while quietly wondering when the unsubscribe threshold hits.

## What's working

- **"HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO."** — the best browse-abandon headline I've seen from Skechers in this entire run; it earns the acknowledge-the-awkward tone without being cloying
- **20% off a single pair** is a real, concrete offer — clearer and more credible than the vague "exclusive offers" that have peppered this cadence
- **No visual clutter or render bugs** — clean layout, dark CTA button has good contrast, footer utility links (Afterpay, Find a Store) are legible
- **Women's Slip-ins appear in the product grid** — at least the secondary recommendations gesture toward my half of the market
- **Subject line is under 40 characters** — mobile-readable, no all-caps or triple-exclamation abuse

## What's weak

- **Hero product is male-coded** — Skechers Garage with a graphic/web design reads as a men's sneaker; if this is truly my browsed item, the demographic mismatch is a data problem, not a design one; if it's not my item, it's worse
- **20% off is below the fold** — the actual offer that might motivate a click is invisible until I scroll past the hero and the full product grid; Charlotte Tilbury leads with the discount at hero level and it's dramatically more effective
- **"Limited availability" with zero evidence** — after six urgency-flavored emails in three weeks, this claim registers as hollow; e.l.f. at least names new shades to back up their scarcity; Skechers just asserts it
- **Seven emails in under three weeks, second day after a double-send** — cadence this aggressive has a cumulative suppression effect that no single email's execution can overcome; Sephora sent me three "ENDS TODAY" emails in a single day and that's the bar for "too much," and Skechers is approaching it from a different angle but still getting there

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 20% off offer above the fold** — make it the hero banner, not a reward for scrolling; right now the best reason to click is hidden behind two content blocks
- 2. **Confirm the hero is genuinely my browsed product** — if it's not, swap it for the women's slip-ins already in the grid; a mis-targeted hero kills the "SEEN THIS BEFORE?" premise immediately
- 3. **Put a real scarcity signal behind the subject claim** — "only 3 left in your size" or "low stock in size 8" is actionable; "limited availability" is noise
- 4. **Back off the cadence** — this email can't save itself from being the seventh interruption; Drunk Elephant managed twelve emails in their extended-sale run but each one had a distinct narrative beat; Skechers has sent variations of the same abandon trigger four times in nine days
- 5. **Rewrite the CTA from "SHOP NOW" to something specific** — "Get 20% Off My Pair" earns the click; "SHOP NOW" loses it
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your size is running low — 20% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Grab it before it's gone (+ 20% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off a single pair while your size lasts`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Low stock on your browsed style — here's your discount`
