# Welcome to ILIA
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ILIA landing in my welcome queue during an active Friends + Family sale is actually smart timing — but "Welcome to ILIA" as a subject line is doing the bare minimum. I'm Sephora Rouge, I've swatched ILIA tinted serums at the counter, I already know who they are. The welcome framing feels like it's written for a stranger, not someone who just opted in mid-sale. The second I opened it though, I was pleasantly surprised — this email is genuinely well-organized. The 20% off banner hits immediately, the "Cart Collage" section is a clever name for a GWP-stacking concept, and the VIP pitch makes total sense in a welcome context.
- That said, I kept doing the math and realized there's no explicit end date on the sale anywhere I can see. ILIA is asking me to urgently shop a sale I can't verify the end date for, which is a trust friction point. Compare that to Drunk Elephant, which at an 8/10 got me on the hook with a clever wordplay subject and then delivered a tight offer — ILIA's body copy does the work but the subject/preview combo buries the lead hard.
- Would I click? Yes — the 20% off sitewide plus a free mini is genuinely compelling for a brand I already intend to try. Would I forward? Maybe to one specific friend who's been asking me about clean beauty finds. But this sits firmly in "glad I opened it, wish they'd earned the open harder" territory.

## What's working

- The top banner leading with "20% OFF SITEWIDE | 20% OFF FOR VIP" is exactly where an offer should live — I see it before I even commit to scrolling.
- "A Cart Collage" is a genuinely creative framing for a gift-with-purchase section; naming the curation behavior makes me feel like a stylist, not a shopper.
- The VIP section with navy background and lash product photography breaks the scroll pattern effectively — it's visually the most premium thing in the email.
- "Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" tagline is doing brand equity work I appreciate — ILIA knows its clean-beauty positioning and repeats it without screaming.
- The footer icons (free shipping threshold, cruelty-free, join VIP, pact recycling) are efficient trust cues that Ulta and Sally Beauty never bother with at that tier.

## What's weak

- "Welcome to ILIA" is one of the most forgettable subject lines I've seen this week — Glossier's "Eau de main character" drove a click on wordplay alone; ILIA gave me nothing to bite on.
- No explicit sale end date anywhere in the visible email — Ulta's mystery offer email at least signals urgency; this sale could end tonight or next month for all I know.
- The "A Cart Collage" product details are small and require close reading on mobile — "full-size bronzer or highlighter to $37" reads like pricing but isn't clearly a free gift vs. an upsell.
- No name personalization anywhere — they have my email, this is a triggered welcome, and yet I'm receiving zero signal that they know anything about me. Charlotte Tilbury at least does the "darling" language to simulate intimacy; ILIA went fully anonymous.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the sale, not the brand** — the welcome moment is the vehicle, not the destination. Lead with what's inside.
- 2. **Add a countdown or hard end date** to the Friends + Family sale — even "ends Sunday" would convert the hesitation into urgency.
- 3. **Clarify the Cart Collage section** — make it instantly obvious these are free gifts with purchase, not upsells. The current copy is too close to product listing layout.
- 4. **Add one skin-type or shade signal** — even a "What's your skin concern?" CTA would start building the personalization loop that makes a welcome email actually valuable long-term.
- 5. **Preheader needs to front-load the offer** — don't bury 20% off behind the brand tagline copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Friends + Family Sale: 20% Off, Free Mini Inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your ILIA welcome gift: 20% off sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends soon — 20% off + a free mini with every order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Friends + Family Sale is live. Stack your gifts.`

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is blank**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Appears as an empty tab label in browser-based opens (Gmail web, etc.). Should be the email subject line.

**CSS `@import` for fonts — not supported in major clients**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (all versions), Outlook, and Apple Mail on iOS. Montserrat will silently fall back to the declared `"Helvetica", sans-serif` stack, but the custom Klaviyo font (`SguDsz`) will fail entirely in those clients with no declared fallback.

**Conflicting `p` CSS rules**

Two separate `p` rule blocks exist in the same `<style>` tag:
```css
/* Block 1 */
p { display: block; margin: 13px 0 }

/* Block 2 — later in same block */
p { margin-left: 0; margin-right: 0; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0; padding-bottom: 1em }
```
Block 2 overrides Block 1's margins. Block 1 is dead code — the `display: block` is the only surviving declaration. This is likely a Klaviyo template artifact but adds unnecessary weight and confusion.

**Link color accessibility**

All five `<a>` states use `color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none`. With no underline and a single color state, links are distinguishable from body text by color only — this fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color). No hover underline is applied either; hover CSS is supported in many modern email clients.
