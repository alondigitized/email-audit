# There's a party in NYC
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Day eleven. I know because I've been tracking this sale like I track my Sephora Rouge points — obsessively. The Glossier send on June 17 ("Friends don't let friends pay full price") was genuinely charming. This one? It's the morning-after version: the party hookline in the subject, then you open and it's basically the same sale extension announcement dressed in an NYC confetti metaphor. I appreciate the creative pivot — anchoring a sale extension to what I assume is Pride weekend in New York is a smart and very Glossier move. But "Hi there," with no name after ten days of being their BFF is a small betrayal. I know my name. They know my name.
- The email is stark — no images, no hero, no button, just bolded copy and hyperlinked product names. That's Glossier's whole thing and I actually respect the restraint. The product suggestions (Five Minute Face trio, Invisible Shield, Balm Dotcom Strawberry) are exactly what I'd reach for in summer. But after eleven days of this sale, I want either something new or a hard deadline, not a soft "we extended it" with no end date. I'm clicking on Balm Dotcom Strawberry because I want it. I'm not clicking because this email earned it.
- Would I forward? No. Would I click? Yes, but for the product, not the email.

## What's working

- **The NYC hook lands.** Tying a sale extension to Pride weekend energy is clever — it reframes "we're still trying to sell you something" as "let's celebrate together." Very on-brand.
- **Product curation is exactly right.** Five Minute Face, summer fragrance duo, Invisible Shield + Balm Dotcom — these are the Glossier hits I actually use and would restock during a sale.
- **The bold offer is immediately readable.** "20% off sitewide or 25% off your qualifying order" — bolded, early, scannable. In a plain-text email this is the visual hierarchy doing its job.
- **The BFF voice is intact.** "xoxo, The Glossier Team" and "BFFs" still feels warm and not performative, even on send eleven.
- **Terms link is present.** Fine print is there, non-threatening. Honest offer.

## What's weak

- **"Hi there," after eleven emails.** If Glossier knows I'm a BFF and I've been in their sale for eleven days, not having my first name feels like a merge token failure or deliberate impersonalization — either way it stings a little.
- **No hard deadline on the extension.** "We've extended" is vague. Charlotte Tilbury and ILIA both showed me countdowns or specific end dates. This email gives me zero urgency to click today versus Thursday.
- **The CTA is just "here."** "You can shop our whole sale here!" — that "here" is the weakest hyperlink text Glossier could have written. Even in a plain-text send, something like "shop the sale" takes two extra words and does actual work.
- **Zero visual break between the copy and the product list.** On mobile, the bullet list runs straight into the opener without any breathing room. My eye had to work to find where the recs started.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline.** "Extended through Sunday" or "48 hours left" does more than any creative hook. The reason ILIA's "Summer Skin Staples" from June 16 worked better than this is that it gave me a reason to act *today*.
- 2. **Fix the CTA text.** Change "you can shop our whole sale here!" to "shop the full sale →" or even a standalone line: "**Shop the sale.**" One small tweak, real lift.
- 3. **Personalize the greeting.** At sale day eleven, "Hi Vera," earns the BFF sign-off. "Hi there," undermines it.
- 4. **Give the product bullets one-line reasons-to-buy.** "Invisible Shield — the one that doesn't pill under makeup" is more useful than just the name + a general use case.
- 5. **Anchor the NYC angle to something real.** If this is a Pride send, say so. Glossier has the brand equity to be explicit about it — the vague "confetti, the noise" feels like hedging when their audience would love the direct call.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% sitewide or 25% on qualifying — ends soon.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is sent via Klaviyo (tracking domain `ctrk.klclick.com`, font CDN `static-forms.klaviyo.com`). ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Opaque Klaviyo redirects — UTM parameters unverifiable**
All 9 visible links resolve through `ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVD9PXV02GXECKFCTZKMZ994_N`. The final destination URLs and any UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the source. If Klaviyo is not configured to append UTM params at redirect time, attributed traffic will be lost.

**`rel="nofollow"` on all email links**
Every `<a>` carries `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow"`. `nofollow` is a web/SEO directive with zero effect in email clients — it does not belong in email HTML and suggests the template was authored or post-processed with web-first tooling. Not harmful, but indicates a template hygiene issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hardcoded `background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)` on every `<p>` and `<ul>`**
Every block element has this inline. Apple Mail, Gmail App (dark), and Samsung Mail will not invert these elements to dark mode — users in dark mode see white boxes over a dark canvas. Fix: remove inline background-color from content elements and handle at the container/wrapper level with `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` or Klaviyo's dark mode block.

**`<p>` inside `<li>` throughout**
The list items wrap content in `<p>` tags: `<li><p ...>...</p></li>`. This is technically valid HTML but introduces double vertical spacing in several clients (Yahoo Mail, older Outlook WebApp). Standard email practice is bare `<li>` text nodes.

**Empty paragraph spacer with zero-width joiner**
`<p ...><span style="font-size: 14px;">​</span></p>` — The `​` (U+200D, zero-width joiner) is used as a visual spacer. This is a rendering hack; spacing is inconsistent across clients and the character may be read aloud by screen readers depending on UA.

**No `lang` attribute visible on `<html>` tag**
Cannot confirm `<html lang="en">` from the provided source. Missing `lang` prevents screen readers from selecting the correct pronunciation engine.

**Custom font loaded via `@import`**
`@import url("https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/Sn6eFZ/custom_fonts.css")` — correctly gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` to exclude Outlook. The inline font stacks (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) serve as fallback, which is correct. No issue if the CDN is reliable, but worth noting the external dependency.
