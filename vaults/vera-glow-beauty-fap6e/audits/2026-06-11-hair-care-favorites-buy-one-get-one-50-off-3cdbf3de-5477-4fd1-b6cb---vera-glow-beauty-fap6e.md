# Hair Care Favorites: Buy One, Get One 50% Off ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- I'm somewhere north of sixteen Sally emails in three weeks — the June 5 "Brighten Up for Summer" send was already flagging fifteen — and this subject actually earned the open. Clean BOGO 50%, no emoji theater, no fake "Last Call" language like the May 29 disaster that immediately led into a flash sale that ran through the 31st. I clicked in cautiously optimistic. And then I'm greeted not by the BOGO that was promised, but by a 10% sitewide box with code SAVE10 ending June 16. Then the BOGO hero with an end date of June 30. Then a Wella Lightener Bundle at $16.99 ending June 15. Three deals. Three deadlines. Zero alignment with what the subject line told me I was coming for.
- That said — the Wella Lightener Bundle at $16.99 is the sharpest thing this email does. That's the reason I shop Sally at all: Sephora doesn't carry professional lighteners, and Ulta's Wella section is a coin flip. Seeing a specific product at a specific price with product imagery made me stop scrolling. That's a screenshot-and-share moment for my group chat.
- Verdict: I'd click on the Wella bundle specifically. I would not forward this email — I'd screenshot the bundle section, caption it "SAVE THIS," and drop it in my color-obsessed group chat. The email itself is doing too many things simultaneously to earn a share.

## What's working

- The Wella Lightener Bundle callout at $16.99 is the MVP — specific product, specific price, product photography. This is how Sally earns a conversion from someone who already knows what she wants.
- The subject line is genuinely clean for Sally — concrete mechanic (BOGO 50%), one emoji, under 50 characters, no "FREE!!!" stacking. That's the most restrained subject line in weeks.
- The blonde model with freshly lightened hair is pitch-perfect demographic alignment for a professional bleach bundle — she's the exact person in the market for this product.
- Free 1-hour delivery with a $35 purchase in the top banner is a real differentiator that competitors can't touch — for a last-minute lightener situation, that's legitimately useful.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, readable without zooming on mobile.

## What's weak

- Three separate offers, three separate end dates (June 15, June 16, June 30) — I'm doing project management, not shopping.
- The 10% sitewide box lands *above* the hero BOGO — so Sally opens by undermining the deal it just promised me in the subject line. That's a bait-and-switch in hierarchy if not in intent.
- Zero Beauty Club points mention anywhere in the email. I'm enrolled. The points math is the whole game. Sephora's June sale (which I scored a 9) at least gestures at Rouge member benefits — Sally can't even tell me if this BOGO stacks with points.
- "Shop now" is the CTA on every single element. Even Lush, which I scored a 9 last week, manages more specific action language. Three "Shop now" buttons in sequence is lazy.
- "Select hair care" in the BOGO hero is vague enough that I have no idea if Redken, Kenra, or Matrix are in the pool — those names would have told me immediately whether this sale is for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lead and commit.** The 10% sitewide box should drop below the BOGO hero or become a "Beauty Club exclusive — stack this with your BOGO" callout. Two co-equal hero offers fight each other and neither wins.
- 2. **Name the brands in the BOGO pool.** Three product tiles — Redken, Wella, Kenra — would tell me in two seconds whether I'm in. "Select hair care" makes me do homework before I can buy.
- 3. **Add one line of points math.** "Beauty Club members earn points on your BOGO purchase" takes seven words and closes me every time. This is the single cheapest lift available.
- 4. **Sharpen every CTA.** "Shop BOGO Hair Care," "Add Wella Bundle to Bag," "Shop the Sale" — anything beats "Shop now" times three.
- 5. **Consolidate the deadlines.** June 15 and June 16 are essentially the same window. Collapsing them removes one cognitive load item without losing any urgency.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Wella Lightener $16.99 + BOGO 50% off — your June hair haul`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wella, Redken, Matrix — name the brands. They're in the sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beauty Club members: your points stack on the BOGO. Ends June 30.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `@font-face` for primary font:**
The global `a {}` rule declares `font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif`, but there is no `@font-face` block loading `Founders Grotesk`. All anchor text will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif in every client. Either add a self-hosted or CDN `@font-face` for Founders Grotesk, or remove it from the stack.

**Issue — Pinned stale Google Fonts URLs:**
All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com` at version `v17` (e.g. `opensans/v17/mem8YaGs126MiZpBA-UFVZ0bf8pkAg.woff2`). Current Open Sans is v28+. Google continues serving old versions but these are subject to deprecation without notice; switch to version-agnostic CSS API URLs (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Open+Sans...`) or self-host.

**Issue — `white-space:pre` on `img` selector:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```
`white-space:pre` is a text property with no effect on replaced elements (`<img>`), but it can interact unexpectedly with inline image rendering in older Outlook versions. Remove it.

**Issue — Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
Responsive block fires at `max-width:499px`. Industry standard is `600px`. Devices with viewport widths 500–600px (tablets, large phones in portrait) will receive the desktop layout without stacking — this includes iPad Mini and many Android phones. Change to `max-width:600px`.
