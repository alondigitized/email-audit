## Executive summary

- Day eleven. I know it's day eleven because I've been counting, and the only reason this one got an open instead of a swipe-archive is that "What the actual h.e.l.f.?" made me literally put my phone down for a second and laugh. - But once I'm inside, the email is a **lot**. I scroll through the hero, then a full product lineup module, then a solo product feature, then the Frizz to Bliss Trio at $27 — and that Trio is genuinely the best thing in here. Twenty-seven dollars for a bundled set is a real price, especially compared to what I'd spend on a single prestige haircare product at Sephora. Drunk Elephant doesn't give me bundle pricing. ## What's working

- "WHAT THE H.E.L.F.?" as hero copy is legitimately funny — the wordplay lands and the surprised-woman visual sells the bit immediately
- Frizz to Bliss Trio at **$27** is a concrete, visible price point — no "starting at" hedging, no fine-print chase
- "look who's e.l.f.ing hair." secondary headline keeps the brand voice playful and consistent through the scroll
- "Top Picks for You" section signals they're at least attempting personalization — better than a cold product dump
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds on mobile

## What's weak

- The $27 Trio offer is buried in module four — Glossier would've led with the deal; this email makes me earn it
- This is the **fourth e.l.f. Hair email** I've seen since the TikTok Shop exclusive launch on June 16; the cadence is eating the goodwill that subject line just bought
- Every CTA says "Shop Now" — ILIA runs specific button copy like "Shop the Lip" or category-specific verbs; "Shop Now" at this frequency just reads as placeholder
- Zero clarity on whether the Trio earns Beauty Squad points or routes me through TikTok Shop again — that distinction is the difference between me buying now vs. waiting for the next Sephora Beauty Insider event
- That fourth module has text that's too small to read comfortably — something about "10" or a count, but I can't tell if it's urgency, a product number, or a sale countdown, which means it's working for no one

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Trio** — put the $27 bundle in the hero or module two, not module four; everything before it is throat-clearing
- 2. **Clarify the purchase path explicitly** — "Available on elfcosmetics.com — earn Beauty Squad points" or call out TikTok Shop if that's the exclusive; the ambiguity is a conversion killer for a points-first shopper like me
- 3. **Retire "Shop Now" from every button** — "Get the $27 Trio," "Shop Frizz to Bliss," anything with product specificity; Sally Beauty would've done this better and that's a bar e.l.f. shouldn't be near
- 4. **Add one real urgency signal** — a "this week only" or a quantity cap on the Trio would've had me clicking before I finished reading

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze (formerly Appboy), sent from the `m.elfcosmetics.com` marketing subdomain with custom click-tracking on `links.elfcosmetics.com`. The visible HTML is structurally sound but has several accessibility gaps, a redundant nested table artifact, and UTM observability cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze legacy CDN domain in use**
- Image asset is hosted at `braze-images.com/appboy/...` — the `/appboy/` path is the pre-rebrand (legacy) Appboy asset path. This still resolves, but it is a legacy origin. No broken link risk today, but worth flagging if e.l.f. ever migrates Braze instances.

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
- The logo CTA routes through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` (Braze click wrapper). Final destination UTM params are appended at redirect time and are not visible in the source. QA should click-trace the link to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on the landing page. This audit cannot confirm or deny.

**No issues** with the click-tracking domain itself (`links.elfcosmetics.com` is correctly branded, not a raw `click.braze.com` fallback).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`tabindex="-1"` on linked logo image**
- Selector: `a[href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..."]` has `tabindex="-1"`, removing this CTA from the keyboard tab order entirely. Keyboard-only users cannot activate the only visible link in the header.

**`outline:none` on the same link**
- `style="outline:none"` suppresses the focus ring, compounding the keyboard accessibility issue above.

**External Google Fonts will not load in Outlook or most webmail clients**
- `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans">` is inside a `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`  conditional, so Outlook correctly skips it. But Gmail strips external `<link>` tags entirely. The fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) will be used universally in practice — acceptable, but the Google Fonts call is wasted bandwidth for every other client.

**Redundant nested `nl-container` table**
- Inside `html_block block-1`, the content re-opens a full `nl-container > row-1 > column > image_block` structure, mirroring the outer table hierarchy. This is a Braze template artifact (an embedded sub-template inside an HTML block). It renders fine but adds ~400 bytes of unnecessary markup and creates unexpected DOM depth that can confuse some older rendering engines.

**Three empty `html_block` tables (block-2, block-3, block-4)**
- Each contains only whitespace: `<div style="..."> </div>`. Dead weight — likely placeholder blocks left in the template. No rendering harm, but should be removed before send.

**`<title>` is empty**
- `<title></title>` — a missing title is a minor deliverability signal (some spam filters weight it) and causes screen readers to announce the email with no name.
