# Summer Must-Haves Are Here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Sally lands in my inbox for what I'm counting as send thirty-four — and the subject is "Summer Must-Haves Are Here." I had to check my archive, because "Your summer must-haves" dropped exactly seven days ago. Seven days. Same concept, one word swapped. And this is coming off July 13's "Summer hair starts with a freebie" and July 14's "Hair care for pool days & heat waves" — Sally has sent me five emails about summer hair in seven days, two of which are essentially the same brief.
- The email itself renders cleanly. Red hero, dark-haired model, six products across three rows, Klarna at the bottom. It looks fine. But there is no offer. No percentage off, no gift with purchase, no loyalty perk — just "Shop now" repeated six times across the scroll. Sally's own July 13th send had an actual hook baked into the subject. This one is a product catalogue in a seasonal costume.
- I'm marking this read. Nothing here earns a click, and I would not forward it to anyone.

## What's working

- The red hero banner is visually dominant — eye goes straight there, hierarchy is clear even if the destination isn't compelling
- The model with dark wavy hair reads late-20s and natural, which is a reasonable demographic signal for me specifically
- Kenra Silk Drops is a legitimate product I've had on my radar — good brand pull-through in the middle section lifts the credibility of the edit
- Klarna integration at the bottom makes sense for the curling iron and deep waver (these are $50+ items where installment framing actually helps)

## What's weak

- "Summer must-haves" was literally the July 9th subject — this is not a follow-up, it is the same send with one word changed, seven days later
- No offer whatsoever — no percentage, no GWP, no member pricing, no threshold — Sally's own July 13th freebie email had more reason to exist than this
- The hero promises "soft, shiny & hydrated / hair care that protects and repairs," then the email ends on hot tools — those are different shopping modes and they undercut each other
- Six products, six "Shop now" buttons, all at identical visual weight — when everything is the hero, nothing is
- Zero loyalty or rewards language anywhere visible — for a brand competing in the same channel space as Ulta's 21 Days of Beauty, that absence is loud

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the "summer must-haves" frame entirely** — it's been used twice in a week; find a specific angle (the scalp care angle from the itch serum, or the "humidity hair" angle) and own that instead
- 2. **Anchor the email on one hero product** with a price point and a why-now reason — the Silk Drops or the bond conditioner alone would make a stronger send than six products at equal weight
- 3. **Add an offer** — even "free standard shipping on $35+" is something; the Klarna integration tells me Sally knows the basket is an issue, but they haven't connected that insight to the email strategy
- 4. **Split styling tools into their own send** — treatments and hot tools are different purchase decisions and different moods; running them in the same email dilutes both
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — the top bar appears to be fine-print shipping copy, which is what leaks into preview text; that's not a hook
- **Subject Alt A:** `Silk Drops + a bond conditioner Sally swears by for summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your humidity hair fix is in here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Frizz control, scalp care, and the waver worth the counter space.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six summer hair saves — including the one your pool days need.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Undeclared font `Founders Grotesk`:**
The global anchor rule `a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a font with no `@font-face` declaration in the `<style>` block. Only Open Sans has declarations. `Founders Grotesk` will silently fall back to Helvetica for all recipients, meaning CTA button typography may not match design intent.

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts version:**
Open Sans is loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/` — version 17 is significantly outdated (current is v40+). While functionally harmless in most clients, it's a maintenance flag.

**Issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`:**
`white-space` has no effect on replaced elements like `<img>`. This is dead CSS, likely a template artifact. Not a rendering risk but indicates template hygiene issues.

**Positive signals:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` present — correct for Apple Mail
