# NEW! Inner Truth Bath Melt
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen. We are at fourteen Lush emails in roughly three and a half weeks, and I want to be fair to this one because the cause commitment here is genuinely legit — 70% of the purchase price going to trans athlete protection organizations is not a vague "we care" gesture. That's a specific, auditable number, and I respect it.
- But I came here for a bath melt and the email never actually sold me on it. The hero image is all soft gradients and movement — beautiful, very Lush — but I cannot tell you what this product smells like, what it looks like unwrapped, or why Inner Truth is the name. The "SUPPORT TRANS ATHLETES" headline completely dominates, and the product itself is subordinate copy. Compare this to the ingredient-focused send from June 8 (the "What makes us smell so good?" one), where I actually learned something about what I was buying. This one swaps product education for values declaration, and after thirteen other emails I need more than values to add to cart.
- Would I click? Probably, but only to find out what the bath melt actually is — not because this email sold me. Would I forward to my beauty group chat? No. Would I screenshot and send to one friend who's deep into trans rights advocacy? Maybe. That's not a commerce outcome; that's a cause-marketing outcome, which is fine if that was the goal.

## What's working

- The 70% donation figure is specific and prominent — Lush didn't hide it in fine print, which builds trust immediately.
- Hero image is gorgeous; the fluid, gradient abstraction is visually cohesive with Lush's identity and Pride timing.
- "NEW!" in the subject does what it needs to do — it signals a launch, not a repeat promo blast.
- App promo banner at the top is a consistent touchpoint; at least the $5-off incentive stays visible to app members.
- June timing for trans athlete advocacy is on-calendar and not opportunistic-feeling — Lush has been consistent enough on this beat that it reads as authentic.

## What's weak

- The product is invisible as a product — no scent cues, no texture language, no close-up of what this bath melt looks like. After the banana peel email and the fragrance-layering deep-dive, this feels like a step backward.
- "Shop Now" is the laziest CTA Lush could have chosen here. "Meet Inner Truth" or "Shop + Give 70%" would do real work.
- The "minus the taxes" caveat on the 70% donation figure is buried body copy — it's the kind of fine-print hedge that undercuts an otherwise clean activism message.
- No product detail whatsoever: scent profile, skin benefits, how to use it. The small icon row at the bottom (vegan, press-free, etc.) barely moves the needle.
- Cadence fatigue is real — email fourteen in three weeks means my finger is hovering over the unsubscribe link and this send doesn't give me enough product pull to override that impulse.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the product, back it with the cause.** Show me a glamour shot of Inner Truth — the actual melt, the color, the fizz. Put "SUPPORT TRANS ATHLETES" as the secondary headline below it. The cause earns more trust when the product looks worth buying.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to earn its purpose.** "Shop + Donate 70%" is one line and does two jobs. "Shop Now" is wasted real estate.
- 3. **Add one scent or ingredient callout.** Even a single line — "Lemon myrtle + cedarwood for clarity and calm" — makes this feel like a beauty email that's also doing good, not a nonprofit email that happens to sell something.
- 4. **Drop the "minus the taxes" hedge or reframe it.** If it has to be disclosed, fold it into the CTA subtext, not the main body copy.
- 5. **Dial back cadence.** Nothing in this review survives fourteen sends in three weeks. Drunk Elephant sent four emails in eight days and I still thought the content was solid — but they weren't sending twice per day in the same week.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lush's newest bath melt donates the majority of every sale to trans athlete orgs.`

## Technical Audit — Lush USA "NEW! Inner Truth Bath Melt"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Braze-delivered table-based email with MSO/VML Outlook compatibility layer. The truncated source exposes several rendering, compliance, and tracking concerns; a full audit would require the complete HTML.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **All links wrap through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com`** (Braze click-tracking). Final destination URLs and any UTM parameters are obscured behind the redirect — cannot confirm UTM hygiene without following each redirect chain.
- **Source is truncated** (`…` at the image block `href`), so not all CTAs could be audited. At minimum the hero CTA and any footer links are unverified.
- **No bare/raw URLs detected** in visible source — tracking wrapper is consistently applied to the one confirmed link.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Empty `<title>` tag | `<title></title>` — some clients (Outlook web, Gmail) surface this in tab/window labels |
| CSS `background-image` on `<table>` element | `style="…background-image:url(https://cdn.braze.eu/appboy/…original.png…)"` on `row-2` table — not rendered by Outlook (any version), Windows Mail, or older Android clients |
| Asset served from EU CDN for US audience | Same Braze EU CDN URL above — latency concern for US recipients; also a signal the asset was uploaded to the EU Braze instance rather than US |
| `alt` attribute on logo image unverifiable | Source truncated before `alt` value; if empty or missing, broken-image state has no fallback label |
| Fixed 500 px container | `width:500px` on all row-content tables — narrower than the common 600 px standard; no functional breakage but worth flagging |

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge/personalization tokens (`{{first_name}}`, `[[EMAIL]]`, etc.) detected in the visible source. The `$5 off` banner appears hard-coded rather than dynamically populated. No issues with unrendered tokens in the auditable portion.

---

### 5. Compliance

- **Unsubscribe link**: Not present in the auditable portion. CAN-SPAM §5 requires a clear, functional opt-out mechanism. The footer is cut off — confirm it contains a working unsubscribe link and physical postal address before send.
- **Physical mailing address**: Not visible in truncated source — required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5). Must be present somewhere in the email body.
- **Sender authentication**: `hello@hello.us.lush.com` is a subdomain sender. SPF/DKIM alignment to `us.lush.com` (not `lush.com`) must be verified; a misconfigured subdomain is a common DMARC failure point. Cannot confirm from HTML alone — check sending domain DNS records.
- **Preheader / subject mismatch risk**: Hidden preheader reads `🏳️‍⚧️ Support trans athletes`; first visible element reads `🏳️‍⚧️ Enjoy $5 off with the Lush app!`. These are semantically different messages sharing the same emoji. In clients that leak preheader text into the visible body (some webmail), this pairing could appear contradictory.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **UTM parameters unverifiable** — all destination URLs are behind Braze redirect wrappers. Must confirm that the Braze link configuration appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before final redirect.
- **No evidence of deep-linking** to the specific product page ("Inner Truth Bath Melt") in the confirmed link — the banner CTA resolves through a generic `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` redirect. Confirm the landing page is the product page, not the homepage.
- **App deep-link**: The banner promotes a `$5 off with the Lush app` offer but links to a web URL, not a universal link or app scheme. If the offer is app-exclusive, web-click recipients will likely hit a page that cannot redeem the offer.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Verify the footer is complete** — confirm CAN-SPAM unsubscribe link and physical address are present before send.
2. **Fix or replace the `background-image` on the row-2 table** — use a foreground `<img>` tag with a proper `alt` attribute so Outlook recipients see the intended visual.
3. **Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment** for `hello.us.lush.com` subdomain — run `dig TXT hello.us.lush.com` and verify `dmarc=pass` in a test send header.
4. **Audit Braze link configuration** to confirm UTM parameters are appended to all redirect destinations, especially the hero CTA.
5. **Resolve the app-offer / web-link mismatch** — if the `$5 off` is app-redeemable only, the link should fire a universal link (`https://` with `apple-app-site-association` branch) or include explicit redemption instructions on the landing page.
6. **Add `alt` text** to all image blocks and verify it is non-empty in the complete source.
7. **Migrate the background asset** to the US Braze CDN instance to eliminate EU-origin latency for US recipients.
