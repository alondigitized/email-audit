# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- It's Skechers in my beauty inbox again. I haven't put a shoe in a cart, and this is the same "second look" browse-abandonment template I got on 11/7 under this exact subject, "This style has limited availability!" The subject promises scarcity. The hero says "How about a second look? Seen this before? We thought so." So the scarcity claim goes nowhere. After the 11/12 "Don't leave these styles behind!" and the 11/13 "This is perfect for you!", it reads as the fourth or fifth nudge in a week.
- The one thing I'd actually do math on is buried halfway down: Black Friday Countdown, BOGO 50% off or 20% off a single pair. That's a real offer, and it beats Sally Beauty's "Black Friday Savings Came Early!" banner, which never put a number in the hero. But the hero is a single Britto sneaker, and the offer sits below six near-identical black and white runners. Ulta puts its $10/$15/$20 ladder at the very top, and I get the value in two seconds.
- Would I click? No. I'd skim it, do the BOGO math (two $100 pairs = $150, so 25% off overall), then mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, since none of my friends are shopping for walking shoes either.

## What's working

- The BOGO 50% off or 20% off a single pair banner is a clear, concrete offer. The navy block with white type is the strongest contrast in the email.
- "Discount auto-applied at cart" removes the promo-code hunt, which Ulta still makes me do with GIFTS.
- The Britto S-1992 hero is a colorful, fun shoe with a clear SHOP NOW button. It's the one place the email has personality.
- Rendering is clean. No broken images, no overlap, and the text is readable on a phone.
- The "Black Friday Countdown" label matches the season, so it doesn't feel stale.

## What's weak

- The subject says "limited availability" but nothing in the body shows stock levels, so the urgency isn't credible. It's the same mismatch I called out on 11/7.
- The offer is below the fold. The hero sells a shoe I never looked at instead of the 50%-off deal.
- The six "more great options" have tiny, hard-to-read captions. They show no prices, no sale prices, and no ratings.
- There's no loyalty or member angle at all, and no point multipliers, so I can't stack anything. Sephora Insider and Ulta always give me a tier hook, and Skechers treats me like a stranger.
- The fine print excludes sale styles and covers only "select regular-priced shoes," and the deadline (11/17) only shows up in that tiny text. "Countdown" with no visible date isn't a deadline.
- The footer is a long stack of nav, app badges, and BNPL logos. It feels like a template, not a campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the hero: "BOGO 50% off, ends 11/17" at the top, with the shoe below it. Right now the offer is the third thing I see.
- 2. Make the subject match the body. If it's a browse-abandonment email, name the shoe or show a price drop. If it's a Black Friday email, lead with the deal.
- 3. Add real scarcity, like "Only a few sizes left in your size" on the hero shoe. Otherwise drop the word "limited."
- 4. Add prices and a quick math line under the grid, such as "2 pairs = 25% off."
- 5. Cut the repeat cadence. After four near-identical nudges in a week, space them out or change the creative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off ends 11/17 — your Britto pair is in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still thinking about the S-1992? 2nd pair 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Auto-applied at cart. Buy one, get one 50% off, online only through 11/17.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Britto Landscapes sneaker plus 5 more picks, all eligible for BOGO.`
