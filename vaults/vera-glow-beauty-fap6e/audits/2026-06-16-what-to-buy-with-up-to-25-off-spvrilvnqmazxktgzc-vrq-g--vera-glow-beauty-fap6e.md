# What to buy with up to 25% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Day six. Email seven. And I will say this honestly: if Glossier's goal was to turn me into a Pavlovian shopper who stops responding to the word "sale," they've nearly succeeded. But — and this is a genuine but — this send is the first one since the June 11 launch that made me actually pause. The "what to buy" framing flips the script from screaming at me to actually helping me shop, and the three-BFF product curation is a real idea. Diverse models, real SKUs, specific shades — Cloud Paint in Soar, Skin Tint in G3, You Dusk. That's editorial. That's content. That's not just a discount countdown.
- The problem is it arrived after six straight days of Glossier in my inbox, so even good creative gets filtered through a "here we go again" lens before I've consciously registered it. My thumb was already halfway to swipe-archive before I noticed the concept shift. And the four identical "Shop now" buttons flatten the whole experience back into bargain-bin territory — the idea genuinely earned section-specific CTAs, and it didn't get them.
- Would I click? One of those BFF picks actually got me — the Skin Tint + Cloud Paint Soar combo under the second model is exactly my summer face. But the button goes to the general sale, so I'd probably just open a new tab and search manually. Would I forward? Yes — I'd DM this to one friend specifically for the shade callouts. That's a win the email doesn't quite know it earned.

## What's working

- **The editorial pivot is real.** "What to buy" framing turns a repeat discount blast into a shopping guide — the first genuine creative evolution in this whole sale arc since the June 11 launch tiles.
- **Model casting maps to product picks.** The darker-skin model has G3 Skin Tint listed; the fair-skin model has Bloom blush — that's intentional personalization through casting, and I caught it.
- **Named products with specific shades** (Skin Tint in G3, Cloud Paint in Soar, Boy Brow in Brown) create real purchase intent, versus the generic "save on Glossier" messaging of every prior send.
- **Clean mobile render.** No overlapping text, no broken images, readable without pinching — noticeably tighter than Charlotte Tilbury's cramped June 14 send.
- **Three looks = three separate entry points.** I identify with the second model's aesthetic, which means the email subtly targets my skin tone tier without any merge-tag personalization. That's smart casting.

## What's weak

- **"Shop now" × 4 is a creative surrender.** Each BFF section built specific intent and then threw it away with a generic button. "Shop Jasmine's picks" or even "Shop this look" would have doubled the editorial payoff.
- **Day seven cadence.** After June 15's two-in-one-afternoon double-send, landing another sale email one day later makes even the most evolved creative feel like background noise. I'm consciously compensating for my own filter just to evaluate this fairly.
- **No sale deadline anywhere in the render.** Drunk Elephant's June 13 send put "ends tonight" in the hero — I clicked on the spot. Glossier keeps running open-ended urgency and it consistently kills the impetus to act now vs. later.
- **Subscription upsell breaks the editorial voice.** After three gorgeous BFF sections, "Start a subscription and lock in 25% off**" with asterisks reads like the email handed the baton to a retention team mid-scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Named CTAs per BFF section.** "Shop this edit" or "Shop [name]'s picks" instead of four identical "Shop now" — links to a filtered collection rather than the generic sale page. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Put the sale deadline in the hero.** One visible date line above the fold. ILIA has done this on every HAIM collab send and it creates genuine now-or-never friction. Glossier keeps leaving it out.
- 3. **Cut the subscription upsell or send it separately.** The subscription 25% and the sale 25% are different value props with different decision journeys — mixing them in one scroll muddies both.
- 4. **Throttle or segment by engagement tier.** This email would have scored a 9.5 on Day 2 of the sale. Sending it on Day 6 means it's doing extra work just to survive the pre-existing fatigue filter.
- 5. **Shade finder link under each BFF.** The casting is already doing personalization — a "find your Skin Tint shade" micro-CTA under the G3 model would close that loop and send me somewhere directly useful.

## Technical Audit — Glossier "Up to 25% off" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is ESP-sent via Klaviyo (`ctrk.klclick.com`) with standard MSO conditional-comment scaffolding and a responsive CSS media query stack. One non-standard tracking element is present that warrants investigation; the truncated source limits full compliance and link auditing.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard tracking element — high priority**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KV6Z44HR4FTGNAR6P8EZJWBF_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on a `<span>`. A `<span>` does not trigger network requests, so this element does not function as a stylesheet loader or open-tracking pixel in any compliant email client. The conventional open-tracking mechanism is `<img src="..." width="1" height="1" />`. If this is intended as a tracking trigger, it is silently failing. If it is a rendering artifact from the ESP template system, it is dead weight in the DOM.

**ESP click-tracking domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo). All click links should route through this domain — verify no bare/untracked destination URLs are present in the untruncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Positive findings:**
- Correct XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE for email
- `lang="en"` on `<html>` ✓
- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` present ✓
- MSO conditional comments for Outlook 2007–2019 ✓
- `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` with `AllowPNG` and 96dpi ✓
- `format-detection telephone=no` ✓
- Gmail fix class (`.gmail-fix`) present ✓
- Responsive breakpoints at 600px and 384px ✓

**Hover/rollover effects (minor):**

```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { display:block !important }
```

`:hover` CSS works only in webmail (Gmail web, Outlook.com). It is non-functional in Outlook desktop, Apple Mail, and most mobile apps. The `rollover-first` state should be the safe fallback visible in all clients — confirm the default (non-hover) state is the intended experience for ~60–70% of opens.

**Alt text:** Not auditable from truncated source — verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, particularly product shots used as CTA drivers.

**Single-letter class names** (`.q`, `.o`, `.n`, etc.) are minified/obfuscated. This is common from ESP builders but makes QA tooling harder to run against specific elements.
