# Chase the sun(screen)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ILIA slides into July 11 with a sunscreen launch email and honestly? The wordplay in the subject got me — I clocked it immediately. But then I opened it and realized this is a product launch I maybe missed, which tells me ILIA is doing some segmented or catch-up send here. The top banner is dangling a free-gift-with-membership deadline of Monday, which snapped my loyalty-math brain to attention right away — that's exactly how you get a Sephora Rouge to stop scrolling.
- What I really didn't expect was the triple-testimonial format. Stephanie, Tamerri, Andrea — real names, real photos, star ratings, actual review text. That's not standard ILIA. That's a "we just launched a new SPF and we need social proof fast" move, and honestly, it works on me more than a model shot would. Mineral SPF 35 in July? I need real people's texture feedback before I pull the trigger.
- The "Stay Sharp" secondary section at the bottom broke the momentum a little — I was in sunscreen mode and then suddenly we're pivoting to pencils? — but not enough to lose the click. I'd click the SPF section and skip the bottom. Would I forward this? If a friend texted me "looking for a clean mineral SPF," I'd screenshot the product section. That counts.

## What's working

- **"Chase the sun(screen)" continues ILIA's hot streak of wordplay** — coming off "shade. set. match." yesterday for Wimbledon, they're proving this is a creative team with an actual point of view, not just a fill-in-the-blank subject line machine.
- **Three testimonials with real photos and star ratings is the right call for a new SPF launch** — mineral sunscreen is a texture-paranoid category and social proof from actual faces (not campaign models) does the heavy lifting Drunk Elephant does with its "come clean" phrasing: it earns trust fast.
- **"Mineral Sunscreen SPF 35" in the product name answers my first question before I ask it** — I don't have to click to find out if it's chemical or mineral. That's real information in visible real estate, and Ulta almost never gives me that upfront.
- **The free-gift-with-membership banner at the top with a Monday deadline** — short, credible, loyalty-aware. That's the kind of math I do automatically.
- **July timing is impeccable** — this is peak SPF replenishment season and ILIA knows it. Sephora's post-Stacking Weekend sends have been drifting editorial; this one has a clear seasonal hook.

## What's weak

- **"Stay Sharp" at the bottom is a vibe whiplash** — I just read three reviews about sun protection and now you want me to pivot to what looks like an eyeliner section? The sectioning feels like two emails stitched together rather than a coherent story.
- **The copy hook "Still looking for the launch... here's what you missed" is slightly passive** — it implies I wasn't paying attention, which feels a little finger-waggy for a brand that's been in my inbox every single day this week.
- **No explicit SPF-focused offer or bundle** — the free gift is loyalty-gated but the SPF itself has no standalone incentive. Given that Sally Beauty literally ran "last day for 20% off" on a Tuesday and Glossier is teasing newness, a clean brand like ILIA could have paired a sample or a launch-week perk to push conversion harder.
- **The product flat lay is small** — given that this is the hero product being launched, I wanted a closer look at the texture and finish. The image didn't give me enough to judge "will this leave a cast on my skin."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the SPF product shot as true hero, pull testimonials directly beneath it** — right now the layout reads: intro copy → testimonials → product. Flip it: product → proof → CTA. The eye should land on the product first, then the validation.
- 2. **Reframe "here's what you missed" to "here's why everyone's switching"** — it's less accusatory and keeps the momentum forward-facing.
- 3. **Cut "Stay Sharp" to a footer module, not a full section** — or save it for tomorrow's email. The SPF launch deserves the full email real estate.
- 4. **Add SPF texture/finish callouts visually** — even a three-icon "lightweight / no cast / reef-safe" row would address the #1 objection for a mineral SPF before I have to go read the PDP.
- 5. **Tie the free gift explicitly to the SPF** — "Get a free gift when you buy the new Sun Serum SPF 35 by Monday" is a more compelling hook than a banner that mentions membership generally.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New SPF just dropped — and there's a free gift`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mineral SPF 35 for summer. Catch it before Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three real reviews + the launch you didn't want to miss`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sun Serum SPF 35 is here — free gift ends Monday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in major clients.**
Two `@import` rules are used inside a `<style>` block:

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail on iOS strip or block `@import`. The fallback at the bottom of the stylesheet (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is correct and will engage, so this is a font-rendering degradation, not a layout-breaking failure.

**Responsive breakpoint is 480px, not 600px.** Mobile media queries fire at `max-width: 480px`. This is narrower than the de facto 600px standard and will leave columns un-stacked on tablets and large phones (e.g., iPhone Pro Max at 430px logical width is just inside; iPad mini at 744px is not).

**Image alt text unverifiable** — no `<img>` tags visible in the truncated source.

**MSO/Outlook compatibility: no issues found.** Conditional comments, `o:AllowPNG`, `PixelsPerInch`, `mso-table-lspace/rspace` resets, and `.mj-outlook-group-fix` are all correctly implemented.

**Preheader: correctly implemented.** Hidden div with `max-height:0; overflow:hidden` and Unicode filler characters (`͏`) to prevent body text bleed-through.
