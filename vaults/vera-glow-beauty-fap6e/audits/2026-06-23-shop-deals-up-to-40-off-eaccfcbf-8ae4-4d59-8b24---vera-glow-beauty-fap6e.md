# Shop deals up to 40% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. That's where we are. Last week Sally sent "Summer's Brightest Finds" (#21) and the week before that was "Don't Miss 50% Off It's a 10" (#20) — the one send in this entire stretch that actually earned a glance because it named a brand in the subject. This one? "Shop deals up to 40% off." That's it. That's the whole pitch. And then I open it and the hero says **20% off sitewide** — so which is it? The subject baited me with 40%, the hero landed on 20%. The 40% is real, it's just buried two scrolls down for select styling goods and ion hair care. That gap makes me trust the email less, not more.
- The imagery is a bunch of hair tools — a round brush, a curling wand, a styling brush — which is fine for a Sally customer but doesn't speak to me the way a skincare drop or a prestige launch would. I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond. I come to Sally for professional-grade hair color and specific brands I can't get elsewhere. "Shop deals" on generic tool imagery is not that.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I'd glance at the Ulta co-branding strip at the bottom (because that's mildly interesting given I'm already Diamond) and then hit archive.

## What's working

- **20% off sitewide is a genuinely stackable offer** — if I'm already planning a Sally run, this is the nudge I needed, and "Ends June 26" gives it a real deadline.
- **"The Big Deals Keep Coming" grid** at least breaks the offers into readable tiles with percentages; the BOGO 60% Tondeur® call-out names a brand, which is the one move that actually earns attention.
- **Free 2-hour delivery** is a legitimate value-add that Sephora doesn't offer in most markets — the tote bag visual is a little random but the offer is real.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, navigation works, hierarchy is readable on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Subject line/hero mismatch is a bait-and-switch feel** — "up to 40% off" in subject, 20% off sitewide in the hero. I know they're both technically true, but my brain clocks the gap immediately and my trust drops.
- **No brand name in the subject** — the #20 email ("Don't Miss 50% Off It's a 10") proved this works. "Shop deals" is the laziest possible phrasing; Sephora's "ENDS TODAY: up to 50% off select beauty" at least signals the scale of the event.
- **Generic CTA copy** — "Shop Deals" is two words that say nothing. Charlotte Tilbury's recent early-access email used "Shop the App Exclusive" and it landed because it gave me a reason to click *that specific button*.
- **Rewards are mentioned but not quantified** — "earn more rewards" means nothing to me without a points multiplier or bonus event tied to it. As someone who does the math obsessively, "more rewards" is noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/hero alignment** — if the hero is 20% off sitewide, say that. If the lead is 40%, lead with that in the hero. Don't put them on different floors.
- 2. **Name a brand in the subject** — the data is right there in the email (#20 proved it works). ion®, Tondeur®, It's a 10 — any of these beats "deals."
- 3. **Specify the rewards incentive** — e.g. "Earn 2X rewards through June 26" or "Bonus points on every order this week" — something I can actually calculate.
- 4. **Swap the hero product shot** — styling tools as the hero skews professional/salon. If they're going to appeal to someone like me, lead with a prestige-adjacent product (fragrance, treatment, color) and let tools be a secondary tile.
- 5. **Tighten the CTA copy** — "Shop 20% Off Sitewide" > "Shop Deals."
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off sitewide + 40% off ion® — ends June 26`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends Thursday: 20% sitewide + BOGO Tondeur`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sitewide savings, free 2-hour delivery, and bonus rewards through June 26.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Sally haul just got cheaper. 20% off everything + 40% off select styling.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Undeclared font family on all anchor elements (confirmed)**

```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```

`Founders Grotesk` has no corresponding `@font-face` declaration and no external stylesheet link anywhere in the `<head>`. Every `<a>` in the email will render in Helvetica/system sans-serif fallback on all clients — including desktop Outlook, Gmail, and Apple Mail. This is likely unintended and affects all CTA buttons that use `<a>` tags.

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on `img` elements**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```

`white-space: pre` is a non-standard property for `<img>` elements (it applies to text nodes). It has no practical rendering effect on images but is dead code that may indicate a template copy/paste error. Low severity.

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts API path**

All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/` — the v17 path from the 2020-era Google Fonts API. These URLs are still served but point to older font file versions. Not a deliverability or rendering blocker, but worth updating if this template is reused.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint at 499px (non-standard)**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```

The industry standard breakpoint is 600px. Devices with screen widths between 500–599px (common on Android) will receive the desktop layout, which may not be designed to reflow at that width. Assess whether this is intentional.

**Positive — Defensive meta tags present**

- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` — prevents iOS from auto-resizing text
- `X-UA-Compatible: IE=edge` inside MSO conditional comment — correct pattern
- Gmail dark-mode workaround present: `u ~ div .pattern { min-width: 100vw !important; }`
