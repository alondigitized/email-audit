# The ultimate hack?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- "The ultimate hack?" lands in my inbox and I know before I even open it that it's DE — I could spot that pink-and-white palette in my sleep at this point. And look, I *have* opened every single one of these because it's Drunk Elephant and that's just where I am with this brand. But I'm also at, what, six? Seven emails in eleven days? I stopped counting somewhere between "Oh, my stars" and "Summer to go." This one at least has a coherent concept — Reboot Camp, overnight routine, three hero products called out by name with actual ingredient copy. I actually appreciate that. The T.L.C. Framboos / B-Hydra / Lala Retro trio makes sense as a system.
- What I don't appreciate is burying the 35% discount below three product descriptions. The entire upper half of this email is editorial — which I like in theory — but if there's a real offer here, lead with it. Glossier made the same mistake with "For your eyes only" on June 24, hiding the 20% behind atmosphere. Meanwhile Ulta just put "Take 20% OFF everything online" in the subject and got to the point. DE has the better product story; they don't need to obscure the deal.
- Would I click? Maybe — the "Making It Rain" GWP section actually got my attention because I'll absolutely do the $125 math on samples plus a bag. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read and come back to it at 11pm when I'm doing my actual skincare routine? Probably yes.

## What's working

- **"Reboot Camp" concept is tight.** Overnight reset routine framed as a camp works — it gives the trio a reason to exist together rather than just three random SKUs.
- **Ingredient copy earns its space.** Calling out "1% salicylic acid," "hyaluronic acid," and "refillable" in Lala Retro is the kind of product detail that makes me trust the send — I want to know *why* these three specifically.
- **"Making It Rain" GWP is legitimately compelling.** $125+ spend threshold for two deluxe samples plus a bag is math I will actually do. This is the kind of thing Sephora Rouge rewards excel at, and DE is playing that game.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loads clean — product images, layout, hierarchy. After the occasional broken-image nightmare in Charlotte Tilbury's June blast schedule, clean render is worth noting.
- **Brand voice holds.** "Is always a good thing." reads like DE copy, not a generic promo blast.

## What's weak

- **35% is buried.** You open with "Welcome to Reboot Camp" — fine — but the actual discount lives below three product descriptions and a SHOP NOW button. If 35% off is real news, it belongs in the hero or the subject line, not halfway down the scroll.
- **"The ultimate hack?" is doing zero work as a subject.** It doesn't say skincare, it doesn't say savings, it doesn't say Drunk Elephant. Glossier sent "For your eyes only" on the same day and that was also cryptic — at some point mystery just reads as lazy. My thumb almost skipped it.
- **No loyalty/points angle.** I'm Rouge at Sephora and DE is carried there. If this bundle qualifies for bonus points or event pricing, I needed to know. The "Still time to save 20%" email from the 17th also missed this beat.
- **"SHOP SHOP SHOP" repeated three times under the 35% section looks frantic.** One CTA. Pick one. It reads like Lush at email 19 — just throwing text until something works.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 35% in the hero.** Move the discount above the fold and restructure: offer first, product education second. The Reboot Camp concept can still anchor the email — just let the price signal earn the open.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to reflect the actual offer or the routine concept.** "The ultimate hack?" could describe literally anything.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your overnight routine: 35% off the trio`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Reboot Camp starts tonight — 35% less`
- 3. **Preheader should do the GWP sell.** Spend $125, get samples + the bag is an actual hook — use it.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $125+ and snag deluxe samples + this bag. Limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `T.L.C. + B-Hydra + Lala Retro — your reset routine at 35% off.`
- 4. **Collapse the "SHOP SHOP SHOP" to one button.** Pick "Shop the Trio" or "Shop 35% Off" — something with a noun that tells me where I'm landing.
- 5. **Add a loyalty callout.** Even a line like "Eligible for Beauty Insider points" does real work for me and the other Rouge-tier readers who are always doing the math.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment delimiters (`<!-- -->`) inside a `<style>` block are invalid. Some email clients (particularly older Outlook and Yahoo) may expose or misparse these as literal text or drop the surrounding rules entirely.

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom on iOS. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation and also penalized by Gmail's accessibility scanner.

**[MEDIUM] `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on wildcard selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Value should be `100%`, not `none`. Setting `none` disables iOS automatic font scaling entirely, causing text to render at unreadable sizes on small screens. Correct value: `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%`.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font loading — redundant requests**
The same three Brown font variants (Bold, Light, Thin) are loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link>` tag from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. `@import` inside `<style>` from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
3. `@font-face` block from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`

This fires 6 redundant external HTTP requests before any body content renders. Remove the `<link>` tags and `@import` rules; keep only the `@font-face` declarations.

**[MEDIUM] Brand fonts served from third-party CDN for 3 of 4 variants**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — a font-redistribution CDN with no SLA. `BrownRegular` correctly self-hosts from `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`. The three CDN-hosted variants should be migrated to the same self-hosted path.

**[LOW] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` present. Required for screen reader language detection and flagged by accessibility validators (Litmus, Email on Acid).

**[LOW] `<title>` is non-descriptive**
```html
<title>Drunk Elephant</title>
```
Screen readers and some clients (Apple Mail) expose the `<title>` as the document label. Should reflect the email subject or campaign purpose.
