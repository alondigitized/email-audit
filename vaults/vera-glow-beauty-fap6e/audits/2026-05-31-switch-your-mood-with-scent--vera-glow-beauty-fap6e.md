# Switch your mood with scent ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Nine. I'm at nine Lush emails in nineteen days, and I want to be fair: this one is the most genuinely interesting content pivot since the Karma email on May 22, which was the last time I actually read past the hero. The fragrance mood guide — Energy Boost, Reclaim Your Calm, Pure Indulgence — is a smart enough framework that I stopped scrolling instead of immediately archiving. That's more than I could say for the flower email on May 27 (no deal, no urgency, still don't know why it existed) or the shower send on May 24.
- Here's the problem though: this is Pinterest content cosplaying as email marketing. There is no offer, no deadline, no loyalty hook, and no reason to actually buy anything today. I am Sephora Rouge. I am always doing the math. ILIA dropped a teaser for their "softest launch yet" landing in my inbox the same week and it had me checking a countdown timer. This had me thinking "oh that's a pretty gradient" and then closing the tab.
- I'd watch the Scent Story video out of curiosity, maybe mentally note Silky Underwear for a future haul, but I would not forward this to a friend and I would absolutely not convert today. Mark as read. Next.

## What's working

- The three-mood architecture (Energy Boost / Reclaim Your Calm / Pure Indulgence) is legitimately a better way to shop fragrance than a generic product grid — it gives me a reason to browse the catalog with intent
- The gradient hero — purple bleeding into warm yellow — is visually distinctive in a way that Glossier's endlessly repeated pink summer palette is not; this actually feels like a Lush email
- "YOUR FRAGRANCES" in the nav bar is the kind of personalization signal that makes me feel like a known subscriber rather than a blast-list stranger
- The "Build Your Scent Story" video anchor is a nice educational hook that makes me linger; Charlotte Tilbury does this editorial-plus-product mix well and Lush is borrowing that energy here
- Free shipping threshold in the top banner is visible, clear, and honest — no asterisk spelunking required

## What's weak

- No offer. Not even a soft one. Charlotte Tilbury's "Did You Miss This Magic?" scored a 9/10 in my recent queue partly because it always gives me something to act on — nine emails from Lush this month and still nothing to click for
- Product tiles show names and mood categories but zero prices, zero reviews, zero stock signals — I'm being asked to add to cart blind, which I don't do
- The Karma line is buried in the "Reclaim Your Calm" section when the May 22 email proved it's Lush's strongest brand anchor — it should be the hero, not mid-page filler
- Cadence is genuinely fatiguing; three emails in five days (May 27, May 29, today) with no deal between them trains me to archive without reading

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie an offer to the mood segmentation** — even a weekend discount attached to one mood category turns browse into buy; "Energy Boost scents: 15% off this weekend" makes the concept work commercially
- 2. **Add price + star ratings to product tiles** — e.l.f. always shows pricing inline and their click rates prove it; asking me to discover price on the product page is one extra step too many
- 3. **Make Karma the hero** — Lush's most recognizable product is sitting in the second section; put it front and center where the May 22 email already proved it resonates with me
- 4. **Cut the cadence by half** — a biweekly send with a hook converts better than daily content noise from the same sender
