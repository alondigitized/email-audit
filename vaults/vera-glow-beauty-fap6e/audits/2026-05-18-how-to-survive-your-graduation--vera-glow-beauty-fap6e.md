# How to survive your graduation
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- After three straight fragrance emails — "Say it with a scent," "A new chapter begins," "Eau de main character" — I genuinely did not expect Glossier to pivot to a graduation Q&A, and I respect the creative gear shift. But I'm 29. I graduated years ago. The moment I parsed the framing I had the same low-grade estrangement I feel when Sephora pushes me the college-dorm skincare bundle — someone in the targeting assumed I was younger, and I felt it.
- That said, the cry-proof makeup brief is legitimately useful beyond graduation: long weddings, all-day family events, any occasion where you can't touch your face. The "Dear Glossier" letter format is the brand doing what it does better than almost anyone — editorial voice-as-product. ILIA and Drunk Elephant don't have this kind of column-style charm. The Lash Slick and Boy Brow recommendations land correctly for the scenario.
- Would I click? Probably once, on Lash Slick specifically, because it's already in my orbit. Would I forward this? No. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The Q&A "Dear Glossier" format earns its word count in a way a standard shop-the-look email never would — this is brand voice as a differentiator, full stop
- Lash Slick and Boy Brow are exactly right for the brief: pocket-sized, long-wear, low-fuss — the product logic is sound
- Coming off the fragrance trilogy, this graduation pivot reads seasonal and fresh; it's May, graduation timing tracks
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no jank; the clean two-column product section works
- The "Get in touch!" section with the smiley-wave emoji is very on-brand — warm, human, not corporate

## What's weak

- The graduation framing skews hard 22-year-old — I'm 29, this demographic signal pushed me away immediately, and unlike Charlotte Tilbury's "darling" language (which ages up intentionally), Glossier's "next chapter in life" energy reads like it's not addressed to me
- Zero offer — no discount, no GWP, no free shipping threshold; Drunk Elephant ran "Let's get something clear" as pure editorial too, but at least that wordplay had brand-building energy; this one has neither offer nor a particularly quotable hook
- "Shop makeup" as primary CTA is as generic as it gets — "Shop Lash Slick" or "Build your kit" would have been more compelling and specific
- The "WE RECOMMEND" product cards feel undersized — two items with minimal copy; I want to know *why* Lash Slick survives a ceremony, not just that it exists

## Recommendations

- 1. **Broaden the hook beyond graduation** — reframe as "cry-proof beauty for the moments that matter" so it reads relevant at 29 (a wedding, a promotion dinner, a funeral you didn't expect) not just a milestone I cleared seven years ago
- 2. **Add a closing offer** — even free shipping over $40 or a two-product bundle nudge would convert this from "interesting read" to "cart open"; Drunk Elephant consistently beats Glossier here: same editorial polish, but they close the transaction
- 3. **Make the CTA product-specific** — "Shop Lash Slick" or "Shop the graduation kit" instead of "Shop makeup"
- 4. **Expand the product rationale** — one sentence of wear-claim per item; ILIA uses press quotes (ELLE, Vogue, Allure) to validate their hero SKUs; Glossier should use use-case proof ("stays on through a four-hour ceremony, fits in any jacket pocket")
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Cry-proof beauty for your biggest days`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Long-wear picks for the moments that count`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Lash Slick. Boy Brow. Your pocket. Done.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `The Glossier picks that actually survive a long day`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard open/tracking beacon implementation**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KRW6629PSS04VCBAG9Z7Y80T_0"></span>
```
`<span>` does not accept `rel` or `href` attributes in any HTML spec. The browser/email renderer will silently ignore both attributes, so this element does nothing. If this was intended as a Klaviyo open-tracking pixel, it must be an `<img>` with `width="1" height="1"`, not a `<span>`. If it was intended as a tracked redirect link, it must be an `<a>`. **This beacon is dead and produces no tracking signal.**

**Click-tracking domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` — Klaviyo's standard redirect domain. No anomaly there.

**Source comments expose design studio:**
```html
<!-- EMAIL CREATED BY GLOSSIER x SORRY STUDIO SORRY.LAND -->
```
Not a security vulnerability, but third-party studio attribution is visible in source to anyone who inspects the email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `:hover` rollover effects are non-functional in primary clients**
```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { max-height:0 !important; display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { max-height:none !important; display:block !important }
```
Hover states are suppressed in Gmail (all surfaces), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. The `.rollover-second` content will never surface for the majority of recipients. If this is swap imagery or a CTA variant, the fallback (`.rollover-first`) must stand alone.

**`lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"` present** — correct, no issue.

**Preheader padding technique** uses U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner `͏`) and soft hyphens (`­`). This is a known valid technique, though some aggressive spam filters penalize high concentrations of combining characters.
