# Meet the Soft Focus family
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's my count for ILIA this month, and "Meet the Soft Focus family" lands as the most consolidation-heavy send of the whole run — a tidy "here's everything" roundup after a launch sequence I've watched evolve from the June 2 drop, through the ingredient story, through the duo pitch, and through yesterday's consumer-study close. The design is clean and quintessentially ILIA: soft peachy neutrals, restrained typography, one genuinely beautiful editorial shot of brush meeting skin that reminded me why I subscribed in the first place. That "plays well with powder" section actually made me stop scrolling.
- Here's my honest read though: this email is doing maintenance, not new lifting. It's confirming something I already know. Yesterday's "the results are in" proof send gave me a real reason to reconsider. This one gives me a poster. After twenty-four sends, a "meet the family" recap functions less like an invitation and more like a Sunday summary newsletter I didn't ask for. No urgency, no event hook, no "this weekend only" to unstick me from fence-sitting.
- I'd click if I were already mid-checkout mindset — the 5% duo bundle is a genuine new signal — but this email isn't building that mindset from scratch. Mark as read, revisit if Sephora runs a bonus points event soon. Not forwarding. My Soft Focus friends already know.

## What's working

- The three-product layout — setting powder, finishing powder, blurring blush — is genuinely useful "can I build a full routine here?" architecture. Clean, scannable, not cluttered.
- The "plays well with powder" editorial section with the brush on model skin is beautiful. ILIA consistently does this better than Drunk Elephant, whose recent sends read like a product catalog shot in a hotel bathroom.
- **Talc-free callout is front-of-body where it belongs** — "formulated without talc + safe for sensitive skin" is the ingredient sentence I actually care about, not buried in a footer disclaimer.
- The 5% bundle save on the skin tint + finishing powder duo is a smart basket-builder. I wasn't thinking about that pairing today, but now I am running the math.
- VIP points banner at the top at least signals I'm being treated like a member, even if the redemption channel is misaligned with how I actually shop the brand.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP IT UP"** on the brush editorial is embarrassingly weak for a prestige clean beauty brand. Charlotte Tilbury would never. Give me a verb and a noun — "Shop the Precision Brush" costs exactly zero effort to write.
- Zero urgency anywhere in the body. "Meet the family" implies evergreen permanence. After a June absolutely saturated with Soft Focus content, I need a concrete reason this weekend is the moment to convert.
- The CUSTOMIZE + SAVE asterisk leads to a vague "See terms for details" at the footer without telling me whether the 5% stacks with VIP points, has a minimum, or excludes my Sephora purchase. That ambiguity is annoying when I'm already doing the loyalty math.
- This is send twenty-four. Recap emails work when there's been silence. After a relentless launch runway, a "family" summary lands as repetitive filler, not editorial depth.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Time-gate the VIP points offer** — "1,000 VIP points through Sunday" transforms this from "nice to know" to an actual weekend reason to visit the site. This is the single change that would lift click rate most.
- 2. **Kill "SHOP IT UP," replace with product-specific copy** — "Shop the Precision Powder Brush" or even just "Add the Brush" gives me something to act on.
- 3. **Surface Sephora availability** — I am Sephora Rouge. I earn 1.25x points there. The ILIA VIP points offer evaporates for my shopping math. Even "Also available at Sephora" in sub-copy would land better.
- 4. **Flip the email order** — put the duo section (5% savings, two products) above the editorial beauty shot. The highest-intent offer shouldn't live in the third panel.
- 5. **Close the loop from yesterday explicitly** — "Yesterday we showed you the science. Today, build your routine." would make this feel earned rather than repetitive.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: save 5% when you bundle the Skin Tint + Finishing Powder`
