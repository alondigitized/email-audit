# 25% off is going....
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Email number nine. NINE. I have a running spreadsheet on this campaign and at this point my pivot table has its own pivot table. But here's the thing — this specific send actually earns its existence, and I say that as someone who's been deeply skeptical since email four. The countdown timer showing **one hour and seven minutes** is not a vibe — it is a deadline. The moment I see that ticking clock, the math in my brain activates: I haven't pulled the trigger yet, my cart is half-built, and the F-Balm GWP at $100+ is genuinely a free full-size I've been wanting to try. That combination — real urgency plus fresh value-add I haven't seen in the previous eight sends — is what separates this from spam.
- The copy on the secondary banner — *"WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS? GO SHOP"* — is the single best piece of copy in the entire nine-email run. It's self-aware, it's Drunk Elephant's voice to a tee, and it essentially calls me out in the funniest possible way. Compare that to Charlotte Tilbury's double-exclamation-point "20% OFF!!" from email eight, which was just loud. DE earned the same urgency with wit.
- Would I click? Yes, but I already clicked an hour ago — this email was the nudge that finished me off. Would I forward? Actually yes, one specific forward: my best friend who missed the earlier sends and still has the Protini on her wishlist. The GWP tier makes it worth texting, not just forwarding.

## What's working

- **The countdown clock is doing real work.** 01:07:55 remaining is not manufactured scarcity — it's a concrete deadline, and it lands. The previous eight emails were building to this moment and the timer delivers the payoff.
- **"WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS? GO SHOP"** — that secondary banner copy is genuinely funny and 100% on-brand. It earns the click more than the CTA button does.
- **The GWP tiers are new information.** Free Hydro + Bright Kit at $100+ and a full-size F-Balm — this is the first time the campaign introduced spend-tier incentives, and it smartly reactivates anyone sitting on a cart. ILIA did something similar with "Layers of free gifts" (their best send in the whole campaign) and it worked; DE is applying that same logic here.
- **The 30% off $75+ detail in the banner** is a tier I've been waiting on. Nice that it's above the fold and not buried in fine print.
- **Layout is clean, no render issues.** Hero → GWP 1 → GWP 2 is a logical scroll path and every section has a CTA.

## What's weak

- **"GET YOU GO" is a vibe, not a CTA.** I love DE's weird brand voice but this button copy tells me nothing. e.l.f. gives me "Claim your free Glow Stick" and I know exactly what I'm getting; "GET YOU GO" requires me to already know what I'm shopping for.
- **No personalization whatsoever.** After nine emails and presumably knowing I'm a returning customer, not a single product recommendation, not my name, nothing. Sephora during their 50%-off sale managed "recommended for you" product blocks — DE is leaving that entirely on the table.
- **The hero product shot is pretty but generic.** A lineup of DE packaging reads as brand-aesthetic, not as "here is the thing you should add to your cart right now." One hero SKU with a "still in stock" signal would convert harder at this point in the campaign.
- **Nine emails.** I'm noting it because the cadence has become wallpaper. The urgency in this send is genuine but the brand trained me to ignore the first six, which diluted the impact.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the CTA to be transactional, not branded.** Swap "GET YOU GO" for "Grab 25% Off Now" or "Shop Before It's Gone" — something that maps to the actual action and the countdown urgency.
- 2. **Add one pinned product recommendation.** Even a single "You viewed this / People like you loved this" SKU would lift click-to-cart dramatically at the close-out stage.
- 3. **Lead with the GWP tiers earlier.** The free F-Balm and Bright Kit are buried below the hero fold. For a $100 spend threshold those should be competing for the hero position, especially since they're the freshest info in the email.
- 4. **Tighten the subject line for the final-hour send.** Ellipsis creates mild curiosity but a clock-specific hook would be harder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `1 hour left: 25% off + free F-Balm`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart expires in 60 minutes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30% off $75+, free full-size gift at $100. The clock is real.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last call sitewide — the Bright Kit is still here but not for long`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comments inside CSS media query block**
Lines inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS. These two rules will be silently ignored by all conformant CSS parsers, meaning `contenttable` and `mobile` columns will not resize on mobile. This is a broken responsive layout bug.

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate font loading — double HTTP requests**
The same three `db.onlinewebfonts.com` font families are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` AND `@import` inside the `<style>` block. This fires six redundant external requests per open.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Apple's iOS 10+ ignores this for accessibility, but it remains a compliance flag.

**[LOW] Global `line-height: 100%` reset**
```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```
This overrides all inherited line heights across every element. For multi-line text blocks it will produce cramped, potentially unreadable copy in clients that respect it.

**[LOW] Missing whitespace before `format()` in `@font-face`**
```css
url("...BrownRegular.woff2")format("woff2")
```
The CSS spec requires a space before `format()`. Most parsers are tolerant, but older WebKit-based clients may skip the format hint and fall back to an incorrect file type.

**[LOW] Third-party font CDN dependency**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. That service hosts redistributed fonts and has no guaranteed SLA. If it goes down or blocks the request, all three weights fall back to the client's default sans-serif. The same files are available from the brand's own Demandware CDN (`drunkelephant.com`) — self-hosting all weights from that origin would remove the dependency.
