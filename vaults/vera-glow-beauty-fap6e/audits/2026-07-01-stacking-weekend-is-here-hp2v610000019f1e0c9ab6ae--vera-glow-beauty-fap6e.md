# ⭐️ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Stacking Weekend is genuinely on my beauty calendar the way other people have dentist appointments — I know it's coming, I plan around it, and I have a cart pre-loaded. So when I opened this, I was already in buying mode before I read a single word. The 30% hero lands exactly where it should — above the fold, big, impossible to miss — and the 2X points badge confirms I'm in the right place. The "Final Sunday 7/5" deadline is doing real work here because I know Stacking Weekend is brief and I have a specific haul in mind.
- That said, this email is trying to do a lot. I'm counting at least four distinct value propositions — the 30% event discount, the Sephora Credit Card 30%-back offer, the Shop Now Pay Later pitch, and the "Earn 25 Points on Must Haves" product module — and they're all stacked vertically like a Russian nesting doll of offers. The DolceGabbana launch drop at the bottom felt bolted on, like the CRM team had a leftover slot to fill.
- Would I click? Absolutely, I already had. Would I forward it to a friend? Only to a fellow Rouge member who I know is doing the math on the card stacking. Anyone else, this email is going to feel like homework.

## What's working

- **30% hero + 2X badge combo is immediate** — my eyes went straight to the offer and confirmed "yes, this is the Stacking Weekend email I've been waiting for," no hunting required.
- **"Final Sunday 7/5" deadline is specific and credible** — Sephora Stacking Weekend actually ends, so this urgency isn't manufactured, and I trust it.
- **Points-tier education module** — the "See What Your Points Can Do" breakdown is genuinely useful. As a Rouge member doing the math before I spend, I appreciate seeing the tier context in the email itself rather than having to open the app.
- **In-store barcode at the bottom** — this is the small detail that separates Sephora from the competition. I can screenshot this and flash it at checkout. Ulta does this too, but it's buried; here it's clean and scannable.
- **"Earn 25 Points on Must Haves" product module** — concrete products with clear incentive. Gives me a starting point for my cart, even if the thumbnails are small.

## What's weak

- **Credit card offer creates two parallel math problems** — I don't have the Sephora credit card, so the "30% back for cardholders" module immediately reads as irrelevant. Worse, it makes the non-cardholder offer feel diminished by comparison. Glossier's recent segmentation was sloppy too, but at least they didn't put the "sorry, not for you" offer above the fold.
- **Shop Now Pay Later placement is a vibe killer** — right in the middle of a loyalty event email, BNPL messaging signals Sephora thinks I can't afford the haul. I'm a Rouge member. I've definitely spent enough to have earned that tier. This belongs in onboarding emails, not Stacking Weekend.
- **DolceGabbana drop feels tacked on** — launching a fragrance or product in the same email as a 30% stacking event creates cannibalism. I'm either here to stack my existing wishlist or I'm here for a new launch. Doing both dilutes the urgency of both.
- **Product thumbnails in the "Must Haves" module are very small** — on mobile I had to squint. e.l.f. consistently shows bigger product images with better contrast. This is a missed chance to drive specific clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the credit card offer out.** Show cardholder math only to cardholders; show me a non-cardholder offer or remove that block entirely. That one change tightens the email for 80% of the list.
- 2. **Cut the BNPL module entirely for this send.** Save it for a cart-abandonment or new-customer flow. It's off-brand for a loyalty event.
- 3. **Consolidate to one hero CTA + one product module.** The email would land harder as: 30% off → here's what to add to your cart → deadline. Pull the DolceGabbana drop into a separate send.
- 4. **Upsize the "Must Haves" product images.** If you're asking me to click into specific products, I need to actually see them. Sally Beauty's BOGO emails set a higher bar here — product images are legible without zooming.
- 5. **Add my Rouge tier status to the hero copy.** "Rouge members: your 30% is live" would make me feel seen instead of just mass-marketed at.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your biggest Beauty Insider discount of the season. Final Sunday 7/5.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Global suppression of `text-size-adjust` — confirmed issue**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to essentially all elements. Overrides user-configured accessibility font scaling in mobile browsers. Acceptable on `body` only; blanket application on every element is excessive.

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow — confirmed issue**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { ... }
```
Current flagship widths (iPhone 15 Pro: 393px, Pixel 9: 412px) fall under this threshold in portrait, but the breakpoint will not fire for any landscape-oriented mobile view or small tablet. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px` as the primary responsive breakpoint.

**`robots` meta value has invalid space — confirmed typo**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a valid directive; correct value is `noindex`. Email clients don't crawl, but this matters if the email is web-mirrored or archived at a public URL.
