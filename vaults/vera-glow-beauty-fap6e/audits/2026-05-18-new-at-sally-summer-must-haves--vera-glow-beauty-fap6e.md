# New at Sally: Summer Must-Haves ☀️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Sally Beauty is back in my inbox — and this is the fourth time in eight days, after the May 15 flash sale, the May 14 Hot Tools blast, and the May 11 beach waves pitch. At this point I have a Pavlovian skim-and-dismiss reflex forming. "New at Sally: Summer Must-Haves" lands soft — no urgency, no dollar figure, no event hook. My thumb was already moving toward archive before the email even loaded.
- What I actually see when it opens: a decent-looking hero ("Your summer routine, covered.") with a dark-haired model, then a product grid that pivots immediately into waxing supplies — Brunette Glam Wax, SugarCoat Hard Wax Beads, Acai Berry Hard Wax Beads — and I'm like, wait, are we doing at-home waxing now? That is a genuinely useful category but it is *not* the vibe I signed up for when I followed Sally on the strength of their professional hair tools and color. Below that, some lip plumping and a dry shampoo section round out the scroll.
- The whole thing reads like a category dump, not a curated new-arrivals edit. Charlotte Tilbury dropped a focused GWP moment last week that told a story in one scroll. ILIA ran a tight brand overview that made me feel like they knew who they were talking to. This feels like Sally opened a warehouse and took a photo. I'd mark it read, not click, definitely not forward.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is clean and on-brand for summer** — the warm palette, the "covered" positioning, and the model's hair are all aesthetically cohesive on first glance.
- **Model is recognizably my demographic** — dark hair, looks late 20s/early 30s, not the 50+ salon-professional default Sally sometimes leans into.
- **Seasonal alignment is real** — "Summer Must-Haves" and the warm color temperature at least signal they're not running a stale promo.
- **No render bugs** — layout held cleanly, no broken images, product grid stacked normally, CTAs were visible.

## What's weak

- **Wax products as the hero category is a hard left turn** — the scroll goes hero → waxing → more waxing → lips → dry shampoo. There's no editorial throughline, and waxing feels like a salon-pro SKU, not a beauty enthusiast pick.
- **Zero offer, zero discount, zero points math** — after two back-to-back percent-off emails (40% on Hot Tools, $20 flash), landing in a full-price "new arrivals" email with no loyalty callout reads like a bait-and-switch in reverse. Why click now?
- **"Shop Now" everywhere, says nothing** — every section's CTA is the same generic button. Compare that to e.l.f.'s "$7 Soft Glam Cream Blush" email from last week where the price was the CTA. One specific number does more work than six "Shop Now" buttons.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is send four in eight days. The May 15 "$20 Off" send already made me note I was building an ignore reflex. This one does nothing to reset that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero product and a reason to care right now.** Pick the lip plumper or the dry shampoo — whichever has social buzz or a new formula — and build the email around that. The wax grid buried in row two belongs in a dedicated segmented send to confirmed wax-buyers.
- 2. **Add an offer or a loyalty hook.** Even "Earn 2x points on new arrivals this week" would give me a reason to click today vs. next week. Without it, "new arrivals" is just a catalog page.
- 3. **Give the CTA copy some specificity.** "Shop Summer Waves" or "See the New Lip Edit" — anything with a noun. "Shop Now" ×6 is dead weight.
- 4. **Slow down the cadence or differentiate harder.** Four emails in eight days only works if each one feels distinct. The May 11 beach waves email actually had an angle; this one doesn't. Rest the list or raise the editorial bar.
