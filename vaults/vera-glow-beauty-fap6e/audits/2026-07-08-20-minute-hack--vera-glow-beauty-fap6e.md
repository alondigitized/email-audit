# 20-minute hack
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- So "20-minute hack" lands in my inbox on July 8th and, credit where it's due, I opened it — but only because I've been trained by eight months of DE opens to expect something clever on the inside. The subject itself is doing almost no work: it's the kind of teaser that requires you to already be in the Babyfacial cult to even guess what the hack is. And after July 6's "Beautiful, glowing skin" — the most aggressively generic subject DE has ever sent me — I was already side-eyeing this sender.
- What's inside redeems it, though. This is a reformulation announcement for Babyfacial, which is actually news worth emailing about, and there's a 15% off code in the top banner which I appreciate more than the June 30 "It kits" email that somehow escalated from 20% to 25% overnight and made me feel like a points sucker for buying early. The ingredient callout (25% AHAs + 2% salicylic acid) and the clinical stats with a disclosed study size are exactly the kind of formulation detail I look for and rarely get from Sephora's editorial emails.
- Would I click? Yes — 15% on Babyfacial is real money and the reformulation is legitimately interesting to me. Would I forward it? Only to my one friend who already uses Babyfacial. As a cold introduction to DE, this email is basically useless.

## What's working

- The **15% OFF CLUB code** in the top banner is visible, clean, and no-exclusion — after all the Ulta GWP fine print I've waded through lately, I notice when an offer is just... an offer
- **"PLEASE EXFOLIATE RESPONSIBLY"** is so quintessentially DE — witty, confident, self-aware — that I trust the brand voice immediately; ILIA did something similar with the HAIM collab energy but DE earns it without a celebrity hook
- **Ingredient specificity** ("25% AHAs, 2% salicylic acid") speaks directly to an ingredient-literate buyer — e.l.f.'s $15 lip combos never give me this level of formulation context, and even Sephora's "Summer's hottest beauty" roundup didn't get here
- **The 90% smoothness stat with a disclosed study size** ("39 people, 8 weeks") is honest in a way that feels earned — they didn't hide the small sample and that builds trust rather than eroding it
- **DRUNK TIPS** (Marula Oil after Babyfacial to restore barrier) is a genuinely useful usage pairing I didn't know — it makes DE feel like a teacher, not just a merchant

## What's weak

- **Fourth email in eight days** — after July 2, 4, 6, and now 8, the cadence is approaching e.l.f.'s lip-product pace; e.l.f. can sustain that at $15 a pop, but Babyfacial is not a weekly impulse buy
- The **15% off is buried in the top banner**, not the hero — someone who skims the "PLEASE EXFOLIATE RESPONSIBLY" block and dips could completely miss the discount
- **"SHOP NOW" three times** across the email is the laziest CTA possible; "Shop the New Babyfacial" would make each link earn its placement
- The **"To go, please!" Littles section at the bottom** feels like a different email stapled on — it fragments the reformulation narrative and forces me to decide what this email is actually about

## Recommendations

- 1. **Merge the discount into the hero.** Don't make me process a banner AND a product story separately. "The Babyfacial you love — now gentler. 15% off this week." in the hero copy kills two birds.
- 2. **Specific CTAs for each section.** "Shop the Reformulated Babyfacial" / "Try the Marula Duo" / "Shop the Littles" — give me a reason to click the right thing, not just any thing.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline on the 15% code.** Right now there's zero urgency on the offer, which means I'm tabbing it and coming back never. Even "through Sunday" would make me do the points math against my Sephora Beauty Insider event timing.
- 4. **Cut or separate the Littles section.** It's a good product but it's a different email — the reformulation story deserves a clean close, not a upsell tag.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Babyfacial formula — 15% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We made Babyfacial gentler. Read why.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `25% AHAs, softer formula, 15% off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The reformulation story — plus your discount code.`

## Technical Audit — Drunk Elephant "20-minute hack"

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based layout with two confirmed code-level defects: HTML comment syntax embedded inside a `<style>` block silently voids two media query rules, and all three custom web fonts are loaded twice via redundant `<link>` + `@import` declarations. Remaining categories require the full (non-truncated) HTML to audit completely.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit — HTML source is truncated before link elements appear.** Review the full source for:
- Open/click tracking pixel (typically a 1×1 `<img>` at top or bottom)
- UTM parameters on all `<a href>` links
- Redirect chain depth on tracked URLs (ESP redirect → destination)

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — Invalid HTML comments inside `<style>` block:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->   ← INVALID
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} --> ← INVALID
```

`<!-- -->` is HTML syntax, not CSS syntax. Inside a `<style>` block it is treated as literal text by CSS parsers — both `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive overrides are silently dropped in every client. The elements those classes target will not reflow at ≤600px.

**Confirmed issue — Duplicate font loading:**

Each of the three Brown font variants is loaded via both a `<link>` tag in `<head>` and an `@import` inside the `<style>` block, targeting the same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` CDN files. This doubles HTTP requests for font assets before render.

```html
<!-- in <head> -->
<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold" ...>

<!-- inside <style> -->
@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold);
```

Remove one mechanism — `@import` inside `<style>` is the one to cut, as it blocks rendering until resolved.

**Minor — Global `line-height: 100%` override:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applied to every element via the `*` selector. On multi-line text blocks this will clip descenders in Outlook and some Android clients. Should be scoped to the specific elements that need it.

**Minor — Non-descriptive `<title>`:**

```html
<title>Drunk Elephant</title>
```

Screen readers and some preview panes announce this. It should match or summarize the subject line ("20-Minute Routine | Drunk Elephant" or similar).

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**

`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` all load exclusively from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. `BrownRegular` is self-hosted on `www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`. If `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is unavailable, three of the four weights fall back to system fonts with no declared CSS `font-family` fallback stack visible in the `@font-face` rules.
