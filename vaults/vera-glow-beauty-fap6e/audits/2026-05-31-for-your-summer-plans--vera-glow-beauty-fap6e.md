# For your summer plans 🩷
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- Five Glossier emails in eight days, and this is the third time the Pink Capsule Collection has landed in my inbox. May 28 was the launch. May 30 was "Fetch a little joy." Today it's a summer activity checklist — same collection, third repackaging. I clocked it immediately, and honestly the editorial confidence is impressive until you realize the "So fetch" CTA is yesterday's pun recycled word-for-word.
- The format is a four-panel lookbook: busy weekend mornings (Orange Bag), walking your dog (four-legged favorite), nighttime adventures (Pink Embroidered Hoodie), plus a general summer hero at the top. The copy is signature Glossier — "Need to run to the grocery store? The Protectors? The flea market?" — specific, a little offbeat, charming. But there's no offer anywhere. No discount, no loyalty hook, no early access for known customers. After a 30% Memorial Day sale ended six days ago, pure editorial without a reason to buy is a hard ask.
- I'd scroll through on autopilot and close it. No click, no forward — my friend got the same email.

## What's working

- **Brand voice is completely locked in.** "The Protectors? The flea market?" is exactly the Glossier cadence — a little weird, very intentional, sounds like a human wrote it.
- **Clean four-panel layout.** Each section is clearly delineated with image, headline, copy, and button. Zero confusion on mobile, easy to scroll.
- **Demographic signals land.** The lifestyle imagery reads like women in my orbit doing actual things — grocery runs, dog walks, outdoor nights.
- **Summer timing is editorially correct.** Pivoting from the sale grind to a "here's summer in our products" frame is the right strategic move for end of May.
- **"So fetch" made me smile before I realized it was yesterday's joke.** The Mean Girls reference earned a half-second of delight.

## What's weak

- **Third Pink Capsule appearance in three days.** The Pink Embroidered Hoodie has its own section here, same as May 28 and May 30. I've either bought it or I'm not buying it — a fourth look isn't moving me.
- **"So fetch" is a 24-hour-old pun.** May 30's subject line was "Fetch a little joy." Recycling the same fetch joke as a CTA the very next day kills the delight — it reads like creative ran dry overnight.
- **No offer.** Charlotte Tilbury's digest email — the one I scored 9/10 — ran a similar multi-section editorial format but still threaded in urgency and product specificity that made clicking feel purposeful. This doesn't give me a single reason to buy now vs. next week.
- **Four equally-weighted CTAs.** "Shop now," "Get yours," "So fetch," "Take a peek" — there's no primary. ILIA builds every email around one bold CTA that carries the whole send. This feels like four emails stapled together.
- **Zero personalization.** Glossier knows my purchase history. Nothing here signals they know who I am — I'm getting the exact same email as someone who's never bought anything. Compare that to Sephora, where even a sale blast will call out my Rouge tier. Glossier just doesn't play that game, and I notice every time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and one CTA for the entire email.** The Pink Hoodie is the most visually distinct product — make it the single star. Four sections with four buttons is noise.
- 2. **Attach even a soft offer.** Free shipping threshold, a Glossier You sample with the hoodie, anything. Editorial-only after a sale period needs something to convert.
- 3. **Rest the fetch pun for at least a week.** The dog section can stay; the lifestyle angle is fun. Swap to "For your four-legged co-pilot" or "Your pup's new walk-day look" — same vibe, no déjà vu.
- 4. **Add a member or loyalty signal.** Even "as a Glossier customer" signals they know me. Sephora Rouge messaging makes me feel seen; this treats me like a cold lead.
- 5. **One consolidation send next time.** This is the third Pink Capsule email — fold the final push into one definitive "last look before summer" send rather than three reframings.
