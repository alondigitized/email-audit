# Okay, Last Chance 😉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Ten. This is Sally Beauty email number ten in roughly thirteen days, and they've landed on "Okay, Last Chance 😉" — which is either the most self-aware subject line in this entire run, or the most accidental one. The winking emoji is doing a lot of heavy lifting: it reads like Sally shrugging and saying *yeah, we know.* I'll give them credit for not screaming at me like Charlotte Tilbury did with "20% OFF!! Now Is The Time To Shop!" — that double-exclamation energy aged badly fast. But a wink is not a strategy.
- The email itself is actually competently assembled. The hero is clean, the 50% off Memorial Day messaging is front and center, and there are concrete price points scattered all the way down (2 for $15, $4.99 kit, 40% off sections). What immediately short-circuits my click, though, is the **dual-promo confusion**: there's a "20% off sitewide" flash sale banner sitting right on top of the "Up to 50% off" Memorial Day hero, and nowhere does the email tell me whether these stack. If they stack, I actually want to do this math. If they don't, the flash sale is just visual noise. That one unresolved question probably costs Sally 30% of their click-through rate.
- The ion cordless tools at 50% off and the Shimmer Lights toning section genuinely caught my eye — those are in-my-wheelhouse products. But I've been trained by nine previous emails not to trust "last chance." I'll probably open the tab, confirm it's the same haul I've seen since May 12, and close it. Would I forward this to a friend? Not a chance — I'd be embarrassed to be the person forwarding Sally's tenth email in thirteen days.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and leads with the number that matters — "Up to 50% off" is visible immediately without scrolling on mobile, which is more than Ulta manages on most of their tiered spend-and-save sends.
- Concrete price points throughout (2 for $15, $4.99 braiding kit, 40% off marked clearly) make the value feel tangible rather than just vague "up to X%" fluff.
- The 50% off ion cordless styling tools is a specific high-ticket category deal — this is the one section I'd screenshot for my group chat.
- The Shimmer Lights toning products section is a subtle segmentation nod — Sally knows some of their subscribers are maintaining color, not just browsing shampoo.
- Renders cleanly with no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no overlapping text — solid execution for a content-heavy grid email.

## What's weak

- Two overlapping promotions (20% off sitewide flash sale + 50% off Memorial Day) are never resolved — I'm doing mental gymnastics about stacking and confusion kills the click.
- "Last Chance" with a winking emoji deflates the urgency it's supposed to create; compare this to Drunk Elephant's "Ending soon: 25% off" (9/10 when I audited it), which had a countdown timer and one clean offer. DE said urgency. Sally's wink said *just kidding.*
- No expiration timestamp is visible — "last chance" without a deadline is meaningless. Midnight tonight? End of day? Give me a number.
- Every CTA is a generic "Shop now" — Sephora typically buttons specific categories ("Shop the Sale," "Shop Skincare") which routes you into the right landing page and earns the click; Sally sends me to the homepage and makes me hunt.
- Zero loyalty-member messaging — tiny club icons buried in the footer don't count. There's no "Club members earn double points this weekend" — nothing that treats me like a known customer rather than a cold email address.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the dual-promo confusion in the hero.** Either run one promo or explicitly say "20% off sitewide, or save up to 50% on select items — whichever saves you more." If they do stack, lead with that: it would be the most effective subject line Sally has sent in two weeks.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline with a timestamp.** "Ends tonight at midnight PT" turns "last chance" from a vibe into a commitment. This is table stakes — e.l.f. does it, Drunk Elephant does it, Glossier did it cleanly on their close email.
- 3. **Pick one hero product and make it the email.** The ion cordless curling iron at 50% off should be the whole point. e.l.f.'s free Jelly Pop Glow Stick email worked because one product, one offer, one click. Sally's giving me twelve decisions and I'm making zero.
- 4. **Upgrade the CTAs.** "Shop 50% Off Styling Tools" beats "Shop now" every time. Takes ten seconds to write, meaningfully lifts click-to-open rate.
- 5. **Surface a loyalty hook.** One line — "Club members: earn double points this weekend" — makes this feel personalized rather than broadcast. That's the difference between a loyalty email and a mass-market flyer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`'Founders Grotesk'` undeclared — anchor font silently falls back.**
```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```
No `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` exists anywhere in the `<style>` block. Only Open Sans variants are declared. Every rendered link in the email falls back to Helvetica/sans-serif. If the design intent was Founders Grotesk on links, it is not loading.

**`box-shadow` on `.boxShadow` renders only in Apple Mail / Outlook for Mac.**
```css
.boxShadow { box-shadow: 0 4px 2px -2px rgba(146,140,140,1) !important; }
```
Gmail (all clients), Outlook for Windows, and most mobile clients strip CSS `box-shadow`. Any element using this class will appear flat in the majority of the recipient base. No table-border fallback is present.

**`:hover` on `.filter-item` is non-functional in most clients.**
```css
.filter-item:hover { background-color: #feb7ae !important; border-radius: 4px !important; }
```
`:hover` pseudo-class only works in Apple Mail, Outlook for Mac, and some webmail. It is inert in Gmail apps, Outlook for Windows, and all native mobile clients. If this interaction is load-bearing UX, it needs a static fallback state.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` is a non-standard property on images.**
`white-space` has no defined effect on replaced elements like `<img>`. This is dead CSS — likely a template artifact, but it increases stylesheet parse weight without effect.

**`-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic` is obsolete.**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; }
```
This property only applied to IE6–7 and has been unsupported for ~15 years. Dead code.
