# A major new era for e.l.f. 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, e.l.f. is now emailing me about *hair* — nine emails in nine days and they've branched into a whole new category on me. My first reaction was genuine surprise: "LOOK WHO'S E.L.F.ING HAIR" is a fun hook, and the influencer-style hero shot (Anna, tousled beachy waves, holding the Never Thirsty bottle like she's mid-GRWM) reads more like a Glossier UGC moment than a typical e.l.f. product-grid blast. That's a nice change of pace after a week of "here's a product, buy it" sends.
- But here's the thing — I'm a Rouge/Diamond girl who lives in color cosmetics and skincare, not shampoo aisles, and there's zero acknowledgment of that. No points math, no tier perk, no "as a Beauty Squad member" nod, nothing that treats me like a repeat customer versus someone opening a cold launch email. Compare that to Sally Beauty's "Repair and replenish with It's a 10" send, which led with an actual $20-off flash sale on a hair line I already use — this one just shows me six SKUs at $6-11 and two identical "CLICK HAIR" buttons with no discount in sight.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about the Humidity Hero anti-frizz spray since my hair genuinely doesn't do humidity well. Would I forward it? No — this reads like brand-extension news, not something urgent enough to share. Marking as read and moving on, same as I do with most of e.l.f.'s daily flood at this point.

## What's working

- The hero image feels authentic and UGC-adjacent — Anna's testimonial framing ("Anna uses Never Thirsty...") is a step up from e.l.f.'s usual flat product-on-white shots.
- The wordplay tagline ("e.l.f.ective, e.l.f.fordable, e.l.f.onic") keeps the brand voice consistent with recent sends like "Glitz with benefits" and "See what's calling your e.l.f.ing name."
- The "meet the mane crew" product grid is clean and color-coded by product line — easy to scan even if I don't shop this category.
- All six hair SKUs are priced in the $6-11 range, so at least the value positioning e.l.f. is known for carries over here.

## What's weak

- No tier or Beauty Squad perk mentioned anywhere, which is exactly the kind of loyalty hook that would've made me feel like a known customer instead of a cold lead for a new sub-line.
- Zero discount or launch offer — just regular pricing. Sally Beauty's recent It's a 10 send had an actual $20-off flash sale; this has nothing to compete with that.
- "CLICK HAIR" as CTA copy twice is a little too cute and not action-clear — I had to look at the "SHOP NOW" buttons under Top Picks to actually know what tapping would do.
- This is yet another e.l.f. email in a week where I've already gotten eight — the cadence fatigue from Glossier's twelve-emails-since-July-7th problem is creeping into e.l.f.'s inbox behavior too.
- The category nav footer (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) is just gray bars with text — no visual interest, feels like an afterthought compared to the vibrant hero above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch offer or tier perk.** Even "Beauty Squad members get double points on Hair this week" would justify treating this as a genuine cross-category push rather than a cold catalog drop.
- 2. **Fix the subject line to actually say "hair."** Nobody scanning their inbox knows this is a new product category from the subject alone — that's a real miss.
- 3. **Swap "CLICK HAIR" for a clear action verb** ("Shop the Hair Launch") so the CTA doesn't require a second look to understand.
- 4. **Space this out from the rest of the week's sends** — nine emails in nine days is Glossier-level territory, and a genuinely new category launch deserves room to breathe, not to get buried in the pile.
