# Lock In For The Longest Day 💕
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I want to be fair here: "Lock In For The Longest Day" actually made me stop my thumb for half a second, which is more than any of the previous CT sends this week managed. The summer solstice tie-in is legitimately clever — setting spray, longest day, "lock it in" — and when I opened, the email does deliver on that premise. Airbrush Flawless Setting Spray as the full hero, two SKUs differentiated, ingredient callouts, dual wear-hour numbers. That's a more focused brief than the "Unreal Icon" parade I've been living through since June 11.
- But here's where I land: there's no actual offer. The 2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts GWP is buried so close to the footer I almost missed it, and even then I have no idea what the spend threshold is. As someone who is absolutely doing the points math on every CT purchase — and who watches Glossier run 25%-off events and Sally Beauty name a brand in the subject line — "look how long-wearing this spray is" without a discount or a front-and-center GWP is just awareness work dressed up as a conversion email.
- I'd mark it read and maybe screenshot the Matte spray for later research. I would not forward this — at this cadence, I'd be embarrassed to inflict another CT email on a friend.

## What's working

- **Solstice hook earns its place** — "longest day" maps directly to the 16-hour and 22-hour wear claims; it doesn't feel like a stretch.
- **Two SKUs with genuinely distinct bullets** — Original (radiant, green tea, anti-pollution) vs. Matte (22-hour oil control, Powder Blur Technology) is real product education, not padding.
- **Ingredient storytelling is on-brand** — the science-y callouts (Aloe Vera, Japanese Green Tea Extract, Anti-Pollution Shield) are what CT does best and they show up on both products.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — spray bottle leads, copy follows, layout doesn't fight itself.
- **GWP offer exists** — "Unlock 2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts" at least gives me an economic reason to visit the site, even if it's buried.

## What's weak

- **Zero price incentive in the hero** — Glossier opened their sale week with "Final Hours: Up to 25% off" right in the subject; Sally Beauty put "50% Off It's a 10" front-and-center. CT gives me "here's a spray that lasts." That's not a conversion argument.
- **Cadence has destroyed the surprise factor** — this is send eight-plus since June 11. Even the clever solstice hook can't fully reset the fatigue. The bar for me to click is now "discount me or genuinely surprise me."
- **CTAs are soft** — "LOCK IN LOOK" and "DISCOVER MORE" don't tell me where I'm landing. Drunk Elephant and ILIA at least write CTAs that name the product. These read like CMS defaults.
- **GWP threshold is invisible** — the free minis offer near the footer doesn't specify the spend requirement. That's the most actionable piece of copy in the email and it's an incomplete sentence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the GWP offer up to a mid-hero band or banner** — "Spend $X, unlock 2 free minis" belongs in the first scroll, not the last. That's the offer. Lead with it.
- 2. **Replace soft CTAs with product-specific verbs** — "Shop the Matte Spray" or "Shop Both Sprays" converts better than "LOCK IN LOOK," especially in an inbox where I'm already CT-fatigued and need a reason to commit the tap.
- 3. **Give me a recommendation split** — "Dry/normal skin → Original. Oily/combo → Matte." One line. I don't want to do the homework across two bullet sets.
- 4. **Make the solstice feel singular** — a line like "one day, one send" or "the only CT email you'll get today" would've acknowledged the moment and made it feel less like email nine in a series.
- 5. **Surface any loyalty angle** — I'm Rouge and Diamond at my other accounts. If CT has a rewards or early-access mechanic, this one-day send is exactly where "member exclusive" language would've converted me.
