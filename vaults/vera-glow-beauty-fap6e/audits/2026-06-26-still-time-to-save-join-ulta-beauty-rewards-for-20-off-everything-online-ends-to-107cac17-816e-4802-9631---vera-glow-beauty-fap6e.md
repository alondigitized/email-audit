# Still time to save 💸️ Join Ulta Beauty Rewards® for 20% off everything online⁺⁺—ends tonight! Use it again (Terms & excl. apply)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- I have to lead with what's sitting on my chest before I say anything else: this is the third time this email has landed in my inbox. I have the receipts. June 23 (twice) and now June 26 — the final day — with "Use it again" literally in the subject line, which is either a self-aware wink or the most passive-aggressive thing Ulta has ever done to me. I genuinely haven't decided which.
- The 20% off hero is doing its job. Orange, bold, above the fold, no hunting required — I clocked the offer in under a second. The product grid below it is price-forward and actually covers the kind of mix I'd browse on a Sunday: hair, skincare, fragrance, a few prestige picks at mid-hundreds. The "stacks with 3,000+ products on sale" copy does the math for me, which I appreciate. Visually? Clean. Competent. Recognizably Ulta.
- Here's where it falls apart for me personally: I'm Diamond. I have been Diamond. I did embarrassing amounts of points math to stay Diamond. And Ulta is in my inbox on the *last day* of this offer treating me like I found a coupon in a parking lot and might need to be reminded what Ulta Beauty Rewards is. Would I click? Maybe on a specific product in that grid if I was already planning a purchase tonight. Would I forward it? Honestly yes — to anyone in my life who isn't already a member. For me? Mark as read.

## What's working

- The orange 20% off hero reads in a single glance — offer clarity on par with Sephora's summer flash sends, maybe better than their recent grid-heavy "Last chance to save up to 50%" layout.
- "Stacks with 3,000+ products on sale" is exactly the kind of copy that makes me actually open the landing page — I need to know the discount isn't cordoned off to the markdown rack.
- Price-on-tile in the product grid is right; no clicking into a PDP just to find out what something costs.
- "Ends tonight" is a real urgency signal on a real end date — even as a third send, I believe the deadline.
- Layout is clean, no broken images, no overlapping copy, nothing fighting for space.

## What's weak

- Ulta sent a "join Rewards" acquisition email to someone who is Ulta Diamond. This is the segmentation equivalent of a restaurant loyalty app asking their most frequent diner to download the app.
- Three near-identical sends in four days. "Use it again" in the subject acknowledges the repeat but doesn't fix the cadence problem — I've emotionally already processed this email twice.
- The subject line is over 130 characters. It reads as: sale copy, then program pitch, then legal, then meta-commentary on the campaign. That's four distinct ideas fighting each other before the first scroll.
- No hero product. e.l.f. dropped "NEW ☁️ So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" in one line and I clicked before I finished reading. This email is a browse destination, not a decision trigger.
- Compared to Charlotte Tilbury's recent "NEW To Sale! Over 50% Off!" — which also has specificity problems — this email at least has product tiles, but CT's subject does more with fewer words. Ulta is losing the inbox competition on both ends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment existing members out of this creative entirely.** Diamond and Platinum members should get a "your points are burning, here's what to buy tonight" version — not a new-joiner acquisition push.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters.** "Ends tonight!" is the hook. Everything after it is waste.
- 3. **Feature one hero product above the grid.** A single prestige pick with a product shot and a specific reason to buy it beats a grid of 12 unnamed tiles for driving a decision.
- 4. **"Use it again" is a genuinely good line** — it's human, self-aware, and honest. Promote it out of the parenthetical and put it first.
- 5. **Acknowledge my tier in the body copy.** Even one line — "your Diamond account is ready" — prevents the stranger-treatment feeling that kills loyalty in loyal customers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: 20% off everything 💸`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Use it again — 20% off, ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stacks with 3,000+ sale items. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Diamond account is ready. This is your last window.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Google Fonts `<link>` stripped in Gmail (webmail)**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:..." rel="stylesheet">
```

Gmail web strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`. DM Sans will silently fall back to whatever the client's default font is. The CSS fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source — verify it includes `Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` or equivalent. No rendering breakage, but brand font will fail in the highest-volume client.

**Issue 2 — Blanket link color suppression in Outlook.com**

```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
}
```

This override removes underlines and inherits color from surrounding text for all anchors inside Outlook.com's message wrapper. Links become visually indistinguishable from body copy unless the containing element provides explicit color contrast. WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires links to be distinguishable without color alone — underline removal without a substitute indicator is a violation.

**Issue 3 — `translate="no"` on `<html>`**

```html
<html lang="en-US" translate="no" ...>
```

Suppresses Google Translate offer globally. May be intentional (branded product names, promo codes), but blocks accessibility for non-English-speaking subscribers who rely on in-client translation. Note, not automatically a bug — flag for deliberate policy confirmation.

**Issue 4 — Image `alt` coverage not verifiable**

The `a img { border: none; }` and `img { outline: none; }` resets suggest images are used extensively. Cannot confirm alt text presence from truncated source. Must be audited in full HTML.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Issue — Ghost token artifact in `<title>`**

```html
<title>Ulta Beauty </title>
```

Trailing whitespace after "Ulta Beauty" is a common residue of a removed merge token (e.g., `[FIRST_NAME]` or `[SUBJECT_LINE]` that was stripped without cleaning trailing space). Does not affect rendering but indicates a template hygiene gap. Subject line preview in some clients may also pull from `<title>` as a fallback.

No other merge token issues visible in the provided source portion.
