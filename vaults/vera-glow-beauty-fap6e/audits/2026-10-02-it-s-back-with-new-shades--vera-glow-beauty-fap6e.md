# It’s back (with NEW shades)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This one lands. "It's Back" with a close-up model and the palette in her hand puts the product in the hero. Compare that with ILIA's 9/30 poppies email, where I scrolled twice before I saw a product. Here a launch with new shades, a visible button and a sold-out-last-year hook all show up before I scroll. I feel the "(But not for long.)" urgency, even though no date backs it up.
- The three looks (Botanical Blur, Coastal Bloom, Poppy Fields) each get their own shade list with "(new)" tags, so I can see what's new without hunting. But the offer is a GWP again: a free mini Limitless Lash Mascara at £100+. That's the same mechanic as the red bar on 9/30 (free mini eye stylus at $75+). The £ threshold also tells me this send isn't built for a US shopper. I'd click, but I'd be doing my points math on a palette price I can't see anywhere.
- I'd forward it to my makeup-obsessed friend for the Poppy Fields shades. I wouldn't forward it for the gift.

## What's working

- The hero is a tight face shot with the palette in hand and a big "It's Back." I know the product and the story in a second.
- Each look has its own named shade list with swatch dots and "(new)" tags. That's the shade-level detail I need to pick a palette, and Sephora rarely gives it to me in an email.
- The Fig. 02, 03 and 04 editorial layout, with a different model for each look, feels like a magazine spread. It's as pretty as the 9/26 "In fall tones" moodboard.
- There are two red "SHOP THE PALETTE →" buttons, one near the top and one after the looks. The CTA copy is a clear verb plus noun.
- The model range is genuinely varied, and the shades are shown on different skin tones. That matters for my personalization checks.
- The "safe for sensitive skin, non-comidogenic, formulated without talc" strip answers my ingredient questions.

## What's weak

- I can't see a price anywhere. A palette with no price and a £100 gift threshold means I can't do my points math.
- "(But not for long.)" has no deadline or stock number behind it. Urgency with no date reads as filler.
- The free mascara is the same type of offer as 9/30 and gets a long section at the bottom. I'd rather see it as a short banner than a whole panel.
- The £100+ threshold feels off for me as a US shopper, and it adds friction.
- The green succulent "housed in a recyclable compact" image is pretty but doesn't do anything. It's a dead scroll that pushes the gift farther down.
- The only member or loyalty cue is the "claim gift" bar. There's nothing like Ulta Diamond or Sephora Rouge perks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the price and a promo deadline in the first screen, e.g. "$XX · limited edition · ends 10/15." That alone would make the urgency credible.
- 2. Shrink the free-mascara section into one strip, and set the threshold to the shopper's currency so the offer fits.
- 3. Cut the succulent compact image and put the Poppy Fields and Coastal Bloom shades closer together. That removes a scroll.
- 4. Add a "shade finder" or "match my skin tone" link under the looks. It would use the diverse casting to do something useful.
- 5. Add a tier-style line for members, like "early access for ILIA Rewards members."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Sold out last year. Back now in 3 new shades 🌸`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Poppy Fields, Coastal Bloom, Botanical Blur. Limited edition, ends 10/15.`
