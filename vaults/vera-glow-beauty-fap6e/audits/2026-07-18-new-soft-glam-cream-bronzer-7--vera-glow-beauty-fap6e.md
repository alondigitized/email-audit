# NEW! Soft Glam Cream Bronzer, $7 🤎
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is e.l.f. email number five in five days — and I'll say this: they picked the right day to launch something actually new. A $7 cream bronzer is a genuinely interesting proposition when Charlotte Tilbury's Filmstar Bronze & Glow is sitting at $58 and ILIA's Multi-Stick is $44. The hero face stops you cold — deep, sculpted, glowing — and the shade range drops immediately underneath. That's a smart sequence. e.l.f. is not wasting my scroll.
- What I'm less sold on is the "INGREDIENTS FOR BUTTER SKIN" section. The 1% shea butter and sunflower vitamin E callouts are fine, but they feel like they're borrowing Clean Beauty Language from ILIA or Drunk Elephant — brands that have actually *earned* the skincare-makeup crossover. At e.l.f., I trust the price before I trust the ingredient narrative.
- Bottom line: I'd click. I'm not forwarding this one — it's a solid launch email, not a "you NEED to see this" moment. But a $7 cream bronzer from a brand I trust to deliver on value? That earns a tap.

## What's working

- **Hero model demographics are dead-on.** Deep skin tone with a luminous bronzed finish is exactly what this product needs to prove it performs. No guesswork about how it'll look on me.
- **$7 is in the subject line AND feels like the hero message.** e.l.f. doesn't bury the price the way Ulta buries GWP thresholds — it leads with it.
- **Shade range is visible and labeled clearly** — four shades from light/medium to deep, with descriptors. That's table stakes for a complexion product in 2026 but it's still done right here.
- **"The Cream Team" cross-sell is genuinely smart.** Positioning the new bronzer alongside existing cream products builds a franchise, not just a one-off SKU.
- **Two different skin tone models.** The switch from the deep-toned hero to a lighter-toned model in the ingredients section shows range without being performative about it.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is at its limit.** This is email five in five days. Yesterday's "Go Jumbo" drag queen email was excellent — 9/10, earned the open. Two days ago was Invisi-Stick SPF. I'm getting close to muting.
- **"INGREDIENTS FOR BUTTER SKIN" feels borrowed.** This is Clean Beauty framing that doesn't quite land on an e.l.f. product. Drunk Elephant owns this language; e.l.f.'s actual moat is price-to-performance, not ingredient stories.
- **No loyalty or member signal anywhere visible.** I'm a Sephora Rouge. When Sephora launches a complexion product, there's at least a hint of "Rouge Early Access" or "collect 10 points per dollar." e.l.f. treats me like a stranger, same as the Ulta email that told me an event was already over.
- **"Top Picks for You" grid at the bottom feels templated.** Four small product tiles in the same layout I've seen in every e.l.f. email this week. If it's truly personalized, nothing signals that — it reads as filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the price-to-prestige gap.** The $7 number is doing heavy lifting in the subject but the body hero doesn't punch it hard enough. A line like "Cream bronzer. $7. No, really." would stop me faster than "Soft Glam Cream Bronzer."
- 2. **Drop "INGREDIENTS FOR BUTTER SKIN" or sharpen it.** Either own the ingredients story with specific claims ("clinically tested for 24-hr wear") or cut it and give the shade range more visual space.
- 3. **Give loyalty members something to see.** Even a line like "e.l.f. Rewards: earn on every $7" acknowledges I'm a known customer.
- 4. **Make "Top Picks for You" actually feel personal.** If that section is algorithmic, surface why — even a label like "because you bought Power Grip Primer" would sell the personalization.
- 5. **Rest for a day.** Seriously. After the Jumbo Grip email yesterday, skipping a day and letting the bronzer land on its own terms would have increased my open rate.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Opaque redirect chain — UTM unverifiable**
The logo/hero link resolves through `links.elfcosmetics.com` using a Braze-encoded redirect:
```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/0bmQ_5YY8yag-Q3GTjx3ZA~~/AAQRxRA~/rxE6Wek...
```

The destination URL and any UTM parameters are Base64-encoded inside the token. They cannot be audited from the HTML source alone. If this link is misconfigured in Braze, it will silently redirect to a wrong or dead URL.

**Action:** Decode or click-test the redirect in staging to confirm the landing page and verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended at the destination.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Samsung Mail, Apple Mail) surface the title in thread previews. An empty value degrades the experience on those clients.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the hero link**
```html
<a href="..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
This explicitly removes the primary CTA link from keyboard tab order, breaking keyboard-only and switch-access navigation. `outline:none` compounds the issue by hiding focus indicators even when focus lands there via other means.

**Google Fonts `<link>` stripped by Gmail**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail (webmail and Android app) strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`. The fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` will engage — confirm Arial renders acceptably in affected clients during QA.

**Double-nested `nl-container` tables**
Inside `html_block block-1` there is a full inner `nl-container → row → row-content → column` stack embedded inside a `<div>` inside the outer container. This structural redundancy is a sign of template copy-paste error and may cause unexpected cell width calculations in Outlook 2016/2019.

**Three empty `html_block` elements**
`block-2`, `block-3`, and `block-4` contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These add DOM weight and can introduce phantom spacing in some clients. Remove or consolidate.
