# NEW 👄 dew more for your lips 
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Nine. Nine e.l.f. emails since May 15 — and now two in a single day, this lip launch arriving on the same Tuesday as the Survivor collab send I already reviewed this morning. Before I even open this, my goodwill account with this brand is deeply overdrawn. That cadence context is impossible to separate from how this email lands.
- That said: the lip hero is genuinely pretty. The dewy, glossy close-up stopped my thumb. "Dew more for your lips" is a cute pun, and I have a real soft spot for a dedicated lip launch from a brand that consistently delivers at drugstore prices. If this were email three or four this week instead of nine, I'd be way more charmed by it.
- But the email can't commit to a single story. It opens on a lip product launch, pivots to something called The Dew Drop with skincare-adjacent positioning, throws in the Survivor collab I literally just received a dedicated send about hours ago, and then ends with a Target retail callout. No price points in the hero. No loyalty mention. No points math. After the Power Grip Rosé send on May 18 — which earned a 10 from me because it committed fully to one beautiful product story — this feels scattered. I'd scroll through the lip section out of genuine category interest, maybe tap once, but I am not forwarding this and I'm actively eyeing the unsubscribe button for the first time with this brand.

## What's working

- The lip hero image earns its top placement — dewy close-up on glossy lips is precisely the aesthetic that makes someone pause mid-scroll
- "Dew more for your lips" is a solid pun; e.l.f.'s wordplay has been a consistent bright spot across this entire run of sends
- Multiple lip SKUs (Sheer Genius, Sheer Bliss) signal a real product family, not a one-off — that's a reason to explore the full line
- The Target retail callout is a smart mass-prestige cross-shopping signal for someone like me who buys launches wherever is most convenient
- Visual layout is clean with no render bugs — no broken images, no overlapping text, the sections stack correctly

## What's weak

- "e.l.f. SKIN" is the sender sub-brand but this email leads with lip makeup — that category mismatch would make me pause in my inbox before opening, not in a curious way
- Zero price points in the hero copy: e.l.f.'s entire brand equity is prestige quality at drugstore prices, and not leading with even a "starting at $12" buries the core value proposition
- The Survivor collab section stitched into the bottom of a lip launch email actively dilutes both stories — I already got that Survivor send today; repeating it here makes this email feel like it's padding out its own word count
- No tier or loyalty mention anywhere — I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond and this email treats me like a complete stranger; ILIA at least tried a VIP loyalty angle in their recent sends, even if the timing was awkward
- Five or six distinct sections creates a scroll marathon for what should be a tight product launch; Charlotte Tilbury's 20% off email last week gave me exactly one story, one button, and I was done in ten seconds

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Survivor section entirely** — it had its own dedicated send today, it's fighting for attention here, and combining them makes both feel like filler
- 2. **Add price anchoring to the hero** — "From $12" or "All under $14" is the hook that closes the loop between wanting the product and buying it; this is the entire reason I cross-shop e.l.f. over Sephora brands
- 3. **Resolve the sub-brand confusion** — either send from the main e.l.f. cosmetics identity for a makeup launch, or lean fully into e.l.f. SKIN and build the narrative around the dew/skin angle rather than straddling both
- 4. **Add a loyalist hook** — even "available now at Ulta and Target for Beauty Insider members" or an early access callout for known customers would make me feel seen
- 5. **Cut to two sections max** — hero lip launch, one supporting cross-sell, one CTA; the Power Grip Rosé send proved that restraint works for this brand

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next lip obsession: $12 and dewy as hell`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sheer Genius. Sheer Bliss. The Dew Drop. Pick your new daily driver.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Starting at $12 — dewy lips don't have to be expensive.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard table-based HTML email built on what appears to be a Braze + Sailthru hybrid stack (Braze image CDN, Sailthru-style link encoding). The truncated source limits compliance and footer verification, but several rendering and tracking issues are identifiable from the header and first content block.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**ESP stack mismatch — confirm intentional**
Images are served from `braze-images.com` (`https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/...`), but click-tracking links use the Sailthru `~~` encoding pattern on `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/`. This is consistent with an ESP migration where Braze assets were not re-hosted, or a deliberate hybrid setup. Either way, the Braze image URL contains a hardcoded timestamp (`?1698158310` = Oct 2023) — if Braze rotates or expires that asset, the image will 404.

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
All links use opaque Sailthru-encoded redirects (e.g., `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/KblwVySJCN0trwghZbiWdQ~~/...`). Final destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain. This should be verified by decoding or clicking a test send.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo link**
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes the logo link from keyboard tab order. Any keyboard or assistive-tech user cannot reach it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the email tab/window title. Empty `<title>` degrades the experience for VoiceOver/NVDA users opening the email in a browser preview or accessible mail client.

**Focus outline suppressed on links**
```html
<a href="..." style="outline:none">
```
Removes the browser's default keyboard focus indicator on the primary CTA/logo link. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.4.7 (Focus Visible).

**Gmail link color override**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This suppresses Gmail's default link styling globally. Any text links in the email body will be visually indistinguishable from body text — a common source of invisible CTAs in Gmail.

**Google Fonts load correctly**
Fonts are gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`...`<!--<![endif]-->` conditionals, with proper Arial/Sans-serif fallbacks in inline styles. No issue.

**`role="presentation"` applied correctly**
All layout tables carry `role="presentation"`. No issue.

**Mobile breakpoint**
`@media (max-width:660px)` against a 640px container — standard buffer. No issue.
