# Have a Lush life 🏖️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Third Lush email in four days — May 12 was the vibe mood board, May 14 was the fragrance parade, and now it's a full summer catalog drop. I'm conditioned to scroll at this point. The hero is genuinely pretty — that yellow is eye-catching, "ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES" lands for late May, and the product is front-and-center — but the moment I scroll down looking for an offer, I'm hitting a product grid with zero deal signal. Big, Passion, Happy Hippo, The Kiss, Super Milk, "We're Bananas for Bananas"... it's charming Lush weirdness but it's also just a catalog. There's no event hook, no loyalty callout, no deadline.
- As a Sephora Rouge member who is actively running Ulta 21 Days math in my head right now, a product-parade email with a "Shop Now" CTA and no price signal is a browse-and-close. Compare this to the Drunk Elephant "Drunk texting?" email I reviewed recently — that one punchy subject and a single hero product got a click. Lush is doing the opposite: many products, soft copy, no pull.
- I'd scroll it, probably enjoy the "Bananas for Bananas" creative for two seconds, and not click anything. Would not forward.

## What's working

- The yellow hero block is seasonal and arresting — "ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES" in May reads on-time, not forced
- Product grid is well-organized; each tile has a name and visual that holds up even at mobile scale
- "We're Bananas for Bananas" is the kind of weird, specific Lush storytelling that makes me like the brand even when I'm not buying
- "Nail the Look" social-proof section adds a real-person layer that's missing from a lot of competitor sends — e.l.f. doesn't do this, Sally Beauty definitely doesn't
- No render bugs, clean layout top to bottom — compared to some of the broken Ulta mystery-offer renders I've seen, this is technically polished

## What's weak

- Zero offer — I am always doing points math and there is literally nothing here to calculate; no % off, no free shipping, no GWP threshold
- "Shop Now" is the vaguest CTA I've been served all season — Shop what? Which product? Even e.l.f.'s VIP blast ("You're an e.l.f.ing VIP!") at least told me what I'd get
- Third Lush blast in four days — the "vibe" aesthetic from May 12 is echoing straight into "ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES" and I'm starting to tune it out like background music
- No loyalty mention anywhere — Lush has a program and I'm subscribed, yet the email treats me exactly like a stranger landing on their homepage for the first time
- Hero copy is doing vibes-only work; "ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES" could be a Revolve email, a hotel resort promo, or a Vacation sunscreen drop — not distinctly Lush enough

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete offer to the hero.** Even "free standard shipping this weekend" turns a catalog into a reason to act today. Sally Beauty's "$20 Off Is Calling" email I reviewed this week is blunt to a fault but at least I know why it's in my inbox.
- 2. **Pick one product and make it the whole email.** "We're Bananas for Bananas" buried at the bottom should have been the send — one weird hero product, why it works, what it does, a single CTA. Drunk Elephant earns clicks by going deep on one thing, not wide on twelve.
- 3. **Acknowledge me as a returning customer.** Even a "back by popular demand" or "your past picks are getting a summer refresh" line signals they know I exist.
- 4. **Build in urgency.** "Summer stock moves fast" or a specific date window ("available through June 1") gives me a reason not to come back tomorrow and forget.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Big + Super Milk just got a summer upgrade`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We're bananas for this drop — new arrivals inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on orders $30+ this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer haircare lineup starts here — shop before it sells out`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Outlook on Windows) surface the `<title>` in the window bar or tab. Should contain the email subject or a descriptive fallback.

**`text-size-adjust: none` on `<body>`**
```css
-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;text-size-adjust:none
```
Disabling text-size-adjust blocks iOS Mail's automatic font scaling and also prevents users with browser zoom preferences from scaling text. This is an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4). The conventional safe value is `100%`, not `none`.

**Link underline suppression via `#MessageViewBody`**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This removes underlines from all links in Gmail's web client. Undecorated links on non-contrasting backgrounds fail WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). Acceptable only if link color contrast vs. surrounding text is ≥ 3:1 everywhere — cannot confirm without full body source.
