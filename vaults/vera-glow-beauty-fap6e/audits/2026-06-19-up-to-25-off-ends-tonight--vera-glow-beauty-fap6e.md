# Up to 25% off ends tonight!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so here's the thing: there is already a Glossier "Up to 25% off ends tomorrow" email sitting in my inbox from this same morning, June 19. And now there's *this* — "ends tonight!" — also June 19. Same brand, same day, contradictory deadline copy. Morning Glossier told me the sale ended tomorrow. Evening Glossier is telling me it ends tonight. I don't know which one is true, and that's a problem that has nothing to do with design and everything to do with trust.
- That said, I opened it, and the content is actually better than I expected for a last-day re-blast. The hero crystallizes the math in a way earlier sends in this series didn't — "20% off sitewide OR 25% off $100+" is a more useful articulation than the vague "up to 25% off" from the June 17 "before it's too late" send. The fragrance product tiles with actual $87 price points give me something to calculate without clicking. And the subscription lock-in offer at the bottom — "lock in 25% off on your first and future subscription orders" — is genuinely new information for day thirteen of a sale, which I didn't expect.
- I'd click, but I'd also make a mental note that Glossier's urgency signals have officially stopped meaning anything. Not forwarding.

## What's working

- **The 20%/25% split in the hero is actually actionable** — I can do the math on whether my cart clears $100 in five seconds, which is exactly how I decide whether to add one more thing before checkout.
- **Fragrance product tiles show real pricing**: $87 for Glossier You 100ml and $87 for Glossier Deux 100ml is specific enough to comparison-shop without clicking. That's rare in a sale email — most brands make you dig.
- **Black Cherry Balm Dotcom tie-in is seasonal and earns its spot** — "Black Cherries are in season" pairs the discount to a product moment. It doesn't feel like filler. I'd grab this on a grocery run joke level.
- **Subscription lock-in at the bottom is genuinely new**: After twelve prior sends pushing the same discount, surfacing a "subscribe and lock this in forever" offer is a different angle. Smart last-day conversion hook.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout with no render bugs** — logo → hero → fragrance → Balm Dotcom → subscription. Each module is distinct. Glossier's minimalism holds here.

## What's weak

- **"Ends tomorrow" this morning, "ends tonight" this evening — same day**: This is the single most damaging thing in the email and it has nothing to do with the design. If I'm tracking this sale (I am), I already don't believe the deadlines. Charlotte Tilbury pulled the same same-day urgency inflation in her June run and I stopped opening those sends entirely.
- **Every CTA says "Shop now" — every one**: Hero, fragrance, Black Cherries, subscription — four modules, four identical buttons. ILIA's recent "This lip color lasts. This gift won't." at least made me feel like different sections had different purposes. "Shop now" four times in a row reads like a template that never got personalized.
- **Thirteen emails in and I'm still a stranger**: No name, no "your favorites are on sale," no category signal that reflects anything about my purchase history. Sephora surfaces my category preferences by day two of an event. After this many touches, Glossier's lack of personalization starts to feel like a choice I resent.
- **Subscription "very, very limited time" is vague on top of vague**: Stacking a vague deadline on an already-suspect deadline makes both feel cheaper. If you want me to subscribe before midnight, say midnight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one June 19 send and make it count** — the "ends tomorrow" / "ends tonight" contradiction is a credibility hole that no amount of good creative can fix. Either commit to one final email or explicitly own the correction: "We meant tonight — here's your real last chance." That honesty would actually stand out.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs by module**: Hero stays "Shop Now." Fragrance module becomes "Shop Fragrance" or "Grab the Duo." Black Cherry section becomes "Shop Balm Dotcom." Subscription close becomes "Subscribe & Lock It In." Takes a single QA pass to fix; makes the email scannable for people who already know what they want.
- 3. **Put a hard time on the subscription offer** — "Available for a very, very limited time" → "Lock in 25% off through June 25 on all future subscription orders." Specificity converts. Vagueness reads as padding.
- 4. **Add one personalization hook at the hero** — even "Your top picks are still on sale" or a category-flavored subject variant. After thirteen sends, I want Glossier to act like they know me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: 25% off ends at midnight tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One last time — 20% off everything, no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your cart won't be this discounted again. Lock it in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: subscribe before midnight and lock in 25% on future orders too.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo (klclick.com tracking domain), using XHTML 1.0 Transitional with standard MSO conditionals. One invalid HTML construct is present in the open-tracking mechanism; the remainder of the infrastructure appears standard but the truncated source prevents full footer/compliance verification.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid open-tracking element**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVFW84NYK85QQKMJ4V8THYPV_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This is Klaviyo's open-pixel injected as a link inside a span — syntactically malformed. Strict-parsing clients (some corporate gateways, certain Outlook versions) may strip or log it as suspicious. Should be an `<img>` pixel (`width="1" height="1"`) or the ESP's standard tracking tag.

**Unbranded click-tracking domain**
All links route through `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo's default shared domain) rather than a branded subdomain like `click.glossier.com`. Shared tracking domains score lower on reputation lookups and expose Klaviyo ESP fingerprint to filters. Klaviyo supports custom tracking domains — this should be configured.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`format-detection` meta incomplete**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection">
```
Only suppresses phone auto-linking. iOS/macOS will still auto-link dates, addresses, and emails. Full suppression: `content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"`.

**Hover-state CSS coverage**
The stylesheet contains 12+ `.bd:hover` / `.rollover:hover` rules. These render only in Apple Mail, Samsung Mail, and Outlook.com web — all other major clients (Gmail app, iOS Mail < 16, Android Gmail) silently ignore them. No functional issue, but these rules add ~1 KB with limited reach.
