# Sleep your way...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so "Sleep your way..." drops into my inbox on July 2nd and I'll admit — I opened it. That subject is doing a little wink-wink work that's very on-brand for DE, and I've been conditioned to open them at this point. But let me be real about where I'm standing right now: "The It Kits" on June 30, "20% off ends soon!" on June 29, "Reveal your best skin tonight!" on June 26, "The ultimate hack?" on June 24 — and now this. That is close to daily cadence from a single prestige skincare brand and my patience is thinning.
- The email itself is clean and classically DE — white space, soft palette, four-step nighttime routine laid out like a skincare class. I know all four products already (Hello Marula, B-Hydra, Bora Barrier, P-Balm), so this isn't discovery for me. The offer is free shipping with no minimum, and then there's a "35% LESS IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING" section I read three times before I understood it's a product formulation claim, not a sale. That is an expensive moment of confusion.
- Would I click? No, not today. I did my points math last week at the "20% off ends soon!" send and passed. Free shipping with no discount on July 2nd — with zero July 4th energy — when ILIA just ran "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now" and Sephora's Stacking Weekend had me pre-loading my cart? This is a mark-as-read, full stop.

## What's working

- The subject "Sleep your way..." earns the open — the double entendre is classic DE voice and it cut through a crowded July 2nd inbox
- The four-step routine is genuinely useful structure; each step names the product, the benefit, and has its own "SHOP" link, so I'm not dumped on a homepage
- Free shipping with no minimum in the header banner is the right hierarchy decision — lead with the perk, not bury it in the footer
- Render is clean — no broken images, no layout blowout, the white space feels intentional and the hierarchy reads well on a quick scroll

## What's weak

- "35% LESS IS ALWAYS A GOOD THING" is the biggest miss in this email — my eye reads that as a sale number before my brain catches up, and realizing it's a product claim (less of... something? Fragrance? Preservatives?) is a genuine bait-and-switch on the hierarchy
- Zero July 4th or holiday weekend angle on July 2nd — when e.l.f. is bundling lip combos at $20 and ILIA is running a timed Buy-3 offer, DE sent me evergreen content that could have gone out any month of the year
- The hero CTA is "SHOP NOW" — after four steps of specific, named product copy, the primary button just goes... somewhere general; that's a wasted moment
- Cadence is at the breaking point — five sends in eight days with no meaningful escalation in offer is making DE feel spammy, and this email does nothing to justify occupying my inbox again this week

## Recommendations

- 1. **Decode the "35% LESS" immediately** — if this is a claim (35% less preservatives, less fragrance, less aluminum, whatever), lead with the noun: "35% Less Fragrance. 100% the results." Mystery is not building intrigue here, it's building confusion and I will scroll past it.
- 2. **Tie it to the weekend** — it's July 2nd. "Wake up glowing for the long weekend" or even a soft "treat yourself before the 4th" framing gives this a reason to exist right now instead of feeling like a queued evergreen send.
- 3. **Upgrade the hero CTA** — "SHOP THE NIGHTTIME ROUTINE" tells me where I'm landing and what I'm getting; "SHOP NOW" tells me nothing. e.l.f.'s "Leveled-up lip combos for only $20" earned a 9/10 in my inbox this week partly because every CTA told me exactly what I was clicking into.
- 4. **Consider skipping this send** — if there's no new offer and no new launch, the goodwill loss from a fifth consecutive DE email outweighs whatever incremental revenue this drives. Sally Beauty ran 27 emails and earned them; DE is burning through trust fast.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue A — HTML comment syntax inside CSS `<style>` block (invalid):**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` inside a `<style>` block is not valid CSS. These are dead rules — the intended responsive widths for `.contenttable` and `.mobile` will never apply. The `@media (max-width: 600px)` block silently loses two layout rules in every client.

**Issue B — Triple font loading for BrownBold, BrownLight, BrownThin:**

Each of these three variants is loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` also inside `<style>`

This fires 9 redundant HTTP requests (3 fonts × 3 load paths). Strip the `<link>` tags and `@import` lines — the `@font-face` declarations alone are sufficient and are already present.

**Issue C — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Suppresses pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue D — `*{line-height: 100%}` universal rule:**

Applied to every element globally. Outlook interprets unitless percentage line-height as exact, collapsing multi-line text. Scope this rule to `td` or remove it entirely and set line-height per element.

**Issue E — Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**

BrownBold/Light/Thin are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a public font redistribution site with unclear licensing. BrownRegular is self-hosted on `drunkelephant.com`. The inconsistency creates a single-point-of-failure dependency on an uncontrolled CDN. Self-host all four variants from `drunkelephant.com` to match the BrownRegular pattern.
