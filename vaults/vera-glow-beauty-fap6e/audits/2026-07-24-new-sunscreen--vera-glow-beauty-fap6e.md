# NEW SUNSCREEN 💦 ☀️ 🏖️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I'll give Sephora this: "NEW SUNSCREEN" in the subject is doing exactly what it says, and landing a sunscreen-focused email in my inbox on a Thursday in late July is genuinely on-calendar for me. I'm actively hunting for SPF before the end of summer, and Supergoop is one of the brands I watch. So the premise is right.
- But then I open it and my eyes have to fight through three different emails crammed into one: a "Get a Mini" fragrance GWP banner at the very top (wait, I thought this was about sunscreen?), then the sunscreen hero, then an Auto-Replenish Hot Deal set, and then — I'm not making this up — a "For Dry Hair" module at the bottom. Sephora literally emailed me *twice* about hair care two days ago. And now it's tucked into my sunscreen email like a little stowaway. The template modularity is showing its seams.
- I would click into the Clinique Whip spotlight because it's a specific product with a price and an SPF number visible — that's exactly what I need to decide if it's worth trying. But I'm not forwarding this to anyone. This is a "mark read and go find the product myself" situation.

## What's working

- **Clinique Whip SPF 50 at $54 is given a proper hero moment** — product name, SPF number, and price visible at a glance. That's how you move SPF product to a beauty shopper.
- **Supergoop! appearing in the product grid** signals that Sephora actually curated this for people who care about sunscreen, not just people who own skin. Supergoop is a brand I track; seeing it here validates the send.
- **Star ratings are visible in the product grid** — social proof right in the email body, no click required to see it.
- **"AUTO-REPLENISH HOT DEAL" with "LIMITED TIME ONLY"** creates legitimate urgency and an actual discount hook — it's not just "shop our new arrivals," it's a reason to act now.
- **Orange/warm-toned hero** reads summer visually without leaning on the tired beach-and-bikini cliché.

## What's weak

- **"Get a Mini" fragrance GWP banner sits above the sunscreen hero** and immediately fractures the message. I clicked to see sunscreen news and the first thing I see is a totally different offer. This is a template slot that should've been suppressed for this send.
- **"For Dry Hair" module at the bottom** is the clearest symptom of Sephora's module-recycling problem — they just sent me two separate hair care emails on July 22nd. Re-skinning those modules into a sunscreen email two days later is lazy and slightly insulting.
- **"SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" is the hero headline, not the sunscreen claim** — it's Sephora going broad when the subject line promised me specific. Sally Beauty's It's a 10 email last week led with a category-specific headline ("Repair your summer hair") and it worked better for exactly this reason.
- **ALL CAPS "NEW SUNSCREEN" in the subject** nudges toward spam-folder energy; I know Sephora is Sephora but the format is doing them no favors with inbox filters.
- **Cadence tax is real** — two duplicate "summer 😎" emails yesterday, two hair care emails the day before. Sephora has been crying wolf all week and I'm conditioned to deprioritize their sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "Get a Mini" fragrance banner for this send.** If the subject is "NEW SUNSCREEN," the first pixel I see after opening should be sunscreen. A mismatched top offer cannibalizes attention from the actual story.
- 2. **Promote the sunscreen into the hero headline.** "Something New Is Here" is filler. Lead with "New SPF Drops Worth the Hype" or "Your Summer SPF Lineup, Updated" — something that connects directly back to why I opened.
- 3. **Drop the "For Dry Hair" module entirely.** You just sent hair care emails. This is your sunscreen send. Respect the segmentation.
- 4. **Make the Auto-Replenish deal name a product.** "AUTO-REPLENISH HOT DEAL" is intriguing but I don't know what I'm replenishing. Naming the hero product in that section (e.g., "Hot Deal: Supergoop! Unseen Sunscreen Trio") would convert faster.
- 5. **Seed a scarcity signal into the product grid** — even "3 left" or "low stock" on one hero SKU would push me toward the tap.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New SPF drops: Clinique, Supergoop + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer sunscreen lineup just got an upgrade`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop new SPF arrivals + a limited Auto-Replenish deal before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clinique Whip SPF 50 is here. So is a hot deal you'll want to grab fast.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on text. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed; email clients that need zoom restriction handle it natively.

**Broad `text-size-adjust: none` declaration (confirmed)**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied across virtually every element. Suppresses OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Acceptable to apply to `td` for Outlook rendering control, but applying to `body` and `a` breaks accessibility in mobile webmail.

**Duplicate `.show` and `.appear` rule blocks**
Both `.show` and `.appear` each contain duplicate `display: block!important; display: table!important;` declarations. Harmless but indicative of template bloat; the second declaration in each pair is a no-op.

### 5. Compliance

**`robots` meta value has an invalid space (confirmed)**
```html

```
The correct directive is `noindex` (no space). `"no index"` is not a recognized token in the Robots meta spec; some crawlers will ignore it and may index the email's web version. Should be `content="noindex, nofollow"`.
