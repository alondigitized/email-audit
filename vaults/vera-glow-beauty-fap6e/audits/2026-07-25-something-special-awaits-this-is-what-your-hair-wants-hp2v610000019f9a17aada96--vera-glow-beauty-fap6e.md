# Something special awaits...This is what your hair wants.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I need everyone to sit down for this one: Sephora sent me this exact creative — pink MUFE lip sale banner, "New & Clean" hero, DAE Dreamriser mousse, the whole Trending Picks grid — earlier today under a completely different subject line. I already reviewed "❤️ We're in love with NEW dae Dreamriser Volumizing Hair Mousse❤️" a few hours ago and gave it an 8. This is the same body, same products, same "National Lip Day, today only" banner, just wearing "Something special awaits...This is what your hair wants." as a new hat. That's not a drip sequence, that's a subject-line A/B test landing in my actual inbox as two separate emails, and I notice.
- Setting the déjà vu aside, the creative itself is honestly fine — clean product photography, the before/after hair shot is a nice trust cue, and the Trending Picks grid is stacked with stuff I'd actually consider (Kosas setting powder, Summer Fridays SPF, that Glow Recipe lip/cream duo at a real discount off $44). ILIA's Limitless Lash mascara even shows up as a tile here, which is funny given I scored ILIA's own email a 4 last week for looking like it forgot it had a design budget — Sephora dresses ILIA's product up better than ILIA does.
- Would I click? Probably, on the DAE mousse or the Glow Recipe duo, not the lip sale the subject didn't even mention. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on, same as the last one — and I'm now mildly annoyed Sephora is testing two subject lines on me in one day when e.l.f. at least varies its actual content across eight emails a week.

## What's working

- The before/after hair shot is a legit trust cue — I can see the volume difference, not just told about it.
- Glow Recipe duo at $30 for a $44 value and the Josie Maran set at $27 for $31 are real, visible discounts, not vague "save big" language.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" and "CLEAN" badges are doing real work for me — that's the exact curation signal I'm scanning for versus Ulta's 21 Days mass-market grab bag.
- Rouge tier is visibly highlighted in the INSIDER/VIB/ROUGE bar at the bottom, so at least someone remembered which tier I'm in.

## What's weak

- I got this exact email, word for word, product for product, under a different subject line today already. That's the same problem I flagged with Sephora's back-to-back "NEW SUNSCREEN 💦☀️🏖️" sends on 7/24 — reused creative isn't a new email.
- The subject promises hair, the actual hero banner is a 50% off Make Up For Ever lip sale — total mismatch between what got me to open and what I actually see first.
- "You have 0 points" at the very top is jarring for a Rouge member — I don't do that much points math to end up at zero, and seeing it undercuts the personalization I'd expect at this tier.
- No reviews or star ratings anywhere on the product tiles, which matters more to me than the badge system when I'm deciding on a $92 Lore fragrance sight unseen.
- This is a grab-bag of eight-plus unrelated products crammed under one subject line — Drunk Elephant's "Feeling sticky?" send stayed on one clear seasonal idea; this one doesn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the duplicate send. If this is an A/B test, pick a winner and send once — I shouldn't be able to catch you testing subject lines on my own inbox in the same afternoon.
- 2. Make the subject match the hero. Either lead the banner with the hair story the subject promises, or write a subject about the lip sale that's actually up top.
- 3. Fix the "0 points" display for Rouge members — either show real point balances or drop the line entirely; a wrong number is worse than no number.
- 4. Add a review count or star rating to at least the hero products — I'm not spending $92 on a fragrance with zero social proof.
- 5. Trim the product grid. Eight-plus SKUs across three unrelated categories dilutes the "New & Clean" hook you led with.
