# Introducing: 24 Hours with Glossier
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails in twelve days, and Glossier just pivoted from product to content. The I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom launch was a moment — I gave it an 8 on July 18th — but today's send is a full gear shift into vlog territory, and honestly, it catches me off guard in a way that doesn't feel intentional. "Introducing 24 Hours with Glossier" is a branded documentary/creator series featuring four NYC-based people. There's no offer. There's no product spotlight. The "Take it for a spin" promo sitting in the header feels stranded — I can barely read the copy, and it's competing with the main event below it.
- I appreciate what Glossier is doing here conceptually. The NYC campaign has had real momentum, and anchoring it with real people living real days in the city is the kind of brand storytelling that usually works for me. But right now, after seven emails priming me on a product launch, I'm in buying mode — not watching mode. Drunk Elephant would have wrapped their founder's "hot takes" content with a product pull. ILIA managed to make a creator moment feel shoppable in their last send. Glossier just... handed me four "Watch the vlog" CTAs and called it an email.
- I'd mark this as read. I might come back to the vlog on my own terms — but not because of this email.

## What's working

- **The NYC continuity is real.** After nine days of I ❤️ NY buildup, this series feels like a natural creative extension, not a random drop. The brand earned the right to tell NYC stories.
- **The people look like me.** Four young women, diverse, stylish, clearly Millennial — I see myself reflected in the casting in a way Sephora's mass sends rarely manage.
- **"Watch the vlog" CTAs are clean and consistent.** Each featured creator gets the same CTA, no guesswork about what clicking does.
- **No render bugs.** The layout reads clearly — grid is clean, photos load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **No product, no offer, no reason to click right now.** After seven product-focused emails, dropping a pure-content send with zero shoppable hooks feels like a missed bridge. Even a "shop what they're wearing" tie-in would fix this.
- **The header "Take it for a spin" promo is confusing noise.** The copy is too small to read on mobile, "Bonus: $0" makes no sense at a glance, and it competes directly with the main editorial below it. It reads like two campaigns collided.
- **Four creators at equal visual weight creates no hierarchy.** There's no "anchor" — I don't know who to care about most, and the bios are too short to give me a reason to care about any of them.
- **Zero urgency or loyalty hooks.** As a Rouge and Diamond member I'm always doing points math — if I'm watching a vlog, what do I get? Nothing here.
- **Cadence fatigue is setting in.** This is email eight in twelve days. Even with the NYC campaign giving it context, I'm starting to feel it. The July 15th "NEW!" send already felt redundant — this pivot needed to land harder to earn the slot.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a shoppable layer to every creator.** Even one sentence — "Aamarni's go-to: Balm Dotcom in Rose" with a direct product link — turns a content email into a revenue email. e.l.f. does this instinctively on every influencer send; Glossier left money on the table here.
- 2. **Remove or redesign the header promo.** The "Take it for a spin" block is illegible and undermines the editorial feel. If it's a meaningful offer, give it its own send. If it's evergreen, move it to footer.
- 3. **Pick a hero creator.** Lead with one person's story above the fold, let the others appear after a scroll. Drunk Elephant's "Hot takes" worked because it committed to one voice — four equal blocks dilutes that.
- 4. **Add a loyalty tie-in.** Even "earn points watching" or "Rouge members get early access to the full series" would make me feel seen.
- 5. **Tighten the cadence.** The next Glossier email should be a breathing beat — either a meaningful offer or silence. The NYC campaign peaked at the July 18th "Savor the Big Apple" send.
