# Pre-order your Advent calendar
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Email thirty-one. Five days ago it was "Boo! Halloween is here!" — and now Lush is landing in my inbox on July 21st asking me to pre-order an Advent calendar. In a single week they've fast-forwarded through two separate holiday seasons. At this pace I'm getting Valentine's Day by August.
- Here's the thing though: the calendar looks genuinely beautiful. The colorful, stacked-box imagery is the most visually satisfying thing Lush has sent me in weeks, and "Ready, Set, Advent!" is a pun I actually respect. The copy even acknowledges the seasonal absurdity — "we know it's summer" — which earns them partial credit for self-awareness. That's more than the Halloween email did.
- But I will not be clicking Pre-Order Now today. There is no price anywhere in this email. No urgency date. No "pre-orders close August X." I can't do the points math — which, for me, is the entire decision — because they didn't give me the numbers. Mark as read. Maybe revisit in October.

## What's working

- **"Ready, Set, Advent!" lands.** The wordplay is clean, the headline earns the visual, and it telegraphs holiday hype without being cheesy.
- **The calendar imagery is genuinely fun** — colorful, stackable, playful packaging that reads as premium-but-accessible. The most arresting hero creative from Lush since the "Spotlight on: Fresh" email (July 13).
- **The self-aware copy disarms the timing objection.** "We know it's summer" is doing a lot of heavy lifting and it works.
- **Lush Club is visible.** Points callout in the lower section is appreciated — at least they're tying the calendar into the loyalty ecosystem.
- **"Pre-Order Now" is an unambiguous CTA** — verb, noun, action. Clean button. Easy tap target.

## What's weak

- **No price anywhere.** Pre-ordering a beauty advent calendar is a $50–$100+ commitment. Asking me to click through before I've seen any dollar figure is a friction wall, not a hook.
- **No deadline makes "pre-order" feel fake.** Ulta and Sephora both know that "limited time" only works when there's an actual time attached. This pre-order has no closing date, no scarcity signal, nothing to act on.
- **Back-to-back out-of-season holiday sends is a pattern now.** The Halloween email (July 16) already felt off. Running Advent five days later confirms this is a content strategy, not a thoughtful editorial calendar. Two holiday jumps in a week cheapens both.
- **The Lush Club callout doesn't connect to the calendar.** "Scoop up points on eligible purchases" is vague — does the advent calendar earn points? Is there member early access? That's the information that would actually move me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me the price in the hero.** Even "From $XX" or "Valued at $XX" gives me the math. No price = no pre-order from me.
- 2. **Give the pre-order a real deadline.** "Pre-order closes August 31" or "Only 2,000 available" transforms this from "interesting" to "act now."
- 3. **Space out the holiday calendar sends.** Running Halloween July 16 and Advent July 21 back-to-back makes both feel like content-calendar filler. Glossier ran a coherent New York theme for a week straight — Lush is skipping entire months in five days.
- 4. **Add a Lush Club exclusive hook.** "Members get access 48 hours early" or "Earn triple points on pre-orders" would make this email worth keeping open. Sephora Rouge would absolutely get that treatment from Sephora on a launch this big.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Advent calendar is here. Pre-orders open now.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25 days of Lush. Pre-order yours today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive collabs, bath bombs & surprises — only X available. Closes Aug 31.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lush Club members: earn points on pre-orders. Ships December.`
