# NEW! Main Stain Lip Marker 👄🖊️ Only $5!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is email fourteen from e.l.f. since May 15th — I have been keeping count since the Survivor collab opened the floodgates — and my inbox reflex at this point is fatigue before curiosity. But I'll give this one its due: a new product format is genuinely different from the flash-sale loop that's been running for two weeks. A lip marker — pen-tip application, stain formula — at five dollars is actual news, not another "6 HOURS ONLY" hero slapped on the same Halo Glow bottle. The "stain power" positioning and the eight-shade grid with real names (Maraschino, Chai Me, Rose Macaroon) read like a proper launch brief. This is the most legitimately useful content e.l.f. has dropped on me since the Jelly Pop Glow Stick offer on Sunday.
- Here's my problem: the Target-exclusive reveal lands mid-email, not in the hero. As someone who runs points math before I add anything to cart, "you can only buy this at Target" is information I needed in the first five seconds. I do shop Target, but I'm earning RedCard 5% or Target Circle cash there — I am not stacking Sephora Rouge rewards or Ulta Diamond perks on a five-dollar lip marker I can't route through either program. The channel is the value proposition for someone like me, and this email treats it like a footnote.
- Would I click? Probably yes — five dollars for a new format pulls even through fourteen-email fatigue. Would I forward? No. My Sephora and Ulta-loyal friends will hit the same math wall I did.

## What's working

- The $5 price is in the hero, not buried in body copy — I know within one scroll whether this is impulse territory, and it is.
- Eight shades with real names (Maraschino, Bawse, Ballgown, Chai Me, Rose Macaroon, Delighted plus two more) give me an actual color story at a glance instead of forcing me to click to discover "Pink 03."
- The pen-nib product imagery is clean and functional — I understand the application format immediately without reading a line of copy.
- "Stain power" framing differentiates this from e.l.f.'s standard lipstick and gloss launches — it signals a formula story worth investigating.
- The model and styling read young and female-coded without overcorrecting into Gen Z territory — this actually hits my age bracket.

## What's weak

- The Target-exclusive reveal is buried mid-scroll instead of anchored in the hero — for a points-math shopper this is the single most important piece of context in the email and it's treated like supporting copy.
- No Beauty Squad or Target Circle points callout anywhere — e.l.f. is emailing me directly and still not giving me a reason to buy here versus waiting to find it at Ulta.
- Zero launch window or availability framing — is this live today? Limited run? Stores only? The absence of any timeline makes this feel like an FYI rather than a launch.
- The "Top Picks for You" section at the bottom uses category headers (HAZE, EYES, SKIN, SKINCARE) that feel auto-generated, not curated — it reads like a recommendation engine that's never looked at my purchase history.
- Coming off Sunday's free Jelly Pop Glow Stick, "here's something to buy for $5" is a step backward in incentive sequencing. ILIA's mid-May "Layers of free gifts" campaign understood how to arc an offer over multiple sends — this e.l.f. run has no arc.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the Target exclusive into the hero.** "NEW at Target: $5 Lip Marker" gives me the channel and the price in one line. Right now I'm building emotional investment before I learn I can't route this through my preferred loyalty programs.
- 2. **Add a points/loyalty angle.** "Stack Target Circle cash" or "Earn Beauty Squad points at elfcosmetics.com" turns the buy-channel ambiguity into an actual offer for a loyalist like me.
- 3. **Establish a launch window.** "Available today" or "launch week only" is honest urgency — infinitely more credible than another countdown clock, and this product legitimately has a launch moment worth naming.
- 4. **Cut the generic "Top Picks" recs section.** Sephora's new-launch emails run clean and single-column — one product story without a recommendation grid fighting for attention at the bottom. This email earns more trust with focus.
- 5. **Pull back on cadence or segment harder.** Glossier ran a tight three-email Memorial Day arc and felt purposeful. Sephora's Stacking Weekend had a reason for every send — day-of messaging with clear countdown logic. Fourteen e.l.f. emails in twelve days with no visible structure is why this five-dollar product has to fight for attention it would have gotten free in week one.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients (Gmail web, Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` as a document label. Empty title degrades accessibility.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo anchor removes it from keyboard navigation**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Combined with `outline:none`, the link is fully inaccessible to keyboard users.

**`<style>` block injected inside email body (block-3 div)**
```html
<div style="..."><style type="text/css">
  .responsive-text { font-size: 16px !important; ... }
  @media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { ... }
</style>
```
Body-embedded `<style>` tags are stripped by Gmail (web), older Yahoo Mail, and Outlook 2016+. The `.responsive-text` mobile overrides will silently fail in those clients, leaving only the inline `font-size:12px` fallback.

**Redundant nested `nl-container` inside `html_block`**

`block-1` (an `html_block`) contains a complete second `<table class="nl-container">` with its own `row-1 > row-content > column-1` hierarchy. This is a copy-paste artifact producing unnecessary DOM depth and can trigger max-nesting limits in Outlook's rendering engine.

**Declared font not loaded**

The nav/banner element uses:
```css
font-family: 'Futura Std', Futura, 'Jost', Verdana, sans-serif;
```
Only Open Sans is loaded via Google Fonts. `Jost` is a Google Font but has no corresponding `<link>` tag. Futura is not a web font. Effective fallback will be Verdana in virtually all clients — which may not match design intent.
