# Summer hair starts with a freebie
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-two. I've now received thirty-two Sally Beauty emails — and after last Tuesday's "Last day for 20% off" urgency cascade, I half-expected another percentage-off hammer this weekend. Instead, Sally switched the frame: "freebie." That word actually made me pause in my inbox, which is more than I can say for the last three subject lines collectively. The BOGO offer in the hero — buy two, get one free on select hair care — is genuinely useful if you're an ion loyalist like me, and "Ends July 29" gives two full weeks to plan a real stock-up run instead of sprinting to checkout before midnight.
- But here's where Sally loses me: after this volume and cadence, the novelty of any single send evaporates fast. The product grid is doing too much — ion color next to L'Oreal Technique next to a literal hot glue gun. That is not curation, that is a warehouse dump. And without any loyalty context — I have no idea how my Beauty Club points stack against a BOGO — I'm trying to do math with missing variables. Sephora's "Obsessed? Same." from earlier this week at least told me exactly who the email was for. This one feels like a blast to the full list.
- I'd click if I need to restock hair color this week. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **"Freebie" breaks the pattern.** After three consecutive summer/sale subjects, that word is a minor but real interrupt — different enough from "must-haves" and "essentials" to earn a second look.
- **BOGO is front and center.** "Buy two, get one free" is immediately legible in the hero with the qualifier right there in the same line — no Ulta-style fine-print-only disclosure on the limitation.
- **The deadline breathes.** "Ends July 29" is two weeks away, which is infinitely less anxiety-inducing than the "ends tonight" loop Sally was running earlier this month. I can actually plan.
- **Service badges are functional.** Free shipping, same-day delivery, and BOPIS callouts near the bottom represent real, tangible options — not just marketing filler.
- **ion is a real product.** Seeing Color Brilliance and the 14V Extreme High Lift Cool Blonde in the grid confirms these are pro-grade SKUs I'd actually consider, not generic drugstore placeholders.

## What's weak

- **Hot glue gun in a summer hair email.** The "Beauty Secrets Drip & Clog Proof Hot Glue" is sitting in this product grid like a craft supply renegade. It completely breaks the editorial coherence of what should be a tightly themed hair moment.
- **No loyalty math anywhere.** I'm in the Beauty Club but there's zero mention of how points interact with BOGO. Sephora always shows me where the double-stack lives. Sally just says "shop."
- **"Shop now" four times, nothing specific.** Every product tile has the same generic CTA. e.l.f.'s recent "Get on the cream team for only $14" gave me a price anchor and a product reason to click. "Shop now" tells me nothing about where I'm landing.
- **Two promo strips compete with the hero.** The "BEAUTY IN A DASH" and "FREE 1-HOUR DELIVERY" banners above the hero fragment my attention before I even reach the actual offer. One strip does the job; two is a tax on the visual hierarchy.
- **Preview text is likely leaking header noise.** Without a visible dedicated preheader, inbox clients would pull from those promo bar strips — not exactly a compelling first sentence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the hot glue and curate the grid.** Every product in this send should be hair. If ion 14V is in the BOGO range, lead with it — specific shades, specific reasons to pick that product over the others. That's the click.
- 2. **Add one loyalty sentence.** "Beauty Club members: your points count." That line turns a generic BOGO into a deal built for me. Drunk Elephant's "Time to come clean" earned a 9/10 partly because the brand voice made me feel like a known customer. Sally hasn't done that in 32 sends.
- 3. **Collapse to one promo banner.** Choose free delivery OR in-store pickup — not both stacked above the hero. The hierarchy needs one clear path in, not three.
- 4. **Give "Shop now" a noun.** "Shop BOGO Hair Color" or "See Qualifying Products" — verb + noun, minimum. "Shop now" is what a homepage button says. An email can do better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy 2 get 1 free — including ion color`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your BOGO starts now (ends July 29)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `ion, L'Oreal Technique, and more — pick three, pay for two.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up on summer hair color before July 29.`
