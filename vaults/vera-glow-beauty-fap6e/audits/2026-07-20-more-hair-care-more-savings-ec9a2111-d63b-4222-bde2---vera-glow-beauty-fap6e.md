# More hair care, more savings
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Email thirty-six from Sally, and for the first time in what feels like months they led with an actual offer structure instead of vibes. "Buy two, get one free on select hair care" — that's a real deal, and the hero makes it legible immediately: the model, the copy, the CTA, all above the fold. I clocked the July 25 deadline before I got three seconds in. That's doing a lot of heavy lifting for a brand that gave me "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" *and* "Hair care for pool days & heat waves" in the same week.
- The problem is the subject line torpedoed the open. "More hair care, more savings" is the kind of copy you write when you have nothing to say. They have a BOGO! That's news. That belongs in the subject. Instead I got a subject so generic it could have been sent by any professional beauty supply chain in the country — and probably was.
- I'm marking this read, not clicking immediately — but I'm bookmarking the July 25 deadline and coming back before it expires if I need ion Color Brilliance. I wouldn't forward it; the deal is real but the packaging is forgettable.

## What's working

- **BOGO hero is unambiguous.** "Buy two, get one free" is in big, readable type — no asterisk-hunting required at first glance, and "Ends July 25" is right there in the sub-copy. This is the clearest offer structure Sally has run in their recent summer streak.
- **Model demographic lands.** She's young, dark hair, red lip — looks like she's shopping this palette, not selling it to someone else's customer. More on-persona than a lot of Sally's recent creative.
- **Product grid is actually useful.** ion Color Brilliance, L'Oreal Technique HiColor, ion Extreme High Lift Cool Blonde — these are real professional-grade picks, not filler. A savvy buyer will recognize the SKUs.
- **Klarna callout is smart here.** A BOGO stack can bump cart value fast; installment payment anchors that upside and reduces checkout friction on a bigger purchase.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, images loaded, nothing broken — after Ulta's expired-event email debacle last week, this is a low bar but Sally clears it.

## What's weak

- **Subject completely buried the offer.** "More hair care, more savings" is recycled filler. The BOGO is genuinely the most compelling thing Sally has sent in weeks — and the subject never mentions it. Drunk Elephant managed to carry narrative momentum through "Golden hour every hour" without even naming a product; Sally is sitting on a concrete deal and hiding it.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Thirty-six emails deep into summer hair content — the brand has trained me to skim past Sally in my inbox. The subject doing no new work makes that worse.
- **No loyalty or PRO account callout.** If this applies to Sally PRO members or there's a points multiplier on the BOGO, I can't see it anywhere. Sephora always surfaces Insider tier benefits in the hero; Sally treats me like a cold prospect every single email.
- **"Select hair care" does the vague-exclusions thing.** The hero says BOGO but doesn't hint at what's in or out. That forces me to click just to discover whether my actual cart is eligible — which is friction disguised as curiosity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the BOGO** — this is the single highest-leverage change. The offer is genuinely good; the subject is wasting it.
- 2. **Fix the preheader** — whatever is leaking into the preview slot (probably the "Beauty in a Dash / buy online" utility bar) needs to be replaced with offer copy.
- 3. **Add a Sally PRO or Beauty Club callout** in the hero or immediately below it — even one sentence like "PRO members, this stacks with your account price."
- 4. **Tighten the product grid** — swap one of the generic hair color cards for something with a quick "bestseller" or "most repurchased" badge. ILIA and e.l.f. are doing social proof in-email; Sally is leaving that on the table.
- 5. **Name the exclusions** — "does not apply to [X]" in the sub-hero copy reduces post-click disappointment and builds trust. Glossier does this cleanly.

- **Subject Alt B:** `BOGO on hair care: shop before the 25th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `ion, L'Oréal, Beauty Secrets and more — mix and match to save`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on $35+ orders. Stock up before July 25.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`white-space: pre` on `img` elements — medium risk**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and has no useful effect, but can produce unexpected whitespace or layout collapse in some WebKit-based clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail). Should be removed.

**`Founders Grotesk` declared but never loaded — low risk**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
`Founders Grotesk` is set as the primary typeface for all links but has no `@font-face` declaration and no external stylesheet link in the provided `<head>`. Every email client will fall back to Helvetica. If intentional (web-safe fallback), the declaration is misleading. If the font should load, the `@font-face` block is missing.

**Google Fonts pinned to stale version v17 — low risk**
All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Open Sans is currently at v40+. Google has historically kept old versioned URLs alive, but hardcoded version paths are a long-term fragility. More critically: virtually no email client executes external `@font-face` URLs (Gmail, Outlook strip them); the fallback stack (`Helvetica, sans-serif`) will render in the majority of clients regardless.

**`font-display: swap` — informational**
Declared in all `@font-face` blocks. `font-display` is a CSS4 property ignored by all major email clients. No functional impact, but adds dead bytes.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px` — low risk**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```
Standard email practice uses `600px` or `480px`. Devices with viewport widths of 500–599px (common on mid-size Android phones and small tablets) will receive the full desktop layout with no responsive overrides applied.
