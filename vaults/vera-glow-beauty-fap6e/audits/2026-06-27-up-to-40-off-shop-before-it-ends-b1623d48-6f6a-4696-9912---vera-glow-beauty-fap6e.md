# Up to 40% Off: Shop Before It Ends
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. I'm calling this email twenty-five because Sally sent twenty-four yesterday — that was the "Going... Going... Gone!" send that already had me checked out — and here we are again with the same "up to 40% off" discount in a new costume. The 4th of July creative at least gives this send a reason to exist in late June, and I'll give them that: the balloon-and-curling-wand hero is cheerful and the timing is defensible.
- What actually surprised me is the sub-tile structure. For the first time in this stretch, Sally broke the headline into something useful: 40% off styling tools, 20% off fragrances. That's more specificity than emails #22, #23, and #24 combined, and the styling tools tier is where Sally genuinely beats Sephora and Ulta — pro-grade hot tools at professional supply pricing is their lane. I felt a flicker.
- But the flicker dies fast. No end date. No loyalty hook. "Shop now" on every button. I'd click the tools tile out of habit and muscle memory, not because this email earned it. I'm not forwarding this — my group chat would revolt.

## What's working

- The 4th of July creative actually earns its place — patriotic colors, balloons, a styled model — the visual fits the moment and doesn't feel lazy.
- Model is a young Black woman working a curling wand, which is inclusive, age-appropriate, and directly tied to the product category — better casting coherence than most Sally sends.
- Sub-offer tiles break "up to 40%" into real specifics: 40% on tools, 20% on fragrances — the first time this sale cadence has told me what's actually discounted.
- Free 2-hour delivery on $35+ is a legitimate convenience edge over Sephora (no same-day standard) and Ulta's DoorDash-only same-day.
- The benefits icon row (In-Store, Online, Free Shipping, 2-Hour Delivery, BOPIS) is clean and readable — good for a skimmer.

## What's weak

- "Shop Before It Ends" with zero end date is meaningless at this point — Glossier pulled the same disappearing-deadline trick in June and I called it a trust burn then; it's a trust burn here.
- 20% off fragrances is weak padding. Charlotte Tilbury ran 50%+ in their last sale; Sephora's Major Summer Deals had deeper fragrance cuts. 20% doesn't clear my "worth opening a browser tab" bar.
- Sally Beauty Rewards is buried in gray footer text — no points math, no member callout, no reason to care. Ulta always leads with your Rewards tier; Sally treats loyalty like a legal disclaimer.
- This is the twenty-fifth send in this cadence with the same offer headline. Even the 4th of July wrapper can't make "up to 40% off" feel urgent anymore.
- Klarna placed directly under the offer tiles signals "we think you need credit to shop our sale," which is a strange vibe for a 40%-off event.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real end date in the email body and subject** — "Ends July 3 at midnight" would do more work than ten urgency words without a deadline.
- 2. **Lead with styling tools as the hero, not the generic sale** — "40% Off Pro Tools for the 4th" positions Sally where they genuinely win against Sephora and Ulta.
- 3. **Add a Rewards callout above the fold** — one line like "Sally Beauty Rewards members: earn double points through July 3" gives me math to do and a reason to shop here vs. Ulta.
- 4. **Cut or upgrade the fragrances tile** — a 20% secondary offer next to a 40% primary makes the email feel padded; match the discount or swap in a category that earns a higher cut.
- 5. **Push Klarna below the fold** — it belongs in product detail pages, not the first scroll of a sale announcement.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your curling iron upgrade is 40% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 2-hour delivery on $35+ — pro tools, hair color, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Styling tools at 40% off, plus free same-day delivery on $35`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on all `<img>` elements:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` is not a valid property for replaced elements (`<img>`). In some rendering engines this is silently ignored; in others it can introduce unexpected inline spacing or whitespace gaps between stacked images. Should be removed.

**Issue — `Founders Grotesk` declared but never loaded:**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` rule loads Founders Grotesk. Only Open Sans is declared via `@font-face`. Every anchor element silently falls back to Helvetica in all clients. If Founders Grotesk is intentional, it needs a corresponding `@font-face` block with a hosted WOFF2 URL (it is not available on Google Fonts).

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts version (v17 → current v40+):**
All six `@font-face` `src` URLs reference `/opensans/v17/`:
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem8YaGs126MiZpBA-UFVZ0bf8pkAg.woff2
```
Google Fonts still serves v17 paths for backward compatibility, but the hashes may redirect or resolve to outdated subsets. Risk is low today but increases over time. Update to current version paths via fonts.googleapis.com CSS API.

**Issue — `font-display: swap` in `@font-face`:**
All six font declarations include `font-display: swap`. Email clients universally ignore this descriptor; it adds no value and contributes minor byte overhead. Should be removed.

**No issues found** — viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `IE=edge` meta, VML/MSO namespaces, and mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px` are all correctly implemented.
