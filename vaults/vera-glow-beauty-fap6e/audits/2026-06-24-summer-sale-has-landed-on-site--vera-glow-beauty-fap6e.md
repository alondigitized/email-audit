# Summer SALE Has Landed On Site! ☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Ten. This is Charlotte Tilbury email ten since June 11, and I know that because somewhere around email seven I stopped calling it a sale campaign and started calling it a content drip with product photography. To be fair to the actual email: it's good. The 45% off kits offer is front and center, the three-tier GWP structure gives me real points math to do, and the peach-and-gold visual is so unmistakably CT that I'd recognize it even if the logo fell off. In isolation, this is a well-built sale announcement.
- But I'm not reading this in isolation — I got "Early Access To SALE Ends Soon!" earlier today, June 24, which was email nine. That was the one telling me the app-only early access window was closing. And now, same day, the sale "lands on site" and I get a whole new email. CT is doing the slow-motion Glossier fade: three weeks of builds, early access, urgency, now open to everyone. By the time "has landed" hits my inbox I've already mentally bought or not bought. This email is collecting credit for intent I formed during email six.
- I'd probably click because I've been watching this sale build for two weeks and I already know what I want. But the email itself isn't moving me — my pre-existing intent is. I'm not forwarding this. Anyone I know who cares about CT also got all ten of these.

## What's working

- The 45% hero is the first thing my eye hits, and the asterisk is at least visible at a glance — CT is being more upfront than Glossier's "20% off" that quietly kept running a week past its stated end date.
- The 3 Free Full-Size Gifts tier is a legitimately compelling GWP hook. I'm a math person — anything that makes me calculate whether I should spend $30 more to unlock the next tier is working.
- Visual hierarchy is clean end-to-end: offer → SHOP CTA → gift tiers → app perks. I never have to hunt.
- "GOLDEN GODDESS GLOW" is full CT brand voice and it works on me. This is not Ulta's "Shop deals up to 40% off" flat-copy energy.
- "SHOP THE SALE" is a solid CTA — verb plus noun, specific, no "Discover" or "Explore" vagueness.

## What's weak

- Two emails on the same day, June 24 — "Early Access Ends" and "Sale Is Live" — is the move that erodes trust. Sephora sent five "Major Summer Deals" emails in two days and I went from opening all of them to scanning the subject lines. CT is building that same muscle memory in me right now.
- "ICONIC KITS*" with an asterisk and I can't easily read the fine print on my phone. I need to know if my Pillow Talk lip kit qualifies or if this is fragrance-kit-only. The exclusion ambiguity is friction.
- The GWP spend thresholds under each tier are too small to read without zooming. That math is the hook — make me feel it.
- Zero end date anywhere in the email body. After nine sends of urgency language, the deadline evaporated on the exact email where it matters most. Is this sale open through the weekend? Through July 4? I don't know.
- No hero product, no personalization, no "based on what you've bought" signal. Sephora at least occasionally gestures at my category history. This is a mass blast and it reads like one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the sale end date in the hero.** "Sale ends July 4" next to the 45% would give me a reason to act today instead of bookmarking and forgetting.
- 2. **Consolidate same-day sends or stagger by at least six hours.** "Early Access Ends" + "Sale Is Live" as two June 24 emails is the exact pattern that burned Sephora's open rates this month.
- 3. **Enlarge the GWP tier spend thresholds.** The gift math is the best part of this email and the spend numbers are body-copy small. Make them 16px bold and watch click rate go up.
- 4. **Clarify the asterisk.** "45% off kits — including Pillow Talk, Flawless Filter + Instant Eye Palette kits" would outperform "iconic kits*" that sends me to fine print territory.
- 5. **Introduce one hero SKU.** Sephora's best sale sends open with a single marquee product at the top discount, then "and hundreds more." A CT Flawless Filter kit at 45% off as a visual anchor would create desire before the category CTA.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font CSS (blocking issue in most clients):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Gmail (both web and app), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets. This means custom fonts will silently fall through to system fonts in the majority of opens. The conditional `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` correctly skips this for Outlook/Word, but the failure mode for Gmail/Yahoo is silent — verify that fallback `font-family` stacks are defined inline on text elements.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Shows as a blank tab label in webmail browser tabs (Gmail, Outlook.com). Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

**Image alt text — all empty:**
Both the desktop logo (`alt=""`) and mobile logo (`alt=""`) carry no alt text. When images are blocked (common in B2B inboxes, Outlook default), the logo area renders blank with no fallback label. Decorative spacers warrant `alt=""`, but the brand logo should have `alt="Charlotte Tilbury"` at minimum.

**`role="presentation"` on all tables:** Correct — suppresses table semantics for screen readers.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present and correct.

**Viewport meta:** Present (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1`). Correct.
