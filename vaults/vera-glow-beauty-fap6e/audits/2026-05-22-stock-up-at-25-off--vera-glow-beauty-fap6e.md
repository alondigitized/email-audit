# Stock up at 25% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I saw yesterday's F&F launch email — "Friends, family: 25% off starts now!" — and I was already in. That one earned a 9 from me. So when this hits my inbox today with "Stock up at 25% off," I know exactly where I am in the sale cycle: this is the mid-sale push, the urgency remind, the "did you sleep on it?" nudge. And honestly? The email itself is doing more than the subject line promised.
- The kits section is where my brain lights up. 35% off kits during an already-25%-off event? That is actually real math. I'm immediately calculating whether the kits are separately discounted or if the 35% replaces the 25% — and the email doesn't resolve that cleanly, which is a miss. But the GWP tiers ($100+ unlocks the Happi and Bright Kit freebie) are exactly the kind of stack-up incentive that makes me put down my coffee and open a new tab.
- What I can't get over is the subject line. After "Drunk texting?" and "When you're bright, you're bright" and "Let's get something clear" — DE has been on a wordplay tear that I have been genuinely impressed by — and then they serve me "Stock up at 25% off!" Like, that's a CVS circular subject line. I'd still click, but I'd click despite the subject, not because of it. Mark as read if I'm busy; open immediately if I'm in DE shopping mode. Would not forward, but I would screenshot the kits section for my group chat.

## What's working

- **25% off sitewide is loud and immediate** — the hero section puts "FRIENDS & FAMILY / 25% OFF / SITEWIDE" front and center with zero visual clutter. I didn't have to hunt.
- **GWP tiers are legitimately compelling** — the $100+ threshold for a free Happi + Bright Kit is the kind of incentivized spend ladder that gets me to round up my cart. Sephora does this with bonus points during Rouge events; DE is playing the same game.
- **Kits section with real prices** — showing the actual retail value vs. kit price on each item is something I wish more brands did. Charlotte Tilbury's emails this week have been all lifestyle, no price anchoring; DE is treating me like I can do math (because I can).
- **"Mask Me Anything" with a full-size GWP** — playful wordplay in the body copy even if the subject bailed on the streak. A full-size F-Balm as a GWP is a high-perceived-value add.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — the grid of kits at the bottom reads clearly, individual SHOP NOW buttons are tap-friendly, nothing is broken.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a total wordplay regression** — "Stock up at 25% off!" after a four-email run of brilliant puns is genuinely jarring. The brand voice DE has built in the subject line just... disappeared today.
- **35% off kits vs. 25% off everything — the math is murky** — are kits an additional discount? Is it 35% instead of 25%? The email implies both promos are live simultaneously but never clarifies. That ambiguity costs me clicks.
- **Preheader is doing subscribe-to-save marketing during a sale email** — the top-of-email text is pitching 15% off your first subscription order. That is a different ask from a completely different customer journey. It dilutes the sale message at the exact moment I should be feeling urgency about 25% off.
- **No sale end date anywhere visible** — "don't wait!" is not a deadline. The Glossier Memorial Day email this week put the end date in the hero; DE should be doing the same.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the sale expiration date in the hero** — one line: "Ends Sunday, May 25" would convert fence-sitters. Glossier and Sephora both do this during their major sale windows; there's no reason DE should leave that urgency on the table.
- 2. **Clarify the kits math explicitly** — a single line like "Kits are already 35% off — stacks with your F&F 25%" (or "kits are priced at 35% off instead of 25%") would immediately spike the click rate on that section. The ambiguity is a conversion killer.
- 3. **Reclaim the wordplay in the subject** — DE built genuine subscriber goodwill with the clever pun streak and this subject blew through it. Even something like "Your Babyfacial fund has a 25% match" keeps the voice alive while communicating the offer.
- 4. **Move subscribe-to-save pitch out of the preheader** — that message belongs in a post-purchase or win-back flow, not competing with a 25% off sitewide event header.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Invalid HTML comment syntax inside CSS `@media` block**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` are HTML comment delimiters, not CSS comment syntax. These two rules are silently dropped by all CSS parsers. Any element with `class="contenttable"` or `class="mobile"` will not resize on screens ≤600px. Fix: change to `/* ... */` or uncomment if intentional removal.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user-initiated pinch-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font-loading for the same assets**
The same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` font URLs are loaded via three separate mechanisms:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold">` (in `<head>`)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...?family=Brown+Bold)` (inside `<style>`)
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca.woff2") }` (also inside `<style>`)

This fires 3 separate HTTP requests per font weight before the email even renders. Gmail strips `<link>` and `@import` tags, so only the `@font-face` blocks survive — making the first two mechanisms dead weight that add parse time in other clients.

**[LOW] `BrownRegular` uses first-party CDN; Bold/Light/Thin use third-party**
`BrownRegular` is served from `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (reliable, first-party). The other three weights load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a font redistribution host with no SLA. Inconsistent reliability; consider self-hosting all weights on `drunkelephant.com`.

**[LOW] Global `* { line-height: 100% }` reset**
Overrides line-height on every element including links and buttons. This is a known cause of clipped descenders and misaligned CTA text in some clients. Should be scoped to layout tables only.

**[INFO] XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE**
Standard for email; not a defect, but VML namespace (`xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`, `xmlns:w`) is only needed if conditional VML is present further in the HTML.
