# Time to come clean
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- So DE slides into my inbox with "Time to come clean" and — honestly — I smiled. That pun is so very *Drunk Elephant* that I didn't even need the sender name. This is a cleanser showcase wrapped in a welcome-to-the-club moment, and the summer angle ("wash away summer grime") is smart timing. I'm a sucker for a good cleansing routine pivot in July, when SPF residue and sweat are a real conversation.
- That said, this is email number five in ten days from DE, and after "20-minute hack" on July 8 — which earned its open — this one feels a notch lower-effort. It's a product grid email. Six SKUs, six "SHOP NOW" buttons, zero guidance on which one is mine. The offer is real (15% off, free Brightfacial mask with $75), but the email doesn't do the homework *for* me the way the July 8 send did.
- Would I click? Yes, on Beste No. 9 specifically because I already know it. Would I forward to a friend? Only if she texted me first asking what cleanser to try. This is a "mark as read and revisit at checkout" email, not a screenshotting-the-deal moment.

## What's working

- **The "welcome to the club" mechanic is smart.** The 15% Off Club promo box at the top is visually distinct, code visible, conditions right there — I don't have to hunt. That's better than what Ulta does, where the GWP terms are buried in a footnote I find six scrolls later.
- **"Summer grime" seasonal hook lands.** Cleanser emails are boring eight months of the year, but in July it's genuinely useful positioning. DE earned this angle.
- **Six products, six CTAs, clean grid.** The product lineup is exhaustive without being chaotic. Each item gets a photo, a name, and a one-liner. Visual hierarchy is intact on the scroll.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loads, nothing overlaps, no lorem ipsum placeholder lurking. After the Sephora duplicate-send disaster I reviewed recently ("Summer's hottest beauty ☀️" sent twice same day), a clean render is not nothing.
- **Brand voice is airtight.** "We're here to do your dirty work" sounds exactly like DE. Consistent tone start to finish — which Glossier also nails, and it matters.

## What's weak

- **Zero skin-type guidance on any product.** Six cleansers and I have no idea which one DE thinks is *mine*. "Our gentle cream cleanser" tells me nothing. Is it for dry skin? Sensitive? Rosacea? ILIA, for comparison, consistently ties ingredients to skin outcomes — DE just names and moves on.
- **No urgency on the 15% offer.** That promo code has no expiry visible. I'm a points-math person and a "does this expire or can I stack it during the next Sephora event?" thinker — without a deadline, this goes in my mental "use later" pile where it dies.
- **The hero CTA "SHOP NOW" is doing nothing.** I'm already looking at six products below. "Shop Cleansers" is still bland but at least it's directional. This is the same mistake Sally Beauty makes when they run a sale — big green button, zero specificity.
- **Cadence is approaching fatigue territory.** July 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 — this is every other day. The July 8 "20-minute hack" email had enough editorial weight to justify its slot. A product grid email on day 10 starts to feel like fill.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add skin-type routing to product descriptions.** Give me one line: "For dry skin" / "For oily or combo" / "For sensitive." I'd know in one pass which one to click. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Put an expiry on the 15% code.** "Valid through July 17" gives me a reason to act now. Without it I defer, and deferred becomes never.
- 3. **Make the hero CTA specific.** "Shop the Cleanse Edit" or "Find Your Cleanser" over bare "SHOP NOW." Glossier always verbs their CTAs with a noun — it's a small thing that moves click rate.
- 4. **Test a more concrete subject line** — "Time to come clean" is charming but doesn't telegraph the offer to a cold inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off + the cleanser for your skin type`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wash away summer grime (and get 15% off while you're at it)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `6 cleansers, one for every skin type — plus a free Brightfacial mask with $75`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 15% Off Club code is inside — use it on your next cleanser haul`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments break responsive CSS:**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS. Every email client that processes the stylesheet will silently discard these two rules. The `contenttable` and `mobile` classes will receive **no mobile width overrides**, causing layout breakage at narrow viewports.

**Triple font loading — unnecessary requests:**

BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each requested three independent times:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` in `<style>`

This produces 9 redundant font HTTP requests per render. BrownRegular is self-hosted from `drunkelephant.com` while the other three weights depend on the third-party CDN `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — an availability dependency outside brand control.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is flagged by iOS accessibility audits.

**Universal `line-height: 100%` rule:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

The `*` selector applies to every element. On multi-line text nodes, `line-height: 100%` resolves to exactly 1× the font-size, producing zero leading between lines. This typically renders body copy as visually collapsed or overlapping in clients that honor `line-height` strictly (Outlook, Apple Mail).

**`.sm { display:none; }` without visible show rule:**

A hide-on-desktop class is declared but no corresponding `display:block !important` override appears in either media query block in the visible source. If `.sm` elements are meant to surface on mobile, the show rule may be missing or further down the truncated source.
