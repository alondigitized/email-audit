# Smooth, hydrated, and radiant
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so DE lands in my inbox again — June 13th, which puts them at five emails in eight days if I'm counting right, and I am — and the subject is "Smooth, hydrated, and radiant." I stared at it for a second in my inbox and thought: is this a Tatcha email? A Neutrogena ad? It could be anybody. There's nothing tying it to DE's irreverent voice until I actually open it and get hit with "EW, GROSS, DRYNESS—NO THANKS." — which, honestly, is the DE I know. That's the brand showing up.
- The B-Hydra ingredient breakdown in the middle is genuinely smart — sodium hyaluronate, pro-vitamin B5, pineapple extract, each with a clear function callout. That's DE's whole ingredient-transparency brand promise, and they execute it cleanly here. The model is a young woman of color applying product at chin level, which reads young and relatable, and the layout doesn't fight itself. No broken images, no lorem ipsum chaos like I've seen elsewhere.
- But here's where I get stuck: there's nothing to *do* here except shop full price. No sale, no event, no loyalty hook that ties to my Rouge/Diamond tier. The "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" section at the bottom with the "You've been upgraded!" tag is intriguing but way too small to parse — it feels like a GWP nudge but I can't confirm what the threshold is or what I'm getting. I'd probably mark this read, not click, and wait for DE to actually send me a real reason to spend money on B-Hydra. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **Hero headline earns DE's voice.** "EW, GROSS, DRYNESS—NO THANKS." is punchy, on-brand, and makes me feel like I'm back in DE's world instead of a generic skincare inbox.
- **Ingredient education section is the email's strongest asset.** Three callouts with specific function copy (not just "hydrates and moisturizes" — actual mechanism language) is exactly why people pay prestige prices for DE.
- **CTA is product-specific.** "SHOP B-HYDRA" tells me exactly where I'm going, not just to the homepage.
- **Model demographic is on-point.** Young woman of color, skincare-moment framing — feels like it was cast for me, not for a 55-year-old anti-aging demographic.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout collapse. After the Ulta D&G double-send situation I audited recently, clean is underrated.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is completely anonymous.** "Smooth, hydrated, and radiant" could be Olay, Neutrogena, CeraVe, literally anyone. The hero copy inside is 10x more DE than the subject that's supposed to get me to open.
- **Zero promotional hook.** After the June 11 "Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!" bait-and-switch (spoiler: there was no sale inside), this full-price awareness send feels even more deflating. My points math brain is just... idle.
- **"SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" section is too cryptic.** The "You've been upgraded!" copy is intriguing but the body text is too small to read in the render — if this is a GWP threshold or a samples upgrade, that should be legible and front-loaded, not buried at the bottom in 9pt font.
- **No urgency whatsoever.** No deadline, no event tie-in, no limited run. This email could have been sent in February and nothing would feel off.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line to match the hero voice.** The "EW, GROSS" energy is what DE owns — lean into it from the inbox. The subject is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. **Add a loyalty hook or threshold nudge.** Even "Free shipping on B-Hydra when you spend $50" gives my points-math brain something to work with. Glossier's "Save up to 25%" on June 13 made me feel rewarded just for being a customer; this made me feel like a stranger.
- 3. **Make "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" legible.** Blow up the copy, define the threshold, show the sample clearly. If this is a GWP moment, it should lead — not trail off at the bottom where scroll fatigue is highest.
- 4. **Tie the ingredient education to a seasonal moment.** Summer + hydration + pineapple extract = a real hook. ILIA managed to make their collab feel like a cultural moment; DE has the ingredients to do the same with a simple seasonal frame.

## Technical Audit — Drunk Elephant "Smooth, hydrated, and radiant"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses an XHTML 1.0 Transitional base with table-based layout, which is standard for broad client compatibility. Two confirmed code-level defects are present: HTML comment syntax embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block silently drops critical responsive layout rules, and fonts are loaded three times via redundant mechanisms.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — the HTML source is truncated before body links appear. Full-source review needed to confirm:
- Tracking pixel presence and domain
- Click-tracking wrapper URLs
- UTM parameter coverage on all CTA hrefs

**Flagged (partial source):** Three `<link rel="stylesheet">` calls to `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com` load before any content. If a corporate mail proxy or security gateway blocks this third-party CDN, the HTTP requests may hang and delay email render, which can indirectly affect tracked open timing.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Commented-out responsive CSS rules:**
Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are not valid CSS and are silently ignored by all CSS parsers. The `contenttable` and `mobile` width overrides **never apply on mobile**, meaning those table columns will not reflow to 414px on small screens. This is a broken responsive layout on any viewport under 600px.

**Triple font loading — redundant and slow:**
`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are each loaded three ways simultaneously:
1. `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(...)` inside the `<style>` block
3. `@font-face { src: url(...) }` also inside `<style>`

This generates 6+ redundant network requests per font family. Most email clients block external fonts anyway; the triple declaration adds overhead without improving fallback behavior. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted from `drunkelephant.com` — the Bold/Light/Thin variants should match this approach.

**Inconsistent font sources — reliability risk:**
`BrownRegular` loads from `https://www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (self-hosted, versioned). The other three Brown variants load from `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/` (third-party CDN, no SLA). If `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is unavailable, the brand font set is partially broken with no consistent fallback declared.

**Global `mso-line-height-rule:exactly` on `*`:**
Applying this to all elements can cause clipped descenders and collapsed line heights on Outlook when computed line-height is smaller than font-size. Should be scoped to specific selectors, not `*`.

**Alt text:** Cannot verify — source is truncated before `<img>` tags appear.
