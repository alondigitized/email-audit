# Rock, paper, save
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Day three. I've been watching ILIA run this buy-three game like they're hosting a summer field day, and I'll say this — the creative team has been earning their salary. Dice on day two, rock-paper-scissors on day three. There's a thematic arc here and it's genuinely clever. But I've now received three consecutive emails about the exact same 20%-off sale, and what finally made me slow down today wasn't the wordplay — it was the product trifectas. Breaking the shopping into pre-curated skincare/complexion/color bundles is the first thing ILIA has done in this campaign that actually does some of the work *for me*. The SPF details, wear times, and mini product descriptions let me scan and decide without clicking to a PDP. That is useful.
- What's still missing: no end date, no points angle, and zero acknowledgment that July 4th is forty-eight hours away. Sally Beauty's holiday email had a hard deadline stamped right in the hero. ILIA is leaving a seasonal urgency hook on the table while I sit on a bookmarked cart. I'd click the COMPLEXION trifecta section because Super Serum Skin Tint is on my restock list — not because this email moved me. I would not forward it; anyone in my group chat has already seen the sale on days one and two.

## What's working

- **Rock-paper-scissors mechanic fits the "buy three" concept perfectly** — the creative arc from dice (day two) to this is coherent and smarter than most brands' repeated hero assets.
- **Trifectas segmented by category (SKINCARE / COMPLEXION / COLOR)** actually make bundle-shopping easy — I can go straight to the section relevant to me without hunting.
- **Code BUNDLEUP is visible and memorable** — no fine print hunting, no mystery discount.
- **"BUILD YOUR TRIO" CTA is action-forward** — verb plus noun, clear, not "Discover."
- **Product detail copy is genuinely useful** — "SPF 40," "12-hr wear," "barrier-building" — these are the signals I want before committing to a bundle price.

## What's weak

- **No end date anywhere in the email** — day three of a sale with no countdown is pure FOMO leakage; Sally Beauty stamped "25% off sitewide" with a clear holiday frame and I felt the deadline. ILIA still hasn't given me one.
- **Zero loyalty or points framing** — I'm always doing the math on stacking a sale with a Sephora BI event or Ulta points multiplier, and ILIA has never once mentioned it across this entire campaign run.
- **Subject "Rock, paper, save" requires a half-second decode** — cute, but it's the least informative of the three sale subjects. Day one's "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now" and day two's "Third one's the charm" both signaled the offer more directly from the inbox.
- **No July 4th hook** — Lush leaned into summer travel, e.l.f. pivoted to a new product lane, Sally went full holiday. ILIA is running a game theme while leaving "independence from full price" untouched.
- **Category CTAs link to sections, not single hero PDPs** — a direct link to the Super Serum Skin Tint PDP would convert faster for someone already near a decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the hero** — one line: "Sale ends July 6" or a countdown element. Every day without one is a day I wait.
- 2. **Lean into July 4th** — swap the scissors graphic for a sparkler or a flag-adjacent color block; reframe as "Declare your trio before Sunday."
- 3. **Add a loyalty callout** — even one line: "Rouge or Diamond? Your 20% stacks with existing points — shop before the event weekend." That's the message that makes a Vera click tonight instead of next week.
- 4. **Hero-link one specific product** — the Super Serum Skin Tint is the brand's anchor. A single "bestseller" callout with a direct PDP link converts the already-decided buyer.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Save 20% on your trio — ends Sunday`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Declare your beauty trio: 20% off this weekend`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Build your skincare, complexion, or color trio with code BUNDLEUP before July 4th.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy 3, save 20% — shop curated trifectas before the long weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` fonts stripped in Gmail:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail removes `@import` inside `<style>`. Montserrat and any Klaviyo custom typeface will not render; fallback chain `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` kicks in. Acceptable degradation, but intentional brand font is absent for ~40%+ of consumer opens.

**Conflicting `<p>` margin rules:**
```css
/* Rule 1 (reset block) */
p { display: block; margin: 13px 0 }

/* Rule 2 (later in same <style>) */
p { margin-left: 0; margin-right: 0; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0; padding-bottom: 1em }
```
Rule 2 overrides rule 1's `margin: 13px 0` to zero. In clients that strip `padding` on block elements (older Outlook, some Yahoo), the `padding-bottom: 1em` fallback also fails, collapsing paragraph spacing entirely.

**Hover/visited state color lock:**
```css
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Visited links are visually indistinguishable from unvisited. Hover provides no feedback. Minor accessibility concern (WCAG 2.4.4 link purpose / 1.4.1 use of color); not a blocker for email delivery.

**`<title>` is empty:** `<title> </title>` — no screen reader label for the document. Low severity in email context.

**Image alt text:** Cannot assess — body truncated.
