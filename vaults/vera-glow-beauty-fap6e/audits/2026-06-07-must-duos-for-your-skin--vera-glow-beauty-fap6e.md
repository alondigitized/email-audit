#  Must-duos for your skin 👯‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Email 24 from e.l.f. since May 15th — I've been keeping count. Coming off yesterday's "dew more for skin" ingredient story (8/10) and two days after the $7 cream bronzer send that genuinely stopped my scroll, this one arrives with a Best Friends Day hook that is at least calendar-legible. "Bestie" is consistent with e.l.f.'s voice, the $14/$10/$10 pricing is squarely in impulse-buy territory, and the two diverse Millennial models in the hero nail the demographic. The intent is clear.
- But after the cream bronzer email that worked *because* it had one product with one price and one reason to care, this feels like a pivot back into catalog mode. Three separate duos, three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons, no hierarchy beyond top-to-bottom stacking — it reads like a product grid that got a party hat glued to it. Compare that to the ILIA "poof the results are in" send that landed today at 9/10: one product, consumer proof data, a single focused hero. Or Charlotte Tilbury's beach bag roundup that managed to feel editorial even while pushing multiple SKUs. This is neither.
- The "stop into Ulta" footer callout does catch my eye — I'm Diamond and I clock anything that touches points math. But it's buried, unactionable, and doesn't tell me whether the duos earn me anything specific. If they'd written "pick up the Brightest Besties Duo at Ulta and stack your Diamond points," I'd have had my Ulta app open mid-scroll. As-is: I mark it read, maybe revisit on an idle Sunday when I'm already in the e.l.f. app. Would not forward to a friend — the $7 bronzer email is still the one I'd text.

## What's working

- **Best Friends Day tie-in is genuinely timely.** June 7/8 is the holiday, the "bestie" copy lands, the two-women hero earns the moment.
- **Three price points under $15 are real impulse triggers for me.** $14 and $10 duos require zero justification at the cart.
- **Hero models hit the demographic.** Diverse, millennial-coded, naturally lit — e.l.f. consistently gets this right and it's worth crediting again.
- **"Stop into Ulta" footer.** Tiny, but it signals cross-channel availability, and I noticed it because I'm always doing the Diamond points math.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, text is readable on mobile, nothing broken. Clean delivery on a high-volume send streak.

## What's weak

- **Three duos, three "SHOP NOW" buttons — no hierarchy.** There's no hero product; the email asks me to choose without helping me choose.
- **CTA copy is completely generic.** "SHOP NOW" appears three times with zero differentiation. After the bronzer email that at least implied *why* to buy, this is a step back.
- **No loyalty hook beyond a footer footnote.** I'm a Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond member — if one of these duos earns bonus points or qualifies for a GWP, that's a click. Silence.
- **The "duo" framing doesn't sell the savings.** I don't know if buying the duo is cheaper than two singles. That math is the entire reason I'd bundle, and it's not here.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Twenty-four emails in under four weeks. The "bestie" framing would land harder if it didn't feel like e.l.f. calls everyone bestie every 36 hours.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one duo as the anchor hero and price-justify it.** Show me "duo saves you $X vs. buying separately." That's the click. The other two become supporting cast, not equals.
- 2. **Replace all three "SHOP NOW" buttons with specific CTAs.** "Grab the Brightest Duo," "Try the Dew-o," "Shop the Drops" — each one distinct, each one a verb + noun.
- 3. **Add a loyalty callout inside the body, not the footer.** "Ulta Diamond members: earn double points on duos today" is a sentence that converts me immediately.
- 4. **Include a soft deadline.** Even "Best Friends Day is tomorrow — grab yours" is enough to create a reason to act now rather than mark-as-read.
- 5. **Pull back cadence.** This is not a change to this specific send, but after 24 emails in three weeks, the brand's "bestie" language is starting to sound like a Sephora cast member who won't let me browse.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Braze redirect wrapping confirmed:**
All hrefs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/…` before resolving to destination. This is expected for click tracking.

**UTM pass-through — cannot confirm:**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` value in tab previews or notification previews. An empty title is a missed slot; not a breakage.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <!--<![endif]-->` to exclude Outlook, which is correct. However, Gmail (web), Yahoo, and most mobile clients strip `<link>` tags entirely — the fallback `Arial,Sans-serif` will be used by the majority of recipients. This is acceptable if visually tested, but worth noting as Open Sans will not render in most clients.

**`#MessageViewBody a` rule strips all link styling in Gmail:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a{color:inherit;text-decoration:none}
```
This CSS selector targets Gmail's message container and resets every `<a>` to inherit body color with no underline. Unless CTAs have explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` styles, links will be **visually indistinguishable from body text in Gmail**. This is a frequent source of click-rate suppression.

**Empty HTML blocks generate unnecessary DOM weight:**
`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4` in row-1 contain only whitespace inside `<div>` wrappers — they add markup with no rendering output. Likely builder artifact.

**Nested `nl-container` duplication:**
The outer `nl-container` wraps an inner `nl-container` inside `html_block block-1`. This double-nesting is a builder export artifact and while it renders correctly, it adds ~200 bytes of redundant table structure per block.

**`role="presentation"` properly applied** to all layout tables. `lang="en"` set on `<html>`. MSO/VML namespaces declared. Responsive breakpoint at `max-width:660px` for a 640px layout — appropriate margin.

**Alt text:**
Logo image uses `alt="e.l.f."` — minimal but acceptable for a brand logo. `title="e.l.f."` is redundant (browsers ignore `title` on images in most contexts) but harmless.
