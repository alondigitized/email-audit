# The art of 20% off forever
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Ten. This is the tenth ILIA email since May 16. I have been counting every single one, and I want to be fair to this send because the concept is genuinely clever — they're using the Friends + Family window to pitch subscribe-and-save as a permanent 20% discount, which is a smarter ask than a one-time percentage-off. The "20% Off Forever" framing is designed to make you feel like you're locking in a deal, not just shopping a sale.
- Here's where it falls apart for me personally: I'm Sephora Rouge. The math on this subscribe-and-save is not math I can do in a vacuum. If I subscribe directly on ILIA's DTC site, every one of those orders skips my Sephora cart — which means I'm losing Rouge points, losing access to Sephora Beauty Insider Event pricing, and losing the ability to stack with Ulta's 21 Days if ILIA ever shows up there. The email doesn't acknowledge that trade-off at all. It shows me a "Great Points" callout that implies loyalty stacking, but that's ILIA's own loyalty, not my existing programs. That's a meaningful omission for anyone who lives in the Sephora/Ulta ecosystem.
- That said — the visual layout is cleaner than Charlotte Tilbury's "Still Deciding, Darling?" guilt trip from earlier this week, and the subscribe-and-save benefits block (free shipping, skip/pause/cancel, points) is laid out better than anything I've seen from Drunk Elephant's current F&F run. I'd mark it as read and come back to do the math. I would not forward this — I'd never push a subscription commitment on a friend without warning her to check her Sephora tier first.

## What's working

- The subscribe-and-save mechanic during an F&F sale is a legitimate hook — it reframes a one-time discount as a loyalty investment, which is genuinely sticky thinking.
- "You Design It — skip, pause, or cancel anytime" addresses the one objection that usually kills subscription CTAs; good that it's above the fold in the benefits block.
- The product carousel (Super Serum Skin Tint, Limitless Lash, True Matte Shadow Stick, Multi Stick, Skin Rewind Concealer, Sun Retouch SPF) gives me the full product scope immediately — I can self-sort into "do I even buy these SKUs regularly enough to subscribe?"
- Visual hierarchy is immaculate — "20% Off—On Repeat" is the first thing I read, the benefits block is easy to scan, and "GET 20% OFF FOREVER" lands cleanly without competing noise.
- "Cart Deco" free-mini add-on is a nice touch that adds low-effort incremental value to the subscribe pitch.

## What's weak

- The "forever" language with a visible asterisk in the top banner ("20% OFF FOR LIFE*") is a yellow flag — if this is truly forever, you don't need an asterisk, and now I'm suspicious about what conditions kill the discount.
- Zero acknowledgment that subscribing DTC trades away Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond point accumulation — for a cross-shopping loyalist, that's the whole decision and the email acts like that category of person doesn't exist.
- Ten emails in eight days. Even a strong pitch gets diluted when I'm fatigued before I open it. Glossier ran their Memorial Day sale with one launch and one reminder — two emails, clean, done. ILIA is running what feels like an onboarding drip and a promotional campaign simultaneously.
- No deadline shown in the email body — "during Friends + Family" isn't a date, and the urgency frame that justifies acting today rather than "thinking about it" is completely absent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the F&F end date in the email body.** "Subscribe before [date] to lock 20% off forever" converts urgency into action. Right now "during Friends + Family" is a vague qualifier, not a countdown.
- 2. **Address the loyalty trade-off directly.** Add one sentence: "Earn [ILIA loyalty] points on every order — and yes, ILIA ships through Sephora too if you prefer to keep your Rouge status." Acknowledging the ecosystem signals customer respect.
- 3. **Explain the asterisk.** "20% OFF FOR LIFE*" in the banner and "20% Off Forever" in the body need to be reconciled visibly. Even a single line — "as long as your subscription is active" — removes the bait-and-switch anxiety.
- 4. **Cut cadence.** The email is solid but it's the tenth send in eight days. Merge the subscribe-and-save pitch into a single, well-considered "here's the full F&F story" email instead of daily drip.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lock in 20% off ILIA — during F+F only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your permanent 20% starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Subscribe during Friends + Family, save forever. Free shipping + skip anytime.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `F+F ends soon — subscribe today to keep 20% off every order.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 3a — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
The title is a single space. Screen readers announce this as the document title in some clients (e.g., VoiceOver + Apple Mail). Should contain the email subject or a descriptive fallback.

**Issue 3b — `@import` for web fonts (two instances)**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Yahoo, and several mobile clients. Fonts will silently fall back to `"Helvetica", sans-serif`. This is expected behavior in email but worth confirming the fallback stack renders acceptably — it does (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is declared at the bottom of the stylesheet).

**Issue 3c — Duplicate `img` reset rule**
```css
/* First declaration (~line 6 of stylesheet) */
img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic }

/* Second declaration (~line 25 of stylesheet) */
img { border: 0; height: auto; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; max-width: 100% }
```
The second rule wins. The first omits `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%`; the second omits `-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic`. These should be consolidated into one rule. No rendering impact expected in modern clients, but it signals a template hygiene issue.

**Issue 3d — Zero link differentiation across all states**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
No visual change on hover or visited state. In web-rendered clients (Gmail browser, Outlook.com) this makes hyperlinks indistinguishable from body text unless they are styled via inline `color` overrides. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires links be distinguishable by more than color alone when no underline is present.
