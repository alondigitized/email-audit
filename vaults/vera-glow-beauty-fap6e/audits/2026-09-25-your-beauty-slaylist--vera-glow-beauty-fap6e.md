# Your beauty slaylist 🎶
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a lot of email for a Thursday morning scroll. e.l.f. is doing a music-album marketing tie-in ("Mirror Mix" with actual named artists — Joaquina, Flau'jae, Nezza — like this is a Spotify release, not a beauty brand) stacked on top of THREE separate product pushes (Power Grip base, blush, lip liner) THEN a second Mirror Mix callout THEN a GWP mascara offer THEN a "Top Picks" grid. It's basically five emails duct-taped into one.
- The GWP — free full-size Clear Brow & Lash Mascara at $50, no code — is genuinely the best thing in here and it's buried two-thirds of the way down, after I've already scrolled past a concert lineup I don't recognize a single name from. Sally Beauty's "Ready For A New Fall Shade" send at least kept it to one clean beat; this is the opposite, it's the "New beauty to obsess over" Sephora wall-dump energy but with a K-pop-adjacent music campaign bolted on top.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the GWP tile specifically, but I'd have to scroll past a wall of stuff that has nothing to do with why I open e.l.f. emails (product drops, price). Not forwarding this — it's busy, not exciting. Marking as read after a skim.

## What's working

- The GWP mascara offer is a clean, honest mechanic — spend $50, get it free, no code, no asterisk gymnastics — same energy as the Glossier "spend $50+" GWP I flagged, and it's the strongest single beat in this email.
- "Top Picks for You" section actually shows prices ($11, $11, $2) and product shots — that's the personalization/utility signal that's usually missing from these brand-campaign sends.
- Product photography on the Power Grip / Soft Glam / Main Stain sections is crisp and the lay-flat product arrangements read clearly even at a glance.
- e.l.f. is a brand I actually shop, so the sender is trusted — I'm not deleting this on sight the way I would an unfamiliar name.

## What's weak

- The "Mirror Mix" music album framing means nothing to me — I don't know these artists, and leading with a full-bleed lipstick-smear album cover before any product or offer is a swing-and-a-miss for a 29-year-old who opens e.l.f. emails for base makeup, not music discovery.
- Way too many competing CTAs — "Listen Now" (x2), "Shop Power Grip," "Shop Soft Glam," "Shop Main Stain," "Shop Now" for the GWP, plus three more Shop Now buttons in Top Picks. Nothing is THE offer.
- No skin-tone or skin-type personalization anywhere despite e.l.f. absolutely having that data on Beauty Squad members — Drunk Elephant's "world is your moisture" send at least gestures at hydration need, this doesn't even try.
- Zero mention of Beauty Squad points or tier status, which is the first thing I check in any beauty email — Ulta and Sephora both work loyalty math into their subject lines or hero space; e.l.f.'s Beauty Squad app is only mentioned in the footer icon strip, an afterthought.
- The GWP — the actual best offer — is stranded below three product sections and a repeated Mirror Mix callout instead of leading.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the GWP. Free full-size mascara at $50 is a stronger opener than an album cover — move it above the fold, first section after the logo.
- 2. Cut Mirror Mix to one section, not two. The repeated "Listen to Mirror Mix" block right before the GWP is redundant — say it once, early, small, and let product carry the rest.
- 3. Pick one hero product push, not three (Power Grip, Soft Glam, Main Stain back to back). If it's a multi-launch week, use a single "New This Week" carousel instead of three full-width sections.
- 4. Put Beauty Squad points math somewhere visible — even a one-line "Beauty Squad members earn 2x points on GWP purchases" would beat the silence.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new drops in Power Grip, Soft Glam & Main Stain`
