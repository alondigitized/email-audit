# HAIM x ILIA
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four ILIA sends into June and they finally break the Soft Focus Education Arc. After weeks of consumer studies, ingredient decks, and the June 7 "Meet the Soft Focus family" mega-roundup, this HAIM collab lands as a genuine tonal reset — and honestly, I needed it. The "one trio. your whole routine." headline stopped my thumb because the double meaning earns it: the band, the routine, the callback to everything ILIA has been pushing. That's real copywriting, not a formula.
- HAIM is *so* Millennial it almost hurts. I've had them in my rotation since "Forever" dropped in 2013. The fact that all three sisters talk about actual, messy skin conditions — acne-prone, reactive, the whole thing — instead of doing the standard glossy "I just feel so confident!" routine makes this feel earned rather than purchased. The photography is editorial-warm, the layout is clean, and the demographic signal hits me directly. Way more authentic than Charlotte Tilbury's recent "There's A Reason It's Called Unreal" send, which was all product boast and zero skin story.
- But here's the problem: I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond. I do points math. And this email has no math to do. The sweepstakes for a signed vinyl and $500 is fun, but it's not moving me to a cart. I'd screenshot the HAIM quotes, send the collab link to one friend who loves both HAIM and clean beauty, and wait for the next ILIA email with an actual reason to spend money. Strong brand impression. Weak purchase moment.

## What's working

- **"One trio. your whole routine." is genuinely clever** — the HAIM-as-trio / beauty-routine double meaning is the kind of wordplay I actually stop and re-read. Earns the email its open.
- **All three sisters have real, specific skin concerns** — Este's acne-prone, Danielle's and Alana's issues follow. Compared to Charlotte Tilbury's latest, which had zero skin story and all product boast, this is a master class in testimonial credibility.
- **Photography reads exactly like my aesthetic** — editorial, warm, indie-cool. Glossier gets close but leans cold-white-minimalist. This warmth is the right call for ILIA's California DNA.
- **Clean layout with no render bugs** — after reviewing Ulta's recent NARS GWP sends (same email, same broken preview, twice in one afternoon), zero broken images and zero layout chaos is genuinely refreshing.
- **The vinyl sweepstakes is culturally coherent** — signed vinyl + $500 is the right prize for who HAIM's audience is. Not a generic gift card. Shows ILIA understands why this collab exists.

## What's weak

- **No purchase incentive anywhere** — I'm always triangulating what a send earns me in points, and there's nothing to math here. Drunk Elephant's lazy "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" at least told me what to do with my wallet.
- **CTA button copy is too small to read at mobile scroll speed** — there are buttons under each HAIM section but I had to zoom to even see them. Ulta's recent NARS gift-with-purchase send had "Add to Bag" readable at thumb-scroll pace. This doesn't clear that bar.
- **Twenty-four sends in June is fatiguing** — even a genuinely good email gets discounted when it's the twenty-fourth. The sweepstakes gambit reads a little like ILIA knows open rates are softening and is trying a new hook rather than slowing the cadence.
- **Sweepstakes competes with product CTAs instead of supporting them** — the vinyl giveaway is placed prominently enough that it dilutes the "this is your routine" message. It should be in the footer, not co-headlining.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product incentive tied to the collab** — "Shop the trio HAIM trusts — free Soft Focus sample with any Skin Tint order" bridges the editorial moment to an actual cart action. The collab is the hook; it needs a close.
- 2. **Make each HAIM-section CTA sister-specific** — "Shop Este's Picks" / "Shop Danielle's Picks" / "Shop Alana's Picks" deepens the editorial structure and adds persona-match signal for whoever identifies most with that sister.
- 3. **Push the vinyl sweep to footer** — the giveaway is a nice brand moment, not the feature. Free it from competing with the skin-routine narrative.
- 4. **Hold one day before the next send** — this email deserves 24 hours of breathing room. Dropping another blast tomorrow burns goodwill the collab just rebuilt.
