# Still time to save 20%
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- The subject "Still time to save 20%" drops into my inbox on June 17, a full nine days after DE's "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" from the 8th, and my brain auto-fills the gap: sale extension, discount continuation, clicking. That mental shortcut is exactly the problem. I open it expecting a promo follow-up — or at minimum, another Littles kit push like the June 10 send — and what I get instead is a SheerID hero program enrollment flow for teachers, nurses, military members, first responders, and students. I am none of those things. I have burned an open on content that has nothing for me.
- Which is genuinely a shame, because the email is well-made. "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." is a strong headline — earned, warm, not overwrought. The five category CTAs are cleanly organized and easy to parse. The model's skin does silent product storytelling without a single visible SKU. If you're a public school teacher or an ER nurse, this probably hits differently than any beauty email you've opened this month. DE deserves credit for building this program at all.
- But I am not clicking, and I am not forwarding this. If my nurse friend came up in conversation I'd text her "DE does a hero discount, Google it" — which tells you exactly how much lift this email generated for me personally. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- "IT SERVES YOU RIGHT." earns its hero position — punchy, grateful without being sentimental, lands without needing explanation
- The five segmented verification buttons are genuinely smart UX; breaking it into "A TEACHER / A STUDENT / A MEDICAL PROFESSIONAL" etc. makes the path feel personal instead of one-size
- Female model's skin is doing product work without a product in frame — that's confidence in the brand and it shows
- "Up from 15% for a limited time only" is smart upgrade framing that rewards anyone who's been sitting on the fence and adds real urgency for qualifiers
- Clean visual hierarchy: hero text → brief explanation → three-step how-to → CTA buttons, nothing fighting for attention

## What's weak

- "Still time to save 20%" is doing bait-and-switch work for anyone who doesn't qualify — after June 8's flash sale subject, this reads as a continuation of a general sale, not a professional program gate
- This is DE email number five or six in eight days, and June 11's "Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!" already pulled the same trick (pulled me in with a discount hint, delivered something different when I opened it) — DE is burning subscriber trust with misleading subject lines twice in one week
- No fallback module for non-qualifiers — Glossier's "Friends don't let friends pay full price" gave everyone something; this gives the 85% of DE's list who aren't healthcare workers or teachers exactly nothing to do with their open
- No product in the email at all — ILIA's "Summer Skin Staples" from last week showed products with ingredient-level detail; DE is asking me to get excited about a 20% discount without showing me a single thing I'd spend it on
- Fine print ("cannot be combined with other discounts") is there, but tiny gray text I had to lean in to read — and I always do the math

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** This email should not be broadcasting to DE's full subscriber base. This single change fixes the bait-and-switch problem at the root — if only likely-qualifying subscribers receive it, the subject misdirect disappears entirely.
- 2. **Add a non-qualifier row.** One section at the bottom — "Not a qualifying professional? Here's what's live right now →" — turns a dead end into a traffic driver. Ulta does this consistently on their GWP sends (even the MUGLER GWP email with the broken banner at least made the offer available to anyone hitting $70). DE is leaving real click-through on the table.
- 3. **Put product in the body.** Show me the Protini, the B-Goldi Drops, the C-Firma Power Serum — something to anchor what the 20% is worth. The program mechanics are clear; the desire is absent.
- 4. **Fix the subject line to signal the real audience:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Teachers, nurses, military — your 20% is ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your hero discount just got better: 20% off now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We bumped it from 15% for a limited time — tap to verify below`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `First responders, healthcare workers, students + more: here's how to claim`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — invalid CSS in `<style>` block:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML comment syntax (`<!-- -->`) is illegal inside CSS. Any parser that doesn't silently discard these will expose literal `<!--` text or drop the surrounding rule block entirely. The `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive width overrides may be non-functional.

**Triple font loading — redundant requests:**

Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded three times:
1. `<link>` tag to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. `@import` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face` declaration (also pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`)

This generates 6–9 redundant HTTP requests per client that supports web fonts in email (Apple Mail, some Outlook versions). It does not affect Gmail/Outlook Windows (which ignore web fonts), but adds load latency where it does work.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**

`db.onlinewebfonts.com` is a font redistribution service, not a first-party CDN. Availability is not guaranteed by Drunk Elephant's infrastructure. The first-party `BrownRegular` is correctly hosted on `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`; Bold, Light, and Thin variants should be migrated to the same host.

**Zoom/accessibility suppression:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Combined with `text-size-adjust:none`, users with low vision cannot enlarge content. Both should be removed; email clients handle zoom independently.
