# NEW ☁️ So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I know it's sixteen because I stopped being casual about it around email eight and started keeping count the way you track texts from someone who doesn't read the room. So when this drops on June 25th I am not a neutral viewer — I'm a subscriber who has been calculating whether e.l.f. has crossed the line, and this email lands right on it.
- Here's what's frustrating: the email itself is actually good. The "So Blurreal" name earns the cloud emoji rather than just tacking it on — blur plus unreal plus surreal in two syllables is clever. Eight shade names laid out with a gradient from "oat couture" to "fudge around" is the right level of detail before I decide whether to add this to a Target run. The "only at elfcosmetics.com & Target" exclusivity line is smart — I Target-run anyway, so this puts the product directly in my path without requiring a dedicated beauty errand.
- But I'm on day sixteen. The email isn't the problem; the volume is. I'd mark this as read without clicking, which I genuinely would not have said at email eight. I'm not forwarding it either. If a friend asked I'd mention it in a text, not forward the email, because I no longer trust that three more versions of this aren't going to hit my inbox before Sunday. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- "So Blurreal" is genuinely well-named — it threads blur finish, unreal quality, and cloud softness into a single word and the ☁️ emoji becomes functional instead of decorative.
- Eight shade names with visible gradient swatches (oat couture → cirrus-cloud → airy berry → fudge around) give me the color range before I click — I'm already scanning for my skintone instead of wondering.
- "Only at elfcosmetics.com & Target" is a meaningful callout; Target-exclusivity is a reason to act rather than wait for a Sephora launch.
- The "Top Picks for You" personalized section shows they're using my purchase history, not blasting cold — I notice that even when I'm fatigued.
- "Shipping's on us!" as a time-bounded add is easy to find and appropriately placed above the fold.

## What's weak

- No price point anywhere. E.l.f.'s entire brand equity is accessible pricing and they hid the number on a new launch. Charlotte Tilbury put "Over 50% Off" in its subject line; e.l.f. could have said "$10" in the hero and I'd have clicked twice as fast.
- "GET IT NOW" twice, both times. That CTA copy is doing nothing — it doesn't say what I'm getting or why now. "Shop So Blurreal" costs nothing and tells me something.
- Zero social proof. No star ratings, no "trending on TikTok," no "fan favorite already" copy. ILIA opened its Crown Affair collab email with influencer signal; e.l.f. ignores it entirely despite being the brand most tuned into social culture in this inbox.
- Day sixteen is the real miss and no email design fixes it. The cadence has already cost them my instinctive open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the price in the hero** — "$10" or "from $10" next to "So Blurreal" flips this from "interesting" to "grabbing one at Target tomorrow." E.l.f.'s biggest conversion advantage and it's nowhere.
- 2. **Rewrite both CTAs** — "Shop So Blurreal" over the shade section, "See All Shades" for the secondary. "GET IT NOW" reads like placeholder copy.
- 3. **Add social proof near the shade swatches** — even a "✨ Already going viral" line or a TikTok view count badge. The blur-matte format travels visually; show me it's landing before I land on a shade.
- 4. **Give the shipping offer a hard date** — "ships free through July 2" is three times more urgent than "limited time only."
- 5. **Acknowledge loyalty somewhere** — even one line: "Rouge members, grab it before it sells out." Sephora would have built a whole tier-access moment around this; e.l.f. treats me like a stranger on send sixteen.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot verify UTM parameters.** All links are wrapped in Braze server-side redirects (`links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...`). Final destination URLs and UTM attribution (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign) are not visible in source. Recommend decoding the primary CTA redirect to confirm UTM tagging is applied downstream.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo CTA anchor:**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
Explicitly removes this link from keyboard tab order. If the logo is a navigable CTA (it links to the site), this is incorrect. If intentionally decorative, the link itself should be `aria-hidden="true"` rather than using tabindex suppression.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Apple Mail (macOS/iOS) and some other clients surface the document title. Should be set to the subject line or brand name to avoid a blank tab/window label.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` in `<head>` — blocked by major clients:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook, and Yahoo strip external `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Fallback stack (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) activates in those clients — functionally acceptable, but `Jost` (loaded but not in the fallback stack) will silently drop in most environments. If `Jost` is used anywhere in the body, add it to the fallback or accept silent degradation.

**Global link style override in Outlook:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This suppresses all link underlines and inherits text color in Outlook/Windows Mail. Any linked text whose color is not explicitly set on the `<a>` element itself risks becoming invisible if it matches the background or surrounding text.

**Empty spacer tables (block-2, block-3, block-4):** Three consecutive `html_block` tables contain only whitespace and serve no layout function. Likely builder artifacts; they add byte overhead with no semantic value.

**Logo alt text minimal but present:** `alt="e.l.f."` — passes the baseline (non-empty), but could be more descriptive for screen readers (e.g., `"e.l.f. Cosmetics"`).
