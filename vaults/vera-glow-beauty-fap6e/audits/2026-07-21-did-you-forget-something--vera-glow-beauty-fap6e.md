# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Three times. This is the third "Did you forget something?" from Skechers in twenty days — July 1, July 15, and now July 21. By the second repeat I was training myself to ignore it; by the third it's wallpaper. What's genuinely frustrating is that my cart has some legitimately cute stuff in it — the Care Bears UNO collab is very much my wheelhouse as a millennial who grew up with that IP, and the pink Arch Fit Arcade is adorable. But Skechers is doing absolutely nothing to close the deal. No discount. No urgency. No "only 2 left in your size." Just the same shoes I already decided to leave behind, staring at me again.
- The layout is clean enough — three hero product shots, a big orange CTA, a "best sellers" scroll. But that best sellers grid skews visibly male (black athletic silhouettes, men's lifestyle shoes), which is a baffling choice for a cart that's full of Care Bears collab and pastel pink. That mismatch reads like a templated block nobody checked against who's actually receiving this send.
- I would not click. And if email number eight lands in my inbox this week with another recycled subject, I'm unsubscribing.

## What's working

- The Care Bears UNO collab is millennial nostalgia bait done right — feminine, playful, and the product actually earns screen real estate
- Three clean product shots of my exact cart items, labeled with full names — no guessing what I left behind
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is as direct a CTA as it gets, no "Discover" or "Explore" waffling
- Afterpay and Klarna visible in the footer — BNPL as a soft close is smart for a shopper in her late twenties watching her budget
- Order number in the header confirms this is session-specific, not a broadcast blast

## What's weak

- Zero incentive to complete the cart — no percentage off, no free shipping, no expiring promo code. Sally Beauty's recent "buy two get one free on select hair care" gave me a concrete reason to act; Skechers gave me nothing
- Best sellers grid is visibly male-coded — dark athletic sneakers and men's silhouettes sitting directly below my pastel Care Bears cart is a demographic whiplash
- No loyalty/points mention anywhere — Sephora Insider emails always surface my tier status and what I'm earning; this doesn't even hint that I'd rack up anything
- "Did you forget something?" has now appeared as my Skechers subject line three times in 20 days. The urgency signal is completely dead
- No scarcity in the body — they ran "This style has limited availability!" on July 16th and had the right instinct, then abandoned it entirely here

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a cart-recovery incentive with a hard deadline** — 10% off this order, code expires in 48 hours. ILIA built genuine click momentum with "The Gift of Glaze" by making the close feel like a reward; Skechers is just nagging
- 2. **Kill this subject line** — three appearances in 20 days, it has no lift left. Anything product-specific would outperform it
- 3. **Replace the best sellers grid with women's or Care Bears-adjacent styles** — I came to this email through a Care Bears cart; show me the rest of that family
- 4. **Add a scarcity signal under each cart item** — "Only 3 left in your size" below the Care Bears UNO would do more than the entire footer section
- 5. **Surface loyalty earning** — even one line: "Earn [X] points when you complete this order" with a tier name
