# Get a Jelly Pop Glow Stick on us! 🍉
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. This is e.l.f. email number thirteen since May 15 — I've been keeping count since the Survivor collab week — and walking into my Sunday inbox with a free Jelly Pop Glow Stick offer is, honestly, a smarter play than the last three sends combined. After back-to-back "6 HOURS ONLY" flash urgency emails on May 24 — the 2-for-$8 Sheer For It and the Halo Glow Liquid Filter one — a free gift hook is at least a different mechanic. The watermelon summer aesthetic actually reads like intentional product storytelling rather than another pink panic-urgency hero. I'm cautiously engaged. Which is more than I expected to say.
- That said, my goodwill for this brand is running critically low. The "on us" language in the subject is doing a lot of heavy lifting — I have absolutely no idea if this glow stick is genuinely free or "free with a $35+ purchase," and that ambiguity could easily flip from delight to disappointment at checkout. The email itself is scroll-heavy: hero, new arrivals shelf, a sale section, an app promo block, top picks, and then a full footer. By the time I hit "FUEL YOUR..." I've completely forgotten what the email was actually about.
- Would I click? Once, cautiously, just to confirm the terms. Would I forward to a friend? Only after I verify it's actually free. Would I unsubscribe? Getting closer every send.

## What's working

- The GWP mechanic is a genuine gear shift — after a week of flash countdown clocks, "here's something free" reads like a reward rather than a liquidation event
- Watermelon summer aesthetic is appropriately on-trend for late May; it doesn't feel like recycled copy the way Charlotte Tilbury's seventh straight "20% OFF!!" send did
- The Jelly Pop Glow Stick is featured prominently as the clear email star — visual hierarchy in the hero is clean and product-forward
- The new arrivals shelf below the fold (Power Grip setting spray, eye product, foundation) gives me useful discovery context without feeling like a random dump
- Inclusive model representation in the hero is consistent with what I expect from e.l.f. and lands right for me

## What's weak

- Thirteen emails since May 15 — at this send cadence, "on us" reads like a hostage negotiation to stop me from unsubscribing, not a loyalty moment
- The "on us" phrasing is vague enough to create real checkout disappointment; if there's a minimum spend trigger, it should be visible in the hero — Ulta's spend-and-save emails I reviewed earlier this week were explicit about "spend $50 get $10 off" and were better for it
- The email is scroll-city: stacking six distinct content modules below a free gift hero loses the thread completely — Glossier's "30% is still on" reminder I saw recently was a masterclass in single-message focus, and it worked
- Zero urgency signal anywhere — no deadline, no "while supplies last" — if this glow stick is genuinely free, give me a reason to act today rather than tab-closing and forgetting

## Recommendations

- 1. **Clarify the GWP condition in the hero** — "Free Jelly Pop Glow Stick with any $30 purchase" is more honest and still compelling; "on us" is a promise that can shatter at checkout
- 2. **Cut this email by 50%** — one story, one scroll depth: the glow stick leads, a single curated product below it, done; six modules dilute everything
- 3. **Add a credible deadline** — even "offer ends Monday" would give me a reason to click now rather than archive it
- 4. **At send #13, acknowledge the relationship** — the subject could do something the last dozen didn't, like speak to me as a real customer rather than another product announcement
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free Jelly Pop Glow Stick is waiting 🍉 — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We're dropping a free glow stick into your next order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed. Add it to your cart and it's yours — summer's must-have launch, on us.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Jelly Pop Glow Stick is the summer launch. Here's yours free — through Sunday only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email renders with standard table-based layout targeting MSO (Outlook VML) and mobile breakpoints. Three confirmed technical issues warrant immediate attention: a stale hardcoded preheader date, a body-scoped `<style>` block that will be stripped by Gmail, and a CDN provenance mismatch between the image host and the link tracking domain.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Possible ESP/CDN mismatch (moderate risk)**
- Click tracking routes through `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/.../~~` — the `/f/a/` path and double-tilde token pattern are characteristic of **Sailthru**.
- The logo image is served from **`braze-images.com`**: `https://braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91543698004cb67f6e/original.png?1697823889`
- Mixing image assets from a former ESP (Braze) with a current ESP's click-tracking (Sailthru) is a migration artifact risk. If the Braze account is deactivated or the asset is purged, the logo goes dark. The image timestamp `?1697823889` resolves to October 2023, confirming this is a legacy-hosted asset.

**Encoded destination URLs**
- All hrefs are Sailthru redirect URLs with base64-encoded payloads. Final landing page URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified from the source alone — confirmed only at redirect resolution time.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<style>` block inside `<body>` `<div>` (high risk for Gmail)**
- `html_block block-3` contains a `<style type="text/css">` block nested inside a `<div>`, not in `<head>`. Gmail's rendering engine strips `<style>` tags that appear in the body. The `.responsive-text` class (controlling `font-size: 14px` on mobile) will silently fail in Gmail for Android/Web, falling back to the inline `font-size: 12px` declaration on the `<p>` tag — smaller than intended on all viewports.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
- `<title></title>` — Screen readers that announce the document title will read nothing. Low severity but a quick fix.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the logo anchor**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
- Explicitly removed from the tab order. Keyboard-only users cannot navigate to the logo link. Should be removed (omitting `tabindex` entirely restores default browser behavior).

**Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>`**
- Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans"...><!--<![endif]-->`. Gmail strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`, so Open Sans will not load in Gmail Web/Android. The declared fallback stack (`Arial, Sans-serif`) handles this gracefully — no breakage, but worth documenting.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Hardcoded promo code**
- `KBWZ-P3DM-R74H-GTCK` appears as a literal string. If this is intended as a unique-per-recipient welcome code it is not personalized — it is a single broadcast code. If that is intentional, no issue. If it was meant to be a Sailthru merge variable (e.g. `{promo_code}`), this is a critical send error.

**No visible unresolved merge token failures** in the truncated source. No `{{`, `{%`, or `*|` patterns found unfired.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Stale preheader date (confirmed)**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;...">HURRY! Ends today, 5/25.</div>
```
- Preheader hardcodes "Ends today, 5/25." Today is **2026-05-26**. Recipients opening this email on or after 5/26 see a false urgency claim in their inbox preview — a potential FTC deceptive advertising concern, not merely a copy issue.

**CAN-SPAM footer / unsubscribe**
- HTML is truncated before the footer. Cannot confirm physical mailing address or one-click unsubscribe link are present. These are required fields — must be verified against the full source.

**Authentication headers**
- Sending domain `m.elfcosmetics.com` — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment cannot be assessed from HTML source alone. Verify via raw message headers (`Authentication-Results` field from inbox).
