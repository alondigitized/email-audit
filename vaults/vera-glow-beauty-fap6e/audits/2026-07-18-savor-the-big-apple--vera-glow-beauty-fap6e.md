# Savor the Big Apple 🍎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Seven emails in eleven days, and I want to say upfront: I've been *with* Glossier on this one. The July 7 tease earned it, the July 14 "Meet I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom" launch moment earned it, and even the slightly redundant "NEW!" resend on July 15 got a pass because the product was real. But "Savor the Big Apple" is unmistakably chapter five of a campaign that peaked two chapters ago. We're now in the merch-and-accessories coda — an Appleberry keychain and a Big Apple cotton tee — which is classic Glossier: extend the IP into lifestyle before the hype fully cools. The hero shot is genuinely gorgeous: the Balm Dotcom tube resting against two perfect lacquered-red apples is minimal and confident, very much their language.
- What actually made me stop was the "PSST. You deserve some free stickers" section. "Yes, any order" is such a disarmingly casual brand move, and the I ❤️ NY sticker sheet visible at the bottom of the email is exactly the kind of thing Glossier gets right — tactile, collectible, cheap to produce, and they make it feel like a gift instead of a loyalty mechanic. Contrast that with the hero CTA "Take a bite," which is fun copywriting and absolutely useless as a button. Take a bite of *what*? Take me *where*?
- I'd click "Claim yours" for the stickers and probably text the keychain photo to my friend who would definitely buy it. I would not buy the tee. I'd mark this one as read before finishing it if the sticker section were at the bottom instead of the middle.

## What's working

- **Product-meets-fruit hero**: Balm Dotcom tube next to two shiny red apples is simple and earned — the Big Apple concept lands without being over-explained.
- **"PSST. You deserve some free stickers"**: Casual headline, "yes, any order" framing, "Claim yours" CTA — this is actually the most compelling conversion moment in the email and it reads like a friend slipping you a bonus, not a brand attaching terms.
- **LIMITED EDITION stamps on both merch items**: Clear, above the product names, credible given the campaign context. Creates real urgency without being loud about it.
- **NYC street photography**: The yellow cab / duty-free shot is editorial and evocative — adds texture without feeling like a stock library pull.
- **"Sweet, crisp and juicy red apples" copy block**: Glossier's playful product mythology writing is better than almost anyone doing it right now; the orchard origin story makes me smile even on read seven.

## What's weak

- **"Take a bite" as the primary hero CTA**: Evocative as copy, completely inert as a button. Drunk Elephant's "Hot takes" had me clicking on vibes alone because the CTA still told me what I was clicking *into*. "Take a bite" doesn't.
- **Merch as the content spine at send seven**: I bought (or am about to buy) the actual Balm Dotcom — pivoting immediately to "now buy the keychain" makes me feel like I'm being monetized in waves rather than served. e.l.f. would never let the accessories outrun the hero product this fast.
- **No pricing signal anywhere**: Not even a nod. Sephora would have had "Earn 4× points on I ❤️ NY products through Sunday" right here, and I would have done the Rouge math in under three seconds. Glossier doesn't have that program, which is fine — but there's *no* offer that makes today feel different from any other day, other than the soft "supplies last" on stickers.
- **Visual hierarchy diffuses mid-scroll**: Hero → NYC editorial photo (no product) → merch grid → copy block → GWP. Five distinct sections fighting for my attention before I've made a single decision. ILIA's "Beauty pre-check" flat-lay kept one visual lane open start to finish; this email has lane changes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote the sticker GWP above the fold** — make it the urgency engine, not the PSST footnote. "Free I ❤️ NY sticker sheet with any order — while they last" in the hero subtext or as a persistent banner would pull people in who missed the Balm Dotcom launch and give repeat openers a concrete new reason to buy now.
- 2. **Rewrite "Take a bite" to a destination-specific CTA** — "Shop Appleberry Balm Dotcom" or "Get yours" are both low-lift and land you with intent. The playful voice doesn't have to die; just pair it with a real noun.
- 3. **Feature one merch item, link to the rest** — the keychain is more impulse-buy-sized than a tee. Lead with the keychain hero and "See all NYC merch →" to reduce the grid's decision tax.
- 4. **Acknowledge the cadence somewhere** — even a single line like "We know we've been in your inbox — here's why this week was worth it" buys enormous goodwill by send seven. Lush never does this and it's one of the reasons their emails start to feel like spam by email twenty-nine.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free stickers with any order — while they last 🍎`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The I ❤️ NY extras you actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited-edition merch + a free NYC sticker sheet. Claim both before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your free I ❤️ NY sticker sheet is waiting — any order qualifies.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking element — `<span rel="stylesheet">`**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXS8D17FT31KQN6ABSDD4WEC_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This appears to be a Klaviyo open-tracking pixel inserted as a span rather than a proper `<img>` or `<link>` tag. The construct is semantically invalid per HTML/XHTML spec. Some clients will silently ignore it; others may surface it as a broken resource request or strip it entirely, breaking open-rate tracking.

**Expected form:** a 1×1 `<img>` tag, or nothing — Klaviyo's own templates use `<img>` for pixel tracking.

**UTM parameters on destination URLs:** Cannot confirm — the link list is not visible in the truncated source. Verify that all `https://glossier.com/...` destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters after Klaviyo redirect unwrapping.
