# PSA—mini totes are back
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- The day after ILIA's "Ends Tonight" email wrapped their five-day Buy 3, Save 20% run — which I have to say was some of the better sale creative I've seen this summer, between the dice on day two and rock-paper-scissors on day three — they pivoted hard. No extensions, no "last chance" retread. Just: the mini tote is back. The reset is smart, and the dusty rose hero image is genuinely pretty. The "PSA" framing lands the way it's supposed to — signals demand, positions the email as insider news rather than blast marketing, makes me feel like I'm being tipped off rather than marketed to.
- That said, I am now six ILIA emails deep in seven days. I tracked every single send in their sale week and now they're immediately asking me for $75. The goodwill math is still in their favor — ILIA earns its opens — but this is the boundary zone. The product grid does the heavy lifting once I'm in: six curated travel essentials gives me a "what can I buy to hit $75" shopping list on a platter. But "tiny window of time" is doing no work as urgency copy. Give me a date.
- I'd click. I'd tell a friend. I'd also wonder, as a Sephora Rouge member, whether I can just buy through Sephora and get my points there instead — and ILIA's DTC email never gives me a reason not to.

## What's working

- **The hero-to-offer pipeline is immediate** — banner reads "FREE MINI TOTE WITH $75+", paired with a hero image of the actual tote, before I've scrolled an inch
- **"Get Your Fill: Travel essentials to tuck in your tote"** — smart framing that turns a six-product grid into a curated kit, making the spend threshold feel achievable rather than arbitrary
- **Creative pivot is sharp** — where yesterday's "Ends Tonight" lived entirely in urgency, this steps back into brand aspiration; neither email feels like the other, which is rare discipline for a brand in sale mode
- **ILIA logo on the tote itself** — the gift does branding work as a physical object; better than a blank canvas tote
- **Planet Partner 2x / VIP 2x at the footer** — it's there, I'd scroll to find it, and it matters to me

## What's weak

- **"Only for a tiny window of time" is not a deadline** — Drunk Elephant sent me an editorial with no offer at all last week and it was less frustrating than vague urgency language; just give me a date
- **"TOTE TIME →" is charming but toothless as a CTA** — it's a noun phrase, not a directive; compare to Sally Beauty's "SHOP NOW" which is bland but at least tells me what to do
- **Loyalty math is buried in the footer** — I'm the customer who opens a spreadsheet for points; showing "2x VIP = $X back on a $75 spend" in the email body would close me faster than anything
- **No tote specs** — dimensions? quantity available? while-supplies-last language? The covet factor is real but it's incomplete
- **DTC-vs-Sephora tension unaddressed** — ILIA is sold at Sephora; I'm Rouge; this email never gives me a reason to buy direct instead of there

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a hard end date in the hero** — swap "But only for a tiny window of time" for "Free tote with $75+ — through Sunday, July 9" and the urgency becomes real
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA** — "SHOP TO CLAIM YOURS →" or "ADD TO YOUR $75 →" beats "TOTE TIME" for conversion
- 3. **Surface the points math above the fold** — a single line like "ILIA VIP? This $75 order = 2x points + a free tote" pulls double duty on loyalty members
- 4. **Add tote specs or a "while supplies last" counter** — scarcity is the whole play here; let the scarcity do the work explicitly
- 5. **Give the product grid one "bestseller" signal** — a small badge or star count on the Skin Tint or Skin Blur would do what the rest of the email can't: social proof at decision time
- **Subject Alt A:** `PSA: Free mini tote with any $75+ order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free ILIA tote is back (for now)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `6 travel essentials to fill it with — shop before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Offer ends Sunday · VIP members earn 2x on every dollar`

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html

```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers cannot infer document language; also flags in WCAG audits.

**`@import` for external fonts will be stripped by Gmail and several other clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail, Outlook (desktop), and Yahoo strip `@import` rules. Fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is present, so degradation is graceful — but Montserrat will not render in Gmail. If brand font consistency is required, inline the `@font-face` block instead of using `@import`.

This is a known padding technique. Some aggressive spam filters flag dense non-printing Unicode runs. Low risk with Klaviyo's sending reputation, but worth noting.
