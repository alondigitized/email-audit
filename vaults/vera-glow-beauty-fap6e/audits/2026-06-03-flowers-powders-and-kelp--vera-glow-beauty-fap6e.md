# Flowers, powders, and kelp
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one ILIA sends in roughly three weeks — and here's the day-after ingredient education email for the Soft Focus Finishing Powder they just dropped yesterday. I have to be honest: I kind of love ingredient breakdown content. The four-panel setup — soft focus complex, edelweiss squalane, winged kelp extract, plant-based squalane — reads like a well-produced Glossier editorial rather than a promotional blast, and the close-up model photography is genuinely beautiful. I'm *interested.*
- But here's the thing: I already got the launch moment yesterday. That June 2 send had the "new thing dropped" energy. This one is asking me to go deeper on a product I haven't bought yet — without a single incentive to actually convert. No price. No percentage off. No "Rouge members earn points this week on new launches." Nothing. Glossier's "hoodie origin story" email had the same problem — great brand storytelling, zero conversion hook — and it landed a 7/10 from me for exactly that reason. This email has better photography and more useful content than that Glossier send, but it has the same structural hole.
- I'll click to browse and wishlist. I will absolutely not buy today. I'm a Rouge member running points math on when the next Sephora Beauty Insider Cash event lands, and ILIA gave me nothing to accelerate that decision. Bookmark and wait.

## What's working

- The four-ingredient breakdown layout is genuinely editorial — each panel has a visual, a name, and a clear benefit sentence, which is closer to a product page than a promo blast and actually teaches me something
- Model photography is excellent for my demographic — warm brown skin, natural glow, inclusive beauty framing that doesn't feel stock-photo generic or airbrushed into oblivion
- "SHOP SOFT FOCUS →" is one of the better CTA lines I've seen in this run — compare Charlotte Tilbury's recent "Discover" button, which is useless; this CTA actually names the product
- The trust-signal cluster on the right side — "Safe for sensitive skin," "Non-comedogenic," "Dermatologist tested" — does real work for a powder I've never used before
- Two CTAs placed logically (after hero, after ingredients breakdown) give me natural off-ramps without feeling like I'm being poked three times per scroll

## What's weak

- Zero offer, zero urgency — Drunk Elephant's "Bestsellers await you" email had an anonymous subject but still surfaced a clear shop incentive; ILIA gave me a beautiful brand story with no reason to buy *today* over any other day
- No loyalty callout whatsoever — I'm a Rouge member, this is a Sephora-carried brand, and the email doesn't acknowledge that I earn points on this purchase; that single line is worth a conversion for people like me
- The subject line "Flowers, powders, and kelp" is clever if you're actively tracking this launch, but anyone who missed the June 2 email has no idea this is a finishing powder email — it reads like a florist newsletter
- Day-after cadence with no new information or offer starts to feel like filler — e.l.f. hit 21 sends in a similar window and I felt the exact same "why is this in my inbox right now" fatigue

- 1. **Add one loyalty hook** — a single line like "Earn points on new launches this week" or a "Rouge member exclusive first look" tag transforms this from a pretty read to an actual purchase trigger; this is the highest-leverage move
- 2. **Give the subject line more specificity without losing the intrigue** — keep the unexpected ingredient angle but name the product category so non-tracking subscribers know why they're opening
- 3. **Add a social proof micro-element** — Drunk Elephant and Charlotte Tilbury both drop star ratings or quote counts in their educational emails; even "4.8 stars in the first 48 hours" would close the gap between "this sounds cool" and "add to cart"
- 4. **Tie to a deadline or seasonal hook** — "Summer's softest powder, arriving just in time" or a simple launch-window scarcity signal gives me a reason to act now vs. bookmarking indefinitely

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) display the `<title>` value in tab labels or accessibility trees. A blank title is a missed label.

**No hover affordance on links**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover state is identical to default state — no underline, no color shift. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color) for link identification without relying solely on color.

**Google Fonts `@import` will not load in Gmail**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `<style>` block `@import` rules. Montserrat will silently fall back to Helvetica for Gmail recipients. This is expected behavior with external fonts in email, but worth documenting. Also uses the older CSS1 API path — not a functional issue since `display=swap` still works, but the CSS2 API (`family=Montserrat:ital,wght@...`) is more expressive if weights/italics are needed.

**Klaviyo custom font import**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Second external font dependency with the same client-support caveat. If this is serving the same Montserrat, it is redundant alongside the Google Fonts import.
