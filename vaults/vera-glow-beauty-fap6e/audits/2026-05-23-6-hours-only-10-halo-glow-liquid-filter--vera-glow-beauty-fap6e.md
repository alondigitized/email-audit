# 6 HOURS ONLY ‼️ $10 Halo Glow Liquid Filter!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is e.l.f. email number ten-plus since May 15 — I stopped counting somewhere between the Survivor collab and the lip launch double-send on May 21, but the math is not good. So when this hit my inbox this morning my first move was the smallest eye-roll, not because the offer is bad but because I am exhausted. Then I actually registered the subject line: the Halo Glow Liquid Filter at $10 is a genuine deal on one of e.l.f.'s most-loved products. No code, a specific PST window, a verifiable discount. That's how a flash sale should run. The May 18 Rosé Setting Spray send earned a 10 from me and a lot of that was the same discipline — product-first, specific mechanics, no mystery.
- The problem is structural. This email is three different emails duct-taped together: a 6-hour flash sale, a Memorial Day weekend free gift offer, and a Target receipt-scanning loyalty pitch. Each section has its own urgency signature (or lack of one), and they fight each other. The flash sale hero bleeds out by the time you get to the evergreen Target rewards section.
- Would I click? Yes, on the HGLF hero, because I already know the product and the $10 price closes the deal before I even scroll. Would I forward? No. Would I feel good about it? Not particularly — this email treats me like a conversion target, not like a person who gave this brand a near-perfect score five days ago.

## What's working

- **Flash sale mechanics are clean**: specific time window (5–11PM PST), exact discount math ($5 off → $10), no code needed. That's the Drunk Elephant F&F playbook but compressed into six hours — airtight.
- **Product choice is the right hero**: HGLF is a franchise product, not a filler SKU. If you're going to flash-sale something, flash-sale the thing people actually have saved to their wishlist.
- **Free gift secondary offer is additive**: $8 jelly pop glow stick, code GLOW, visible and legible. It extends value over the Memorial Day weekend without cannibalizing the lead urgency.
- **Visual hierarchy lands immediately**: eyes go products → "6 hours to score!" → offer copy → CTA. No buried lede, no scroll required on the hero block.
- **Beauty Squad + Afterpay both visible in the footer**: two signals I consciously track as a cross-shopper doing points math.

## What's weak

- **Zero shade information on the flash sale hero**: I own at least three HGLF shades. Not showing me which are included — or a shade grid at all — means I have to click through just to find out if my shade is even in the deal. Sephora's sale emails would surface the exact shade range on the hero image itself.
- **Three messaging zones at three different urgency levels**: the 6-hour window screams urgency, the Memorial Day glow stick offer is weekend-long and relaxed, and the Target receipt section is basically evergreen. They shouldn't share an email.
- **"SHOP NOW" is lazy CTA copy next to a named product at a named price**: Drunk Elephant wrote "Stock up at 25% off" on their F&F emails — specific verb, specific deal. "SHOP NOW" next to a $10 HGLF is a missed layup.
- **"Top Picks for You" grid is stale**: Power Grip Primer ($11), Plant-Based Sponge ($12), Clean Glide Lip Liner ($3) — I have seen this exact product trio in e.l.f. emails before. It reads like an automated fallback, not a curated recommendation.
- **"6 HOURS ONLY" in ALL CAPS + ‼️ in the subject**: the May 18 Rosé spray email didn't need any of that — it let the product name carry the opening. The ALL CAPS reads as panic, not excitement, and the double exclamation emoji is the kind of signal that makes Charlotte Tilbury's inbox presence feel more refined by comparison.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a shade row to the flash sale hero**: even three or four HGLF swatch chips with labels would tell me whether this deal is worth a click. That's the single change with the highest conversion upside.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA to name the product and price**: "Get HGLF for $10 →" or "Claim Your $10 Glow →" earns the urgency the headline promises. "SHOP NOW" doesn't.
- 3. **Cut the Target receipt section from this send**: put it in a dedicated loyalty email. A 6-hour flash sale cannot afford cognitive detours.
- 4. **Replace the "Top Picks" grid with shade-specific HGLF thumbnails**: link each shade directly to its PDP, not to the category page. That's how Sephora handles shade-range emails and it's why their click rate on product launches is reliably higher.
- 5. **Cool the subject line temperature**: drop the ALL CAPS and the ‼️. The product name and price do the work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 Halo Glow Liquid Filter — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: your fave Halo Glow for $10`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed. Shop 5–11PM PST today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `6 hours to grab it — no code, just $10.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Many spam filters penalize missing title tags. Set to a descriptive value (e.g., the subject line).

**Google Fonts `<link>` tags load in non-MSO clients but are stripped by Gmail**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail's rendering engine strips `<link>` tags from the `<head>` in all display modes. The font stacks (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` and `'Futura Std', Futura, 'Jost', Verdana, sans-serif`) will correctly fall back, but any pixel-precise typographic design relying on Jost or Open Sans will degrade in Gmail (the dominant client for this sender segment).

**Nested full email template inside `html_block`**
Inside `block-1`, a complete `nl-container` table structure is re-embedded — including its own `row`, `row-content`, `column`, and a second full `image_block`. This looks like the outer wrapper was not stripped before the block was inserted. Some email clients will double-render or apply conflicting widths.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints**
- Main `<style>` in `<head>`: `@media (max-width:660px)`
- Inline `<style>` inside `html_block` (block-3): `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)`

These two breakpoints trigger at different viewport widths. Elements in block-3 won't collapse until 480px while the rest of the layout collapses at 660px — visible seam on mid-size screens (480–660px range, common on tablets).

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link**
```html
<a href="…" target="_blank" style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
```
This removes the logo link from keyboard tab order entirely, failing WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 2.1.1 (Keyboard). Combined with `style="outline:none"`, any keyboard user cannot access the primary CTA if it is this link.

**Sparse alt text on hero image**
```html
<img … alt="e.l.f." title="e.l.f.">
```
The hero image is the primary communication vehicle for the offer. Alt text `"e.l.f."` provides no offer context for screen reader users or image-blocked clients. Should reflect the email offer (e.g., `"$10 Halo Glow Liquid Filter — App Exclusive, 6 hours only"`).

**`<style>` block injected inside `html_block` body (block-3)**
A `<style type="text/css">` block defining `.responsive-text` appears inside the email body HTML, not in the `<head>`. Gmail's inbox rendering (non-Promotions tab) strips body-level `<style>` blocks in some modes. The `.responsive-text` rule may be silently ignored.
