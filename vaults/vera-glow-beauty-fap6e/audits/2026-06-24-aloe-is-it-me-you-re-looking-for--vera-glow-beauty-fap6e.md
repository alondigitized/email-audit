# Aloe, is it me you’re looking for? 🌿
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. I'm at twenty Lush emails this month and I am not keeping count casually anymore — I'm keeping count the way you keep count of texts from someone who doesn't read the room. The pun is genuinely good. "Aloe, is it me you're looking for?" is Lionel Richie-coded in a way that made me smile before I even tapped, and "ALOE AND BEHOLD" as the hero headline is peak Lush brand voice. I get it, I appreciate the wordplay, and then I scroll through seven products and a bath bomb grid and realize... there's nothing to *do* here. No offer. No event. No points bonus. No urgency. Just: here is aloe. It is summer. Please buy.
- As someone who does the points math before she adds anything to cart, this is an email I appreciate aesthetically and immediately close. The Sanrio collab at #18 earned its place because it was actually news. This one is a product showcase with a cute subject line. That's a different thing.
- I'd mark this as read without clicking. Would not forward — nothing to tell a friend about that they couldn't find themselves browsing the site.

## What's working

- **"ALOE AND BEHOLD"** — hero headline is confident, legible, and completely on-brand; Lush nails the pun-as-identity better than anyone in my inbox right now
- **Subject line pun lands** — "Aloe, is it me you're looking for?" is mobile-friendly, charming, and under 40 chars; it got me to open when email #20 absolutely did not deserve it
- **Product grid is organized** — Grease Lightning, Skin Drink, Argan, Honey I Washed The Kids: legible names, clear images, no broken assets or layout chaos
- **Summer-relevant angle** — aloe is the right ingredient story for late June; it's at least seasonally coherent
- **"A SOLID CHOICE" section** — the bath bomb/soap array at the bottom is visually fun and breaks the monotony of the 2-col grid

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero math** — no GWP, no % off, no loyalty points tie-in; for a Rouge/Diamond cross-shopper, this email has nothing to act on; Charlotte Tilbury's sale emails and even Drunk Elephant's "ultimate hack" send both gave me a reason to click
- **Generic CTAs throughout** — "Shop Now" and "Learn More" appear on everything, telling me nothing about why *this product* at *this moment*; e.l.f.'s recent hair launch gave each CTA a specific verb + noun and it showed
- **No loyalty hook** — Lush has a rewards program; it is completely invisible here; Ulta buries it too but at least they *mention* the Rewards tier
- **Product copy is too small** — the descriptions under each product tile are rendering at a size that requires zooming on mobile; I should not have to work
- **Email #20 without a reason to exist** — cadence fatigue is real; the Sanrio email justified its send with actual news; this is filler

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a hook that means something** — if there's no sale, make the hook editorial: "Aloe is the ingredient your summer routine is missing — here's how we formulate it differently." Give me information I can use, not just product tiles.
- 2. **One featured product with depth** — Grease Lightning deserved the hero slot with a real ingredient story and a link to reviews, not a 2-line blurb; seven equal-weight products = zero products
- 3. **Add a loyalty moment** — even a line like "Lush Labs members: double stamps this week" would give me a reason to click; right now there's nothing
- 4. **Trim the grid** — "A SOLID CHOICE" bath bomb section feels like a second email stapled on; one strong aloe narrative, not two separate pitches
- 5. **Kill one of the two CTAs per product** — "Shop Now" vs "Learn More" on every tile creates decision paralysis; pick one intent per product
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your summer skin called. It wants aloe.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Grease Lightning → the aloe gel that actually works`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The ingredient your routine's been missing — plus the products that actually use it right.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Aloe-infused picks for summer skin. No filler, just the good stuff.`
