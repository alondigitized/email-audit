# Last call: 20% off VIP sale
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- Marissa's back — for the fourth time in about a week (9/14, 9/17, 9/20, and now 9/22... I mean 9/23) — with the exact same plain-text personal-email cosplay. No hero image, no product shot, no logo, no color, nothing that says "beauty brand" instead of "email from a coworker." At this point I'm not even annoyed by the format anymore, I'm just numb to it — it's become the ILIA house style and it's a bad one.
- The actual offer buried in there is decent — 20% off sitewide, free Multi-Stick, free Velvet Makeup Bag at $100+, up to 40% off select — but it's wrapped in bullet-point plain text that reads like a Google Doc, not a "final hours" sale email that should feel urgent. And the ask is bizarre: this is a "VIP sale" but you have to sign up for VIP (free) to access it, which is a weird ask to bury this deep in copy with zero visual weight on the join step.
- I'd skim it because "final hours" caught my eye, but I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here worth sharing, no visual to screenshot for a friend. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- "20% off sitewide" is bolded and the one visual anchor in an otherwise flat wall of text, so my eye does land there first.
- The perks list is genuinely well-structured — free Multi-Stick, free Velvet Makeup Bag at $100+, subscription discount, up to 40% off — that's a real stacked offer if you can parse it.
- "Redeemable points on every purchase" and "Early access to launches + sales" at least gestures at loyalty-program value, which matters to someone like me who's already doing points math for Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond.

## What's weak

- Zero product photography, zero brand color, zero logo — this is the same Marissa-cosplay plain text ILIA ran on 9/20 ("This only happens once a year"), 9/19, and 9/17. Compare that to ILIA's own 9/19 send, "Safe for sensitive eyes," which had that genuinely styled flat-lay hero — they clearly know how to do visual, they're just not doing it here.
- The core mechanic is confusing: it's called a "VIP sale" but you have to become a (free) VIP member mid-email to unlock it — that's a two-step ask hidden in prose, not surfaced as a clear CTA.
- Sephora's "4X points on fragrance" send hits me with a concrete, quantified loyalty number in the subject; Ulta's "20% off one item" leads with the barcode-hero and a code. ILIA gives me no code, no visual, and asks me to sign up for something before I even see what I'm buying.
- "This sale is about to check out for good" is a weirdly vague urgency line — no actual deadline, no countdown, nothing time-stamped, especially odd for a "last call" subject.
- No demographic or personalization signal at all — no name, no skin-tone/shade callout, nothing that treats me like a known Rouge/Diamond-tier shopper instead of "friend."

## Recommendations

- 1. Build an actual hero image — a product shot of the Multi-Stick and Velvet Makeup Bag GWPs front and center, because that's literally what's driving the offer and it's invisible right now. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Put a real countdown or explicit deadline (e.g., "ends tonight at midnight ET") instead of "about to check out for good" — vague urgency reads as filler, not pressure.
- 3. Lead with the VIP-signup CTA as its own button, separate from the shopping CTA — right now "join VIP" and "shop the sale" are tangled together in one paragraph.
- 4. Drop a code or explicit "no code needed, applied at checkout" line — Ulta and Sephora both make this crystal clear and ILIA leaves it ambiguous.
- 5. Retire the Marissa personal-email format for sale sends specifically — save it for smaller "friend" nudges, use real creative for anything claiming "VIP sale" or "last call."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Multi-Stick, free makeup bag at $100+, up to 40% off — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Join free VIP now to unlock 20% off sitewide before it's gone`
