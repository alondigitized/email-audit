# 💫 Layering just makes good scents
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twelve. This is email twelve from Lush in just over three weeks — and I say that not to dismiss it, because the fragrance-layering concept is genuinely the most editorial send they've done since the Karma email on May 22. Organizing the whole email around mood families — Love & Confidence, Energy & Joy, Calm & Comfort — and then showing specific product pairings within each is smart beauty thinking. It's the kind of framework I actually use at the Sephora fragrance wall: anchor on a vibe, then build out.
- That said, there is zero offer here. No percent off, no GWP, no loyalty event — just editorial content with a generic "Explore Fragrances" CTA. And since Lush doesn't have a points program I'm stacking like Sephora Rouge benefits, I can't even do the math on whether clicking makes financial sense right now. For a brand that's already burned through my attention budget with eleven prior sends, an editorial-only email has to hit a lot harder to earn the click.
- I'd mark this as read with mild interest and probably screenshot the mood-family framework for my own reference. I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward — but I wouldn't unsubscribe over it either.

## What's working

- The **mood-family architecture** (Love & Confidence / Energy & Joy / Calm & Comfort) is a legitimately useful fragrance-shopping framework — better product merchandising than what Charlotte Tilbury's digest wrapper pulled off in their last send.
- **"BETTER TOGETHER"** hero copy is clean, punchy, and doesn't over-explain — the visual does the work.
- The **"NEW, LIMITED EDITION SCENTS"** section with Banoffee Pie and Million Dollar is buried at the bottom but it's the most compelling pull in the email — new launches always get a second look from me.
- The **aesthetic** (neon lighting, bold color blocking per section) is fully Lush and matches the age/vibe of who they're talking to — I don't feel like this was designed for my mom.
- No visible render bugs — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** No discount, no GWP, no event tie-in. Lush has no loyalty program I'm earning points in, so purely editorial content with no financial hook doesn't give me a reason to click today instead of never.
- **"Explore Fragrances" CTA is doing the bare minimum** — compared to e.l.f.'s "$7 cream bronzer, shop now" that made me actually stop scrolling, this is a directional shrug.
- **The limited-edition scents are below the fold** — that's backwards. If Banoffee Pie is new and limited, lead with it. ILIA put their new Skin Tint duo in the hero and it worked; same logic applies here.
- **Cadence is unsustainable.** Twelve emails in three weeks means even a solid concept like this one feels routine. Sally Beauty's "Last Call" trick already burned my goodwill on inflated urgency — Lush's frequency is doing the same thing to my patience without even needing the fake urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the limited-edition scents to the hero.** "Banoffee Pie is new. It won't last." — that's the hook that earns the click, not mood categories.
- 2. **Add a concrete reason to shop today** — even a "free shipping over $X" or "new arrivals, just dropped" framing gives me a peg. The mood architecture is editorial, not transactional, and for email twelve I need both.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific to the limited editions** — "Shop Banoffee Pie" or "Grab it before it's gone" beats "Explore Fragrances" every time.
- 4. **Reduce email frequency or segment harder** — a fragrance-only send to confirmed fragrance purchasers at half the current cadence would land much better than blanket blasts.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your new signature scent is probably one of these`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New, limited-edition scents + how to layer them for your mood`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Million Dollar. Banoffee Pie. Two new scents, very limited stock.`
