# ✨ The root of good hair
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six. I have now received twenty-six Lush emails, and this one — "The root of good hair" — is genuinely a pivot I wasn't expecting. After two summer-beach-travel emails back to back (July 4 and July 7), they switched lanes entirely into a scalp-care editorial. I will give credit where it's due: the hero creative is clean, the "ROOT FOR YOUR HAIR" wordplay is cute without being cringey, and the product segmentation — Scalp Saviors up top, length-focused products below — actually makes editorial sense. This is the most organized Lush email I've seen in weeks.
- That said, as someone who does points math before she does literally anything else in a beauty email, there is no offer here. No percentage, no gift-with-purchase, no free shipping threshold. This is pure content marketing dressed up as a promo, and the absence of any deal is glaring when Sally Beauty just ran summer hair care with a clear discount hook and Drunk Elephant is out here doing "20-minute hack" editorial *with* clickable SKUs attached to savings. Lush is asking me to shop at full price with zero urgency and no loyalty reward signal.
- I would click "Shop Scalp Care" because I've been meaning to try the Roots treatment — but that's me, not the email doing the work. I would not forward this. Mark as read with a mild "okay, interesting" energy.

## What's working

- **Hero is the clearest Lush has been in weeks** — "ROOT FOR YOUR HAIR" is front and center, models show real hair texture, the green palette reads instantly as Lush without being garish.
- **Product segmentation is smart** — Scalp Saviors (Roots, Ginger) as one category, length/shine products (Jumping Juniper, Flower's Elbow Soufflé, Squeak, Superbalsam) as another. That's a logical editorial split most brand emails skip entirely.
- **Diverse hair types and models** — I can see curly, wavy, and straight hair represented across sections. That matters for a hair care email. Sally Beauty's summer send showed a narrower casting and felt generic by comparison.
- **"Top Tips" video section at the bottom** — educational anchor adds credibility and gives me a reason to engage beyond the buy button.
- **No render bugs, clean layout** — after the Ulta "Beauty Break / this event is over" disaster, a clean render feels like a win in itself.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero urgency** — I am a Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond. I do the math. There is no math to do here. Lush is asking me to click through to full-price scalp care on a random Wednesday with no hook. Drunk Elephant's July 8 editorial at least had a structured product recommendation anchored to an actionable moment.
- **The hero quote block is muddy at email scale** — whatever customer quote or copy lives in that green callout box under the hero is not readable without zooming in. The hierarchy breaks right after the CTA button.
- **No loyalty/member signal** — I have clearly been on this list for months (twenty-six emails!). Not a single send from Lush has ever told me I have points, a tier, anything. It's treating me like a cold prospect on send twenty-six, and that gap gets more annoying the longer the relationship goes.
- **Cadence catching up with itself** — three emails in seven days and now four in nine days. Even a good email feels like inbox pressure at this volume. Glossier ran a teaser arc that built anticipation; this just lands.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add any offer at all** — even free shipping on scalp orders, a bundle, or a loyalty double-point moment. Right now this email asks for a click and gives nothing back. A "3 for 2 on scalp care" or "free mini Ginger with $40" would convert this from browse to buy.
- 2. **Make the quote/callout box legible** — whatever copy lives in that green box needs to be bigger or cut entirely. Right now it's visual noise between the hero and the CTA.
- 3. **Acknowledge the subscriber relationship** — "You've been with us long enough to know your scalp deserves this" or any nod to being a known customer. Lush has the send history; use it.
- 4. **Tie scalp care to July specifically** — summer sweat, heat, sun exposure on your part line. Sally Beauty did it with "summer hair care essentials" framing; Lush left the seasonal hook completely on the table.
- 5. **Reduce cadence or stagger content vs. promo sends** — this editorial belongs in a bi-weekly digest format, not sandwiched between two beach-emoji promo emails with no breathing room.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) render `<title>` as the browser tab label. An empty value produces a blank or domain-derived tab title. Should contain a descriptive string matching the subject line.

**Mobile breakpoint at 520px (non-standard)**
The single media query fires at `max-width:520px`. The de facto email standard is 600px. Clients rendering at 521–600px viewport width will receive the desktop layout, which may overflow on mid-size screens (e.g., tablets in portrait, some Android clients).

**Link underline suppression in Gmail**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This strips underlines from all anchors inside Gmail's `#MessageViewBody`. Combined with body-text-colored links, this fails WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color as the only visual differentiator). If CTAs rely on button styling this is lower risk, but inline text links become indistinguishable.
