# 20% off ends in 3, 2, 1...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth ILIA send for the same 20% offer, and I've lost count of how many "last chance" emails I've had from them. I had "Add 3, subtract 20%" on 10/9, "Buy 3, save 20%—limited time only" on 10/10, the plain-text "Friend, your 20% off trio awaits" on 10/11, and now this one. The creative is clean and the offer is obvious. But "last chance" lost its force somewhere around the third send.
- The hero does its job. "buy 3 = 20% off" is huge, the code BUNDLEUP is in the top banner, and the button says "Add 3 to cart." I can do that math in two seconds. After that, there's nothing. I get no product names, no prices, and no shade info. The big "ILIA Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" tile is a logo block that does nothing for me. I also get no VIP points mention, so I can't compare this to my Sephora or Ulta stacking math.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I'd been holding off on a skin tint. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read, because I already know the code and the deal from 10/9. I'll probably buy the three things I want on the last day if I still care, and not because of this email.

## What's working

- "buy 3 = 20% off" is huge in the hero, and the code is repeated in the banner and in the body. I never have to hunt for it.
- The "last chance" tag plus "Today's your last chance" gives a clear end date, which is better than the vague "limited time only" on 10/10.
- "ADD 3 TO CART →" is a real verb + noun in a wide burgundy button. It's easy to tap on a phone.
- The hero photo has a tint, a blush stick and a mascara. It's on-brand and makes me think about which three I'd build my bundle with.
- The "Personalized Shade Match" and "Join ILIA VIP" icons at the bottom are at least a nod to skin-tone personalization and loyalty.

## What's weak

- This is the fourth send in five days for the same offer, and the 10/11 plain-text version already felt desperate. "Last chance" lands as a bluff by now.
- There's no product detail anywhere. No names, no prices, no shades. I can't tell what a $90 bundle looks like, so I can't do real math.
- The big pink "ILIA Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" tile is dead space. It could have been three product cards.
- The VIP mention is just a tiny icon at the bottom. I'm after points math, and I get nothing on whether VIP stacks with BUNDLEUP.
- "Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details" is buried in tiny footer text. For a code-based offer, I'd like exclusions in plain view.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the pink logo tile with three shoppable product cards: Super Serum Skin Tint, the blush stick and the mascara, each with a price and a shade swatch. Then I could build a bundle and see the total.
- 2. Use a real countdown timer. The subject says "3, 2, 1..." but the body has no timer to make the urgency credible.
- 3. Add a single line on how VIP interacts with the code, e.g. "VIP members still earn points on bundle orders." That's what I'd check before buying.
- 4. Cap the series at two sends per offer. By the third resend I stop reading.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: any 3 ILIA, 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your ILIA trio ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use BUNDLEUP at checkout. Skin tint, blush and mascara all count.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build your three, save 20%, and still earn VIP points.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **No broken links reported.** 12 material links were probed and 7 were skipped by the click-likelihood ranking. Those 7 are mostly footer, utility and social links, so their status is unknown, not failed.
- **Tracked image URL:** `https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01M4TRR5MPCBQQSPYSV6KH1QX8` is served from the sender's branded tracking domain. It looks like an open-tracking pixel, though the QA output doesn't say so. If it is, it's expected and not a defect.

Many clients strip `@import`, and the stack falls back to `"Helvetica", sans-serif`. Low severity.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed by QA):**
  - `https://d3k81ch9hvuctc.cloudfront.net/company/SguDsz/images/c9cefca7-6b18-443a-896b-9e98ec5d842a.jpeg`. This is a content image, so with images blocked the recipient sees nothing and screen readers announce the filename.
