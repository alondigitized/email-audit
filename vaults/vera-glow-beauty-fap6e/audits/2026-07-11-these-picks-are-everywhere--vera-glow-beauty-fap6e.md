# These picks are everywhere
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so we're nine Sephora emails deep in eleven days — I've been tracking since the Stacking Weekend marathon — and this is the third editorial pivot in a row after those two "Summer's hottest beauty" sends on July 8th. I get what they're doing: de-escalate from the urgency circus, remind me they're a curator, not just a discounter. Fine strategy. But "These picks are everywhere" after eight Stacking Weekend emails — including multiple "🚨 ENDS TODAY" subjects — lands like a shrug. I opened it, obviously, because Sephora Rouge is practically a personality trait at this point, but there was zero pull from the inbox.
- The body is better than the subject earns. The HOT ON SOCIAL hero with the One Size by Patrick Starrr launch is genuinely interesting — that's a creator collab that's been all over my FYP and the inclusion angle with four shades is relevant to me. The rhode section near the bottom caught my eye too, because Hailey's SKUs are always backordered and knowing they're on the shelf at Sephora is actually news. But the email tries to do six things at once: product launch, editorial grid, Trevor Project give-back, 4X points credit card push, bundled beauty, and rhode spotlight. It's a catalog, not a story.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing in here that screams "share this with your group chat." If the One Size launch had its own dedicated send, that I might forward.

## What's working

- The HOT ON SOCIAL hero headline is smart positioning — social proof as the hook is right for 2026 and it maps to how I actually discover products.
- Patrick Starrr / One Size as the launch hero is a solid editorial choice; creator collabs with inclusive shade ranges are exactly what I pay attention to at this tier.
- The rhode section — even buried — is a genuine news beat. I didn't know rhode was expanding at Sephora and that's actually pull-worthy information.
- Beauty Insider + GET 4X POINTS loyalty signals are present, which earns baseline trust from a Rouge member.
- Clean layout, no broken images, no lorem ipsum — more than I can say for Ulta's July 8th "this event is over" disaster of a send.

## What's weak

- "These picks are everywhere" is a subject line written by algorithm, not by someone who knows me. Compare this to DE's "Time to come clean" (a 9/10 I audited recently) — that's one word doing triple duty: cleanser, confession, welcome. This subject is one word doing zero duty.
- The email has no single offer or outcome I can action. After Stacking Weekend trained me to look for a price signal, this editorial pivot needs a harder editorial hook to compensate — not just "picks."
- rhode is buried BELOW the Sephora credit card push. The sequence logic is wrong — drop the card pitch earlier and let rhode be the second hero, not the fourth section.
- "Four outfit shades" copy for the One Size hero is confusing without context — is this a setting spray? A concealer? The product category isn't immediately legible.
- GET 4X POINTS is credit card acquisition dressed up as a loyalty benefit. As a Rouge member who already does the points math, that section reads like a sales pitch I didn't ask for.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the Patrick Starrr launch its own email.** A creator collab + give-back story (Trevor Project) + four-shade range is a full send, not a hero panel in a catalog. Fragmenting it here dilutes the moment — Glossier's "Something new is coming" (9/10) showed that anticipation and focus outperform catalog dumps every single time.
- 2. **Lead with rhode as a co-hero OR give it its own send.** "rhode is now at Sephora" is actually news. Burying it under a credit card push is a hierarchy crime.
- 3. **Collapse the credit card block or move it to footer.** Rouge members don't need to be sold a Visa in the editorial zone — it kills the curator credibility.
- 4. **Subject needs a product noun or a cultural hook.** "These picks are everywhere" could be anything from a grocery circular to a tech newsletter. ILIA tied their editorial to Wimbledon — find the cultural moment and name it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Pinch-zoom blocked (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming on iOS. Remove this attribute; Apple's Mail and Safari will handle scaling correctly without it.

**Issue 2 — Universal `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to the entire element set, this suppresses OS-level font scaling for users who have set larger system text sizes (common for low-vision users). Restrict to `table, td` only, which is the legitimate Outlook fix target.

**Issue 3 — Malformed robots meta (minor)**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Value should be `noindex` (no space). Inconsequential for email delivery but indicates a template copy-paste defect.
