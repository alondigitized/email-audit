# Boo! Halloween is here!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. That's how many Lush emails I've now tracked, and email twenty-nine is dropping Halloween content on July 16th. Not a teaser, not a "mark your calendar" — "Boo! Halloween is here!" as though I haven't been sweating through a summer heatwave since June. I actually stopped and checked the date on my phone because I thought I'd blacked out three months. I hadn't.
- The frustrating part is that the creative inside is genuinely fun. "Ghost the Grind" is a clever hook — the subhead leans into hustle-culture burnout, which *is* a very millennial millennial thing to touch. Pumpkin scrubs, spicy scents, "fearsome fizzies" — the collection sounds like exactly the kind of autumn cozy drop I'd get genuinely excited for. In October. When I want pumpkin anything.
- I will not be clicking, and I won't be forwarding this. I might save it to my "what not to do" mental file. Compare this to what Lush pulled off on July 11th — "Zero packaging. Double points." gave me actual math to run, an action to take, something that felt timed and intentional. This feels like someone misconfigured a scheduling tool.

## What's working

- **"Ghost the Grind" headline is genuinely witty.** The burnout angle is on-brand for how Lush talks to me, and the wordplay lands even if the season doesn't.
- **CTA copy earns its place.** "Get Some Treats" is thematic, specific enough to read as more than "Shop Now," and the green button has real visual weight.
- **Product specificity in the body copy.** Pumpkin scrubs, spicy scents, fizzies — I know what I'm clicking into. That's more than ILIA gave me with "That flushed feeling," which was gorgeous editorially and vague commercially.
- **Clean render, strong visual hierarchy.** Eye goes straight to the hero; no image breaks, no overlapping text. Lush consistently wins on layout.
- **App $5 off banner at top.** Small but there — gives me something tangible before I even hit the hero.

## What's weak

- **July 16th is not Halloween.** This is the entire review. The subject line reads as a scheduling error, not a campaign.
- **Male-presenting model in the hero.** I'm a 29-year-old woman shopping beauty. The demographic signal doesn't match me — Sally Beauty and Sephora both index heavier on relatable female models for prestige bath launches.
- **No loyalty math anywhere.** After "Zero packaging. Double points." trained me to look for a reason to stock up, there's nothing here to calculate. Ulta would have at least dangled 2x points as a hook.
- **"COSY SEASON" in the nav confirms this wasn't an accident.** That's a deliberate fall campaign label in a July email, which makes the timing feel less like an error and more like a strategy choice I fundamentally disagree with.
- **No deadline, no urgency frame that works.** "Halloween is here!" in July is false urgency — it undermines trust rather than building it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this email until September 15th minimum.** The creative is good. The send date is wrong. Nothing else on this list matters until that's fixed. A Halloween collection teaser in mid-September builds anticipation; the same email in July reads as a glitch.
- 2. **Swap the hero model for someone who reads as the target buyer.** A cozy-autumn-bath-girlie, not a bare-chested male model with gourds. Even Drunk Elephant's body copy lands more "for me" than this visual does.
- 3. **Add a loyalty peg.** If points are double, say it. If the collection is Insider/app-exclusive early access, lead with that. Give me the math Lush gave me on July 11th.
- 4. **The subhead is doing the right job but too abstractly.** "Screen time report scaring you?" is fun — anchor it to a specific product so there's a reason to click, not just a mood.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Halloween haul drops early — pumpkin scrubs + spooky fizzies are in.`

### 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on Braze with table-based layout and MSO/VML conditionals for Outlook. The truncated source is sufficient to flag several infrastructure and rendering issues; compliance section is partially blocked by the truncation.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**EU CDN serving US audience**
All images are served from `cdn.braze.eu`. For a Lush USA send, routing through a European CDN endpoint adds unnecessary latency and increases the risk of client-side image load timeouts. Confirm Braze account is configured to use the US CDN endpoint (`cdn.braze.com`).

Evidence: `src="https://cdn.braze.eu/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6a4e266f165ce1008393d37d/original.png?1783506542"`

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All hrefs route through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` Braze click-tracking redirects with encoded destination URLs. UTM parameters on landing pages cannot be audited from the HTML source. Recommend verifying via decoded destinations (see §6).

**No issues** with the click-tracking domain itself — `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` is a properly subdomain-isolated tracker, reducing deliverability risk.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is blank. Webmail clients (Gmail web, Yahoo) surface this as the browser tab title when a recipient opens the email in a browser tab. Should be set to match the subject line or a short descriptive label.

**Non-standard desktop width (500px)**
`row-content stack` is constrained to `width:500px`. Industry standard is 600px. At 500px, the layout has unusually wide gutters on desktop clients and may render disproportionately in wide-viewport preview panes (Outlook desktop, Apple Mail).

Evidence: `style="...width:500px;margin:0 auto" width="500"`

**Mobile breakpoint mismatch**
The media query fires at `max-width:520px` but the desktop container is 500px. This means the breakpoint activates at a width only 20px wider than the desktop column — functionally correct, but if the desktop width is ever updated to 600px, this breakpoint would need adjustment.

**Logo `alt` text is acceptable** — `alt="Logo: LUSH"` is present and non-empty. No issue.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables** — correct implementation. No issue.
