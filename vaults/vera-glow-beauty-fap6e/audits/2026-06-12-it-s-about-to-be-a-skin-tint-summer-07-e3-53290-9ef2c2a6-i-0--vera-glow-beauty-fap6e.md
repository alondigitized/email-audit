# It’s about to be a skin tint summer 😎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "skin tint summer" is a genuinely good season hook and I'll give e.l.f. the credit — especially coming right off the June 11 Halo Glow Highlighter send. Two consecutive Halo Glow drops is a lot, but the skin tint and the highlighter serve different moments in a routine, so I'm not immediately rolling my eyes at the cadence the way I did when Glossier basically resent me the "Friends Sale is LIVE" email two days running. This one actually has something distinct to say.
- What I'm genuinely into: the 18-shade grid with the "choose your shade family, then pick your undertone" framing. That's not filler — that's the kind of inclusive merchandising that makes me trust a brand is thinking about more than just one face. The ingredient callout (niacinamide, bisabolol, allantoin, no white cast mineral SPF 30) is prestige-brand coded at a $14 price point and e.l.f. *knows* it. That's the quiet flex this email is built around.
- But there's no event, no offer, no reason to buy *today* over next Tuesday. It's a beautiful product showcase that stops just short of being a conversion email. I'd look at it, appreciate it, probably not click through — and definitely not forward it, unlike the BFe.l.F.s send from June 8 that I genuinely sent to my group chat.

## What's working

- The 18-shade grid paired with "choose your shade family" is smart self-service segmentation — ILIA has been doing celebrity-forward single-shade launches and it's starting to feel limiting by comparison.
- Mineral SPF 30 + "no white cast" is real anxiety relief copy for deeper skin tones — that's a promise, not filler, and it earns trust.
- The ingredient stack (niacinamide, bisabolol, allantoin) reads like Drunk Elephant territory at $14 — the value contrast is the whole pitch and they let it land.
- Multiple models across the shade range make the scroll feel relevant to me specifically — Sephora often does a cleaner hero but the shade diversity here is better.
- "It's a skin tint summer" as an in-email headline matches the subject and creates cohesion — no visual dissonance when I open.

## What's weak

- No offer, no urgency, no reason to act today — $14 is already the price, it's not on sale, there's no GWP, no Insider points event. This is awareness, not conversion.
- "About to be" in the subject is doing too much vague work — summer is *here*, it's June, commit to the moment.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA is generic; with 18 shades and a shade-finder framing, a "Find My Shade" button would match the actual ask.
- Coming off the June 11 Halo Glow Highlighter email, back-to-back Halo Glow products could blur together in recall — the naming is doing heavy lifting and might not stick.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a soft urgency layer** — even "new for summer" or "in stores now" would help; without it this reads as a brand awareness send, not a shop-today send.
- 2. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Find My Shade"** — the whole middle section is a shade education moment, make the CTA match the customer job-to-be-done.
- 3. **Tie in points or member pricing** — Vera is always doing the math; even "earn 1.5x points through Sunday" would push this from browse to buy.
- 4. **Tighten the subject** — "about to be" softens the urgency unnecessarily for something that's available now.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `18 shades, mineral SPF, no white cast — find yours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buildable coverage + niacinamide for $14. Shop now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — Apple Mail and some webmail clients surface this as the document title in thread view. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

**Global link decoration stripped**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This rule removes underlines from all links across the email body. Users relying on text decoration as a link affordance (low-vision, cognitive accessibility) get no visual indicator. At minimum, CTA buttons are unaffected (they use background-color), but any inline text links would be invisible as links.

**Google Fonts via `<link>` — Gmail incompatibility**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and iOS/Android app) strips `<head>` `<link>` tags, so Open Sans will never load there. The fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` fires instead. This is a known limitation; acceptable if the fallback is intentional, but worth confirming the design holds in Gmail's rendering environment.
