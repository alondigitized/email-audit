# Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject pulled me in — that "but 10% off?!" construction is very current, TikTok-coded, and honestly kind of cheeky coming from Drunk Elephant. But then I open it and immediately clock that this isn't a sale email. This is a subscribe-to-save announcement. The actual 10% off is buried in Step 4 of a five-step explainer. Three days ago, DE was in my inbox with "Grab 20% Off Before It's Gone!" — a one-time purchase, no strings attached. Now they want me to commit to an auto-replenishment subscription and they're dangling half that discount as the carrot. I did the math in about four seconds and it didn't math.
- The "SUPERSIZE YOUR ORDER" block at the bottom adds a second pitch — upsize your bottles — which is genuinely interesting to me as someone who goes through B-Hydra fast. - **The subject is genuinely scroll-stopping.** "Drunk Elephant but 10% off?!" is the internet-native "but [twist]" sentence structure done cleanly — short, no emoji spam, just enough weird energy to earn the open.
- **Five-step layout is clean and scannable.** The numbered steps give the subscriber-to-save concept structure that actually makes it feel low-effort rather than high-commitment.

## What's weak

- **The offer is a step down from three days ago.** DE sent "20% Off Before It's Gone!" on June 8th — a clean one-time discount. This is 10% off locked behind a subscription commitment. Asking me to do more for less, immediately after a better deal, is backwards sequencing.
- **10% off doesn't surface until Step 4.** The hero is entirely about the subscription *concept* — the actual offer (10% + free shipping) doesn't appear until I've read through three steps of onboarding instructions. Sephora would have led with the number.
- **No personalization, no product specificity.** There's a lineup of generic DE bottles in the hero shot but no "your favorite B-Hydra is now on subscribe-to-save" hook. ILIA has been serving personalization at scale; this feels like a broadcast.
- **The asterisk on "every order!*" immediately flags me.** What's the exclusion? Limited quantities? The fine print is in a tiny font at the bottom and I can't read it at this size. That asterisk does quiet damage to trust.
- **Cadence math is brutal.** This is email five from DE in eight days. June 5th was a double-send. By now I've trained myself to skim rather than read, which means the subscribe pitch barely registers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the specific product and the subscription discount together.** Something like "Never run out of B-Hydra — 10% off, always" with your most repurchased SKU as the hero image. Right now the email is about the feature, not about my skin.
- 2. **Reorder the value sequence.** 10% off + free shipping is strong enough to be the first thing I read, not the fourth. Flip the hero and the steps — show me the number, *then* explain how to sign up.
- 3. **Cut the SUPERSIZE block or make it the hero.** It's fighting with the subscribe pitch. If the story is "commit to a subscription and also go bigger on size," that's actually a compelling bundle argument — but only if it's presented as one cohesive offer, not two separate asks stacked vertically.
- 4. **Address the 20% → 10% regression explicitly.** Something like "Subscribe lock in 10% forever vs. a one-time sale" acknowledges the math and reframes it as long-term value. Right now it just feels like a downgrade.
- 5. **Kill the asterisk or surface the exclusion clearly in the email body.** If "every order" has a caveat, tell me what it is before I click.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block — confirmed bug**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!--` / `-->` are HTML comment delimiters, not CSS. Inside a `<style>` tag they are treated as parse errors in standards-compliant clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, modern Outlook). Both `.contenttable` and `.mobile` responsive width overrides will be silently dropped, likely breaking mobile layout.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font loading — same assets requested three times**

The same Brown font variants are loaded via:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` (3 `<link>` tags in `<head>`)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>` (3 `@import` rules)
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` (3 `@font-face` blocks)

Each open event triggers up to 9 font requests to `db.onlinewebfonts.com` instead of 3. Most email clients block external CSS/fonts anyway, making all 9 no-ops — but for clients that do load them (Apple Mail), this is unnecessary latency.

**[MEDIUM] `.sm { display:none; }` is unconditional**

```css
.sm{ display:none; }
```

This rule is declared outside any `@media` query, meaning `.sm` elements are hidden on all screen sizes, not just desktop. If `.sm` is the mobile-reveal class (common pattern), this inverts the intent. Verify against actual usage in body markup.

**[LOW] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**

`<html xmlns="...">` has no `lang="en"`. Screen readers will default to the OS language, which may cause incorrect pronunciation for international subscribers.

**[LOW] `max-height:1000000px` applied globally**

```css
* { max-height:1000000px; }
```

This Gmail clipping hack applied to `*` can interfere with elements that intentionally have `overflow:hidden` or constrained heights. Scope it to `body` or a wrapper `td` rather than the universal selector.
