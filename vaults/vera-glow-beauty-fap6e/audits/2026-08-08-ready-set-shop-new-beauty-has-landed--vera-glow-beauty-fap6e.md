# Ready, set, shop—new beauty has landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "new beauty has landed" — this is Sephora's Tuesday drop email and my first reaction is genuine interest, because unlike the Ulta 20%-off-one-item send I keep getting on loop (7/26, 7/26 again, and now the "ENDS TODAY!" version), this one's actually about *product*, not a coupon code doing the heavy lifting. Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez launching a new fragrance line stops my scroll immediately — I'm Rouge, I track her drops, and "Rare Beginnings" pear-gardenia-vanilla sounds like something I'd actually try in-store before committing $78 to a full bottle.
- That said, six brand blocks deep (Rare Beauty, Josie Maran, Patrick Ta, Innisfree, Marc Jacobs, Burberry) is a lot of scrolling for zero single hero offer. Sephora's own "Discover the art of body care" send from 8/6 had one clean FREE-trial-size hook I could act on in two seconds — this is more of a catalog than a campaign, and by the time I hit Burberry at $192 I've lost momentum.
- Would I click? Yes, on Rare Beauty specifically, because it's new and it's Only-At-Sephora. Would I forward it? No — this reads like a "browse when bored" email, not a "you need to see this" email. Mark as read after one tap into Rare Beauty.

## What's working

- Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez as the lead launch is a smart flex — that's exactly the kind of exclusive I cross-shop launches for, and it's a name Ulta can't touch.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" stamped on nearly every block reminds me why I don't just default to Ulta 21 Days for this stuff — this is the mass-prestige exclusivity I'm actually loyal to Rouge for.
- Clean/Planet Aware badges on Josie Maran and Innisfree are a nice trust cue for me — I actually read ingredient callouts, and "Jeju Green Tea + Hyaluronic Acid" on the Innisfree SPF is the kind of detail that gets me to tap.
- Product photography is clean and consistent, Sephora's black-and-white brand frame never wavers block to block — way steadier than Sally Beauty's five-different-angle-in-a-week chaos.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer — this is six launches stacked with no discount, no bundle, nothing that moves the needle on my points math the way the 8/5 "4X points on foundations & concealers" email did (that one I actually forwarded).
- The 4X points banner near the bottom is for Pureology — haircare, app-only, dated 8/1-8/12 — buried after five other brand blocks and gated behind a restriction I have to remember to redeem in-app. Compare that to the foundation 4X send: hard number, my exact category, no app gate. This one's an afterthought.
- Zero personalization — no "Vera," no "you're Rouge," no points balance. The Insider/Rouge tier bar at the bottom is just a generic nav toggle, not "here's where you stand." Sephora usually does better on this than this send shows.
- Burberry fragrance at $192 dropped in cold with no price context (no "starting at," no sample offer) feels like a jump from $16 body butter to a luxury price point with nothing bridging them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero — either Rare Beauty (biggest name recognition) or the 4X points offer — and build the whole top fold around it instead of a flat six-brand list. Glossier's "the perfect lip product doesn't exi-" send only scored a 5 from me because the payoff was thin, but the instinct to lead with one strong hook over here.
- 2. Move the 4X points banner above the fold and drop the app-only restriction, or at least flag it clearly up top — right now it reads like an afterthought instead of an incentive.
- 3. Add a Rouge-specific line ("You're 340 points from Rewards Bazaar" or similar) — I do this math myself every week, do it for me and I'll actually engage.
- 4. Give Burberry a bridge — "starting at $38 travel size" instead of leading with the $192 price point.
- 5. Swap the generic "SHOP NOW" repeated eight times for product-specific CTA copy ("Shop Rare Beginnings," "Try the SPF") so each block feels like its own decision, not a copy-paste button.
