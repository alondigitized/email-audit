# One match. $2,000 of ILIA.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the same shade-match sweepstakes ILIA ran on 11/9 in the "Gift ideas loading…" send, and it now has its own email. The 11/9 version only had a thin red banner. This one makes it the hero, which is the right call. The quiz is a free entry, the $2,000 prize is easy to see, and "GET SHADE MATCHED" is a clear black button. But "one match" and "$2,000" is a contest pitch, not a deal. My points math is still empty. I can't tell what I'd earn or save, and nothing mentions ILIA VIP beyond a tiny icon at the bottom.
- The real money is the free mini Eye Stylus in Beam with a $75+ order. That's the same offer as the 11/7 "Not your garden-variety holiday look" send, just under a new "Gift of Shimmer" headline. Third send in four days with the same free-mini offer. It's pretty, and the eye close-up is lovely, but I've seen it.
- Would I click? Probably the quiz, since it's free and takes a minute. I wouldn't forward it. A giveaway isn't something I'd text a friend about, and the free mini isn't new.

## What's working

- The hero is clean: "ENTER TO WIN $2,000 of ILIA" over a flat-lay of the product. It reads in one second.
- The black "GET SHADE MATCHED →" button is unmistakable, and the copy is a verb plus a noun.
- The "FREE MINI" badge on the eye close-up makes the second offer visible before I read the copy.
- The $75+ threshold is stated plainly, so I could do the math fast. It's one block, easy to follow.
- The footer trust row (free shipping over $50, Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, Personalized Shade Match, VIP) works as quick reassurance.

## What's weak

- The free mini is a repeat of the 11/7 holiday email. The 11/4 "$15 for your thoughts" and 11/3 "1, 2, or 3 sweeps?" sends at least felt different from each other. This one feels like a leftover.
- There's no VIP or points math. Sephora Insider and Ulta tell me what I earn. ILIA shows a "Join ILIA VIP" icon at the very bottom.
- The two asks compete. Quiz-for-sweepstakes and $75-for-a-mini each want a different action, so I'm not sure what the email is trying to get me to do first.
- The big dusty-rose "Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" block is a wasted slab of space with no link or product.
- Fine print is gray and tiny ("See site for details", sweepstakes terms). That's a lot to hide for a $2,000 giveaway.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one primary offer. Lead with the free mini if the goal is sales. If the goal is quiz data, say what the match gets me (a shade, a code, a discount), not only a lottery ticket.
- 2. Make the quiz pay out for everyone: "Get matched, get 10% off your match."
- 3. Add VIP math: "VIP members earn X on $75+."
- 4. Cut or replace the brand block with a hero product, a review, or a shade-match testimonial.
- 5. Put the deadline in the body. There's no end date on the sweepstakes or the mini.
