# Lock the e.l.f. in with this NEW duo ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Day twenty-one, and e.l.f. switched lanes — which I respect, even if my inbox is fatigued. After back-to-back lip content (July 1 "Leveled-up lip combos," June 30 "lips & cheeks pop"), landing in setting spray/complexion territory is a genuine pivot. The hero image is doing serious work — that skin looks luminous in a way that makes you stop scrolling and actually read the copy. "Lock the e.l.f. in" made me smirk: it's a clean pun, it's not trying too hard, and it lands better than "So Blurred/Blurreal" from the June 30 email where I genuinely couldn't tell if they mistyped the product name.
- The dual-product structure — "Set It Bright" at $15 and "Power Grip Reset Setting Spray" at $10 — is smart because it gives me a routine concept, not just a random drop. But there's no discount, no loyalty hook, no deadline. Prices are transparent, which I appreciate, but $15 and $10 are list prices on new launches, not a reason to buy today over next week. Compare this to ILIA's "Buy 3, Save 20%" email, which created a real urgency engine; this one creates awareness but not action.
- Would I click? Yes, probably on the "Set It Bright" section — four shades for soft-matte radiance at $15 is impulse territory and the ingredient copy in the third section (niacinamide, pore-refining) gives me skincare-brain justification. Would I forward? To one specific friend who's been asking me for a drugstore setting spray rec, yes. Would I treat it as urgent? No — this is a "save-for-later" open, not a "clear my cart" open.

## What's working

- The hero model's skin is the entire argument — luminous, dewy, healthy — you understand what you're buying before you read a word.
- "Lock the e.l.f. in" is fresh enough to cut through at email twenty-one; no recycled "don't miss out" energy.
- Both products have visible, prominent pricing ($15 and $10) with no buried-scroll to find the number — Glossier has trained me to scroll three screens before I find out what something costs, so this is a relief.
- The niacinamide and "pore-refining + skin-soothing" callout in the Power Grip Reset section speaks to a skincare-aware buyer, not just a color buyer — that ingredient specificity builds trust.
- "Top Picks for You" with category tabs (EYES, LIPS, FACE, SKINCARE, HAIR) feels more curated than Ulta's flat "you might also like" grid usually does.

## What's weak

- $15 and $10 are prices, not offers — there's no discount, no bundle savings, no urgency reason to buy now instead of waiting for the next sale window.
- "double set it:" as the hero sub-positioning is slightly ambiguous — is this a sold-together bundle or two separate items? The layout suggests separate, the language implies duo, and that friction costs clicks.
- Twenty-one consecutive sends creates real cadence fatigue; even a well-executed email suffers when the sender is already filtered to background noise in my brain.
- "Set It Bright" claims four shades for soft-matte radiance but I can't see a shade swatch anywhere — for a shade-range product, that's a missing visual that would have closed the loop for me on whether this works for my skin tone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bundle-price the duo explicitly in the hero** — "Both for $25" or "The full setting routine: $25" makes the two-product concept feel intentional and gives me a math shortcut. Right now it reads like two separate launches crammed into one email.
- 2. **Add a lightweight urgency mechanism** — "just launched" with a timestamp, "limited first-run inventory," or a 48-hour window gives me a reason to act today. Sephora's Stacking Weekend email worked because I knew exactly when the window closed; this email has no equivalent signal.
- 3. **Show the four Set It Bright shades** — a small swatch row or "swipe to find your shade" CTA would immediately tell me if this is for me, which is the actual conversion question I have.
- 4. **Add a loyalty-layer line** — even "earn points at elfcosmetics.com" or a note about Ulta/Target availability where I'm already earning rewards would speak directly to how I actually shop. Right now this treats me like a stranger.
- 5. **Tighten cadence post-launch window** — three emails in seventy-two hours (June 30 / July 1 / July 2) compresses the signal and trains me to ignore the sender. The June 28 skin tint email had clean runway and scored high because of it.

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout built with BEE editor, delivered via Braze (image hosting + click-tracking redirects). HTML is truncated in the provided source, so footer-dependent compliance checks (unsubscribe, physical address) cannot be fully confirmed.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All click-tracking URLs are Braze redirect-wrapped** — e.g.:
```
https://links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/hPx-6XYKucGWjkUs1B9Ilw~~/AAQRxRA~/tk1UI5rM...
```
- UTM parameters are not visible in the raw `href`; they are presumably injected server-side at redirect time. This should be verified — if the final destination lands on `elfcosmetics.com` without `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`, attribution will be dark.
- The redirect URL is ~400 characters. Some legacy spam filters penalize excessively long URLs; low risk but worth noting.
- The logo CTA uses `tabindex="-1"`, which explicitly removes it from keyboard tab order. If this is the primary top-of-email CTA, it is inaccessible to keyboard-only users.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Apple Mail, older Samsung clients) display the `<title>` value in the tab bar or notification preview. An empty title is a missed signal and can cause "Untitled" display artifacts.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
This renders only in Apple Mail and some WebKit clients. Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore external stylesheet links. The fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` will render in all other clients — functionally fine, but the `<link>` fetch is wasted overhead for the majority of opens.

**Three empty `html_block` elements** (block-2, block-3, block-4) are present in row-1:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These appear to be unpopulated template slots left in the build. They add dead bytes and could confuse some parsers, but are otherwise inert.

**Nested `nl-container` inside `html_block block-1`:** The outer BEE-generated table wraps an inner full `nl-container` table structure. This double-nesting is structurally redundant and could produce unexpected rendering in strict clients, though it is unlikely to break layout in practice.

**Mobile breakpoint at 660px, fixed content width at 640px:** The 20px gap is standard and intentional for handling scrollbar widths. No issue.
