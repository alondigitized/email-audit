# Last Call for Beauty Deals ⚡
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. At minimum eleven Sally Beauty emails in two weeks, and today's hook is "Last Call for Beauty Deals." Three days ago — May 25 — the subject was "Okay, Last Chance 😉," a line so self-aware I almost respected it. And now here we are, with another last call. The credibility of the urgency died somewhere around email eight.
- What kills me isn't the frequency alone — it's that the frequency comes with nothing. The hero says "Hair, Color & Beauty Must-Haves" and the tagline is "Great results at home, made to fit your routine and budget." That is a brand manifesto. That is not a deal. Sephora's May 26 "ENDS TODAY" send said "up to 50% off select beauty" right in the subject. Drunk Elephant said "25% off" — both in the subject *and* the hero. Sally gives me curly hair vibes and zero receipts.
- I'm not clicking this. I'm not forwarding it. I'm genuinely evaluating whether this brand has earned a filters rule at this point.

## What's working

- The hero model is a young woman with gorgeous natural curly hair — demographically on point for Sally's textured hair care range and genuinely appealing
- "Shop now" CTA button is bold red and clearly tappable — zero ambiguity about where to go
- Klarna callout is well-placed for Sally's audience; the $35 threshold is realistic for a single hair color kit purchase
- Visual hierarchy is clean — the eye lands on the hero, then the CTA, then the secondary "Shop all deals" block, in that order without hunting
- Free 2-hour delivery with a $25 minimum is a functional benefit that's front-loaded in the banner, not buried

## What's weak

- No specific offer anywhere in the hero — "Must-Haves" and "fit your routine and budget" is a slogan, not a sale
- The nav strip reads "ALL · LAST CALL · LAST CALL · LAST CALL · LAST" — three identical labels in a row looks like a broken loop, not intentional design
- After "Okay, Last Chance 😉" on May 25, "Last Call" three days later reads as fiction; the urgency signal is fully burned out
- No product callouts, no category tiles, no specific items — "Shop all deals" asks me to click into a void with no reason to expect what I'll find
- Rewards Club or Beauty Club points aren't mentioned anywhere — which is the one lever that would actually make me choose Sally over Ulta or Amazon for a restock

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real number in the hero, full stop.** "Up to 40% off hair color and care" beats "Beauty Must-Haves" in every single A/B test ever run. Charlotte Tilbury names a product; Drunk Elephant names a percentage; e.l.f. names a shade. Sally can do at least one.
- 2. **Collapse the "LAST CALL" nav repetition to one.** Three consecutive identical labels looks like a render error. One creates emphasis; three creates confusion.
- 3. **Add product grid or category tiles below the hero.** Hair Color / Textured Care / Nail tiles with deal callouts give me a destination to click *toward* rather than a generic deals page to wade through.
- 4. **Lead with Rewards Club.** "Earn double points on everything marked down" would be the single fastest way to convert a high-cadence-fatigued subscriber who's already seen every variation of this sale.
- 5. **Cut the send frequency in half immediately.** The email itself isn't the problem. Eleven sends in two weeks is the problem. One well-timed send with a real offer will outperform three consecutive "last calls."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off hair color — tonight only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Rewards points are waiting. Deals end tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final hours on shampoo, color kits, and nail faves — shop the list`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn points on everything marked down. Gone at midnight.`

**Issue 2 — `white-space: pre` on `img` elements.**
`white-space` is a text layout property; applying it to `img` is non-standard and ignored by most rendering engines, but it creates unnecessary parse noise and could interact unexpectedly with some Outlook-on-the-web renderers.

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```

**Issue 3 — Stale Google Fonts version (v17).**
All six Open Sans `@font-face` declarations point to `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/`. Current Open Sans is v40+. v17 URLs remain live as of today but Google does not guarantee perpetual hosting of old font versions; a CDN expiry would silently break font rendering for Apple Mail and iOS clients (the only major email clients that honor `@font-face`).

**Issue 4 — Gmail hack uses `100vw`.**
```css

```
This Gmail Dark Mode / clipping workaround sets `min-width: 100vw`, which can trigger a horizontal scrollbar on narrow viewports (< 320px) or inside Gmail's preview pane.

**Note — Mobile breakpoint at 499px.** Industry standard is 600px. At 500–600px (common tablet/landscape phone widths) desktop styles will apply. Minor but confirm intentional.
