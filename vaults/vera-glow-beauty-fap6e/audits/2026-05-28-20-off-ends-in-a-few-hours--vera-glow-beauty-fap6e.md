# 20% off ends in a few hours
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's where we are. And today alone is a double-send day — "Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" already hit my inbox this morning, and now here comes "20% off ends in a few hours" as a follow-up from Marissa. I understand the final-hours psychology but mailing me twice in the same day trains me to stop opening the first send because I know a second is coming anyway.
- The format is the most striking thing about this email: pure plain text, no hero image, no product photography, no CTA button in sight. I've seen Marissa's name and voice earn opens all campaign, and I actually respect the personal-letter energy on a sale deadline. But there's a contradiction I cannot look past: the email opens "Hi VIP" and then the P.S. invites me to join the VIP program. I'm either a VIP or I'm not. If this is going to a mixed list and "VIP" is just a warm salutation, that's a segmentation call ILIA should've resolved by now.
- The actual content has some genuinely new hooks — 40% off select items is the first scarcity signal this campaign that felt earned, and the subscription lock-in deal is a value prop I haven't seen ILIA surface before. But with no button, no visual, and no direct product link, the email is doing the intellectual work of a good offer without doing the emotional work of making me want to click right now. I'll probably hit the site eventually for the Barrier Build because I'm already near $175 — but this email didn't push me there. Mark as read.

## What's working

- Tiered free gift structure is specific and well-ordered — Mini Limitless Lash with any order is an easy impulse add, and the $175 Barrier Build tier is a real purchase motivator if you're already a spender
- "40% off select items (heads up, these are going fast)" is the first genuine scarcity signal this campaign; previous sends leaned on deadline urgency, but stock urgency hits differently
- The subscription lock-in offer — "lock in the sale price + free shipping forever" — is a genuinely new hook and the most compelling line in the email for a regular ILIA buyer
- "Marissa from ILIA" sign-off maintains the personal voice that's made this campaign feel less robotic than, say, Sephora triple-sending "ENDS TODAY‼️" on May 26

## What's weak

- Zero visual design: no hero image, no product photo, no CTA button — "final hours to shop the sale" as blue hyperlinked text is not a button, and Charlotte Tilbury's email eleven had an actual product moment to click into; this has nothing
- "Hi VIP" opening immediately undercut by a P.S. asking me to join the VIP program — that's a contradiction that makes "VIP" feel like a mass salutation, not evidence ILIA knows who I am
- Second ILIA email today, seventeenth of the campaign — double-sending on the final day is the fastest path to unsubscribes and trains subscribers to wait for the second send before opening the first
- Three asterisked exceptions in eight bullet points ("ends tonight,\*", "Barrier Build with $175+\*", "free shipping forever\*") all resolved with "See site for details" — that's enough fine-print stacking to make a careful reader hesitate

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the VIP contradiction first:** Segment the list. VIPs get "Hi [first name]," no P.S. Non-VIPs get the P.S. but don't get "Hi VIP." This is one merge tag swap and it doubles the trust signal.
- 2. **Add a single product image:** Even one inline photo of the Barrier Build or the Limitless Lash gives this email a visual hook. e.l.f.'s sixteenth email this cycle was cluttered but at least showed me the product — plain text works as a final-hours format only if there's a reason to click, and a product visual creates that reason.
- 3. **Replace hyperlinked text with a real button:** "Claim your free gift — 20% off ends tonight" as an actual button would meaningfully outperform the inline "final hours to shop the sale" link.
- 4. **Kill the second same-day send:** Either "ends tonight" or "ends in a few hours" — not both. If A/B testing send times is the goal, pick a winner; don't mail both to the full list.
- 5. **Inline one asterisk clarification:** The most confusing one is "free shipping forever\*" — just write "free shipping on subscriptions, US only" or wherever the catch is; it takes five words and removes friction.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free gifts stack — last hours of 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Barrier Build is free if you hit $175 tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mini Limitless Lash with any order, full-size bronzer at $100, Barrier Build at $175`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off select items going fast — free gifts unlock at $100 and $175`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — ILIA "20% off ends in a few hours"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Plain-prose Klaviyo send with three redirect CTAs through `trk.send.iliabeauty.com`. The three "broken links" in QA output are quoted-printable decoding artifacts in the relay pipeline, not live link failures — but they mask whether the actual destination URLs are healthy. CAN-SPAM physical address is absent, and compliance headers were not captured.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**QA-flagged broken links — pipeline artifacts, not confirmed live breaks:**

| QA URL | Root cause |
|--------|-----------|
| `https://iliabeauty.com/c=` | URL truncated; trailing `=` is an unescaped QP soft-wrap character |
| `https://iliabeauty.=` | Severely truncated; same QP encoding corruption |
| `https://manage.kmail-lists.com/subscriptions/unsubscribe?a=3DSguDsz&=` | `=3D` is QP-encoded `=`; checker ran against encoded form rather than decoded URL |

**Assessment:** The QA tool is not decoding quoted-printable before URL validation, producing false positives. The three links in the live HTML — `_0`, `_1`, `_2` suffixed — were not independently verified and may be fine. However, the Klaviyo unsubscribe redirect (`_2`) ultimately resolves to `manage.kmail-lists.com`, which should be spot-checked against a live send.

**No UTM parameters are visible in source.** All links pass through the Klaviyo redirect layer; UTM append must be verified in account settings (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **`<html>` tag has no `lang` attribute.** Screen readers cannot determine document language. Should be `<html lang="en">`.
- **`<head>` missing `<meta charset="utf-8">` and `<meta name="viewport">`.** Absent charset risks encoding misinterpretation in some clients; absent viewport affects mobile scaling.
- **Every text node is wrapped `<p>` → `<span>` with identical inline styles repeated per element.** Functionally harmless but increases payload size and can produce inconsistent margin rendering across clients due to varying `<p>` default margin resets.
- **Tracking pixel alt text:** HTML has `alt=""` on `<img src="https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01KSR9NXWQJDNWRB1PTBN8K3PJ">`, which is correct for a decorative/tracking pixel. QA flag is a false positive.
- **Custom font (`Karla`) loaded via `@import` inside an MSO conditional comment.** Correct pattern — Outlook will fall back to Arial as declared; no issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- **Greeting renders as literal `"Hi VIP,"` with no Klaviyo merge tag.** Cannot determine from source whether this is intentional segment-level addressing or a failed/missing `{{ first_name | default: 'VIP' }}` token. If intentional, no issue; if fallback-only, first-name personalization is absent.
- No other dynamic tokens or unfilled placeholders visible in source.

UTM parameters, if configured, would be appended by Klaviyo on redirect. **UTM pass-through cannot be confirmed from this source alone.** Destination landing page alignment (sale page live, correct discount applied, GWP thresholds matching copy) must be verified by clicking through from a test send.
