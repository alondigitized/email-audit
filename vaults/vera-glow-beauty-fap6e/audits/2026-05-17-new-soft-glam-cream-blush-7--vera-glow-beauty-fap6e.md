# NEW! Soft Glam Cream Blush, $7 💗
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay — sixth e.l.f. email since May 15. I want to be upfront about that before I say anything else, because the cadence is genuinely starting to tax my goodwill. That said? This one earns more grace than most of the recent batch. The subject line is doing real work — a specific product name AND a specific price in under 35 characters is genuinely clean, and $7 cream blush is a number that makes my brain do a little math (that's cheaper than my Charlotte Tilbury Perk-Up Powder, by a factor of like six). The hero model is applying blush in a way that's aesthetically right for me — warm skin, diffused flush, nothing editorial-alien about it.
- The scroll story actually holds together: hero product → ingredients callout → "The Cream Team" dual-use brush angle → a mascara cross-sell → Target shelf placement → rewards nudge at the bottom. It's a lot, but it doesn't feel like chaos. The "INGREDIENTS FOR BUTTER SKIN" section is smart — e.l.f. knows their skincare-obsessed shopper and that angle lands.
- That said, "LEARN MORE" as the CTA is soft for a launch email, the mascara section feels grafted on, and after five emails in three days I'm already doing the mental math on whether to snooze this sender. I'd click the blush. I would not forward this — it reads like a product launch, not a "you need to see this" moment.

## What's working

- **Subject line is doing its job.** Product name + $7 price point in one line — specific, scannable, no fluff. Compared to Charlotte Tilbury's vague "A Magical 15% Off Just For You!" this is a much cleaner value proposition upfront.
- **Hero image is demographically on point.** The model looks like someone in my feed — real flush, realistic skin, relatable application angle. Not the over-airbrushed editorial vibe that pushes me away.
- **"Ingredients for Butter Skin" section.** Leading with niacinamide/squalane adjacent language to a skincare-crossover beauty shopper? That's earned attention. I notice this more than a typical ingredient callout.
- **"The Cream Team" brush angle.** Telling me I can sculpt AND flush with one brush is the kind of low-friction product logic that gets me to add-to-cart rather than "save for later."
- **Layout is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, nothing leaking — Sephora's "WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS" email from a few days ago had more visual clutter than this.

## What's weak

- **"LEARN MORE" CTA is too passive for a launch.** This is a new product with a specific price — "Shop Now, $7" or "Get the Blush" would close so much harder. "Learn more" feels like I'm being handed a brochure, not a product.
- **The mascara section is a distraction.** We're launching a cream blush. Why am I suddenly looking at a mascara mid-email? It reads like a cross-sell that editorial didn't have the courage to cut. Glossier would never muddy a single-product story like this.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is send six in three days. The birthday email on May 16 bought some goodwill — but we're burning through it fast. ILIA's welcome email felt like a considered moment; this one feels like e.l.f. hitting send on a conveyor belt.
- **No price contextualization in the body.** The subject promises $7 but I don't see that anchored clearly in the hero. Show me the price again in the scroll — don't make me hunt for confirmation that I read the subject right.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "LEARN MORE" with a price-forward CTA.** "Shop the $7 Blush" or "Add to Bag — $7" — anchor the value while driving the action. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Cut the mascara section or make it a tiny footer strip.** This is a cream blush launch. Give it the full scroll. If mascara needs to be here, a two-product footer tile is all it earns.
- 3. **Echo the $7 price in the hero copy.** Put it right under the product name in the scroll — "New. $7. Cream blush that actually stays." Removes any doubt that the subject was clickbait.
- 4. **Add a shade carousel or shade names.** I want to know if my shade exists before I click. "Cherry Glaze," "Peach Please," whatever — even two shade chips would reduce the friction between me and the cart.
- 5. **Cadence: this should have been Tuesday's email, not Saturday's.** Space the sends and each one would hit harder.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably newer Outlook builds and Gmail tabs) surface the `<title>` value. An empty title degrades accessibility for screen reader users who navigate by document title.

**9px font size injected via mobile media query**
```css
@media (max-width:660px) {
  .row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size:9px!important }
}
```
9px is below any accessible minimum (WCAG recommends ≥14px for body text; headings should be larger). This appears to be a layout hack (collapsing a duplicate header block on mobile) but the `!important` override could suppress any other corrective rule. Confirm the target element is truly meant to be invisible, and if so use `display:none` or `max-height:0` instead of a near-invisible font size.

**Link affordance suppression**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color:inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This strips underline and color differentiation from all links in Gmail's Android app (which injects `#MessageViewBody`). Inline `color` and `text-decoration` on individual `<a>` tags would be needed to override this intentionally, but the rule as written applies globally.

**Google Fonts blocked by most email clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" ...>
```
Outlook (all versions), Gmail app, and some corporate clients block external stylesheets. Fallback `Arial, Sans-serif` is specified inline, so rendering degrades gracefully — but Jost (a brand font) will never render in Outlook. Acceptable if intentional; flag if brand consistency in Outlook is a requirement.
