# Leveled-up lip combos for only $20 💋
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Day twenty. Or close enough. I know because June 30 was "NEW drops make lips & cheeks pop" — and now it's July 1 and I'm looking at another lip email, same brand, same week. To be fair, this one earns its place in the rotation. "For only $20" actually made me stop scrolling, because unlike most e.l.f. sends lately, this one leads with a real number and a real concept — curated duos, not just "new thing, buy it." The Berry Iconic and Keep It Mauve-ing naming is doing work. The hero model is stunning — bold, dark skin, glossy lip — and the dark background makes the product photography pop in a way e.l.f. doesn't always pull off.
- That said, I'm doing the math in my head already: is this $20 total for both pieces? $20 each? The copy says "for only $20" but then I have two separate "SHOP NOW" buttons, which makes it feel like $20 per combo rather than some kind of stacked deal. I want ILIA's offer clarity — their "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now" email made the math instant. This one makes me guess, which introduces friction right at the buy decision.
- Would I click? Yes, probably — the Berry Iconic one is genuinely calling to me. Would I forward to a friend? Only if she's an e.l.f. devotee who already got this in her inbox ten minutes ago, which she did.

## What's working

- **The price anchor up front** — "for only $20" in the hero copy is the strongest thing in this email; it's the signal I needed to keep reading
- **Two distinct duo options with personality-coded names** — Berry Iconic vs. Keep It Mauve-ing gives me a choice without overwhelming me; these names are doing actual demographic targeting
- **Model casting is excellent** — the hero model is striking, young, and styled in a way that feels editorial, not catalogue; this is e.l.f. at its visual best
- **"Top Picks For You" personalizes the tail** — signals behavioral targeting, which is more than the Ulta email from yesterday managed (that one heroically promoted an event that was already over)
- **Category nav at the bottom is clean** — EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE / HAIR gives me a natural escape hatch if the lip combo isn't landing

## What's weak

- **The offer math is ambiguous** — "for only $20" with two separate SHOP NOW buttons; I need to know if that's $20/combo or $20 total, and right now I'm guessing
- **Zero loyalty signal** — I'm a creature of points. Sephora's Stacking Weekend email made me feel like a VIP with layered benefits visible in the first scroll; this email treats me like a stranger who wandered in off TikTok
- **"FIND A DUO +" is soft for a CTA** — the "SHOP NOW" buttons below it are doing the actual work; the hero button is vague when it could say "Get the $20 duo"
- **Cadence fatigue is accumulating** — June 30 was also a lip email (lips and cheeks), so two lip-focused sends back-to-back is starting to feel like Glossier's "ends tonight" / "ends tomorrow" loop from mid-June that I clocked immediately
- **No shade swatch or finish detail** — I can see product names and combo titles, but as a Rouge/Diamond-tier buyer who reads ingredients, I want a finish call-out (matte? glossy?) at a glance

## Recommendations

- 1. **Clarify the $20 math in the hero** — rewrite to "Two new lip duos. $20 each." or "Get both for $20" — one sentence, zero ambiguity; this is the single highest-lift change
- 2. **Swap "FIND A DUO +" for offer-specific CTA copy** — "Shop Berry Iconic – $20" and "Shop Keep It Mauve-ing – $20" on the hero buttons, the way ILIA wired their offer directly into the button copy
- 3. **Add a finish/formula one-liner per product** — even two words ("Glossy liner + matte color") below each combo name would cut the friction for buyers who need to visualize the result
- 4. **Surface the e.l.f. rewards angle** — if this $20 combo earns points, say it; "That's X Beauty Points back" would have Sephora Rouge brain-worms in me reaching for my wallet faster
- 5. **Differentiate cadence from June 30** — the prior send was also lips; either hold this 48 hours or explicitly frame the July 1 send as "your July 4th lip look" to make the back-to-back feel intentional

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics "Leveled-up lip combos for only $20"

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Nested duplicate `nl-container` table structure (high confidence bug):**
The outer layout wraps `row-1 > html_block block-1`, and inside that block the builder emitted a *complete second* `nl-container` table with its own `row-1 > row-content > column-1 > image_block`. This double-scaffold is a builder artifact and adds redundant nesting. In Outlook (MSO Word renderer), extra `<table>` nesting can produce unexpected cell padding or column width collapse.

```html
<!-- outer scaffold -->
<table class="nl-container">...<td class="column column-1">
  <table class="html_block block-1">
    <div>
      <!-- inner scaffold — this is the problem -->
      <table class="nl-container" width="100%">...<table class="image_block">
```

**Empty content blocks (blocks 2, 3, 4):** Three `html_block` rows contain only whitespace `<div>` wrappers with no content. Each adds a full table row to the DOM. Negligible on rendering but inflates raw HTML size and is a builder hygiene issue.

**Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
External stylesheet loading is blocked in Gmail (web/Android), Outlook (all), and Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection active. The fallback chain `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` renders fine, but the `<link>` tag itself is wasted bytes in most clients.

**Keyboard / focus accessibility:**
```html
<a ... style="outline:none" tabindex="-1">
  <img src="..." alt="e.l.f.">
</a>
```
Both `outline:none` and `tabindex="-1"` are set on the hero logo link. `tabindex="-1"` removes the element from the tab order entirely; `outline:none` removes the focus ring. Together these make the logo unfocusable by keyboard. Acceptable for a decorative logo only if the link destination is reachable another way — but if this is the only nav path to the homepage, it fails WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.1.

**`alt` text on logo image:** `alt="e.l.f."` — minimal but technically sufficient for a logo.

**`<title>` is empty:** `<title></title>`. Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface the title tag. Should be set to the subject line or a meaningful value.

**`#MessageViewBody a` global override:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This suppresses Gmail's blue auto-link styling but also strips all default link affordances email-wide. Every CTA and inline link in the body must have explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` styles or they will be invisible as links. Confirm all downstream CTA buttons and text links have inline style declarations.
