# $10 off your $50 purchase⁺ or $20 off your $100 purchase⁺ in store or online (Terms & excl. apply) ends today!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Four. This is the fourth time this exact tiered spend-and-save has hit my inbox in five days, and Ulta's framing it as a "friendly reminder" like I somehow missed it the first three times. Respectfully, I did not miss it — I just haven't acted on it yet, and calling it a friendly reminder doesn't change that math.
- That said, there IS something new in this send that I didn't clock as clearly in the earlier versions: the "free mystery gift" box is actually pretty front-and-center here, sitting right below the barcode block. If that's a genuine add-on and not the same mystery gift they've been dangling all week, that changes my calculation a little — because the $20 off a $100 basket is already a 20% effective return, and a mystery gift on top of that tips my Diamond brain toward "okay, fine, I'll build a basket." But I'd need to know if the mystery gift is new to this send or if it was always buried.
- The product grid below the fold is doing what Ulta product grids always do — showing me seventeen things across every category at once — and it doesn't help me make a decision. I'm not clicking a product. I'm either going to screenshot the barcode or I'm not. Mark as read, screenshot the barcode for the store run I was already planning.

## What's working

- **The offer is immediately legible.** "$10 off" and "$20 off" in large, high-contrast blocks with a barcode I can screenshot — no hunting for a promo code, no "apply at checkout" ambiguity.
- **"Friendly reminder" framing is softer and less aggressive** than the straight promo subject lines from May 21 — it registers as "last chance" without feeling like a fire sale.
- **Free mystery gift is a genuine hook.** Positioned prominently in the hero stack, it gives me a reason to act today vs. the earlier identical sends that didn't have this front and center.
- **Rewards section is present.** Even at the small size I can see the tier / points block at the bottom — Ulta knows I'm Diamond and it keeps that relationship visible.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, product images loaded, barcode is legible.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is exhausting to read.** I had to scan it twice to find where the actual offer ended and where the fine print disclaimer started — compare to e.l.f.'s "Get a Jelly Pop Glow Stick on us! 🍉" which told me exactly what I was getting in seven words.
- **Offer fatigue is real.** This is the fourth deployment of this exact creative in five days. Charlotte Tilbury caught heat in my notes for eight "20% off" sends in ten days — Ulta is heading toward that territory.
- **The product grid below the hero adds noise, not focus.** Seventeen products across skincare, makeup, and haircare with no editorial POV is the opposite of what ILIA did with "Layers of free gifts" — ILIA gave me a specific progression, Ulta just threw the store at me.
- **No personalization above the fold.** The May 21 "Marisol" and "Rae" sends at least had a name in the subject. This one treats me like a stranger on the last day of the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the mystery gift to differentiate from the prior three sends.** If it's a new incentive, make it the subject — "Last day: $20 off $100 + a free mystery gift" is a materially different email than the four prior ones and gives a real reason to act now vs. procrastinate again.
- 2. **Cut the subject line in half.** The current subject is ~85 characters and front-loads the offer amounts but buries the urgency. Flip it: put "ends today" first, let the discount follow.
- 3. **Collapse the product grid.** Pick three hero products that are eligible with the offer and link each directly. Sephora's sale emails do this well — curated selection vs. the whole store wall.
- 4. **Add my tier name somewhere above the fold.** "Diamond member exclusive" or even just "your Diamond offer" would make this feel like it was sent to me, not blasted at a segment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends today: $20 off your $100 + a free mystery gift`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call, Diamond: $10/$20 off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your barcode is ready — mystery gift included with qualifying purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop in-store or online before midnight — offer disappears at 11:59 PM.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` (blocked in production):**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail on iOS, and Yahoo all strip or block external stylesheet `<link>` tags. DM Sans will silently fall back to the declared stack (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`). This is survivable but means the font is never actually used — the `<link>` is dead weight.

**Issue 2 — Global link style removes all visual affordance (WCAG 2.1 failure):**
```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
}
```
This makes every link invisible as a link — no underline, no distinguishing color — unless the individual element explicitly re-declares it. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) and SC 1.3.3. Affects screen reader users and low-vision users who rely on non-color cues.

**Issue 3 — Dead commented-out code shipping in production:**
The full hamburger menu implementation (~60+ CSS rules) is commented out with a ticket reference (`91925`) but still included in the sent payload:
```css
/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu
   |BEGIN Hamburger Menu
   ... [~60 rules] ...
   END Hamburger Menu */
```
Adds unnecessary bytes to every send. Strip before production.

**Issue 4 — Store dropdown `z-index: 0` may render behind other positioned elements:**
```css
#menu-wrapper-store { z-index: 0; position: absolute; ... }
```
The commented-out hamburger used `z-index: 10`. The active store dropdown uses `z-index: 0`, which will place it behind any sibling with `position` set. This is a likely bug if the dropdown ever renders.

**Issue 5 — Truncated CSS property on `#menu-wrapper-store`:**
The source ends mid-property: `position: absolute; t…` — this suggests the HTML was cut before `top:` was declared. Cannot verify whether the positioning is complete.

**No issues found:** `lang="en-US"` on `<html>` is correct. MSO conditionals and `o:AllowPNG` are present. Viewport meta is declared. Dark mode meta (`color-scheme: light dark`) is implemented in both `<meta>` and `:root` CSS — correct dual declaration.
