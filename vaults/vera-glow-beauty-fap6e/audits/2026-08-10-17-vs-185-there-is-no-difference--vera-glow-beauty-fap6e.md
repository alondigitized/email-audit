## What's working

- The "$17 vs $185" dupe framing is a genuinely fresh hook after weeks of e.l.f. sending the same "e.l.f.ssential" single-product teasers.
- Satisfaction stat table (68% vs 68%, tied) is a smart, skimmable trust cue — feels like real testing, not just marketing copy.
- Clear single hero product (Vitamin C+E Ferulic Serum, $17) with a clean product shot on the vanity flat-lay.
- "Find your skincare" quiz block is a nice mid-email personalization nudge even if it's generic.

## What's weak

- The comparison table's "alternative" column never names the $185 brand — feels like it's dodging a claim it can't fully back up, which undercuts the whole stunt.
- No skin type/tone callout anywhere despite this being a serum — I have combo-sensitive skin and I have zero idea if this will break me out.
- "Top Picks for You" (Off Makeup Remover, Thirst Burst, Daily Cleanser) reads totally generic, not actually personalized to what I clicked or bought last.
- The category nav bar (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) is just gray text blocks — visually dead weight, zero hierarchy, easy to scroll past without noticing.

- 1. Name the "$185 alternative" brand (or at least tease "the cult $185 vitamin C serum you know") — right now it reads like they're hiding something, and that kills the dupe-culture trust this format is supposed to build.
- 2. Add a one-line skin-type/tone fit note under the hero product — Ulta and Drunk Elephant both do this and it's the #1 thing that gets me to actually buy a serum sight-unseen.
- 3. Swap "Top Picks for You" for genuinely purchase-history-based picks, or at least badge them "bestsellers" instead of faking personalization.
- 4. Give the influencer twins a follower count or "as seen on TikTok" tag — right now "Veronica" and "Vanessa" mean nothing to a stranger.
