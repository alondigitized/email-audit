# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Third Skechers email in nine days and this one is pulling the cart-abandon card — "Did you forget something?" is the most textbook trigger subject in email marketing, and I've seen it from every brand I've ever browsed without buying. I'm not mad at the mechanic; it's there for a reason. But the execution here is doing the *idea* no favors. I'm looking at three featured shoes: a red Glide-Step Pro, an Airy Stride in white, and then — wait — *Mommy & Me*? I do not have kids. If this email is supposed to be showing me what I left in my cart, how is a matching-shoe-with-your-toddler moment in my cart? That detail alone tells me this is a semi-generic trigger with some product blending, not a true personalized cart recover.
- The $5 reward on $75+ buried mid-scroll is the only real offer hook, and I almost missed it. The hero section is a golden ticket graphic that says "READY TO CHECK OUT?" which is cute in theory but feels more like an airport departure board than urgency that moves me. Compare that to Charlotte Tilbury's GWP email from two days ago — that one had a velvet hero, a specific gift at a specific spend threshold, and I knew in two seconds what I was getting. This Skechers email asks me to scroll to even understand what the incentive is.
- I'd mark this as read, not click. No forward. If the $5 reward was in the hero, this is a different conversation.

## What's working

- **Cart-abandon framing is the right mechanic.** The "READY TO CHECK OUT?" hero and dual "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTAs create a logical flow for someone who actually browsed.
- **Women's shoe selection is on-brand for my gender.** The top two featured styles (Glide-Step Pro, Airy Stride) are clearly targeted at women, not a generic catalog dump.
- **Members reward is visible.** The "$5 REWARD ON $75+" section is legible and the gold banner treatment gives it some visual weight.
- **No layout bugs.** Images load, buttons render, the hierarchy is clean. After the broken/lorem-ipsum disasters I've seen from other senders, clean is worth crediting.
- **Afterpay + Klarna visible in the footer.** BNPL options are there for the "I want it but I'll split it" shopper.

## What's weak

- **Mommy & Me product in a cart-abandon targeting me.** That's a hard miss. I'm 29, no kids, and if the ML thought this belonged in my cart I'm questioning the entire personalization stack.
- **The $5 reward is buried below three product rows.** The one actual incentive to complete my order doesn't appear until I've already scrolled past the hero, two featured shoes, another CTA, and a row of largely *men's* styles.
- **That row of small-thumbnail shoes reads mostly male.** Men's sandals, men's slip-ons, men's boat shoes — all crammed in just before "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS." That's not my next click.
- **Zero urgency mechanism.** "Did you forget something?" with no expiry date and no "items in your cart are going fast" inventory signal is just a polite nudge, not a reason to act now. Drunk Elephant's wordplay emails create more urgency than this, and they're not even doing cart recoveries.
- **Preview text appears to be leaking a tracking identifier** ("SEE 003940") rather than real copy. That's the first thing I see in my inbox next to the subject, and it's doing nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the $5 reward into the hero.** "Your cart + $5 reward on $75" is the actual hook. Put it on the golden ticket, not mid-scroll.
- 2. **Drop or swap the Mommy & Me product.** Either show me three women's styles or acknowledge that this is a recommended product — don't serve me a parent-targeting SKU in a cart-recover without context.
- 3. **Add inventory or time urgency.** "Items sell out fast" or even a 48-hour cart-hold countdown would convert better than an open-ended ask.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** Whatever "SEE 003940" is, it needs to be replaced with real copy that complements the subject. Even "Your cart is waiting — members earn $5 back on $75+" would carry weight.
- 5. **Cut the men's thumbnail row entirely.** This is a women's cart-abandon email. The male styles undermine the signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your cart's still here — grab your $5 reward`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Leaving these behind? Earn $5 back today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members earn $5 on $75+ — your styles are waiting`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Complete your order + stack your reward before it's gone`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Did you forget something?" (Cart Abandonment)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Attentive-served cart abandonment email with MSO/VML Outlook conditionals and responsive breakpoints. The truncated source reveals one confirmed protocol issue and several items worth flagging before sending.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking redirect (high)**
The click-tracking wrapper uses `http://` not `https://`:
```
href="http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6…"
```
Modern browsers and spam filters penalize plain-HTTP redirect hops even when the final destination is HTTPS. Attentive supports HTTPS tracking — this should be the CNAME/SSL-enabled endpoint (`https://skechers.attentivemail.com/…`).

**Web version link also HTTP** — same domain, same issue. Both the "web version" anchor and all CTA links in the visible source share the `http://` prefix.

**No visible UTM parameters in source** — Attentive may append UTMs post-redirect, but they are not embedded in the raw `href`. See Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) surface the `<title>` as the document label. Should be set to the email subject or brand name.

**Preheader padding technique**
The preheader uses a mix of Unicode variation selectors (`U+034F ͏`) and soft hyphens (`U+00AD ­`) to push whitespace:
```
͏ ͏ ͏ … ­ ­ ­ …
```
This is common but inconsistent across clients. Gmail occasionally renders stray characters. A single `&zwnj;` sequence is cleaner and less likely to surface.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — correctly applied; no issues.

**MSO conditionals** — present and correctly structured (`<!--[if mso]>`, `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`). No issues.

**Mobile breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:620px)` with explicit `row-20`/`row-21`/`row-23` column overrides. Font-size overrides (`19px`, `18px`) present. Cannot audit image alt text or button contrast from truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens (first name, cart product name, price, thumbnail) are visible in the provided HTML. For a cart abandonment trigger, the absence of a product name or customer name in the subject (`"Did you forget something?"`) and visible preheader (`"You left something in your cart"`) reduces relevance signal. Cannot confirm whether tokens appear lower in the truncated body — **verify that at minimum `{{product_name}}` or equivalent renders correctly and has a fallback value if the cart session is stale.**

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe / Authentication)

**Cannot confirm from truncated source:**
- Physical mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)) — typically in footer; not visible in provided excerpt.
- Unsubscribe link and one-click List-Unsubscribe header — must verify footer and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers.

**Sending domain:** `hello@msgs.skechers.com`
- `msgs.skechers.com` should be covered by Skechers' SPF record and a DKIM selector provisioned by Attentive.
- DMARC alignment requires the `d=` in the DKIM signature to match `skechers.com` (relaxed) or `msgs.skechers.com` (strict). Verify with:
  ```
  dig TXT msgs.skechers.com   # SPF
  nslookup -type=TXT _dmarc.skechers.com
  ```

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

**UTM parameters not embedded in source links.** If Attentive appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` post-redirect, verify they arrive at the Skechers landing page by curl-following the redirect chain:
```bash
curl -sIL "http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6…" | grep -i location
```
Expected final URL should include at minimum `utm_source=attentive&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=cart_abandonment` (or equivalent). If UTMs are missing, sessions land as `(direct)` in GA4/analytics, making cart recovery attribution invisible.

**Landing page alignment** — cannot audit without full link list, but cart abandonment emails must deep-link to the specific cart or product page, not the homepage. Confirm the resolved destination is `/cart` or a product PDP, not `skechers.com/`.
