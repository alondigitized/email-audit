# Beautiful, glowing skin
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay. So DE just sent me "Beautiful, glowing skin" — the most generic subject line they've written since I started auditing this inbox — and the email inside is an editorial two-step routine explainer with zero discounts, zero sale language, and zero urgency. After "20% off ends soon!" on June 29th, "The It kits" with 25% off on June 30th, and the Fourth of July beach energy on the 4th, we've now pivoted to… educational content? On a Sunday? I respect DE's brand confidence, but this feels like a filler send in a week they've already been heavy in my inbox.
- The actual email is clean — really clean. "YOUR BEST SKIN IN TWO STEPS" hero, a beautiful model, Step 1 (cleanser) and Step 2 (moisturizer) with the full core lineup laid out: Pekee Bar, Juju Bar, Protini Cream, Lala Retro, Bora Barrier, Virgin Marula Oil. It's a well-executed editorial. But it's asking me to shop at full price when three days ago they were dangling 25% off, and I'm someone who does the points math before every DE purchase.
- I'd scroll it, appreciate the clean layout, maybe click on Lala Retro because I've been curious about the texture description — but I would not be converting at full price today. I would not forward this. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Hero layout is sharp.** "YOUR BEST SKIN IN TWO STEPS." with the model hero stops the scroll — that "two steps" framing is compelling shorthand and works as a routine hook.
- **Step-by-step structure is genuinely useful.** Breaking cleanser → moisturizer as a matchmaking exercise actually adds value for someone who doesn't know DE's lineup.
- **Product descriptions are concise and specific.** "Lightweight, firming gel cream packed with peptides" under Protini Cream tells me exactly what I need to know in one line. That's well-edited.
- **Model demographic is a match.** Young woman, clear glowing skin, tone-inclusive — feels like it's talking to me, not at a 45-year-old.
- **The "SKIN GETS THIRSTY, TOO." footer teaser** adds a layer without cluttering the main flow.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer after three promotional emails.** Dropping from 25% off ("The It kits," June 30th) to full-price editorial four days later is a jarring gear shift. ILIA ran a full week of "Buy 3, Save 20%" and at least held the offer through the send. DE just pulled the rug.
- **Subject line is dead weight.** "Beautiful, glowing skin" tells me literally nothing new — it's what every DE email implies. Sephora's worst subjects are still more specific than this.
- **No loyalty hook.** I'm a Rouge-equivalent customer. This email didn't acknowledge that I exist at any tier. Sally Beauty's recent sends have done a better job of at least nodding to membership math.
- **"SHOP NOW" and "SHOP" CTAs are lazy.** Five individual "SHOP" links scattered below each product with no specificity — where am I going? The product page? A routine builder? A collection? ILIA's "Ends Tonight" CTA was one button, fully clear.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a members-only nudge or a point reminder.** Even one line — "Rouge members, your Beauty Insider points stack here" — would have earned a click from me. This email treats me like a stranger.
- 2. **Put one hero product with a specific hook at the top** instead of leading with the routine frame. Pick Protini Cream or Lala Retro, make it the star, then cascade to the rest.
- 3. **Consolidate CTAs.** Replace five "SHOP" links with one "Build My Routine" button that lands on a curated routine page. That's a better UX and better for conversion tracking.
- 4. **Fix the subject line entirely.** Give me something to open for:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 2-step DE routine, matched to your skin`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Protini or Lala Retro? Here's how to pick.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Step 1: pick your cleanser. Step 2: find your moisturizer match.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cleanser-to-moisturizer pairing, simplified.`
- 5. **Tie it to a soft offer** — even "spend $X, get a deluxe sample" keeps the transactional door open without blowing the editorial tone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[BUG — Dead CSS rules]** Two mobile width overrides inside the `@media (max-width: 600px)` block are wrapped in HTML comment syntax, making them inert:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

Inside a `<style>` block, `<!-- -->` is treated as a CSS comment. Elements using `.contenttable` and `.mobile` will not receive the 414px mobile override. This likely causes layout breakage on narrow viewports.

**[BUG — Triple font loading]** The Brown font family loads three ways in the same document, generating redundant HTTP requests:
1. `<link>` tags in `<head>` pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. `@import` rules inside the `<style>` block (same URLs)
3. `@font-face` declarations in the same `<style>` block

All three fire on parse. Remove the `<link>` tags and `@import` rules; keep only `@font-face`.

**[ACCESSIBILITY — zoom disabled]** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**[ACCESSIBILITY — text-size-adjust disabled]** `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applied globally. This overrides the OS-level text scaling that low-vision users rely on.

**[RENDERING — global line-height]** `*{line-height: 100%}` applied to every element. This collapses descenders on multi-line body copy and can clip text in certain email clients (notably Outlook 2016+).

**[RELIABILITY — third-party font CDN]** BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a CDN not operated by Drunk Elephant. Corporate email proxies and some ISPs block third-party font CDN domains. The self-hosted `BrownRegular` at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` is the correct pattern — extend it to the other weights.
