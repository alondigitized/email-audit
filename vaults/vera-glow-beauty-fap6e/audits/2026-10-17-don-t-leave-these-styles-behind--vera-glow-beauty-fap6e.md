# Don’t leave these styles behind!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is a cart-abandon email for a cart I never filled. "We saved these styles for you" is a lie. I've never put anything from Skechers in a cart. It's the same "Don't leave it behind" beat as 8/24 ("Don't leave a good thing behind.") and the 9/29 "This is perfect for you!" send. The toy-truck sneaker is gone, which is a relief, but a random grid of six shoes still isn't personalization.
- The 20% off is the only thing here I'd do math on, and it's buried below the product grid. It's also member-gated and full-price only, and it ends 10/21. I'm a Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond, so I'm used to the offer and the tier benefit being up front. Here I have to scroll past six unrelated shoes to find the discount, then dig through gray fine print to learn that "Members" means Skechers Plus.
- Would I click? Probably not. I might tap "Shop Now" out of curiosity, but I wouldn't forward it. It's a 4 or 5 for me, since the layout is clean even though the targeting is generic. I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The "WE SAVED THESE STYLES FOR YOU" headline is big, blue and readable without zooming. The hierarchy at the top is clean.
- The "SHOP NOW" button is a solid block that's easy to tap on a phone.
- The 20% OFF band is high contrast on navy, so once I reach it, the offer is unmistakable.
- The valid-through date (10/21) is stated right in the band, so the urgency is credible.
- Each product has a text label with the exact style name. At least there are specifics, and the women's shoes skew toward me.

## What's weak

- The "saved for you" framing implies I left these in a cart. I didn't, so it feels fake. It's also the third abandonment-style rerun since August.
- The 20% off is below the product grid. Sephora puts the offer in the hero. I shouldn't have to scroll to find the deal.
- There's no price or sale price on any product. With 20% off, I want to see "$80 → $64", because I always do the points math.
- There's a "Girl's Slip-ins Mommy & Me" shoe in a grid that's otherwise women's styles. It looks like a random mix, not a curated one.
- The "Members" label is vague. Is that Skechers Plus? The answer is in tiny gray print at the bottom. The exclusions (sale items, select styles) also read as bait.
- The bottom half is a long stack of nav links (WOMEN, MEN, KIDS...), app badges, BNPL logos and a text-club ask. That's a lot of generic filler after the offer, and nothing in it is personalized.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% member offer into the hero, above the product grid. Lead with "Members: 20% off through 10/21", then show the products.
- 2. Show real prices and the discounted price under each product, so I can decide fast.
- 3. Don't claim "saved for you" unless it's true. If it's browse-based, say "Based on what you looked at". If it's generic, call it "Fall favorites".
- 4. Make the grid consistent (all women's, or clearly split by category). Drop the kids' Mommy & Me shoe from a women's grid.
- 5. Name the loyalty program in the hero ("Skechers Plus members") and say what the exclusions are in one plain line.
- 6. Cut the footer clutter (BNPL, app badges, curbside) down to one row.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skechers Plus: 20% off ends 10/21`
- **Subject Alt B:** `6 fall sneakers, 20% off for members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-price styles only, online through 10/21. Here are 6 to start with.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your member 20% is live. Slip-ins, UNO and more, picked for fall.`
