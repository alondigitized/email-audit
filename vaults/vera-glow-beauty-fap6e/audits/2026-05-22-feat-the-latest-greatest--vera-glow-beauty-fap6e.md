# Feat. the latest + greatest
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Eight. Eight ILIA emails in seven days. I have been counting since May 16: welcome, awards editorial, brand overview, finish quiz, Marissa's disarming plain-text note, the cold VIP loyalty pitch, yesterday's skincare-focused sale push, and now this. The goodwill from that Marissa note is officially worn down to a nub.
- Here's the thing though — the actual email is clean. The top banner immediately surfaces "20% OFF SITEWIDE | 25% OFF FOR VIPs," the four-product grid is organized and descriptive, and the Cart Deco GWP module at the bottom ("free mini with every order — no minimums") is genuinely compelling. The Overglaze Hydrating Lip Gloss and the Skin Blur Serum Concealer both caught my eye — ceramides, 12 shades, 24-hour wear is the kind of product detail that earns a click. The execution is not the problem.
- The problem is "Feat. the latest + greatest" as a subject line for email #8 in a week. That subject tells me nothing. No product name, no deadline, no urgency signal — just a decorative phrase that forces my inbox to do the heavy lifting. The preview text ("20% OFF SITEWIDE | 25% OFF FOR VIPs") is doing almost all the work that the subject refuses to do. I'd read this because I'm tracking the brand; I would not forward it. Mark as read, not mark as spam — but barely.

## What's working

- **VIP tier discount surfaced immediately in the top bar**: I see my extra 5% without hunting for it. That's the exact points-math signal I'm always doing; Sephora's sale emails nail this same beat with Rouge/VIB tier pricing and ILIA gets credit for it here.
- **Product grid with real specs**: SPF 50 mineral zinc, ceramide-infused 12-shade lip gloss, 12-hour concealer coverage — these descriptions actually tell me something. Compare to Charlotte Tilbury's launch emails which often just say "iconic" and call it a day.
- **Cart Deco GWP module**: "Free mini with every order — no minimums" is a low-friction bonus buy that requires zero threshold math. Drunk Elephant's F+F send scored high on my radar partly because of GWP stacking logic — ILIA lands the same beat here, clean and fine-print-free.
- **Breathing room in the layout**: One hero, four product tiles, one GWP section, footer. Sephora's sale emails cram 20 products above the fold and somehow feel like less. This email knows what it's promoting.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is pure decoration**: "Feat. the latest + greatest" at email #8 in a week is a creative indulgence I don't have the patience for. e.l.f. named their actual lip launch in the subject line and scored a 9; ILIA had named products right here (Overglaze, Skin Blur) and buried them below the fold instead.
- **No prices, no "from $X" anchor**: I can see the 20% off but have no idea if these products are $28 or $68. That's a click just to baseline-check, which adds friction instead of removing it.
- **Zero sale deadline visible**: Neither the subject, banner, nor any product copy tells me when F+F ends. Drunk Elephant's follow-up "stock up at 25% off" at least implied a closing window. This reads "sale exists" not "act now."
- **No social proof for new launches**: Four presumably new products, zero reviews or star ratings visible. If I'm trying something new at even a discounted price, I want the "5.0 ★ from 47 reviews" signal. Without it, the Overglaze Lip Gloss is a pretty picture with good copy, not a confirmed buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the actual product name**: The Overglaze Lip Gloss is a new ceramide launch — that belongs in the subject line, not buried in a product tile. One specific product name changes the inbox calculus entirely.
- 2. **Add an F+F end date to the promo bar**: "Sale ends Sunday" or "3 days left" in that same top banner costs nothing and transforms the email's urgency posture.
- 3. **Anchor each product tile with a "now $X" price**: Even one price on the hero product collapses the click-to-check friction. I shouldn't have to leave the email to know what I'm buying.
- 4. **One star rating under each new product**: It doesn't need to be a full reviews widget — even "★★★★★ 94 reviews" as micro-copy under the product name is enough to convert a "curious" into a "cart."
- 5. **Rest the cadence after this send**: The May 19 plain-text note from Marissa worked because it felt rare. Eight emails in a week makes every subsequent send feel automated regardless of how good it is.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `VIPs get 25% — sale ends Sunday`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**`@import` for external fonts will be stripped by major clients.**
Both font imports are inside a `<style>` block:

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Samsung Mail strip `<style>` block contents or block external `@import` calls. Any Montserrat or Klaviyo custom font rendering depends on fallback stacks (`"Helvetica", sans-serif`), which appear to be set — but this should be verified visually in those clients.

**Body links not auditable** — HTML is truncated before product/CTA links appear. UTM coverage and redirect chain health cannot be confirmed from this source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Screen readers and some accessibility tooling use `<title>` as the document label. Should be set to the email subject or a descriptive string.

**All link states suppress hover affordance:**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration: none }
```
Hover state is identical to default — no visual feedback. Not a compliance issue, but breaks expected interactive behavior in web-based clients (Gmail, Outlook.com).

**`<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` is inside a conditional comment:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><meta content="IE=edge" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
The comment syntax here is malformed — the closing `<!--<![endif]-->` should be `<![endif]-->`. This is a known MJML quirk; functionally harmless in modern clients but technically invalid.

**No issues found** with MSO/VML shims, viewport meta, or responsive breakpoints (`max-width: 480px` / `min-width: 480px`).
