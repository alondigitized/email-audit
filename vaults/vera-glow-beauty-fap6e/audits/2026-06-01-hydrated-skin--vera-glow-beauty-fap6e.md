# Hydrated skin
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. That is where we are. And I want to acknowledge that because the shift in tone here is actually notable — DE just ended what I can only describe as a two-week hostage situation (fourteen Friends & Family emails, an "Adios," an extension, a "Final Hours," a second extension, another final hours) and they're opening June with... "Hydrated skin." Two words. No offer. No urgency. The subject line alone almost lost me.
- But here's the thing: once you're inside, this email is actually doing its job. "PLEASE HYDRATE RESPONSIBLY" is the cleverest hero copy DE has written in this entire campaign. The ingredient breakdown for B-Hydra — Vitamin B5, sodium hyaluronate, pineapple extract — is genuinely useful content, not just filler copy between product tiles. The social proof stats (97% felt smoother, 93% felt more hydrated, 93% more radiant) are right where they need to be, and the "B-HYDRA PLAYS WELL WITH OTHERS" pairing section is smart brand-building.
- Would I click? Probably yes, because I've been curious about B-Hydra as a lightweight summer serum and the ingredient story lands for me. Would I forward it? No — it's too product-specific and the subject is too weak to sell it to a friend. Mark as read after clicking the serum CTA.

## What's working

- **"PLEASE HYDRATE RESPONSIBLY"** is genuinely funny and on-brand — best DE headline of the whole May campaign, and I've seen fourteen of them.
- **Ingredient storytelling** — breaking down exactly what B5, sodium hyaluronate, and pineapple extract each do in plain language is the kind of content I actually want from a prestige skincare brand.
- **97% / 93% / 93% clinical stats** are placed right where I'm debating the click — smart positioning, not buried in footer fine print.
- **"B-HYDRA PLAYS WELL WITH OTHERS" pairing module** — showing exactly how B-Hydra stacks with C-Firma, Protini, and Marula Oil is a smart upsell that respects my existing DE shelf.
- **"SUMMER SKIN, SORTED"** section anchors the product in the right seasonal frame without being aggressive about it.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "Hydrated skin"** is two words of nothing after two weeks of sale emails — it reads like a draft that shipped by accident. Charlotte Tilbury sent "Did You Miss This Magic? 💌" and I opened it; "Hydrated skin" needed to work ten times harder given where DE left us.
- **The "welcome to the club / B-HYDRA CLUB" header** with what appears to be an offer code is visually confusing — if there's a discount here, it should be leading, not buried in teeny header text that looks like a loyalty tier label.
- **No clear offer signal** in the above-the-fold zone — after fifteen emails the last seven of which had a concrete % off, pivoting cold to a no-discount send without acknowledging the shift feels abrupt.
- **"SHOP B-HYDRA"** is the only CTA and it's fine, but "Shop" is the laziest verb in email marketing. "Get glowing skin" or "Build your hydration routine" would match the storytelling tone they built in the body.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line** — the body is doing real work; the subject is throwing that away. Either lead with the wordplay ("Hydrate responsibly this summer") or tease the ingredient story ("The B-vitamin your skin is craving").
- 2. **Make the header offer scannable** — if there's a promo code in that "B-HYDRA CLUB" section, give it a line of its own: "Club exclusive: 15% off B-Hydra today." Don't make me decode it.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP B-HYDRA" with action copy** — "Build your summer routine" or "Try the serum 97% of users loved" converts the social proof directly into a CTA, which is what this section is setting up anyway.
- 4. **Add a "new to DE?" anchor** near the ingredient breakdown — this email is doing a lot of education that feels targeted at a potential new buyer, but loyal Rouges already know B-Hydra; lean into pairing recommendations for existing customers and flag it as a first-buy option for newcomers separately.
- 5. **Acknowledge the sale-to-editorial shift** — one sentence like "Sale's over, skin goals aren't — here's what we're building toward" would land with anyone who survived fourteen F&F sends.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `B-Hydra: 97% said their skin felt smoother. Meet your summer serum.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comment syntax inside CSS block — rules silently dropped**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!--` / `-->` is HTML comment syntax, not CSS comment syntax (`/* */`). CSS parsers treat these as parse errors and drop the rules entirely. The `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive width overrides are non-functional on all clients.

---

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate font loading — `<link>` tags AND `@import` for same URLs**

The three `onlinewebfonts.com` font URLs are loaded twice: once via `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>`, and again via `@import url(...)` inside the `<style>` block. This doubles HTTP requests against a third-party CDN on every open.

Affected URLs:
- `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/4150d76a852e1c349e0b6fe15f29d9ca?family=Brown+Bold`
- `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/6145ed704c26a497680e82c477f40b46?family=Brown-Light`
- `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/68d0f9847ef9a5695d6efa71521067ec?family=Brown-Thin`

---

**[MEDIUM] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` — disables all text scaling**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```

`none` fully prevents the OS from scaling text on mobile, including for users who have set a larger system font size for accessibility reasons. Should be `100%`.

---

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

---

**[LOW] Universal `line-height: 100%` override**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applying `line-height: 100%` to every element via universal selector causes text clipping on descenders in several email clients (notably Outlook 2016+, Gmail Android). Should be scoped to specific selectors.

---

`BrownRegular` loads from self-hosted Demandware static (`drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...`). `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, `BrownThin` load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — a third-party CDN with no SLA guarantees.
