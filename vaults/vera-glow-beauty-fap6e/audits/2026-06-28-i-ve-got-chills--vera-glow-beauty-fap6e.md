# I've got chills 🧊
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. I know it's twenty-two because I've been logging Lush sends the same way you log texts from a friend who really needs to hear about mailing frequency. And here's the thing — "I've got chills" with a little ice cube emoji genuinely made me pause at the inbox. Good pun energy. Then I opened it and got "MINT CONDITION" as the hero copy and honestly? That's two puns layered on top of each other and they both land. Lush's copywriters are working hard and I can see it.
- But the email is entirely editorial. There is no offer here. No percentage off, no member pricing, no BOGO, nothing. Just a beautiful grid of minty, cooling products with a "Shop Now" button that takes me to... probably a collection page. For a brand that's already burned twenty-one emails at me this month, sending email twenty-two as a zero-incentive brand moment is a swing I respect creatively and resent practically.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't click. I absolutely would not forward it — Charlotte Tilbury was running 50%+ off sale items this same week, Sally Beauty was doing 40% off, and even Sephora's major summer deals emails at least told me the number before asking me to shop. Lush is out here doing vibe-only marketing when I need a reason.

## What's working

- **The double pun is actually clever.** "I've got chills" subject → "MINT CONDITION" hero. That's earned, not forced.
- **Seasonal relevance is perfect.** Cooling, minty products in late June for summer heat? Absolutely on calendar. This is the right products for the right moment.
- **The grid layout is clean and scannable.** Pink Peppermint, Dirty, Olive Branch, Sports Rub, Breath of Fresh Air — I can identify distinct product personalities without squinting.
- **The warm orange palette against the mint theme creates real contrast.** Unexpected but it works visually and doesn't look like every other green-on-green "natural beauty" email.
- **"What Are Fresh Cosmetics (And Why Should You Care?)" section** is smart brand education that differentiates Lush from mass market — I appreciate that this exists even if I scroll past it.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** None. In a week where Charlotte Tilbury is cutting 50% and Sally Beauty is running 40% off, a pure "vibes + shop now" email from an overactive sender feels like a waste of my attention.
- **"Shop Now" is the laziest CTA Lush could have written after those puns.** "Get in Mint Condition" or "Chill Out, Shop Mint" would have kept the brand voice alive through to the button.
- **The product grid names are very small at mobile scale.** I had to squint to read "Breath Of Fresh Air" and "Whoosh" — if I'm on my phone in a bright room, I'm not reading those labels.
- **Twenty-two emails this month and still no loyalty acknowledgment.** I have no idea if Lush even has a points program, and after this volume of sends they still haven't told me why I should feel special as a subscriber.
- **The "Fresh Cosmetics" section at the bottom breaks the pacing.** It reads like a landing page appended to an email — switching from pun-driven product copy to educational FAQ copy mid-scroll is a tonal gear-shift I wasn't ready for.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one real offer.** Even a small one — "free shipping on mint orders this week" or "new subscriber: 10% off your first mint pick." Anything that gives me a reason to click beyond aesthetic appreciation. e.l.f. Hair's "frizz to rizz" email pulled a 9/10 partly because the product benefit was concrete and action-able, not just pretty.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" with on-theme CTA copy.** The pun infrastructure is already built — finish the job. "Stay Cool, Shop Mint" or "Enter Mint Condition →" takes 20 seconds and keeps the voice coherent.
- 3. **Surface a hero product instead of a collection.** Sephora's best summer deals emails anchored on one hero SKU with urgency language, then showed the grid as supporting cast. Lead with Dirty or Pink Peppermint as the hero, then cascade.
- 4. **Cut or relocate the Fresh Cosmetics education block.** Move it to a welcome series or a dedicated CRM touchpoint — don't drop it into what's supposed to be a summer product moment email.
- 5. **Renegotiate the June send cadence.** Twenty-two emails in a month is depleting whatever goodwill the puns build. Even the best-executed send suffers when I've already half-checked-out from the brand.
