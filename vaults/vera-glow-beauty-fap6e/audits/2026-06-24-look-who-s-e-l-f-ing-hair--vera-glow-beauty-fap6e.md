# Look Who's e.l.f.ing Hair 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Day fifteen of the e.l.f. streak. I know it's fifteen because I stopped being casual about it around day eight and started keeping count. Two days ago "Hair it is! 💁‍♀️ NEW e.l.f.ing launch" earned its 8/10 for being a genuine new-category announcement — clean product shelf, the "as cheap as my ex" energy e.l.f. does better than anyone at mass, real price anchoring. This email is the follow-up, and it is doing exactly what a follow-up does: drumming the same product lineup, reinforcing free shipping, running a "Top Picks for You" personalization module below the fold. The problem is that a follow-up needs a reason to exist beyond "we exist."
- The bones are solid. Blonde woman tossing hair, "LOOK WHO'S E.L.F.ING HAIR." in white on teal, the "meet the mama mane" product shelf with actual $7–$9 prices visible, a clear free-shipping CTA. Visual hierarchy holds. Charlotte Tilbury at email ten still managed to vary her creative — at fifteen, e.l.f. is running near-identical hero framing to the June 22 launch, same model energy, same SHOP NOW button in the same teal. I notice.
- I'd skim-archive this one. The June 22 send told me e.l.f. Hair existed and what it cost. This one tells me shipping is free. That's not nothing, but it's not enough to reopen a tab I probably already closed.

## What's working

- **Price anchoring in the product shelf** — "$7–$9" visible on the "meet the mama mane" row without scrolling; I didn't have to guess whether this was a prestige-price trap
- **Visual hierarchy reads in sequence** — hero → product shelf → offer → personalization module; nothing fights for attention, which is more than Ulta's cluttered Summer Deals sends can claim
- **"Top Picks for You" cross-category module** — the Face/Eyes/Skincare/Hair grid at the bottom is doing real work for a cross-category shopper like me; Sally Beauty and Lush never bother with this
- **Demographic fit is clean** — young woman, playful energy, accessible price anchors; zero "who is this even for" confusion
- **Free shipping offer is clearly staged** — dedicated section, "shipping's on us," with its own SHOP NOW — the offer isn't buried

## What's weak

- **It's the second e.l.f. Hair email in 48 hours and the creative barely moved** — the June 22 launch send had the same hero vibe; this needed one new hook (a bestseller rank, an early review, a "we restocked X already") to justify landing in my inbox again
- **"Limited time only" with zero date attached** — Glossier just burned me with a fake-urgency countdown that didn't end on June 19; "limited time" with no anchor date reads as exactly the same pattern now
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest CTA possible** — Charlotte Tilbury got specific with her sale language; "Shop e.l.f. Hair" or "Get the $9 Shampoo" gives me something to actually grab onto
- **No loyalty hook** — Sephora opens every email reminding me I'm Rouge; e.l.f. has Beauty Squad and never mentions it; that lever is just sitting there unpulled

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard date to the free shipping offer** — "Ships free through Sunday" is one line that turns "limited time" from a faint signal into a real countdown; this is the single highest-lift change
- 2. **Give the follow-up a reason to exist** — pull one early-traction signal (a bestseller claim, a "sold X in 48 hours," a first customer review) so send #2 feels like news, not a repeat
- 3. **Swap "SHOP NOW" for something specific** — "Shop e.l.f. Hair" or "Try the $9 Shampoo" — any verb-noun combo that tells me what I'm clicking into
- 4. **Surface the Beauty Squad hook** — even one line earns the loyalty math that lives rent-free in my head and tips close decisions

- **Subject Alt B:** `5 hair products. Under $9 each. Free shipping.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the mama mane: shampoo, conditioner & more for $7–$9`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on every e.l.f. Hair order — offer ends soon`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook Web) surface the title element. An empty title degrades screen reader context.

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` in `<head>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" ...>
```
Gmail (all variants), Yahoo, and Outlook strip `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Fonts will silently fall back to `Arial, Sans-serif`. This is acceptable only if the design is validated at fallback — confirm no broken spacing or visual hierarchy at fallback weight.

**Nested `nl-container` inside `html_block`:**
The first `html_block` (block-1) contains a fully self-contained `nl-container` table tree duplicating the outer wrapper structure. This double-wrapping is a template builder artifact and creates extra DOM nesting that can misfire in Outlook's Word renderer.

**Three empty `html_block` elements (block-2, block-3, block-4):**
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These appear to be blank spacer blocks left in from template editing. They add DOM weight with no layout value; remove or replace with proper `padding` on adjacent cells.

**Global link color/decoration override:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This resets all anchor styling in Gmail. Intended to prevent Gmail's blue auto-links, but will suppress underline and color on any text CTAs or the unsubscribe link unless those are explicitly re-styled inline. Verify unsubscribe link remains visually distinguishable.
