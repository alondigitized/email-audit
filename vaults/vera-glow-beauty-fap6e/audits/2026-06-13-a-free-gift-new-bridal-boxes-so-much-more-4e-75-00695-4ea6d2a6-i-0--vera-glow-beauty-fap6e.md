# A Free Gift, NEW! Bridal Boxes + So Much More! 💌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury is back, and today they decided to send me everything at once. One email, one scroll: a free gift offer, a wine-and-glam competition, a new mascara drop at 10% off, the new bridal boxes, a 20% off foundation deal, and a setting cream bundle. The "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" header is trying to frame this as a curated digest, but it reads less like a thoughtful edit and more like someone cleared the entire June campaign calendar into a single send.
- This lands as the fifth Charlotte Tilbury email in seven days — following the theatrical "TOO HOT To Load" glitch screen on June 11 and the "RSVP to Free Magic Skincare" drop on June 12. Compare that to how Glossier ran their Annual Sale arc this week: one email per beat, one clear message each time. Or how e.l.f. landed "skin tint summer" with a single product and a single vibe. Charlotte Tilbury is doing the exact opposite right now, and the result is a send that feels like inbox compensation rather than inbox strategy.
- I'd probably scroll through and bounce without clicking — unless I noticed the 20% off foundation deal, which is buried near the bottom and is genuinely the only offer in here that would stop my thumb. That's a real saving on a product I actually use, and Charlotte made me hunt for it.

## What's working

- The "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" framing at least signals what kind of email this is — a digest — so you're not confused expecting a single-hero promo
- The 20% off complexion deal is the strongest commercial hook here; that's real money on a prestige staple
- The bridal boxes section reads seasonally right for June, and Charlotte Tilbury genuinely has authority in the bridal beauty space
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapse despite packing in six distinct modules

## What's weak

- Subject line is kitchen-sink chaos: three competing value props ending with "so much more" — that closing phrase is the laziest thing in email marketing, full stop; compare to ILIA's "Get ready w/ HAIM," which says one thing with full conviction
- The free gift threshold is displayed in £ (British pounds) — if I'm a US subscriber, that's an immediate signal this copy wasn't built for me
- The wine-and-beauty competition module feels like padding; it's not driving a purchase, and it dilutes the 20% off offer that should be the hero
- No loyalty callout for existing customers — Sephora's "STARTS TODAY" sale email this week explicitly surfaced Rouge-tier priority access; Charlotte treats me like a cold prospect
- Five emails in seven days, and this one tries to hit every single topic simultaneously — that's not curation, that's backlog dumping

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and build the whole email around it.** The 20% off Magic Foundation Pro is the strongest commercial offer here — it should be the subject line, the hero image, and the only CTA above the fold. Everything else earns its own dedicated send or gets cut.
- 2. **Localize the currency or drop the threshold entirely.** Either flip £55+ to $65+ for US segments or write "free gift with purchase" without the amount. The pound symbol is an instant signal that I'm reading repurposed UK copy.
- 3. **Replace all "Shop Now" buttons with specific CTAs.** "Shop Foundation," "Claim Your Gift," "Explore Bridal Boxes" — each module needs a verb + a noun, not a generic placeholder.
- 4. **Kill or split the competition module.** "Win Glam & Wine" is a distraction from purchase-intent offers. Either give it its own standalone email or cut it from this one.
- 5. **Dial back the cadence before the unsubs start.** Five emails in a week is approaching Ulta territory during 21 Days — tolerable when each email has a clear, differentiated reason to exist; exhausting when they blur together like this.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off Magic Foundation Pro — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your CT bridal glow-up (+ a free gift) is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: new bridal boxes and a free mini with orders over $65`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your biggest Charlotte Tilbury savings — one place, right now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html

```
`<title>` is displayed in some webmail preview bars and browser tabs when the email is opened in a browser. An empty value is a missed opportunity and may show as blank in those contexts.

**External font `<link>` stripped by Gmail**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
The `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`  guard correctly excludes Outlook, but Gmail (the largest email client by share) strips `<link>` tags entirely. If `fonts.css` is the only source for custom typefaces, Gmail recipients will fall back to system fonts with no declared fallback stack visible in the `<head>`. Confirm that inline `font-family` stacks in body text include safe web-safe fallbacks (e.g., `Georgia, serif`).

**`box-sizing: border-box` on `*`**
```css
*{box-sizing:border-box}
```
Non-standard in email; not supported in Outlook (Word renderer ignores it). Unlikely to cause layout breaks since Outlook layout is handled via table/VML, but worth noting if precise padding math is load-bearing on hybrid cells.
