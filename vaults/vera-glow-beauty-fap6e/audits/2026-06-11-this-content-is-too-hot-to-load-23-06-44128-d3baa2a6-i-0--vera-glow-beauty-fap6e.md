# This Content Is TOO HOT To Load 🔥
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury just pulled the "your phone is overheating" fake glitch screen on me, and I have to respect the creative ambition even as I clock exactly what they're doing: they're withholding the product behind an app download. The UNREAL Highlighter isn't available to buy in this email — you have to download the CT app to "be first to shop" when it drops. That's not a launch, that's a lead-gen funnel dressed up in a summer glow editorial.
- Two days ago this was "There's A Reason It's Called Unreal" and now it's "Content Is TOO HOT To Load." Same product, back-to-back, with the second one being more friction, not less. The June 3rd four-free-eyeshadows send genuinely earned its 8/10 because it led with a gift you could actually claim. This is the opposite energy — tease the product, hide the CTA behind an app store redirect.
- I'm marking this read. I'm not downloading the app for a highlighter I could just go to their website for. I would not forward this to a friend unless she was specifically asking "has CT released the new UNREAL Highlighter yet," and even then I'd just text her the name.

## What's working

- The overheating phone gimmick is genuinely clever art direction — the fake system alert ties the subject line directly to the hero visual in a way that at least made me look twice.
- "Like Skincare In A Stick. NEW! Glow-Boosting Glaze." is clean, benefit-led product copy. That framing would convert me if it went somewhere.
- The "Free Mini Magic Gift at $115 on the App" is a concrete offer — spend threshold is clear, the gift product is visible in the image.
- Summer badge in the corner is a nice light touch on seasonal relevance without screaming SUMMER SALE.
- Layout is clean, no broken images, no overlapping text — technically solid render.

## What's weak

- The whole email's primary CTA is **download an app**, not shop a product. That's two extra steps between me and the highlighter I'm already being teased with.
- They already sent me "There's A Reason It's Called Unreal" on June 9 — this is effectively the same product launch beat, two days later, with more friction added. Repetition fatigue is real.
- The UNREAL Highlighter itself is a headline and some product copy floating on the left — there's no close-up product shot, no swatches, no shade range. For a highlighter launch, that's a miss. e.l.f. SKIN's "Take spray into your day" email showed the product three different ways and it scored an 8.
- Zero loyalty angle. I'm doing Sephora Rouge math in my head constantly, and Charlotte Tilbury has no point system to speak of. The "magical savings up to 30%" feels vague versus Ulta Diamond's actual tier benefit clarity.
- The app pitch for "exclusive beauty secrets" is weak. That's not a reason to download anything in 2026.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the product shoppable from the email.** Add a direct "Shop UNREAL Highlighter" CTA that goes to the website, then a secondary "or shop first on the App." You're leaving conversions on the table by forcing the app detour.
- 2. **Show the product properly.** One close-up swatch shot — glowy skin, the stick itself — would do more work than the phone gimmick. Glossier's editorial-to-product pipeline is the benchmark here; they never let a concept bury the thing you're supposed to want.
- 3. **Differentiate from the June 9 send.** If you're emailing me twice about the UNREAL Highlighter in 48 hours, send two the second gives me something the first didn't — a shade reveal, a skin type match, a waitlist count. This one just repeats the tease.
- 4. **Tighten the bottom section.** The "Discover the Magic of charlottetilbury.com + App" grid of four benefits is filler. Every brand says "find your perfect shade" and "exclusive secrets." Cut it or replace it with one high-conviction line about the highlighter.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New UNREAL Highlighter drops on the app first — be ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Glow-Boosting Glaze is coming. App access only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh, Healthy Glow Stick lands this week — download the app to shop it first`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $115 on the app, get a free mini gift. New launch inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Apple Mail, webmail) surface the `<title>` value. An empty title is a missed fallback and may display placeholder text in certain environments.

**External font stylesheet (blocked by major clients):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (both web and Android) and all Outlook versions strip `<link>` tags. If the CSS is not also inlined or duplicated in the `<style>` block, custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts — or worse, break layout if any sizing/spacing rules are font-dependent. Conditional comment correctly gates this from MSO, but Gmail is the primary concern.

**Image alt text:** The logo `<img>` inside the first `<a>` tag is visible but the `alt` attribute value is not shown in the truncated source. All product images in `image_block` rows should be verified to carry descriptive `alt` text — critical for screen readers and image-blocked render environments (Outlook default blocks images).

These are mismatched in intent — the subject implies exclusivity/mystery, the preheader implies a generic signup call. While not a rendering bug, this is a technical configuration error in the preheader slot.
