# Third one’s the charm
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Day two of the same Buy 3, Save 20% sale — and honestly, ILIA's creative team earned their check today. "Third One's the Charm" with the dice visual is a genuinely smart way to frame the BUY THREE mechanic on the follow-up send. Compare that to yesterday's "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now," which just said the thing. Today's version at least makes me smile before I get to the offer. The "keep your eyes on..." section — focused entirely on eye products — reads like an actual edit, not a full-catalog dump. That restraint is doing work.
- But here's where I go full spreadsheet brain: ILIA doesn't have a loyalty program, which means buying DTC means giving up Sephora Rouge points on the same products. The email never once acknowledges that I have that math running in the background. "20% off" is a good number, but so is "2× points on a $100 ILIA haul during a Sephora Stacking Weekend." I need a DTC reason that closes that gap, and this email doesn't offer one. Also — "Too low for skinny." floating below the first CTA button is the kind of copy fragment that makes me stop and squint at my screen. Is that an exclusion? A minimum-order note? A design artifact? I genuinely don't know, and that moment of hesitation is friction ILIA didn't need to create.
- I'd click — the Eye Stylus Trio has been on my radar and Buy 3 is the right math to pull the trigger on a trio purchase. But I would not forward this to a friend, because she already got this offer yesterday.

## What's working

- **"Third One's the Charm" + dice visual**: the subject pun connects cleanly to the BUY THREE mechanism without just shouting "REMINDER: SALE STILL ON." It's the kind of playful creative that Drunk Elephant does well and ILIA doesn't always pull off. Today they pulled it off.
- **Code in the hero banner, top of email**: "BUY 3, SAVE 20% WITH CODE BUNDLEUP" sits right at the top before anything else. No hunt required. Drunk Elephant buried their code below three product shots last week — ILIA's layout wins here.
- **"keep your eyes on..." section**: organizing the product showcase around an eye theme is a tight editorial choice. Makes the email feel curated, not "we sell eight categories, here's all of them."
- **"Award-winning Eye Stylus Shadow Stick" claim**: social proof I can act on. Not "fans love it," an actual credential.
- **Product descriptions are specific**: "12 hours without creasing," "visibly brightens, hydrates, smooths + depuffs" — real performance claims I can evaluate against what I already own.

## What's weak

- **"Too low for skinny." below the first CTA**: I don't know what this means. Category exclusion? Minimum order note? It reads like truncated fine print or a broken merge token and it creates a hesitation beat right before the click.
- **Same offer, day two, nothing new**: No end date added, no new product highlight, no "selling out fast" hook. After eleven-plus consecutive ILIA sends, a day-two repeat with zero escalation reads as cadence pressure, not editorial urgency.
- **Zero DTC rationale**: Sephora and Ulta carry ILIA. I'm doing points math in my head and the email offers no answer. Even one line — "These trios ship directly, no waitlist" or "This bundle isn't available at Sephora" — closes that mental tab.
- **No end date anywhere**: "20% off" without a deadline is the beauty industry's equivalent of "limited time offer." I'll add to cart, forget, and the sale will end while I'm deep in a Sephora haul instead.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an end date — anywhere, any size.** "Ends Sunday night" in the hero subheadline would make me act today instead of adding to cart and ghosting. Sally Beauty puts countdown language in almost every email I reviewed this week — even something that simple would lift this.
- 2. **Fix or kill "Too low for skinny."** Either write a proper exclusions note ("*Excludes items under $X and existing sale") or remove the fragment entirely. Confusion at the CTA is the worst place to create doubt.
- 3. **Give me one DTC advantage.** A single line addressing why to buy here instead of banking Sephora points. e.l.f. doesn't have this problem because their DTC prices are often genuinely better — ILIA needs to make the same argument in their context.
- 4. **Escalate the second-wave creative.** Day two of a sale should bring something new: a "top-seller moving fast" callout, a real customer quote, or a single hero SKU singled out as almost gone. e.l.f.'s July 2 send ("Lock the e.l.f. in with this NEW duo") introduced genuinely new product to break cadence fatigue — ILIA could do the same even within an ongoing sale.
- 5. **Clarify "the power of free sale" hero text.** "Free sale" reads oddly — if this is the campaign name, it needs one more word of context; if "free" is referencing a badge element, the hierarchy isn't landing on mobile.
