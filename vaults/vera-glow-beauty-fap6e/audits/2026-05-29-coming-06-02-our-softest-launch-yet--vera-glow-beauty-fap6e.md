# Coming 06.02—our *softest* launch yet
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay. So. ILIA just sent me three emails today alone about their Friends + Family sale ending — I've been tracking, and this is genuinely email nineteen from them this month — and now, within hours of the final "20% off ends tonight" drumbeat, they pivot to a teaser launch. The whiplash is real. But here's the thing: I'm not mad about it. The creative direction earns the reset. That powdery, skin-textured hero with ILIA spelled into the dust and "shift your focus" underneath? That's not accidental. "Softest launch yet" plus a visually tactile, swept-powder aesthetic is doing actual communication work — my brain immediately goes to a new setting powder, blurring skin tint, or an ultra-light complexion product, and I want to know which.
- The problem is that ILIA keeps all that intrigue to themselves. No product name. No ingredient peek. No "here's one thing you need to know" hook. Just JOIN THE WAITLIST. With zero context. After drowning me in sale urgency all day, they're now asking me to take an action purely on vibes. Compare that to Charlotte Tilbury's recent "Complete Your Exagger-Eyes Look With FREE Minis" — Charlotte earned the click by giving me a concrete reason to move. ILIA is banking entirely on brand love, and while I do have brand love, this sequence is testing it.
- I'd probably click the waitlist anyway — because "softest launch yet" + that dusty powder visual is exactly my kind of beauty intrigue. But I wouldn't forward this. There's nothing concrete enough to share, and my friends would open it and go "what even is this?" Mark as read, file it mentally as June 2 on the calendar, revisit then.

## What's working

- **The hero image is doing real product communication.** The swept, dusty powder texture physically signals "soft finish" before a single word registers — it's genuinely smart creative direction.
- **"Shift your focus" lands as a double entendre.** "Focus" for a soft-focus finish, plus a directional pivot away from the sale. That's intentional and it works.
- **The date is prominent and specific.** "Sweeping in on 06.02.26" gives me exactly the calendar peg I need — as someone who tracks the beauty event calendar, I appreciate the precision.
- **CTA is unambiguous.** "JOIN THE WAITLIST +" is dark on light, centered, clear verb-noun — no confusion about what clicking does.
- **The aesthetic matches me.** Clean, skin-tone palette, no clutter, no gimmick fonts — feels like a brand that knows its customer is a 28-35-year-old who shops clean beauty at Sephora, not Forever 21.

## What's weak

- **Zero product context is a significant ask.** After nineteen emails from ILIA — many of which led with specific products and percentages — asking me to join a waitlist with no product name, no category callout, no "here's a hint" is a lot. Glossier's Pink Capsule launch email scored a 5 with me for the same sin: beautiful teaser, no substance.
- **The cadence whiplash is jarring even if the pivot is smart.** Three "sale ends" emails today followed immediately by a calm teaser is good strategy but still creates a rough gear shift in my inbox.
- **Footer value props are illegible at mobile size.** The row of benefit icons — VIP, Shade Match, Auto-Replenish — is so small I can barely parse them. If ILIA wants to communicate membership value, it deserves space.
- **No preheader signal to complement the curiosity subject.** The subject does intrigue work; if the preview text either leaked a hint ("a complexion product unlike anything we've done before") or added urgency, it would push the open rate meaningfully.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give me one hint in the body.** Even a single line like "a new complexion formula — arriving June 2" would close the gap between "intriguing" and "I need this on my calendar." The waitlist ask needs a reason.
- 2. **Let the preheader do storytelling work.** Right now it's either generic or not optimized — see alternatives below.
- 3. **Upgrade the footer benefit row.** Either remove it (clutter at small size adds nothing) or give each icon a real readable label. Personalized Shade Match is a genuine differentiator; don't bury it.
- 4. **Consider one product texture/ingredient tease in the lower hero.** Drunk Elephant consistently drops an ingredient or format name in their launch teasers — "barrier-supporting," "serum-infused" — and it does real work for beauty junkies.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some screen readers (NVDA + Thunderbird) announce the document title. A space is better than nothing but still fails accessibility expectations.

**Duplicate `img` CSS rule:**
The `<style>` block declares `img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; ... }` twice — once in the standard reset block and once in the Klaviyo-specific block. The second declaration adds `max-width: 100%` which is intentional, but the duplication is unnecessary dead weight.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` — unreliable in email clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Gmail (web and iOS), Apple Mail (some versions), and all Outlook versions. Montserrat will silently fall back to Helvetica/Arial in the majority of opens. The `display=swap` parameter is a web browser hint with no effect in email rendering. Fallbacks (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) are present and will apply — this is a rendering fidelity issue, not a breakage.

**All anchor states locked to identical style:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Visited links are visually indistinguishable from unvisited. Hover state provides no affordance. This is a common email pattern to suppress blue underlines, but it removes all interactive feedback for sighted users.

**No `alt` text visible on images** in the truncated source. Cannot confirm — must audit full render.
