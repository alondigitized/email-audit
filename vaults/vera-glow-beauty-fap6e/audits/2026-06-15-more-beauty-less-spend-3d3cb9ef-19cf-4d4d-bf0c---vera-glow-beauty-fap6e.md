# More Beauty, Less Spend 💕
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Eighteen. That's where I am. The June 12 "Fresh Finds for Summer" was seventeen, and before that was the June 11 BOGO Hair Care send — still sitting unread two folders down — and now Sally comes back with "More Beauty, Less Spend." Pink heart emoji. That's the swing they took on email number eighteen in three weeks. Not "20% off It's a 10 today only," not "BOGO gel polish — this weekend only," just... a vague promise that beauty will cost me less. Cool.
- Which is annoying, because the email inside isn't actually terrible. The hero is clean: a woman with that effortless summer-blowout energy, "The summer looks you love for less" in big legible type, a red "Shop now" button that loads first. Below that, real deals — 20% off It's a 10 treatments, BOGO 50% on hair care, buy two get one gel polish. These are things I'd actually buy. The Sally Rewards callout (earn $5 when you spend $50) is visible mid-scroll, and the "keep your color looking fresh" block at the bottom is legitimately useful for anyone on a toning routine. Fine! This is fine!
- But then it keeps going. Six separate promotions stacked with basically equal visual weight, ending in a clippers and trimmers section that has genuinely nothing to do with me. The deal avalanche creates choice paralysis instead of click momentum, and the Sally Rewards 10% return rate gets awkward when my Sephora Rouge welcome email offered early sale access and triple points. I'll bookmark the gel polish BOGO, mark this read, and open the next email knowing full well another one is coming in roughly 48 hours.

## What's working

- Hero image lands clean and aspirational — woman, great summer hair, tight headline, unmissable red CTA button right at the top
- Concrete deals are real: 20% off It's a 10, BOGO 50% hair care, buy 2 get 1 gel polish — actual percentages I can do math on
- Sally Rewards loyalty callout is visible and placed well mid-email, not buried in the footer
- The toning/blue shampoo "keep your color fresh" block at the bottom is seasonally smart for color-treated hair (split ends, brass control)
- No render bugs, no broken image placeholders, layout holds cleanly

## What's weak

- "More Beauty, Less Spend" is a philosophy, not a subject line — no product, no amount, no deadline; the June 12 "Fresh Finds for Summer" was already a lazy punt, and this is worse
- Six equal-weight deal blocks create an undifferentiated scroll-fest; nothing gets hero treatment, so nothing pulls a confident click
- The clippers and trimmers section is dead weight for my profile — that's a barber supply add, not a prestige-adjacent beauty email
- Sally Rewards "Earn $5 when you spend $50" is a 10% return rate, which is fine on paper, but gets flattened next to Sephora Rouge or even Ulta Diamond benefits I'm used to seeing flexed in emails
- Eighteen sends in three weeks with zero cadence variation — Glossier did five consecutive days in June with the same fragrance subject line twice and I almost muted them; Sally is approaching that same edge

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one deal and hero it.** The buy-two-get-one gel polish is a genuinely good hook for at-home nail people — make that the entire hero, show a product shot, and write a CTA that says "Shop Gel Polish Deals" not "Shop now." Kill the six-stack.
- 2. **Put a number or a deadline in the subject.** "More Beauty, Less Spend 💕" tells me nothing. Charlotte Tilbury gave me "Get Your Summer Glow To Go, Darling!" on June 14 and it still had a specific product angle — even that is better than pure aspiration with no offer signal.
- 3. **Cut the clippers/trimmers block** for any segment skewing hair-and-nail beauty consumer versus cosmetology professional. Replace it with more nail content, a skin-prep summer tie-in, or a second color-care feature.
- 4. **Pull the cadence back to 2×/week max.** Eighteen sends in three weeks is list attrition in progress. e.l.f. sent six consecutive emails in June, executed much better creative, and I still nearly muted them — Sally's creative can't carry this frequency.
- 5. **Lean into the rewards rate transparency.** "Earn $5 when you spend $50" is buriable fine print right now; frame it as "10% back on every order — add to cart" and it actually sounds like a reason to transact.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font fallback gap — confirmed issue:**
```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```
`Founders Grotesk` is referenced as the primary link font, but **no `@font-face` declaration for it exists in the `<head>`**. Only Open Sans variants are declared. In clients that don't load external fonts (Gmail, Outlook, most mobile), this silently falls back to Helvetica — acceptable, but the intent and the implementation are mismatched. If Founders Grotesk is expected to render, it needs either an `@font-face` block or a hosted woff2 URL.

**Outdated Google Fonts version — minor:**
All Open Sans `@font-face` rules reference `opensans/v17/`. Current CDN version is v29+. v17 assets are still served by Google but the `local()` fallback hints (`local('OpenSans-Regular')` etc.) use old PostScript names that no longer match the system font on macOS 13+ / Windows 11. Result: the `local()` short-circuit will miss on modern OS installs, forcing a CDN fetch that could be avoided.

**Mobile breakpoint — minor:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)
```
Industry standard for email is `600px`. Devices with viewport widths 500–599px (common on small Android phones) will receive the desktop layout. This affects stacking, image scaling, and font-size overrides.

**`img` CSS anomaly:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is a no-op (images are replaced elements) but is technically invalid for this element type. Not a rendering risk, but a template hygiene flag.

**Alt text:** Cannot evaluate without full source — verify all `<img>` tags have non-empty `alt` attributes, especially hero and product images, for screen reader and images-off rendering.
