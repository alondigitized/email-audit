# Save up to 50% on select beauty 💄 Plus spend $75 to earn 500 bonus points.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Stacking Weekend" — I see what Sephora is doing here and honestly, the math is the thing that hooked me. Fifty percent off *and* 500 bonus points when you hit $75? As someone who tracks every Rouge tier threshold, that stacking mechanic is exactly the kind of offer that makes me open a calculator before I even scroll. The "TODAY ONLY" banner at the top creates real urgency, which is a sharp pivot from the last two Sephora sends — those "WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS" editorial pieces on May 17 were content-forward and cool, but this is a sale email that actually sells.
- The problem is the email tries to do too much. Once you get past the hero, it fractures into "Save on These Must Haves," then "Spot It. Shop It.," then Frizz-Smoothing Picks, then Body Mints — the product mix is so broad it stops feeling curated and starts feeling like a browse-everything landing page jammed into an email. I want to know which *specific* prestige launches are included in the 50% off, because "select beauty" is doing a lot of heavy lifting and I've been burned by that language before.
- I'd mark it read and click through to check what's actually discounted — but I wouldn't forward it. Compare that to the Drunk Elephant Friends & Family send I reviewed recently (25% off, clean hero, one CTA, straight to the sale page) — that email made me text three people immediately. This one feels more like homework.

## What's working

- **The stacking mechanic lands.** 50% off + 500 bonus points for $75 is actually two discrete wins in one send, and Sephora puts both numbers right in the hero — no scrolling to find the secondary offer.
- **"TODAY ONLY" creates genuine urgency.** It's above the fold, in a banner, no asterisk hedging it into oblivion. That's credible.
- **The barcode at the bottom is a nice touch.** In-store redemption signal tells me Sephora is thinking omni-channel — I shop the Stacking Weekend in-store too, so seeing a scannable code matters.
- **Visual hierarchy at the hero is clean.** The 50% number and the 500 points badge are both visually distinct and don't compete with each other.
- **Beauty Insider loyalty framing is present.** The points structure assumes I'm a member — it treats me like a known customer, not a cold prospect.

## What's weak

- **"Select beauty" is vague and trust-eroding.** After every Rouge event where I've hunted exclusions, I instinctively distrust "select." Tell me which brands or categories are in — even one example hero product would help.
- **The product mix is all over the place.** Frizz-Smoothing Picks *and* Body Mints *and* skincare must-haves in one scroll? ILIA's "The base layer: 20% off skincare" — one category, one CTA — made it so much easier to commit to a click.
- **"Shop Now" CTA copy is lazy.** It's Sephora's most generic button. For a "Stacking Weekend," something like "Stack My Savings" or "Shop the 50% Off Edit" would actually match the campaign name.
- **The subject line is doing too much.** Two separate offers crammed into one line plus an emoji — it reads like two emails got merged. Charlotte Tilbury's "20% OFF Charlotte's Best-Selling Beauty Icons!" is half the length and tells me everything I need.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the 50% off to at least one named hero product or category in the email body.** Show me the Tatcha or the Drunk Elephant item that's actually half off — vague "select" language makes me expect a page full of no-name brands I don't want.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to match the "Stacking" theme.** "Stack My Savings" or "Shop the 50% Edit" — something that pays off the campaign name instead of defaulting to a generic button.
- 3. **Cut the email to three sections max.** Hero offer → one curated product grid (prestige, not mass) → loyalty/barcode block. Right now the Frizz and Body Mints sections dilute the urgency the hero built.
- 4. **Surface the exclusions or, better, the inclusions.** Even a "Includes: [3 brand logos]" strip under the hero builds way more trust than "select beauty."
- 5. **Split-test the subject — the secondary offer deserves its own send or the primary needs to own the line.**

- **Subject Alt B:** `TODAY ONLY: 50% off + 500 bonus points`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75, earn 500 Beauty Insider points — only today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Rouge perks are stacking. Shop the 50% off edit now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — zoom disabled (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. Combined with the next issue, text becomes unresizable for visually impaired users.

**Issue — text-size-adjust: none applied globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Suppresses OS-level font scaling across essentially every element. This is the standard Sephora shell pattern but, paired with `maximum-scale=1`, fully blocks text resizing.

**Issue — missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers (JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver) require this to select correct pronunciation rules.

**Issue — malformed robots meta value:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a recognized directive; the correct token is `noindex`. Inconsequential for email rendering but signals a template copy-paste error.

**Minor — narrow mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { ... }
```
Devices wider than 480px logical pixels (e.g., most Android tablets, some large-phone landscape views) receive the desktop fixed-table layout. Industry standard has moved to 600px.
