# BFF + SPF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ILIA just made a hard pivot. Four emails in a row were HAIM, HAIM, Este Haim quote, HAIM beauty album — and then June 16 rolls in with "BFF + SPF" and two real women named Emily and Aissa instead of a celebrity collab. Honestly? The creative breath of fresh air landed. The "same but different" concept — two friends, different skin tones and needs, same Skin Tint SPF — is genuinely the kind of thing I'll pause for in my inbox. It's a summer story that makes sense right now, and it doesn't feel like ILIA is just running the same HAIM asset on a loop.
- That said, this email is doing brand storytelling, not driving a transaction. There's no offer. No points bonus. No "last day" energy. It's a beautifully executed editorial piece, and I respect it, but I'm not necessarily clicking through to buy — I'm more likely to screenshot the "same but different" headline and send it to my friend group chat as a vibe check.
- I'd read it. I wouldn't click to buy right now. I'd forward it to one friend specifically as a "okay but this is US."

## What's working

- **"Same but different" concept is sharp** — the two-friends, different-skin-needs-but-same-SPF angle is inclusive without being performatively so. It feels earned.
- **Real people testimonials** — Emily and Aissa's quotes feel authentic in a way that the HAIM collab didn't; this isn't a celebrity using a product I'll never fully relate to.
- **Summer timing is perfect** — SPF content hitting June 16 is exactly right. Compare this to how Glossier's sale emails this week felt season-agnostic; ILIA is anchoring to the calendar.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero image drops into headline, into testimonials, into product shot. My eye knows exactly where to go.
- **No render bugs** — after a week of emails where Sephora's sale renders felt rushed and cluttered, this one is crisp.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — I'm a Rouge and a Diamond. I am always doing points math. There's no incentive layer here — no GWP, no shade-finder CTA, no "new shades now available." Compare this to e.l.f.'s "Bestie" BFF-coded send earlier this month that still had a concrete offer underneath the cute copy. ILIA is leaving conversion on the table.
- **CTA specificity is fuzzy** — "Shop the Skin Tint" gets me to the product page, but after a testimonial-heavy email I want something more specific like "Find your shade" or "See Emily's shade + Aissa's shade." The generic shop CTA feels like a missed moment.
- **No urgency signal at all** — not even a soft one. SPF has seasonal logic built in; ILIA could've leaned on "summer's here" or "limited shades" without it feeling fake.
- **HAIM pivot is abrupt with no bridge** — four HAIM sends in five days and then cold-turkey switch to Emily and Aissa. A small nod ("not just for rockstars") would've helped thread the arc.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a soft offer or loyalty hook** — Even "Earn 2x points on Skin Tint this week" buried below the hero would activate my Rouge brain without disrupting the editorial feel. Right now this is a brand awareness play in a conversion medium.
- 2. **Make the CTA do more work** — Change "Shop the Skin Tint" to "Find your shade — Emily's or Aissa's?" Mirrors the concept, reduces decision paralysis, feels like a natural extension of the "same but different" story.
- 3. **Add a shade-range signal** — I can see two skin tones represented but I can't tell from this email whether my shade is in there. One line — "Available in 30 shades" — would close that loop.
- 4. **Thread the HAIM arc** — One sentence acknowledging the pivot would make this feel intentional rather than random. ILIA's brand voice can handle that kind of meta-awareness.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Same SPF. Different skin. One Skin Tint.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your BFF's SPF is your SPF`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Emily's dry. Aissa breaks out. Both swear by the same shade.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Skin Tint that works for two very different skin types — find yours.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some assistive technologies and tab labels surface the title. Should be set to the email subject or brand name.

**CSS `@import` for web fonts — cross-client failure risk:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (both web and app) strips `@import` entirely. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica`/`sans-serif` for the majority of recipients. This is expected behavior for email, but worth knowing if pixel-perfect font rendering is a goal. No fix needed unless brand requires it — just document the fallback.

**Inconsistent heading font stacks:**
- `h1, h2, h3`: `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`

`h4` elements will never render Montserrat even when other headings do.
