# Look who we ran into
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so ILIA skipped the promo format entirely today and just... sent me vacation photos. Sasha ran into the Crown Affair founder poolside in Ibiza, cute story, but it takes four selfies and three paragraphs before I even get to "here's a product." This is email number ten-ish in eleven days from this brand and today's beat is "founder had a nice trip" — after yesterday's confusing sensitive-skin brief and the plain-text "2 free minis" disaster, I genuinely can't tell what ILIA's inbox strategy is anymore.
- The Crown Affair collab itself is actually interesting to me — I don't own their Leave-In Conditioner or the hair towel, and pairing it with ILIA's Face Milk and Limitless Lash into "The Out-the-Door Ritual" is a smart bundle idea. But there's no discount, no bundle price, no button — just an underlined text link buried in paragraph two. Compare that to Ulta's "20% off one qualifying item" email that scored a 9 with me last week — barcode hero, clear code, done in five seconds. This one asks me to read a personal essay first.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about the towel. Would I forward it? No — this reads like a brand newsletter, not something a friend needs to see. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The photo grid at the top is genuinely well-shot and feels aspirational without being try-hard — matches ILIA's whole "effortless" branding.
- Sasha signing it personally with a handwritten-style signature is a nice trust cue, feels more authentic than a faceless "ILIA Team" send.
- The Crown Affair collab concept — pairing hair care and makeup founders — is a clever story hook, more original than most of what's landed in my inbox this month.
- Footer trust badges (1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, Personalized Shade Match, ILIA VIP) are all still there, so at least the loyalty program signal exists somewhere on the page.

## What's weak

- Zero discount, zero urgency, zero CTA button — just a text link. Even Glossier's balm email last week, which I dinged for having no offer, at least had product photography doing the selling.
- The subject "Look who we ran into" tells me nothing about beauty, launches, or points — I almost skipped it.
- No mention of my Rouge or Diamond status anywhere, not even a passing "VIP" nod tied to the collab.
- This is the third or fourth format ILIA has cycled through in under two weeks (plain-text, GWP countdown, education brief, now founder-story) — the inconsistency itself is starting to feel like noise, not strategy.
- The hero images are 100% travel content — if I'm skimming on my commute I could scroll past the first screen and have no idea this is a product email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the product, not the vacation — flip the structure so The Out-the-Door Ritual bundle and a real CTA button sit above the fold, then let the Ibiza story live as flavor text underneath for people who want it.
- 2. Add an actual offer — even a "bundle and save" price on the three-piece ritual would give me a reason to click today instead of "someday."
- 3. Put a shop button under the product image at the bottom instead of leaving me to hunt for the underlined "Crown Affair x ILIA collection" link in body copy.
- 4. Tie the VIP badge to something concrete in the body — "ILIA VIP gets early access to the towel restock" — instead of just leaving it as a static footer icon.
