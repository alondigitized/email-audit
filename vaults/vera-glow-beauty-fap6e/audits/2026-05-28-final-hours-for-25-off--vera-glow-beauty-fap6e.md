# Final hours for 25% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Email thirteen. I have to acknowledge that before I say anything else — this is the thirteenth Drunk Elephant Friends & Family email, and it arrives the morning after email twelve told me, in writing, "We extended the sale…but this is it." That quote lives in my brain now. "This is it." And yet here we are: "Final hours for 25% off!" The urgency has been cried wolf so many times at this point that the countdown timer — which is genuinely good design, I want to credit it — lands more like set decoration than a real deadline.
- That said, I'll give credit where it's earned: the email itself is clean and fast. The countdown is front-and-center, the 25% off hero is unmissable, and the "Mask Me Anything" GWP section is the first genuinely new creative hook in at least four emails. A full-size F-Balm with $175 is compelling because the F-Balm is beloved and it's positioned as a product-specific reward, not just a discount.
- Would I click? Possibly — if I had a cart sitting there from three days ago. But it's not because this email earned it. It's because Drunk Elephant is a brand I genuinely use and the discount is real. The email didn't do that; the product relationship did. I would not forward this to a friend, because I've already sent them email seven and I'm embarrassed enough.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is front-loaded and live** — 6 hours showing means I'd glance at the clock and calculate whether I have time before work. That's behavioral, not just decorative.
- **25% off hero is completely unambiguous** — big, centered, no scroll required. Zero confusion about the offer.
- **"Mask Me Anything" GWP is new creative** — spending thirteen emails on the same 25% off message and finally introducing a product-specific free-gift hook is smart. The F-Balm is a recognizable prestige SKU.
- **Drunk Elephant's voice is intact** — "No FOMO here!" and the playful "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS?" banners feel like them, not a generic retailer. Compare to how Ulta's tiered spend reminders read like a bill statement.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks** — product photos stack well, the hierarchy goes countdown → offer → CTA → bonus offer. No overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- **"This is it" credibility destroyed** — I literally screenshot yesterday's email because it said "but this is it" and now I'm looking at "Final hours." This is the bait-and-switch version of urgency: not fraud, but not trustworthy either. ILIA's "Time to tie up 20% off" (also deep in their run) at least had subject-line wit that felt intentional, not an accidental extension.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible CTA** — after thirteen emails, "SHOP NOW" asks for a lot of goodwill. Charlotte Tilbury's sends never just say "Discover" either — they at least tie the CTA to a specific product. This could be "Claim Your 25% Off" or "Shop Before It Ends" and get more pull.
- **No price anchoring on hero products** — there are visible product shots but no "The Protini: $56 → $42" type callout. Sally Beauty's final-hours energy always includes a price drop to anchor the click. Drunk Elephant has the product lineup to do this but doesn't.
- **"SHOP SHOP SHOP!" preheader is breathless filler** — this is the inbox preview and it reads like a meme, not a closing statement from a prestige brand.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to a hard timestamp** — the countdown is good design but "final hours" is a phrase, not a promise. "Sale ends tonight at 11:59pm ET — no extensions" is the version that has actual teeth. The word "extensions" does heavy lifting here given the campaign history.
- 2. **Lead with the GWP** — "Free full-size F-Balm with $175+ — final hours" is a stronger subject than the thirteenth iteration of "25% off." It's new information. Use it.
- 3. **Upgrade the CTA** — "Get My 25% Off" or "Claim 25% Off Sitewide" over "SHOP NOW." Verb + specific noun. The rubric exists for a reason.
- 4. **Add one hero product with price callout** — anchor the deal to something real. Pick the Protini or the Babyfacial, show $56 → $42, make someone do math in their head. That's how prestige beauty emails get clicks.
- 5. **Kill "SHOP SHOP SHOP!" as a preheader** — it reads like it was written by someone caffeinated at 6am. Replace with the GWP hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"6 hours. Free F-Balm ends tonight too."`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"It genuinely ends tonight. (We mean it this time.)"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Free full-size F-Balm with $175+ — plus 25% off sitewide until midnight"`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Your last real window: 25% off + a free F-Balm at $175+"`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: HTML comments embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block**
Two rules inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block are wrapped in HTML comment syntax:
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are invalid inside `<style>` blocks. These rules are silently dropped by all conforming CSS parsers, meaning `contenttable` and `mobile` elements will **not** resize on mobile. This is a functional rendering bug.

**Triple font loading — same fonts requested three times each:**
Brown Bold, Brown-Light, and Brown-Thin are each loaded via:
1. A `<link>` tag in `<head>` pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`
2. An `@import` inside `<style>` pointing to the same URLs
3. An `@font-face` block inside `<style>` pointing to the same URLs

This generates redundant HTTP requests on every open, inflating load time. `<link>` and `@import` for the same resource is doubly wasteful; combining both with a third `@font-face` block is an authoring error, not intentional fallback.

**Third-party font CDN (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`):**
Three of the four font families are served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN not operated by Drunk Elephant. `BrownRegular` is correctly self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/`. The three externally-hosted fonts are subject to CDN outages, privacy proxy blocking, and corporate firewall filtering — all of which will silently fall back to the stack's web-safe fallback. Acceptable risk in email if fallbacks are defined, but the inconsistency is an authoring gap.

**`.sm { display:none; }` — no corresponding show rule visible:**
A `.sm` class hides elements by default. No `display:block !important` counterpart is visible in the provided media queries for `.sm`. If used on mobile-only content, those elements may be permanently hidden. Requires full-source verification.

**Aggressive global CSS reset:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; max-height: 1000000px; }
```
The `max-height: 1000000px` hack on `*` is a Gmail clipping workaround, implying the rendered email is at or near Gmail's 102KB clip threshold. Should trigger a file-size audit of the full HTML.
