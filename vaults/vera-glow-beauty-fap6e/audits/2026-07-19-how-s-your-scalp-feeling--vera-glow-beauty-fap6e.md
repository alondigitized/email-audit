# ✨ How's your scalp feeling?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Email thirty. I've been tracking Lush the way I track the Sephora Beauty Insider countdown, and thirty is a number. "How's your scalp feeling?" is at least asking me a real beauty question — which is more than I can say for the "Boo! Halloween is here!" drop that landed three days ago on July 16th. Coming off that particular unhinged moment, this haircare pivot feels like Lush remembered what it is. Scalp care in summer? Sensible. "CALM YOUR CROWN" with a gorgeous natural-hair model and a clean product grid? Actually nice to look at. Credit where it's due.
- But I've been here before. Lush does beautiful and Lush does conceptual and Lush never gives me math. Sephora hit me with 4X points on all haircare this week, and I'm a spreadsheet-energy loyalty shopper — I was doing the Rouge math in my head before I even finished reading it. This email has no offer, no threshold, no points hook, nothing to calculate. The one date in the entire send — the Moomin collaboration, "Available from July 30th" — is buried at the bottom and it's actually the most action-relevant signal in here. That's the email. A launch date is hiding in the footer while "Shop Haircare" sits in the hero like it's doing the work.
- I'll mark this read. I won't click, and I won't forward it, because there's nothing urgent to share.

## What's working

- **"CALM YOUR CROWN" is a clean, specific hero** — scalp care in July when the sun's been wrecking my roots is actually timely, and the natural-hair model is a real beauty moment, not stock filler.
- **Super Milk gets a featured visual** — giving one product a bigger grid card helps it break out from the seven-product lineup; it pulls the eye and earns the spotlight.
- **Moomin collab has a date** — "Available from July 30th, 2026" is the only calendar peg in this entire email, and for someone who tracks beauty event launches the way I track Ulta 21 Days, that's a genuine hook — even if it's buried.
- **Zero render issues** — layout is clean, no broken images, no overlapping copy, no lorem-ipsum bleed. Lush's visual identity is reliably tight.
- **Subject has no spam energy** — question format is actually novel at email thirty; under 30 characters, no exclamation stacking, reads clean in a crowded inbox.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — no percentage off, no GWP, no points multiplier, no threshold. Sephora dangled 4X points on haircare this week; Lush is standing next to that with a category button and good vibes.
- **"Shop Haircare" is the laziest CTA they could have written** — it's a department, not a destination. It doesn't confirm the scalp thesis or tell me what I'm shopping *toward*.
- **The Moomin launch is the most interesting thing in the email and it's at the bottom** — July 30th is 11 days away; that's a calendar event, and it belongs in the hero or in a strong secondary slot, not as the footer dessert.
- **Product copy is illegible at mobile size** — the grid shows me "Rehab," "Avocado," "Strand" — but no ingredients, no sizes, no prices. If I don't already know these, I have no reason to click.
- **The natural-hair creative narrows the audience without going deep** — the CALM YOUR CROWN frame is strong for textured-hair customers, but the email doesn't actually explain *why* these products work for scalp health, which means it's also not landing the science for straight-hair customers with scalp concerns. It ends up in the middle.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Elevate the Moomin collab to a hero block or strong secondary** — swap "Shop Haircare" out of the hero, let the launch date carry the urgency. "Lush x Moomin drops July 30 — mark your calendar" is a reason to open on July 30th too.
- 2. **Add one offer mechanic** — even a tiered GWP ("spend $X, get a travel-size Avocado free") gives me something to reason about. After the "Zero packaging. Double points." send on July 11th — which I stopped for precisely because I could do the math — this no-offer email feels like regression.
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA** — "Find Your Scalp Routine" or "Start With Scalp" at minimum confirms the email knows what it's doing. e.l.f. gives me a $7 cream bronzer with a product name in the subject; Lush can do better than a category link.
- 4. **Give Super Milk one ingredient line** — it's getting the visual attention; it deserves one sentence. "Powered by oat milk and quinoa" in the featured block would justify why I'm clicking there versus any of the other six products.
- 5. **Commit to the natural-hair lane or cast wider** — either go full curly-girl editorial (ingredient deep dive, curl type callouts) or reframe as a universal summer scalp health story. The in-between serves neither customer well.
