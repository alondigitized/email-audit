# The power of the massage bar
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. That's where I am with Lush in June, and I want to say it plainly before I say anything else about massage bars, because the number is the full context for how I'm reading this. The hero pun — "Knot a Want but a Knead" — is exactly the kind of wordplay I expect from Lush and it doesn't bother me. What bothers me is that there is no offer anywhere in this email. Not a percent off, not a free gift with purchase, not a loyalty-point promo. It's a straight editorial email about massage bars laid out like a lookbook, and I'm a Sephora Rouge who does the points math before she adds anything to her cart.
- The Sanrio collab section shoved at the very bottom is the most interesting thing in here, and that's a problem — because this email is supposedly about massage bars. That section earned a 9/10 from me when Lush sent it as its own dedicated send (email eighteen), and now it's being recycled as footer content. That feels like a downgrade. The "2-in-1 Exfoliation" module with Scrubee and Fluffy is also a non-sequitur — I thought we were talking about massage bars?
- I am not clicking. I am not forwarding. I'm marking this read and moving on, the same way I did with the June 25th "Over 800+ responses" email that also gave me a lot of brand voice and zero reason to shop.

## What's working

- The hero image is striking — warm tones, diverse skin shown being massaged, the lifestyle photography is genuinely good and I can tell immediately what the email is about.
- "Knot a Want but a Knead" is Lush at their pun-best; it made me smile even on email twenty-one.
- The product grid is well-organized — "The Bump," "Woozy Magic Muscles," "Shimmy Shimmy," "Coconut Dream" are all individually named with text blurbs, so I can see differentiation between SKUs without hunting for it.
- "Cocoa Butter from Bean to Bar" is a genuinely interesting editorial section; the ingredient storytelling is what sets Lush apart from something like Ulta's private-label body line.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, text is legible, images are clean, layout is structured.

## What's weak

- There is no offer. None. Sally Beauty sent a 40% off email and Charlotte Tilbury dropped a sale — meanwhile Lush is asking me to be educated about cocoa butter with no incentive to buy right now.
- The email is trying to do four different things: massage bars, cocoa butter education, exfoliation products, and a Sanrio collab. That's not an email, that's a scroll-through quarterly catalog.
- The Sanrio collab section at the bottom feels like an afterthought — it was the best send of the month and it's now being quietly recycled in a footer module with a "Discover" button that goes... somewhere.
- "Shop Now" and "Learn More" are the only CTAs and neither one tells me what I'm shopping for or learning about. e.l.f. managed to put "So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" in their subject line and make me feel like I was buying something specific. This feels like I'm being directed to a homepage.
- No loyalty mention whatsoever — not a points event, not a member-only price, nothing that acknowledges I'm a subscriber who keeps opening these.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the email on one product and one reason to buy it today.** Pick Shimmy Shimmy or The Bump, make it the hero, and give me a reason this week specifically — limited stock, seasonal scent, new formula. One product > five products.
- 2. **Add an offer.** Even "spend $35, get free shipping" changes my behavior. This email is asking me to browse with no financial nudge, which I won't do when I'm waiting for my next Sephora Beauty Insider event to stock up.
- 3. **Cut the Sanrio section or give it its own send.** It earned a 9 when it had room to breathe. Shoved at the bottom of a massage bar email it earns nothing.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA buttons.** "Shop The Bump →" or "Get Shimmy Shimmy →" over "Shop Now." Name the product, give me a destination.
- 5. **Acknowledge the cadence.** Twenty-one emails and not a single one has said "you've been with us — here's something for you." ILIA does loyalty-signal copy better than this at half the volume.
