## Executive summary

- Okay, seventeen-plus days into this e.l.f. streak now and this one's a genuine BOGO — "buy 2 skin e.l.f.ssentials, get 1 on us" with code REFRESH, which is a real mechanic, not the usual single-item teaser. The hero product (Save Your e.l.f. Hypochlorous Mist) gets the "NEW! Online only" badge treatment and then the email just... keeps going, stacking four separate products with individual prices ($11, $12, $12) before finally landing on a "Top Picks for You" grid at the bottom. It's a lot of scroll for what is essentially one offer.
- The water-droplet hero and the girl laughing under a spray are cute and on-brand for "skincare routine reset," but there's zero shade-range or skin-type signal here — which stands out hard after yesterday's "Set It Bright" send nailed five shade ranges in one hero. This is e.l.f. going back to generic product-drop mode after actually doing personalization work 24 hours ago.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I actually needed a cleansing balm — the "buy 2 get 1" math is decent value and I do points math for a living. But I'm not forwarding this to a friend; it's not distinctive enough. Mark as read, maybe tap through to the mist later.

## What's working

- The "buy 2, get 1 on us" offer is a real, calculable discount — better than Ulta's "20% off one qualifying item" mechanic I've now seen three times this month.
- Individual pricing is visible on every product ($11, $12, $12, $8, $13, $6) — no hidden math, which is more transparent than Drunk Elephant's SheerID-gated 20% off.
- "find your skincare" quiz CTA near the bottom is a nice light-personalization touch, even if it's generic placement.
- Clean, consistent black-CTA-on-white layout — no render bugs, everything loads, buttons are all legible "SHOP NOW" tap targets.
- "e.l.f. is for every eye, lip, and face" plus cruelty-free/vegan badges reinforce brand trust cues I already associate with them.

## What's weak

- No shade or skin-tone/skin-type personalization anywhere — a real step back from yesterday's "Set It Bright" send, which is disappointing this soon after they proved they could do it.
- Four products stacked vertically before the actual offer code is repeated or reinforced — I have to scroll past Holy Hydration, the Hypochlorous Mist, AND the Glass Slick Serum Stick before I even hit the "Top Picks" grid. That's a lot of real estate for one BOGO.
- The category nav bar (NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE / HAIR) is just gray text blocks — no icons, no visual interest, feels like a placeholder compared to Sephora's "Wash / Hydrate" body-care layout I flagged as one of their prettiest sends.
- "REFRESH" code isn't restated near the bottom CTAs — if I scroll straight to Top Picks without reading the top, I've lost the code entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. Repeat the REFRESH code as a sticky reminder near the Top Picks grid — I shouldn't have to scroll back up to redeem the actual offer.
- 2. Cut one of the four featured products — Holy Hydration OR the Serum Stick — and let the Hypochlorous Mist (the actual "NEW" hero) carry more visual weight instead of getting lost in a lineup.
- 3. Add a skin-type or skin-tone cue somewhere, even a one-line "for sensitive/blemish-prone skin" callout under the mist — they clearly know how to do this after "Set It Bright."
- 4. Give the bottom category nav actual icons or thumbnail imagery — right now it reads like unstyled placeholder text.
