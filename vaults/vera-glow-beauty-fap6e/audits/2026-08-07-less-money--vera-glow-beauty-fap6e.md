**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Bundle Up, People!" — this is the most straightforward, actually-useful email DE has sent me all week, and I say that as someone who just docked them a 4/10 for the SheerID gatekeeping stunt and a 5/10 for the recycled Subscribe hero. This time there's a real 25% off, two clearly built AM/PM routines with dollar values stamped right on them ($179 value, $156 value), and a legit free-gift-with-purchase kicker at $85+. No teaser fog like "Something big..." (which I gave a 9 for exactly because it committed to mystery), no repeat hero shot like the Subscribe pitch — this is a clean, math-forward send that respects that I'm the type doing points-and-value math in my head before I click.
- What I like: the "in the a.m. / in the p.m." split does real work for me. I'm not guessing what goes with what — Gold Comfort (C-Luma + Protini + B-Goldi) is my morning, Saving Face (T.L.C. Framboos + Lala Retro) is my night, and the value badges do the "why should I care" math for me instead of making me open a calculator app like I do with every Ulta "20% off one qualifying item" email. It's basically the DE version of what Sephora's "Discover the art of body care" send did with its Wash/Hydrate structure — routine-as-narrative — except DE ties it to a hard discount instead of just vibes.
- Would I click? Yeah, actually. Would I forward it? Maybe to my roommate who's been asking about a night routine — the p.m. bundle with Lala Retro is a good nudge for her specifically. Marking as read after skimming, not deleting.

## What's working

- The $179/$156 value badges do the points-math for me instantly — no mental gymnastics required, unlike Ulta's coupon-code-with-exclusions approach.
- AM/PM structure gives the bundles a reason to exist beyond "buy more stuff" — it's routine logic, not just a discount slapped on random SKUs.
- 25% off + free shipping + free deluxe samples at $85+ is a real, stackable, honest offer with a visible expiration window in the fine print (8/3–8/8).
- Two "SHOP BUNDLES" CTAs (top and bottom) mean I don't have to scroll back up on mobile.
- Clean product photography with the cream-swipe hero — feels premium and consistent with DE's brand voice, not a stock-photo bolt-on.

## What's weak

- No shade/skin-tone or skin-type personalization at all — this is a blanket "everyone gets the same bundle" send, which stings a little coming right after ILIA's "Eye Color Theory" matrix that actually matched product to me.
- Zero Rouge/Diamond tier callout — DE doesn't have a loyalty program the way Sephora/Ulta do, but there's also no "you've bought Protini before" personalization, so it reads like it's blasted to DE's whole list, not to me specifically.
- The fine print buries "excludes gift cards" and "one per order/per person" in tiny gray text I'd have to zoom to read on my phone.
- This is DE's third distinct campaign concept in under two weeks (Lippe Lock → Subscribe pitch → SheerID discount → now Bundle Up) — the cadence is starting to feel scattered rather than building toward something.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a "your skin type" filter or at least a one-line "best for combo/dry/oily" tag under each bundle — ILIA proved this kind of matrix pulls me in, and DE has the ingredient data to do it easily.
- 2. Move the free-gift threshold ($85+) into the hero copy instead of the top banner strip — I almost skimmed past it since it's in small caps on a busy gray bar.
- 3. Put the expiration date ("ends 8/8") somewhere visible in the body, not just buried in the legal footer — urgency works better when I don't have to hunt for it.
- 4. Test a subject line that leads with the specific discount number instead of vague "Less money!" — "less money" undersells a genuine 25% off two-bundle deal.
