# The base layer: 20% off skincare
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- ILIA, I have been counting. This is email six since May 16 — welcome, awards editorial, brand overview, finish quiz with the F+F sale, Marissa's plain-text note, yesterday's cold VIP loyalty pitch, and now this. By the time this landed I *should* be done. But the "base layer" framing — positioning skincare as the foundation you build makeup on — is a genuine POV and not just "skincare sale," so I leaned in a little further than I expected to.
- What's dragging it down: "SALE EXTENDED" in that top banner. I remember getting the original Friends + Family email with a deadline. The moment a brand extends a sale, they're essentially admitting the first deadline was artificial, and I notice that. It's the same erosion move Charlotte Tilbury did with their 15% → 20% escalation across May 16–20, and it's doing the same trust work here. The plain-text note from Marissa yesterday felt human. This feels like the machine clocked back in the next morning.
- Would I click? Probably yes — the Super Serum Skin Tint is legitimately on my radar and 20% off on a $48 product is $9.60, which I will absolutely take. Would I forward? No. Would I mark as read without opening? Not quite — the subject does enough to pull me in. This is a "quick scan, one purposeful click" email, not a "send to the group chat" email.

## What's working

- The "base layer" concept is a real skincare philosophy, not marketing fluff — it positions the sale around *routine order* rather than just discounting inventory, which feels more editorial than Glossier's Memorial Day send that just screamed "SALE STARTS NOW" with stars.
- Individual product tiles are curated and specific: Base Face Milk, Bright Start Retinol Eye Cream, Super Serum Skin Tint, NEW Sun Serum. Feels like a considered lineup, not a catalog dump.
- "Cart Deco" free gift section with no minimum required is a genuinely strong incentive layer buried at the bottom — a GWP with no purchase floor is rare and real.
- The visual aesthetic is clean, minimal, and cohesive — photography and color palette match the ILIA brand voice I've come to expect, and the demographic signals (clean beauty, soft palette, not maximalist) feel right for me.
- 20% off stated plainly in both the banner and the hero copy — no visible asterisks or "select items only" fine print in the body.

## What's weak

- "SALE EXTENDED" in the top banner is a trust leak. Every experienced email subscriber knows this means the first deadline was manufactured urgency, and I'm exactly the subscriber who noticed the original F+F email.
- Six emails in six days is brutal cadence, especially after Marissa's personal note yesterday — dropping back into a product-grid email the very next morning undoes all that warmth instantly.
- Every individual product CTA reads "SHOP NOW >" — generic, and Drunk Elephant's Friends + Family email (25% off, May 19) used specific per-product linking that felt more intentional than this.
- No price points visible on the product tiles. I want to see "$48 → $38.40" math right on the card; right now I have to click through to feel the discount, which adds friction Sephora would never let slip through.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the discount math on every product tile** — "$48 → $38.40 with 20% off" on each card would do more conversion lifting than any headline rewrite. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Replace "SALE EXTENDED" with "More time to stock up"** — same information, none of the trust erosion. Never remind the subscriber that the deadline was fake.
- 3. **Elevate Cart Deco** — a free gift with no minimum is legitimately exciting; right now it's buried below five scrollable product tiles. Move it up as a secondary banner or embed it between product rows.
- 4. **Retire the generic "SHOP NOW >" CTAs on product tiles** — "Shop Base Face Milk" takes three extra characters and signals intentionality.
- 5. **48-hour spacing after a personal-tone email** — Marissa's note was a trust deposit; spending it overnight on a product grid is a waste.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Base layer first — skincare, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Base Face Milk, Super Serum Tint + more — plus a free gift, no minimum`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your daily routine and save 20% on every step`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` block — confirmed issue**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (all variants), Yahoo Mail, and some Outlook builds. Both the Klaviyo custom font and Google Fonts Montserrat will silently fail to load in these clients. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` handles graceful degradation, but this is a delivery reliability gap for the primary typeface.

**All anchor pseudo-states locked to identical color — confirmed**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
`a:hover` returns no visual change. No underline on any state. Links are indistinguishable from body text for users who rely on hover feedback or text-decoration as affordance. WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires non-color differentiation.

**Duplicate `img {}` rule block — confirmed**
The `img` rule appears twice in the `<style>` block (once in the base reset, once in the Klaviyo section). The second declaration is redundant but not harmful; it adds unnecessary payload bytes.

**Empty `<title>` element — confirmed**

Intentional (prevents tab label leakage in webmail), but technically a void title. Some accessibility tools flag this.
