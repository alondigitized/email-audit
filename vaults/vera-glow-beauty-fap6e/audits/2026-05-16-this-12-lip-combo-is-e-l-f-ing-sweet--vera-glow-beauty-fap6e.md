# This $12 lip combo is e.l.f.ing sweet 👄😋 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Fifth e.l.f. email in three days — I want to be clear about that up front, because the cadence is officially becoming a problem for me. That said, the subject line is doing something I actually respect: a specific price anchor ($12), a product category I care about (lips), and the brand's signature wordplay. The hero delivers on the promise — "punch up your pout." with a massive pink lip liner is exactly what the subject sold me on, and the "line & shine." duo grid underneath is genuinely good product storytelling. Liner + gloss combos at a value price point? That's the e.l.f. move I'm here for.
- But then I kept scrolling and the goodwill evaporated a little. There's a mascara section ("more lash. less cash.") that is, I'm almost certain, recycled creative from the May 15 mascara send. As a subscriber who opened that email, I notice. It breaks the lip-focused narrative this email had going and makes the whole thing feel like two emails duct-taped together. The SPF detour and the Target rewards block add more noise on top of that.
- I'd click on the lip liner grid — genuinely. But I wouldn't forward this to my friend because the second half of the email undercuts the first half too much. Mark as read after tapping the lip section, ignore the rest.

## What's working

- **"punch up your pout." hero is tight** — the lip liner visual is big, pink, and instantly validates the subject line promise. No bait-and-switch.
- **"line & shine." duo concept is smart product storytelling** — showing the liner-gloss pairs together communicates the $12 bundle logic visually without needing copy to explain it.
- **Shade range on display** — the product grid spans a full spectrum of nude-to-bold, which signals to me that there's something for my skintone. That's a quiet inclusivity win.
- **"Not wearing SPF? uncomfortable." copy** — genuinely clever phrasing. I paused on it.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — everything loads, images are crisp, CTAs are visible. Basic but not guaranteed.

## What's weak

- **Mascara section is a direct recycled lift from the May 15 send** — "more lash. less cash." appeared in that email verbatim. I opened that one. This feels lazy and makes the send feel like a content dump, not a curated send.
- **The $12 price point never shows up prominently in the email body** — the subject hooked me with "$12" but the hero just says "punch up your pout." I had to hunt for the price validation. That anchor needs to be above the fold.
- **"Top Picks for You" is just category navigation** — NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE is not personalization, it's a nav bar. Glossier's approach of surfacing a specific product with my skin profile in mind would smoke this.
- **Too many themes fighting for attention** — lips, lashes, SPF, Target rewards, and personalized picks in one email is the Ulta template problem. Sephora's product-launch emails usually pick one story and own it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the mascara section entirely** — it was its own email yesterday and it dilutes the lip story. Commit to the bit.
- 2. **Put the "$12" callout in the hero** — big, bold, right below the "punch up your pout." headline. The price is the reason I opened. Mirror it immediately so I feel like I landed in the right place.
- 3. **Collapse the SPF and Target sections to a single slim footer row** — they're not bad ideas but they're not this email's job.
- 4. **Make "Top Picks" actually personal** — show the specific shades I've browsed or purchased, not a category grid.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Your $12 lip duo is waiting — liner + gloss, e.l.f.ing perfect`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Line it. Gloss it. $12 and done.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `The lip liner + gloss combo that goes with everything — grab it now`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Shade range, $12 price point, and zero excuses to skip the gloss`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — mobile h1 at 9px:**
```css
@media (max-width:660px) {
  .row-6 .column-1 .block-1.heading_block h1 { font-size:9px!important }
}
```
9px is below the 11px minimum legible threshold and will fail WCAG 1.4.4. This selector is almost certainly a residual override from template copying — verify intended value.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo anchor:**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
Removes the logo CTA from the keyboard tab order. Keyboard-only users cannot activate it. Remove the attribute or set `tabindex="0"`.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the tab/window title on open. Empty title provides no context; use a descriptive string matching the subject line.

**Google Fonts via `<link>`:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost" ...>
```
Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` so Outlook skips it, but Gmail (webmail), Apple Mail, and most mobile clients will block external stylesheet requests. Fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is present and will engage — not a breakage, but custom fonts will not render in most inboxes.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are not visible in the raw tracking URLs (e.g., the logo CTA: `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/fo7NJJ661_...`). They may be appended by Braze at redirect time, but this cannot be confirmed without following the redirect chain.
