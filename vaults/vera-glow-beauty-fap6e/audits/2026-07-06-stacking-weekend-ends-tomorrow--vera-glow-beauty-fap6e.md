# Stacking Weekend ends tomorrow 😳
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Six. We are on email number six (at minimum) of Skechers — wait, wrong tab. Six Sephora Stacking Weekend emails since July 1st, and I'm supposed to feel the 😳 emoji urgency right now? The subject line is doing real work — "ends tomorrow" is a legitimate last-day hook and I'll give the copywriter credit for not just recycling the July 5th subject verbatim. But the moment I opened this I felt déjà vu in my bones. The hero is that same pink circular badge energy, the product grid below it is essentially the same cast of Sephora Collection products I've seen four times this week, and the only thing that's changed is the clock.
- What keeps me from fully checking out is that this one *does* have a job to do. Last-day pushes are real. My cart has been sitting there since July 1st with an Saie concealer and a handful of Sephora Collection items and I genuinely need the "it's now or never" kick. The 😳 emoji is doing actual emotional work — it's not aggressive-capslock urgency, it's that "oh wait, I forgot" friend-text energy that fits Sephora's voice. So I'd probably open. I'd scroll fast. I might click. But I wouldn't forward — by this point my friends are as saturated as I am.
- Verdict: read it, click the cart link, buy my Sephora Collection picks, move on. Do not screenshot for the group chat.

## What's working

- **"Ends tomorrow" + 😳 subject combo** actually landed — the emoji reads like a genuine alarm rather than a marketing shout, which is exactly the register you want for a closing-day send
- **The hero pink badge graphic** is visually distinct enough that it reads as "new creative" even if the underlying offer hasn't changed — better than e.l.f., which recycled the Soft Glam grid image three times in a single week
- **Multiple product rows visible immediately** — the grid format means I don't have to scroll to start product-scanning, which matches how I actually shop emails
- **Sephora Collection products prominently featured** — the skincare tubes, serums, and color items are exactly what I stocked up on at prior Stacking Weekends, so the curation feels right for my basket
- **Sephora brand = instant trust signal** — unlike Glossier's "Set your sights here" send that gave me almost no reason to click through, Sephora still has the program credibility and tier recognition that makes me take the offer seriously

## What's weak

- **Creative fatigue is now the dominant emotion** — the hero layout, the product grid, the pink palette — this is the same visual language I've seen since July 1st. Drunk Elephant changed their hero creative across their campaign days; Sephora did not
- **No offer callout visible in the hero at a glance** — I have to already know this is a 30% off event to understand what I'm being urged to act on. A new opener who's missed prior sends would get zero context from the top section
- **No tier callout** — I'm a Rouge member. The July 1st send had the Sephora Shopper framing; by day six I'd want to see "Rouge: your final window" or stacked savings math. ILIA's "Ends Tonight" closer named the exact deal terms in the first line — Sephora is not doing that here
- **The email is long** — for a "do it now or lose it" closer, I want one scroll to CTA, not a full catalog browse. Sally Beauty's "Last Call for Holiday Savings" (scored 9) got this right: single-focus hero, big CTA, short scroll. This email asks me to browse when I should be buying

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the exact offer and deadline in the hero copy** — "30% off Sephora Collection. Ends tonight." on top of the badge, not a visual-only design element. New visitors to this email have no context.
- 2. **Compress the email for closing day** — a last-day send should be tight: hero + offer + single CTA + maybe one product row. The full catalog scroll belongs in the launch email, not the finale.
- 3. **Add Rouge/tier personalization** — "Rouge, your Stacking Weekend window closes at midnight" would make me feel seen in a way six generic sends have not.
- 4. **Swap the pink badge for a countdown or deadline graphic** — a "ENDS TOMORROW" stamp or a clock visual would communicate urgency faster than a circular brand badge that reads as decorative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 30% off Sephora Collection ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart expires tomorrow — grab the 30% off now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rouge members: this is your final Stacking Weekend window. Don't leave points on the table.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `30% off everything Sephora Collection + 2X points — gone tomorrow.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Impacts users with low vision who rely on browser zoom. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Broad `text-size-adjust: none` override:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to every element, not just tables. While this is common ESP boilerplate, the blanket application on `body` and `span` suppresses OS-level text scaling on iOS/Android. Scope to `table, td` only.

**Issue — `robots` meta typo:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Value has a space: `"no index"` is not a valid robots directive — parsers treat it as unknown and may ignore it. Should be `"noindex"` (no space). Affects the "view in browser" hosted version.

**No issues found:** VML namespaces for Outlook, `.ExternalClass` line-height fix, Gmail data-detector suppression, and Apple data-detector overrides are all correctly applied.
