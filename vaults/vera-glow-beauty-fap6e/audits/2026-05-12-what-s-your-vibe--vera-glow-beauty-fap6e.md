# What's your vibe? 💫
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Lush hits me with a mood-board email and my first reaction is — cute aesthetic, zero reason to open my wallet. The "Vibe Shift" hero is genuinely pretty and I recognize the Rose Jam / Karma scents immediately, but this entire email is pure brand storytelling. No sale. No offer. No "hey Rouge member, here's your early access." It's six fragrance collections stacked on top of each other with a different gradient per section, which sounds fun until I'm scrolling past the fourth one going "okay I get it, you have vibes."
- The demographic vibe is fine — young, colorful, fashion-forward. I'm clearly in the target age window. But as someone who literally plans her Sephora hauls around the Beauty Insider event calendar, a no-offer Lush email competes with seventeen other inbox items that *do* have a discount attached. Lush is not on a major loyalty platform, so there's no points math to do here, which is a strike against it.
- I would mark this as read, not click. I would not forward it — there's nothing to forward, no deal to share.

## What's working

- **Recognizable product names** — Rose Jam, Karma, Super Milk all clock immediately. Lush has real brand equity on those and just seeing the names is a trust cue.
- **Visual coherence** — each section has its own color palette that reads as intentional, not chaotic. It doesn't feel broken.
- **"Shop To Smell" CTA copy** — actually kind of charming for a fragrance email. Better than another "Shop Now."
- **Models are age-appropriate and diverse** — feels like it's talking to me, not at me.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer anywhere** — this is a full-scroll editorial with no sale, no GWP, no free shipping threshold. There's nothing to act on today.
- **Six separate collection sections is too many** — by section four ("Run the World") I've lost the plot. The hierarchy collapses into infinite scroll.
- **No loyalty hook** — Lush doesn't live inside Sephora or Ulta so there's no points angle, but even a "earn double points on lush.com this week" or "first time buyer code" would give me a reason to click now vs. later (never).
- **Product names but zero context** — I know these scents but "Sticky Dates" and "We're Again" are going to mean nothing to anyone who hasn't already smelled them. No fragrance descriptor, no "if you love X try Y."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one offer.** Even free shipping over $X or a GWP with first purchase. An editorial email with no offer is a brand-awareness play that belongs on Instagram, not my inbox.
- 2. **Consolidate to two sections max.** Pick the hero launch and one returning favorite. The six-vibe format buries the lead every time.
- 3. **Add a scent-profile snippet** under each product name — one line, like "warm amber, fig, skin-like." That's the product detail that would actually make me add-to-cart.
- 4. **Personalize the subject** — "What's your vibe?" could be literally any brand, any category. At minimum tie it to a product.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next Lush scent is in here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `6 moods, 6 scent families — find yours + free shipping over $50`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Vibe Shift collection is here. Your match might surprise you.`
