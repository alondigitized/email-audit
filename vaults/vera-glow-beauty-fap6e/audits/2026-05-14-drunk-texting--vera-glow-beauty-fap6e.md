# Drunk texting?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- OK, Drunk Elephant — I see you. "Drunk texting?" is genuinely one of the cleverest subject-line puns I've seen all season. If you're a DE devotee, the wordplay between the brand name and the literal concept of drunkenly texting at 2am is *instantly* recognizable, and I opened it with zero hesitation. The hero copy "STAY CLOSE" extends that theme beautifully — this whole email is a cohesive, well-branded SMS acquisition play, and I respect the commitment to the bit.
- That said, I hit my wall pretty fast: I buy all my Drunk Elephant through Sephora because I am absolutely not abandoning Rouge points on a brand that earns me real Insider rewards. This email is speaking to me as though I'm a DE.com buyer, and I'm not. The "15% off your next order" is a genuine offer, but my brain immediately does the math — 15% off DE direct versus my Rouge discount + Savings Event stacking + points accumulation — and it's not as obvious as DE thinks it is. The email doesn't acknowledge any of that. It doesn't know me, it just knows I opened it.
- The Littles section at the bottom is a nice touch — that kit is one of DE's most beloved giftable products and it's genuinely relevant — but it's stapled onto the SMS pitch without a logical bridge. I'd screenshot the offer for later consideration, scroll to The Littles, think "I've been meaning to try this," and then... mark as read without clicking. Smart email, but it's talking to a version of me that doesn't exist.

## What's working

- **The subject line pun is elite.** "Drunk texting?" from Drunk Elephant is brand-voice perfection — playful, immediately recognizable to fans, under 20 characters. One of the best wordplay subjects I've seen this month.
- **"STAY CLOSE" hero copy is clean and thematically tight.** The visual of a hand holding a phone reinforces the SMS concept without being heavy-handed. The creative direction holds together end-to-end.
- **The 15% offer is clearly stated and not buried.** "15% off your next order" is right there in the body copy, asterisked but visible — not hidden in fine print, not vagued out into "save big."
- **The Littles kit is exactly the right product to feature.** It's a gateway product for new buyers and a replenishment item for loyalists — showing it alongside an "early access to sales" value prop is smart product staging.
- **No visible render bugs.** The layout reads clean — no overlapping text, no broken image gaps, no placeholder copy.

## What's weak

- **This email doesn't know I shop DE through Sephora.** There's zero acknowledgment that DE is a Sephora staple, that signing up for DE texts might be redundant for someone already plugged into Sephora Insider alerts, or that buying direct means losing points. It talks to me like a DE.com native.
- **"TEXT ON THE LIST" is a weak CTA label.** The phrasing is cheeky but it's not instructional — what am I actually doing? What happens next? "Text GLOW to 80005" or even "Join the Text List" gives me a mental model of the action I'm taking.
- **The Littles section feels tacked on.** There's no narrative thread connecting "sign up for our texts" to "here's The Littles kit" — are you showing me what I'll get early access to? Is this a separate offer? I genuinely can't tell, and the two halves of the email feel like a Frankenstein merge.
- **Zero urgency on the 15% offer.** No deadline, no "limited time," no "first 1,000 subscribers." That 15% just floats there indefinitely, which completely removes the impulse to act now rather than maybe-later.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the SMS CTA tactile and instructional.** "TEXT ON THE LIST" is an abstraction. "Text GLOW to 80005" is an action I understand immediately — and it makes the opt-in feel smaller and easier.
- 2. **Give the 15% a credible deadline.** Even "available through Sunday" turns a passive read into a timed decision. This is the single highest-leverage fix.
- 3. **Bridge the Littles section to the SMS value prop.** One sentence like "Get The Littles drop delivered straight to your phone before it sells out" makes the product section *earn* its place rather than feel like padding.
- 4. **Acknowledge multi-channel shoppers.** A single line — "Shop at Sephora? Sign up and still be first to know about DE exclusives and launches" — would hold onto people like me who aren't going to switch purchase channels but would absolutely take an early-access alert.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout. Two confirmed rendering defects are present: CSS rules wrapped in HTML comment syntax will be silently dropped by all email clients, and custom fonts are loaded redundantly from two separate third-party CDNs with no controlled fallback. The HTML source is truncated so link, compliance, and UTM sections are partially assessable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Partially assessable** — HTML is truncated before body links appear.

- No UTM parameters or click-tracking wrappers are visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm presence or absence across CTA links.
- Font asset URLs point to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN not controlled by Drunk Elephant. If that CDN is blocked by a corporate firewall or link-scanner, the `<link>` prefetch requests will fire before the email is opened — registering a false open event in some ESP tracking systems.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — CSS rules silently dropped (mobile layout broken):**

Inside the `@media only screen and (max-width: 600px)` block, two rules use HTML comment syntax instead of CSS comments:

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS. Every email client will silently discard these two rules. Elements with `class="contenttable"` and `class="mobile"` will not receive their mobile-width overrides, likely causing layout overflow on small screens.

**Font loading — redundant and split-source:**

`BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin` are declared twice each: once via `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` and again via `@font-face` in the `<style>` block, both pointing to `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. This doubles HTTP requests per font. `BrownRegular` is loaded only from `drunkelephant.com` (self-hosted), creating an inconsistent dependency model — three weights rely on an external CDN, one does not.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**

`db.onlinewebfonts.com` is a public font distribution service. Its uptime and caching headers are not under the sender's control. All four `@font-face` stacks lack a reliable system-font fallback defined in the actual element-level `font-family` declarations (not assessable from truncated source, but a known risk pattern for this architecture).

**Global `line-height` reset:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

Applied after `ExternalClass` line-height rules. The `*` selector overrides inherited `line-height` on all elements including `<td>` and `<p>`, which can compress multi-line text in Outlook. This is a known Outlook rendering hazard.

**`lang` attribute:** The `<html>` element has no `lang` attribute. Screen readers will default to the user's OS language. Not a blocker but flags in WCAG 3.1.1.

**Partially assessable:** Image `alt` attributes, button/CTA contrast ratios, and link text cannot be evaluated from the truncated source.
