# Save on smoothies (15% off!)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Drunk Elephant in my inbox with a 15% off code — I'm already conditionally interested because this brand is in my regular rotation. The yellow banner at the top is doing its job: the code is right there, legible, no hunting. The smoothie-as-skincare-routine concept is very DE — playful, a little science-y, instantly recognizable if you've ever used a Lala Retro or a Protini. I smiled at it.
- Then I hit the fine print. "First-time subscribers only." I've been buying from drunkelephant.com for three years. This 15% off code is — for me personally — completely worthless. That's the most infuriating thing a brand can do: put a discount in the subject line, put the code in a banner, make me salivate, then bury the exclusion in sub-10pt text at the bottom. It's not a discount. It's a welcome series email that accidentally landed in my existing-customer inbox.
- The birthday data-capture in the middle of a promo email is also jarring timing. I'd mark this as read, maybe screenshot the code to try it anyway (it won't work), and not forward it. If a girlfriend asked "should I try Drunk Elephant?" I'd tell her to sign up for the list — not because of this email, but because she'd actually get the 15% off.

## What's working

- **The offer banner is the first thing you see.** Yellow background, bold text, code right there — no scroll required to find the discount.
- **"KEYS, WALLET…WHAT ARE YOU FORGETTING?"** is a millennial-coded hook that actually works as a preheader. Feels like something a friend texted you, not a legal department.
- **Drunk Elephant's brand voice is intact.** The "biocompatible," "mix a little of this and some of that" copy sounds exactly like their DTC site and socials. Consistency builds trust.
- **Clean render, no layout breakage.** The color blocking — cream, yellow, lilac — is easy on the eyes and matches DE's known aesthetic. Nothing's clipped or broken.

## What's weak

- **"First-time subscribers only" is the original sin here.** The headline screams 15% off; the fine print whispers "jk, not for you." Sending a new-subscriber offer to your existing list is a trust-eroding mistake.
- **Zero product specificity.** No SKU, no collection name, no hero item. Just "mix things." As someone who does the points math on specific SKUs before buying, this gives me nothing to act on.
- **The birthday module in the middle of a promotional email kills the flow.** Pick a lane — this is either a discount email or a profile-enrichment email. Both at once feels opportunistic.
- **No loyalty or tier acknowledgment.** DE knows I'm a repeat customer (I'm assuming they're segmenting, they're not). No mention of existing rewards, no "as a valued customer" framing. Treated like a stranger.
- **"MIX IT UP" CTA is vague.** Where does it go? A collection page? A quiz? A landing page? A specific CTA like "Shop the Smoothies" with a destination I can predict would convert better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this properly — do not send new-subscriber offers to returning customers.** The moment I see "first-time subscribers only" buried in fine print while the banner screams "YOUR 15% OFF COUPON," I'm gone. Split this into two flows: welcome series for new subs, loyalty offer (points multiplier, birthday bonus, early access) for existing customers.
- 2. **Replace the hero with a specific product or bundle.** Lead with a Smoothie Set or a "your next mix" based on purchase history. Specificity = click. "Mix things" = scroll past.
- 3. **Move or remove the birthday module.** If you want my birthday, send a dedicated birthday-capture email. Don't interrupt a promo email mid-flow with a data-grab.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Shop the Smoothie Routine" or "Find Your Mix" beats "MIX IT UP" — more directional, sets expectations for landing page destination.
- 5. **Surface loyalty context for existing customers.** Even a line like "as a DE subscriber, here's what's in it for you" changes the tone from "stranger discount" to "member moment."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Biocompatible, stackable, and now 15% off. Don't wait.`

```css

```
`font-display` is not supported in any email client. All six declarations carry dead CSS. Remove to reduce payload.

**Potential issue — `supported-color-schemes: light dark only`:**

The `only` keyword tells Apple Mail to suppress its automatic color inversion and trust the email's own dark mode CSS entirely. If explicit `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides are absent in the body styles (not visible in the truncated source), Apple Mail dark-mode users will see un-adjusted light styles with no fallback auto-correction.
