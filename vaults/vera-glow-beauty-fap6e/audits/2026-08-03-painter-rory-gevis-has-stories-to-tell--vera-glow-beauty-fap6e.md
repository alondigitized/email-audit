## What's working

- Real artist, real photos of her actual studio and hands — this doesn't look like every other beauty-brand influencer flatlay, and it's a nice change of pace after ILIA's last few sends.
- "4X VIP Points — NEW Thru Line Eyeliner" badge up top is the one piece of loyalty math I need, and it's there, even if it's small.
- "SHOP THRU LINE" at the bottom is clear and unambiguous when I finally get to it.
- The pull-quote typography (serif italics on navy) is genuinely pretty and on-brand — more elevated than the Squeeze Me watermelon duo e.l.f. is running right now.

## What's weak

- I have to scroll through four "artist statement" quote blocks before there's any product detail — shade range, finish, price, none of it shows up until the very end.
- "STEP INTO THE STUDIO" as the first CTA doesn't tell me if I'm buying anything or reading a blog post — compare that to Sephora's Beauty Insider Celebration send, which announced itself as a shop-the-sale event within one screen.
- No urgency, no date, no "new arrival" framing beyond the tiny top badge — I don't know if this eyeliner has been out a week or launched today.
- This is basically the fourth different creative treatment for the same Thru Line launch in under two weeks (launch page 7/28, "last day" urgency 7/26, ingredient deep-dive 7/30) — I'm starting to feel like ILIA doesn't know if this is a product launch or a content series.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a real product shot with shade/finish info above the fold, right under the VIP points badge — I shouldn't have to scroll past three quotes to see what I'm being sold.
- 2. Trim the artist story to one strong quote and one photo, then get to product faster — the story is good but it's fighting the sell, not supporting it.
- 3. Add a launch-week or limited-time frame ("NEW — first two weeks") so there's a reason to act now instead of just admire.
- 4. Rename the first CTA so it signals shopping, not editorial.
