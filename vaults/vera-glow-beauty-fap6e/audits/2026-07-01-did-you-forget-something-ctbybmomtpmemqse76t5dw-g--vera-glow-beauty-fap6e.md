# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- So here we are — Skechers number five in the rotation of abandon triggers, and this one finally switched tactics. Instead of "limited availability" or the very familiar "This is perfect for you," we get the classic cart-abandon guilt trip: "Did you forget something?" Honestly, I've seen this subject line so many times across so many brands that it practically disappears into the inbox noise, but at least the email body follows through on the premise. There are actual named products at the top, a clear "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button, and a 20% off banner that lands before I have to scroll. Points for that.
- The problem is the products. I'm staring at three featured shoes, and one of them is "Mommy & Me: Glide-Step Pro – Rosy Glow." I don't have kids. If those were genuinely my cart items, that's a strange browsing session I don't remember. If they're algorithmic recommendations, that's a segmentation swing and a miss. Compare that to Sephora's Stacking Weekend email — which knew exactly who I was and put my tier benefits in the hero — and the gap in personalization is stark. Skechers is treating me like a household, not a person.
- The 20% off is real and visible, which is better than June 30's "limited availability" urgency-without-offer approach. But I spotted "SKECHERS PLUS MEMBER EXCLUSIVE" in the footer fine print, and the banner up top says nothing about that. If the 20% is gated to Plus members, show me that upfront — don't hide the asterisk two scrolls down. Mark-as-read and maybe come back if I was genuinely shopping. Not forwarding this one to anyone.

## What's working

- **"COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is unambiguous** — clear verb, clear action, no vague "Discover" energy anywhere near that button
- **20% off is above the fold** — the gold banner lands in the first scroll zone on mobile, not buried below the nav links
- **Product names are legible and specific** — "Slip-ins: Glide-Step Pro" and "Slip-ins: BOBS Sport Solid – Airy Strobe" give me enough to recognize what I was looking at
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, readable type sizes, images load, nothing overlaps or breaks
- **Hero tone has more energy than the June 30 send** — "READY TO CHECK OUT?" with the warm gold treatment is at least attempting a personality, which beats the generic browse-trigger from two days ago

## What's weak

- **"Mommy & Me: Glide-Step Pro – Rosy Glow" as a featured product is a demographic miss** — if that wasn't in my cart, it's an algorithmic rec that skews toward a life stage I'm not in
- **The 20% off is apparently member-exclusive but the banner doesn't say so** — "SKECHERS PLUS MEMBER EXCLUSIVE" is in footer fine print while the banner runs it large; that's buried exclusionary language and it reads as bait-and-switch
- **No expiration on either the cart or the offer** — Sally Beauty ran "BOGO 50% Off" with a tight deadline baked into the subject line and that email had real urgency; this one just floats with no deadline pressure
- **Two competing CTAs with different destinations** — "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" vs. "SHOP NOW" on the 20% banner; I don't know which one finishes my cart and which one drops me on a generic PDP
- **Subject line could have come from any brand** — no Skechers identity, no product name, no offer; just the most generic cart-abandon phrasing possible

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 20% member offer next to the cart CTA** — put "Your 20% Plus member discount is applied — complete your order" in the hero instead of separating the offer into its own lower banner; the discount should be the reason to click right now
- 2. **Add a cart expiry or offer deadline** — even a "your cart is saved for 24 hours" or "20% off expires midnight tonight" would create real urgency; right now there's zero pressure to act today vs. next week
- 3. **Audit the product recs** — if Mommy & Me wasn't in my cart, it shouldn't be in the hero row; if the brand doesn't have my actual cart items, show me bestsellers from the category I browsed, not lifestyle segments I don't fit
- 4. **Disclose the member gate in the banner** — "20% OFF — SKECHERS PLUS EXCLUSIVE" right on the badge; hiding it in fine print kills trust and feels like the kind of thing that makes me screenshot and tweet
- 5. **Collapse the CTA architecture** — one button, one destination, one action per email
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your cart + 20% off, but not forever`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Glide-Step Pro you left behind`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Plus member discount is ready — finish your order before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your size might sell out before you decide`
