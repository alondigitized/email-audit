# NEW drops make lips & cheeks pop 💋💥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Twenty. Or close enough. I know because June 25 was email sixteen — "So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" — and now it's June 30 and I'm staring at something called "So Blurred Lip & Cheek Whip." The names are close enough that I actually stopped and squinted. Is this the same product with a copy error? A different shade? A matte sibling to the Blurreal? I shouldn't have to do investigative work at email twenty to figure out whether I've already seen this SKU. That confusion is the single biggest thing dragging this otherwise solid send down.
- That said — the two-product drop structure earns its keep. Pout Goal Lip Plumping Pre-Shimmer plus So Blurred side by side, both $8, makes the "NEW drops" subject feel substantiated in a way that a single-sku push wouldn't. The model with the pink glossy lip in the middle section reads exactly like my FYP. "Top Picks For You" at the bottom is the right signal even if I can't verify it's actually trained on my purchase history. The price points are so low the mental math takes about two seconds.
- If this were email five I'd click both add-to-carts without overthinking it. At email twenty, I'm clicking out of habit, not excitement. I'm not forwarding it — the Blurreal/Blurred naming thing alone would get a "wait, didn't they already do this?" response from any friend I'd send it to.

## What's working

- Two new $8 SKUs featured back-to-back gives the "NEW drops" subject real substance — more weight than the single-product Sephora tease format.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs sit directly beneath each product description with no hunting required — clean and tap-friendly.
- Pink-lips model in the mid-email section is a demographic bulls-eye: young, female, expressive color look that doesn't skew older or teen.
- Pricing is upfront and visible — $8 on both products means I don't need to do any Sephora Beauty Insider event math before deciding.
- "Top Picks For You" section closes the email with a personalization gesture that rewards long-term openers.

## What's weak

- "So Blurred Lip & Cheek Whip" lives five days after "So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" (June 25, email sixteen) — the name overlap is too close. Without disambiguation copy, this reads like a re-promotion or a misprint, which is corrosive at streak-length.
- Zero loyalty acknowledgment anywhere — no points, no Rouge/member mention, no "you've been opening these for three weeks here's something for you." ILIA explicitly made me feel like a known customer on June 22; e.l.f. still treats me like a cold prospect.
- The first CTA, "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS," is a collection link — vague and category-wide. At email twenty, I don't want to browse, I want to buy the two specific things you led with.
- The cadence is starting to read as noise. Even Ulta's aggressive streak breaks pattern with double-point windows; this streak just keeps arriving without a loyalty reward beat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Disambiguate So Blurred vs. So Blurreal in-copy** — one line: "meet the matte-finish sibling to So Blurreal" turns potential confusion into a collection story and rewards the subscribers who've been paying attention since June 25.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" with product-specific CTAs** — "SHOP POUT GOAL" and "SHOP SO BLURRED" keeps the double-drop energy and doesn't orphan me on a collection page.
- 3. **Insert a loyalty hook** — "Grab both for $16 and earn 16 points" or a member-early-access callout. At email twenty I need something that's about *me*, not just the drop.
- 4. **Pull the "Top Picks For You" section up** — it's personalization, which is the scarcest signal in this streak; burying it below the fold wastes it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Two new $8 drops: lip plumper + cheek whip`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your lips called. Two new drops answered.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pout Goal Lip Plumper + So Blurred Cheek Whip — both $8, both genuinely new.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The shimmer-to-matte duo you didn't know you needed — $8 each.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html

```
Some email clients (Outlook Web, Apple Mail) render the `<title>` value in tab labels or preview UX. An empty title is a minor but avoidable gap.

**Nested `nl-container` tables (likely template duplication).**
The outer email shell opens `<table class="nl-container">`, and `html_block block-1` immediately opens a second `<table class="nl-container">` inside it. This double-nesting suggests a content block was copied with its wrapper intact, producing redundant layout scaffolding. While most clients render through it, it adds ~800 bytes of unnecessary markup and can cause MSO rendering quirks.

**Three consecutive empty html_blocks (block-2, block-3, block-4).**
```html

<table class="html_block block-3">...<div>&#160;</div>...
<table class="html_block block-4">...<div>&#160;</div>...
```
