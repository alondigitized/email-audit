# Beach for the sky 🏖️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four sends in. I've been counting Lush the way I count how many times Ulta restacks the same "ends tonight" headline — with a little spreadsheet energy and a lot of brand loyalty that's being tested. And I need to say this up front: they earned the last few sends. The Sanrio collab on June 30th was legitimately a 9. "I've got chills" was clever. But now I'm looking at the second beach-forward email in three days — "Planning a trip?" dropped July 2nd, and here we are again with sun, sand, and the same warm orange palette — and I have to talk about what's happening to the creative seam.
- The editorial architecture is genuinely good. Before Sun → Full Sun → After Sun → Touch Your Toes is a routine framework that works as a discovery moment. Rose Jam, Ocean Salt, Eau Roma Water, Brazilliant — real Lush heroes getting honest real estate. The "Practice Safe Sun" pun in the hero is exactly the Lush voice I subscribed for, and the pedicure callout at the bottom was a surprise that made me actually scroll back up. The photography reads warm, summery, and demographically close enough to me that it doesn't feel like it was targeted at my mom.
- But here's my problem: it's Fourth of July, Sally Beauty just handed me 25% off sitewide with zero asterisks, and ILIA ran three days of fresh creative with actual savings in the subject line. Lush gave me a well-styled routine breakdown with no price anywhere. No offer. No Lush Rewards callout. Nothing for a loyalty-obsessed shopper to latch onto. It's beautiful editorial on a holiday when my inbox is screaming deals. I'd mark it as read and move on. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **"Practice Safe Sun" hero pun** lands immediately — it's exactly the cheeky Lush tone that keeps me subscribed, and it differentiates the brand from generic "hydrate + protect" SPF messaging.
- **Before / Full / After sun structure** gives the email clear logic — I know exactly what I'm supposed to do with this information, and it makes the product selection feel curated rather than dumped.
- **"Touch Your Toes" pedicure section** is the sleeper hit — unexpected category pivot at the bottom that made me genuinely pause; "Shop Pedicure" is one of the more specific CTAs I've seen in Lush's recent rotation.
- **Color palette and model photography** are seasonally right — the warm peach-orange tones and the female-skewing casting reads 25-35, not 45+, which is where I actually live.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum anywhere in the frame.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no urgency, on a holiday weekend** — this is the one that stings. It's July 4th and my inbox is a discount buffet. Lush showing up with zero price signal while Sally Beauty is running 25% sitewide feels tone-deaf to the moment.
- **Second beach email in three days** — "Planning a trip?" already used the beach hook on July 2nd. Coming back with another sun-protection theme without a fresh creative angle makes this feel like a campaign brief that ran out of ideas, not a new chapter.
- **No loyalty or rewards visibility** — I'm a beauty tier tracker. If Lush has a points program, this email gave me zero reason to click because of it. Compare to Sephora's Stacking Weekend emails — even their survey email managed to surface 100 BI points in the preheader. Lush left that lever entirely unpulled.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is too generic** — the pedicure section earns its "Shop Pedicure" button; the hero deserves equally specific copy. "Shop Sun Essentials" or "Build My Routine" would do actual work here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single offer or exclusive hook** — even a "spend $50, get free shipping this weekend" or a "Rouge-equivalent loyalty multiplier" gives someone like me a reason to act on a holiday. Drunk Elephant ran a "Sleep your way…" editorial on July 2nd with no discount and it still worked because the subject created curiosity pull; Lush needs either the creative intrigue OR the offer. Right now it has neither.
- 2. **Differentiate from the July 2nd send** — if you're running two beach emails back-to-back, they need clearly distinct angles. July 2nd was travel-sized products; this one should be "full-size routine build" or "post-beach recovery." The distinction needs to land in the hero copy, not just the subject line.
- 3. **Swap "Shop Now" for a routine-specific CTA** — "Build My Sun Routine," "Shop the Before-After Set," or even "Start with Before Sun" — anything that respects the editorial structure the email already built.
- 4. **Surface the loyalty layer** — even a one-line callout ("Lush Rewards members earn on every order") at the bottom of the hero section changes the calculus for points-focused shoppers.
- 5. **Let the "Touch Your Toes" section breathe earlier** — the pedicure hook is genuinely differentiating for a summer-at-home moment; putting it fourth in a scroll hierarchy means most mobile readers won't reach it.
