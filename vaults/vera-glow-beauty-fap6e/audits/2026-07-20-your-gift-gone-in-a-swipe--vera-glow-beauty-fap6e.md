# Your gift, gone in a swipe
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I'll give ILIA this: "Your gift, gone in a swipe" is the sharpest subject line they've written in this entire weekly streak. The pun earns it — mascara swipe plus urgency, two meanings landing at once. I opened it genuinely curious, which is more than I can say for yesterday's send, which used their brand tagline as a subject line and earned exactly zero points from me for effort.
- But the email is doing too much at once. The hero promises one free gift, the body delivers four separate bundle modules all with different CTAs, and then a standalone "Free Mascara Duo" section lives at the bottom with yet another button. I could not figure out whether the mascara in the hero is the same mascara in that bottom module, or whether the $35+ threshold applies to both, or just one. That kind of confusion is a conversion killer — I'm not doing homework for a brand that's sent me six emails in eight days.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. No click, no forward. The subject was the best part and the email didn't earn it.

## What's working

- "Gone in a swipe" in the hero cursive is genuinely pretty — ILIA's aesthetic is consistent and the script typography with the mascara wand illustration is elevated
- "15% OFF THE DUO + FREE GIFTS" is legible, specific enough, and passes the two-second read test — Ulta buries their offers below the fold, ILIA gets credit for surfacing it
- "BUILD YOUR CART" is a real CTA — verb plus noun, no vague "Discover" energy
- Products shown (mascara, liner, tint, skincare SPF duo) map squarely to what someone like me actually buys, no weird demographic mismatch
- The "Makeup that makes your skin better™" branding is at least cohesive with ILIA's positioning even if I'm exhausted hearing it

## What's weak

- Day six (at minimum) of daily ILIA sends, and this is the second "free gift" angle in four days — July 18 was literally "Two gifts are better than one," so the mechanic is already stale
- Four separate product modules with four CTAs plus a fifth standalone section = decision paralysis; the hierarchy collapses and there's no clear hero product to anchor on
- "Makeup that makes your skin better™" appears *again* inside the body copy after ILIA used it as their subject line yesterday — that's copy-paste filler that signals creative fatigue
- Zero loyalty or points language anywhere — I'm a Sephora Rouge and an Ulta Diamond and I'm always doing the math; ILIA gives me nothing to calculate with
- "While supplies last" is vague urgency that undercuts the "gone in a swipe" subject; the subject implied NOW but the email delivers no deadline

## Recommendations

- 1. **One offer, not four.** Collapse everything into: "15% off + free Limitless Lash Mascara on orders $35+ — today only." Build the entire email around that single value prop and remove the three other bundle modules entirely.
- 2. **Make the free gift the visual hero.** The mascara illustration in the hero is small and decorative. Swap it for a full product shot — that's what I'm supposedly claiming; show it to me.
- 3. **Add a real deadline.** "Ends tonight at midnight" converts infinitely better than "while supplies last." The pun promises urgency; the body doesn't deliver it.
- 4. **Rest the gift mechanic for a send or two.** Three free-gift emails in five days trains me to wait for the next one rather than click on this one.
- 5. **Drop a loyalty line.** Even one sentence like "Rouge members earn points on every bundle" gives me a reason to prefer this over waiting for a Sephora event.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free Limitless Lash Mascara — with any $35+ order, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gone in a swipe: your free mascara expires tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Claim it before someone else does. Free mascara with $35+ — no code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `15% off your favorite ILIA duo + a free mascara. Ends at midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A — Google Fonts loaded via `@import` (high impact)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Montserrat will silently fail to load for the majority of recipients. The fallback chain `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` on `p, h1, h2, h3` is correct, so rendering degrades gracefully — but the intended typeface is effectively unused.

**B — Klaviyo custom fonts also via `@import` (same issue)**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same blocking behavior. If a custom brand font is served here, it will not render in Gmail or Outlook.

**C — Hover state suppressed on all links**
```css
a:hover {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Hover color is identical to default state. Users in hover-capable clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) get zero visual affordance that text is a link. This is a UX regression, not a rendering bug, but it's enforced at the CSS level.

**D — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Minor: some accessibility tools and screen readers surface `<title>` as the document name. A meaningful value (`ILIA — Last call for your free mascara duo`) improves screen reader context.
