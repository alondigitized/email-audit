# 3 lip shades, 20% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Day four. We're officially in the "remind the stragglers" phase of ILIA's Buy 3, Save 20% run — and today they shifted the creative from gamified (dice on day two, rock-paper-scissors on day three) to something more utilitarian. "Color by Numbers" is a clever enough concept, and the numbered product listings (01, 02, 03) reinforce the mechanic, but honestly the creative peak was "Rock, paper, save." This one lands more like a product grid with a theme attached.
- What I'll give them: the Red Scale section at the bottom is legitimately useful. COOL/NEUTRAL/WARM with actual shade names and undertone labels is exactly the decision-making aid that makes picking a three-product bundle feel achievable rather than overwhelming. That's smart, practical beauty content — closer to what I'd expect from a Sephora editorial than a promotional email.
- What I won't forgive: it is July 4th, the sale has been running since July 1st, and there is zero urgency language anywhere in this email. No "ends tonight," no "last day," nothing. Sally Beauty dropped a full holiday creative reset today and scored a 10 for it. ILIA just... kept going. I'd mark this as read and circle back later, which is exactly the wrong behavioral outcome for a day-four sale push.

## What's working

- **Hero model is my demo.** Young, freckled, natural skin — she looks like someone I'd follow on Instagram. Very aligned with the 29-year-old Millennial ILIA has been targeting all campaign.
- **The Red Scale is genuinely clever.** COOL → Blue Note (Cherry Red), NEUTRAL → Case Study (Classic Red), WARM → Piazza (Poppy Red) — that's a real tool for picking three shades without second-guessing yourself, which is the whole friction point of a buy-three mechanic.
- **Code visibility is excellent.** "USE CODE: BUNDLEUP" is in the first scroll, and it repeats in the banner. No hunting for the offer; it's right there.
- **Product specs earn their column inches.** "12-hour poppy red," "24 hours of hydration," "buildable, breathable" — short, scannable, benefit-led. Not filler copy.
- **Ulta Beauty Certified badge in footer** lands for me specifically as a Diamond member. Tiny, but it registers.

## What's weak

- **Zero holiday acknowledgment.** It's July 4th. Sally Beauty did a full thematic reset today that felt intentional. ILIA sent the same sale email with a slightly different framing. A single line — "celebrate with 20% off" — would have cost nothing and created context.
- **No deadline, no urgency, day four.** Glossier ran fake "ends tonight" language for over nine days straight and I still wanted to click earlier in the week just because of the pressure. ILIA is doing the opposite — zero urgency — which is more honest but far less converting.
- **Creative downshift is noticeable.** After dice and rock-paper-scissors, "Color by Numbers" reads flat. It's a workable concept but the visual execution is basically a numbered list. The playful energy that made days two and three score 9s is gone.
- **The asterisk on "any three shades.*"** doesn't ruin the email, but I notice it. If you're day-four of a sale and still footnoting your core offer, that's a micro-friction moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** Day four of an unanchored sale needs an end date, full stop. Even "Sale ends July 5" would create the behavioral lift this send is missing. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Lean into the holiday hook.** "20% off three reds — because summer doesn't stop for anything" is fifteen seconds of copy that makes the send feel intentional rather than automated.
- 3. **Move the Red Scale above the product grid.** It's the most useful piece of content in the email and it's buried below three product descriptions. Lead with the decision tool, then let the products fill in the blanks.
- 4. **Make the hero concept land harder visually.** "Color by Numbers" could show literal numbers overlaid on the lip shades or the model — right now the numbered list (01, 02, 03) is doing all the conceptual work and it's not punchy enough.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Red looks better in threes — 20% off ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pick your red: 20% off any 3 shades`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `COOL, NEUTRAL, or WARM — find your red + save before it's over.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code BUNDLEUP. Three shades. One perfect red lip. Ends July 5.`
