# Crown Affair x ILIA—say yes to summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Eight. This is ILIA number eight in nine days, and I want that on the record before I say anything else — because it is absolutely the lens I'm reading this through. The fatigue from back-to-back "Skincare Mode: Summer" and "A gift for you" and "This lip color lasts" sends is real, and it dulls everything that follows. That said — I actually paused on this one. Crown Affair x ILIA is a genuinely surprising pairing, and "surprising" is something ILIA has not managed to serve me in about a week.
- The production is clean, the collab story makes sense (hair ritual into skin ritual, five steps, two brands), and "The Pink Towel" is specific enough to become a thing. Drunk Elephant did something similar with their "Summer to go" kit framing and it landed because the product told the story. Here the towel does the same work. I also appreciate that this feels like actual new content — not the same SPF hero I've seen four times this month.
- But there's no price anywhere. No urgency hook beyond "limited edition" with no deadline. And there's copy at the very bottom that reads "Making Fall Moves for Fall Skin" which stopped me cold — in a June summer collab email that's either a stale template block or editorial drift, and neither is a great look. I'd click on the Pink Towel to check the price. I would not forward this until I knew whether it was $28 or $128.

## What's working

- Crown Affair x ILIA is genuinely unexpected — this isn't the sixth SPF roundup; a hair-meets-clean-skin collab is worth an email slot
- "The Pink Towel" as a named, standalone hero product has real personality and specificity; Charlotte Tilbury names its kits obsessively for the same reason — named things sell
- The five-step ritual layout earns the collab story; I can trace the hair-to-skin pipeline and understand why these two brands share space
- Models skew 20s-30s with a natural, clean aesthetic — the demographic signal matches me exactly, which Ulta often misses by going broader
- Visual hierarchy is tight — the collab header lands first, the towel hero lands second, the ritual builds below it; no broken images, no layout drift

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere — for a limited-edition collab I need a price anchor before I'll click; e.l.f. always puts the number in the hero and I never have to wonder
- "Making Fall Moves for Fall Skin" in the footer breaks the summer season signal and reads like a left-in template block that no one caught before send
- "Win the Whole Routine" giveaway is buried below three product callouts — if that's the low-friction conversion hook, it belongs in the first scroll, not after the ritual module
- Eight emails in nine days — this collab deserved to be the only ILIA email this week; sharing the week with seven others means I'm grading on a curve just to stay fair

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add price anchoring in the hero** — "The Pink Towel, $XX" next to the CTA removes the biggest click barrier; Glossier does this consistently and it reads as confidence, not clutter
- 2. **Scrub the fall footer copy** — "Making Fall Moves for Fall Skin" has no business being in a June summer collab email; replace with a clean collab-specific footer or a restock/waitlist signup
- 3. **Surface the giveaway earlier** — move "Win the Whole Routine" above the ritual product grid; it's the lowest-friction entry point and it'll pull readers into the full scroll
- 4. **Give "limited edition" a deadline** — "launching June 23, limited quantities" or "available while supplies last — quantities won't be restocked" forces a real decision
- 5. **Dial back the cadence** — this email would have hit an 9 as the standalone week send; as email eight it's fighting itself

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts (confirmed issue)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (both web and Android) and ignored by Outlook. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica, sans-serif` for a significant portion of recipients. This is expected Klaviyo behavior but worth flagging if brand font fidelity matters.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Screen readers and some preview panes surface the `<title>`. A whitespace-only value degrades accessibility. Should be "ILIA × Crown Affair — Say Yes to Summer" or similar.

**No mobile font-size reduction for headings**
```css
h1 { font-size: 40px }
@media (max-width: 480px) { h1 { font-size: 40px !important } }
```
The mobile override explicitly re-asserts 40px — same as desktop. At 320–375px viewport widths this can cause overflow or forced scaling. h2 (32px) and h3 (24px) have the same pattern.

**All anchor states share identical color, including `:hover`**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
No visual hover feedback. Minor; common in email but worth noting for webview/browser rendering.
