# Take spray into your day 💦
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Coming off back-to-back wins — the BFe.l.F.s National Best Friends Day send legitimately earned a 10 in my inbox two days ago, and the blush besties follow-up was still cute at an 8 — I opened this expecting more of that energy. What I got is something quieter: a clean, well-structured skincare system email that's actually genuinely useful, even if it's a little beige after the party we just had.
- The "summer skin soothing system." headline does what it needs to. Four steps, four products, prices right there — no "starting at," no hover-to-reveal, no asterisk math. I can see the full routine costs $11 + $12 + $11 + $18 and make a decision. That's exactly the transparency I wish Ulta would bring when they push a full category story — their D&G fragrance send yesterday looked gorgeous but buried the price entirely. This is the opposite of that problem.
- Would I click? Probably step 2 — the mist — because the subject promised a spray moment and I want to see what it is. Would I forward it? No. This is a browse-and-wishlist email, not a "you have to see this right now" moment. Mark as read, maybe revisit at the next Sephora Insider event when I'm building a cart.

## What's working

- **4-step layout with visible pricing throughout** — I can do the bundle math instantly without leaving the email, and that's rare; Drunk Elephant's recent "Easiest Glow Ever" had beautiful copy but made me click through just to find a price
- **Hero model** — glowing skin, spray bottle, summer light on a Black woman — is exactly right for the product story and feels genuinely aspirational rather than generic stock-photo energy
- **"summer skin soothing system."** lands as a calm, intentional brand statement; the lowercase period does the brand voice work without feeling try-hard
- **Each product step has its own SHOP NOW** — I'm not forced into a "shop the collection" landing page when only one product caught my eye
- **Clean render, zero visual clutter** — the step-by-step format keeps the eye moving naturally and nothing is competing for attention

## What's weak

- **Subject promises spray, body delivers a full 4-step system** — the mist is step 2 of 4; the hook and the content don't quite match, and that's a small betrayal of the open
- **Zero urgency, zero offer** — no sale, no Insider event tie-in, no "just launched" signal; after DE ran "20% Off Before It's Gone" last week, a straight-price email sitting next to it in my inbox feels like a missed activation lever
- **"Top Picks for You" at the bottom** is the algorithmic drag mat that shows up on nearly every e.l.f. send at this point — after email 26 in roughly 25 days, this section has fully become wallpaper for me
- **No loyalty acknowledgment** — I'm a known subscriber with history and there's nothing here that nods to that; even a quiet "earn points" line would make this feel like it was sent *to me* rather than blasted to the list

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the subject around the system value**, not just one product type — the $52 full-routine math is the actual story and it goes completely untold in the subject line
- 2. **Add a loyalty or event angle** if one exists — even "stock up before [event]" gives me a reason to act this week instead of bookmarking and forgetting
- 3. **Give the mist a headline moment** — if the subject is spray-forward, let step 2 have one differentiated benefit line the other steps don't get (ingredient callout, bestseller badge, anything)
- 4. **Swap the "Top Picks for You" module** for a social proof block — a star rating or a "top-rated in skincare" badge on one or two of these products would convert harder than the algorithmic grid
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 4-step summer skin routine 💦 from $11`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The spray that anchors your whole summer skin routine`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Holy Hydration Mist + 3 more steps — full routine under $55`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cleanse, hydrate, eye cream, moisturize — summer skin sorted`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Tracking domain:** All links route through `links.elfcosmetics.com` (Braze click tracking). UTM parameters are encoded inside the Base64 redirect payload and are not inspectable from source — downstream UTM hygiene cannot be confirmed from this audit.
- **Image CDN asset age:** Logo image is served from `braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6537d6e7f775160058956f5a/original.png?1698158310`. The cache-buster timestamp `1698158310` resolves to **October 24, 2023** — this appears to be a reused asset from ~3 years ago. Verify the asset renders the correct current creative.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` is blank. Apple Mail and Gmail web render this in the browser tab and in some notification surfaces; a missing title is a minor gap.
- **Redundant nested `nl-container`:** The logo image block is wrapped inside a full `nl-container > row > row-content > column` table tree, itself nested inside an `html_block` div inside the outer `nl-container`. This double-nesting is non-standard and adds unnecessary DOM depth that can cause layout fragmentation in Outlook 2016–2021 when table widths compete.
- **Empty spacer blocks:** `html_block block-2`, `block-3`, and `block-4` are fully empty `<div>` elements adding byte weight with zero rendered output. Likely builder artifacts that were not cleaned up.
- **Link color/decoration suppression:** `#MessageViewBody a{color:inherit;text-decoration:none}` removes all visual link affordance globally. This degrades accessibility for users who rely on underline/color cues to identify links.
- **Google Fonts via `<link>`:** Loaded via `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans">`. Most email clients (Outlook, Gmail Android) block external CSS; the fallback `Arial,Sans-serif` is present and will render correctly.
