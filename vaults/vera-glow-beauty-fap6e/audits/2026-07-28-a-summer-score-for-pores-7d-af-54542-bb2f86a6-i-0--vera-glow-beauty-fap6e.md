# A summer score for pores ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- Ten. Days. Straight. e.l.f. SKIN is now the most persistent presence in my inbox, full stop, and today it's a single-ingredient product story — PLZ Clarify Facial Oil with 2% salicylic acid, "a summer score for pores." The green-and-model hero is pretty, the ingredient callout is legit dermatologist-adjacent copy, and $16 is an easy add-to-cart. But I've officially lost count of how many times this brand has shown up this week, and nothing about the tier program, points, or my purchase history is anywhere in this email — it's a stranger pitching me a serum, not a brand that knows I'm a repeat customer.
- I'd click "get it for $16" purely because the price is low-risk and the ingredient story is clear, but I would not forward this — it's not exciting enough to share, just competent. Compare that to Ulta's "20% off one qualifying item just landed in your lap" send I got, which had an actual discount and playful voice; this is just a $16 oil with a checklist of benefits and zero urgency.

## What's working

- The green-tinted product-in-hand hero photo is genuinely scroll-stopping and on-brand for e.l.f.'s current aesthetic.
- "Droppin' by to clarify" pun section breaks the benefits into four quick icon+text bullets (pore refine, lightweight, blackhead prevention, all skin types) — easy to skim on my commute.
- The close-up dewy skin shot with the oil droplet is a smart, specific visual that actually shows what the product does rather than just modeling it.
- "Top Picks for You" carousel at the bottom adds real product variety (moisturizer, makeup remover, drops) at three different price points.

## What's weak

- Zero personalization — no name, no skin type quiz result, no "since you loved X" callback, despite my Sephora Rouge/Ulta Diamond purchase history living somewhere in a CRM.
- No urgency, no discount code, no bundle math — just a flat price. Sephora and Ulta both lean on points multipliers or timed offers; this reads flat by comparison.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is the tenth e.l.f. email in ten days (following yesterday's hair launch and the lipstick gift the day before), and today's "find your skincare" quiz banner feels like a retread of engagement tactics I've already seen this week.
- "e.l.f. is for every eye, lip, and face" tagline at the bottom is a nice sentiment but does nothing functionally — feels like filler before the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pull back the send frequency — ten emails in ten days is Lush-level noise without Lush's color-grid payoff; even one skipped day would help open rates recover.
- 2. Add a loyalty hook — "e.l.f. Beauty Squad members get double points on PLZ Clarify" would instantly make this feel like it's for me instead of a cold blast.
- 3. Put a real deadline on the $16 price — "through Sunday" or a bundle discount with the Top Picks items would drive urgency Ulta already nails weekly.
- 4. Move the "find your skincare" quiz higher or drop it — it's competing with the hero offer instead of supporting it.

# Technical Email Audit — e.l.f. SKIN "A summer score for pores ✨"

## 1. Technical Summary
Automated QA passed only 27% of checks: 4 CTA links resolve to relative (non-absolute) URLs and fail redirect resolution, the plain-text MIME part is empty, and both unsubscribe-related headers are absent from the captured payload.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 broken redirect targets (FAIL):** All primary links are wrapped in a `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...` click-tracking redirector (Cheetah/CM Group-style). Four of them decode to **relative paths with no scheme/host**, which the redirect resolver rejects as "unknown url type":
  - `/products/holy-hydration-face-cream?Size=Mini+0.529+oz+%2F+15+g` ("Shop Now")
  - `/products/holy-hydration-thirst-burst-drops?Size=Regular+1+oz+%2F+30+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/account/login?return_to=%2Faccount` ("Receipt scanning — Earn rewards anywhere you shop e.l.f.")
  - `/pages/mobile-app?pid=Email&utm_campaign=072826_plz_clarify_summer_break_ooo&bxid=6a013826e53882007d6a1618&c=email&shortlink=email&` ("beauty squad — Download the e.l.f. app")
  
  These need to be fully-qualified (e.g., `https://www.elfcosmetics.com/...`) at the point they're loaded into the ESP's link-wrapping step, or every client that follows them will hit a dead/misrouted redirect rather than the product page.
- The mobile-app link's decoded query string ends in a dangling `&c=email&shortlink=email&` with no trailing key=value — either a truncated param or a template variable that failed to populate.
- 12 of 41 total links were probed (footer/social/utility links deprioritized per QA methodology); an unsubscribe link was confirmed present but not enumerated in the findings above.
