# Back-to-school e.l.f.ssentials under $8 😍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "back-to-school" is doing a lot of work for a subject line in September — I'm 29, I haven't bought a Trapper Keeper in over a decade, so that framing already feels like it's talking past me to a Gen Z or mom-of-a-tween audience. That said, once I actually look at the email, e.l.f. redeems itself fast: "Get both for only $10" is dead simple math, the mascara is $4 and the blush tint is $6, and I can do that addition without opening a calculator app. That's more than I can say for the Ulta send I logged this week where I needed to decode a coupon code and exclusion list just to figure out what 20% off actually applied to.
- The rest of the scroll is a competent little cascade — mascara hero, blush tint hero, a GWP banner for spending $50+, then a random music-album ad for "Mirror Mix" wedged in the middle like an intrusive thought. That album insert is the same beat e.l.f. ran on 9/25's "Your beauty slaylist" — they're clearly leaning hard into this artist tie-in campaign, and honestly it's starting to feel like the same marketing meeting output twice in three days.
- Would I click? Probably yes, on the $6 blush tint — that's an easy add-to-cart at my price point. Would I forward it to a friend? No, this is a "browse later" email, not a "you need to see this" one. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The $10-for-both math up top is genuinely the clearest offer framing I've seen from e.l.f. all month — no code, no fine print hunting.
- Two distinct, well-lit hero shots (mascara wands fanned out, blush tints tumbling) — I can tell exactly what I'm buying at each scroll stop.
- The GWP banner ("full-size Clear Brow & Lash Mascara on us when you spend $50+, no code needed") is a real incentive that nudges me toward a bigger basket, and "no code needed" removes friction Ulta never seems to remove.
- "Top Picks for You" section with real prices ($11, $11, $2) gives me a personalization signal, even if it's probably just recent-browse retargeting.

## What's weak

- "Back-to-school" as the entire campaign hook doesn't land for a 29-year-old Sephora Rouge / Ulta Diamond shopper — I'm not buying dorm makeup, I'm buying my usual reorder.
- The "Mirror Mix" album ad in the middle of a makeup email is a total non-sequitur — it breaks the shopping flow and I almost bounced right there. This is the second time this week e.l.f. has shoehorned the same artist-launch tie-in into a product email (see 9/25's "slaylist" send).
- The bottom third is just a wall of flat gray category-nav bars (New/Eyes/Lips/Face/Skincare/Hair) — zero visual interest, feels like filler after a genuinely strong top half.
- No mention of my Beauty Squad tier status or points anywhere — as someone who does the points math religiously between Sephora and Ulta, e.l.f. treating me like a cold lead instead of a repeat customer is a miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or relocate the "Mirror Mix" album banner — it doesn't belong in a product-offer email and actively interrupts the purchase path. If e.l.f. wants to keep pushing this tie-in, give it its own dedicated send like 9/25 already was.
- 2. Ditch "back-to-school" as the framing for non-student segments — swap in a generic "2 steps, $10" hook that doesn't alienate anyone who isn't shopping for a dorm room.
- 3. Surface Beauty Squad points or tier status near the top — even a "you're 200 points from your next reward" line would beat silence.
- 4. Replace the flat gray nav bars at the bottom with actual product thumbnails per category — that's dead space right now.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 for $10: mascara + blush tint, no code`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your lashes + cheeks, sorted for $10`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: free full-size mascara when you spend $50+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No code needed — just add to cart`

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses standard Braze-generated MJML/HTML with an ESP link-tracking wrapper (`links.elfcosmetics.com`); automated QA returned only a 27% pass rate, primarily due to four broken destination links, a missing/empty plain-text MIME part, and absent unsubscribe/authentication headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 4 of 12 probed material links fail resolution with "unknown url type" errors. The tracking redirector (`links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/...`) is unwrapping to **relative paths instead of absolute URLs**, e.g.:
  - `/products/power-grip-primer?Size=Regular+0.8+oz+%2F+24+ml` ("Shop Now")
  - `/products/cream-glide-lip-liner?Color=Mauve+Aside+-+Warm+Rose+Mauve` ("Shop Now")

- These are missing the `https://www.elfcosmetics.com` (or equivalent) host prefix before the redirect target was encoded — a link-building/template bug, not a dead page per se, but it will render as a broken/unresolvable link in most click-tracking and preview tools, and may fail in-app deep-linking on the mobile CTA.
