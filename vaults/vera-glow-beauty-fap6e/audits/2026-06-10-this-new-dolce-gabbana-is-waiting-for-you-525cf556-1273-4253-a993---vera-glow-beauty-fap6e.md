# 🌊🍋🛥️ This *new* Dolce&Gabbana is waiting for you...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- I've already opened this email today. I know that because I reviewed the identical subject line — same boat emojis, same D&G asterisk — earlier this afternoon and scored it a 6. Ulta did this exact same move with NARS yesterday (sent the same 4-PC GWP email twice in an afternoon), and now they're doing it with D&G. At this point it's not a glitch, it's a habit, and it's starting to erode the goodwill I've built up as a Diamond member.
- The email itself is fine — the Light Blue visual is summery and clean, the "Beauty Break" framing gives it a lifestyle context, and the 10% off band at the top is the kind of concrete offer I can work with. But I've already done the math on this one. The first send was my decision point; this one just gets a swipe-left.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd archive it and add a mental tally to the "Ulta send frequency is getting out of hand" column.

## What's working

- **D&G Light Blue hero is seasonally perfect** — the lemon-and-ocean visual speaks exactly to June, and it's cohesive with the 🌊🍋🛥️ subject emojis in a way that actually earns those emojis.
- **10% off offer is front-and-center** — visible before I hit the main D&G section; that's the offer math I care about as a Diamond member.
- **"Beauty Break" lifestyle framing** is light enough to not feel cheesy — it gives context without overselling.
- **Layout is clean and uncluttered** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no render junk. Ulta's execution is technically tidy.
- **Star ratings visible lower in the email** — at least they're threading in some social proof, even if it's buried beneath the fold.

## What's weak

- **This is the second send today.** That's the thing that doesn't work. There is no version of this where I didn't notice, especially after the NARS double-send yesterday.
- **"New" D&G when it appears to be Light Blue** — Light Blue is not new. It's a classic. The asterisk in the subject implies a new launch but the hero reads like a classic fragrance feature. That small bait-and-switch on "new" is a trust nick I don't forget.
- **No urgency signal in the body** — the 10% offer doesn't have a deadline visible, so there's no reason to click now vs. next week (or vs. the Sephora Insider event I'm already timing my spend for).
- **Fragrance product detail is missing** — notes, flanker distinction, bottle size — nothing that would make me choose this over popping into Sephora and testing it myself.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress the duplicate send.** This is the highest-leverage fix. It's not a creative problem, it's a list-management problem. One send per day per campaign, full stop. NARS got two sends yesterday; D&G gets two today. That's not a coincidence — it's a platform config issue that's burning subscriber trust faster than any bad subject line could.
- 2. **Make "new" earn its claim** — if this is a flanker, name it. If it's a relaunch, say "back by demand." Don't let "new" float without an anchor; it reads as clickbait when the hero resolves to a classic.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the 10% off** — even "through Sunday" would create a reason to act now instead of tabling it. Compare to how Drunk Elephant's June 8th "20% Off Before It's Gone" send worked: the "before it's gone" framing did the urgency work even without a specific date.
- 4. **Swap the generic product grid for curated "complete the ritual" logic** — ILIA's HAIM collab email on the 10th did something smart: it told me *why* these products belong together. Ulta's grid below the D&G hero is algorithmic noise by comparison.
- 5. **Pull the star ratings above the fold** — social proof at the bottom is wasted; if Light Blue has strong reviews, lead with that on a fragrance email where I can't sniff through the screen.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External Google Fonts blocked by major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:...&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web + Android), all Outlook desktop builds, and Samsung Mail strip external `<link>` stylesheets. DM Sans falls back silently to the `.helvetica` rule (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`). Not breaking, but the intended typeface will not render for the majority of recipients.

**Global link-style suppression — WCAG 1.4.1 violation**
```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
}
```
This Gmail-wrapper rule removes all visual link differentiation. Any anchor without explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` will be visually indistinguishable from body text — a confirmed failure under WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color).

**`translate="no"` on `<html>` root**
```html
<html lang="en-US" translate="no" ...>
```
Suppresses Gmail/Chrome auto-translate for non-English speakers. Acceptable only if this send is scoped exclusively to English-language audiences.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>` suggests unresolved token**
```html
<title>Ulta Beauty </title>
```
The space after "Ulta Beauty" is consistent with a merge token (e.g., `[[FIRST_NAME]]`) that rendered empty. Surfaces as the tab/preview title in some webmail clients. Verify against the ESP template source and confirm a non-empty fallback is set if the token is dynamic.
