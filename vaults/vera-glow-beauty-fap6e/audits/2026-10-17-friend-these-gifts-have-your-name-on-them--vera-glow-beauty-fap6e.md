# Friend, these gifts have your name on them
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the same two-gifts VIP email as yesterday's "The (2) gifts of VIP," in a new outfit. The velvet makeup bag and mini mascara are both back, and "Double Take" replaces the old hero. After four 20% sends and then yesterday's gifts, a second gifts email in two days feels like ILIA is out of ideas. The subject also opens with "Friend," which is the same fallback greeting as the 10/11 "Friend, your 20% off trio awaits." I read that as a mail-merge default, not personalization.
- I do the points math on everything, and this one made me stop and recalculate. The mini mascara needs $75+ and the velvet bag needs $100+. The banner at the top says "free velvet bag with $100+," the body copy says "with any $100+ VIP order," and the product tile says "any $75+ order." That's three thresholds in one email, plus a separate 15% off on the sets and an asterisk pointing to "details" on the site. Sephora Rouge would never make me decode a gift tier. Sephora Insider puts the dollar line right under the hero.
- The pretty parts are real. The rust velvet bag is cute, the "Perennial Beauty" art collab is a genuinely nice section, and the layout is clean. Would I click? Probably one tap on the bag tile to see if the $100 math works with my usual cart. Would I forward it? No. It's a repeat, and it's not a launch.

## What's working

- The velvet bag tile with the "A VIP EXCLUSIVE" tag is the strongest gift visual. It reads as a real perk.
- The close-up faces in the hero are diverse and clearly aimed at a younger beauty shopper like me.
- The "Eyeing These?" product grid is clean, with big tiles and obvious "shop now" buttons. It's easy to thumb through on a phone.
- The "Perennial Beauty" section with the Marleigh Culver artwork gives the email some brand personality. That's a real limited-edition hook, and Ulta's mass-prestige sends rarely have one.
- The footer trust row (Planet Partner, Leaping Bunny, Pact, Shade Match) is a handy reminder of why people buy ILIA.

## What's weak

- Three different spend thresholds ($75 and $100, with the $100 bag shown as VIP-only) make the offer hard to parse. The fine print is also just "See ILIAbeauty.com for details."
- It repeats yesterday's gifts creative. I got "The (2) gifts of VIP" on 10/16 and this on 10/17. Glossier did the same with its second 20% email in three days.
- The "15% off sets" badge is tucked into the middle of the email. I only noticed it on a second pass, so the one real discount is buried.
- "Join free + shop" in the hero doesn't tell an existing member anything. I'm a Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond who already has loyalty accounts, and nothing here acknowledges tier status or points.
- The page runs very long, with too many sections after the gift pitch ("In Safe Hands," Clean at ILIA, Shop All). The urgency is gone by the third scroll, and there's no deadline anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. Collapse the gift tiers to one clear line, like "Free velvet bag at $100+, free mini mascara at $75+," and say it once in the hero and once above the buttons. I'd also put a real end date next to it.
- 2. Stop sending the same gifts two days in a row. Space them out, or change the hook. A "bag back in stock" angle or a member points boost would feel like news.
- 3. Move the 15% off sets up beside the gifts. A tiered deal like "15% off sets + free gift" is something I'd actually do the math on.
- 4. Drop the "Friend" greeting unless there's a real first name behind it.
- 5. Cut the email by about a third. The "In Safe Hands" and Clean sections belong in a welcome flow, not a promo.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free velvet bag at $100 — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your VIP gift: mini mascara + velvet bag`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75 for the mascara, $100 for the bag. 15% off eye sets too.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIP gifts end Sunday. See which tier your cart hits.`
