# What's that you smell?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Lush showing up in my inbox with a fragrance showcase — I'm not mad at it, but I'm not reaching for my phone either. This is a full product-line parade: Rose Jam, Dirty, Super Milk, Sticky Dates, Seize the Spray, and 29 High Street all crammed into one scroll. It feels like Lush printed their product catalog and hit send. There's no hook, no discount, no "you specifically should care about this right now" energy.
- What really gets me is that there's not a single offer. No limited-edition drop, no sale percentage, no gift-with-purchase — nothing. And that's actually very Lush of them (they almost never discount), but then you'd better compensate with editorial storytelling or exclusivity. This email doesn't do that. It's just... here are six things we sell. Six things I could find by clicking their homepage.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't click, I wouldn't forward it. Maybe I'd screenshot the Rose Jam banner because that aesthetic is genuinely pretty, but that's not a conversion, that's just me saving a mood board for zero dollars.

## What's working

- **Rose Jam section lands visually** — the warm, feminine, pink-toned layout for that product line is consistent with why people love that scent; it actually speaks to me
- **Hero "GOOD COMMON SCENTS" headline is clever** — the pun earns a micro-smirk even if it doesn't move me to click
- **CTAs are specific per product line** — "Shop Rose Jam," "Shop Dirty," etc. are better than a generic "Shop Now"
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images loaded, text is readable at mobile scale
- **Brand identity is unmistakable** — from the black-bar logo to the bold typography, this is clearly Lush and only Lush

## What's weak

- **Six product sections = zero priority** — when everything is featured, nothing is featured; my eye doesn't know where to go
- **Zero offer, zero urgency** — no sale, no event, no "launching today," no countdown; nothing tells me *why now*
- **The Dirty section with the male model is a misfire for me** — if this isn't segmented, it reads like a unisex blast; I don't buy Dirty for myself
- **No loyalty or rewards acknowledgment** — Lush has a program; this email acts like I'm a stranger off the street
- **Subject line is all pun, no payload** — cute, but it tells me nothing about what's inside; I might just let it sit

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and commit.** Rose Jam is their most beloved feminine scent — lead with it, make the whole email *about* it. Everything else can live in a secondary row.
- 2. **Give me a reason to click today.** Even without a discount, "back in stock," "limited run," or "new in the Rose Jam line" creates urgency. Right now there's none.
- 3. **Acknowledge my loyalty.** One line like "As a Lush loyalty member, you're seeing this first" would change the entire feel from generic blast to insider send.
- 4. **Cut Dirty from this specific send** — or put it in a separated "for gifting" frame. As is, it breaks the cohesive feminine aesthetic of the rest of the email.
- 5. **Preheader is wasted** — whatever it says (I couldn't read it at this resolution), lean into a specific product or scent name.
