# Hot takes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- "Hot takes" lands in my inbox two days after "Help your skin. Save the planet." — and honestly, Drunk Elephant is doing that thing where the subject line is so stripped-down it's almost daring me to ignore it. I opened anyway, because eight months of DE training had me curious. Inside? A summer skin-recovery editorial: hero copy reframes sun damage as something your skin needs to *rest* from, then drops a product-by-concern grid — retinol for sun damage, the Bouncy BrightFacial for spots, F-Balm for tight skin, Virgin Marula at the end as a flex. It's cohesive and DE-coded through and through. But the 10% offer is buried in a "Subscribe to Save" pitch at the very bottom, which feels like a bait-and-switch compared to the confident, offer-free hero.
- I'd click — probably the A-Passioni or F-Balm link because I'm actively shopping both. I would not forward this. There's no urgency, no launch moment, no reason for a friend to act right now.

## What's working

- **The hero concept earns it.** "GIVE IT A REST." is peak DE — smart, a little sarcastic, seasonal. Pairing it with the summer-moisture-loss explanation in the subtext makes the product logic feel like editorial advice, not a hard sell.
- **Concern-based product grid is genuinely useful.** "For sun spots and hyperpigmentation," "For dry, super-dry, or tight skin" — this is how I actually think about shopping. It maps to my decision-making process, not just DE's SKU catalog.
- **Visual aesthetic is fully on-brand.** Warm tones, clean white product shots, the model with glowy summer skin. No identity confusion here — this could only be DE.
- **Virgin Marula Oil as the "AND!" surprise at the end** is charming. It's a flex written like an aside and it works.

## What's weak

- **"Hot takes" tells me nothing.** Compared to "Damn, your skin!" or "Time to come clean," this subject has zero information. I opened on brand loyalty alone — a less-trained subscriber would scroll past.
- **The 10% off is a subscription pitch buried in the basement.** The hero has no offer. The grid has no offer. Then "OH, HI. 10% OFF" appears at the very bottom — and it's tied to a Subscribe to Save commitment. That's not a deal; that's a conversion funnel disguised as a discount. Glossier runs cleaner standalone offers than this.
- **"SHOP NOW" everywhere becomes noise.** Every single product section has the same CTA. No variation, no "Learn more" for a complex product like A-Passioni, no "See full routine." It's lazy when the product-concern copy is doing real work.
- **No urgency, no event hook.** ILIA's "That flushed feeling" at least had a visual reason to exist in the moment. This feels like it could have been sent in June or August — the summer theme is seasonal but not time-sensitive.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real offer in the hero or cut the pretend offer at the bottom.** If Subscribe to Save is the play, introduce it in the hero alongside the skin-recovery framing — "recover your skin AND your wallet." Don't bury it as an afterthought after five product blocks.
- 2. **Swap every "SHOP NOW" for a concern-specific CTA.** "Fix my spots" / "Hydrate overnight" / "Start retinol" — these convert better and match the editorial voice DE already established.
- 3. **Give the subject a product or benefit anchor.** "Hot takes" worked for a viral tweet, not a skincare email. Tie the wit to something tangible.
- 4. **Add urgency or a limited signal.** Even one "Back in stock" or "Summer-only bundle" somewhere in the grid changes the calculus for a click.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments inside `<style>` block break two mobile rules:**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
`<!-- -->` syntax is invalid inside a CSS `<style>` element. Modern parsers ignore these declarations entirely. The `.contenttable` and `.mobile` width overrides do not apply on screens ≤600px. This is a layout regression on mobile.

**Duplicate font loading — 6 extra HTTP requests:**
The same three fonts (`Brown Bold`, `Brown-Light`, `Brown-Thin`) are loaded via both `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags and `@import url()` in the same `<style>` block. Each font is fetched twice before rendering begins. Remove the `<link>` tags or the `@import` blocks — not both.

**Mixed font origins — third-party CDN dependency:**
- `BrownRegular`: self-hosted at `drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/...` (Salesforce Commerce Cloud)
- `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, `BrownThin`: served from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` (third-party)

`db.onlinewebfonts.com` is not a controlled asset. If it rate-limits or goes offline, three of four font weights fall back silently. No explicit `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the `@font-face` rules.

**`.sm { display:none; }` has no recovery rule:**
The class is globally hidden. There is no `@media` block in the visible source that re-shows `.sm` elements. If this class is applied to mobile-only content, that content is hidden on all viewports.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and affects users with low vision.

**`*{line-height: 100%}` universal override:**
Applied to every element. Causes text clipping on multi-line blocks in several clients (notably Samsung Mail and older Android WebView). Should be scoped to specific selectors.
