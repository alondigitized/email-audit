# Fresh Finds for Summer ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. We are at seventeen Sally emails in three weeks — I'm counting this one and the June 11 BOGO Hair Care send is still fresh in my inbox — and the subject line they chose was "Fresh Finds for Summer." Not "30% off sitewide." Not "Root touch-ups + press-ons starting today." *Fresh Finds.* That's a placeholder subject line, and for a brand that finally earned an open last week with a clean mechanic, it feels like a regression.
- To be fair to the email itself: the body actually delivers. The 30% off sitewide banner hits immediately, the hero model is gorgeous (natural curls, perfect gel nails — very much my vibe), and the structural sections are tight: roots, press-ons, L.A. Girl, eyes, a BOGO. The Pride Month panel at the bottom is timely and not forced. The email knows who it's for.
- But "Fresh Finds for Summer" in the inbox, after seventeen sends, is a skip candidate. I'd have caught this on cadence fatigue alone. I would open it because I know Sally's 30% sitewide is a real number, but only because I'm on Brand History 17 and recognize the pattern. A normal subscriber who's half-zoned-out? Gone. I wouldn't forward this — I'd text my hair friend the specific L.A. Girl liner or the press-on section link, but the email itself doesn't earn a forward.

## What's working

- **30% off sitewide is the first thing I see** — the red banner doesn't make me scroll or squint; the offer is the hero before the hero image, and that's the right call.
- **The model casting is on point** — Black woman with natural curl texture and done nails is the exact demo for a DIY root-touch-up email; feels intentional, not stock-photo random.
- **Section architecture is clean** — roots → press-ons → L.A. Girl → eyes → BOGO flows like an actual beauty routine, not a random product dump.
- **Pride Month panel is earned this month** — it's June 13, it doesn't feel tacked on, and the color blocking integrates with the L.A. Girl palette sections above it.
- **"No salon required" is a great brand line** — it's specific positioning that separates Sally from Sephora and Ulta, and it's doing real work in the hero subhead.

## What's weak

- **The subject is doing zero heavy lifting** — "Fresh Finds for Summer" tells me nothing about the 30% sitewide or any specific product; compare this to ILIA landing an Este Haim quote or e.l.f. announcing a new Halo Glow shade by name. I had to open to find out there was an actual deal.
- **BOGO 50% appears again** — the June 11 email was built around BOGO 50% hair care. Running it a second time a day later dilutes the urgency of last week's send and starts to feel like Sally's default mechanic.
- **L.A. Girl is prestige-adjacent in the Sally context but still drugstore** — as a Sephora Rouge shopper, I'm not excited by L.A. Girl the same way I'd be excited by a Redken or a Wella professional exclusive; it's a slight category mismatch for aspirational positioning.
- **No deadlines visible** — the 30% off has no end date I can see, which softens the urgency. Glossier's Friends Sale and Sephora's "STARTS TODAY" both showed me exactly when the window closed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 30% sitewide in the subject line, full stop.** The email has a real offer; the subject is hiding it. That's the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Add an expiry date to the 30% banner** — even "this weekend only" or a specific date creates the scarcity the cadence has been eroding.
- 3. **Retire BOGO 50% for one cycle** — last week's mechanic was BOGO. Running it again this week under a different subject trains subscribers to wait for it instead of acting.
- 4. **Pull one hero product into the subject or preview** — e.l.f. naming "Halo Glow Silky Powder Highlighter" in its subject outperformed vague seasonal positioning. Sally has a $9.99 steal or a specific press-on line somewhere in here; name one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off sitewide — summer roots, press-ons & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer refresh: 30% off everything at Sally`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Root touch-ups, press-on nails, L.A. Girl faves — no salon needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday — shop the summer essentials you actually use.`

**[LOW] `img { white-space: pre; }`**
`white-space` is not a valid property for replaced elements (`<img>`). It's harmless in compliant renderers but may produce unexpected whitespace behavior in older Outlook or WebKit-based clients. Remove or scope it.

`@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)` is below the common 480px threshold and misses devices in the 500–599px range that won't receive mobile layout overrides. Standard practice is ≤600px for a single breakpoint.
