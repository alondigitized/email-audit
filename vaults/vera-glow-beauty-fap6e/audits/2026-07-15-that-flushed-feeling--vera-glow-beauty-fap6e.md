# That flushed feeling
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- ILIA shows up with "That flushed feeling" and honestly, for a second I was charmed. The whole email is aesthetically on point — warm peachy-pink tones, that editorial blush-skin-melting-into-a-blush-product visual language they've been perfecting all year. The "we blush easily" hero copy is very ILIA, very wink-y, and I appreciated it.
- But here's my issue: this is the fifth ILIA email I've reviewed this week, and I'm starting to clock a pattern. Yesterday it was the mini brown mascara (which I genuinely loved), the day before was the sunscreen, before that it was the Wimbledon editorial. Now we're back to an editorial-first blush scroll with zero concrete offer. The "mini trio is back" tease at the bottom is the only hook that makes me feel like there's something to *do* right now — and it's buried below the fold. Beautiful email, but ILIA is starting to feel like that friend who tells a great story but never gets to the point.
- Would I click? Maybe, for the mini trio. Forward to a friend? Only if she's already an ILIA fan who does the loyalty points math the way I do. A stranger wouldn't know what to do with this.

## What's working

- **"We blush easily" hero copy** — self-aware, on-brand, and genuinely clever. It earns the open.
- **Seasonal alignment is locked in** — "that summer flushed feeling" is exactly the July 15 energy and doesn't feel recycled or stale.
- **The art of layering section** — a model actually demonstrating blush layering is useful, product-forward, and answers a real question I have about how to use their flush products together.
- **"The mini trio is back" at the bottom** — restocking a fan-favorite mini is a concrete reason to act, and the word "back" implies sell-out history, which signals desirability.
- **No render bugs** — layout renders cleanly, text is legible, images load, nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer anywhere visible** — no %, no dollar amount, no GWP, no free shipping threshold. For a Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond who is always doing the points math, there's nothing to calculate. Compare this to how Drunk Elephant pulled off "Help your skin. Save the planet." by leading with emotional hook *and* sustainability messaging — ILIA leads with vibes only.
- **CTAs are evocative, not actionable** — "glowy" and "in the flush" as apparent section labels are adorable but they're not buttons I can click with clarity. Sephora's product-category CTAs tell me exactly what I'm walking into; these feel like mood boards, not shop links.
- **Mini trio is buried** — the most click-worthy hook in the entire email is at the very bottom. That's backwards.
- **No loyalty signal anywhere** — I'm a known subscriber. There's no acknowledgment that I might have ILIA points, that this mini is exclusive to a tier, or that there's any reason *I specifically* should feel urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bring the mini trio up to the hero or a strong secondary slot** — "The mini is back" is the single most action-triggering line in this email and it's sleeping at the bottom. Swap hero to lead with the blush editorial *and* the mini CTA in a two-block structure above the fold.
- 2. **Add one concrete product detail to the layering section** — shades visible on the model, a specific layering sequence (flush first, then glowy on top), or even a "shop the look" tag. Right now it's just a beautiful lifestyle photo that doesn't convert.
- 3. **Give loyal subscribers something to feel** — even "as an ILIA subscriber, you're seeing this first" would do it. Drunk Elephant does this well. ILIA is treating me like a stranger.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTAs** — "Shop the flush" or "Get the mini set" beats "in the flush" every time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The mini blush trio is officially back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer flush routine, layered`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three ILIA shades, one flush moment — restock is live`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We blush easily. You should too — mini trio restocked`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` block — moderate risk:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` is stripped by Gmail (webmail), Yahoo Mail, and several others. The fallback `"Helvetica", sans-serif` is correctly defined and will render cleanly, but the custom Klaviyo font and Montserrat will not load in these clients. This is expected behavior for email but should be verified against design intent.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A whitespace-only title. Some screen readers announce this. Prefer omitting or setting a meaningful value (e.g., the subject line).

**Link color contrast — unverifiable without full DOM**, but `#5f4b3c` on `#faf7f4` background yields approximately 5.4:1 contrast ratio, which passes WCAG AA. However, since `text-decoration:none` is enforced on all states including `:hover`, links rely solely on color differentiation — fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color alone).
