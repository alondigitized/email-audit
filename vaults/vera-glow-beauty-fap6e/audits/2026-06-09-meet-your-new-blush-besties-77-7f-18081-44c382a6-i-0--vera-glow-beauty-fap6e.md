# Meet your new blush besties 💖
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-six e.l.f. emails in roughly 25 days, and here we land on "blush besties" — exactly one day after the "ultimate beauty besties" National Best Friends Day send scored a 10 in my inbox. The wordplay was earned on June 8th. Today it reads like someone recycled the creative brief without checking the calendar. That said, this email actually *does something* the BFD one didn't: it's a genuine product differentiation guide — three blush formats, three personalities, all under $8 — and that's legitimately useful content for someone who needs to know which formula to prioritize at the next Ulta 21 Days drop.
- The $7 price anchor on the new Soft Glam Cream Blush is doing real heavy lifting here, same way the $7 bronzer stopped my scroll on June 5. e.l.f. has found their unlock and they keep using it. Charlotte Tilbury's "UNREAL product" vibe emails give me zero information; this at least tells me whether I'm a soft-and-moody person or a dewy-and-intense one before I ever click.
- I'm not clicking today — the besties fatigue is real and there's no urgency forcing my hand. But I'd save this for reference before the next Sephora BI event to remember which formula I actually want. Forward to a friend? Only if she's specifically shopping drugstore blush and paralyzed by the format choices.

## What's working

- **The product comparison matrix is genuinely useful.** Three personalities, three formulas, all under $8 — this is the decision framework I wish more brands would build instead of just pushing one hero product with a vibe.
- **$7 on the NEW Soft Glam Cream Blush lands hard.** After June 5's bronzer email got me with that exact price point, e.l.f. clearly knows their value proposition. Price-in-copy is the strongest thing here.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Hero → three sequential product panels → nav. Eye flows exactly where it should, no cluttered banner ads, no off-topic categories getting in the way.
- **Model demographic fits.** Warm-toned, flushed, fresh-looking — she's clearly wearing these blushes, not posing near them. Much more relatable than ILIA's editorial HAIM campaign, which earned its 8/10 but would never make me think about a $7 drugstore pick.
- **No render bugs.** Clean load, no broken images, readable without zooming.

## What's weak

- **"Besties" is doing double duty 24 hours later.** The June 8 BFe.l.F.s send built its hook on a real calendar moment. Today it just reads like the concept didn't get retired in time. If this send ran June 11, I'd have forgotten the hook and it would've felt fresh.
- **No loyalty context.** I have e.l.f. rewards points sitting there and this email never acknowledges them — no points-per-dollar reminder, no reward to burn, nothing. Ulta would have at least dropped a points-math hint somewhere in the footer.
- **"SHOP NOW" is lazy CTA copy, three times over.** The personality-match quiz format sets up a perfect handoff — "Try Cream Blush," "Get the Glow Wand," "Shop Liquid Blush" — and instead we get three identical generic buttons that abandon the bit.
- **No urgency signal on the new launch.** Is $7 an intro price? Is this week-one only? The June 5 bronzer email radiated new-launch energy. This one just… exists, with no FOMO trigger.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give "besties" a 48-hour rest minimum.** Move this send to June 11 or rename the hook entirely. Two back-to-back besties concepts trains the subscriber to tune out the wordplay — the concept still works, it just needs space.
- 2. **Make CTAs product-specific.** Swap "SHOP NOW" × 3 for "Try Cream Blush" / "Get the Glow Wand" / "Shop Liquid Blush" — the personality-match format earns a specific payoff, not a generic button.
- 3. **Add a launch urgency signal under the $7 price.** Even "introducing now" or "limited-launch price" under the Soft Glam Cream Blush would create pull without feeling fake.
- 4. **Surface one loyalty line.** A single "earn points on every blush" cue in the footer would serve someone like me who's always doing the math on whether to buy now or wait for a multiplier event.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo link** — removes the element from keyboard navigation order:
```html
<a href="..." tabindex="-1" style="outline:none">
```

**`outline:none` on the same link** — eliminates the focus ring for keyboard/switch users. Combined with `tabindex="-1"`, this link is completely inaccessible to non-pointer users.

**Google Fonts `<link>` tag will not render in production clients:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail (web and Android), Apple Mail on iOS, and Outlook all strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets. Open Sans will silently fall back to Arial in the majority of recipients' inboxes. This is a known email limitation, but the fallback stack (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) is correctly specified.

**Three consecutive empty HTML blocks** (`block-2`, `block-3`, `block-4`) contain only whitespace `<div>` wrappers — likely leftover spacing artifacts from a Braze drag-and-drop template. They add ~300 bytes of dead markup and no visual or functional output.

**Nested `nl-container` table structure inside `html_block block-1`** — the inner block embeds a full standalone email table layout (including its own `nl-container`, `row-1`, `column-1`). This is valid but indicates the block was likely copy-pasted from another template rather than built natively, which increases nesting depth and parse complexity in older Outlook versions.
