# Our top shelf
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Drunk Elephant went full product-catalog mode on me today — "Our top shelf" is basically their entire skincare wall laid out like a museum exhibit, eight hero products in two neat rows with the "Strengthen with... Brighten with... Hydrate with..." verb ladder. It's clean, it's very on-brand DE (that colorful bottle army is genuinely satisfying to look at), and the GWP is real: free 5-piece set on $110+ orders, deluxe sample on $50+ subscriptions. That's a legitimate incentive, not a vague "treat yourself."
- But here's my issue — this is a top-shelf brand-awareness email pretending to be a conversion email. There's no % off, no dollar amount off, just "spend to unlock a free gift," which only moves me if I was already planning to drop $110. Compare that to Ulta's "20% off one qualifying item just landed in your lap" send I got — that's an actual discount with zero spend threshold, and it scored a 9 from me because the ask was instant, not conditional. DE also skipped my launch-day/beauty-event context entirely — no "new for August," no Sephora Rouge points math, nothing tied to the calendar I actually shop by.
- I'd scroll through this once out of habit (I do like DE), but I'm not clicking unless I'm already mid-cart. Not forwarding — this is a "mark as read, maybe revisit if my basket hits $80 anyway" email.

## What's working

- The product grid is genuinely well organized — eight SKUs, each with a one-word skin benefit, no clutter, easy to scan on the subway.
- The GWP mechanic is specific and real: "free 5-piece set with all orders $110+" plus a deluxe sample kicker for $50+ subscriptions — that's an actual incentive, not vague brand-speak.
- Color-blocked category shots (Moisturizers, Serums, Cleansers, Masks & Treatments, Kits & Bundles) make it dead simple to jump straight to what I need without hunting.
- "Shop Best-Sellers" as a secondary CTA is smart — it routes people who don't want to browse eight tiles straight to the proven winners.

## What's weak

- Zero % or $ discount anywhere — the whole email is a spend-to-unlock-a-gift play, which only converts people already planning a big order. I'm not one of them today.
- No skin type or skin tone personalization at all — every product just says "for skin" essentially, no "for oily skin" or "for dry/sensitive" targeting, even though DE's whole brand pitch is skin-smart formulas.
- No urgency beyond buried fine print at the bottom (offer valid 7/27–8/3) — that deadline should be in the hero, not six scrolls down in 8pt gray text.
- This is the fourth-ish DE send in a row and it's the least punchy subject of the bunch — after "Golden hour every hour" and "Feeling sticky?," "Our top shelf" reads flat and generic, like a shelf display, not a hook.
- Model diversity is thin — one lifestyle shot for Moisturizers, everything else is product-on-surface, so there's no real demographic signal that this is for someone my age vs. anyone's mom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the actual offer mechanics in the hero, not buried in fine print — "Free 5-piece gift, $110+" should sit right under "EASY, PEASY," not three screens down.
- 2. Add a real urgency line up top — "Ends 8/3" belongs near the CTA button, not in paragraph six of legal copy.
- 3. Layer in skin-type tagging on at least the top row of products ("Protini for dry/dehydrated," "C-Firma for dullness") — this is DE's whole differentiator and they left it on the table.
- 4. Swap one of the flat-lay category banners for a face/skin close-up — I want to see texture and glow, not just an orange on a table.
- 5. Tighten the subject line — "top shelf" doesn't tell me there's a gift attached, and that's the actual news here.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 full-size faves, free with qualifying orders — ends 8/3`
