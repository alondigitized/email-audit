# Friends, family: 25% off starts now!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- OK Drunk Elephant — I see the escalation play. Two weeks ago they hit me with "Drunk texting?" and I audibly gasped at the cleverness. Then the 15% off "Save on smoothies" a week ago, and now we're at Friends & Family 25% sitewide. The arc is clean. The yellow banner screaming "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS! GO SHOP!" is such peak DE chaos energy that I actually did laugh, and the product grid — Protini, Babyfacial, Lala Retro, Virgin Marula with prices already discounted — is doing the math for me. I'm a Sephora Rouge who will absolutely calculate whether this beats my Beauty Insider event stacking, and a sitewide 25% is a serious answer to that question.
- That said, the subject line is a quiet L after a three-subject wordplay streak that had me showing these emails to my group chat. "Friends, family: 25% off starts now!" is clean and functional, but compared to "Drunk texting?" and "When you're bright, you're bright," it might as well be a Ulta mass blast. DE built equity in wit and then cashed it in for a transactional F&F header. I get the logic; I just wish they'd found a smarter way to do both.
- Bottom line: I would click, I'd load up a cart, and I'd probably screenshot the product grid for my group chat. But I wouldn't forward it the way I'd have forwarded "Drunk texting?" — the subject doesn't earn that share-the-email energy.

## What's working

- The yellow banner "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS! GO SHOP!" is unmistakably DE — no brand confusion, no carbon-copy vibe, fully earned personality
- "25% OFF SITEWIDE" in the hero is huge, immediate, and requires zero parsing — Charlotte Tilbury's May 16 send made me do exclusion-clause gymnastics; DE skips that friction entirely
- The $100+ GWP tier (free Hydra-Vibe + Bright kit) is a smart mechanic that pushes me from cherry-picking one item to actually stocking up — that's loyalty-event thinking done right
- Product tiles with sale prices already applied — Protini at ~$29, Babyfacial at ~$35, Marula at ~$33 — is the exact math I'd do in my head anyway, surfaced in the email
- Multiple tiered sections (sitewide hero, spending bonus, mask GWP, product grid) give me different reasons to click depending on where I land

## What's weak

- Subject line breaks the three-send wordplay streak cold — after "Drunk texting?" and "Let's get something clear," this reads like a different brand wrote it
- Zero personalization throughout — "Are you a friend or a family?" is cute but I'm comparing this to Ulta's "Marisol, get $10 off" email that landed the same week and at least knew someone's name
- No visible end date in the hero — "starts now" builds FOMO but "ends Sunday" would close carts that night instead of next week
- "HAPPY SHOPPING!" is the weakest CTA copy for a brand with this much snark in their DNA — it's what a 2009 email newsletter button looked like
- The fine print block is long and dense; after ILIA's exclusion mess last month I now clock this as a potential trust flag even if the offer is clean

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop an end date or countdown in the hero** — "25% off ends Sunday" or a literal timer would do more work than any copy rewrite. "Starts now" without a close date is a one-legged urgency play.
- 2. **Replace "HAPPY SHOPPING!" with a CTA that earns the brand voice** — "SHOP THE F&F SALE" at minimum; better would be something like "GRAB YOUR 25% OFF (seriously, go)" — peak DE would be "STOP READING. CLICK."
- 3. **Surface the exclusions in plain language up top** — "25% off sitewide, including sets, no exclusions" in the banner next to the discount code buys more trust than dense legal footer text
- 4. **Add at least first-name personalization** — this send clearly has a list behind it; even a tier callout ("Hey Rouge—your cart is about to look very good") would lift the feel from mass to segmented
- 5. **Keep the wordplay streak alive in the subject** — the offer is strong enough to survive a clever line; you don't have to choose between wit and conversion

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] HTML comments inside CSS `@media` block — rules are dead**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important; }
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- ... -->` is not valid CSS comment syntax. Both `.contenttable` and `.mobile` responsive width rules are silently ignored in every client. Any element using those classes renders at its default width on narrow viewports.

**[MEDIUM] Triple font-loading for the same typefaces**

For `BrownBold`, `BrownLight`, and `BrownThin`, the email loads the same font via three stacked mechanisms:

1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` (HTML `<head>`)
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` (inside `<style>`)
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` (also inside `<style>`)

This generates 6+ redundant HTTP requests per font family at render time. Most email clients block external CSS/fonts entirely, so all three mechanisms fail in Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail unless images are enabled — but the redundancy still inflates page weight and parse time in clients that do execute them (e.g., Apple Mail on iOS).

`BrownRegular` is the only weight loaded cleanly via `@font-face` alone from `drunkelephant.com`'s own CDN — this pattern should be standardized.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user pinch-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires that text can be resized up to 200% without loss of functionality. This also triggers accessibility warnings in some inbox previewers.

**[LOW] Universal `line-height: 100%` override**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

A unitless percentage `line-height` on the universal selector collapses inherited line-height across all elements to 1× the element's own font-size. This is intentional for tightly-controlled table cells but will compress multi-line text blocks and cause clipping on descenders in some clients. Scope this to specific selectors rather than `*`.

**[LOW] `<title>` has no campaign context**

```html
<title>Drunk Elephant</title>
```

Inbox previewers and browser-view renders use the `<title>`. A campaign-specific value (e.g., "Friends & Family 25% Off — Drunk Elephant") is a minor improvement for preview fidelity.
