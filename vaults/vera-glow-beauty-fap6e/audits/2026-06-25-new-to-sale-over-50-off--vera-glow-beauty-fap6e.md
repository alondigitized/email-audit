# NEW To Sale! Over 50% Off! 🛍️💖
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. I know it's eleven because I've been logging CT sends since June 11 like a habit I can't quit, and now "NEW to Sale" shows up in my inbox and honestly? I sat with it for a second. Not because I'm impressed — because I had to decide if "new to sale" is meaningfully different from the last three "the sale is happening" emails I already read this week. The travel bag GWP is the hook that almost got me: a free Mini Glow + Go Bag at some spend threshold is the kind of math I actually do at checkout. But I'm looking at asterisks stacked on asterisks, no deadline visible, and an app push at the bottom that feels like a separate email got pasted in.
- The visual hierarchy is clean — CT always shows up looking polished. Pink palette, warm summer light, minis laid out on the bag. It reads feminine and aspirational in a way that genuinely matches how I shop. But I've had 45% off kits in my face since June 22. At some point "new to sale" needs to show me the actual new SKUs, not just reframe the same event in a different subject line.
- I'd click for the travel bag. I would not forward this. I'd call it a "worth-a-scroll" open, not a drop-everything moment.

## What's working

- **Free travel bag GWP is a concrete incentive I can act on** — seeing the minis inside laid out visually makes me want to reverse-engineer which spend tier unlocks it.
- **"Over 50% OFF" in the hero** reads immediately; CT earned points here vs. the Glossier sale that never officially ended — at least this has a directional number up top.
- **Visual execution is consistently CT** — warm pink, editorial feel, clean typesetting. It doesn't render like a Ulta batch blast.
- **Tiered gift ladder ($69 / $119 / $169) gives me a spending target** — that's smart GWP architecture; I do the math automatically.
- **"New to Sale" framing actually does something** — if there are genuinely fresher SKUs in this drop, that's a reason to go back to a sale I already browsed.

## What's weak

- **No specific SKUs named "new to sale"** — if you're telling me products are newly discounted, show me the products. Don't make me click through to find out if it's worth it.
- **Asterisk city** — "Over 50% OFF*" and "45% OFF ICONIC KITS*" both carry asterisks I can't read at this resolution. Fine print erodes trust on every single send.
- **No sale end date in the body** — Sephora's "Last Chance" email from June 24 was annoying, but at least "last chance" implies a deadline. CT is just floating in urgency-free summer vibes.
- **The app section feels bolted on** — "Discover the Magic of CharlotteTilbury.com + APP" with a diamond badge at the bottom of a sale email is a second email's CTA stuffed in the wrong place.
- **Eleventh email in 14 days** — cadence has made me partially immune. Even a genuinely good offer has to fight through "oh, another one."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the new SKUs** — the word "NEW" in the subject earns nothing unless the email body immediately shows me what dropped. One row of "these specific products just hit sale" with product names and before/after prices would triple my intent to click.
- 2. **Kill one of the two CTAs** — "UNLOCK 50% OFF" and "SHOP ALL SALE" in the same hero do the same job. Pick one. "UNLOCK 50% OFF" is better; keep it.
- 3. **Add a visible deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" anchors urgency without feeling fake. The Glossier non-deadline debacle (sale "ended" June 19, kept running through June 24) is exactly what happens when you don't commit.
- 4. **Move the app push to its own email or footer footnote** — it's diluting the sale narrative.
- 5. **Tier math in plain English** — instead of "1 GIFT / 2 GIFTS / 3 GIFTS" with dollar amounts in small print, try "spend $69 → 1 gift, $169 → all 3" as a legible summary line. I shouldn't have to squint.
