# Barrier Build is back
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- ILIA's third full-template send in two days, and my first reaction was that the hero is genuinely pretty. That huge close-up of the Barrier Build cream under a serif "barrier build *is back*" has real shelf appeal. But I'm back to the points math I did on 11/23 ("Your picks, up to 25% off") and earlier today ("Double the gifts"). The banner says 20% sitewide / 25% for VIPs, and the hero says 20% off*. The free-gift block then adds a second deal on top. It's the same Black Friday promo with a new product on top.
- The email does give me a reason to care: a restock. If I'd been waiting on the barrier cream, "back in stock" is the one real hook. Still, I've now seen this offer four times this week. I'd skim it, maybe tap through for the cream, and I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The hero is a clean, tactile cream shot with big serif type. It's the best-looking ILIA creative I've seen this week and far less cluttered than the plain-text "Marissa" notes.
- "Back in stock just in time" is a real news hook, which is better than yet another generic sale blast.
- "Shop Barrier Build" is one clear button with a specific verb and noun.
- The free complexion stick with any order is a low-friction perk, and the free mini at $100+ gives me a basket threshold to work toward.
- The footer trust row has Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, shade match and the VIP link. The free shipping over $50 line is a nice touch.

## What's weak

- The 20/25 math is still muddy. The banner says 25% for VIPs, but nothing in the body tells me how to get VIP or what it costs me. Sephora Rouge makes tier benefits obvious, and this just says "Join ILIA VIP" in the footer.
- The hero cream shot is soft and blurry, and the jar label is nearly unreadable. I can't see the product name on the product.
- The fine print is everywhere: "*See site for details" three times, plus "Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details." The "in studies of 21 people" claim also undercuts the "clinically proven" line.
- There's no price, no reviews, no rating and no ingredient callouts for the cream. Drunk Elephant's VIP email gave me a flat 30% with the code in a block, and this makes me hunt for the terms.
- The big pink "ILIA Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" block is dead space. It's not clickable and it adds nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the exact VIP math in the banner or hero: "20% off, 25% with ILIA VIP, ends Cyber Monday." Ulta's 11/24 tier ladder (50/40/30) did this much more clearly.
- 2. Replace the dead pink brand block with 2–3 real products or a review snippet (stars, a skin-barrier quote) and a price.
- 3. Use a sharper product image that shows the label, plus a "back in stock" badge, so the restock pops.
- 4. Collapse the repeated asterisks into one clear terms line.
- 5. Space out the sends. Four near-identical ILIA promos in 48 hours is why I'm numb to this one, much like e.l.f.'s ninth email in eight days.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, QA WARN):** `https://d3k81ch9hvuctc.cloudfront.net/company/SguDsz/images/c9cefca7-6b18-443a-896b-9e98ec5d842a.jpeg` is a content image served from the Klaviyo CDN. It has no alt attribute, so screen readers and image-blocked clients get no description.
- **Open pixel (minor):** The tracking pixel `trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01M87M0Y1B8BGDWFD7CY1SH1T5` also lacks alt. A decorative pixel should have an explicit `alt=""`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** The root element is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:o=… xmlns:v=…>`. This affects screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Empty title:** `<title> </title>` is blank.
- **Links look like body text:** The `<style>` block sets `a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }`. That is the same colour as the heading and body text (`h1–h4 { color:#5f4b3c }`). Inline links have no colour or underline cue, which fails WCAG 1.4.1 (use of colour) for any text links.
- **External font `@import` rules:** The email imports `static-forms.klaviyo.com/.../custom_fonts.css` and `fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat`. Gmail and several other clients strip `@import`. The declared fallback is `"Montserrat","Helvetica",sans-serif`, so the failure is graceful.
- **Mobile and Outlook handling:** The email has a viewport meta tag, `max-width:100%` on images, and MSO conditionals (`OfficeDocumentSettings`, `mj-outlook-group-fix`). Nothing here is flagged.
- **Preheader:** It is correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0; opacity:0`). The `͏` padding characters are standard.
