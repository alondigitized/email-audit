## Executive summary

- So "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" is back — the same digest wrapper they used on May 30 — and I'm staring at it thinking: did they just recycle the format *and* the franchise? The subject teases "NEW! Exagger-Eyes," but my inbox has had Exagger-Eyes content since May 28. What IS new is apparently the mascara variant, which the email does surface, but Charlotte is banking on me not noticing she's called everything in this franchise "new" for three straight weeks.
- That said — the free Pillow Talk hook is real bait. I'm not immune to Pillow Talk. No one who knows what Pillow Talk is is immune to it. If I hadn't already been conditioned by fourteen previous sends to treat Charlotte's "free" claims with the same energy I bring to a sample sale table, I'd have tapped through immediately. - Scroll-heavy digest format, multiple SHOP NOW buttons, no single product hero doing the heavy lifting. I'd click for the Pillow Talk offer. ## What's working

- **The Pillow Talk free gift is a legitimate hook.** "ONLINE ONLY! LUCKY TILBURY & PILLOW TALK" lands early enough in the scroll that I clocked it before tuning out — and Pillow Talk has enough brand equity that it earns a second look every time.
- **The mascara close-up renders beautifully.** The "NEW EXAGGER-EYES EYESHADOW MASCARA" section has a face-forward model shot that actually sells the product visually — lashes look full, skin looks real, it's compelling.
- **Clean rendering across the board.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy. Fifteen emails in and the production quality hasn't slipped, which I'll give them.
- **The lip close-up at the bottom is a scroll stopper.** Even buried at the end of a digest, that shot earns a pause.

## What's weak

- **"Charlotte's Magic Highlights" digest format is being reused too fast.** They ran this exact wrapper on May 30. Six days later it's back. That format worked once because it felt like a curated roundup — now it reads like a template they didn't bother to swap out.
- **"NEW! Exagger-Eyes" in the subject is wearing thin.** I got "NEW! Exagger-Eyes Easy Eyeshadow Sticks Have Landed" on May 28. Calling anything in this franchise "NEW!" nine-plus days later requires more specificity — the mascara is genuinely new product, but they've cried that wolf too many times.
- **Too many SHOP NOW CTAs, no hierarchy.** e.l.f.'s $7 cream bronzer email had one button, one product, one price — I knew exactly what I was clicking. Here I've got a SHOP NOW for Magic Highlights, a SHOP NOW for the Lucky Tilbury offer, a SHOP NOW for the mascara, and then more. The call-to-action math doesn't work when everything is equal priority.
- **No loyalty or member-tier signal.** I'm a Sephora Rouge and I do my Charlotte Tilbury spend at Sephora for the points. There's nothing here acknowledging that channel, and no reminder of what loyalty tier unlocks this offer specifically — Ulta's Charlotte Tilbury GWP email at least surfaced the GWP structure more cleanly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Isolate one hero and kill the digest format for this cycle.** The mascara launch deserved its own email — a single product, one CTA, the close-up lash shot as the full-width hero. The Pillow Talk free gift deserved its own email. Combining them into a digest dilutes both. Pick one, kill the other, come back tomorrow (they clearly will anyway).
- 2. **Rename the mascara offer specifically in the subject.** If the Eyeshadow Mascara is the actual new SKU, say that. "NEW: Exagger-Eyes Mascara Is Here — Free Pillow Talk With Purchase" is more honest and more clickable.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the Pillow Talk offer.** "Online only" is not urgency. "Online only, through Sunday" is urgency. ILIA's duo send gave me a cleaner value proposition than this with less copy.
- 4. **Surface Rouge/Diamond member pricing or a loyalty multiplier.** This email treats me like a stranger. I've opened fourteen of your emails in three weeks — act like it.
