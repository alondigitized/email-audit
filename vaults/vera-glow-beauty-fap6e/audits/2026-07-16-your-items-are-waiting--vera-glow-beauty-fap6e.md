# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Let me be real upfront: this is at minimum my fifth Skechers email in three weeks, and I've now watched the cart-abandon playbook cycle through "This style has limited availability," "Did you forget something?" (twice), "This is perfect for you!" (twice), and now "Your Items Are Waiting!" The subject rotations are doing their job on paper, but I know the genre by now. That said — the headline copy stopped me. "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US..." is the first genuinely self-aware line Skechers has served in this whole sequence. It's a little flirtatious, a little cheeky. I don't hate it.
- Below that, though, it's the same email dressed up differently: browse-abandon hero shoe, recommendation grid, 20% off Skechers Plus banner, a "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" button, and a Mommy & Me module that has absolutely nothing to do with me. The 20% off for Plus members with code SUMMER is real incentive — that's the kind of concrete offer I keep saying I want — but there's no deadline on it, so the urgency is theoretical. Sephora would have stamped "Ends Sunday" on that offer; Drunk Elephant would have built a countdown into the hero. Skechers just lets it sit there.
- Mark as read. Won't forward. If I'm feeling motivated later and the code still works, maybe — but nothing in this email makes me move now.

## What's working

- "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US..." is the best Skechers headline I've seen in this whole sequence — charming, self-aware, much warmer than the guilt-trip "Did you forget something?" from July 1st and July 15th.
- The 20% off for Skechers Plus with an explicit code (SUMMER) is a concrete incentive that's actually visible above the fold — I'm not hunting for the offer.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is a specific, action-oriented CTA — real improvement over the generic "SHOP NOW" from the June 30th send.
- The email renders cleanly: no broken images, no overlapping text, readable typography on mobile.
- "Summer Favorites Sale" keeps it seasonally grounded; feels current, not stale.

## What's weak

- The "Mommy & Me: Glide-Step Pro" section is targeting someone else's life — I don't have kids, and nothing in my browse history suggested I was shopping coordinating family looks.
- The recommendation grid below the hero is cluttered: at least six products including what appears to be Men's Slip-ins dropped into a women's cart-abandon email.
- No deadline on the 20% off code — I need "SUMMER ends July 21" or I'll keep procrastinating. Sephora doesn't leave this on the table.
- The five-email-in-three-weeks cadence has diluted every urgency signal Skechers is trying to fire. I identified the trigger pattern before I finished reading the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Mommy & Me module** unless she's browsed that category — showing irrelevant family products to a solo shopper breaks the "we know you" premise the headline just established.
- 2. **Add an expiry to the code** — "SUMMER · ends July 20" costs zero extra effort and doubles the click pressure. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 3. **Trim the recommendation grid to 3 products, all women's, all same category as hero** — fewer options, higher relevance, less scrolling.
- 4. **Merge the subject and offer** — the subject should hint at the discount so the open is pre-motivated.
- 5. **Strip Men/Kids/Clothing from the nav bar** — I was browsing women's athletic footwear; the nav links should reflect that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your cart + 20% off — come grab it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We held your Glide-Step Pro. Here's 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus code SUMMER works on your saved items — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cart hasn't gone anywhere. Neither has your discount.`

## Technical Audit — Skechers Cart Abandonment Email

### 1. Technical Summary
Email is sent via Attentive ESP (`skechers.attentivemail.com`); all links are wrapped in Attentive's click-tracking redirector. HTML is truncated, so compliance elements (footer, unsubscribe) and image alt text cannot be fully verified, but several issues are confirmed from the visible source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All links route through Attentive's redirector:**
```
https://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...
```
This is expected for Attentive. However, final-destination UTM parameters are encoded inside the `upn=` payload and cannot be verified without following the redirect. No raw destination URLs are exposed in the visible source to confirm UTM coverage.

**Web version link** is also wrapped in the same Attentive click tracker — this means a click on "web version" registers as a tracked click event, which inflates click metrics artificially.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag confirmed:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (Outlook web, Apple Mail tabs) display the `<title>` value. An empty title produces a blank window label or falls back to the subject line inconsistently.

**Preheader padding uses two distinct Unicode whitespace types mixed together:**
- `͏` (U+034F, Combining Grapheme Joiner) — used for first ~150 characters of padding
- `­` (U+00AD, Soft Hyphen) — used for the remaining ~200 characters

Mixing both types is non-standard. Some spam filters flag soft-hyphen padding specifically. Standardize on a single padding character (U+200C or U+00AD, not both).

**Image alt text:** The CSS rule `.image_block img+div{display:none}` confirms image blocks are present, but alt attributes cannot be verified from the truncated HTML. This must be checked on the full source.

**MSO/VML compatibility:** Correct — `xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`, `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, and `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` all present. Outlook conditional comments are properly structured.

**Responsive breakpoint** at `max-width:620px` is present with appropriate column stacking.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No personalization tokens (`{{first_name}}`, cart item names, product images, prices) are visible in the truncated HTML for what is a cart abandonment email. If the full HTML also lacks these, this is a significant issue — a cart abandonment email with no personalized cart contents reduces relevance and defeats the trigger's purpose. The subject line "Your Items Are Waiting!" implies personalized product recall; the source should confirm dynamic product blocks are present.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

HTML is truncated before the footer — **physical postal address and unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from the provided source.** These must be verified in the full HTML. CAN-SPAM requires both.

Authentication headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) cannot be assessed from HTML source alone — requires inspection of raw email headers (not provided).

Sender domain `msgs.skechers.com` is a subdomain used for ESP sending, which is consistent with Attentive's infrastructure. DKIM alignment should be verified against `skechers.com` as the organizational domain.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

UTM parameters on all links are buried inside Attentive's base64/encoded `upn=` parameter and cannot be validated without live redirect resolution. The QA process should include decoding or following at least one representative link to confirm:
- `utm_source` (e.g., `attentive` or `email`)
- `utm_medium` (`email`)
- `utm_campaign` (cart abandonment campaign ID)
- `utm_content` (distinguishes CTA buttons if multiple are present)

If UTMs are absent from the final destination URL post-redirect, cart abandonment attribution will be lost in analytics.
