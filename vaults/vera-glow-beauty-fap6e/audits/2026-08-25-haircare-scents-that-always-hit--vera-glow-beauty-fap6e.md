# Haircare scents that always hit 🍊🌸
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, e.l.f. Hair back again with "Haircare Scents That Always Hit" — and honestly, the fruit-and-flower styling here is the most effort I've seen this brand put into a hair send. Peach and blackberries for the "bright, fruity scent," peonies for "layers of vanilla and peony," citrus dripping in oil for the styling cream — it's actually pretty. But it's still hair, still $9-$10 a pop, and my brand history already flagged e.l.f. Hair as a sub-line I didn't know existed until 8/21's "Crew for a Great Summer 'Do." This is the second hair send in five days.
- Nothing here is for me specifically — I'm Rouge/Diamond, I'm doing points math on Sephora and Ulta buys, and e.l.f. is a mass-tier impulse add, not a considered purchase. The scent-marketing angle is a decent hook but there's zero mention of who this is actually formulated for (curl type, color-treated, fine vs. thick) — just vibes and produce metaphors.
- I'm not clicking. This is a scroll-past-and-archive email, not a forward-to-a-friend one — unless that friend specifically complains about boring drugstore shampoo smell, in which case maybe.

## What's working

- The produce/floral photography is genuinely gorgeous and on-brand for a scent story — peach and blackberries next to the shampoo callout is a smart visual metaphor.
- Each product has a clear price ($9, $10, $9) right under the name, so I'm not hunting for cost.
- "SHOP NOW" buttons repeat after every single product block, so there's no ambiguity on what to tap.
- "Top Picks for You" at least gestures at personalization, even if the picks (lip liner, primer, setting spray) have nothing to do with the hair theme above it.

## What's weak

- Three near-identical product-then-CTA blocks in a row (shampoo/conditioner, treatment oil, styling cream) with no differentiation in layout — it reads like a scroll of sameness by the third one.
- No ingredient or hair-type callouts (curly, color-treated, fine) — as a Sephora Rouge/Ulta Diamond shopper I expect at least a formulation hook, not just "smells like a peach."
- "Top Picks for You" totally breaks the hair narrative by dropping in a lip liner and face primer — feels like a generic module bolted on, not real personalization.
- This is e.l.f.'s second hair-focused send in less than a week (8/21 was "Crew for a Great Summer 'Do," 6/10) — the cadence is starting to feel like they're pushing a line nobody asked to hear about twice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the redundant product-block repetition — combine the three hair products into one scrollable carousel instead of three full-width stacked sections; it'll shorten the email and keep the scent story from feeling padded.
- 2. Add one line of real product info per item (hair type, "sulfate-free," "for humidity/frizz") — right now it's all scent, no substance, and that's a miss compared to how Drunk Elephant at least leads with an ingredient story even when repositioning old products.
- 3. Fix "Top Picks for You" so it's actually hair-adjacent (brushes, scalp serum, dry shampoo) instead of a random lip/face grab-bag that undercuts the whole theme.
- 4. Since this is the second hair send in five days, either space these out or bundle scent + styling into one comprehensive send — Ulta's App Fest send at least clustered its offers into a single clear calendar instead of drip-feeding sub-line content.
