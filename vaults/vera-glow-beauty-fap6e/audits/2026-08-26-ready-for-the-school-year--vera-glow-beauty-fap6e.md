# Ready for the school year?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- Same trope again, just wearing a different hat this week — Glossier already burned "Going back to school?" on me on 8/20, and now it's "Ready for the school year?" pushing basically the same bundle logic (kits, sets, "start the semester off right"). I'm 29, no kids, not in school, so the actual hook is zero for me — but unlike the 8/20 send, this one at least leans on product logic instead of pure nostalgia: it's just kits (No-Makeup Makeup Kit, Glossier Icons, Skincare Icons) repackaged with a back-to-school label slapped on. If I ignore the seasonal copy, the kits themselves are fine, familiar Glossier hero products I actually own versions of.
- No actual discount anywhere that I can see — "Shop + save" implies one but never states a number, which after Sephora's App Fest calendar and Glossier's own Cheek Week (20% off, hammered for two straight weeks) just reads like undercooked copy. This is a soft-touch, browse-and-maybe-buy email, not a "stop scrolling" one.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to a friend — there's nothing here worth passing along. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- The kits are legit Glossier hits — Balm Dotcom, Boy Brow, Milky Jelly, Invisible Shield — so even without wanting the "school" framing, the products themselves are trustworthy and on-brand.
- Clean, uncluttered layout with generous whitespace — easy to skim on my phone without feeling crowded, unlike some of Sally Beauty's confetti-dot chaos.
- Consistent Glossier visual identity (millennial pink, serif logo, minimalist product photography) — I know exactly whose email this is at a glance.
- The "Glossier Icons" travel pouch set clocks the after-gym/on-the-go use case, which is at least a plausible everyday moment instead of a purely academic one.

## What's weak

- "Ready for the school year?" — I logged the nearly identical "Going back to school?" from Glossier just five days ago (8/20). Two back-to-school sends in less than a week from the same brand is lazy, not a campaign.
- Zero concrete offer. "Shop + save" promises savings but never says how much — Sephora and Ulta would never let a CTA imply a discount without a number attached.
- No loyalty or rewards messaging at all — I'm cross-shopping Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond, and there's nothing here acknowledging repeat customers, points, or member perks.
- The right-side product images are literally cut off in my inbox render (the hero shot and the "No-Makeup Makeup Kit" headline text both run off the edge) — feels like a broken layout, not intentional design.
- No urgency, no dates, no "ends soon" — after two weeks of Cheek Week countdown messaging, this just feels flat by comparison.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the back-to-school framing entirely or commit to a real discount — right now it's a repeat of 8/20's angle with none of the urgency Glossier used for Cheek Week. Either put a number on "Shop + save" or drop the seasonal hook and sell the kits as "your everyday essentials, bundled."
- 2. Fix the render — the hero image and the "No-Makeup Makeup Kit" title are getting clipped on the right edge. That's a trust issue before it's even a copy issue.
- 3. Add a loyalty nod — even a line like "Earn double G points on kits this week" would give Rouge/Diamond members like me a reason to open over Sephora's App Fest emails.
- 4. Give at least one kit a real anchor offer (e.g., "$10 off any Icons set") instead of three flat "Shop now" CTAs with identical weight.
