# ILIA VIPs get more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- After five days of genuinely impressive ILIA storytelling — award editorial, brand overview, finish quiz, and then that disarming plain-text note from Marissa yesterday — this is what lands on May 20: a cold loyalty acquisition email. It reads like it was scheduled by a different team that didn't know Marissa just wrote me a personal note. The arc they had built (smart, editorial, warm, human) just got interrupted by a form letter asking me to "sign up" for a program I've never heard explained in dollar terms.
- I'm Sephora Rouge and Ulta Diamond. I live in loyalty math. But the loyalty math here is completely missing — 50 points for signing up means literally nothing without a redemption table. Sephora tells me exactly what 500 points converts to. ILIA gives me "collect points, don't worry they never expire" and expects me to leap. No product shot, no launch tie-in, no urgency, no connection to the Friends + Family sale that was dangled in both May 19 emails. It's a pitch vacuum.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read and move on? Immediately. The ≠VIP branding is design-school clever but the email earns none of the attitude it's trying to project.

## What's working

- ILIA's brand aesthetic is fully intact — ivory/blush palette, editorial type hierarchy, minimal layout — it looks expensive and premium even when the content underdelivers
- "SIGN UP NOW" is an unambiguous CTA, no mystery about the action
- The Join / Earn / Enjoy three-step breakdown is clean and scannable — I can parse the program structure in under ten seconds without effort
- Trust badges in the footer (Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, Pact Collective) are exactly why I care about ILIA over a generic beauty brand — they earned a spot on the page
- No render bugs — clean layout, readable text, nothing broken

## What's weak

- Zero personalization — this reads like a subscriber-zero cold acquisition email, not a loyalty upgrade email for someone who just got a personal note from the founder 12 hours ago; Drunk Elephant segments existing customers and the tone difference is night and day
- "50 points for signing up" is meaningless without a redemption chart — Sephora's Insider program makes the math obvious (500 points = $10), ILIA gives me nothing to anchor on
- No product photography anywhere — coming off the most editorial ILIA week I've ever seen in my inbox, there is nothing to covet in this send; e.l.f. ties loyalty incentives directly to hero products and I always respond
- The cadence break is jarring — Marissa's plain-text note yesterday felt like a peak moment in a beautifully sequenced welcome series; this feels like the CRM queue picked back up and didn't check what just went out
- The Friends + Family sale referenced in yesterday's emails is completely absent — if the F+F event is still live, connecting loyalty enrollment to it would have made this email actually urgent

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the dollar value of 50 points** — one line: "50 points = $X toward your next order" would turn a vague program pitch into a real offer. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Connect loyalty to the F+F sale moment** — "Sign up now and earn bonus points on your Friends + Family order" bridges the narrative from yesterday's email and creates urgency that currently doesn't exist
- 3. **Personalize the entry point** — If I'm already a customer, say so: "You've already earned X points — here's how to unlock them." Charlotte Tilbury fumbles this too; don't be CT
- 4. **Feature a hero product** — Even one product shot with a "earn 2x points on this launch" mechanic gives me a reason to click that has nothing to do with bureaucratic sign-up energy
- 5. **Decode the ≠VIP symbol** — One line of copy ("≠ = not your average loyalty program") or even a hover-state explanation would help; right now it reads like a broken merge token to anyone who didn't catch the brand's prior messaging
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 50 welcome points are waiting — here's what they're worth`
- **Subject Alt B:** `ILIA VIP unlocks early launches and real rewards`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Points that never expire + early access to every launch — join free`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sign up during Friends + Family and earn on every order you place this week`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts via `@import` (high risk)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` is stripped by Gmail and several other clients. Montserrat will silently fall back to the declared stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` — acceptable fallback — but the import itself is wasted and **exposes recipient IP addresses to Google on open**, which is a GDPR/privacy signal concern.

**Klaviyo custom font import also present**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Redundant with the Google Fonts import for Montserrat. Two external font requests on open increases render latency.

**Link text-decoration suppressed across all states**
```css
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Removing underlines and using a near-body-text brown (`#5f4b3c`) for all link states eliminates visual affordance. Inline text links (if any) will be indistinguishable from body copy for low-vision users and in plain-rendering environments.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some clients (notably Outlook on Windows) surface the title tag. A whitespace-only value may display awkwardly; should be set to a meaningful string or removed.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot assess; body is truncated. Flag for verification.
