# Ready, set, shop—new beauty has landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "new beauty has landed" — I've apparently already opened this exact one today (it's sitting right there in my own history at 8/10, calling it "Sephora's Tuesday drop email"), and rereading it doesn't move me much either direction. It's the classic Sephora new-arrivals catalog: hero banner, then six brand blocks stacked one after another — Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez, Josie Maran, Patrick Ta, Innisfree, Marc Jacobs Beauty, Burberry — each with a price and a one-line hook. It's pretty, it's on-brand, and it's completely offer-free until you scroll almost to the bottom.
- What actually stops my thumb is the "Spot it. Shop it." block near the end — 4X points on Pureology through 8/12 with code PURE4XPTS. That's a real, dated, points-math offer, exactly the kind of thing I built my whole Rouge strategy around. Problem is it's for haircare, which isn't my lane, and it's buried under six brand carousels I have to scroll past to find it. Compare that to Sephora's own 4X points on foundation/concealer send from 8/5 — same mechanic, but that one led with the offer and a hard "4X" badge up top. This one buries its best asset.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of habit, to peek at the Rare Beauty layering balm since I'm a sucker for a fragrance line extension — but I'm not forwarding this to anyone. It's a nice scroll, not a nice email.

## What's working

- The hero "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" plus the exclusivity flags ("NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA") hit my FOMO button — I do care about what I can't get at Ulta.
- Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez up top is a smart lead; it's a name-brand launch I'd actually stop scrolling for.
- Prices are listed right under every product ($16-$36, $30-$78, $34, $20, $29-$26, $192-$38) — no hide-the-price games, I can do my mental cost-per-use math instantly.
- "Clean at Sephora" and "Planet Aware" badges on Innisfree and Josie Maran are a nice trust layer I actually notice.
- The Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider / VIB / Rouge) at the bottom at least nods to the loyalty program, even if it's generic.

## What's weak

- No hero offer — the top of the email is all launch hype, zero discount, so there's no reason to open this over the six other Sephora emails I've gotten this week.
- The one real offer (4X points on Pureology) is stranded two-thirds down the page in a tiny two-column block, easy to miss on a phone scroll.
- Six back-to-back brand blocks with identical layout (image, name, "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA", price, Shop Now) start to blur together — no visual hierarchy telling me which one Sephora actually wants me to care about most.
- Nothing here is tier-specific to me as Rouge — no "your Rouge perks," no birthday gift reminder, nothing that says they know I'm not a first-time Insider.
- "Lip's Latest Lineup" teaser at the bottom is a total mystery box — no product shown, no name, just "NEW" and a Shop Now, which reads lazy next to how specific the rest of the email is.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 4X points Pureology offer (or a bigger, more-me-relevant version of it) into the hero. A dated, coded offer beats "meet your next haul" every time — Sephora's own 8/5 foundation/concealer send proved this works.
- 2. Pick ONE hero product (Rare Beauty is the obvious choice) and give it the full-bleed treatment; demote the other five brands to a "more new drops" grid below so the eye has a clear first stop.
- 3. Add a Rouge-specific line — even just "as a Rouge member, you get early access" — since I'm already doing the tier math every time I open a beauty email.
- 4. Actually show the "Lip's Latest Lineup" product instead of teasing it blind — Sephora has the assets, use them.
- 5. Tighten the subject/preview pairing so the preview earns its slot instead of reading like static shipping boilerplate.
