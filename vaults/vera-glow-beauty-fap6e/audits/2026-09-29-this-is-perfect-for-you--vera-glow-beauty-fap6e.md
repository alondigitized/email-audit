# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- "Still thinking about it? You've got great taste!" is a cart-abandon line, and I haven't put anything from Skechers in a cart. It's also the same subject line as 7/28 and 8/18, so this feels like the third rerun of one template. This time the product is at least in my lane: a burgundy women's Slip-ins bootie, not the die-cast-truck kids' sneaker from 8/19 or the men's Workout Walker from 8/16. I'm a Sephora Rouge / Ulta Diamond shopper, so footwear isn't my core category. But fall boots and slip-ins are the kind of impulse buy I'd make for myself.
- The offer is the best thing in here, and it's buried. The 15% guest / 20% member split sits below the fold, under six tiny thumbnails. Ulta puts its 20%-off code on the hero, and even Drunk Elephant's "$158 for $99?" gave me a price to do math on before I opened it. Here I'd click "Take Another Look" and hit the banner on the way down. I wouldn't forward it, and I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- The hero is a single clean product shot with a big navy headline and one button, so I know what to tap.
- The 15% guests vs 20% members split is a tier-math hook I like. It makes me want to be logged in.
- The women's styles feel seasonal: the UNO Rugged boot, the bootie and the loafer all say fall.
- The Afterpay/Klarna and curbside pickup blocks are useful trust and convenience cues.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The subject and headline are recycled. "This is perfect for you!" is the same line as 7/28 and 8/18, and it doesn't say what's perfect or why.
- The discount banner is below the fold, after the 6-shoe grid.
- The fine print contradicts the banner. The banner says "Thru 10/1," but the footer says 20% off boots and outdoor styles is valid 9/23–9/28. That expired yesterday, so I don't trust the 20%. It also says "online only," and a specific category is excluded from the headline offer.
- The grid captions are tiny and have no prices, no sale prices and no member-price math. I can't tell what 20% actually saves me.
- The floral slip-in hero skews older than me. There's no shade, fit or review detail, and the nav block (Women / Men / Kids / Clothing) is oversized filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 15%/20% banner directly under the hero headline and give it a real deadline that matches the fine print.
- 2. Reconcile the dates. If it's boots-only, say "20% off boots" in the banner instead of hiding the limit in the footer.
- 3. Show a was/now price on every product, with the member price called out, so I can do the points math.
- 4. Replace "Take Another Look" with something specific, like "Shop Slip-ins Boots."
- 5. Retire the recycled subject and hero. Personalize it to the product I actually viewed, and cap the grid at 3 items with larger type and star ratings.
