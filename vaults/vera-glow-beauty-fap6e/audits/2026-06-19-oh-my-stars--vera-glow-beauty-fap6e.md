# Oh, my stars
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because it's Drunk Elephant and at this point they've colonized my inbox — but "Oh, my stars"? I genuinely thought this might be a Sephora crossover or a zodiac skin quiz before I clicked in. The subject is a pun on the 5-star review theme inside, and while I appreciate the wit, it does zero work in a crowded inbox where I just saw a Glossier "ends tonight!" and two Sephora sends on the same day. In the inbox, cute is not a strategy.
- Once I'm in, the layout is clean and the B-Hydra product shot reads well. But here's the problem: two days ago DE sent me "Still time to save 20%" and now I'm getting a full-price product push with a GWP buried so far below the fold I almost missed it. That whiplash is real. I spent the first scroll thinking this was just a UGC round-up with zero offer attached.
- The "MAKING IT RAIN" GWP section at the bottom — deluxe samples of Amino-Rain Shampoo Serum and Lala Retro Whipped Cream plus the superskin bag for a $100+ purchase — is actually the most interesting thing in here. And it's the last thing I see. I would not forward this to a friend. I'd probably click through to peek at the bag, but I'm not buying at full price two days after a 20% off sale.

## What's working

- **Product hero is clean and immediate.** The B-Hydra shot is crisp, the pink-on-white palette reads premium, and "WELL DONE, B-HYDRA!" is punchy enough DE brand voice that I didn't hate it.
- **"SHOP B-HYDRA" is a specific CTA.** Not "Discover" or "Learn More" — it names the product, which is more than Sephora's own house emails usually manage.
- **The GWP bag image is genuinely cute.** The lilac/pink tote with the sample products staged next to it made me pause. If that had been the hero, different email.
- **Three distinct reviewers feel credible.** Real names (C, Jodi, Juli), specific use cases (acne-prone combo skin, arid climate hydration), not marketing copy.
- **No render bugs.** Everything laid out correctly, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **"At 45, my body is needing a daily routine of nourishment..."** — I'm 29. The very first review opens with an age that is 16 years older than me. Charlotte Tilbury at least runs aspirational models; this is just demographic targeting gone sideways.
- **The GWP is buried after a long review scroll.** The actual incentive to spend — the bag, the samples — doesn't appear until the third section. ILIA's "This lip color lasts. This gift won't." kept their GWP in the hero. DE put theirs in the basement.
- **Zero loyalty acknowledgment.** I'm a Sephora Rouge. DE sells at Sephora. There is no mention of Beauty Insider points, no Rouge early access, nothing that makes me feel like a known customer versus a cold prospect.
- **No discount after a sale.** June 17 was "Still time to save 20%" and now it's June 19 with full price. My brain is still in sale mode. This email needed to either extend the offer or make a very explicit case for why I'd pay full price right now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the GWP, not the UGC.** The bag + samples for $100+ is the actual hook — make it the hero. Restructure: hero = MAKING IT RAIN + bag image + spend threshold, then product callout below, then a review or two for social proof.
- 2. **Pull in a reviewer who sounds like me.** "At 45" should never be the opening quote in a send that's going to millennial Sephora Rouge members. Find Juli (acne-prone combo skin) or someone younger and put her first.
- 3. **Add a subtle loyalty tie-in.** Even one line — "Earn Rouge points on your $100+ purchase" or "Your Beauty Insider perks apply" — converts this from a cold sell to a member moment.
- 4. **The subject needs to telegraph something.** It can still be clever, but give me a hook:
- **Subject Alt A:** `B-Hydra's fan mail is unreal (+ a gift with $100+)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $100, get the bag. Here's why B-Hydra's worth it.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real reviews + the superskin bag waiting for you — only while supplies last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $100+ haul comes with Amino-Rain samples and the cutest tote`

## Technical Audit — Drunk Elephant "Oh, my stars"

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-layout email with several infrastructure-level concerns: triple-loaded custom fonts create unnecessary HTTP overhead, and invalid CSS comment syntax inside `<style>` blocks can cause silent rendering failures in strict parsers. Core structure is functional but fragile.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Third-party font CDN dependency (db.onlinewebfonts.com)**
All four custom font variants (BrownBold, BrownLight, BrownThin + Brown variants) load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`. This CDN is not controlled by Drunk Elephant. If it goes down or rate-limits, all branded fonts silently fail across the entire send.

**Triple font-loading redundancy**
Each custom font is requested three separate ways in the same `<head>`:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` 
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }`

This generates 3x the HTTP requests per font for clients that honor all three. The `<link>` + `@import` duplication alone is redundant — pick one.

**Cannot audit CTAs/product links** — HTML is truncated before body content. UTM parameters on outbound links are unverifiable from this source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTML comments inside `<style>` block (invalid CSS)**
```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment delimiters (`<!--` / `-->`) inside a `<style>` tag are syntactically invalid in CSS. These rules are silently ignored by any client that correctly parses CSS, meaning the `contenttable` and `mobile` responsive overrides never apply on mobile. This is a dead-code bug hiding as working code.

**`text-size-adjust` conflict**
`body` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%` but the wildcard rule immediately overrides it with `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`. The `none` value (rather than `100%`) actively prevents text scaling on some mobile clients and is the more aggressive/incorrect setting.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This locks user zoom on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. Users with low vision cannot pinch-to-zoom.

**Global `line-height: 100%` reset**
`*{line-height: 100%}` applied universally collapses line-height to font-size across every element. For multi-line text this renders lines touching or overlapping. Should be scoped or removed.

**Missing `lang` attribute**
The `<html>` tag has no `lang="en"` attribute. Screen readers cannot determine language; affects pronunciation and WCAG 3.1.1 compliance.

**`<title>` is generic**
`<title>Drunk Elephant</title>` — non-descriptive for screen readers that expose the document title.
