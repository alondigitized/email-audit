# These ingredients dew more for skin 👩‍🔬 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's where we are. I've been counting e.l.f. emails since May 15th and this is the one that arrives the day after the $7 cream bronzer — which, for reference, got my full attention because it had a *price on it*. Today it's an ingredient send and the creative is genuinely nice: "DRIP DRIP, SKIN SO LIT." in chunky yellow over a model whose glowy skin actually makes the product case. The casting is on point — she looks like someone I'd follow for a morning routine — and the layout moves cleanly from hero to product callout.
- Here's the problem: e.l.f. gave me a spec sheet with no receipt attached. "1% Hydrating Mission Complex & 8% niacinamide" is the kind of ingredient detail that should come with a price, a sale window, or at minimum a "Beauty Insider points event through [date]" hook. I'm Sephora Rouge — I stockpile when there's a multiplier, I don't impulse-buy off a "SHOP NOW" button with no number attached. ILIA's "NEW Skin Tint + Finishing Powder Duo" email last week gave me the ingredient story *and* a reason to move on it. This one just shows up and expects the interest to convert on its own.
- Mark as read. I might save Thirst Burst Drops to a wishlist for the next Sephora event, but there's zero click here today. Wouldn't forward — everyone in my group chat already knows e.l.f. exists.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is strong** — "DRIP DRIP, SKIN SO LIT." in that bold display font lands well and the model's skin is an actual demonstration of the product promise.
- **Ingredient specificity in the product section** — listing "1% Hydrating Mission Complex & 8% niacinamide" plus four concrete use cases (serum, primer, under-eye, foundation booster) is the kind of detail I actually read.
- **"Shop with Sephora" in the footer** — I noticed it, I'm Rouge, and seeing that partnership acknowledged in the same email that's pitching a skincare ingredient story is a quiet credibility signal.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — everything loaded, hierarchy holds on mobile, no visual garbage to scroll past.

## What's weak

- **No price anywhere above the footer** — I cannot do the math, and doing the math is my primary hobby. This is a dealbreaker for a stockpile shopper.
- **"SHOP NOW" is a nothing CTA in 2026** — Charlotte Tilbury puts the product name in the button; e.l.f. sends me to wherever and makes me find the PDP myself.
- **Thirst Burst Drops is not a new product to me** — this feature has appeared in roughly six of the last twenty sends and the bullet points have not evolved. "Four ways to use it" was fresh the first time.
- **No loyalty activation hook** — there's a "beauty squad" icon buried in the footer but it's about receipt scanning, not about a points event. Given the "shop with Sephora" callout, a "Rouge members earn 2x through [date]" line would have paid off.
- **Category nav tiles (NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE) are dead weight on mobile** — five grey buttons that lead nowhere specific, sitting between the product and the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the retail price and a loyalty hook directly under the hero CTA** — even a simple "Thirst Burst Drops, $xx — available at Sephora" line converts my interest into math I can act on.
- 2. **Make the CTA name the product** — "Get Thirst Burst Drops" routes me to the right PDP; "SHOP NOW" routes me to a decision fatigue spiral.
- 3. **Anchor the ingredient claims to a comparison** — "8% niacinamide — more than 2x what's in The Ordinary 4% formula" makes this feel like a briefing, not a brochure.
- 4. **Pull the Beauty Squad / receipt scanning CTA into the body** — anyone who hasn't started scanning receipts will never see it in the footer.
- 5. **Drop the category nav block or replace it with a curated "pairs well with" module** — two product recommendations that cross-sell within the skincare line would use that space better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Thirst Burst Drops: 8% niacinamide + 4 ways to wear it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your skin's thirsty — we have the drops`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `1% Hydrating Mission Complex, post-shower radiance, and yes — it works as a primer.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The dewy skin shortcut your routine is missing. Here's how it works.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Gmail web) surface the title in tab labels. Should contain the subject or brand name.

**Dead content blocks:** Three consecutive empty `html_block` divs (block-2, block-3, block-4) contain only whitespace:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These are builder artifacts from Braze's drag-and-drop editor. They add ~300 bytes of no-op markup and can occasionally trigger spam filters that flag tag-to-content ratios.

**Nested `nl-container` tables:** The hero image block wraps a full `nl-container`/`row`/`row-content` table structure inside an outer `html_block` — effectively two complete layout layers. This is a Braze template composition issue (embedding a full template inside an HTML block) and may cause unexpected width behavior in edge-case clients.

**Alt text:** `alt="e.l.f."` on the hero image is present but generic — if images are blocked, the hero renders as a single letter-period string with no product context. Acceptable for a logo lockup, but the hero appears to be a full-bleed creative, not just a logo.

**Responsive breakpoint:** Media query fires at `max-width:660px` against a 640px container — correct and consistent.

**Google Fonts loading:** Conditional comment correctly excludes Outlook from the `<link>` tag:
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
Fallback stack `Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif` is well-formed.
