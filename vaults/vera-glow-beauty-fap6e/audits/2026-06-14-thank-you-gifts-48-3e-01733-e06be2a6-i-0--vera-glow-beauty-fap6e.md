# 🍎 Thank you gifts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen. I'm at sixteen Lush emails in roughly a month, and I want to give this one a fair read because the concept isn't wrong — mid-June is legitimately thank-you season. Graduation gifts, teacher appreciation, end-of-year "you're great" gestures. But "🍎 Thank you gifts" with a green apple arrives two days after a Father's Day last-chance send and asks me to browse a curated roundup of six products with no prices, no urgency, and no clear reason this needed to exist *today* as opposed to any random Tuesday.
- What actually stopped my thumb was the Lush x Sanrio section buried at the bottom. A Sanrio reunion collab on bath products is a genuinely smart millennial-nostalgia play — that's a whole editorial email hiding at the bottom of a generic gift catalog. That's the email Lush should have sent.
- I'm clicking nothing in the main body. I might screenshot the Sanrio section and send it to two friends. But "Shop Now" on a gift guide with zero pricing? That's a hard pass.

## What's working

- **Lush x Sanrio collab is a real thumb-stopper.** "Soak Up Our Sweetest Reunion Yet" — for a 29-year-old with residual Cinnamoroll feelings, this hits. It's the most differentiated content Lush has sent since the Karma fragrance education email on June 8.
- **Hero layout is clean and hierarchy holds.** "Gifts They'll Love" reads fast, the grid of products follows logically, nothing is fighting for attention at the top.
- **App offer banner is present.** "Shop up to 25% off via the app" is there — tiny, but it's a concrete incentive and more than the last few sends offered upfront.
- **Seasonal relevance is real.** A thank-you gift guide in mid-June maps to actual life: graduations, end-of-school, teacher gifts. That's smarter calendar work than some of the random May sends.
- **No render issues.** Bright, saturated Lush color story renders clean throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no mystery blank boxes.

## What's weak

- **No pricing anywhere in the product grid.** I am always doing the math. Super Star, Blissful, Garden Party — cute names, zero dollar signs. I can't mentally complete a purchase without at least a starting price point.
- **Apple emoji misdirects.** 🍎 reads as "teacher appreciation" specifically, narrowing the gift angle before the subject even finishes. If the guide is broader than that, the emoji is working against you.
- **The Sanrio collab is buried below the fold.** Your best content is playing second string to a six-product catalog grid. Glossier would have led with the collab — full hero, single focus.
- **Sixteen sends.** I know Lush can't see my count, but after the genuinely strong ingredient-education send on June 8 (which earned a 9 from me), these more catalog-style emails feel like the brand is coasting. ILIA sent four HAIM collab emails in five days and each one built on the last — this feels like Lush is just filling the calendar.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the Sanrio collab to the hero.** If you have a millennial-nostalgia bath product reunion, that IS the gift story — build the whole email around it. The generic gift guide competes with every other brand's June guide; Lush x Sanrio is actually unique.
- 2. **Show prices — even "from $X."** Charlotte Tilbury pulled off a luxury gift guide recently by anchoring the browse with clear price entry points. Lush's price range is competitive; hiding it is a missed conversion.
- 3. **Give the Sanrio section a web CTA, not just the app.** "Tap For The App" gates your most compelling content behind an install. Add a parallel "Shop the Collab" link for people who won't download an app to browse a gift.
- 4. **Kill the apple emoji or commit to the teacher framing.** Pick a lane — if this is a teacher appreciation gift guide, say so and own the seasonality. If it's broader, drop the emoji entirely.
