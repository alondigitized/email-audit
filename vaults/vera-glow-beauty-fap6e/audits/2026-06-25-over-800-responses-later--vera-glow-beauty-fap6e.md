# Over 800+ responses later …
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one. I'm at twenty-one Lush emails this month and I want to put that number right in front of the subject line before I say anything else — because "Over 800+ responses later …" is doing a lot of work as the hook for a product launch email and I genuinely had a moment where I thought, *is this a company-wide announcement? A PR apology? What responses?* Opened anyway because the ellipsis has a "we're about to reveal something" energy that I'm apparently still susceptible to even at send #21.
- What's inside is actually good news: limited-edition shower gummies, fan-favorite scents sourced from community votes, bright colorful product shots that look like candy I want to eat. The "Get a Wiggle On" hero copy is fun and very Lush. The concept — community voted on the scents, here are the results — is a genuinely smart use of the 800+ number because it makes the product feel earned. But the subject buries the reveal entirely. If I'm already in inbox fatigue from twenty of these, "800+ responses later" reads like a podcast episode title, not a product drop.
- I'd mark this as read after opening, maybe forward the shower gummies info to one friend who's been asking about Lush body care. I would not click today. I'd let it sit and come back if the limited-edition scarcity angle actually kicks in.

## What's working

- **Community-sourcing angle is real social proof.** "800 responses" inside the email, plus the "Choices, Choices" framing, signals these scents were actually validated — that's more compelling than Lush just saying "we think you'll love this."
- **Hero photography earns attention.** The colorful gummy shapes — red/coral, teal-green, leafy green — pop immediately against the white background. They look fun and genuinely different from the bath bomb-and-soap aesthetic I've seen in the last twenty sends.
- **Gary Shears product inventor quote** adds texture. It's specific ("firmer than jelly, bouncier than soap") and grounds a novelty product in someone's actual voice. I trust that more than anonymous copy.
- **Lush Club loyalty block is present.** Points math is visible. I see it. I appreciate it even at the bottom of the scroll.
- **"$5 off with the Lush app" banner at the top** — conditional but it's there, which is more than the June 24 aloe email offered.

## What's weak

- **The subject completely obscures the product.** "Over 800+ responses later …" could be feedback data, a policy update, or a sale reveal. If I don't open (and after 21 emails this month, that's a real risk), I'll never know shower gummies exist.
- **"Shop Drops" CTA is vague.** What drops? How many? Where do I land? Compare to e.l.f.'s "NEW ☁️ So Blurreal Lip & Cheek Whip" from June 25 — that email told me exactly what I was buying in the subject. Lush is making me work for it at every level.
- **The app requirement for the $5 off creates friction.** I already have the Lush app but the banner feels like a conditional loyalty tax, not a welcome-back gift.
- **No urgency on the limited edition claim.** "Limited-edition" appears in body copy but there's no end date, no quantity signal, nothing that makes me act today vs. scrolling past and forgetting this existed by Friday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the product in the subject.** The community reveal angle is clever — lean into it. Something like: "You voted. Shower Gummies are here." earns the callback to the 800+ responses AND tells me what I'm clicking into.
- 2. **Add a real deadline or scarcity signal.** "Limited edition" means nothing without a frame. "Only while supplies last" or "dropping for 2 weeks only" gives me a reason to act now instead of bookmarking and forgetting.
- 3. **Make the CTA more specific.** "Shop Shower Gummies" beats "Shop Drops" every time. Or name a scent — something like "Shop: 5 Fan-Voted Scents" gives me a taste of what I'm walking into.
- 4. **Either pull the $5 offer into the hero or cut it.** A banner-only conditional offer competes with the launch story without supporting it. Pick one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You voted. Shower Gummies are officially here.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `5 limited-edition scents you picked 🍬`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fan-favorite scents, jelly-to-gummy texture, dropping now — grab them before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `800+ of you chose the scents. Here's your reward.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking domain:** All links route through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` using long encoded redirect URLs (e.g. `https://clicks.hello.us.lush.com/f/a/oEuORMJfR34k68fVdow6IQ~~/...`).

- **UTM parameters unverifiable:** The redirect URLs are opaque base64-encoded blobs. Final destination URLs and any UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from source alone. Decoding one sample redirect is required to verify campaign tracking is intact.
- **No bare/untracked links detected** in the visible portion — all hrefs pass through the click domain. Confirm this holds for the unsubscribe and social links in the footer (commonly missed).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` renders as a blank tab title. Some email clients (Outlook on Windows, Yahoo web) surface the `<title>` value in the UI or as the notification text. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.
- **Preheader duplication:** Two consecutive `display:none` preheader divs are present — one with the visible preview text ("Something bouncy was born") and one containing Unicode spacer characters (`͏ ‌ ͏ ‌ …`). The spacer block is a deliberate padding technique to suppress body text from leaking into the preview, which is fine, but confirm the spacer div comes *after* the text div (correct order is visible here — text first, spacers second). Swap would suppress the intended preview.
- **Fixed 500px container:** `width:500px` on the row-content table is narrower than the 600px industry standard. This is a deliberate Lush choice but may clip slightly in some Outlook preview panes. No breakage, just a flag.
- **`lang="en"` present** on `<html>` — correct.
- **`role="presentation"` on all tables** — correct.
- **Alt text:** Cannot confirm from truncated source. Images must have `alt=""` (decorative) or descriptive alt text for accessibility and images-off rendering.

### 5. Compliance

- **Physical mailing address:** Not present in the visible HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. Confirm it appears in the footer (outside the truncated range).
- **Unsubscribe mechanism:** Not visible in truncated source. CAN-SPAM and CASL require a functional unsubscribe link. Confirm the footer contains one and that it links through the click-tracker correctly (tracked unsubscribe clicks can interfere with list-unsubscribe processing if misconfigured).
- **List-Unsubscribe header:** Cannot verify from HTML alone — must be confirmed at the SMTP/header level. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required for bulk senders under Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 sender requirements. Verify this header is present on the raw message.
- **Sending domain:** `hello@hello.us.lush.com` — subdomain of `lush.com`. Confirm SPF, DKIM (signed on `hello.us.lush.com`), and DMARC (`_dmarc.lush.com`) are all passing. A `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` DMARC policy on the parent domain with a misconfigured subdomain alignment is a common deliverability failure point.
