# “It felt like a godsend.” —Este Haim
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's where I'm counting with ILIA in June, and yet — I have to be honest — Este Haim's "godsend" quote as a subject line got my thumb to pause. After the HAIM x ILIA collab drop on June 9 introduced the partnership with energy and launch clarity, this follow-up reframes the whole campaign through Este's personal skin story, and that's a genuinely smart pivot. It moves the needle from "limited edition product" to "this is what it did for her skin," which is exactly how I decide whether to restock a tint.
- The email itself is a significant scroll. Hero glow-skin model at the top, the "skin tint that makes your skin better" anchor line, and then a full clinical breakdown — barrier-boosting hydration, sensitive skin proof, daily stressor defense — before finally landing on the big Este pull-quote in that blocky treatment. The "Milk This Gift" section at the bottom reads like a promo or GWP, but I had to scroll past five content sections to find it, and the actual offer terms still aren't spelled out clearly. At that point in the scroll, my click intent has either formed or it hasn't.
- Would I click? Yes — I'm still in the active purchase window after the June 9 launch and the clinical ingredient list speaks directly to how I evaluate a skin tint. Would I forward it? Only to my friend who's been debating whether to drop the Skin Tint or just go back to her Drunk Elephant combo — because Este Haim's "godsend" carry real weight in our friend group, and the sensitive-skin claim closes one of her last objections.

## What's working

- **The Este Haim quote as the emotional anchor is earned.** After the June 6 "*poof* the results are in" consumer-study email and the June 7 "Meet the Soft Focus family" roundup, leading with a celebrity personal testimony adds a layer of social proof the clinical data can't replicate alone.
- **The ingredient/claim bullet list is exactly my pre-cart checklist.** Non-comedogenic, barrier-boosting, dermatologist tested, fragrance-free, cruelty-free — this is the exact five-point mental checklist I run before adding a skin tint. Seeing it assembled in one tight column removes friction from my decision.
- **ILIA's warm editorial aesthetic is perfectly dialed.** The glow-skin model photography at the hero feels aspirational without being untouchable — it's the kind of "your skin but better" promise that differentiates ILIA from Charlotte Tilbury's more theatrical "glam transformation" messaging.
- **"Skin tint that makes your skin better" holds up as a positioning line.** Against a full Sephora display case where every brand claims radiance or coverage, the "actually improves your skin" angle is a genuine differentiator — and this send reinforces it without feeling like a repeat.

## What's weak

- **The offer is completely buried.** "Milk This Gift" shows up after five full content sections — by then I've already decided whether I care. Drunk Elephant's June 10 "Easiest glow ever" send led with a product benefit AND a clear click path in the first screen. ILIA made me work for whatever the deal actually is.
- **No loyalty angle.** I'm Sephora Rouge and the Skin Tint lives at Sephora, but there's zero mention of points, double-dip events, or Rouge-exclusive access. The Sephora Beauty Insider "STARTS TODAY: up to 50% off" send earlier this month at least reminded me why buying through that specific channel matters. ILIA doesn't connect those dots at all.
- **The email re-litigates ground I've already covered twice.** The June 6 consumer-study email and the June 7 family roundup both walked through the clinical proof in detail. This email covers much of the same territory — barrier function, daily defense — before getting to the Este differentiator that actually earns the send.
- **"Milk This Gift" is too clever for its own good.** I genuinely couldn't tell on first pass whether it was a GWP, a collab bundle, or a themed gift set. The wordplay is cute in a HAIM-band-name riff kind of way, but it creates confusion where the offer should create urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the offer above the clinical scroll.** Whatever "Milk This Gift" resolves to — GWP, bundle, free shipping — put a one-line teaser directly under the hero: *"Free [gift] with Skin Tint purchase — scroll to shop."* Don't make me hunt for the reason to click.
- 2. **Let Este's testimonial do the credentialing, and trim the clinical sections.** After three back-to-back clinical emails, this send doesn't need to re-prove barrier function. Trust that subscribers remember the June 6 "results are in" email and let Este's voice carry more of the page.
- 3. **Add a Sephora-specific loyalty CTA.** Even a footer line — *"Earn 4× points at Sephora through [date]"* — closes the loop for Rouge and VIB members who are deciding where to buy. Ulta would never let a launch email go out without a points mention; ILIA should borrow that habit.
- 4. **Clarify "Milk This Gift" with specifics.** Name the gift or the savings — "free deluxe mini with $50 purchase" or "buy Skin Tint + Finishing Powder, save 15%" — so the final CTA has purchase intent behind it when I arrive.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Several email clients (Yahoo, AOL, some Outlook versions) surface the `<title>` value as a tooltip or fallback subject. An empty title is a missed fallback and may trigger accessibility warnings in screen reader testing.

**External `@import` CSS will be stripped**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (desktop), and Samsung Mail strip `@import` rules entirely. `Montserrat` will silently fall back to `Helvetica`/`sans-serif`. This is expected behavior for email, but the fallback stack must be visually acceptable — confirm it renders acceptably without the webfont.

**Google Fonts v1 API endpoint**
```
https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap
```
The v1 endpoint is deprecated in favor of `css2`. It still resolves, but Google has signaled eventual removal. Low urgency but worth updating to `css2?family=Montserrat:wght@400;700&display=swap`.

**h4 excluded from Montserrat font stack**
```css
h4 { font-family: "Helvetica Neue", Arial; ... }   /* no Montserrat */
p, h1, h2, h3 { font-family: "Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif }
```
`h4` elements will always render in Helvetica Neue regardless of webfont load, while all other text elements attempt Montserrat first. Likely unintentional inconsistency.

**All link states styled identically, including hover**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
No hover differentiation exists. This removes the interactive affordance for mouse users in webmail clients that support `:hover` (Outlook.com, Gmail web). Not an error per se, but worth flagging as an accessibility concern for WCAG 2.4.4 (link purpose discernibility).
