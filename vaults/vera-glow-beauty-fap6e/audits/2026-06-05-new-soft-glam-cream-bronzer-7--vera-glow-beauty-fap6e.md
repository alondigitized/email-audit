# NEW! Soft Glam Cream Bronzer, $7 🤎
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. That's my running count since May 15th, and this is the one that actually made me stop scrolling — not because e.l.f. suddenly figured out inbox pacing, but because a $7 cream bronzer with a real shade range is a genuinely smart product pitch. The hero is clean: model, product name, price, button. That's it. If I bracket out the three-week email marathon, this one lands.
- But there's a ceiling, and it's the loyalty math gap. I'm Sephora Rouge — I run points calculations in my head before checkout. e.l.f. has Beauty Squad points, and there is zero mention of them anywhere in this email. Not one "earn X points" nudge, not a tier-member moment, nothing. Ulta would have worked the 21 Days of Beauty logic in here, or at minimum told me the points per dollar. That omission stings more than it should.
- I'd click. I wouldn't forward — there's nothing in here with the group-chat energy that the May 31 "e.l.f.ing sweet" candy send had. This email is solid, functional, and forgettable in the best possible way. Mark read, open new tab, add to cart.

## What's working

- **$7 price in the subject line AND the hero copy** — no click-through required to find the number. "Only $7" closes the hero sentence. That's the right call.
- **Shade range is front and center** — fair through deep/rich, six swatches readable without scrolling. More transparent on inclusivity than Charlotte Tilbury, who makes me hunt through a wall of Pillow Talk variants to confirm my shade even exists.
- **Ingredients callout** — "5% shea butter helps moisturize" and "castor oil helps smooth" in a scannable layout. This is ILIA-adjacent behavior, and I respect it on a mass-price product. It lifts the perceived quality ceiling.
- **Dual model casting** — one deeper-toned model in the hero, one lighter-medium model in the "Cream Team" section. Both are in the bronzer's demographic sweet spot. I see myself and my friends in here.
- **Visual hierarchy doesn't fight itself** — hero leads, shade range follows, ingredients section supports, secondary CTA closes. My eye didn't have to do any work to understand the story.

## What's weak

- **Zero loyalty math anywhere** — e.l.f. has Beauty Squad. I'm in it. Not one mention of points, rewards, or member pricing in the entire email. This is the single biggest miss for someone like me who treats points like a second currency.
- **No urgency signal** — "NEW!" is a freshness flag, not a deadline. Is this a limited shade run? A 48-hour launch window? Low stock warning? Nothing. I'll bookmark it and come back in two weeks, which means I might not come back at all.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing the bare minimum** — Coming off the "e.l.f.ing sweet" send, where the wordplay was actually fun, landing on two generic "SHOP NOW" buttons feels like a creative gear-shift in the wrong direction. "GET THE $7 BRONZER" ties the action directly to the price anchor they already established.
- **No social proof whatsoever** — No star rating, no "TikTok made me try it," no Allure mention, nothing. Drunk Elephant leans hard on clinical credibility signals in their ingredient emails. This email just asserts the product is good and expects me to agree. On a new, unfamiliar SKU, that's asking a lot.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the loyalty nudge** — Add "Earn 7 Beauty Squad points" directly under the price in the hero. Keeps the $7 number front-of-mind as both a price AND an earning opportunity. Highest ROI change in this email.
- 2. **Rewrite both CTAs to match the price anchor** — "GET IT FOR $7" or "SHOP THE BRONZER" over generic "SHOP NOW." Makes the decision feel concrete, not browse-y.
- 3. **Inject a social proof signal** — One TikTok view count, one earned media badge, one star rating from Beauty Squad reviews. Even one line: "Shoppers are calling it their new everyday bronze." Glossier uses community voice as credibility glue — e.l.f. has the audience to do the same.
- 4. **Create urgency without faking it** — "New launch: first 48 hours free shipping on any order" or "Limited shades — shade [X] already trending" would give me a reason to click today instead of archiving this for consideration.

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics "Soft Glam Cream Bronzer" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze (evident from `braze-images.com` CDN and `links.elfcosmetics.com` click-tracking format) using a drag-and-drop builder (likely Beefree/BEE). A nested duplicate table structure in the first row is a builder artifact and the primary structural concern.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Nested `nl-container` produces duplicate click wrapper.**
`block-1` (`html_block`) contains a full second `nl-container` → `row-1` → `image_block` chain. The logo image anchor appears once but sits inside two stacked table layouts — a builder export artifact. Low rendering risk but inflates DOM size and can confuse link-auditing tools.

**`tabindex="-1"` on logo CTA link.**
```html
<a href="https://links.elfcosmetics.com/..." tabindex="-1">
```
This explicitly removes the logo link from keyboard tab order. Any subscriber navigating by keyboard cannot reach this link. Remove the attribute entirely.

**UTM parameters not observable in click-tracking URL.**
The Braze redirect URL (`links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/YuAF...`) does not surface UTM params in the raw `href`. They may be appended server-side post-redirect, but this cannot be confirmed from source alone — see §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts `<link>` tag stripped by Gmail.**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail and several other clients strip `<link>` tags from `<head>`. The declared fallback stack (`Open Sans, Arial, Sans-serif`) is correct and will activate, but the intent to render Open Sans will silently fail for a large share of recipients.

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some accessibility tooling read `<title>` as the document label. Populating it (e.g., `<title>e.l.f. — Soft Glam Cream Bronzer, $7</title>`) costs nothing and improves screen-reader context.

**Global link decoration suppression.**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This rule overrides all link styling in Gmail's `#MessageViewBody` scope, including any footer unsubscribe links. If those links rely on underline or color differentiation for recognizability, this rule strips it. Scope this rule more narrowly or add explicit footer-link overrides.

**Three consecutive empty `html_block` elements (blocks 2–4).**
All three render as empty `<div>` wrappers:
```html
<div style="font-family:Open Sans,Arial,Sans-serif;text-align:center" align="center"> </div>
```
These are dead spacer blocks from the builder. They add no layout value here — remove or replace with explicit `padding` on adjacent cells.

**Logo `alt` text is minimal.**
`alt="e.l.f."` passes the "not blank" bar but provides no context about the promotional image. A descriptive alt (e.g., `alt="e.l.f. Cosmetics – Soft Glam Cream Bronzer"`) better serves image-off rendering.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Footer not present in provided source (truncated).**
Physical mailing address and one-click unsubscribe are required by CAN-SPAM. These are absent from the truncated HTML. Verification against the full source is required — confirm both are present in the rendered footer before sending.

**Sending subdomain authentication.**
The `From` header uses `m.elfcosmetics.com`. Confirm SPF and DKIM records cover this subdomain specifically (not just the apex `elfcosmetics.com`). A DMARC record at `elfcosmetics.com` with `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` and `sp` alignment should cover the subdomain — verify `sp` is not set to `none`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

UTM parameters are not present in the raw `href` of the logo CTA. Braze can append them via redirect rules, but this should be confirmed:
- Check the Braze campaign link settings for UTM appending configuration.
- Manually resolve `links.elfcosmetics.com/f/a/YuAF...` and inspect the final destination URL for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content`.
- If absent, the product launch traffic will appear as direct/none in analytics rather than email, making ROI attribution unreliable.
