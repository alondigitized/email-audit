# Repair and replenish with It's a 10
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I recognize this one immediately — It's a 10 Miracle line, which I've used and actually like. The hero is clean, the "Repair your summer hair" angle is timely, and the flash sale at the top (up to $20 off) is a real number attached to a real threshold. So the bones are here.
- But here's my immediate math problem: It's a 10 is also at Ulta. If I spend $76 at Ulta on this exact product line, I'm earning Diamond points toward my next redemption tier — at Sally, I'm just spending $76 and getting $20 back. That's not a bad deal, but for a loyal Ulta Diamond who is always running the math, Sally has to beat Ulta's points math *plus* offer something I can't get there. This email doesn't make that case.
- And look — this is the fifth Sally email in eight days. July 16, 17, 20, 22, and now today. I'm fatigued. I'd mark this read, probably not click, and I'd definitely not forward it. Glossier's GWP reminder earlier this week made me feel like I was about to lose something; this makes me feel like Sally is just trying to move product.

## What's working

- The It's a 10 brand spotlight is a real step up from the generic "summer must-haves" loop — this is specific, and the Miracle line is recognizable at a glance.
- The flash sale numbers ($10 off $50 / $20 off $76) are upfront in the header, not buried — I see the deal before I even hit the hero.
- The "Repair your summer hair" copy is seasonally relevant and actually speaks to a real problem (heat damage) rather than just vibes.
- The product grid is organized and each item has its own "Shop now" link — that's better UX than the last ion Luxe email, which mostly pointed to a category page.
- The young, wavy-haired model matches the aesthetic I actually aspire to — this email is at least aimed at someone in my demographic.

## What's weak

- Zero loyalty messaging — no Sally Prestige rewards angle, no "earn X points on this purchase." Compare this to Sephora's emails where the Beauty Insider tier is woven into literally every send.
- The product grid has six SKUs but zero callouts for bestsellers, ratings, or "as seen on TikTok" hooks — just product names and a generic "Shop now." e.l.f. SKIN's "Glitz with benefits" email this week showed me social proof signals right in the product tile.
- "Shop now" appears seven times in this email. Seven. That's not CTAs — that's wallpaper.
- The Afterpay/buy-now-pay-later banner ("3 interest-free payments, available for orders above $25") at the bottom feels like an afterthought and slightly off-brand for a prestige hair care send — it pulls the email downmarket.
- The cadence has broken me. This is Sally's fifth email in eight days. The July 22nd L'Oreal spotlight was genuinely the most interesting thing they've sent in weeks, and they immediately followed it with this. I needed a two-day break.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a loyalty/points callout.** Even one line — "Earn Beauty Club points on every dollar" — anchors the purchase reason for subscribers who care about loyalty math. Right now there's zero reward for buying at Sally vs. Ulta.
- 2. **Replace generic "Shop now" tiles with a hero product.** Pick one It's a 10 product — probably the Miracle Leave-In since it's the hero SKU — and give it a featured treatment with a real claim ("over 100K sold," "45-second ritual"). Six tiles with identical CTAs flatten the hierarchy.
- 3. **Slow the cadence or differentiate harder.** If Sally is going to send five emails in eight days, each one needs a hook the prior email didn't have. The L'Oreal spotlight two days ago was earned. This one feels like a calendar fill.
- 4. **Kill the BNPL banner or move it to a contextual placement.** It undercuts the "premium hair care" positioning of It's a 10.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `It's a 10 for summer damage — flash sale inside`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `$20 off when you repair with It's a 10`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `The Miracle line is on flash sale — ends July 29`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to $20 off It's a 10 Miracle shampoo, mask, leave-in + more`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on `img` elements:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space` is a text property; it has no effect on `<img>` elements and signals a copy-paste error. No rendering breakage, but it's dead/incorrect CSS that should be removed.

**Issue — `Founders Grotesk` not loaded:**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the `<style>` block. Every link in the email silently falls back to Helvetica. If this font is intended to render, it must be declared (or loaded via a hosted stylesheet, which most email clients will block anyway).

**Issue — Open Sans loaded at version v17:**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Current Open Sans on Google Fonts is v40+. v17 URLs still resolve but are served from a stale CDN path. Low risk of breakage today, but these URLs are not guaranteed permanent.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`:**
Industry standard for email responsive breakpoint is 600px. A 499px cutoff means devices between 500–599px wide receive desktop layout at mobile viewport width, causing horizontal overflow on common screen sizes.
