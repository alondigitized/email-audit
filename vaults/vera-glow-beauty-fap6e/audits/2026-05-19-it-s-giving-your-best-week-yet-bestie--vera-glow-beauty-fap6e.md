# It’s giving your best week yet, Bestie 💗
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Seven e.l.f. emails in five days — I've been keeping count — and this one opens with the exact same pink setting spray that starred in yesterday's launch send, the one I genuinely loved. That's the tell. When you recycle yesterday's hero creative into today's "weekly highlight reel," you've already lost me before I get to the first product tile. The "Highlight Reel" framing is doing a lot of diplomatic work to cover the fact that there's no actual hero moment here: no featured launch, no offer in the spotlight, just every product they carry arranged in a grid.
- The "Bestie" in the subject could feel warm if there were two or three days of breathing room between sends. Right now it reads like e.l.f. is manufacturing intimacy on the cadence of a Domino's push notification. The welcome code at the very top is the most useful thing in this email and it's buried in a thin all-caps banner competing with the photography.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. No forward, no click — unless I was actively hunting that welcome code, in which case I'd grab it and close the tab.

## What's working

- Pink sparkly aesthetic is fully cohesive with e.l.f.'s current visual identity — palette matches the setting spray send and the blush send before it.
- "SHOP BEST SELLERS" / "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" are clean, unambiguous CTAs — verb plus noun, no "Discover" vagueness.
- The perks footer (free shipping at $35, free gifts, Afterpay) is scannable and reminds me why I like buying e.l.f. in the first place.
- Email renders perfectly — single-column, no broken images, no layout drift.
- Beauty Squad app section is a smart cross-channel nudge that doesn't feel forced.

## What's weak

- The setting spray is the hero image — again. I literally got its launch email yesterday scored a 10, and now it's pulling anchor duty in a "weekly reel." That creative burn is wasteful and makes this feel like a resend.
- Zero prices anywhere in the email. e.l.f.'s entire value proposition IS the price point. "Soft Glam Cream Blush, $7" got me to open and click on May 17. Stripping the prices out of the grid turns their biggest strength into a generic prestige-adjacency play — and they are not a prestige brand.
- "YOUR NEW OBSESSIONS" into "HOT AS E.L.F. THIS WEEK" into "NEW / EYES / LIPS / FACE / SKINCARE" is homepage navigation dressed up as editorial. That category-nav section at the bottom is literally just their site menu in pink accordion boxes.
- Subject line has no hook. Charlotte Tilbury gave me "Unlock Mini Magic Gifts" — specific noun, specific action. Drunk Elephant gave me "Let's get something clear" — wordplay with a product wink. "It's giving your best week yet" is TikTok filler masquerading as a subject line.
- Seventh email in five days is too many. The cadence is the silent conversion killer here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull the setting spray out of the hero.** Yesterday was its moment. Promote one of the new product tiles to hero status with a clear price callout — the lip product or the primer tile would both work.
- 2. **Put prices on the top two product tiles minimum.** "$7 blush. $12 lip set." Those specific numbers are what differentiate e.l.f. from every mid-tier brand in my inbox this week. ILIA names the specific finish type; Glossier calls out the specific use case. e.l.f. should be calling out the specific dollar amount.
- 3. **Make the welcome code the hero** — if this is a welcome-series touchpoint, center it. Big type, clear expiration date, a single CTA button. Right now the code string looks like lorem ipsum in a banner.
- 4. **Cut the category-nav accordion.** Replace it with one curated "editor's pick" product with a face-cut image — something that earns the word "obsession."
- 5. **Throttle. The. Cadence.** Two-day silence before this email and it would have felt fresh. Right now it's the seventh send in five days and it shows.

- **Subject Alt B:** `6 picks to shop before Sunday 💗`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your new obsessions — with prices you'll actually love`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Trending this week + your welcome code inside`

## Technical Audit — e.l.f. Cosmetics "It's giving your best week yet, Bestie 💗"

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze with standard MSO/VML compatibility scaffolding. Two notable code defects are present: an unresolved font-family template variable and a duplicate/inconsistent Google Fonts load. No unsubscribe block is visible in the supplied truncation — compliance elements could not be fully verified.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**All CTAs route through `links.elfcosmetics.com`** (Braze click-tracking redirects). No issues with the redirect mechanism itself.

**UTM parameters**: The final destination URL is encoded inside the Braze redirect token and is not readable from the HTML source alone. Cannot confirm whether UTM params survive the redirect chain without resolving the link. Flag for QA to trace one live click to verify `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present on the landing page.

**Image asset URL**: Logo is served from `braze-images.com/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6532bc91543698004cb67f6e/original.png?1697823889`. The numeric suffix is a Unix timestamp (Oct 2023) — this is a cache-buster, not a bug, but the asset should be confirmed still live before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` in `<head>`. Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface this as the window/tab label. Should be populated with the email subject or brand name.

**Unresolved font-family variable** — inline style on outer wrapper div reads:
```
font-family:'',Verdana,Sans-serif
```
The first value is an empty string. This is a template artifact where a custom font variable was cleared or never resolved. Fallback to Verdana will occur in all clients. Low rendering impact, but indicates a template authoring gap.

**Duplicate and inconsistent Google Fonts load**:
- `<head>` loads: `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Jost` (legacy API, no weight range)
- A `<style>` block nested inside a `<div>` (invalid HTML placement) loads: `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Jost:ital,wght@0,100..900;1,100..900&display=swap` (full variable font spec)

`<style>` outside `<head>` is invalid per spec; behavior is client-dependent. The two loads request different font variants, creating a race condition on which weight spec wins. Consolidate to a single `<link>` in `<head>` using the `css2` spec.

**`tabindex="-1"` on the header logo link** — removes the branded header from keyboard tab order. Not critical, but reduces keyboard accessibility.

**`#MessageViewBody a { text-decoration: none }`** — suppresses underlines on all `<a>` tags globally in Gmail. Inline links in body copy (if any exist in the untruncated HTML) will lose their underline affordance, which is an accessibility concern for low-vision users.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No unresolved Braze Liquid tags** visible in the supplied HTML. Preheader ("Discover your new e.l.f.ssentials!") and subject-line salutation ("Bestie") are hardcoded — no first-name personalization present. This is a strategy choice, not a code defect, but noted for completeness.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Unsubscribe link and physical address**: Not present in the truncated source. Cannot confirm compliance. Must verify the untruncated footer contains:
- One-click unsubscribe or clearly labeled opt-out link
- Sender's physical mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5)

**Authentication headers**: Not derivable from HTML source. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail must be verified via raw message headers. Sending domain is `m.elfcosmetics.com` — confirm DKIM is signed for this subdomain, not just `elfcosmetics.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM verification is blocked by Braze's encoded redirect URLs (see §2). QA action required: resolve at least one CTA link and confirm:
- `utm_source=email` (or equivalent)
- `utm_medium=email`
- `utm_campaign` matches this deployment's send ID or campaign name

Landing pages should not require login to view promoted products — if any linked page gate-checks session state before showing content, bounce rate will spike.
