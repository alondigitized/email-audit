# A-peeling and nourishing 🍌
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. Lush just sent me their eleventh email in under three weeks, and the pun in this subject line genuinely made me smirk before I opened it — "A-peeling" is the kind of wordplay I will always give a second for. Once inside, the yellow is a whole mood: the hero is bold, the product grid is cohesive, and "Peel the Benefits" over a cascade of bananas lands exactly like a Lush email should. Credit where it's due: this is a well-executed thematic edit that actually holds together across hair, body, and bath.
- But here's my problem. I am coming off the "Bath bombs in the sock drawer" email, the grooming-dads email, the flowers email, the scent-mood email, and roughly six I've already forgotten. After that cadence, the bar is no longer "is the content good?" — it's "did you give me a reason to buy today?" There is no offer in this email. No points double-up. No "Sephora Beauty Insider event is coming — stock up" equivalent. Nothing to trigger a cart. If the May 31st scent email was the peak of this run, this one is coasting on fumes and banana emoji.
- I'd mark this as read. I might forward the "Banoffee Pie" product shot to a friend as a funny food-looking beauty thing. I would not click through to buy.

## What's working

- **"Peel the Benefits" hero headline** is the right energy — instantly communicates the theme, the pun is earned, and the all-yellow layout backs it up without feeling random.
- **Thematic cohesion across the product grid** — Banana shampoo bar, Sympathy For The Skin, Banoffee Pie, King of Skin — this is a curated ingredient story, not a "stuff we wanted to move" dump. Better curation than e.l.f.'s recent Halo Glow roundup, which felt like they threw a viral moment at a wall.
- **"Shop Banana" CTA copy is specific** — verb + noun, doesn't say "Discover" or "Explore." Glossier's "Choose your own fragrance adventure" CTA from last week was more fun, but this one at least tells me what I'm getting into.
- **"A SOLID CHOICE" closing section** is chef's kiss for Lush brand voice — doubles as a pun on their solid product format and lands perfectly as a secondary beat without feeling tacked on.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — after some of the multi-image grid emails I've seen recently (Charlotte Tilbury's had spacing issues), this one loads cleanly.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer anywhere.** Ulta just landed a Charlotte Tilbury GWP email in my inbox the same week — a specific gift, specific threshold, clear redemption path. Lush gave me a color palette and a pun. Those are not the same thing.
- **Two CTAs in the hero ("Shop Banana" AND "Learn More") split the click** — if I'm interested, I'm going to "Shop Banana." "Learn More" is a hesitation button that only exists because someone wasn't confident the hero copy did enough heavy lifting.
- **No loyalty hook whatsoever** — I am a customer who tracks points. The "Stop and smell the flowers" email on May 27 had the same problem. Not a single mention of Lush loyalty rewards, points multipliers, or member perks. Sephora would never miss that CTA on a product-range email.
- **Cadence is doing real damage now.** Email eleven in three weeks means I'm reading this with reviewer eyes instead of shopper eyes. The May 31st scent pivot was interesting enough to re-engage me; this one doesn't build on that momentum.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete offer to the banana range** — even a "free banana sample with $40 purchase" or "double points on banana products this week" would give me a reason to click and buy, not just browse. The product curation is already done; the conversion layer is just missing.
- 2. **Kill "Learn More" — it's a confidence hole.** Make the hero copy do the work, or expand the headline, then let "Shop Banana" be the sole CTA above the fold.
- 3. **Surface a single hero product before the grid.** Lead with the banana shampoo bar or Sympathy For The Skin as *the* product of this email, then let the grid be discovery. Right now it reads as a range ad, not a product moment. Sally Beauty's "Summer Hair Survival Kit" email nailed this — there was a clear entry point before the product list.
- 4. **Preview text needs to be actual copy** — from the top of the rendered email, the navigation links ("Wishlist | Stores | Bestsellers") are the first readable text after the logo, which is exactly what leaks into inbox preview. Write a real line.
- 5. **Consider a 3–4 day email rest.** Not a design suggestion but a business one: the May 31st scent email earned an 8 from me because the content shift felt intentional. This banana email would hit harder if I hadn't already been numbed by ten prior sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — empty `<title>` tag:**
```html

```
Several clients (Samsung Mail, some webmail views) surface the `<title>` in the tab or window header. Screen readers also use it. Should be populated with the email subject or a descriptive string.

**Minor — preheader spacer uses raw Unicode zero-width characters:**
```html

```
Characters U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner) and U+200C (ZWNJ) are used to pad the preview snippet. This is an accepted pattern, but some aggressive spam filters penalize high ratios of non-printable characters. Acceptable risk if deliverability metrics are healthy; worth monitoring open rates against a baseline.

**Responsive breakpoint at `max-width: 520px`** — below the de facto 600px standard. Devices with screen widths between 521–599px get desktop layout on a smaller viewport. Not a critical bug, but check rendering on 6-inch Android devices.

**Sending domain concern:**
`hello@hello.us.lush.com` — the `From` domain is `hello.us.lush.com`. For DMARC `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` to pass, the ESP's sending infrastructure must be authorized in the SPF/DKIM records for `hello.us.lush.com` (not just `lush.com`). Confirm:
