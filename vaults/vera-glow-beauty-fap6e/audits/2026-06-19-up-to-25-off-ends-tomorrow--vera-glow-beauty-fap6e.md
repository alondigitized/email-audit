# Up to 25% off ends tomorrow
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Day twelve. I'm counting because I've been watching this Glossier sale stack up in my inbox since June 15, and I need to be honest that context colors everything about how I read this email. That said — "All good things must come to an end" is the first hero copy in this entire run that actually made me feel something other than promotional fatigue. It's self-aware in a way that works. And "ends tomorrow" is the one phrase that finally made me open my cart tab and do the math.
- The email itself is doing what Glossier does well: clean white space, editorial product grid, no visual clutter. What surprised me was the subscription pitch at the bottom — "Start a subscription and lock in 25% off" — because that's genuinely better math than the one-time sale, and it's buried where most people won't reach it. That's the most interesting offer in the email and it's an afterthought in the layout.
- I'd click through to my own cart, not forward this to a friend. The June 17 "Friends don't let friends pay full price" send was the forwardable one — it had social energy. This is a countdown clock. Useful, but not shareable.

## What's working

- "All good things must come to an end" hero copy is the first line in twelve sends that reads like a human wrote it, not a sale scheduler
- "Ends tomorrow" does real urgency work without asterisk soup or buried exclusion language — the deal reads clean
- Product grid is specific — actual SKUs with visible price tags, not a mood board pointing at the homepage
- The subscription pitch, buried or not, is genuinely the best loyalty math in this email: 25% forever versus 25% once is a different offer, and I noticed it
- Layout is clean with zero render issues — Charlotte Tilbury's recent sends have been a mess of stacked banners; Glossier still holds the clean-execution standard

## What's weak

- Twelve emails deep and I'm still getting a "Shop now" CTA — ILIA's "This lip color lasts. This gift won't." send had specific CTA language tied to the actual product; Glossier at $22 price points should clear that bar
- No personalization at any level — not my name, not a "you've opened the last four of these" acknowledgement, not a "your cart" reference; Sephora at least surfaces my Rouge points balance in sale emails to remind me I'm a real customer
- The subscription offer is the most compelling thing in this email and it's below the fold, after the product grid — that's a hierarchy miss for a returning buyer
- Social proof is completely absent — by day twelve of a sale, "3,000 people bought this in the last 48 hours" or a community-picks callout would add real dimension; Drunk Elephant at least gestures at bestseller ranking
- No indication of what inventory looks like — "selling fast" or "limited stock" would add texture to a "last day" email without feeling manufactured

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote the subscription offer to hero position for returning openers** — or at minimum A/B test a version where "subscribe and keep the 25% after the sale ends" is the close argument. One-time sale ends; the subscription retains me. That's the unlock.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA for the close** — "Final hours — shop the 25% off" or "Grab yours before midnight" is specific to the moment. "Shop now" is what every sale email says.
- 3. **Add a cart-abandon hook** — even a generic "still thinking it over?" module above the footer would convert anyone who's opened this multiple times and hasn't pulled the trigger (me).
- 4. **Add one social proof signal per product card** — a "fan favorite" badge or community pick flag in the grid adds trust without redesigning the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today's the last day — 25% off Glossier`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart just got a deadline`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The sale ends tonight. Here's what to grab first.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Or subscribe and keep the 25% after the sale ends.`
