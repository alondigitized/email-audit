# Summer skin staples
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Context first: I already had an ILIA email today. "BFF + SPF" dropped on June 16, and now here's "Summer Skin Staples" — same sender, same day. That's the lens I'm reading this through. And honestly, the email is beautiful. The hero photography, the tight copy ("Sun, heat, travel—don't sweat it"), the three well-chosen summer SKUs — Base Face Milk, Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40, Balmy Tint Lip Balm. ILIA's aesthetic is impeccable and I still trust this brand.
- But this email has no engine. There's a GWP offer — free mini multi-stick with $75+ — squeezed into the tiniest top banner I've ever had to squint at, and the body never mentions a price, a deadline, or a reason to move today instead of next Thursday. Contrast that with Glossier's 9/10 send where every product had a savings number attached and the urgency was baked into the layout. ILIA gives me gorgeous editorial and three "SHOP NOW" buttons that go nowhere specific. As someone doing points math on every cart, I need to know if I can hit $75 with these three items. I have no idea.
- I'd maybe click the Skin Tint SPF 40 — but honestly only because BFF + SPF already primed me for it this morning. This email didn't close the deal; the previous one did. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- "Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40" callout is legitimately specific — "lightweight, dewy coverage + hint of mineral sun protection" is benefit-led copy that earns the space
- "HOT TIP: Store in the fridge for an extra cooling + soothing application" is the kind of insider usage tip that makes me trust the brand knows its own products
- Hero copy "Sun, heat, travel—don't sweat it" is tight, seasonally accurate, and doesn't condescend
- Close-crop of warm-toned skin in the hero feels intentional and on-brand, not stock — matches ILIA's audience
- Email renders clean; no broken images, no overlapping text, the three-product flow is easy to scan

## What's weak

- Second send same day — BFF + SPF already used up my ILIA attention budget for June 16; this arrives with the tank empty
- The GWP is the most compelling thing in this email and it's locked in a banner so small I could see through it — that's a hierarchy failure
- No prices anywhere in the body — I can't do the $75 GWP math without knowing what any of these products cost
- "SHOP NOW" on every product card is interchangeable, tells me nothing about where I'm clicking or what I'm going to find
- The top banner copy ("FREE MINI MULTI-STICK IS AT LAST! 8/ ANY $75+ ORDER*") reads oddly — "IS AT LAST!" and "8/" look like a rendering artifact or garbled character; this is a trust ding if it hits the inbox preview

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the GWP into the hero** — "Free mini multi-stick when you spend $75" belongs as the lead offer under the headline, not in a 10px banner nobody reads
- 2. **Add pricing or an anchor** — even "from $42" or a "spend $75 with these three" total helps me decide; without it the GWP threshold floats in space
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with product-specific CTAs** — "Shop Base Face Milk," "Shop Skin Tint SPF 40" — one sentence each, destination is clear
- 4. **Give the GWP a deadline** — "Offer ends Sunday" is eight words that turn a maybe into an add-to-cart
- 5. **Don't send two emails in one day** — either roll the summer staples picks into BFF + SPF as a secondary module, or save this for June 17

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title> </title>` — whitespace only. Some clients (Outlook Web, Apple Mail) surface this as the window/tab title. Should be a descriptive string or omitted.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` (will silently fail in Gmail)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail, Yahoo, and most webmail clients strip `@import` at parse time. Montserrat will not render for the majority of recipients. The declared fallback chain (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is correct — fallbacks will fire — but this should be noted as expected behavior, not a bug.

**Klaviyo custom font `@import` has the same stripping risk**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same failure mode. Fallback fonts will render instead.

**Links are visually indistinguishable from body text (WCAG 1.4.1)**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link       { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:visited    { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:active     { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:hover      { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Every link state removes underline and uses the same color as body text. WCAG 1.4.1 requires links to be distinguishable from surrounding text by more than color alone when underlines are removed. This fails that criterion. Hover state (`:hover`) also has no differentiation, which is irrelevant in most email clients but indicates the template was not designed with accessibility in mind.
