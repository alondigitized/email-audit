# Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Sephora lands in my inbox with "Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase." — and I'll be honest, after the double "💘JUST YOUR TYPE💘" yesterday and the double "New products have arrived" on the 13th, my thumb was halfway to the archive before I even registered the offer. Cadence fatigue is real with this sender right now.
- But I opened. Because 10% off is actual money, and as a Rouge member stacking this against my Beauty Insider points is always worth doing the math on. Here's where it gets frustrating: the subject promises a 10% off deal, and the email's hero is... a **Free Hair Sample Set**. Those are two different offers. I had to actively locate the 10% off banner at the top because the scroll-stopping visual is the hair sample set entirely. The subject and the hero are not talking to each other, and that dissonance cost them credibility immediately.
- I'd mark it as read, maybe click the 10% redemption if I can confirm it stacks with my existing points, but I wouldn't forward this. The email is doing too many things at once — free sample set, 10% off, Bundle & Save, new 100-point rewards — and as someone who does the points math obsessively, I need clarity, not a menu.

## What's working

- **"Today only" urgency is real.** I clock that immediately — it creates genuine FOMO and is specific enough to feel credible rather than manufactured.
- **The 10% off $100+ threshold is clean math.** As a Rouge member, I know exactly what I'd put in my basket. This offer respects my intelligence.
- **Beauty Insider visibility at the footer** is appreciated — "We Belong to Something Beautiful" with the loyalty tier callout reminds me this is member-exclusive, which matters.
- **New 100-Point Rewards section** is the kind of content I actually want from Sephora. As someone who hoards points for the Sephora Beauty Insider event, new redemption options are genuinely relevant — this is more useful than most of what they've sent this week.
- **No render bugs** — the layout holds, images loaded, text is readable. That's not nothing after seeing Ulta send me an email last week telling me a "Beauty Break" event had already ended.

## What's weak

- **Subject and hero are misaligned.** The subject sells me 10% off; the hero sells me a Free Hair Sample Set. I had to hunt for what I was actually promised. Drunk Elephant's recent "Help your skin. Save the planet." at least led with what the email actually opened on.
- **Four competing offers in one scroll.** 10% off, hair sample set, Bundle & Save, 100-point rewards. Sephora is doing the same thing ILIA did in "That flushed feeling" — too many messages, no clear hierarchy of what they actually want me to do.
- **The hair focus is oddly narrow.** I'm Rouge, I buy across skincare, makeup, fragrance — leading with a hair sample set as the visual hero when the actual offer is a sitewide % off feels like a merchandising decision, not a targeting one.
- **Cadence is officially broken.** This is at minimum the fifth Sephora email in four days. After the two duplicate-subject sends I've seen this week, the "today only" urgency loses credibility. Sally Beauty pulls this too — by send thirty-something, "limited time" feels more like a tic than a signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Align the hero to the subject line.** If 10% off is the lead offer, make that the visual hero — not a competing sample set. The subject line bought the open; let the body fulfill that promise immediately.
- 2. **Pick ONE secondary offer.** Either the hair sample set OR the Bundle & Save section. Not both. Not three. This email is doing too much.
- 3. **Add the redemption code or member-exclusive language above the fold.** As a Rouge member, I want to know if the 10% requires a code, applies at checkout, or is auto-applied. That single line of clarity would move me to cart immediately.
- 4. **Rest the cadence.** Sephora has sent me this email type so many times this week that "today only" barely registers. Glossier keeps better send discipline with launch-specific timing. A less-frequent send would make "today only" feel like something.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Rouge member? 10% off $100+ ends tonight.`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your 10% off window closes today — shop it.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Free hair sample set included. No code needed — discount applied at checkout.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack your points, add the hair set, save 10%. Today only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1` or add `user-scalable=yes`.

**Broad `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` suppresses user text scaling**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to `body` and block-level elements, this overrides OS-level text size preferences. Accepted as a legacy email hack for iOS font inflation, but WCAG 1.4.4 non-conformant. Scope should be narrowed to specific cells where inflation is actually a problem, not applied globally.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Violates WCAG 3.1.1. Screen readers will guess the language. Fix: `<html lang="en" ...>`.

**`robots` meta value is malformed**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a valid robots directive token. The correct value is `noindex`. Low impact in email clients, but if this email is web-hosted or mirrored, the directive is silently ignored and the page may be indexed. Fix: `content="noindex"`.
