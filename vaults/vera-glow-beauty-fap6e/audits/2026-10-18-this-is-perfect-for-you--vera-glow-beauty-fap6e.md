# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- "Still thinking about it? You've got great taste!" is a cart-abandon line, and I never put anything from Skechers in a cart. It's also the same subject line as 9/29, 8/18 and 7/28. It also comes right after "Pick up where you left off!" and "Don't leave these styles behind!" That makes four "you forgot something" emails in under three weeks for a cart that doesn't exist.
- The hero is a kids' Skechers Garage sneaker with a toy truck in the sole. I'm a 29-year-old woman, so that is not for me. The six women's styles under it are a better match, but the grid is tiny and I can't see a price on any of them. The one real hook, 20% off for members through 10/21, sits in a dark block halfway down the email.
- Would I click? Probably not. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and wait for the next one. If Skechers had put the 20% up top over a women's shoe, I might have tapped.

## What's working

- The 20% OFF block is big, high-contrast and easy to read. It has a deadline ("Valid thru 10/21"), so my points-math brain registered it.
- The women's grid (Slip-ins Mark Nason, BOBS B Cute, Arch Fit Arcade) finally shows shoes I might wear.
- The render is clean. There are no broken images or overlapping text, and the category list (Women / Men / Kids / Sale) is easy to tap.
- The fine print is spelled out: online only, dates, exclusions, and that you need to be logged in.

## What's weak

- The hero is a kids' toy-truck sneaker, which has nothing to do with me. It's also the third toy-truck-adjacent hero I've seen in these sends.
- The cart-abandon framing is false. "Still thinking about it?" means nothing when I never added anything.
- The offer is buried below the hero and the product grid. A mobile reader has to scroll past a kids' shoe to find out there's a discount.
- There are no prices, ratings or sizes on the women's grid. I can't tell what 20% off actually saves me, and the product tiles are small.
- The "SKECHERS PLUS MEMBERS" wording and the "must be logged in" fine print make the offer feel like a membership gate, not an open sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% member offer above the hero and make a women's shoe the hero. Segment kids' heroes to parents only.
- 2. Stop sending cart-abandon copy to people with no cart. Use "New fall arrivals" or "Your 20% member perk ends 10/21" instead.
- 3. Add prices, strike-through savings and star ratings to the six-shoe grid. I want to see what the deal is worth.
- 4. Change "TAKE ANOTHER LOOK" to a specific verb and noun, like "Shop women's Slip-ins".
- 5. Don't repeat a subject line that has already gone out three times.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New women's Slip-ins, 20% off for members`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Skechers Plus perk on select full-priced styles, ends Tuesday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOBS, Arch Fit and Slip-ins picks, plus how to stack your 20%.`
