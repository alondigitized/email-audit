# SMS SOS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "SMS SOS" — cute, but I opened this expecting an actual sale emergency and got... an ask to sign up for texts. DE has been on an genuinely great subject-line streak lately ("Hot takes," "Golden hour every hour," "Feeling sticky?") and this one breaks it — it's clever wordplay with zero payoff information. The whole email is basically a house-list growth play wearing a "glow all in" banner as a disguise.
- That said, once I'm actually in it, it's not a wasted open — the product grid at the bottom (Protini, B-Hydra, D-Bronzi drops, C-Firma) has real star ratings and "720 people are checking this out" social proof, which is the kind of FOMO signal that actually gets me tapping. But the primary CTA — "GET ON THE LIST" — isn't shopping, it's just another channel ask. I'd scroll past the SMS pitch straight to the product grid and probably tap into Protini since I already know and love that jar.
- Verdict: I wouldn't forward this to a friend — it's not exciting enough — but I'd probably click through to one product in the grid before archiving. Not a delete, not a save.

## What's working

- The top banner front-loads a real, dated offer (free deluxe samples on $75+, deluxe sample on new subscribe $50+) instead of burying it — cleaner than Ulta's recent "this event is over" fumble.
- The product grid at the bottom has star ratings AND live social-proof counters ("255 people are checking this out") — that's a legit trust cue Sephora Daily Deals didn't bother with in their recycled hair-mousse send.
- Ingredient callouts are baked into the product names (Polypeptide, Hyaluronic Acid, Vitamin-C) — I don't have to click through to know what I'm buying, unlike ILIA's wall-of-text email that gave me nothing visual to go on.
- The SMS phone mockup is a nice, on-brand way to preview what I'm signing up for before I commit my number.

## What's weak

- The subject "SMS SOS" tells me nothing — no offer, no product, no real urgency — a big step down from "Hot takes" and "Golden hour every hour," which actually earned their opens.
- The whole top half of the email (banner + STAY CLOSE hero + phone mockup) is about texting, not shopping — as a Rouge/Diamond member doing points math, there's zero tier or loyalty language here, which feels like a miss for someone who's clearly already spending with them.
- Primary CTA "GET ON THE LIST" isn't a shopping action, so if I'm skimming on my commute I might bounce before I ever reach the actual products.
- The fine print with real dates (7/19–7/26, 7/20–8/21) is buried at the bottom in tiny text instead of being used to create visible urgency near the CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual product or sale hook in the subject, not the SMS ask — save the texting pitch for a mid-body module. Glossier gets away with 12 emails in a row because each one has a distinct mechanic; this one just feels like list-building filler.
- 2. Move the product grid up, above the SMS phone mockup — that's the section with real click intent (ratings, social proof, ingredients).
- 3. Tie the offer to loyalty status somewhere — even a line like "Rouge members get early access" would make this feel like it's talking to me specifically instead of any random subscriber.
- 4. Turn the fine-print dates into a visible countdown or bold deadline line near the top offer, not buried in the terms.
