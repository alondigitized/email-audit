# Last day for 25% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Email number TEN. Ten Drunk Elephant Friends & Family emails in — I want to be clear that I've been cataloguing this campaign since send #6, and at this point Drunk Elephant owns a dedicated folder in my inbox. "Last day for 25% off!" is functionally correct but it's the most obvious thing they could have written for a last-day send. The creative inside, though? "Friends till the end" with that banner copy yelling "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS AD SHOP!" — that's the self-aware DE voice I actually like. There's a meta-humor quality to this one that the previous couple sends (the "Ending soon" and "25% off is going..." emails) didn't quite have.
- The problem I cannot get past is the countdown timer. It's sitting at 00:00:00:00 — completely zeroed out — and that's the same credibility crater I called out when Charlotte Tilbury did it on their ninth email. Nothing says "we scheduled this blast and didn't QA it" faster than a clock that already hit midnight. On the last day of a sale, a live timer is the one asset with actual urgency leverage, and it's running on empty.
- I'd click. The $75+ full-size F-Balm gift got me and the product grid at the bottom has my items with real prices attached. But I wouldn't forward this — too far into the campaign, too routine a close.

## What's working

- **"Friends till the end" + the meta banner copy** — the self-referential "WHY ARE YOU STILL READING THIS AD SHOP!" is DE's personality at its best; I actually laughed and it made me feel seen as a subscriber who's been here for all ten
- **Tiered gift structure is clean and visible** — $75+ for full-size F-Balm and $100+ for the Violine/Bright-Sol bundle is laid out in scannable blocks; this is more layered gifting than ILIA pulled off in email 14
- **Product grid with real prices** — "The Best of Friends" section with individual products and "SHOP NOW" buttons is immediately shoppable; I can see what I'm getting and at what price
- **Hero 25% off is unmissable** — visual hierarchy is correct, the offer dominates, nothing is fighting it

## What's weak

- **Countdown timer = 00:00:00:00** — this is the Charlotte Tilbury problem. A dead timer on a last-day send is urgency theater that backfires; it actively reads as "the sale might already be over"
- **Subject line fatigue** — "Last day for 25% off!" is accurate but completely interchangeable with what Sephora, Ulta, and Glossier all sent during their own closing days; after nine prior DE emails, I needed DE's voice in the subject, not a category default
- **Possible offer discrepancy in the small banner** — there's text near the top that appears to show different numbers alongside the 25% hero; if I'm reading tiered percentages up there, the inconsistency with the hero creates a split-second "wait, which is it?" moment
- **No personal name or account callout** — Sephora has been signing things with my member tier on closing sends; DE treats me like a generic list entry on email ten, which stings a little

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or remove the countdown timer before send** — a zeroed clock is worse than no clock; replace with a static "Offer ends midnight tonight" text line if you can't guarantee a live timer QA
- 2. **Put DE's voice in the subject** — the "Well, hi, 25% off!" subject from email 7 outperformed precisely because it had a personality hook; something like "This is goodbye (from your wallet)" or "The cart doesn't wait forever" would carry the campaign voice forward
- 3. **Add Rouge/Diamond-tier callout to the hero** — as a Sephora Rouge cross-shopper I respond to being acknowledged as a high-value customer; "For our best Friends" with a nod to repeat purchasers would have landed differently
- 4. **Lead the tiered gift math in the subject or preheader** — the $75+ and $100+ gift tiers are genuinely better than what Ulta was running with their $10/$20 tiered saves, but nobody knows from the inbox; surface it upfront
- **Subject Alt A:** `Friends & Family ends tonight — free full-size F-Balm with $75`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call, friend. 25% off + a free gift.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75, get full-size F-Balm free. $100 unlocks even more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% sitewide + tiered gifts. Gone at midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- Font assets load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com` — a third-party host outside Drunk Elephant's controlled domain. This creates an unintended third-party request that some corporate spam filters flag.
- Sending domain is `cheers.drunkelephant.com` (subdomain). Cannot verify DKIM/SPF alignment from HTML alone (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — invalid HTML comment syntax inside `<style>` block:**

```css
<!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
<!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

HTML `<!-- -->` comments are not valid inside `<style>` tags. CSS parsers in Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook will either silently drop these rules or (in older WebKit/Blink clients) parse the selector text as literal CSS tokens. The intended mobile-width overrides for `.contenttable` and `.mobile` **will not apply reliably**. These must be converted to CSS comments (`/* ... */`) or removed.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```

Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on the `*` selector disables user-initiated text scaling on all iOS WebKit clients. Replace `none` with `100%` on both properties.

**Duplicate font loading — three separate mechanisms for the same assets:**

1. `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags in `<head>` (three tags → three HTTP requests)
2. `@import url(...)` inside `<style>` (same three URLs — redundant)
3. `@font-face` blocks also referencing `db.onlinewebfonts.com`

This means up to 9 external requests for fonts that most email clients will block anyway. Collapse to a single `@font-face` declaration per weight and remove the `<link>` + `@import` duplication.

**Missing `alt` text confirmation:** The source is truncated before `<img>` tags are visible. Cannot confirm alt attributes — flagged for manual verification.
