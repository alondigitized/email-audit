# Your summer must-haves
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-one. I am thirty-one Sally emails deep, and this one landed in my inbox roughly twelve hours after "Save on summer hair care essentials" — which, if you're keeping score at home, means Sally sent me two summer-hair emails in under a day. I know July gets hectic on the content calendar, but this is the kind of self-sabotage that makes me wonder if anyone is looking at the send schedule holistically. It's the email marketing equivalent of posting the same Instagram Story twice with a slightly different caption.
- Inside, it's actually decent. The four-category editorial structure — swim protection, heat defense, scalp soothing, color bonding — tells a real story and doesn't feel like a random product dump. The hero model has good energy: young, curly-haired, summer-ready. The SALLX "Buy More, Earn More" block is doing the points-math work I want to see. But the 20% off offer is buried *below* four product rows — I have to scroll to find the actual reason to buy — and "20% off (almost) everything" hits very differently when Sally ran an unhedged 25% off sitewide on July 3. That word "almost" is working overtime right now.
- I'd skim this, tap through to the Swimmer's Shampoo out of post-pool-season habit, and close it. I wouldn't forward it. I wouldn't open it with any excitement.

## What's working

- The four product categories (swim, heat, scalp, color) give this a genuine editorial arc — it reads like a summer survival guide, not a SKU dump.
- Hero model is right: young, curly hair, summer energy — she looks like someone I'd follow on Instagram, and that's the bar.
- "Buy More, Earn More / 4x on $35+" in the SALLX block speaks directly to a points optimizer — tier math is visible at a glance without having to dig for it.
- "20% off" is a specific, no-ambiguity discount number in the offer banner — better than the "up to 40% off" Sally was running earlier in the month.
- Clean render: no broken images, no layout issues, tap targets are reasonably spaced for mobile.

## What's weak

- "Your summer must-haves" landing twelve hours after "summer hair care essentials" is a cadence failure — these should have been combined or spaced by at least three days.
- The 20% off offer is below the fold after four product rows — by the time I see it, my peak attention is already spent.
- "(Almost) everything" is an instant trust flag when last week's benchmark was 25% off the *whole* site — now I'm running mental exclusion math instead of just clicking.
- Zero urgency cues — no expiration date, no "this weekend only," nothing. Without a deadline, 20% off reads like a standing price, not a reason to act today.
- The SALLX loyalty block doesn't show my current point balance or tier status — it's generic enough that a non-member reads it identically to me. Ulta Diamond emails always lead with my actual balance; that's the move Sally is missing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this send 48–72 hours.** Two summer-hair emails in one day is self-cannibalizing. Drunk Elephant spaces similar content by at least a week and their cadence builds anticipation — Sally is burning open rate by running these back to back.
- 2. **Move the 20% off into the hero.** The offer should be the first thing I see, not the fourth. Sephora reliably leads with the discount number in the hero *before* any product editorial — that's why I'm conditioned to open Sephora on sale days with intent.
- 3. **Kill the "(almost)."** If there are exclusions, name them. If it's 20% off everything, say so. Glossier's sale emails always felt clean because they committed to the offer language — this hedge does the opposite.
- 4. **Add an expiration date.** Even "through Sunday" creates urgency. The July 5 "Last Call" email worked because there was a clear end state — this one has none of that, and it reads like a shrug.
- 5. **Put the offer in the subject, not a category label.** "Your summer must-haves" is what you write when you have nothing else to say. At Sephora my Rouge status gets referenced; I want at minimum a number and a deadline.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```

`white-space:pre` is a text-layout property and has no valid effect on `<img>` elements. It is harmless in most clients but is noise that could interact unexpectedly with image-inside-`<td>` whitespace handling in older Outlook builds. Should be removed.

**Confirmed issue — `Founders Grotesk` declared but never loaded**

```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```

No `@font-face` rule for Founders Grotesk exists in the `<head>`. All anchor text falls back to Helvetica/sans-serif silently. If brand consistency requires Founders Grotesk on CTAs, a self-hosted woff2 `@font-face` block (with an `mso-` guard for Outlook) is needed.
