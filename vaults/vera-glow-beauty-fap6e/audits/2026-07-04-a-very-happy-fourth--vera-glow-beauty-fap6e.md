# A very happy Fourth!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Drunk Elephant shows up in my inbox on the Fourth of July dressed in blue stripes and beach energy, and honestly — the hero copy "BEACH, PLEASE." is so very DE that I smiled for a second before I started scrolling. The problem is this email is basically a product catalog in a straw hat. Six alternating feature blocks, all of them screaming SHOP NOW, and then — buried almost at the bottom — a "35% LESS ON JUMPS & EVERYTHING" offer that stopped me cold. What is a jump? Is that a product? A category? A typo? Because if I don't know what I'm getting 35% off, I'm not clicking anything.
- This is DE's fifth email in eight days. I've seen "Reveal your best skin tonight," "20% off ends soon," "The It kits," "Sleep your way…" — and now a beach catalog. I'm not unsubscribing because I genuinely love this brand and B.Hydra serum has been in my routine for two years. But the cadence is doing real damage to my open urgency. Compare that to ILIA, which ran a tight three-day "Buy 3 Save 20%" series where every send reinforced the same clear offer — scored an 8 both days. DE is throwing me a different creative every 36 hours and I've lost the thread of what I'm even supposed to be buying.
- The free shipping with no minimum, surfaced immediately in the preheader, is the strongest signal in this whole email. I'd mark this as read, maybe come back for the 35% if I can figure out what it covers, but I'm not forwarding it to anyone.

## What's working

- The preheader does the heavy lifting: "GET FREE GROUND SHIPPING ON ALL ORDERS—NO MINIMUM REQUIRED" loaded before I even fully opened, and that's a real offer communicated cleanly
- "BEACH, PLEASE." as hero copy is punchy, seasonal, and immediately DE — brand voice is fully consistent
- The alternating image/copy layout reads clean on mobile, no broken images or obvious render issues
- The B.Hydra serum and Lique Balm feature blocks speak to real products I use — not filler
- Fourth of July beach timing is on-the-nose; e.l.f. SKIN scored an 8 today for the same instinct, and DE earns the same credit here

## What's weak

- "35% LESS ON JUMPS & EVERYTHING" — I have no idea what "JUMPS" is and that confusion kills purchase intent on the spot; the most compelling number in the email is undermined by unclear language
- Five emails in eight days is past the point where I'm reading carefully — I'm skimming DE now the way I skim Ulta's third "ends tonight" of the week
- The 35% offer is buried below five feature blocks — they led with the vibe and hid the deal, which is backwards when the deal is actually good
- Zero loyalty acknowledgment — I'm a prestige beauty customer doing points math every time I see a discount, and this email treats me like a stranger who just found the website
- Subject "A very happy Fourth!" is a holiday greeting, not an email — Sally Beauty just sent a clean Fourth of July send that opened with the offer in the subject itself and scored a 10; DE left that opportunity sitting on the sand

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 35% offer in the hero** — "BEACH, PLEASE. 35% off your summer essentials." is the email. The beach visual works either way; the offer needs to ride up front, not after five scrolls
- 2. **Fix "JUMPS" copy immediately** — if it's a product category or a size tier, name it explicitly in the same line; ambiguity in an offer is a conversion killer
- 3. **Cap the send cadence or tie the creative to a through-line** — five sends in eight days works if each one escalates a single offer; right now each email is its own standalone event and I've lost the story
- 4. **Add a loyalty or devotee line** — even one personalized sentence ("as a DE regular, here's your head start on the 35%") would signal that they know who I am
- 5. **Consolidate the SHOP NOW buttons** — six identical CTAs competes with itself; pick one hero product, feature it, make one CTA the star

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments inside `<style>` block:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```
HTML comment syntax (`<!-- ... -->`) is invalid inside a CSS `<style>` block. Both rules — `contenttable` width and `mobile` width — are dead code. Any email client that parses CSS strictly will silently drop them, breaking the responsive layout for those two container classes on screens ≤600px.

**Triple font loading (performance):**
BrownBold, BrownLight, and BrownThin are each loaded three times:
1. `<link href="https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...">` in `<head>`
2. `@import url(https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/c/...)` inside `<style>`
3. `@font-face { src: url("https://db.onlinewebfonts.com/t/...") }` also inside `<style>`

This triples HTTP requests for each of the three third-party fonts before the email body renders. Remove the `<link>` tags and `@import` lines; keep only `@font-face`.

**Third-party font CDN dependency:**
BrownBold/Light/Thin load from `db.onlinewebfonts.com`, a third-party CDN with no SLA. BrownRegular correctly loads from `www.drunkelephant.com` (brand-controlled). If `db.onlinewebfonts.com` is unavailable, three of four weights fall back silently — inconsistent with the one weight that is self-hosted.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. This is an accessibility violation for users with low vision. Change to `initial-scale=1` without `maximum-scale`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` (universal selector):**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
`none` disables all automatic text scaling in WebKit/Blink clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail). The standard safe value is `100%`, which prevents unwanted scaling while permitting user-initiated zoom. Already correctly set on `body`; the `*` override contradicts it.
