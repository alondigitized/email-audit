# Drunk classics are 25% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Day three of the DE Friends & Family campaign, and this is the fourth email since Tuesday. I want to be upfront about where I'm standing: I already bought on launch day. The F&F invite earned a 9 from me, "Stock up at 25% off!" landed yesterday morning, "25% off the whole enchilada" showed up yesterday afternoon, and now here's a fourth send. My tolerance is wearing thin — but I'm genuinely glad I opened this one anyway.
- Buried about halfway down is content that's actually *new*: GWP tiers. Spend $50+ and get a free Hydra kit. Spend $75+ and get a **full-size** T.L.C. Sukari Babyfacial plus Lala Retro Whipped Moisturizer. The Babyfacial alone is $90+ at retail. If I'm already at $65 in my cart, I am adding something to hit $75 and I am not sorry about it. That's the kind of threshold math I live for, and neither the previous three F&F emails surfaced it this explicitly.
- What I'm side-eyeing: the top banner is pitching me Subscribe to Save like I just discovered DE exists. "Hi, Friend! Sign up for Subscribe to Save and get 25% off your first subscription order." I've been opening their emails since before the DE x Sephora exclusivity era. That acquisition message is eating the preview text real estate that should be screaming about the free Babyfacial. It's the right email with the wrong lede. I'll click. I won't forward — anyone in my group chat who wanted in already got three invitations.

## What's working

- The GWP tier structure ($50+ / $75+) is genuinely new campaign content — unlike ILIA, which has sent nine emails since May 16 cycling the same 20% off headline with zero added incentive
- "FRIENDS & FAMILY 25% OFF SITEWIDE" hero is impossible to miss — large type, clean layout, anchors the eye before the product grid
- Product grid selection (Protini, C-Firma, B-Hydra, D-Bronzi) is my actual DE routine; these aren't random picks, they're the repurchaseable classics that justify the "classics" subject framing
- Full-size Babyfacial GWP at the $75 threshold is the hardest a GWP has worked in any beauty email I've seen this week — Glossier's Memorial Day sends don't come close to this on threshold incentives
- Sender display name "Drunk Elephant Friends & Family Sale" is unambiguous in the inbox

## What's weak

- Top banner S2S acquisition pitch ("Hi, Friend! Sign up for Subscribe to Save...") is aimed at strangers, not three-day F&F loyalists — it's the wrong message for this audience segment
- "Hi, Friend!" reads like a merge field that gave up; four emails in and DE still hasn't used my name
- No explicit sale end date anywhere visible in the email — "when does this expire" is a real friction point and the answer isn't anywhere in the hero
- Four F&F emails in three days is officially entering the Charlotte Tilbury / ILIA fatigue zone I flagged this week; the cadence is the brand's biggest deliverability risk right now, not the creative

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the GWP tiers in the hero** — "Spend $75 → free full-size Babyfacial" is a more compelling reason to open day four of a sale than "here are the products you already know exist at 25% off"
- 2. **Add an explicit end date** in the hero section — even "Ends Sunday, May 25" removes the hesitation loop entirely
- 3. **Swap the S2S banner** for a loyalty-forward message: "You're in — here's how to make it count" with a direct GWP callout for customers already in the funnel
- 4. **Segment the preview text** for people who've opened the prior three sends — "Still shopping F&amp;F?" acknowledges the cadence without apologizing for it

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free Babyfacial is waiting (spend $75)`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $75, get full-size T.L.C. Sukari — yes, really.`

## Technical Audit — Drunk Elephant Friends & Family Sale

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with three separate font-loading mechanisms for the same typefaces; the most critical code issue is HTML comment syntax injected directly inside a CSS `@media` block, which silently kills two responsive layout rules.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Truncated source — full link audit not possible.** From what is visible:

- No UTM parameters confirmed on any link in the rendered portion; assessment deferred to full source (see §6).
- Font assets are served from `https://db.onlinewebfonts.com` (third-party CDN) — requests to this domain will appear in recipient mail client logs and could be flagged by corporate security gateways.
- Font assets also served from `https://www.drunkelephant.com/on/demandware.static/…` (Salesforce Commerce Cloud). If the Demandware static versioning timestamp (`v1719565389232`) is stale or the path is invalidated by a platform upgrade, all custom fonts silently fall back to system fonts.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — HTML comments inside CSS block break responsive rules:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 600px) {
  *[class=hide] { display: none !important;}
  <!-- *[class=contenttable] { width:414px !important;} -->
  <!-- *[class=mobile] { width:414px !important;height: auto !important;} -->
```

`<!-- -->` is HTML syntax; inside a `<style>` block it is invalid CSS and is not parsed as a comment by any CSS engine. Both the `.contenttable` and `.mobile` responsive width overrides are silently dropped. Affected classes will not resize at ≤600px viewport widths.

**Triple font loading — duplicate HTTP requests:**

The same three Brown font variants are requested three times each:
1. `<link>` tags in `<head>` (`db.onlinewebfonts.com`)
2. `@import` inside `<style>` (same `db.onlinewebfonts.com` URLs)
3. `@font-face` declarations (also `db.onlinewebfonts.com` + separate `.eot`/`.woff2`/etc. stack)

This fires 6–9 redundant HTTP requests per open, adds latency on first render, and will cause inconsistent font resolution ordering across clients.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector:**

```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```

Using `none` (not `100%`) prevents iOS Mail users from manually zooming text — a WCAG 2.1 §1.4.4 violation. Change to `100%`.

**`line-height: 100%` on universal selector:**

```css
*{line-height: 100%}
```

This blanket override is known to collapse line-height inheritance in Outlook and produce clipped descenders on multi-line text. Should be scoped to specific elements, not `*`.

**`max-height: 1000000px` on universal selector:**

Standard Gmail clipping hack — no issue, intentional.
