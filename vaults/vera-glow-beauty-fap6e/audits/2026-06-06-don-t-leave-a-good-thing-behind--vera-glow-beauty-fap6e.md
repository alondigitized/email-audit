# Don't leave a good thing behind.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Skechers' classic "selling fast" abandon trigger — a hero shoe, a "SHOP NOW" button, and a row of algorithmic recs underneath. I've now counted eight of these in under three weeks from this sender, and the formula hasn't changed: urgency headline, no price, no offer, category nav grid at the bottom. The hero this time is the Skechers Garage — a chunky, streetwear-coded high-top that honestly reads more like it was pulled from the men's catalog. There's nothing in this email that signals Skechers knows anything about me beyond "she visited a product page."
- What bothers me most isn't the email itself — it's what's missing. No discount. No price. No loyalty points callout. ILIA just closed a 23-email June run with a consumer-study proof send that felt earned. This feels like a pipeline firing because a timer expired. The "SELLING FAST" urgency is completely unverifiable, and after the May 26th double-send (two cart-abandons in one inbox session), my trust in Skechers' scarcity claims is basically zero.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to anyone. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- **Clean layout, no render issues.** The hierarchy is straightforward — logo, hero shoe, single CTA, then recs. Nothing overlapping or broken.
- **"SHOP NOW" button is unambiguous.** Blue, centered, clearly labeled — tap target is fine on mobile.
- **Skechers Garage is a specific product.** At least there's a named hero item linked, not just a generic category page.
- **BNPL options (Afterpay + Klarna) in the footer.** Nice utility signal — acknowledges how people actually shop at this price point.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** No price shown, no discount, no points event, no free shipping callout. Charlotte Tilbury leads with the GWP before you even blink; Skechers gives me a shoe and a timer I can't verify.
- **Hero product is clearly not targeted at me.** The Skechers Garage is a chunky streetwear high-top that reads masculine/Gen Z. I'm 29, I buy slip-ons and D'Lites. The recommendation row has women's styles but they're so small I need to pinch-zoom.
- **"NO:25383" is leaking at the top of the email.** That tracking number is visible at the very top of the render — it's going to hit the preheader slot in most clients. That is not a good first impression.
- **Cadence is completely out of control.** This is email eight (at minimum) in under three weeks. The May 26th double-send — two cart-abandons in one inbox session — burned whatever goodwill Skechers had left. "Don't leave a good thing behind" feels hollow when I've already left it behind six times and you keep emailing anyway.
- **No loyalty signal.** I don't know if Skechers has a program or whether I'm getting anything for engaging. Ulta would never send an email to a Diamond member without at least acknowledging my tier.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price or an offer — full stop.** "Selling fast at $89" or "Extra 15% off this style today" transforms this from noise into a decision. The hero shoe with no price is a dead end.
- 2. **Swap the hero to a women's style.** If behavioral data says I looked at the Garage, fine — but if this is a broadcast, the hero should match the highest-value female segment. The recommendation row women's styles should be the hero.
- 3. **Suppress this trigger if I've been emailed twice in 24 hours.** The double-send on May 26th should have auto-killed any cart trigger for at least 48 hours. Build that logic.
- 4. **Fix the preheader / "NO:25383" leak.** Hide that tracking ID. It renders as the preview line and immediately signals "you are a number to us."
- 5. **Fold the category nav grid.** WOMEN / MEN / KIDS / CLOTHING / NEW ARRIVALS / SALE takes up a third of the email for zero incremental value if you already have a hero CTA above it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Garage pick is almost gone — $[price]`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still thinking about these? Here's a reason to decide.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Skechers Garage is moving — grab your size before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Selling fast: your saved style + 6 we think you'll love`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — empty. Screen readers and some email clients surface this as the document label. Should be set to something meaningful (e.g., `"SKECHERS — Don't leave a good thing behind"`).

**`#MessageViewBody a { text-decoration: none }` global rule**
This CSS block strips underlines from all links in Gmail's web client. Links that rely solely on color for differentiation from body text will fail WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). If any body copy contains inline links (not CTA buttons), they will be visually indistinguishable.

**Preheader padding — soft hyphens (`­`, U+00AD)**
The preheader filler block mixes COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER (`͏`, U+034F) with SOFT HYPHEN (`­`, U+00AD). Soft hyphens are rendered as visible dashes in some assistive technologies and older email clients. Recommend replacing all U+00AD characters with zero-width non-breaking spaces (`&#8203;`) or additional CGJ characters.

**Image alt text**
The HTML is truncated before the image blocks render. If `alt=""` is omitted on product/hero images (not explicitly set to empty string), clients with images blocked will show broken image icons instead of blank space. Verify all `<img>` tags carry explicit `alt` attributes (empty string acceptable for decorative images).

**MSO/VML and mobile breakpoints**
VML namespace declarations and `[if mso]` conditional comments are present and correctly structured. Mobile breakpoints fire at `max-width:620px` against a 600px content width — correct. No issues found here.
