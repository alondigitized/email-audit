# Summer Sale: 200+ picks added
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's second consecutive Summer Sale email in 48 hours — arriving the morning after yesterday's Summer Series pivot (July 8 free-class program), which makes the cadence feel slightly whiplash-y for any subscriber paying attention. The hook is "200+ picks added," a direct numerical escalation of the July 2 "150+ new picks added" formula. That's the second time the same creative mechanic has run inside a week, and after yesterday's deliberate pivot away from sale content, returning to it the very next day signals the brand blinked on its own course correction.
- The email earns its keep through a clean gender-segmented CTA structure, named bestsellers in the hero copy (Define, Zeroed In, Align), and a competent product grid. But the creative concept is thin: "more stuff added to the sale" is a lazy drum to keep banging. The hero copy is actually meaningfully better than the banner suggests — naming specific product lines is real — and that gap between the subject's volume claim and the body's product specificity is a missed opportunity.
- Bottom line: solid execution of a formula that's visibly fatiguing. The brand had a chance to deepen the Summer Series narrative from yesterday; instead it reset back to catalogue mode.

## What's working

- **Gender-segmented CTAs** — "Shop Women's Summer Sale" and "Shop Men's Summer Sale" side by side removes all ambiguity about where to go.
- **Specific bestseller names in hero copy** — Define, Zeroed In, and Align named by product line give the "new additions" claim a concrete hook beyond just a number.
- **Strong visual hierarchy** — the orange Summer Sale banner, warm-toned hero model, then product grid; eye path is immediate and obvious.
- **Clean product grid with visible prices** — multiple tiles render without gaps or broken images; real browsing energy.
- **Brand-consistent visual identity** — orange palette, model photography, and typography feel unmistakably lululemon throughout.

## What's weak

- **No savings anchor in the hero** — "Summer Sale" implies a discount but never surfaces one; no headline %/$ off means there's no urgency hook above the fold.
- **The escalating-count formula is worn out** — "200+ picks added" is the July 2 "150+ new picks added" with the number bumped; subscribers who've been on this journey since June 22 will recognize the playbook.
- **Cadence whiplash vs. July 8** — the Summer Series email positioned itself as a pivot to experience-first content; reverting to a sale catalogue 24 hours later abandons that signal entirely.
- **"Don't miss the moment" is soft urgency** — implies a deadline without stating one; no end date means no real compulsion to act today vs. tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a savings anchor to the hero** — "New to the lineup: up to 40% off Define, Zeroed In, and Align" turns this from a browsing email into a buying email. The number is the first thing a sale-trained subscriber needs.
- 2. **Give the urgency section a real deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" or "These just dropped — they won't last" with a specific close date converts "maybe later" into "right now."
- 3. **Carry one thread from Summer Series** — a single line ("Free classes still available →") keeps the July 8 program alive instead of silently abandoning it. Tonal consistency across consecutive sends matters.
- 4. **Hero one product, not three** — the hero copy names three lines; pick the single freshest drop (Align Maxi? Define in the new colour?) and link it directly. One product with a CTA outconverts a list with a category link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale: Align, Define + 200 new picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in sale: your favourites in fresh colours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 40% off new arrivals — sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Define, Zeroed In, buttery soft Align — all just dropped in sale.`

## 1. Overview

This is lululemon's second consecutive Summer Sale email in 48 hours — arriving the morning after yesterday's Summer Series pivot (July 8 free-class program), which makes the cadence feel slightly whiplash-y for any subscriber paying attention. The hook is "200+ picks added," a direct numerical escalation of the July 2 "150+ new picks added" formula. That's the second time the same creative mechanic has run inside a week, and after yesterday's deliberate pivot away from sale content, returning to it the very next day signals the brand blinked on its own course correction.

The hero copy is actually meaningfully better than the banner suggests — naming specific product lines is real — and that gap between the subject's volume claim and the body's product specificity is a missed opportunity.

Bottom line: solid execution of a formula that's visibly fatiguing. The brand had a chance to deepen the Summer Series narrative from yesterday; instead it reset back to catalogue mode.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font CDN — 403 Forbidden (5 assets)**

All five custom fonts fail with HTTP 403:

```
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Light.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Regular.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Medium.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-SemiBold.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/KHGiga-Light.woff
```

Impact: every client that attempts `@font-face` loads falls back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman`. The `guest-voice-*` serif display classes are particularly affected.

**Tracking links — 32 skipped**

32 click-redirect URLs were not probed. No confirmed broken destination links, but no confirmed live ones either. These should be spot-checked manually or via a click-redirect resolver before deployment.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 3 instances**

The footer arrow icon (`SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right`) appears three times with no `alt` attribute:

```
src: https://images.lululemon.com/is/image/lululemon/SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right?$pngstandard$
```

If the image fails to load, these links render as invisible/unlabeled click targets. Fix: add `alt=""` (decorative) or a descriptive string like `alt="→"`.

**Custom font weight mismatch**

`font-weight: 380` is declared on `p`, `li`, `h1–h5`, and `a` elements, but `Saans-Regular.woff` is broken (403). Browsers that don't synthesize weight 380 will snap to 400, affecting the intended typographic weight across the full email.

**Dark mode — no issues found**

Dark mode targeting uses three correct patterns: `u + .body` Gmail hack, `[class~="x_cta-white"]` for Outlook.com, and `\0` hack for the Outlook Android app. Implementation is solid.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not found**

The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured through the AgentMail relay. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced for >5k/day senders) and RFC 2369.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — not found**

One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) could not be confirmed. Google and Yahoo require this for bulk senders as of February 2024.

SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the relay did not surface the `Authentication-Results` header. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a relay capture gap, not a confirmed deliverability failure — but it must be verified against raw headers in SFMC or the ESP sending report.
