# Updates to our Terms of Use and Privacy Policy
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a legal-notice email, not a marketing send — a policy update on Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, Gift Card Terms, and Return Policy, effective September 24, 2026. It's a hard reset in tone from lululemon's usual run of new-arrival editorial (Fast and Free™ on 8/18, EasyFive Pants on 8/12, Scuba Evolve on 8/11) — no product, no model, no offer, just a muted taupe hero panel and legal copy. That's appropriate for the content type, but it means every rubric signal built around persona targeting, hero offers, and CTAs simply doesn't apply here.
- Judged as what it is — a compliance notification — it's clean and legible: clear hierarchy, working links, no render bugs. Judged as an email competing for attention in an inbox that's seen five product launches from this brand in three weeks, it will get a fast open-then-archive and zero engagement. That's fine; this genre isn't meant to drive clicks. The one real flaw is functional, not creative: the "here" link (paragraph 3) and the "Terms of Use"/"Privacy Policy" links at the top point to the same underlying policies with no visible differentiation, so a reader trying to actually find out what changed has to click through multiple links to piece it together.

## What's working

- Clean, on-brand visual identity — muted taupe hero with white wordmark keeps it recognizably lululemon even without product photography.
- Effective date is stated plainly ("on or after September 24, 2026") — no ambiguity about when the change takes effect.
- What's changing is itemized as a short bulleted-style list (Gift Card Terms, Return Policy) rather than buried in a wall of text.
- Full contact surface is provided (Guest Education Centre email, live chat, two phone numbers, dedicated Privacy Team email) — appropriate for a compliance notice.
- No overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage.

## What's weak

- No preview/preheader text visible — inbox will likely show boilerplate or nothing, wasting valuable inbox real estate.
- "AI" is mentioned only in passing ("including our use of AI") with no elaboration — for a privacy-law change this is the single most newsworthy line and it's underplayed.
- Multiple similarly-styled underlined links (Terms of Use ×2, Privacy Policy ×2, Gift Card Terms, Return Policy, "here") with no visual distinction between "what changed" and "read the full policy" — easy to lose track of which link does what.
- No specific summary of *what* actually changed in the Return Policy or Gift Card Terms — just a link, forcing the reader to hunt through full legal text.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a one-line preheader that states the concrete change (e.g., "Updated Return Policy and Gift Card Terms — effective Sept 24") so the inbox preview does useful work instead of showing nothing.
- 2. Add a single plain-English sentence per changed policy (e.g., "Returns now require original receipt within 30 days") — even compliance emails should tell you what's different, not just link to where to find out.
- 3. Differentiate the repeated links visually or by label (e.g., "Read the updated Terms of Use" vs. "See what's changed") so the two Terms of Use / Privacy Policy links aren't redundant-looking.
- 4. Expand the one-line AI disclosure slightly — a sentence on what AI is now used for and why it affects privacy will preempt the most likely support inquiry.
