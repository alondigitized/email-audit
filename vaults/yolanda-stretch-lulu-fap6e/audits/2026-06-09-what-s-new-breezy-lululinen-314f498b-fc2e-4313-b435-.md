# What’s new: breezy LuluLinen™
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's LuluLinen™ fabric spotlight — a format that should feel familiar to anyone who got the June 2 Nulu™ email. Same playbook: editorial hero, clean copy about fabric technology, then a product grid to convert. The photography is predictably excellent — wide-leg pants in cream, a model in shorts, that muted linen palette — and the fabric story lands clearly: linen + modal, breezy, now softer.
- The problem is context. A second fabric-technology send in seven days, with no price anchor and no urgency, means this competes for attention in a mailbox that already contains the Nulu™ story. Subscribers with any inbox fatigue will treat this like a continuation of last week rather than a reason to click today.

## What's working

- Hero copy is specific — "LuluLinen™" names the product, and the subhead ("new blend of breezy linen and smooth modal") delivers the why in one sentence.
- Photography earns its space — the wide-leg silhouette on the opening image communicates the drape and ease of linen without a single word of stylist copy.
- "Softer than ever" is a credible claim for a repeat subscriber who remembers the original LuluLinen™ — this is a real upgrade message, not just a relaunch.
- Layout is clean and bug-free: hierarchy flows top to bottom without broken images, overlapping text, or gaps.

## What's weak

- No price anchor anywhere — a fabric spotlight without even a "from $X" line leaves the subscriber with nothing to act on besides general curiosity.
- "Shop Now" is the weakest possible CTA for a fabric-forward email; it could run on any email lululemon has ever sent.
- Heavy cadence risk: after Nulu™ on June 2, another fabric story on June 9 reads as brand filler rather than news.
- The product grid at the bottom ("Breeze, now circulating") fragments the focus — after a two-act editorial top, the grid shifts to catalog mode without a clear through-line.
- No urgency or scarcity signal makes this feel like evergreen content, not a reason to click today.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to one product with a price.** Pick the wide-leg LuluLinen™ pant from the hero image, link directly to its PDP, and add "From $128" in the subhead area. It turns an editorial into a shopping moment.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Shop LuluLinen™ Styles" beats "Shop Now" on specificity; "Try the New LuluLinen™" beats both on intrigue.
- 3. **Add a credible deadline.** Even "New arrivals, limited colorways" gives the grid section a reason to exist and a reason to click before tomorrow.
- 4. **Consolidate the product grid.** Three hero SKUs with a clear link > eight thumbnails competing for attention. If the email is about LuluLinen™, every item in the grid should be LuluLinen™.
- 5. **Differentiate harder from June 2.** Nulu™ email was about softness and stretch; LuluLinen™ should be angled on breathability and heat management — more weather-specific copy would separate it in memory.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS unit — `letter-spacing` with `%`:**
```css
/* .guest-voice-lg, .guest-voice-md */
letter-spacing: -2%;
/* .guest-voice-sm */
letter-spacing: -1%;
```
Percentage is not a valid unit for `letter-spacing` in CSS. The property accepts `<length>` (px, em, rem) or `normal`. Browsers and email clients will ignore these declarations and fall back to default letter-spacing, breaking the intended typographic compression. Should be `-0.02em` / `-0.01em`.

**Non-standard `font-weight` values:**
`font-weight: 380` and `font-weight: 570` appear throughout (e.g., `.eyebrow`, `h1–h5`, `p`). These are valid only for variable fonts; if the custom fonts (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Semibold`, `KHGiga-Light`) fail to load, user agents will round to the nearest standard weight (400 or 500), which may produce visible weight shifts in fallback rendering.

**Custom font declarations without visible `@font-face`:**
Five custom families are referenced (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Light`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Semibold`, `KHGiga-Light`) but no `@font-face` rules appear in the visible CSS. If loaded elsewhere in the `<head>` (truncated), confirm each src URL is HTTPS and the host allows cross-origin font loading. If absent, all text falls back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman`.

**Dark mode — `h4` excluded from commented-out override:**
```css
/*h1, h2, h3, h4, p, ul, ol, li {color:#fdfeff;}*/
```
The comment suggests this block was intentionally disabled, but `h4` has no separate dark mode rule in the visible CSS. If dark mode `h4` elements appear in body content, text color `#140F0F` (near-black) on a dark background may fail WCAG contrast.

**`<title>` is bare brand name only:**
```html
<title>lululemon</title>
```
Not an error, but some clients (e.g., Outlook desktop) surface the `<title>` as the window/tab label. No functional issue.
