# What's new: iconically plush puffers
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Fall 2026 outerwear push, led by Lewis Hamilton (labeled "Seven-Time World Champion") in a black puffer, with the headline "Quiet the weather." It is pure mood and has no price, no promo and no deadline. The photography is excellent and the brand voice is consistent. It's also very long, with four separate image grids stacked on each other.
- The email gives a shopper no reason to act today. There are two product stories, Wunder Puff and Featherweight, but no prices, no ratings and no sizes. The CTAs are split across five dark pills and several small text links. It looks like the Smooth Spacer send on 2026-09-29 and the Dance Studio Pants send on 2026-09-22. It's a good editorial piece but weak at driving revenue.
- Judgement: a handsome brand-awareness send that is average as a promotion. Seasonally timed, but it asks the reader to browse without giving them a reason to.

## What's working

- The Hamilton hero is a strong, high-contrast opening, and the black puffer fills the frame.
- "Quiet the weather." is a concise, on-brand headline.
- Two named products (Wunder Puff™, Featherweight) give the shopper specific things to look at.
- The Wunder Puff copy carries real product detail: "600-Fill-Power Down" and "water-repellent SoftMatte™ fabric".
- The timing suits the season, with outerwear sent in early October.
- The two button pairs, "Shop What's New" / "Shop Coats & Jackets" and "Shop Women's Outerwear" / "Shop Men's Outerwear", are legible and split by gender.
- Nothing is visibly broken. The images load and the layout holds.

## What's weak

- There is no price, offer, member benefit or deadline anywhere, so there is no reason to click now.
- Product images aren't labeled with names or prices, and the editorial crops make it hard to tell what is being sold.
- Five button pairs and text links compete. "Shop Wunder Puff →" and "Shop Featherweight →" are tiny text links that are easy to miss.
- The email is very long, with four photo grids. The Featherweight and bottom sections repeat the same Hamilton imagery and add little.
- There are no reviews or social proof.
- The hero says "Lewis Hamilton" but gives no product or price context.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete hook near the top, such as a price ("Wunder Puff from $X"), a member early-access perk or a shipping offer. This would lift the email more than anything else.
- 2. Label the products with name and price under the hero grids, and add a star rating to Wunder Puff.
- 3. Cut the email to hero, Wunder Puff and Featherweight, and drop the repeated Hamilton grids.
- 4. Make "Shop Wunder Puff" and "Shop Featherweight" real buttons, and keep one primary CTA in the first screen.
- 5. Add a color or size availability note, such as "Cream and black, XS–XXL."
