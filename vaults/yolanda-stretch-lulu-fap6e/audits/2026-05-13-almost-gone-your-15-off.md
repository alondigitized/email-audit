# Almost gone: your 15% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a triggered reminder email — the clock is ticking on a personal 15% off and lululemon is nudging you to use it before Thursday. The brand does its job: clean layout, legible hierarchy, and a hard deadline that's credible because it has a real timestamp. The offer is also honestly disclosed — exclusions (accessories, bags, gift cards, We Made Too Much) appear in the hero paragraph rather than buried below a fold.
- The problem is the experience this email describes is unnecessarily laborious. Four instructional steps to redeem a discount is a red flag for friction. The CTA button says "Sign in and Shop Now," which is accurate but reveals that this offer lives behind an authentication gate. A customer who lands the email on mobile and isn't already logged in has more steps ahead of them than the offer seems worth.
- The email is solid execution of a bad UX situation. If the redemption mechanics were simpler, this would be an excellent triggered send. As is, it explains a process that shouldn't require explanation.

## What's working

- **Deadline specificity is strong** — "Thursday, May 14, 2026 at 11:59pm PT" is precise and credible, not "limited time."
- **Exclusions are disclosed upfront** — named in the hero paragraph, not tucked into fine print.
- **Dual CTA placement** — one button above the instructional block, one below; catches both skimmers and readers.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — large "15% off ends soon." lands the offer on first glance.
- **"Not working?" fallback section** — acknowledges copy-paste as a real recovery path without being apologetic.

## What's weak

- **No product imagery whatsoever** — a lululemon offer email with zero aspiration or product context is a missed opportunity to connect the discount to something the recipient actually wants.
- **The "Here's how it works" block is 4 steps long** — a discount redemption process that needs a tutorial undermines confidence in the offer.
- **Both CTAs say "Sign in and Shop Now"** — the sign-in requirement is the first thing the user sees before shopping, which is friction before the hook.
- **Preheader text is too small to read clearly** — appears to bleed into the hero copy rather than carry its own lift in the inbox view.
- **No social proof or product recommendation** — nothing to anchor what to buy, leaving the 15% floating without purchase intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reduce friction at the CTA** — if technically possible, pre-authenticate via a tokenized link so the button becomes "Shop Now — 15% applied at checkout." The 4-step explainer exists because the current system requires it; fix the system.
- 2. **Add one hero product shot** — even a lifestyle crop or single featured item gives the discount something to attach to and raises click intent sharply.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA copy** — "Shop Before Thursday" beats "Sign in and Shop Now" because it emphasizes the deadline, not the friction.
- 4. **Collapse the "how it works" block** — if sign-in friction can't be removed, collapse the 4 steps to a single line: "Sign in, build your cart, and your 15% applies automatically."
- 5. **Sharpen the preheader** — make it do work the subject can't: surface the exclusions or the category the recipient should shop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Thursday only: your 15% off expires tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your lululemon 15% off expires in 2 days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Valid on full-price styles — not accessories, bags, or WMTM`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One purchase, any full-price item, ends Thursday at midnight PT`
