# Expand your range in new Flow Y styles
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Flow Y bra launch email — editorial in tone, fabric-forward in structure, and clearly cut from the same template as the Nulu™ spotlight (June 2) and LuluLinen™ spotlight (June 9). Three sections: a moody hero with buttery-soft minimalist copy, a Nulu™ fabric callout with the four-way stretch claim, and a product grid. At the bottom, a LuluLinen™ cross-promo tacks on a second brand story that competes for attention rather than reinforcing the lead.
- The subject line does real work — "Expand your range" is a yoga double-entendre (range of motion / range of styles) and pairs cleanly with the preheader "Flow it back it." which teases the Y-back design. That's a tighter inbox pairing than most lululemon sends this month. The creative itself is dark, high-contrast, and model-forward, which suits the brand but gives buyers almost nothing to judge fit or style variation beyond the hero shot.
- No discount, no deadline, no social proof. That's consistent with lululemon's pricing strategy, but it means this send lives or dies on how well the hero pulls someone into a browse session. The hero delivers. The third email in three days after June 15 (Father's Day urgency) and June 16 (golf launch) is the real risk — subscriber fatigue at this cadence is a genuine friction point.

## What's working

- **Subject + preheader pairing is genuinely clever.** "Expand your range" teases the yoga motion benefit; "Flow it back it." teases the signature back design. Both under 50 characters, no spam signals.
- **Hero photography lands the product.** The dark-background, single-model shot in the blue Flow Y bra is visually commanding and draws the eye immediately.
- **Nulu™ fabric callout earns its place.** Calling out four-way stretch and breathability gives the product a functional reason-to-buy that the hero image can't convey alone.
- **Product grid creates browse momentum.** After the editorial sections, a multi-item grid gives the shopper a surface to explore styles and colorways.
- **LuluLinen™ cross-promo rewards loyalists.** Anyone who opened the June 9 LuluLinen™ email (9/10) will recognize the callback — it reinforces the fabric innovation story without repeating the full pitch.

## What's weak

- **No price, no deal, no urgency.** "New styles this week" is the only deadline signal — barely credible and easy to dismiss.
- **Product grid is thumbnail-small.** Individual items are too small to assess color, fit, or design detail without clicking — that's friction between curiosity and click.
- **LuluLinen™ section splits the send.** Ending with a separate fabric line turns a focused Flow Y launch into a two-subject email — dilutes attention at the moment of conversion.
- **No social proof anywhere.** A single star-rating or "X reviews" badge under one hero product would do meaningful work here, especially for a new style.
- **Third email in three days.** After June 15 (Father's Day last-chance) and June 16 (golf launch), this is fatigue territory for anyone who got all three — the send cadence is doing quiet damage to engagement.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one credible time signal.** "New this week" is not urgency. "Just dropped — shop before sizes sell out" or a restock-window callout ("only in-stock through Sunday") would give a reason to act now rather than bookmark for later.
- 2. **Cut the LuluLinen™ section or move it to a footer module.** The Flow Y deserves a dedicated send. The cross-promo competes at the exact moment the subscriber is deciding whether to click into the bra.
- 3. **Make the product grid browsable.** Slightly larger thumbnails + one visible colorway count per item ("12 colors") would convert curiosity into a click far more reliably than the current grid density.
- 4. **Add one social proof element.** Even "★★★★★ – 4.7 stars across 2,300 reviews" in a single line under the hero would lower purchase hesitation on a new style.
- 5. **Pull back cadence.** The June 17 send would have landed harder after a one-day gap. Bundling the Flow Y grid into the June 16 golf email as a "New for her" module — or waiting until June 18 — would have preserved open rates.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid `letter-spacing` unit (confirmed in source):**

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }
```

`letter-spacing` does not accept `%` values in CSS. The spec requires a `<length>` (px, em, rem). This silently falls back to `letter-spacing: normal` in all email clients, so the intended tight kerning is discarded. Fix: convert to `em` equivalents (e.g., `-0.02em`).

**Non-standard `font-weight` values:**

```css
font-weight: 380;   /* p, h1–h5 */
font-weight: 570;   /* .eyebrow */
```

These are numeric variable-font weights and will only render correctly if the loaded font (Saans) is a variable font with a `wght` axis. Since no `@font-face` declaration is visible in the source, email clients will round to the nearest standard weight (300 or 400 for 380; 500 or 600 for 570) or apply system font fallbacks. If Saans is served via a stylesheet link not shown, this is acceptable; if not declared at all, the fallback is Helvetica Neue at standard weights.

**No `@font-face` declarations visible:** Custom fonts (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Light`, `Saans-Semibold`, `KHGiga-Light`) are referenced throughout but no `@font-face` or external stylesheet `<link>` is present in the truncated `<head>`. Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and most mobile clients will ignore custom fonts without this declaration and fall back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Times New Roman`. The fallback stack is defined and appropriate, but the intent (brand typography) will not render in the majority of inbox environments.

**`supported-color-schemes` CSS property:**

```css
:root { supported-color-schemes: light dark; }
```

`supported-color-schemes` is not a valid CSS property — only `color-scheme` is. This line is a no-op. The `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` tag in the `<head>` is also non-standard (it's a CSS-only property), though it is a common email-industry workaround. Not a functional issue, but creates dead code.

**`<title>` tag:** Value is `lululemon` — generic. Some preview pane renderers surface the `<title>` as a tooltip; consider a descriptive value for accessibility.

**Positive notes:** `format-detection` meta disabling auto-detection, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and the three-tier dark mode CSS targeting (Gmail `u+.body`, Outlook.com `[class~="x_"]`, and Outlook Android `\0` hack) are all correctly implemented.
