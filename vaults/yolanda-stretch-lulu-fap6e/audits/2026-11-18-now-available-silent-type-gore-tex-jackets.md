# Now available: Silent Type™ GORE-TEX® jackets
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident product-launch send. The hero is a model in a pale GORE-TEX parka in falling snow, and the "Introducing Silent Type™" headline sits right under it. The copy is short, the product story is clear (soft, quiet, waterproof), and the layout is easy to follow. It looks like a premium outerwear launch, not a promo.
- The email is built for browsing, not buying. There is no price, no offer and no reason to act today beyond "available Nov 20". The three "Shop GORE-TEX®" buttons are split by gender, which is sensible. But the first button sits well below the hero, and the grid captions ("Shop GORE-TEX® Rain Jacket →") are tiny, light-grey links. The "black friday sale: early access" banner at the bottom is the only urgent, discount-flavored element, and it is buried at the very end and points to the app. Compared with the 11/16 Early Access send (8/10), this one is prettier but less persuasive.
- Verdict: a strong brand-building send that will do well with outerwear shoppers. It is mid-range for conversion because it asks nothing of the reader and gives no reason to act now.

## What's working

- The snow-dusted hero and the "Introducing Silent Type™" headline make the launch obvious in about two seconds.
- The tech claims ("WATERPROOF. WINDPROOF. BREATHABLE." and "WEATHERPROOF AND WHISPER-QUIET") sit on the photos, so the reader learns the benefit without reading any copy.
- The Women's and Men's CTAs split the browsing path cleanly, and the dark pill buttons stand out against the white background.
- The close-up face shots with water droplets show weather performance convincingly.
- The red Black Friday banner is a visual jolt that ties this send to the 11/16 and 11/15 early-access emails.

## What's weak

- There is no price, offer or incentive anywhere in the body. "Now available" is the only hook, and it carries no urgency.
- The first CTA button falls below the hero and intro. Many readers will scroll past the model shot with no button in view.
- The per-product links are small grey text with arrows, and the product images give no sense of price, colours or sizes.
- The email is very long, with four stacked image blocks, and repeats the same "Shop GORE-TEX®" label across five links, so they blur together.
- The Black Friday member banner contradicts the launch story. It also shows no concrete discount, and the app-install push crowds the end of the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a "Shop GORE-TEX® jackets" button directly under the headline and make it the dominant CTA. Right now the first one sits below the fold.
- 2. Add one concrete reason to act: a launch-week line like "Free shipping for members" or "Members get early access to Silent Type™", plus a deadline tied to Nov 20.
- 3. Show prices and colour counts in the product captions, and label them as distinct products instead of repeating the same text.
- 4. Cut the length: keep one women's and one men's block, and drop the coat block (the last jacket image) or move it below the banner.
- 5. Move the Black Friday banner up, or make it a real offer. Either lead with it or drop it, because as it stands it competes with the launch.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

  - If the arrows are decorative, they need an explicit `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
  - If they sit inside links, the link needs an accessible name.
- The `<title>` is the generic "lululemon". This is minor and does not affect rendering.
- The CSS uses `font-weight: 380`/`570` and `letter-spacing: -2%`. Many email clients ignore or mishandle variable-weight and percentage letter-spacing values, so they fall back to default weight and spacing. Custom fonts (`Saans-*`, `KHGiga-Light`) do have fallback stacks, so this is a low-severity fidelity risk rather than a defect.
- No issues found with the doctype, `lang`, viewport, dark-mode meta/CSS, or `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` handling.
