# Ready to rally: cool, new tennis styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's second consecutive tennis email — yesterday was "New, fast-drying tennis kits are a must-get," today is "Ready to rally: cool, new tennis styles." The creative follows the same clay-court editorial template: moody photography, ambassador spotlight (Alek Aleman), multiple "Shop Now" links, no price. The heat-dissipation theme ("Keeps you cool," "Let the heat of the moment move through you") is well-constructed and the ambassador integration adds legitimacy. But without a discount, a deadline, or a clear product anchor, this is a browsing prompt rather than a conversion email.
- The render is clean and the brand voice is intact. What's missing is any reason to act *now*. Two near-identical tennis sends back-to-back dilutes urgency — a subscriber who didn't click yesterday has less reason to click today unless the new product or the ambassador is doing enough lift by themselves.
- Verdict: competent but forgettable. The creative is doing its job; the strategy isn't.

## What's working

- Ambassador integration (Alek Aleman) adds real social proof and differentiates this send from yesterday's generic tennis drop.
- The "cool/heat" thematic thread runs end-to-end — subject, preheader, hero copy, product benefit — giving the email a consistent editorial identity.
- Multiple distinct tennis contexts (clay court, indoor, training) signal product breadth without feeling padded.
- "Shop Now" CTAs appear at each content break so there's never a long scroll to a link.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum anywhere visible.

## What's weak

- No price point, discount, or any offer — this is 100% editorial with zero purchase incentive.
- Cadence problem: subscribers received a tennis email yesterday; same clay-court creative, same category, same no-discount structure makes this feel like a duplicate.
- "Shop Now" as repeated CTA copy is generic — none of the buttons are differentiated by the product they link to.
- The bottom fourth section (warm pink/coral tones, different product) feels disconnected from the tennis theme — it reads like a last-minute cross-sell block that wasn't integrated into the editorial concept.
- Subject is on-theme but unmemorable — "cool, new tennis styles" could describe almost any athletic launch in this season.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single product anchor.** Pick one hero SKU — the Alek Aleman wear or a specific fast-drying short — and name it with a price. Even "$88 tennis shorts" in the hero CTA creates a reason to click.
- 2. **Differentiate from yesterday.** Either delay this by 3 days or frame it explicitly as the "men's" complement to yesterday's send. Right now a subscriber sees two identical lululemon tennis emails in their inbox.
- 3. **Make the Alek Aleman section the hero, not a mid-email slot.** The ambassador is the strongest differentiator here; burying him below the fold wastes the social proof.
- 4. **Cut or integrate the bottom cross-sell.** The pink section belongs in a separate send or needs editorial copy that ties it to the tennis theme. As-is it fragments the narrative.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Built to move heat, not collect it`

| 2 | `.guest-voice-sm` CSS | Same: `letter-spacing: -1%` — same invalid unit. |
| 3 | `<head>` styles | No `@font-face` declarations visible for `Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Light`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Medium`, or `KHGiga-Light`. Web font loading in email is only reliable in Apple Mail and some iOS clients. All other clients will fall through to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman` fallbacks. This is expected behavior for email, but confirm fallback stack renders acceptably at target font sizes (h1 at 56px, `.guest-voice-lg` at 56px). |
