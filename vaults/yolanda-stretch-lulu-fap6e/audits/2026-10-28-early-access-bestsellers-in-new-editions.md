# Early Access: Bestsellers in new editions
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's first Membership Early Access send in the recent history, and it's the strongest of the batch on intent. A women's Define Jacket hero opens the email, with "Membership Early Access" set vertically in the top-right corner. The headline "Bestsellers in new editions." names two real products, the Define Jacket and the Everywhere Belt Bag. The "members get first pick" line gives a reason to act now. Earlier sends (Oct 27 "contoured workout sets", Oct 20 "Groove goes further") were pure mood boards, and this one has a hook.
- It still stops short of a real offer. There's no price, no percentage off, and no deadline for the early access window. The two product stories are well shot, with texture close-ups, a back view and colorways, but nothing says how long the access lasts or why members should hurry. Both CTAs are the same "Shop Early Access" button.
- Judgement: a good, targeted member send that's better than the brand's recent 5-6 range. It would be clearly better with one concrete detail, such as a price, an end date or a colorway count.

## What's working

- The "Membership Early Access" vertical lockup and "Members get first pick" give a real exclusivity hook, which the Oct 27 and Oct 20 sends lacked.
- The headline and body copy name specific hero products (Define Jacket, Everywhere Belt Bag), not a vague "new styles".
- The product copy shows detail: "velvety knit or cozy insulation" and "removable strap and security clip".
- The photography is clean and consistent. The close-ups and the back view show fit and texture, and the pink and green belt bag colorways add variety.
- The black pill CTA is clear, and it's repeated after the content so it's reachable at the top and the bottom.

## What's weak

- No price, discount or deadline is visible, so the early access has no urgency and no stated value.
- Both buttons say "Shop Early Access", which is generic, and the second one is identical to the first.
- The images don't show product names, prices or colour options, and no reviews or social proof appear anywhere.
- The membership terms are only in the footer fine print, so it's unclear what early access actually gives.
- The footer links ("Download the App", "Shop What's New", "Find a Store") are large and compete with the main CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add an end date for the early access window and say when it opens to everyone. That's the single change most likely to lift clicks.
- 2. Add price and colour count under each hero, for example "Define Jacket from $128 · 6 colours".
- 3. Make the CTAs product-specific: "Shop the Define Jacket" and "Shop Everywhere Belt Bags".
- 4. Add a star rating or "bestseller" proof line near each product block.
- 5. Shrink the footer links so they don't pull attention from the CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members first: new Define Jackets`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your early access: Define + Belt Bag`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New insulated and velvety-knit styles, open to members before everyone else.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two bestsellers, redesigned. Shop before they open to all.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Three images are flagged for missing alt text.** All three are `SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right`, with src `https://images.lululemon.com/is/image/lululemon/SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right?$pngstandard$`.
  - These are almost certainly decorative arrows beside footer links. The correct markup is an explicit `alt=""`. If the attribute is absent entirely, screen readers may announce the filename or URL, and some clients show raw alt-less placeholders when images are blocked.
  - The QA check can't tell an absent `alt` from `alt=""`, so confirm in the full source.
- **Custom fonts.** The CSS uses `'Saans-Regular'`, `'Saans-Medium'`, `'Saans-Semibold'` and `'KHGiga-Light'`. The fallbacks are `Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` and `'Times New Roman', serif`. The `Helvetica Neue` fallback is unquoted and not followed by `Arial`, so it falls to generic `sans-serif` on Windows and Android. This is minor.
- **Non-standard font-weight values.** `font-weight: 380` and `570` are variable-font values. Most email clients ignore them or round them, so rendering may vary by client.
- **Dark mode.** The `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` meta tags and the `:root` declaration are present. The Gmail, Outlook.com and Outlook Android hacks use `.cta-white\0` and `u + .body` selectors. These are intentional and low risk.
