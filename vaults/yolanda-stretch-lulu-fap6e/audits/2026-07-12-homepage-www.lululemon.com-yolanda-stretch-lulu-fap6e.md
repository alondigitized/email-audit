# Homepage snapshot · www.lululemon.com · 2026-07-12
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so lululemon is leading with tennis right now — which, respect, Madison Appel is cute in that mesh look — but I came here to shop yoga, not serve aces. The brand still looks premium and the hero is clean and energetic, but it's pulling me toward a category I'm not shopping. That said, the 15% off banner at the top immediately caught my eye before anything else.

## What's working

- **The 15% off banner** — that is the first thing I read. I shop We Made Too Much every single week, so I am wired to notice discounts. This lands immediately.
- **"Shop Women's What's New" CTA** — that is exactly where I'd go anyway. No friction to start exploring.
- **Hero photography** — the movement, the hair-flip energy, the mesh texture — it looks like a brand I trust. Clean, modern, editorial-quality shot.
- **Search bar placement** — front and center below the nav, I can pivot to "ABC pants" or "Define jacket" in one tap if the hero doesn't hook me. That matters on mobile.
- **"Shop the Monthly Edit"** — I actually check the Monthly Edit. Seeing it as a secondary CTA feels like they know how I browse.

## What's weak

- **Tennis as the hero when I'm a yoga teacher** — I know lululemon does tennis, but my whole wardrobe is ABC pants and Define jackets. This sends me a message that the homepage isn't really for me right now.
- **No loyalty or Like New mention above fold** — I literally use Like New to trade in old Defines and get credit toward new ones. That program is one of their stickiest differentiators and it's nowhere visible.
- **No free-shipping callout** — I almost always qualify for free shipping, but there's nothing telling me that. A tiny "Free shipping on $150+" strip would reduce any hesitation before I tap.
- **"Gain the Ace Advantage" headline doesn't translate to me** — I get what it means, but it's a tennis pun and I'm not in tennis mode. It doesn't invite me to stay, it tells me who this campaign is for.

## Recommendations

- **Add a secondary hero slide (or swap the sub-copy) for yoga/studio** — even a simple "Studio to Street" callout under the tennis hero acknowledges that yoga shoppers exist and are welcome.
- **Surface the Like New program in the banner or as a loyalty strip** — one line like "Trade in. Get credit. Shop What's New." would stop returning shoppers like me cold.
- **Add a "Free shipping on orders over $X" line to the sticky header or banner** — it's one of the most conversion-positive trust signals and it costs one line of text.
- **Swap one of the two CTAs to something category-specific during a tennis campaign** — "Shop Yoga & Studio" as the second button alongside "Shop Women's What's New" immediately gives me a lane without penalizing tennis shoppers.
