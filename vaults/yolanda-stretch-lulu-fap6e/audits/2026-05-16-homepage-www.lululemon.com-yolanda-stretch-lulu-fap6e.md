# Homepage snapshot · www.lululemon.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Now I'll write the review as Yolanda Stretch.
- I opened lululemon.com and the first thing I got was a full-screen email capture modal — so I can barely even see what campaign they're running right now. The brand comes through immediately (clean typography, that omega logo, the 15% hook is hard to miss), but as someone who already lives in ABC pants and Define jackets, the "New here?" framing feels like lulu is assuming I've never been inside a studio. The homepage is doing a lot of work to grow their list before they bother showing me a single product.

## What's working

- **The top announcement bar** — "Get 15% off regular-priced items. Sign Up" is concise and high-contrast; I see it immediately even with the modal open
- **"Stretch the possibilities" headline** — the yoga pun is on-brand and earns a small smile from me, even when I'm annoyed by the modal
- **Email field + "Email Me 15% Off" CTA** — the button copy is specific and action-oriented; no ambiguity about what happens when I tap it
- **14-day validity window** — the time bound is stated clearly, which is more honest than the typical "limited time" vagueness

## What's weak

- **The full-screen modal is the entire above-the-fold experience** — I cannot see a hero, a featured collection, or a single product. For someone like me who already knows lululemon, this is pure friction
- **"New here?" framing actively alienates loyalists** — I've been shopping lulu since the Align Pant had exactly two colorways; this positioning is a miss for anyone returning
- **Zero loyalty or member pricing callout** — no mention of lululemon membership, points, or returning-customer offers. The reward program exists, so why isn't it on the homepage?
- **The 15% offer has an asterisk stack** — "full-priced items," "exclusions apply," "valid 14 days," "additional terms" — by the time I read all that I've lost confidence in the headline number
- **No category navigation visible** — the hamburger menu is there but no shortcut tiles to Women's / Yoga / We Made Too Much, which is where I'd actually go

## Recommendations

- **Dismiss the modal on second visit** — use a cookie or fingerprint to suppress this for returning shoppers; show a loyalty/member-pricing strip instead
- **Surface the We Made Too Much entry point above the fold** — that rail is a weekly ritual for me and plenty of loyal customers; it deserves a tap target, not a buried nav click
- **Swap the "New here?" hook for a dual-audience banner** — something like "New? Get 15% off. Back again? Check your member rewards." covers both cohorts without alienating the loyalists who drive LTV
- **Add a visible free-shipping threshold** — even a small "free shipping on orders $75+" strip would reduce cart abandonment for someone like me who's always debating whether to add one more pair of socks

## 1. Executive Summary

I opened lululemon.com and the first thing I got was a full-screen email capture modal — so I can barely even see what campaign they're running right now. The brand comes through immediately (clean typography, that omega logo, the 15% hook is hard to miss), but as someone who already lives in ABC pants and Define jackets, the "New here?" framing feels like lulu is assuming I've never been inside a studio. The homepage is doing a lot of work to grow their list before they bother showing me a single product.

## 2. Business Impact Score (1-10)

**5/10**
- + Hero copy pun "Stretch the possibilities" lands for a yoga teacher — that's intentional and I noticed it
- + One concrete offer is absolutely visible above the fold: 15% off both in the top banner and in the modal
- + One unmistakable primary CTA: the black "Email Me 15% Off" button
- + No render bugs — the modal itself is clean, nothing broken
- − Returning-shopper hook: the entire modal is framed "New here?" — I'm not new, I know my size in everything
- − Loyalty/membership benefits: zero mention of the Member Rewards program above the fold
- − Demographic/lifestyle signals: can't see the hero at all, so no way to tell if it speaks to my yoga-teacher life
- − Current campaign/season: entirely obscured by the modal
- − Visual hierarchy: the modal hijacks it — my eye has nowhere useful to go
- − Offer honesty: asterisk on 15% with "exclusions apply" + "valid online for 14 days. Additional terms and conditions apply." is fine-printy

## 3. What's Working

- **The top announcement bar** — "Get 15% off regular-priced items. Sign Up" is concise and high-contrast; I see it immediately even with the modal open
- **"Stretch the possibilities" headline** — the yoga pun is on-brand and earns a small smile from me, even when I'm annoyed by the modal
- **Email field + "Email Me 15% Off" CTA** — the button copy is specific and action-oriented; no ambiguity about what happens when I tap it
- **14-day validity window** — the time bound is stated clearly, which is more honest than the typical "limited time" vagueness

## 4. What's Weak

- **The full-screen modal is the entire above-the-fold experience** — I cannot see a hero, a featured collection, or a single product. For someone like me who already knows lululemon, this is pure friction
- **"New here?" framing actively alienates loyalists** — I've been shopping lulu since the Align Pant had exactly two colorways; this positioning is a miss for anyone returning
- **Zero loyalty or member pricing callout** — no mention of lululemon membership, points, or returning-customer offers. The reward program exists, so why isn't it on the homepage?
- **The 15% offer has an asterisk stack** — "full-priced items," "exclusions apply," "valid 14 days," "additional terms" — by the time I read all that I've lost confidence in the headline number
- **No category navigation visible** — the hamburger menu is there but no shortcut tiles to Women's / Yoga / We Made Too Much, which is where I'd actually go

## 5. Recommendations

- **Dismiss the modal on second visit** — use a cookie or fingerprint to suppress this for returning shoppers; show a loyalty/member-pricing strip instead
- **Surface the We Made Too Much entry point above the fold** — that rail is a weekly ritual for me and plenty of loyal customers; it deserves a tap target, not a buried nav click
- **Swap the "New here?" hook for a dual-audience banner** — something like "New? Get 15% off. Back again? Check your member rewards." covers both cohorts without alienating the loyalists who drive LTV
- **Add a visible free-shipping threshold** — even a small "free shipping on orders $75+" strip would reduce cart abandonment for someone like me who's always debating whether to add one more pair of socks

## 6. Bottom Line

I'd close the modal and keep scrolling — but only because I know what's behind it; if I were a first-timer, this wall of fine print might have sent me straight to Alo.
