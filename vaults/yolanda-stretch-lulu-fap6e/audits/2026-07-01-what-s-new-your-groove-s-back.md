# What’s new: Your Groove’s back
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's July 1 send — landing one day after the June 30 "Summer Sale's hottest picks" catalogue blast. The email leads with the Groove Wide-Leg as hero, but is really three emails in a trench coat: a Groove feature story, a Ruched Tote Bag spotlight, a Summer Sale remainder banner, and a "Coming in hot" new-arrivals teaser. The photography is excellent and the design is clean, but the multi-topic structure means no single message lands with full force.
- The Groove also starred in the June 21 restock email ("This restock's ready to play"), making this the second Groove-centric send in 10 days. For an engaged subscriber, this risks feeling like déjà vu, especially when the Summer Sale section at the bottom offers no specific discount amount — just an orange banner. If July 1 is the day after the Summer Sale peak email, this send should either close the sale urgently (with a real deadline and %) or step aside and let a new story breathe.
- Verdict: solid execution diluted by indecision. The Groove hook is strong; the everything-else freight is not.

## What's working

- Editorial photography is genuinely elevated — the wide-leg silhouette reads clearly against clean white backgrounds.
- "Experience the Groove." hero headline is confident and product-specific, matching the subject line.
- Nulu™ fabric callout ("Buttery soft...stretches above and beyond") gives the product a technical differentiator worth clicking for.
- "Shop Groove" CTA is verb + noun — specific and unambiguous.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The Summer Sale banner appears below the fold with no specific percentage or dollar off — a missed conversion opportunity the day after the sale catalogue.
- The Ruched Tote Bag section is a non-sequitur inside a bottoms-focused email; it dilutes the Groove narrative without earning its place.
- "Coming in hot" introduces yet another story too far down; subscribers who haven't clicked by then are done.
- Groove was also the June 21 hero — two Groove-led emails in 10 days, with the June 30 sale email sandwiched between, creates cadence fatigue.
- No visible price, discount, or urgency anywhere in the Groove sections — there's nothing that makes you act today vs. tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one story.** Either this is a Groove email (cut bag and "Coming in hot," add colorway names and a size urgency callout) or it's a Summer Sale closing push (lead with a % off, deadline, and shop-the-sale CTA). Mixing both weakens both.
- 2. **Surface a specific Summer Sale number.** Even "Up to 40% off" in the body above the orange banner would turn a passive banner into a click driver.
- 3. **Add urgency to the Groove sections.** A line like "Limited sizes remaining" or a colorway name ("new in Heritage Black") gives subscribers a reason to act now rather than at the next email.
- 4. **Rest the Groove after this send.** The product has had three email appearances in 10 days (June 21, this email) — space the next mention out by at least two weeks.
- 5. **Delay or merge this send.** Running on July 1, the day after the Summer Sale peak email, burns goodwill. Batching a Summer Sale close + Groove colorway as one send every 3–4 days is cleaner.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Sale ends soon — Groove is waiting`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — invalid `letter-spacing` values:**
Three classes use percentage values for `letter-spacing`, which is not a valid CSS unit for that property (only length values like `px`/`em` are accepted per spec):

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* invalid */
```

These declarations will be silently ignored by all rendering engines, meaning the intended tight tracking on the serif "guest voice" headlines won't apply. Replace with `em` values (e.g., `-0.02em`).

**Web fonts — fallback chain is sound, but load in email is client-dependent:**
Five custom fonts are referenced (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Light`, `KHGiga-Light`) with no `@font-face` or `<link>` visible in the truncated `<head>`. Gmail strips `<link>` tags and `@import` rules, so custom fonts will not load in Gmail. The fallback stacks (`Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` / `Times New Roman, serif`) are correct and will render safely — no action required unless brand requires serif rendering in Gmail.

**`h4` excluded from heading reset:**
`h1, h2, h3, h5` share a combined reset rule; `h4` has a separate rule using `'Saans-Light'` instead of `'Saans-Regular'`. This appears intentional (stylistic differentiation) but is worth confirming against the design spec since it's easy to introduce `<h4>` elements expecting the standard reset.
