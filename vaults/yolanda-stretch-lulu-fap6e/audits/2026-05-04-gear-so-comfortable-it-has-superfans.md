# Gear so comfortable, it has superfans
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a newcomer onboarding / collection showcase email from lululemon. The hero positions it explicitly for people who are new to the brand, walking through four signature collections (Align™, Steady State, Scuba, Daydrift) with model photography and soft "shop the collection" CTAs. There is no promotional offer — no discount, no urgency, no free shipping threshold. The subject line teases social proof ("superfans") but the email never delivers it: no reviews, no ratings, no customer quotes are visible. The result is a clean, on-brand scroll-through that reads more like a catalog page than a conversion-oriented email.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** The hero image is full-width and commanding. The transition from hero → "Meet our all-stars" → individual collection modules is logical and scannable.
- **Collection-level copy is concise and benefit-led.** The Align™ module calls out Nulu™ fabric and "barely-there" feel. Scuba gets "breathable, warm and cozy." Each description earns its space without over-explaining.
- **Brand consistency.** lululemon's aesthetic — muted palettes, athletic lifestyle models, minimalist type — is maintained end-to-end. No module feels out of place.
- **Membership visibility.** Surfacing the membership section near the bottom adds long-term retention value for a newcomer-targeted send.

## What's weak

- **Subject line promise goes undelivered.** "It has superfans" primes the reader to expect testimonials, reviews, or social proof. None appear in the email body. The disconnect between the subject and the hero ("New to lululemon?") is jarring — these are targeting two different mindsets simultaneously.
- **No offer, no urgency.** This email asks for clicks without giving a reason beyond "we exist and our stuff is nice." Even a soft incentive (free standard shipping on first order, 10% off for newcomers) would convert far better from an acquisition audience.
- **Email is very long.** Four full collection modules + "Keep it local" + membership + app download + Shop What's New stacks a lot of asks. For a newcomer, the cognitive load is high. A single hero collection with one strong CTA would outperform this scattershot approach.
- **"Shop [Collection] clothes →" links are weak CTAs.** Generic arrow-style links read like navigation, not conversion prompts. Specificity ("Find Your Align™ Size" or "Try the Scuba Hoodie") would drive more clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the subject line.** Either add a brief social proof module (star ratings, a pull quote from a real review, a "X million sold" stat) or rewrite the subject to match the actual content.
- 2. **Add a newcomer offer.** A first-purchase incentive (free shipping, a small credit) converts acquisition emails. Surface it in the hero and repeat it in the CTA.
- 3. **Cut the email in half.** Lead with one signature collection (Align™ is the obvious choice — most iconic, most-reviewed). Let newcomers discover the rest through browse.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTAs.** Replace "Shop Align™ clothes →" with something action-specific ("Find Your Fit" or "Explore Align™ Leggings").
- 5. **Separate the audiences.** The "New to lululemon?" hero and the "it has superfans" subject target different people. Segment and send two versions, or pick one angle and commit.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`font-weight: 380` used pervasively — will not render as intended.**
Email clients do not support variable font weight values. The value `380` appears on `p`, `ul`, `ol`, `li`, `h1`–`h5`, `h4`, `.ed-headline`, and `.guest-voice-*`. All will silently snap to `400` (normal) in every major email client (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail). If the design depends on a slightly lighter-than-regular weight, it will not be honored.

```css

h1, h2, h3, h5 { font-weight: 380; }
h4 { font-weight: 380; }
```

`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Light`, and `KHGiga-Light` are referenced throughout but no `@font-face` declarations appear in the visible `<head>`. If they are not embedded later in the document or loaded via a `<link>` (not visible in truncated source), all text will render in the fallback stack (`Helvetica Neue` / `Times New Roman`). Confirm `@font-face` blocks exist in the full source.
