# New yoga styles with more space
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean editorial launch email from lululemon introducing a new "roomier fit" yoga collection. The "Find your space" theme is well-executed — hero imagery, copy, and ambassador quotes all reinforce the same idea without repeating each other. The layout is uncluttered, brand voice is on point, and the photography is genuinely good.
- The catch: there is no offer. No discount, no urgency, no deadline. This is a pure awareness / new-arrivals send. That's a legitimate strategy for lululemon (they rarely discount), but it means this email competes entirely on product appeal and curiosity — no purchase-trigger levers are pulled. For someone already in the lululemon orbit, this is pleasant. For anyone on the fence, there's no hook beyond "new stuff exists."
- The testimonial quotes mid-scroll are a smart trust signal, but the transition from hero to quote to product grid loses a little momentum. The "Shop new styles this week" header implies time pressure that the rest of the email doesn't back up with any deadline.

## What's working

- **"Find your space" theme is cohesive** — headline, hero image (woman on dock, open sky), and body copy all land on the same idea without redundancy.
- **Ambassador/customer quotes are genuine** — the quotes read natural and reinforce the "room to move" value prop directly.
- **Hero photography is strong** — the outdoor yoga shot reads aspirational and earthy, consistent with current lululemon positioning.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text is legible, no overlapping elements.
- **Subject line is under 50 characters** — mobile-friendly at 31 chars, no spam triggers.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer** — no price, no discount, no free shipping threshold. This is purely editorial, which limits purchase motivation.
- **"Shop new styles this week" headline implies urgency that isn't there** — no deadline is given, making it feel like generic filler copy.
- **CTA button copy is generic** — appears to be "Shop Now" or similar; doesn't carry the "space / freedom to move" theme forward.
- **No loyalty or member callout** — lululemon has a membership program; no hint of member-exclusive access or early access to this collection.
- **Product grid below the fold is small and hard to distinguish** — the mosaic of tiny thumbnails doesn't give any individual item room to breathe or stand out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Carry the theme into the CTA button** — "Find Your Space" or "Shop the Looser Fit" beats generic "Shop Now" and reinforces the campaign concept at the moment of decision.
- 2. **Add one anchor product with a name, price, and size range visible** — even a single called-out item ("The Softstreme Relaxed Pant — $128, XS–3X") gives the grid section a reason to exist.
- 3. **Give "this week" a real deadline or drop the phrase entirely** — "New this week" without dates reads as boilerplate; swap for "Just Dropped" or add "available through Sunday."
- 4. **Surface the membership angle** — even one line ("Member early access") would give loyal subscribers a reason to act now instead of bookmarking.
- 5. **Restructure the quote section** — one strong quote, attributed with a photo, will outperform two short quotes in a row with no visual anchor.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`font-weight: 380` and `font-weight: 570`** — Used throughout on `<p>`, `<h1>`–`<h5>`, `.eyebrow`, `.product-callout`, `.ed-headline`. These are variable font axis values, not standard CSS weight keywords. Variable fonts have near-zero support in email clients (supported only in Apple Mail, recent Samsung Mail). Outlook (all versions), Gmail Android, and Yahoo will silently substitute the nearest named weight. The declared fallback fonts (Helvetica Neue, Arial) will render at the browser-defaulted weight, potentially breaking the intended weight hierarchy. This is a known risk if the design depends on weight contrast.

**`background-color: #000001` (`.black` class)** — Intentional workaround; some clients treat `#000000` as transparent. Confirm this is documented in the template system as deliberate, not a data entry error.

**`color: #fdfeff` (`.white`, `.fnt-white`)** and **`background-color: #fdfeff`** — Same pattern; near-white workaround. No contrast issue in practice (delta from `#ffffff` is imperceptible), but should be documented.

**`<title>lululemon`** — Minimal but acceptable. No issue.

**`lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"`** present on `<html>` — Correct.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`** meta present — Correct.

**`format-detection` meta** (`telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no, url=no`) — Present and correct.

**Dark mode targeting** — Three separate targeting methods present (`u + .body` for Gmail, `[class~="x_"]` for Outlook.com, `\0` hack for Outlook Android app). Coverage is thorough.

**Custom `@font-face` declarations** — Not visible in the truncated excerpt. Confirm `@font-face` rules for `Saans-*` and `KHGiga-Light` are present in the full `<head>`. If absent, all text falls back to system fonts immediately with no web-font attempt.
