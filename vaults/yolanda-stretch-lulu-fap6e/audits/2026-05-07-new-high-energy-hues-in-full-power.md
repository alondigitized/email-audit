# New high-energy hues in full power
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-color-launch email from lululemon — bright, visually-driven, product-grid-heavy. The entire email is a visual catalog organized by collection row (Align, Hotty Hot, Clean Lines, Run Yours), anchored by a bold "New colours showing up now" hero. There's no promotional discount; the pitch is pure newness and energy. The Airflow product module at the bottom adds a secondary feature story. Execution is clean and on-brand, but the lack of any price signal and the generic subject line limit its conversion potential.

## What's working

- **Strong visual impact.** The red-dominant hero image is arresting. The grid of model shots across multiple product rows communicates range quickly.
- **Clear CTA above the fold.** "Shop New Colours" button appears in the hero before any scrolling — no ambiguity about the ask.
- **Organized product architecture.** Four named shop-rows (Align, Hotty Hot, Clean Lines, Run Yours) let customers self-sort by category without reading copy.
- **Airflow secondary module** adds product substance and a second entry point for a different buyer mindset.
- **No render issues.** Every image block loads, text is crisp, no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- **No price signal anywhere.** "New colors" is awareness-stage messaging. Without at least an anchor price, members have no reason to click beyond curiosity.
- **Generic subject line.** "New high-energy hues in full power" is evocative but vague — it could describe a Pantone deck or a paint brand.
- **No loyalty / member callout.** lululemon has a members program; none of it is visible here. A missed hook for a high-LTV segment.
- **Copy is thin.** Aside from collection names and "Shop New >" links, there's almost no body copy selling the products — no material call-outs, no fit claims, nothing that would differentiate to a buyer who already owns multiple Aligns.
- **Demographic targeting is neutral.** The models are diverse and fit-focused, but there's no signal this was segmented — it reads as a broadcast.
- **Urgency is absent.** No "limited colorways," no "available now while stocks last" — the email could have been sent any week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or member perk** — even "starting at $68" or "members get early access" gives a reason to click vs. bookmark for later.
- 2. **Tighten the subject line** — name a specific product or color (e.g., "Align in Tango Red is here") to drive sharper curiosity and click-through from the inbox.
- 3. **Add one urgency signal** — "New colorways — quantities limited" in the hero banner or a countdown badge costs nothing and lifts clicks materially.
- 4. **Use at least one material or fit claim** — "four-way stretch," "buttery-soft nulu" — lululemon's brand equity lives in product feel, not just visual; one line per collection row would help.
- 5. **Surface loyalty perks** — if this was sent to members, say so ("As a member, you're seeing this first"). Reinforces the program's value.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — invalid `letter-spacing` percentage values:**

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }
```

`letter-spacing` does not accept `%` values in CSS — only `<length>` units (`px`, `em`) or `normal`. All email clients will silently ignore these declarations. The intended tight tracking on the serif display type (`KHGiga-Light`) will not apply; rendered kerning will be looser than designed.

**Fix:** Convert to `em` equivalents. `-2%` at 56px ≈ `-0.02em`; at 40px ≈ `-0.02em`; `-1%` at 30px ≈ `-0.01em`.
