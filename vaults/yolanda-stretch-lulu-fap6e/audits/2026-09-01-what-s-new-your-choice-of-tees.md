# What’s new: your choice of tees
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's tee category launch — "Four fits, all ease" — a straightforward product-education send walking through four tee silhouettes (Little Ribbed, Classic Jersey, Forever Favourite, Going Steady) with a fit-comfort callout for each. It's clean, consistent, and easy to scan, but it's also entirely generic: white tees on white/neutral backgrounds, no price, no promo, no urgency, nothing that says "email" rather than "catalog page."
- Compared to the brand's recent run — the tennis launch (2026-08-25), the Hannah Berner send (2026-08-26), and the Yoga send (2026-08-29) — this one is visually the most restrained and least distinctive. It reads as pure merchandising content, which is fine for a "what's new" cadence but does nothing to earn a click over just browsing the site later.
- Verdict: well-organized and on-brand, but low urgency and low differentiation. It'll get skimmed, not clicked, unless the recipient is already actively tee-shopping.

## What's working

- Clear repeating pattern — image, short line ("The one that holds close"), one-sentence fit description, text link — makes scanning fast.
- Four distinct silhouettes are genuinely differentiated by copy (slim/ribbed, balanced/organic cotton, relaxed/boxy, oversized/draped), which helps a shopper self-select fit.
- Grid recap near the bottom (2x2 product shots with names) reinforces the four options after the individual sections, useful for a mobile scroller who forgot what came first.
- Consistent minimal styling (muted sage/red/olive backgrounds, all-white tees) keeps the whole email visually cohesive.

## What's weak

- Zero offer — no price, no % off, no member pricing anywhere in the render; this is pure catalog copy with no reason to act now.
- Hero and hero hero-copy is thin: "Four fits, all ease" doesn't hint at what's actually different about the tees until you scroll several screens.
- "Free to float" section (pants) interrupts the tee narrative right before the "Shop new styles this week" grid — a mixed-message closer that dilutes the single-category focus.
- The bottom "Shop new styles this week" grid has small, low-context thumbnails with no labels — it's more filler than a real CTA.
- No social proof, no size/fit guidance beyond one adjective, no urgency of any kind.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a reason to act, not just a reason to browse — add member pricing, a bundle offer ("mix & match tees, 2 for X"), or at minimum a "new colors just dropped" hook in the hero.
- 2. Cut or move the "Free to float" pants section — it breaks the single-category promise of the subject line and dilutes focus; save it for its own send or move it after "Shop new styles this week."
- 3. Add a one-line fit-finder CTA near the top ("Not sure which fit? Take our 30-second quiz") — the entire email is structured around fit differentiation, so make that discovery actionable, not just descriptive.
- 4. Label the bottom grid thumbnails or drop it — an unlabeled 6-pack of unrelated products at the very end reads as clutter, not merchandising.
- 5. Sharpen the subject/hero copy to name the fit spectrum up front, since that's the actual content of the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4 tee fits. Find yours.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Slim, relaxed, or oversized — new tees in every fit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From ribbed and slim to boxy and oversized — breathable cotton, every way you wear it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New tee drop: four fits, zero guesswork.`
