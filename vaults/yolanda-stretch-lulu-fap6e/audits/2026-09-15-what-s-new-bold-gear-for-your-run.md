# What's new: bold gear for your run
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's running-category push for the Seamless Stripe Tank and Short, and it's a solid step up from the moody editorial sends dominating this brand's recent cadence (2026-09-10 "fall 2026 is here" at 5/10, 2026-09-08 Megan Roup Edit at 5/10). It leads with a clear hero product, a real athlete quote for social proof, and a genuinely long, well-organized scroll covering three distinct product stories (Seamless Stripe, half-zip fleece, new styles grid) before closing on the Megan Roup Edit and an Amex cross-sell.
- The problem is length and focus: by the time a reader hits "Shop new styles this week" they've already scrolled past two full product stories, and the email never resolves into one unmistakable CTA. It reads more like a category lookbook than a single-offer promotional email — no price, no discount, no urgency anywhere in the render.
- Net: strong visual execution and a genuine athlete testimonial lift this above lululemon's recent editorial-heavy sends, but the lack of any offer and the sprawling structure cap how hard this pulls a click.

## What's working

- Clean hero shot of a model mid-stretch with clear "FALL 2026" eyebrow and specific product name (Seamless Stripe Tank and Short) in the headline.
- Colleen Quigley quote ("It felt perfectly airy") with named title (Track & Field Athlete, Champion, Mentor) adds credible social proof rarely seen in this brand's other recent sends.
- Individual product-detail shots (tank top, short) with direct "Shop [Product]" links let the reader click a specific SKU, not just a category page.
- "New styles this week" grid is a nice merchandising touch that signals freshness/newness without needing a discount hook.
- No visible render bugs — images, type, and spacing are all clean.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing, discount, or promo mechanic anywhere in the visible email — it's pure lookbook, no reason to act today.
- No urgency or time-bound framing at all.
- The email covers three separate product moments (Seamless Stripe, half-zip fleece, new arrivals) plus a Megan Roup Edit teaser and Amex cross-sell — too many competing CTAs for one send.
- Subject says "bold gear for your run" but a large chunk of the body (half-zip fleece, "rest day" styling) isn't run-specific, which undercuts the promised focus.
- The Amex module at the bottom is a jarring tonal shift after four product stories — reader fatigue is likely by then.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one hero story (Seamless Stripe running kit) and move the fleece/new-styles/Megan Roup content into a follow-up send — this brand already has the cadence to spread stories across separate emails (see 2026-09-06 Polartec fleece as its own dedicated send).
- 2. Add a concrete offer or at minimum member-exclusive framing near the top — right now there's no reason to act now versus next week.
- 3. Move the Colleen Quigley quote higher, directly under the hero — it's the strongest differentiator in this email and is currently buried below the fold.
- 4. Tighten the subject to match the body's true focus, or trim the body to match the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Seamless Stripe running kit, tested by Colleen Quigley`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Run bold. Meet the Seamless Stripe collection.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"It felt perfectly airy" — see why athletes love the new Stripe Tank and Short.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh from Fall 2026: lightweight run gear built for movement.`
- 5. Drop or relocate the Amex module — it dilutes the running-gear narrative and should live in a dedicated finance-offer send.
