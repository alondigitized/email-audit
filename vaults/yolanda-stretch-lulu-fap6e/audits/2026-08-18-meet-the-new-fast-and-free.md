# Meet the new Fast and Free™
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Fast and Free™ running gear launch — a full category takeover (shorts, tops, tights, bras, accessories, shoes) built around motion-blur action photography rather than a single hero product story. The hero image is genuinely strong: two runners mid-stride against a blurred background, magenta and navy pops against neutral tones, and it's the most kinetic, "get outside" creative in the recent run — a step up from the static studio shots of the July 22 Everyday Cotton-Blend Tee send and the locker-room stillness of the Steady State SuperLoft launch.
- Where it falls down is structure: this is a 2x2 category grid (8 product tiles) stacked under a second "Fresh gear to get you out the door." section with 4 more images and a single generic "Shop Now →" link — no distinct CTA per item, no price, no member benefit surfaced despite the footer disclaimer referencing Membership. It reads as a catalog scroll, not a single persuasive narrative, which will cost it on mobile scroll fatigue even though the top-of-fold hero and shorts CTAs are excellent.
- Net: strong open trigger (recognizable sender, punchy subject, gorgeous hero), average click follow-through once you're past the first CTA block because the second half of the email dilutes focus with an unexplained "Fresh gear" grid that duplicates the same category (shorts, tanks, tights) without adding new information.

## What's working

- Hero image is dynamic and on-brand — motion blur + two diverse runners sells "Fast and Free" better than static product-on-white ever could.
- Two immediate, unambiguous CTAs ("Shop Women's Run" / "Shop Men's Run") sit right under the headline, above the fold — no scrolling needed to act.
- Clean gender-paired grid (Women's/Men's Shorts, Tops) makes it easy to jump straight to your category.
- No render bugs — images load, text is crisp, no overlap or broken layout.
- Consistent black/white/magenta-navy palette carries through the whole email, reinforcing brand recognition.

## What's weak

- The second "Fresh gear to get you out the door." section repeats shorts/tank/jacket imagery already implied by the first grid, with only one vague "Shop Now →" link for four products — no idea what's actually new here.
- Zero pricing, discount, or promo signal anywhere — this is pure editorial, consistent with the brand's recent run, but there's no urgency lever at all.
- No product names under the "Fresh gear" tiles (unlike the first grid, which at least labels each tile) — makes those four images unclickable-feeling.
- Membership disclaimer sits buried in the footer in tiny type, but nothing in the body actually surfaces a member perk or price — feels like legal boilerplate rather than a real benefit.
- Long vertical scroll (12+ product images) with no differentiation in why "Fast and Free" specifically outperforms other run lines — the copy never explains the actual product benefit (fabric, fit, weight).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "Fresh gear to get you out the door." section or merge it into the main grid with labeled links — right now it's dead weight that dilutes the single clear CTA the top of the email earns.
- 2. Add one concrete product detail (fabric tech, weight, or a fit callout like "four-way stretch") near the hero copy — "Bold new running styles let everything else fade away" is mood, not information a runner can act on.
- 3. Label every tile with a specific link, not a shared "Shop Now →" — each of the four "Fresh gear" images should get its own product name link like the first grid has.
- 4. Introduce a price anchor or member-exclusive early access line to give Membership shoppers a reason to click now instead of "someday."
