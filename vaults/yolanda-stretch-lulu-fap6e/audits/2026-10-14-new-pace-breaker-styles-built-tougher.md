# New Pace Breaker styles, built tougher
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-shot men's running send for the Pace Breaker line. It's the only one of the last four lululemon emails (Oct 6, 7, 13 and today) that names a real fabric benefit. The hero is a profile shot of a runner against a blue-hour sky, and the headline "Pace Breaker's new strength." is clean. The copy "Now in water-resistant Glyde™ fabric, our do-it-all short and jacket just got tougher" tells me what changed and why I'd care. It reads as product news, not just a mood board.
- The send still has no price, no promo and no deadline. Almost all the weight sits on the photography. The lower half is a six-tile mood grid and a six-tile "Shop the latest drop" grid, with no product names or prices. The only Pace Breaker-specific link is a tiny "Shop Pace Breaker →" text link under the second hero. The main buttons just say "Shop What's New." This is a good brand email that's soft on conversion, and it lands where most of this brand's recent sends do.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The runner is silhouetted against a dusk sky, and the eye lands on it first.
- The Glyde™ water-resistant callout is a concrete product reason to click, which Oct 13's "Steady State's stripes" and Oct 6's puffers did not offer.
- The layout is clear: hero, headline, button, 2x2 grid, second hero, then the product grid. Nothing overlaps, no images are broken, and the render is clean.
- The imagery is consistent. The same models, location, gear and colour palette (slate, stone, neon yellow shoes) run through every frame.
- The fall season fits, with a weather-resistant layer for cooler running.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or urgency anywhere. Nothing gives the reader a reason to click today.
- The CTAs are generic. "Shop What's New" appears twice and "See all" once. The Pace Breaker link is small grey text that's easy to miss.
- The two grids have no product names, prices or colour or size cues, so the reader can't tell what they're clicking.
- The creative is all men's and all one body type. If the recipient isn't in the men's running segment, nothing here speaks to them.
- The send follows closely after Oct 13, Oct 7 and Oct 6, all of them mood-led "What's new" emails. That's a lot of editorial with little commerce.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a specific product and price under the first hero, for example "Pace Breaker Lined Short, $98." Then add a button reading "Shop Pace Breaker" in place of "Shop What's New."
- 2. Add product names and prices under every tile in "Shop the latest drop." Without them the grid is decoration.
- 3. Make the Glyde benefit more prominent, for example a short "Water-resistant. Wind-ready. Still light." strip with a real detail such as DWR or weight.
- 4. Add one loyalty or urgency hook, such as early access for members or a "first to run in it" message. The member fine print is only at the bottom.
- 5. Space out the mood-only sends, or alternate them with a send that carries a clear commercial hook.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Run through drizzle: Pace Breaker in Glyde™`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The do-it-all short and jacket, now water-resistant and wear-tested.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Glyde™ fabric, same light feel. Shop the short and jacket.`
