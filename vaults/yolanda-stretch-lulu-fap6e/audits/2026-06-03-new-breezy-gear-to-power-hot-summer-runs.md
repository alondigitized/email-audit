# New, breezy gear to power hot summer runs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's summer running gear launch — a tight, editorial email anchored on the "Outpace the heat." hero and structured around three distinct product stories: the Textured Mesh Running shirt (women's), the Fast and Free Wavy Speed Tank (men's), and the Split Shift shoe. It's the brand's most running-specific send in recent memory, which earns it genuine relevance for anyone who opened the swim trunk and Breezily Shorts emails this past week with polite indifference.
- The format is identical to every other category launch lululemon has run this month — full-bleed dark editorial hero, brief functional copy per section, minimal CTA, footer app promo. That template works, but it's starting to feel like wallpaper. There's no discount, no urgency, and no social proof, so the email's entire job is to get someone excited enough about new running technology to click through at full price. The product copy does enough of that work — mesh, ventilation, forefoot rocker cushioning — but only barely.
- Compared to the May 30 restock email (the strongest recent send), this one lands softer. Restocks create urgency by nature; a new colorway launch does not. Still, this is a clean, competent send that does what it needs to do for a committed lululemon runner.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is punchy and seasonal.** "Outpace the heat." is the best two-word copy in this email — direct, timely for June, and sets the thematic tone before anything else loads.
- **Three-product structure keeps the scroll moving.** Womens / Mens / Shoes as separate sections avoids the trap of a single hero SKU that loses half the audience immediately.
- **Functional copy earns its place.** "Open-knit zones... help you stay dry and focused" and "forefoot rocker" are specific enough to give a runner a reason to click — not just vibes.
- **Section-level CTAs are appropriately segmented.** "Shop Womens Run >" and "Shop Men's Run >" direct audiences rather than dumping everyone on the homepage.
- **Clean render, zero visual bugs.** Every image loaded, no broken layout, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No urgency and no offer — the email has nothing to close with.** Every other top-performing send in this brand's recent history had either a restock hook, a seasonal deadline (Father's Day), or a sale. New arrivals at full price need more pull.
- **"Shop What's New" hero CTA is generic.** For a running-specific launch email, a button that says "Shop Running" or "Shop the Launch" would have more signal.
- **Cadence is getting heavy.** Seven lululemon emails in 13 days — this is the moment subscribers start tuning out. The subject does nothing to differentiate itself from last week's sends.
- **No social proof anywhere.** A star rating or a single quote ("Best running shorts I've owned") on even one product would give a fence-sitter a reason to act.
- **The "Shop new styles this week" product grid is too vague.** The section headline promises breadth but gives the reader no reason to care about any individual item in it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete hook to the hero** — even a soft one. "Fresh drop: built for heat" with a "Limited first run" or "Just landed" label creates the impression of scarcity without requiring an actual sale.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** Replace "Shop What's New" → "Shop the Running Drop" or "Shop New Run Gear." Same click destination, triple the signal.
- 3. **Pull one runner review into the copy.** Even a single attributed quote ("I ran a 10K in 90° heat in these — game changer") in the Textured Mesh section turns a feature into a verdict.
- 4. **Trim or kill the "Shop new styles this week" grid.** It dilutes the running focus and doesn't tie back to the "Outpace the heat" theme. Either make it running-only or cut it.
- 5. **Acknowledge cadence in the subject.** This is the seventh email in two weeks. A subject that signals *why this one is different* ("The running kit we built for June heat") earns the open more than a continuation of the same formula.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Off-spec color values — low risk, intentional:**
- `.black` = `#000001` (not `#000000`) and `.white` = `#fdfeff` (not `#ffffff`) — a known near-black/near-white trick to suppress dark mode color inversion in some clients. Functionally acceptable, but the `#fdfeff`-on-`#000001` pairing should be verified against WCAG AA (4.5:1 minimum); the 1-step offset does not meaningfully affect contrast, but the pair should be spot-checked.

**Heading selector inconsistency:**
- `h1, h2, h3, h5` are grouped in one rule; `h4` is styled separately with `Saans-Light` fallback. `h5` also picks up the grouped rule, so if any `<h5>` element is rendered, it inherits `letter-spacing: -1px` from the group — which is likely intended, but the grouping excludes `h4` without comment.

**Custom font fallback chain — acceptable:**
- `'Saans-Regular', Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` and `'KHGiga-Light', 'Times New Roman', serif` are correctly defined. Since most email clients block web fonts, rendering will fall back to system fonts; no functional issue.
