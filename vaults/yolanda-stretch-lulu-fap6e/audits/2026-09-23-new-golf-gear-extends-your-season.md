# New golf gear extends your season
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's fall golf category push, anchored by athlete talent Connor Bedard (NHL player, oddly, not a golfer) and Roger Steele ("Golf Advocate"). It's a men's-led golf gear scroll — rugby shirts, water-repellent pants, polos, jackets — dressed up as celebrity endorsement, then it tails off into the same generic "shop new styles this week" grid and Cotton-Blend Crew callback this brand has run every single send for weeks.
- The category itself is a departure from the brand's recent moody-studio/dance/running cadence (2026-09-22 Dance Studio Pants, 2026-09-16 Cotton-Blend Crew, 2026-09-15 running gear) — golf-as-fall-extension is at least a fresh angle. But execution is the same formula: hero athlete shot, product grid, "shop new styles," then a bolted-on Amex ad and a second unrelated Cotton-Blend Crew plug at the bottom. It reads like two emails stapled together.
- For a golf-focused subscriber this could land well; for anyone else on this list, half the email (Amex card pitch, repeated crewneck plug) is dead weight. Middle-of-the-road execution on a slightly better hook than recent sends — call it a 5.5, rounding to 5.

## What's working

- Clear seasonal hook ("extends your season") gives fall relevance a concrete reason to exist, unlike vague "new arrivals" copy.
- Athlete-led hero (Connor Bedard) with bold "Game for another round" headline creates a distinct visual identity vs. the brand's recent editorial-moody sends.
- Product shots are clean, well-lit, and each item is individually labeled and linked (Golf Pant, Sport Polo, Rugby Shirt, Golf Jacket).
- Two named athlete/advocate call-outs (Bedard, Roger Steele) add a credibility layer most of this brand's recent sends skip.

## What's weak

- Connor Bedard is a professional hockey player, not a golfer — the credibility hook is confusing and undercuts the "golf gear" premise on inspection.
- The email sprawls into unrelated content: a full "shop new styles this week" grid, then a second "Softness, doubled up" Cotton-Blend Crew section (literally the hero of the 2026-09-16 send), then an Amex card pitch — none of it golf-related.
- No visible price, discount, or offer anywhere in the entire scroll — pure lookbook, zero purchase incentive.
- Cadence fatigue: this brand has sent a near-identical "hero look + shop grid + see all" structure in every recent send (Dance Studio Pants, personalized picks, Cotton-Blend Crew, running gear) — nothing here breaks that pattern structurally, just the category skin.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email at "Shop new styles this week" — drop the Cotton-Blend Crew repeat and Amex ad entirely; a golf-gear email diluted by three unrelated pitches loses whoever opened for golf specifically.
- 2. Swap or contextualize the Bedard placement — either lead with Roger Steele (an actual golf advocate) or add a line explaining why a hockey player is fronting golf gear, since it reads as a mismatched celebrity slot otherwise.
- 3. Add one concrete offer — even a modest "new colors, new fits" isn't enough; if there's no discount, at least surface member early-access or a specific price point on a hero item.
- 4. Trim the product density — two multi-item collage sections back-to-back (4 pieces, then 6 more in the grid) before any CTA variety is a lot of scrolling with no offer payoff.
