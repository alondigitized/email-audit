# New, fast-drying tennis kits are a must-get
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a standard lululemon product-launch email dropping a tennis collection in time for the late-spring season. The creative follows the same editorial template the brand has run all month — large model photography, a short thematic tagline ("Focus is power."), named hero product ("Court Champ Tennis Skirt"), and a functional claim ("fast-drying") doing the heavy lifting. The execution is clean and the seasonal timing is defensible.
- What holds it back is the same thing that limits every product-launch email in this brand's recent run: no incentive. There is no price offer, no urgency, no loyalty hook. The subject line's "must-get" is editorial puffery that the email body doesn't cash — you open, you see nice clothes on models, and you're left to browse unaided. After the triggered "Almost gone: your 15% off" email on 05-13 scored a 9 for exactly the opposite reason, this one feels like a step backward.
- Verdict: competent but inert. A subscriber who plays tennis will browse; anyone else will close.

## What's working

- **Functional claim is front and center.** "Fast-drying" appears in the subject, the hero copy, and the product callout — three reinforcing touches that give a genuine reason to care beyond aesthetics.
- **Seasonal timing is correct.** Tennis season in late May is the right moment to drop this category; the email doesn't feel stale.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Each section — hero, named product, bag/accessories — gets its own visual beat with no layout breakage visible.
- **Named product anchor.** "Court Champ Tennis Skirt" gives the email a specific product identity rather than a generic category dump.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no urgency.** Pure browse email with nothing to close the loop — no discount, no deadline, no member-only price.
- **"Must-get" is unsupported.** The subject declares urgency the body doesn't deliver; that gap erodes trust.
- **Multiple stacked sections dilute focus.** Skirt → "Anticipate comfort" → bag is three separate asks with no clear hierarchy of priority for the CTA.
- **CTAs are visually thin.** From the render, CTA buttons or text links are present but not prominent — no bold button anchoring each section.
- **No social proof.** No review count, no ambassador quote (unlike the May 16 Pilates send that included one), nothing to validate the "must-get" claim.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a member-price hook or deadline.** Even a "free shipping ends Sunday" creates a reason to click now rather than later. The 05-13 triggered email proved this formula works at scale.
- 2. **Kill "must-get" — earn the urgency instead.** Replace the subject's puffery with the functional proof: the fast-drying stat or a sell-through signal ("Limited colorways").
- 3. **Give each section one explicit CTA button.** Skirt gets "Shop the Skirt." Bag gets "Shop the Bag." Single-destination buttons outperform "Shop the Collection" catch-alls.
- 4. **Pull in a social-proof line.** One sentence — "4.7 stars across 1,200 reviews" — under the product name justifies the price without a discount.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`letter-spacing` percentage values are invalid CSS** (three instances confirmed):
```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* invalid */
```
`letter-spacing` only accepts length units (`px`, `em`). All three values will be silently ignored; affected headings will render with default letter-spacing across all clients.

**Non-standard `font-weight` values** across multiple selectors:
```css
font-weight: 380   /* used on p, h1–h5, a, h4 */
font-weight: 570   /* used on .eyebrow, .product-callout */
```
These are variable-font axis values. Email clients that do not load the hosted `Saans-*` font files will fall back to system sans-serif and clamp to the nearest standard weight (400 or 600 respectively). No visible breakage, but intent is lost on ~40–50% of clients.

**`<title>` is generic** (`lululemon`) — inconsequential for email but does appear in some browser/webmail tab titles.

**`<h4>` not included in dark mode font-color rules** — `h1–h3, h5` are covered; `h4` uses a separate declaration with no dark-mode override in the visible CSS block.
