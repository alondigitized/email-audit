# New swim trunks are comfortable from the jump
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's swim trunk launch email — the day after yesterday's Breezily Shorts send (05-26). The format is identical to every other category-launch the brand has run this month: large editorial hero, minimal copy, single CTA, secondary grid below. The black-and-white hero photograph is visually arresting and the body copy ("Summer won't wait, and with ultra fast-drying swim trunks—neither will you") lands cleanly — urgency without oversell. Fabric callouts mid-scroll (water-repellent, mesh liner, fast-drying) do real work substantiating the claim.
- The problem is drift. After the strong swim trunk hero and its CTA, the email breaks into a "Shop new styles this week" module showing what appear to be dark hoodies or jackets — nothing that reads as swim or summer. Then a third "Keeps your cool" section follows. Three distinct product pitches in one email works against the focused category-launch formula that has driven this brand's better recent scores.
- No discount, no member pricing, no deadline. This is a full-price product introduction and it reads like one. Perfectly competent, but the lack of any promotional hook caps how much lift it can deliver.

## What's working

- Hero copy is punchy and rhythm-forward — the double meaning of "from the jump" earns its place without feeling forced.
- "Shop Swim Trunks" CTA is specific, visible, and above the fold — verb + noun exactly right.
- Fabric story (water-repellent, mesh liner, fast-drying) appears at the right scroll depth to reward the opener without cluttering the hero.
- Mid-email product imagery shows multiple colorways flat-laid, giving the shopper a quick palette read.
- No render bugs: layout is clean, images load, no overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- "Shop new styles this week" module showing what appear to be dark outerwear items directly undercuts the swim/summer framing and splits attention at the critical conversion moment.
- Subject line pun ("from the jump") is too soft — it reads as generic comfort copy unless you already know the diving/pool context.
- Back-to-back bottom-half sends (Breezily Shorts yesterday, swim trunks today) risks inbox fatigue in the bottom-wear segment; a subscriber who opened yesterday has mild déjà vu.
- No pricing, deadline, or urgency signal anywhere in the email — nothing makes this week's trunks feel different from last week's.
- Hero is black-and-white, which looks premium but hides the colorways. Swim trunk color is a primary purchase driver.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the "Shop new styles this week" module entirely.** One category per email. The swim trunk focus is the email's strength — don't trade it for incremental click surface on unrelated items.
- 2. **Add color to the hero.** Move the B&W shot to a secondary position; lead with the product in its most appealing colorway. Swim purchases are highly color-motivated.
- 3. **Add a member-price hook or lightweight urgency.** Even "Members get early access" or a limited colorway angle gives the reader a reason to act now rather than bookmark.
- 4. **Sharpen the subject line.** "Comfortable" is a weak lead word that every brand uses.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fast-dry swim trunks — summer's not waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New swim trunks: from pool to wherever`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Water-repellent, mesh-lined, and built for all-day wear`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `3 new colorways. Fast-drying. Ready before you are.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — invalid `letter-spacing` percentage values:**

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* line ~48 */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }
```

`letter-spacing` only accepts `<length>` values (`em`, `px`, etc.) — percentages are invalid CSS and silently ignored by all email clients. These three classes will render with default (0) letter-spacing instead of the intended tight tracking. Fix: convert to `em` equivalents (e.g., `-0.02em`).

**Dark mode — no issues:** Gmail (`u + .body`), Outlook.com (`[class~="x_*"]`), and Outlook Android (`.cta-white\0`) are all targeted. Off-black `#000001` and off-white `#fdfeff` are intentional dark-mode inversion workarounds, not color errors.

**Custom fonts — partial concern:** `Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Light`, `KHGiga-Light` are referenced throughout but no `@font-face` declarations appear in the visible CSS. If `@font-face` blocks are absent from the full source, these fonts will never load in any client. Fallback stacks (`Helvetica Neue`, `Arial`, `Times New Roman`) are present and safe. Verify `@font-face` blocks exist later in the `<head>` or in a linked stylesheet.

**Heading hierarchy — minor:** `h1, h2, h3, h5` are grouped in one CSS rule; `h4` is styled separately with a lighter weight. `h5` is styled before `h4` in source order. If templates use `h4` and `h5` interchangeably for visual weight, confirm semantic heading order is not inverted in rendered modules.

**Alt text — cannot audit:** HTML truncated before image tags. Verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes; decorative spacers should use `alt=""`.
