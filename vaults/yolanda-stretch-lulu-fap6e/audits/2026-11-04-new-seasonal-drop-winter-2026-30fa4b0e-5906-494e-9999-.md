# New seasonal drop: Winter 2026.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure mood send. The hero is a man and a woman running down a dirt road under a heavy, overcast sky, with "winter" and "2026" overlaid in red at the left and right edges. The photography is cohesive and cold-toned, and it feels like a real seasonal brand moment. But there is no offer, no named product, no price, and no reason to click today.
- Below the hero, a mosaic of five or six lifestyle shots runs down the page, with no captions or product callouts. The only copy is "Mindful designs invite a more intentional season of movement." Two identical "Shop Winter 2026" buttons sit at the top and bottom of the mosaic. A red "Give the feeling" gift block follows. The email looks good and sells nothing.
- Judgement: a competent brand-awareness piece. Compared with the Oct 28 "Early Access" send (7/10), which had a membership hook, this one has no incentive. It is the third mood-only send in a row after Nov 3 (reflective running) and Oct 27 (Wunder Train). A subscriber would see a pattern of beautiful emails with nothing to act on.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. Two runners on a bleak road make a cohesive winter story, and the red "winter 2026" overlay ties it to the brand colour.
- The photography is consistent across the scroll, with muted olive, brown and grey tones and real poses (running, lounging, seated).
- The coral CTA buttons contrast well against the dark and white backgrounds and are easy to find.
- The gift block uses a red panel to break up the monotone mosaic and adds a second, seasonally relevant message.
- The footer links (Download the App, Shop What's New, Find a Store) are tidy and readable.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, product name or fabric benefit anywhere. "Mindful designs invite a more intentional season of movement" is brand-speak and says nothing concrete.
- The mosaic is about 1,400px of untitled images with no product labels, so nothing tells me what to buy.
- Both CTAs read "Shop Winter 2026", which is generic and points at a whole season. The second one repeats the first.
- The gift block's "Wish list, fulfilled. Best-ever reactions, received." is cute, but the "Explore the Gift Guide →" link is small and low-contrast on the red panel.
- The footer's membership fine print appears with no visible member benefit in the body, so it reads as boilerplate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to act, such as a hero product with a named benefit (for example "Wunder Puff, windproof to -10°C"), a price, or a member perk. This would lift the email more than anything else.
- 2. Label the mosaic. Give each image a product name and price, or link each tile to its own product page.
- 3. Make the CTAs specific: "Shop men's winter running" and "Shop women's layers", not two identical "Shop Winter 2026" buttons.
- 4. Give the Gift Guide link button treatment and a date hook, such as "Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery."
- 5. Cut the hero copy to a line that names a product or benefit.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your winter run kit, built for the cold`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wind-ready shells and warm layers, plus our holiday gift guide.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New winter styles for the run, the commute and the couch.`

## Technical Audit

- The QA report lists no broken links, but that only covers links that were probed.
- No other link issues can be confirmed from the truncated source.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Three instances of `SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right` have no alt text. The `src` is `https://images.lululemon.com/is/image/lululemon/SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right?$pngstandard$`.
- If the arrows are decorative, they should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip them. If they sit inside links, the link needs an accessible name.
- Fixing this is low effort.
- Positives: `lang="en"` is set, `user-scalable=yes` allows zoom, and `img{display:block;border:0}` is applied.
- The doctype is HTML 4.01 Transitional with VML/Office namespaces, which is normal for Outlook compatibility. It is not an issue.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible source shows no unresolved merge tokens such as `%%...%%`, `{{...}}` or `${...}`. The source was truncated, so the rest of the body is unchecked.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` was not found.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) was not found.
- `Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
- The QA output itself attributes all three to the AgentMail relay possibly not capturing or preserving headers. The sender, `hello@e.lululemon.com`, is a dedicated sending subdomain, which is good practice.
- None of these is a confirmed sender-side defect. Verify the headers against the raw message at the source or in a seed inbox before treating them as real.
- The footer's unsubscribe link and physical address were not in the truncated HTML, so a CAN-SPAM body check was not possible.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity**
No UTM or landing-page analysis was possible. The 25 tracked links were skipped, so their final URLs and campaign parameters are unknown. Nothing in the data shows a mismatch for the "Winter 2026" seasonal drop.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add `alt=""` (decorative) or descriptive alt text to the three footer arrow images.
2. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` in the raw original message, bypassing the relay. If they're absent there, add them. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
3. Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment from a seed inbox's original headers rather than the relay copy.
4. Resolve the 25 tracking redirects with a redirect-following probe. Confirm they land on live pages and carry consistent UTM parameters.
5. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to cover the unsubscribe link, the physical address and the merge tokens.
