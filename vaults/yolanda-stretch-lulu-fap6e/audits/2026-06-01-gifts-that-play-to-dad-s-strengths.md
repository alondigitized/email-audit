# Gifts that play to Dad’s strengths
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Father's Day gift guide, landing June 1 — two weeks out from the holiday, which is the right window to capture gift consideration. The hero anchors on an NBA father-son narrative (Jordan & Dell Oakwood), a thoughtful emotional device that separates this from the generic "gifts for Dad" pile. The product grid executes well: organized by activity category (casual, run, golf, train), with a "Gifts Under $60" secondary section that acknowledges the shopper's primary question.
- The central miss is the complete absence of any promotional offer. Every competitor will be running Father's Day discounts, and lululemon sends a pure editorial guide with no price hook — no free shipping, no percent off, no deadline. Compare this to the May 30 restock email (9/10) which drove urgency through scarcity on a single SKU; this email has neither scarcity nor savings.
- Verdict: a polished, on-brand seasonal send that earns opens on name recognition and timing, but doesn't earn clicks without a reason to buy today over next week.

## What's working

- The Jordan & Dell Oakwood father-son athlete framing gives the email genuine emotional specificity — it's about *connection*, not just product
- Category organization (casual / run / golf / train) does the mental work for the shopper; no guessing what to look at first
- "Gifts Under $60" price bucket directly answers the gift-buyer's main question without requiring a discount
- Visual hierarchy is clean and unambiguous: hero → category grid → price buckets → gift cards; reading order never stalls
- June 1 timing is correct — early enough for decision-making, late enough to feel urgent

## What's weak

- No promotional offer anywhere — no free shipping, no percent off, no gift-with-purchase; the email gives a gift-shopper no competitive reason to choose lululemon now
- Category grid links to sections, not individual products — no prices visible, no hero SKU called out, which makes the grid feel like a site navigation shortcut rather than a shoppable email
- Zero urgency or delivery guarantee copy — "Order by June X for guaranteed delivery" is absent and its absence will cost conversions in the final week
- Subject sets up a "strengths" hook that the email doesn't pay off — the body is about emotional connection and category shopping, not any meaningful mapping to personal strengths

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-bounded free shipping offer** — "Free shipping on all orders through June 14" is the single change that converts a gift-guide browse into a purchase decision and gives the subject/preheader something concrete to carry
- 2. **Activate the athlete story into a shoppable moment** — if Jordan & Dell Oakwood are a real ambassador pairing, show the specific items they're wearing with a direct PDP link; right now the storytelling is decoration
- 3. **Add delivery deadline below the primary CTA** — "Order by June 12 for Father's Day delivery" would lift late-window sends materially; this is a standard ask lululemon is leaving on the table
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to deliver on "strengths" or go more concrete** — the current subject creates mild curiosity but the email doesn't fulfill the implied premise

- **Subject Alt B:** `For the dad who never skips a workout`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Run, golf, train — shop by his sport. Order by June 12 for delivery.`
