# Gifts for Dad, done right
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's second Father's Day email in nine days — the June 1 send ("Gifts that play to Dad's strengths") got there first, and this one lands on June 10 with Jordan and Cali Clarkson as the editorial anchors. The format is identical to the June 1 send: large editorial hero, narrative section headers, product grid, product story. That repetition is the single biggest liability. A subscriber who opened June 1 will see the exact same playbook — and more damningly, the same headline copy ("Gifts that play to Dad's strengths") appears *inside* this email as a section header, nearly word-for-word recycling the previous subject line.
- The celebrity pairing adds something new: the Clarksons give this a different face than the Dell/Jordan Oakwood angle from June 1, and "Father's Day in the New Parent Bag" is a fresh product angle (travel/accessories vs. apparel-forward June 1). But lululemon buried it. That New Parent Bag section is the most differentiated thing in the email and it lives at the bottom.
- Bottom line: competent execution on a stale brief. The email would perform better as a single, well-targeted send rather than the second hit in a 9-day Father's Day drum roll.

## What's working

- **Celebrity tie-in is well-cast.** Jordan and Cali Clarkson give the email a specific identity; the basketball imagery is on-brand and differentiated from a generic gift guide.
- **Seasonal timing is accurate.** June 10, eleven days before Father's Day — still inside the high-consideration window.
- **"Father's Day in the New Parent Bag" is a sharp angle.** Bag + slides + accessories speaks to a specific dad archetype that the June 1 apparel-heavy send didn't address.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout failures visible anywhere in the email.
- **Editorial hierarchy holds.** The eye flows naturally: hero → section headline → product grid, top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **The headline inside the body is a near-verbatim repeat of the June 1 subject line.** "Gifts that play to Dad's strengths" appeared as the subject on June 1 and reappears as a section header here. Subscribers will feel déjà vu.
- **No offer.** No discount, no free shipping threshold, no promo code, nothing to create purchase intent beyond "it exists."
- **The strongest new story (New Parent Bag) is buried at the bottom.** It should be the hero angle if the brand wants differentiation from the June 1 send.
- **Subject line doesn't signal anything new.** "Gifts for Dad, done right" could have run June 1 just as easily — there's no hook that says "this is different from the last Father's Day email you got from us."
- **Footer CTAs are distracting.** "Download the App" and "Buy a Book" sitting at the same visual weight as the product sections dilute the purchase path right before the close.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the New Parent Bag the hero.** Flip the structure: lead with the bag/travel accessories angle as the differentiated story, pull the Clarksons into that narrative ("Cali's pick for the dad who's always on the go"), and let apparel be the supporting grid. This immediately answers "why am I getting *another* Father's Day email?"
- 2. **Retire the "Gifts that play to Dad's strengths" headline from this email.** It was the June 1 subject line. Use "Cut from Dad's cloth, picked by Jordan and Cali" instead — it's already in the email and it's more distinctive.
- 3. **Add one time-bound hook.** Even a soft "Order by June 18 for standard delivery" or a free shipping note transforms this from browseable to actionable.
- 4. **Add a product-specific CTA.** One anchor product (e.g., the New Parent Bag with a "Shop the Bag →" button) outperforms a wall of editorial sections with no price signals.
- 5. **Demote or remove the app/book footer CTAs** — they're attention leaks at the decision moment.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The New Parent Bag — and everything else Dad needs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Father's Day gifts from someone who gets it — ft. the Clarksons`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order by June 18 for delivery before Father's Day`

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not captured**

The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not detected by the AgentMail relay. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, Gmail and Yahoo Mail will not surface their native unsubscribe UI, which is required for bulk senders under Google/Yahoo's 2024 sender requirements.
