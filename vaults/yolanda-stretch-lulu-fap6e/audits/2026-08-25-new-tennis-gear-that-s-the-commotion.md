# New tennis gear. That’s the commotion.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's tennis category launch, built around a genuine roster of pro ambassadors — Ethan Quinn, Leylah Fernandez, Frances Tiafoe, and wheelchair tennis pro Alfie Hewett all get individual "Shop [Name]'s Look" hero shots. That's a smart, differentiated hook: real athletes in live-action shots rather than generic studio models, and the Hewett inclusion is a genuinely rare and welcome representation signal in a mainstream retail send.
- The problem is everything below the concept. There is no offer anywhere in this email — no %, no $, no free-shipping, nothing. It's four consecutive full-width action photos with near-identical "Shop [Name]'s Look" CTAs, then a product grid, then done. For a subscriber who isn't already a tennis player, there's nothing pulling them past photo #2. This reads as a lookbook, not a marketing email, and against the brand's recent run (Fast and Free™ at 7/10, Scuba Evolve at 7/10) it's thin on hierarchy and heavy on repetition.
- Verdict: strong creative concept, weak email execution — no offer, no scannable value prop, and a wall of similar hero shots that will lose non-tennis subscribers by the second scroll.

## What's working

- Real named pro athletes (Fernandez, Tiafoe, Quinn, Hewett) in dynamic, in-action shots rather than static studio poses.
- Alfie Hewett's inclusion is genuine, unforced representation — a real differentiator versus competitor sends.
- Clean, on-brand minimalist layout with consistent black/white typography and clear "Shop [Name/collection]" link pattern.
- Bottom product grid gives a quick visual scan of new SKUs (skirts, tops, jackets) after the athlete features.
- Subject line's "That's the commotion" has a bit of playful voice consistent with lululemon's tone.

## What's weak

- Zero visible offer — no discount, no free shipping, no bundle deal; nothing to click for beyond curiosity.
- Four back-to-back athlete hero shots with the same CTA pattern creates monotony rather than urgency — no reason to keep scrolling.
- No visible pricing anywhere in the email, including the product grid — a real click-friction point.
- Hero copy ("Gear so light, your whole game turns up") is vague fitness-jargon; it doesn't tell a non-player what's actually new or why now.
- Category is narrow (tennis-only) with no cross-sell or "new to tennis? start here" entry point for browsers outside that niche.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete offer or urgency element near the top (e.g., "new tennis styles, limited release" or a launch-week % off) — right now there's zero reason to act versus browse later.
- 2. Cut the four-athlete sequence to two, and use the reclaimed space for a "shop by category" strip (rackets/tops, skirts/dresses, shoes) so non-tennis-obsessed subscribers have a faster path to relevance.
- 3. Add visible pricing under at least the hero product grid — "no price shown" is a consistent click-suppressor across recent lululemon sends.
- 4. Tighten the hero headline to name the actual product story (e.g., a specific new fabric/tech), not generic "gear so light" copy.
- 5. Consider swapping the plain "Shop What's New →" text link for a stronger visual button — right now the only button-styled element is "See all" at the very bottom.
