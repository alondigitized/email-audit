# What’s new: mesh tennis sets
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's July 7 send — arriving one day after the "Summer Sale's softest sets" editorial (July 6) and part of a seven-email run that started June 22. Unlike the recent Summer Sale drumbeat, this email makes a smart editorial pivot: it leads with a product story (new mesh tennis styles anchored by Tennis Coach Madison Appel) and buries the Summer Sale module at the bottom as a secondary hook. That's the right instinct — a new-arrivals email breaks the sale monotony without abandoning the campaign.
- The product storytelling is genuinely strong. "Gain the Ace Advantage" → ventilation feature copy → specific product callout (Ace Advantage Cutout Tennis Dress) is a clean three-act structure. Madison Appel as a real coach gives the creative credibility the June sends' generic imagery lacked.
- The weakness is cadence math. Seven emails in sixteen days, two in two days — even a strong send lands softly when the inbox is this saturated. The subject ("What's new: mesh tennis sets") is honest and specific but carries zero urgency and no hook beyond the category name. A subscriber who's been following the Summer Sale may open on autopilot, but they're also one tap from unsubscribing.

## What's working

- **Ambassador anchor is concrete.** "Tennis Coach Madison Appel" gives the mesh feature story a real person, not just a model. The "plays it cooler than ever" copy earns its pun.
- **Feature copy is functional, not vague.** "All-over ventilation has the cooling capacity to keep you in the zone" is product-benefit language, not brand-speak filler.
- **Specific product named.** "Ace Advantage Cutout Tennis Dress" is called out by name — subscribers know exactly what they're being asked to consider.
- **Editorial break from sale fatigue.** Positioning this as "What's New" rather than another sale push was the right call the day after a Summer Sale email.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout failures visible anywhere in the email.

## What's weak

- **Summer Sale module at the bottom contradicts the email's editorial premise.** The email tells a product story, then suddenly becomes a sale email. The tonal whiplash undercuts both messages.
- **"Shop What's New" CTA is generic.** Three CTA instances in this email and none of them are specific to the product being shown. "Shop the Ace Advantage Dress" or "Shop Mesh Tennis Sets" would convert better.
- **No pricing or offer anchor in the new-arrivals section.** The mesh tennis section is full-price new arrivals — that's fine, but with Summer Sale competition right below, the full-price ask needs more justification (fit detail, exclusive colorway, limited drop language).
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Day seven of a sixteen-day run, with two sends in two consecutive days. Even good creative gets tuned out at this volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Sharpen the CTA to match the hero product.** Replace "Shop What's New" with "Shop the Ace Advantage Dress" on the primary module. One product, one link, one purpose.
- 2. **Either commit to editorial OR commit to sale — not both in the same email.** If this is a new-arrivals email, drop the Summer Sale module or footnote it. If this is a sale email, lead with the discount and use the ambassador story as secondary support.
- 3. **Add a scarcity or freshness hook** ("Just dropped," "Limited colorways," "First to shop") to justify full-price consideration when the Summer Sale is still running.
- 4. **Stretch the send cadence.** Two emails in two days from the same brand is inbox aggression. A 48-hour gap would have improved open rates here.
