# Summer Sale: 150+ new picks added
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's third Summer Sale email in three consecutive days — following the July 1 "Your Groove's back" editorial and the June 30 catalogue blast. The "150+ new picks added" hook is the smartest thing about this send: it's a legitimate return-visit signal targeted at subscribers who already browsed the sale and moved on. It answers the objection "I already looked" without saying it.
- The email itself is a clean product grid — nine items across five rows, gender-split hero CTAs, and the now-familiar orange Summer Sale brand. Nothing breaks, nothing surprises. The weakness is that all three days of Summer Sale emails are structurally identical and none of them tells you how good the sale actually is. "Summer Sale" without a discount percentage is table stakes for lululemon, whose core shopper is comparing against Vuori and Alo at full price.
- The hero copy — "With these just-added styles, you're on track for a win" — is doing nothing. "On track for a win" borrows sports energy without landing anywhere specific.

## What's working

- **"150+ new picks" is a specific, credible number** — it quantifies the refresh and earns a second open from a subscriber who already dismissed the June 30 send.
- **Gender-split CTAs in the hero** ("Shop Women's Summer Sale" / "Shop Men's Summer Sale") reduce decision friction before the grid even loads.
- **Orange Summer Sale branding is fully consistent** across all three days — the campaign has coherent visual identity.
- **Product grid is real inventory**: named products, actual models, visible prices — no placeholders or broken images.
- **"Don't miss the moment" footer CTA** echoes the hero without repeating it verbatim, giving the scroll-all-the-way-down shopper a clean exit ramp.

## What's weak

- **No discount percentage visible anywhere** — three Summer Sale emails and not once has lululemon said "up to X% off." A shopper who doesn't know the deal depth has no reason to prioritize this over a competitor with a posted number.
- **Three-in-three-day cadence is at the fatigue threshold** — July 2 after June 30 and July 1 is where unsubscribes start. The refresh hook partially justifies it but doesn't fully earn it.
- **Hero copy is inert** — "you're on track for a win" is vague sports-adjacent filler with no connection to the actual product or the sale.
- **No urgency signal** — "Don't miss the moment" is prose urgency with no date, no countdown, no stock signal. It costs nothing to add "Sale ends Sunday."
- **Preheader is not visible in the render** — cannot confirm whether it's real copy or leaking footer/whitelist text.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the discount depth in the subject and hero** — even a range ("up to 40% off") converts browsers into buyers. This is the single change that lifts the whole campaign.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Sale ends July 6" in the hero and subject closes the loop on the urgency that "Don't miss the moment" gestures at without landing.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero headline** — something like "150+ new styles just landed in the Sale" mirrors the subject, is specific, and doesn't need to borrow sports metaphors.
- 4. **Rest the cadence after today** — skip July 3 or send only a behavioral trigger (abandoned browse, wishlist restock). A three-day streak has pushed the goodwill account close to zero.
- 5. **Surface one marquee product in the subject or hero** — the June 21 "This restock's ready to play" had the Groove as a named anchor. This email has nine products and no hero; picking one would sharpen the hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale: up to 40% off — 150+ styles just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `150 new picks in the Sale. Sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh drops landed this morning — styles going fast at sale prices.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Just added: the styles worth coming back for.`
