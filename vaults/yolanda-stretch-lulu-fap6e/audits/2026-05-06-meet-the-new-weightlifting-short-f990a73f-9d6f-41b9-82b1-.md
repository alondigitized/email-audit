# Meet the new Weightlifting Short
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- A clean, well-targeted lululemon product-launch email for the new Weightlifting Short. The creative is moody and on-brand, the hero module is focused, and the product copy leans into functional specifics (abrasion-resistant, hard-wearing stretch). Where it loses ground: the email has three competing product stories before you hit the fold, CTAs are generic, there's no social proof, and the one concrete discount offer ($50 off) is buried in a secondary section rather than anchoring the hero.

## What's working

- **Hero focus.** The opening module does one thing: introduce the Weightlifting Short with specific functional language ("abrasion-resistant," "defies distractions"). That's a cleaner hook than most activewear launches.
- **Creative tone.** Dark, high-contrast photography reads as aspirational gym content — consistent with lululemon's premium brand voice.
- **Preheader works.** "Lift heavy. Move freely." is punchy, specific, and earns the open by complementing the subject without repeating it.
- **Product detail copy.** Three distinct product sections each lead with a functional claim (abrasion-resistant, hard-wearing stretch, full range of motion) — more persuasive than pure lifestyle imagery alone.
- **Subject line is right-sized.** 33 characters, no spam signals, product-specific.

## What's weak

- **Three competing heroes.** Weightlifting Short → unnamed shorts collection → Warm Up Sleeveless Gym Hoodie → new styles grid → $50 off → App download. By module four, the Weightlifting Short — the subject line's entire premise — is already a memory.
- **Offer is decoupled from the hero.** "$50 off" lives in a standalone section disconnected from the hero product. A reader who opens for the Weightlifting Short has no idea if the discount applies to it.
- **CTA copy is generic.** Every button reads as "Shop Now" or "Shop" — no verb+specific-noun pairing. "Shop the Weightlifting Short" or "Get Yours" would sharpen the click intent.
- **No social proof.** Zero reviews, ratings, or UGC in an email asking you to buy a brand-new, $70–90 technical short.
- **"Nothing failure" copy** in the second module appears to be a render/truncation artifact — the sentence doesn't parse cleanly at thumbnail size.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One hero, one offer.** If this email's job is to launch the Weightlifting Short, the $50 offer should appear beneath the hero copy, not two scrolls down.
- 2. **Swap one of the secondary product sections for a rating.** Even "4.7 stars from 312 reviews" beneath the hero would do more work than the Gym Hoodie module.
- 3. **Make CTAs specific.** Replace "Shop" with "Shop the Weightlifting Short" on the hero button and "See New Styles" on the grid module to reduce decision paralysis.
- 4. **Clarify the offer scope.** Add one line under "$50 off" that anchors it to the featured product or explains what it covers. Vague discounts erode trust.
- 5. **Trim to three modules max.** Hero → Grid → Offer. The App download can live in the footer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-standard font-weight values (confirmed):**
The stylesheet declares `font-weight: 380` and `font-weight: 570` on base elements and utility classes:
```css
p, ul, ol, li { font-weight: 380; }
.eyebrow { font-weight: 570; }

```
CSS Fonts Level 4 allows arbitrary numeric weights for variable fonts, but the vast majority of email clients (Outlook, Gmail app, iOS Mail pre-iOS 16) will silently round or ignore these values, falling back to the nearest keyword weight (380 → 400/normal, 570 → 600/semibold). If the custom `Saans-*` fonts are variable fonts hosted via `@font-face`, the intent may be preserved in supporting clients, but no `@font-face` declaration is visible in the truncated source to confirm.

**Custom web fonts — no guaranteed rendering:**

Without confirmed `@font-face` declarations (not visible in truncated source), these will silently fall through to `Helvetica Neue`, `Arial`, or `Times New Roman`. This is expected behavior for email, but the fallback metrics (width, x-height) differ from Saans, meaning line breaks and layout proportions will shift in Outlook and most Android clients.
