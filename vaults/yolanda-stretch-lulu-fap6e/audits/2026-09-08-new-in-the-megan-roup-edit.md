# New In: The Megan Roup Edit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's ambassador-collaboration launch — "The Megan Roup Edit," a co-branded capsule tied to The Sculpt Society founder, built almost entirely on moody black-and-white editorial photography with two saturated burgundy call-outs breaking the monochrome. It's a step up in narrative coherence from the recent run of generic "what's new" drops (2026-09-06, 2026-09-04) — there's an actual person, a quote, a founder story, and a tangible cross-brand incentive (a free trial of The Sculpt Society plus 20% off). That's more substance than this brand has shown in a while.
- But it's still a scroll-heavy, product-light send: nine-plus images before you get real garment detail, two black pill CTAs that both just say "Shop," and no visible price or specific discount on the apparel itself. The Sculpt Society offer is the most concrete hook in the whole email, and it's buried two-thirds down, in small print, after a wall of lifestyle shots.
- Judgement: a well-executed editorial piece for a brand loyalist who already knows who Megan Roup is, but it under-serves the person deciding whether to buy something today. Ambassador storytelling shouldn't replace fit/price info — it should sit alongside it.

## What's working

- Genuine editorial hook: a named ambassador (Megan Roup, Sculpt Society founder) with a personal quote gives this send a story the recent generic drops lacked.
- Clear brand-partnership incentive is present: 5-day free trial + 20% off first month of The Sculpt Society, a real value prop beyond "buy clothes."
- Visual consistency — full black-and-white photography with two burgundy color-block sections creates a cohesive, premium look with no render bugs (no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content).
- Movement-in-action shots (boxing, kettlebell, stretching) support the product's functional use case rather than just static posing.

## What's weak

- No visible price, specific product name/style callouts, or % off on the actual apparel — only the affiliated Sculpt Society membership has a concrete number.
- Both primary CTAs just say "Shop the Edit" / "Shop What's New" — generic verbs, no product or benefit specificity.
- Extremely long, image-heavy scroll (9+ photos) before any real substance; mobile readers have to scroll past several near-duplicate action shots to reach the offer.
- The one genuinely quantified offer (20% off, free trial) is visually deprioritized — small text, low contrast against the burgundy block, near the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Sculpt Society offer (free trial + 20% off) up near the hero, and make the numbers bold — that's the only concrete value prop in the email and it's currently buried.
- 2. Replace at least one "Shop the Edit" CTA with product-specific copy tied to a hero item (e.g., "Shop the Sculpt Bra") so clicks land somewhere more decisive than a category page.
- 3. Trim the editorial photo sequence — 4-5 well-chosen shots would carry the same story with far less scroll fatigue.
- 4. Add a visible price or "from $X" on at least one featured piece so browsers can gauge fit against budget before clicking.
