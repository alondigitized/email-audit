# What’s new: Spacer Tracksuit for post-workout bliss
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Spacer Tracksuit launch — a clean, product-led send built around the "post-workout bliss" cooldown theme. It's a straightforward category push: individual product shots of the track jacket, pant, and short, then a lifestyle shot of the full tracksuit, followed by a "you'll also love" grid and a secondary SuperLoft fleece plug. No overlapping copy, no broken images, consistent moody-neutral studio palette matching the brand's recent cadence.
- The good news is this is more product-forward than the last several sends (the Megan Roup editorial, the fall early-access teaser) — you can actually see the jacket, pant, and short individually with named CTAs. The bad news: there's zero offer, zero price, zero urgency anywhere in this email, and this is now the sixth-plus lululemon send in ten days with essentially the same structure (hero lifestyle shot → product grid → "we think you'll also love" → secondary category plug → Amex banner). Fatigue is setting in.
- Verdict: solid, competent product email, but interchangeable with the brand's last three sends and offers no reason to act now versus later.

## What's working

- Individual product callouts (Track Jacket, Track Pant, Track Short) are each clearly labeled with their own CTA link.
- Full-look lifestyle shot ("A relaxed fit brings total comfort") sells the tracksuit as a complete outfit, not just isolated pieces.
- Visual hierarchy is clean — hero image, then headline, then black "Shop What's New" button all land in the first screen.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, spacing is even throughout.
- "We think you'll also love" grid adds cross-sell value without cluttering the primary offer.

## What's weak

- No price, no discount, no member benefit anywhere in the body — pure editorial/product email, low urgency.
- Subject and hero copy ("The rest, is comfort") are generic wordplay, same soft-launch tone as the last five sends (SuperLoft "rest day," Polartec "soft new fleece").
- The email tries to do three product stories at once (Spacer Tracksuit, "also love" grid, Steady State SuperLoft) which dilutes focus on the actual subject-line product.
- Amex banner at the bottom is the only "offer" in the entire email, and it's not even a lululemon offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual incentive in the hero — even a soft one ("Free shipping on orders $100+" or member-early-access framing) would differentiate this from the last five product-only sends.
- 2. Cut the secondary SuperLoft block entirely — it's competing with the primary Spacer Tracksuit story and this brand already sent a dedicated SuperLoft launch on 9/2.
- 3. Tighten the subject line to name the product benefit instead of a mood ("post-workout bliss" is vague) — lead with fabric/fit specifics that differentiate Spacer from the Polartec and SuperLoft lines already sent this month.
- 4. Add fit/sizing detail (e.g. "relaxed fit," fabric weight) directly under a product shot — sizing info is currently absent, which hurts confidence to click through.
