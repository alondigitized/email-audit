# New, relaxed golf gear in full swing
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is lululemon's Relaxed-Fit Golf Shorts product launch, anchored by Golf Advocate Roger Steele on a desert course. It lands June 16 — one day after the Father's Day last-chance golf email ("One day left to tee up Dad's gifts") — which is an aggressive cadence pivot from gift urgency to personal-purchase consideration. The transition works narratively: Dad's Day is over, now buy for yourself.
- The editorial photography is strong — dark, cinematic tones against a dramatic landscape, and Roger's personality adds texture beyond a generic product drop. The copy lands well ("Roger's always laid back and never laying up in this gear" is exactly the tone this category needs). What it doesn't have is any transactional pull: no price, no offer, no deadline. That's a conscious lululemon brand choice, but it means this email leans entirely on desire-creation, not conversion.
- The weakest executional call is the "Shop All Golf" CTA. The entire email builds a case for the Relaxed-Fit Golf Shorts specifically — then sends the click to a category page. That's a missed conversion opportunity.

## What's working

- Roger Steele as an editorial anchor makes this feel like a story, not a catalogue drop — the "never laying up" copy earns the lifestyle positioning.
- Dark, moody desert-course photography is visually distinct; breaks from the bright summer palette of the June 3 running email and June 9 LuluLinen™ send.
- "Cut to stay cool." as hero tagline is punchy and benefit-first, not just aesthetic.
- Product grid at the bottom ("Shop new styles this week") efficiently layers discovery after the hero narrative.
- No render bugs; clean mobile layout with clear visual hierarchy from hero → copy → CTA → grid.

## What's weak

- "Shop All Golf" CTA is too broad — the email sells one product story (Relaxed-Fit Golf Shorts) and then drops the click into a category bucket, diluting intent.
- No offer or pricing signal anywhere in the email — at lululemon's price point this is defensible, but it eliminates the transactional urgency the CTA needs.
- Back-to-back golf content (June 15 then June 16) will feel repetitive to golf-light subscribers and may nudge unsubscribes.
- No social proof or star ratings visible despite Roger Steele being present — a single quote or review snippet would reinforce the product claim.
- "Shop new styles this week" grid copy is generic filler — no framing on why these specific styles belong together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Change "Shop All Golf" to "Shop the Relaxed-Fit Short"** — match the CTA to the product story being told; deep-link to the PDP, not the category page. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Add a Roger Steele pull-quote or star rating** near the Relaxed-Fit Short callout — "Roger's always laid back" is brand copy, not social proof. A real user review or even a Roger quote about fit would sharpen conviction.
- 3. **Frame the product grid** — "Shop new styles this week" means nothing. Replace with something like "What Roger wears with it" or "Complete the look" to turn the grid into editorial upsell rather than a generic dump.
- 4. **Re-cadence the golf sends** — the Father's Day urgency email was June 15; this drops June 16. Give it 48–72 hours, especially for subscribers who opened the previous send.
