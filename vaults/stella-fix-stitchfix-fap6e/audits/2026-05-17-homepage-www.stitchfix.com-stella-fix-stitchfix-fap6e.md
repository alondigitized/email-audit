# Homepage snapshot · www.stitchfix.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is pure acquisition mode — every pixel above the fold is designed to convert a stranger, not welcome me back. The fit-frustration hook ("Why can't I find jeans that fit?") is real and relatable, but I've already answered that question; I pay Stitch Fix every month to answer it for me. As a returning subscriber landing here, I'm immediately hunting for a Sign In button instead of feeling like the brand sees me.

## What's working

- **The fit-frustration headline** — "Why can't I find jeans that fit?" is an honest, specific pain point that got me to Stitch Fix in the first place. It still lands emotionally even if it's not aimed at me right now.
- **The $20 offer is prominent twice** — in the teal dismissable banner AND baked into the CTA button. Hard to miss.
- **Clean, fast-loading render** — the page looks tight on mobile. No weird layout shifts, imagery loads, nothing broken.
- **Imagery feels real** — the collage includes a woman who looks like she actually wears these clothes, not a runway cutout. The knit top, white pants, and light denim read "dressed but not trying too hard," which is basically my entire wardrobe goal.

## What's weak

- **The entire page is a new-customer funnel** — as a returning member I have to hunt for "Sign In" in the top-right corner. There's no "Welcome back" state, no shortcut to my Fix preview, nothing that acknowledges I exist.
- **The $20 off is not for me** — "First Fix offer" means I get nothing from this promotion. The most prominent offer on the page literally excludes returning subscribers.
- **No navigation categories visible** — the hamburger is collapsed, so I can't see if women's, men's, or categories are even here without tapping. One extra tap to find what I want.
- **No social proof above the fold** — no "4.5 stars from 500K reviews," no "free returns," no trust signals that would reassure a fence-sitter or reinforce my own choice to stay subscribed.
- **No mention of Freestyle or Fix preview** — I'm skeptical of the Freestyle push, and landing here with zero context about what's new doesn't help. I have no idea if the page has evolved or if it's just the same quiz I took years ago.

## Recommendations

- **Add a returning-member banner or conditional CTA** — if I'm not signed in, show "Pick up where you left off" or "Sign in to see your next Fix" alongside the new-customer offer. Two audiences, one page.
- **Swap the sub-headline for a stat** — "Your Stylist sends pieces in your size, style and budget. Easy." is generic. Try "Styled by a real human. Average keep rate: 3 of 5." That's specific and speaks to my decision loop.
- **Put free-returns language in the hero area** — even one line ("Free returns, always") removes the risk objection for new customers and reminds me why I stay.
- **Surface one category tile below the fold** — even a single women's / men's / kids tap target below the hero collage would give me somewhere to go if I'm not ready to take the quiz.
