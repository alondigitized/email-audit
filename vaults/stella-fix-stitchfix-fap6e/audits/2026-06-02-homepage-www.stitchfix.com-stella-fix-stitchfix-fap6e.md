# Homepage snapshot · www.stitchfix.com · 2026-06-02
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Stitch Fix is running a hard acquisition push — "$30 Off Your First Fix" in the biggest text on the page, a matching promo banner, and a quiz CTA. It's a clean, simple layout that would land well for someone who's never heard of Stitch Fix before. As a current monthly subscriber? I opened this page expecting to see my queue, maybe a preview of what my stylist is pulling, or at least a sign that they know I exist. Instead I got a welcome mat that has zero idea I'm already a customer.

## What's working

- **The $30 off offer is impossible to miss** — large type, plus a banner strip above it, so it reads as a real deal even on a small phone screen
- **The modal card layout** keeps the value prop focused; no competing elements stealing attention from the core message
- **The cut-out collage of models** feels current and casual — the millennial women in everyday basics (white tops, jeans, linen) is genuinely my aesthetic and doesn't look like stock-photo filler
- **"Take the quiz, get $30 off"** is about as unambiguous as a CTA gets — I know exactly what happens when I tap it

## What's weak

- **The entire hero is for strangers, not me** — I'm a paying subscriber and the page treats me like I've never clicked on a Stitch Fix ad before; there's no "Sign In to see your Fix" shortcut, no account-aware state, nothing
- **"Sign In" is just a tiny text link** in the top-right corner — for a returning customer that should be the most prominent element above the fold, not an afterthought
- **"Limited time" in the banner is vague** — no end date, no countdown, nothing that makes me believe the clock is actually ticking
- **No trust signals anywhere** — no reviews, no "free returns," no "no commitment," nothing that would reassure even a first-time visitor who made it past the ad
- **The Freestyle store isn't mentioned at all** — which honestly is fine by me, but it tells me this page hasn't been updated to reflect whatever they're pushing right now

## Recommendations

- **Surface an account-aware state for returning visitors** — even just a "Welcome back, Stella — your next Fix ships soon" banner or a sticky "Sign In" button that visually competes with the new-user offer would make the page feel like it knows me
- **Add a genuine urgency signal** — swap "Limited time" for an actual end date or countdown on the $30 offer; right now it reads as permanent, which kills the urgency
- **Add one trust line below the CTA** — something like "Free returns · No commitment · Over 1M happy customers" takes up one text line and does real work for skeptical first-timers
- **Replace the hamburger-only nav with at least 2 visible category chips** (e.g., "Women" / "Men") so returning visitors who bypass the hero don't have to dig into a menu just to get anywhere
