# Homepage snapshot · www.stitchfix.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- I'm a paying, monthly Stitch Fix member and this homepage greeted me like I've never met them before. The entire above-the-fold experience is a new-customer acquisition pitch — "$30 off your first Fix" — which means the brand is basically talking over my head to the person standing behind me. Visually it's clean and the copy is mercifully short, but as someone who already has a stylist and already gets Fixes, I have zero reason to interact with any of this.

## What's working

- **The white card overlay** — the "$30 OFF / Your First Fix" card on top of the lifestyle photo is clean and reads instantly on a small screen. Good mobile design instinct.
- **The sub-copy** — "Your Stylist sends pieces in your size, style and budget. Easy." That single word "Easy." is doing real work. It speaks to exactly why I signed up. If I were new, this would hit.
- **The teal CTA button** — high contrast against the white card, button copy is specific ("Take the quiz, get $30 off"), not vague. The action is obvious.
- **The lifestyle photo** — real people, casual setting, women who look like they actually live lives. Not a sterile white-background product shot.
- **The dismissible banner** — the X on the "Limited time First Fix offer!" strip is right there. No trap.

## What's weak

- **The entire hero is for strangers, not members** — I've been getting monthly Fixes for however long and the homepage looks me dead in the face and says "take the quiz." That's a huge miss. There's no "Sign In to see your next Fix" or "Your stylist picked something new" hook anywhere above the fold.
- **"Sign In" is hidden top-right in body-weight text** — the only pathway back to my account is a tiny "Sign In" link in the nav bar. It's not a button, it has no visual weight, and it competes with nothing. For a service built on repeat subscriptions, this is backwards.
- **No personalization signal** — even a generic "Already a member? See your Fix" line under the CTA would redirect people like me. Instead the whole page assumes I've never heard of Stitch Fix.
- **No urgency that lands for me** — "Limited time" in the banner means nothing without a date. And again, the offer isn't even for me, so the urgency is moot.
- **No category entry point** — there's no visible nav to Women's, Men's, Kids', or any department. The hamburger is there but nothing is surfaced. If I wanted to browse Freestyle I literally can't do it without tapping into a hidden menu.

## Recommendations

- **Add a returning-member intercept** — below the hero card, a simple line: "Already a member? → Sign in to see your next Fix" as a tappable link styled like a secondary CTA. This costs nothing and stops loyal customers from immediately bouncing.
- **Give "Sign In" button weight** — swap the plain-text "Sign In" for an outlined button or give it a distinct color. It's the primary action for most people landing on this page on a phone mid-subscription.
- **Swap the hero for a split-audience module** — top half: returning member ("Your next Fix is ready — sign in"), bottom half: new customer pitch. The $30 offer can stay, but it shouldn't be the only thing above the fold.
- **Surface one navigational shortcut** — a single row of chips ("Women · Men · Kids · Freestyle") just below the hero would let me self-sort in one tap instead of digging through a hamburger menu.
