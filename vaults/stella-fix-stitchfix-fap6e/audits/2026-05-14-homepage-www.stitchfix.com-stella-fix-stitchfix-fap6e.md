# Homepage snapshot · www.stitchfix.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident new-customer acquisition page — the kind of thing a brand runs when it's trying to fill the top of funnel. As a monthly subscriber who already has a Fix queued, I've basically landed on the wrong experience. The $20-off quiz hook is sharp and the copy is genuinely relatable, but the whole thing is pitched at someone who has never heard of Stitch Fix, not at me.

## What's working

- **"Why can't I find jeans that fit?"** — This is genuinely the headline that made me start a Fix in the first place. It's a real thing I say out loud. Full credit.
- **The $20 offer banner** — Teal, dismissable, not intrusive. Doesn't shove itself in my face and block content.
- **Visual hierarchy on the hero card** — White card on a lifestyle backdrop keeps the eye moving in exactly the right order: problem → solution → action.
- **The model mix** — I can see a woman who looks like she's in her 30s-40s in relatable casual wear. Not aspirational runway, not Gen Z aesthetic. That's me.
- **CTA button copy** — "Take the quiz, get $20 off" bundles the value prop right into the action. That's smart writing.

## What's weak

- **This entire page ignores me as a subscriber.** I'm here monthly. I've taken the quiz. I have a Fix on the way. There's no "Check your Fix" or "Manage your delivery" or any returning-member entry point except a tiny "Sign In" link styled identically to a ghost button. I feel like I showed up to my regular coffee shop and they handed me a "first-time customer discount" flyer.
- **No Freestyle callout — which is actually fine, but notable.** The brand pushed Freestyle as a big thing and it doesn't appear here. Either they've deprioritized it or it's below the fold. Good instinct given my skepticism, but it means I don't know where they're going with the store experience.
- **No trust signals above the fold.** No review count, no "4.7 stars from 400K women," no "free returns." For someone on the fence, this is a gap.
- **The nav is completely opaque.** Hamburger only. I can't tell from this screen if there's a women's vs. men's split, if I can browse categories, or how to jump to my account without hunting.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Sign In to see your Fix" CTA in the header or below the hero** — members landing here should have an obvious returning-user path, not just a ghost-link "Sign In" in the corner. Even a small "Already a member? →" beneath the quiz button would do it.
- **Swap the hero card copy for a two-audience version** — the quiz headline works perfectly for new customers, but a logged-in detection or cookie-based redirect could show me a "Your Fix is almost ready" card instead. That's a one-week engineering lift with serious retention impact.
- **Add one trust signal to the hero card** — something like "Join 4M+ women who outsource Mondays" right below the subtext. One line. Huge lift for hesitant new visitors without cluttering the design.
- **Make the banner dismissal persistent** — if I've been a subscriber for two years, showing me the "First Fix offer" every time I open the app is friction. Set a cookie and stop showing it to logged-in users.
