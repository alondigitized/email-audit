# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-05-27
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Article is doing what Article does best — confident, editorial staging with clean copy and a patio-season push. The vibe is right for the brand, but I came here looking at their sofa lineup and they've got me staring at outdoor dining chairs. No visible promo anywhere on this screen, which is the thing that would actually get me to stop scrolling and start clicking.

## What's working

- **"Perfectly pre-paired." headline** — the pun is earned, the copy is confident without being try-hard. Millennial tone, short, clever. I read it and didn't cringe.
- **"SHOP OUTDOOR DINING SETS" CTA** — pill button, white on dark hero, unmistakable. No vague "Discover" nonsense; it tells me exactly where I'm going.
- **Lifestyle hero image** — outdoor dining space with that warm ambient lighting, teak-adjacent table, linen-white chairs, giant glass walls behind. This is the kind of setup I'd post on Twitter if I owned it. It's aspirational without being unattainable.
- **Seasonal timing** — pushing outdoor dining at the end of May is smart. This campaign is actually synced to the calendar, which I notice because so many brands are still running January vibes in spring.
- **Search bar placement** — search sits right below the logo, above the hero. High-tech-comfort me appreciates not having to hunt for it.

## What's weak

- **No promo, period** — I am literally the customer who opens Article's site waiting for the 10%-off email moment. Nothing. Not even a "free shipping over $X" line. The email banner says "promotions in your inbox" but that's a tease, not a hook.
- **Wrong category above the fold for me** — outdoor dining sets are not why I'm here. I want to get to sofas in one tap and there's no shortcut visible. The hamburger menu is my only option.
- **No trust signals** — no review counts, no "as seen in," no white-glove delivery callout, nothing that would reassure me I'm buying from a credible operation. The FAQ I always read before a big purchase? Not even hinted at.
- **No human in the hero** — the furniture looks great but there's no lifestyle anchor, no sense of scale or how a real person lives with it. The chairs especially are hard to gauge without a human frame of reference.
- **Email subscribe banner** — "Subscribe now!" as the first thing I see before the actual brand feels a little pushy. I get why it's there, but it puts a chore above the experience.

## Recommendations

- **Add a persistent free-shipping threshold strip** ("Free delivery on orders over $999" or whatever the actual number is) — one line below the nav would do it, and it immediately answers the question I have before I add anything to cart.
- **Swap the hero CTA for a split-category option** — one button for "Shop Outdoor" and a secondary text link for "Shop Sofas & Seating." You're leaving sofa-intenders like me with nothing to do except dig through the hamburger menu.
- **Drop a social-proof signal into the hero subheadline area** — even "Over 500,000 homes furnished" or a ⭐ 4.8 aggregate rating badge. This is a considered, high-AOV purchase; I need reassurance before I scroll further.
- **Surface the white-glove delivery callout above the fold** — a single icon strip ("Delivered assembled · 30-day returns · Free shipping") would do enormous work for someone like me who reads every delivery FAQ before committing.
