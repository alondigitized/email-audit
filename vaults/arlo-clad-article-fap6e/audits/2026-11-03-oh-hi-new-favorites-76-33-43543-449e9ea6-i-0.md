# Oh, hi, new favorites
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a polished seasonal new-arrivals catalog with no offer. Article is the sender, the hero says "Fresh for fall," and the email walks through three rooms: living, dining and bedroom. Each room has a headline image, a product image and a short line of copy. Each ends with an orange "SHOP [ROOM]" pill, and a small "Add a finishing touch" decor tile closes the body. The photography is warm and consistent. The structure is easy to scan.
- It is also generic. Nothing in it gives a reason to click today. There is no price, no deadline and no sale, and the subject "Oh, hi, new favorites" says nothing concrete. It sits in the same run of pretty, no-discount sends as "Steal some style inspo" (10/27) and "Give guests a cozy stay" (10/23). The three-room structure is also close to "Fall is for full tables" (10/13), and none of them carries a hook. It is a competent brand email that is easy to ignore.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "Fresh for fall" with a dining-table scene and an orange pill makes the season and the action obvious.
- Each room block follows the same pattern of headline image, product image, one line of copy and a pill CTA. Nothing is confusing.
- The CTA copy ("SHOP LIVING ROOM," "SHOP DINING ROOM," "SHOP BEDROOM") uses a verb plus a noun, and the orange pills stand out against the neutral photos.
- Product names are captioned on the images (Jonas Power Recliner Sofa, Ellory Bar Cabinet, Glen Queen Velvet Bed), so shoppers can see what is being featured.
- The visual voice is consistent with prior sends, and I see no broken images.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, deadline or loyalty perk. The closing orange banner, "These new arrivals bring comfort, character, and stay-a-while energy to every room," is pure brand copy.
- The white headlines "New tablemates." and "Upgrade your downtime." sit over light wall and cabinet areas. Contrast is weak and the text is hard to read.
- The product captions on the images are tiny, white and low-contrast, especially "MARIBEL CORDED DINING CHAIR & ELLORY EXTENDABLE DINING TABLE" over the busy rug and table.
- Only the room-level CTAs are visible, so the email never sends you to a single hero product. It is long, with three near-identical sections, and the "Add a finishing touch" decor tile at the bottom is small and easy to miss.
- No reviews, ratings or social proof are visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to act now, such as a launch-week perk like "Free shipping on new arrivals through Sunday" or a member price. Put it in the hero and the banner.
- 2. Fix the text contrast. Use dark text or a subtle scrim behind the white headlines, and enlarge or put the product captions on a solid strip.
- 3. Link the hero and the product captions directly to the featured products (Jonas sofa, Ellory cabinet, Glen bed) so the click lands on a product, not a category.
- 4. Cut the email to two rooms or lead with the single best seller. Add star ratings or "best seller" labels as social proof.
- 5. Make the subject specific. "Oh, hi, new favorites" is charming but says nothing about fall, furniture or Article.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New fall arrivals, free shipping this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Jonas sofa just landed (plus 2 more)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining and bedroom pieces for slow mornings and cozy evenings.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh for fall: see what's new in living, dining and bedroom.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a table-based layout with dark-mode support and click tracking through two redirect domains. The visible issues are a malformed dark-mode `<style>` block, missing alt text on three images, and a URL-heavy plain-text part. The HTML is truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe link, and landing URLs could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Two redirect domains.** Links use `https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/…~~/AAQVbhA~/…` and `https://links.email.article.com/s/eo/…`. Only the second is on the sender's own domain. The first is a third-party ESP domain, which weakens domain alignment and can affect reputation and filtering.
- **Plain-text URL ratio (QA WARN).** 2,447 of 3,455 characters (about 71%) are URLs. The long encoded tracking tokens inflate the plain-text part, which is a spam-score risk.
- **Incomplete link probing (QA).** 12 links were probed and 19 were skipped, so the status of the skipped links is unknown.
- **Final destinations not checked.** The redirect tokens are opaque, so the final URLs and their HTTP status were not confirmed from the source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA WARN, 3 images):**
  - `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/DwDWXxhDHzz5KPP-E1OITg~~/…`
  - `links.email.article.com/s/eo/tOWozhyO0lO5hMZmQmPDKabm_…`
  - `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/mpPaX8OJ_iztunxOwJ0oog~~/…`
  - If any of these are content images, screen readers and image-blocked clients get no description. If they are tracking pixels, add `alt=""` so they are marked decorative.
- **Empty `<title></title>`.** This is minor, but it is an accessibility and web-view gap.
- **Malformed dark-mode CSS.** The first `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block has three syntax errors:
  - `h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}` has a trailing comma, so the selector list is invalid and the rule is likely dropped.
  - `a{color:#fff;!important}` has a misplaced `!important`, so the declaration is not marked important.
  - The block ends at `a .link{color:#fff!important}</style>` with the `@media` closing brace missing. Unclosed at-rules are usually recovered at `</style>`, but some clients may drop the whole block.
  - Heading and paragraph colors may therefore not invert in dark mode. The `[data-ogsc]` rules for Outlook are valid and may mask this in some clients.
- **Invalid value.** `line-height:auto!important` in `.dark-img` is not valid CSS and is ignored.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unrendered tokens such as `{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`.

Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe, so verify the headers against the raw message.
- **Authentication-Results.** The header is missing, and the QA note attributes this to the relay. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown from this data.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address.** QA states an unsubscribe link was always probed, but the footer is outside the truncated source, so neither could be verified in the HTML.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
The destinations are hidden behind redirect tokens, so UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) and landing-page alignment could not be assessed. Check the resolved URLs after redirect to see whether the UTMs survive.
