# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-07-15
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Article is leading with their Made-to-Order program — customization, personal choice, "you do you" energy. It feels like a brand that knows exactly who it's talking to: someone who agonizes over fabric swatches and wants a sofa that's theirs. The aesthetic is restrained and confident, which tracks with Article's whole vibe. Not a sale page, not a clearance dump — this is a brand-first hero targeting the considered buyer.

## What's working

- **"You do you." headline** — punchy, self-directed, perfectly pitched at the millennial who hates being told what to buy. It doesn't talk down or oversell.
- **"For the detail-oriented" sub-copy** — I literally read every white-glove delivery FAQ; calling me detail-oriented is flattery that lands because it's true.
- **Fabric close-up hero image** — a rolled bolt of warm linen textile is exactly the kind of shot I'd screenshot and send to my partner. It sells texture without showing a single completed product.
- **"SHOP MADE TO ORDER" CTA** — unambiguous. No "Discover" or "Explore" fluff. I know exactly where this goes.
- **Free Interior Design Plan banner** — genuinely useful for a one-big-purchase-a-year buyer who wants help getting it right. Low-friction service offer.
- **Search bar prominence** — I'm going to search "Sven leather" before anything else, and it's right there.

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal above the fold** — I'm here specifically because I wait for a rare 10%-off promo. Nothing tells me one is happening or how close I am to earning a reward.
- **No trust signals** — no review count badge, no "4.8★ from 12,000 reviews," nothing. For a $2,000 sofa, social proof should be baked into the hero.
- **No navigation visible without tapping a hamburger** — I can't see category labels (Sofas, Sectionals, Bedroom). That's an extra tap to confirm Article even sells what I came for.
- **No returning-customer hook** — Article has my account; I've browsed before. No "Welcome back," no wishlist count surfaced, nothing that makes me feel known.
- **Hero imagery is abstract** — beautiful fabric, but no finished product. Someone cross-shopping Joybird wants to see a sofa, not just its raw material.

## Recommendations

- **Overlay a subtle promo ribbon on the hero**: "Current members: 10% off select sofas through Sunday" — even a hint of a deal stops comparison-shoppers from bouncing to Joybird first.
- **Add a social-proof strip just below the hero**: "4.8★ · 14,000+ reviews · Free white-glove delivery" — one line, massive trust lift, especially for first-time Article visitors arriving from cross-shopping.
- **Expand the nav or add a category strip below the CTA**: four icon-buttons — Sofas, Sectionals, Beds, Dining — so I can jump straight to what I care about without opening the hamburger.
- **Swap or stack the hero image**: show the finished Sven (or equivalent hero sofa) alongside the fabric close-up. Texture sells the material; a lifestyle shot of the completed piece sells the aspiration.
