# New arrivals just landed
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Article makes the smart pivot here: after four consecutive 4th of July sale emails ending July 6, this July 10 editorial send resets the tone cleanly. The leather sofa hero ("That's so you.") is warm, confident, and immediately signals lifestyle intent rather than discount hunting — the right gear-shift after a promotion blitz. The room-by-room structure (living, bedroom, dining) mirrors the editorial cadence Article ran through most of June.
- That said, this email coasts on brand equity without giving a subscriber a reason to click *today*. "New arrivals just landed" is accurate but anonymous — you don't know if it's sofas, lighting, accent chairs, or dining tables until you're already inside. The three room sections each get a clever headline ("Self-centered," "Styled to taste") but no product name, no price anchor, and no quantity urgency. It's a browse invitation, not a decision prompt.
- The render is clean and the hierarchy is solid. But as a post-sale re-engagement send, it needed more specificity to recapture the attention the sale trained subscribers to give.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns the open.** The cognac leather sofa scene is rich and immediately shoppable — Article's lifestyle photography remains one of the best in DTC furniture.
- **Tone reset is well-timed.** Four promotional emails in ten days warranted an editorial breather; this drops smoothly back into the "magazine from a furniture store" identity.
- **Room-by-room structure is scannable.** Living → bedroom → dining gives the eye clear landing spots with distinct CTAs per section.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no text overlap, no layout breakage — the email displays correctly.
- **"Self-centered" copy.** Of the three section headlines, this one actually earns a smile — tight wordplay on bedroom-as-personal-sanctuary.

## What's weak

- **Subject tells you nothing about what arrived.** "New arrivals just landed" could be any category at any brand — a subscriber with low intent will skip it.
- **No product names or prices anywhere.** Three room sections and not a single SKU, item name, or price point visible — this is browse-mode content, not purchase-mode content.
- **"Show off a bit." section is ambiguous.** The orange accent section feels like a detour — it's unclear if this is a separate new collection, a sale item, or just a mood image.
- **Zero urgency signals.** After the sale sequence conditioned clicks with deadlines, this email offers no temporal hook whatsoever — not even a "limited stock" nudge.
- **CTA copy is all verbs, no category specificity.** "Shop New Arrivals," "Shop in Bedroom," "Shop in Dining" are functional but interchangeable with any Article email from the last six months.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name at least one hero product in the subject and body.** "New arrivals: cognac leather sofas, new beds + more" gives subscribers a reason to open; "The Kova Sofa is here" gives them a reason to click. The current copy implies new things exist but doesn't prove it.
- 2. **Add a single price anchor in at least one section.** Even "Starting at $799" breaks the full-browse barrier and converts editorial energy into transactional intent.
- 3. **Inject soft urgency into the bedroom or dining section.** "Limited quantities" or "Just 40 available" on a hero piece would recapture the urgency muscle the sale sequence built.
- 4. **Replace "Show off a bit." with a named collection intro.** If there's a new accent or lounge collection, name it — don't make subscribers guess what they're looking at.
- 5. **Make "Find your style" footer actionable.** The footer teaser block ("Finishing touches to the big bits of personality") doesn't have a CTA — missed conversion.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cognac leather, new bed frames, and the dining table you've been waiting on.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three rooms, all new. Browse before stock thins.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error — invalid selector (confirmed):**
```css
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma before `{` is invalid CSS. Strict parsers (WebKit, Gecko) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text invisible on dark backgrounds.

**CSS syntax error — malformed `!important` (confirmed):**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
Semicolon before `!important` is invalid. The declaration will be ignored, meaning anchor color won't override in dark mode.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several screen readers and email clients (notably VoiceOver on iOS) announce the `<title>` as the document label. An empty value produces a blank or filename announcement. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.

**Missing closing brace in `prefers-color-scheme` block:**
The `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){...}` block in the source does not close with `}` before the next `<style>` block begins. This may be a truncation artifact, but if present in the live send, every subsequent style rule would be absorbed into the dark media query.
