# Complete your home with new decor
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Article's post-Memorial-Day-sale pivot — a full-price editorial catalog introducing new decor categories (pillows/throws, shelving, planters, outdoor covers). After six consecutive sale emails from May 16–25 hammering 40% off and countdown urgency, this is the right tonal reset. The brand voice is consistent, photography is clean, and the section headers ("Decor values," "Shelf life," "Branch out") are genuinely charming.
- The problem is structure and absence of any commercial hook. Four equally weighted sections with four separate CTAs fragment attention into nothing. There is no price, no offer, no urgency, and no "here's what's new since the sale" framing to give a returning subscriber a reason to click rather than scroll and bail. The email reads as a brand lookbook, not a conversion vehicle.
- Net verdict: well-executed creative, weak commercial chassis. Fine as a palette cleanser after the sale blitz, but a missed opportunity to re-engage non-buyers.

## What's working

- **Section headers are punchy and brand-consistent** — "Decor values," "Touchable textiles," "Shelf life," "Branch out" are memorable and fit Article's Scandinavian-inflected voice.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no layout drift, consistent typography throughout.
- **Hero "SHOP NEW DECOR" CTA is unambiguous** — clear button, clear copy, lands above the fold.
- **Lifestyle photography is cohesive** — sofa, textiles, shelving, planters, and the darker promo band all share the same warm, editorial palette.
- **Cadence reset is well-timed** — sending a non-sale email four days after "last chance" ends is exactly right.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — after six emails training subscribers to expect 40% off, a full-price catalog with zero pricing signal is a cold shower.
- **Four competing CTAs dilute the email** — SHOP NEW DECOR → SHOP PILLOWS & THROWS → shop shelves → shop planters → SHOP OUTDOOR COVERS. No single action wins.
- **No "new arrivals" framing** — nothing anchors why these items are appearing now. "Complete your home" is generic; "Just in: summer decor" would give the same products a news hook.
- **Outdoor covers section feels tacked on** — the darker banner for protective covers is visually and categorically disconnected from the warm lifestyle sections above it; it reads as a separate email jammed into the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero category and go deep** — choose planters or pillows, show 3 specific products with prices, and add "new arrival" or "just landed" language. The multi-section catalog format works better in a digest; a single-send should have one primary action.
- 2. **Add a price anchor** — even one "starting at $49" or "new arrivals from $35" under the hero copy reassures subscribers that the sale is over but the value isn't gone.
- 3. **Reframe the subject around "new"** — the current line is generic; "new in: summer decor" has the same length but signals fresh inventory, which is the implicit promise of this email.
- 4. **Cut or separate the outdoor covers section** — it reads as a non sequitur. Either blend it into the body as a fifth category or save it for its own send.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: summer decor`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals to finish every room — starting from $35`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New decor is in. No sale needed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**CSS syntax error #1 — trailing comma in selector list (dark mode block):**
```css
/* BROKEN */
@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){
  h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma after `p,` before `{` is invalid CSS. Strict parsers (including some webmail renderers) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` uncolored in dark mode.

**CSS syntax error #2 — semicolon before `!important` (dark mode block):**
```css
/* BROKEN */
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
`color:#fff;!important` is invalid. The semicolon terminates the declaration before `!important` is parsed, making the `!important` flag inert. The `a` color declaration will still apply (without `!important`), but specificity override behavior is unreliable across clients.

**Minor — empty `<title>` tag:**
```html

```
Some clients (notably older Android Mail) display the `<title>` content as a subject-line preview fallback. Not critical, but worth populating.
