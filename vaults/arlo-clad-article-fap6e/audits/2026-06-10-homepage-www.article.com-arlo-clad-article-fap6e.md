# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-06-10
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Article's mobile homepage is clean, editorial, and immediately telegraphs that they know how to art-direct a room — exactly the aesthetic confidence that made me consider them alongside Joybird and Inside Weather in the first place. Right now they're pushing outdoor dining hard, which is seasonally on-point for June, but as someone who came here to scope sofas and maybe catch a rare promo, the above-the-fold experience gives me a beautiful room and nothing else. No offer, no price hook, no path to living room furniture without digging through the hamburger menu.

## What's working

- **"Perfectly pre-paired" headline** — punchy, self-aware, doesn't take itself too seriously. That's the Article tone I recognize and trust.
- **The hero lifestyle photo** — warm wood table, white chairs, floor-to-ceiling glass and greenery. It's the kind of room shot I screenshot and save. Immediately establishes Article as a design partner, not just a retailer.
- **Seasonal coherence** — pushing outdoor dining in early June is exactly right. It doesn't feel like a stale campaign.
- **Search bar above the fold** — high tech comfort means I'll use this. Having it right under the nav means I can search "Sven" immediately rather than hunting nav links.
- **Clean nav icons** — wishlist (heart) is visible at a glance. I use that to park pieces while I comparison-shop.

## What's weak

- **No offer above the fold** — I'm a guy who waits for 10%-off promos. If one is running right now, I can't see it. The top banner is a subscribe pitch, not a deal.
- **Outdoor dining, not living room** — my whole reason for opening Article is sofas. The hero pushes me into a category I'm probably not buying this year.
- **Hamburger nav hides my category** — I have to tap the menu to even see "Sofas" or "Living Room." No shortcut chips or category tiles visible.
- **Zero social proof above the fold** — no review counts, no "as seen in" badges, nothing. Article's quality story lives in their reviews, and none of it is visible here.
- **Newsletter banner feels like friction** — it's the first element my eyes hit before even the logo. I know I'll dismiss it, but it's a micro-annoyance before I even see what's on the page.

## Recommendations

- **Add a thin promo strip below the nav** — even "Free shipping on orders over $X" or "10% off this week only" would immediately signal there's a reason to browse now rather than come back later.
- **Swap the hero CTA seasonally but add a secondary link** — keep "Shop Outdoor Dining Sets" as primary, but add a secondary text link like "Shop Living Room →" so furniture-focused shoppers aren't stranded.
- **Surface a category shortcut row** — four tappable chips (Sofas, Dining, Outdoor, Beds) directly below the hero. Article's competitors do this; it collapses the distance between landing page and category browse from two taps to one.
- **Drop a star-count trust strip above the fold** — "4.7 stars across 40,000+ reviews" costs one line of space and does a lot of work for a brand competing on quality against Joybird and Interior Define.
