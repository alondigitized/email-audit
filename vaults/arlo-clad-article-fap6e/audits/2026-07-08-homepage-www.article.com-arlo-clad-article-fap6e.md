# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-07-08
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Article knows exactly who they're talking to. The second I land on this I see a close-up of what looks like a boucle or teddy-fabric sofa arm — the kind of textile you don't scroll past if you've spent three weekends Googling "Article Burrard vs. Joybird Briar." The "You do you / For the detail-oriented" headline is doing real work for a guy who obsessively compares fabric swatches before pulling the trigger. This is a direct pitch at the considered, custom-furniture buyer — Article is leading hard with their Made to Order program right now.

## What's working

- **The hero image:** A tightly cropped boucle/upholstered sofa form is exactly the kind of tactile, material-focused shot that stops a furniture obsessive mid-scroll. It communicates fabric quality before I read a word.
- **"For the detail-oriented" sub-copy:** This isn't generic "express yourself" fluff — it's a direct appeal to the person who reads every color-and-fabric option. That's me. I feel called in.
- **"SHOP MADE TO ORDER" CTA:** Clear, pill-shaped, high contrast against the hero. Tells me exactly what I'm clicking into — not "Explore" or "Discover," which I'd immediately distrust.
- **Free Interior Design Plan banner:** I actually would use this. I'm building out a living room and a free consult from a brand stylist beats a $300/hr interior designer for sanity-checking a sofa choice.
- **Dismissible announcement bar:** The X is right there — no forced engagement, no guilt trip. Clean and respectful.

## What's weak

- **Zero social proof above the fold:** No star ratings, no "10,000+ reviews," no "as seen in" badge. For a $2,000+ sofa purchase I'm already thinking about whether strangers trust this brand — nothing here addresses that.
- **No price anchor or offer visible:** Article does run promos (I've been waiting for one). Nothing here signals "right now is a good time to buy." There's no threshold, no savings, no urgency.
- **Navigation is completely hidden:** The hamburger menu gives me nothing. I can't see "Sofas," "Sectionals," or "Living Room" without tapping — which means extra friction before I can evaluate whether their category depth is worth my time.
- **No returning-visitor recognition:** I've been to Article's site before. There's no "pick up where you left off" hook, no wishlist reminder, no saved configuration prompt. Feels cold for a brand that knows me.
- **Hero image is abstract:** The close-up is beautiful but borderline ambiguous on first glance — it reads as fabric/upholstery but not obviously "sofa." For someone less obsessed with furniture textures it might not register.

## Recommendations

- **Add a thin category rail below the hero:** Three tappable chips — "Sofas," "Sectionals," "Accent Chairs" — so I can route myself in one tap without hunting through the hamburger. This is the single highest-friction fix on mobile.
- **Surface one price signal in the announcement bar:** Even "Free shipping on all orders" or "Current promo: 10% off Made to Order" would activate buyers who are already in wait-for-a-deal mode. Rotate it into the existing dismissible banner.
- **Swap abstract boucle close-up for a styled living-room hero shot:** Keep the boucle texture but pull back to show it on a finished sofa in a real room — gives the detail-oriented buyer context for scale, proportion, and styling, which is the actual decision they're trying to make.
- **Add a review count trust signal near the CTA:** Something like "★ 4.8 · 12,400 reviews" tucked under the button would halve my hesitation before tapping. It costs zero real estate and converts fence-sitters like me.
