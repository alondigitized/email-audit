- But there's no price, no percentage, no dollar figure anywhere in this email — "save more" is the closest thing to an offer, and it's vague. ## What's working

- Consistent room-by-room structure (hero → lifestyle shot → isolated product shot → CTA) repeated four times, easy to scan
- Named collections (Heidi, Leif) and named chair SKUs (Bea Velvet, Loa Shearling, Aguila Leather) with a clean 3-up comparison grid
- The pun thread ("Clocking in," "Team player," "Transferable skills.," "Long days, solved.") gives the email a light, cohesive voice without being try-hard
- Bundle CTA at the bottom is a genuine secondary offer, not just a repeat of "shop now"
- No render bugs — images load, text is legible, spacing is even throughout

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer — no %, no $, no BOGO anywhere; "save more" in the bundle banner is the only pricing hint and it's non-specific
- Four separate CTA buttons (Shop New Home Office, Shop Heidi, Shop Leif, Shop Office Chairs) plus two more at the bottom dilutes the primary action — no single clear "click here"
- All-neutral/olive-and-walnut palette across every shot makes the sections visually blur together on a fast scroll
- Subject "New for home office" is generic — doesn't hint at collections, price, or the pun-thread hook that actually carries the email

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real number to the bundle banner — "Save 15% when you bundle 2+ pieces" beats "save more," and gives the whole email an actual reason to click today.
- 2. Cut the CTA count — pick one primary hero CTA ("Shop New Home Office") and let the mid-email buttons be secondary/text-link weight, not five equal buttons.
- 3. Move the bundle offer up — it's the strongest hook in the email and it's buried second-to-last, after four full sections.
- 4. Tighten the subject to promise something concrete instead of just a category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Home office, upgraded — new Heidi & Leif collections`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Modular shelving, real desks, chairs that don't look like chairs.`
