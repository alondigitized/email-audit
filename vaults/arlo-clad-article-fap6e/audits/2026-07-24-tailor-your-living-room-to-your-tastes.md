# Tailor your living room to your tastes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Article's July 24 send is a clean Made To Order feature email — a continuation of the editorial reset that started July 10 and has now touched new arrivals, entryway, home tour, dining room, and now living room personalization. "Have it your way." is a confident hero headline and the six category tiles give clear pathways. The layout is tight, no render bugs, and the "Shop Worry Free" returns section is a smart objection-handler for a program where commitment anxiety is real.
- The weakness is structural: this is the fifth consecutive editorial send in 14 days, and for the third time in a row (July 17, July 21, July 24), there's no price signal anywhere in the email. The Made To Order message is genuinely useful brand education, but without even a secondary offer or urgency hook, there's nothing that makes today the day to click. The July 10 new arrivals send scored 9/10 precisely because it had a clear editorial reset energy; by send five in the same tone, diminishing returns are setting in.
- A good email for brand building. A weak email for converting. Article needs to decide whether this sequence is awareness or acquisition — and act accordingly.

## What's working

- "Have it your way." is punchy, brand-consistent, and builds on the "Personal taste." editorial thread Article has owned since July 10.
- "SHOP MADE TO ORDER" CTA is specific and action-oriented — verb + program name, not lazy "Learn More."
- Six category tiles (Sofas & Loveseats, Lounge Chairs, Sectionals, Modules, Sofa Beds, Swivel Chairs) give subscribers multiple pathways based on what they actually need.
- "SHOP WORRY FREE" / 30-day returns section directly addresses the real friction with MTO orders (commitment risk) — smart placement before the footer.
- Clean render: consistent type sizing, no broken images, no overlapping elements, balanced two-column grid.

## What's weak

- Zero price signal across the entire email — no offer, no threshold, no "free shipping on MTO orders" to create a reason to act today vs. next week.
- No specific product hero: the main image shows a room, not a named sofa in a specific fabric — a missed chance to anchor the MTO concept to something tangible and desirable.
- No social proof — reviews, ratings, or a "X customers configured this sofa" line would reinforce the MTO program's quality at the exact moment someone is deciding whether to trust a customization process.
- Five editorial sends in 14 days with no promotional break is starting to feel monotonous for engaged subscribers; the July 21 dining room send hit the same "personalize your space" note just three days ago.
- Body copy below the hero ("Tell how you want with our Made To Order styles") reads slightly awkward at this resolution — the phrasing feels like it's missing a word.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single lightweight offer to create urgency** — even "Free shipping on your Made To Order configuration" or "Complimentary fabric swatches on MTO orders this month" gives someone a reason to click now. Without it, this email is all awareness, no conversion.
- 2. **Anchor the hero to one specific configurable product** — show the Burrard sectional (or whichever MTO bestseller) in a specific fabric colorway, name the fabric, and link directly to that product page. It makes the abstract "personalization" promise concrete.
- 3. **Add a single review pull-quote** near the category tiles — something like "I was nervous about ordering a custom sofa online — the process was actually seamless." One line of social proof would dramatically reduce commitment friction.
- 4. **Break the five-editorial streak** with a light promotional beat — a members-only MTO preview window, an early-access fabric drop, or a time-limited configuration discount. The July 10 send proved Article can reset tone cleanly; this sequence needs a similar punctuation mark.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
An empty title causes some push-notification previews and Gmail tab labels to show "Untitled." Should contain a meaningful fallback string matching the subject line.

**Issue 2 — CSS selector syntax error (trailing comma) in dark-mode block**
```css
/* Inside @media(prefers-color-scheme:dark) */
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The trailing comma before `{` is invalid CSS. Parsers that aren't lenient (Apple Mail on macOS, some WebKit builds) will drop the entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text dark-colored on a black background in dark mode — invisible text.

**Issue 3 — CSS property syntax error (semicolon before `!important`) in dark-mode block**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
`!important` must be part of the value declaration, not a separate token. The correct form is `color:#fff!important`. As written, the `color` declaration is terminated by the semicolon and `!important` is an orphan token — the rule is dropped by spec-compliant parsers, leaving links uncolored in dark mode on Outlook.com.

**Issue 4 — Missing closing `}` for `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block**
```css
@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){
  ...
  a .link{color:#fff!important}
</style>  ← media query never closed
```
The `@media` block is not closed with `}` before `</style>`. Browsers auto-recover, but strict parsers (older Outlook WebView) may discard the entire block or bleed styles into adjacent rules.

**Issue 5 — `[data-ogsc]` block closes correctly** — the Outlook.com dark-mode override block has proper closure; no issue there.
