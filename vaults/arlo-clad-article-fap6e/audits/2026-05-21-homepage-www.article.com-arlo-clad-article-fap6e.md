# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the exact homepage I want to land on in late May — Article is running a proper Memorial Day sale and not hiding it. Clean, confident, Scandi-minimal brand codes I associate with them are intact. That said, the above-the-fold experience is almost entirely a sale announcement, not a product story, and for someone like me who's been eyeing a specific sofa for six months, there's nothing here that says "this is the moment to pull the trigger on *that* piece."

## What's working

- **The "MEMORIAL DAY SALE" hero headline** — I wait all year for this kind of event on Article. Seeing it in big, confident type immediately makes me want to tap in.
- **The sage green color palette** — On-brand for Article's Scandinavian aesthetic and genuinely calming on mobile. Doesn't feel like a frantic Black Friday page.
- **The dismissible top banner** — It's there as a second reinforcement of the sale offer but doesn't block anything. I can close it. No dark pattern.
- **The dining table lifestyle photo at the bottom of the fold** — That light wood table with the upholstered chairs is exactly the kind of piece I browse. Even partially cropped, it gives the sale context and makes this feel like a home, not a warehouse.
- **The search bar** — High on the page, above the hero. As someone who already knows what I want (the Sven, the Ceni), I can skip the sale browse and go direct.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP NOW" is doing too much heavy lifting** — It could go to the full sale, or to sofas, or to outdoor furniture. I have no idea. For someone who cross-shops Article vs. Joybird vs. Inside Weather, I want a CTA that says "Shop Sofas" or "Shop Living Room" so I know I'm heading to my section.
- **"for a limited time" is not a deadline** — I buy one big piece a year and I wait for the right window. If there's no end date visible, I'll assume the sale runs through Monday and come back later. Probably from Joybird's site.
- **No delivery / white-glove info visible** — This is a furniture brand. Delivery is a huge part of the purchase decision. I read every delivery FAQ before I buy. Not a whisper of it above the fold.
- **No social proof** — No review stars, no "12,000+ reviews" badge, nothing. On a $900–$2,000 purchase, I want some reassurance before I even tap through.
- **The sale categories ("Outdoor, Dining Room, Bedroom, and more") bury living room/sofas in "and more"** — That's the highest-intent category for someone like me. It should be named explicitly.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "SHOP NOW" with two or three category CTAs** — "Shop Sofas," "Shop Dining," "Shop Outdoor" as a three-button row. I tap the one that matches what I'm here for instead of landing on a generic sale page.
- **Add a sale end date to the hero subhead** — Change "for a limited time" to "Ends Monday, May 26" (or whatever the real date is). Creates real urgency without being manipulative, and gives me a reason to decide today instead of Sunday night.
- **Drop a one-line delivery badge under the CTA** — Something like "Free delivery + 30-day returns on orders over $999." One line. Doesn't take space, immediately addresses the biggest furniture purchase anxiety.
- **Add "Living Room" to the listed categories in the hero copy** — Right now it says Outdoor, Dining Room, Bedroom. Sofas are my primary target and they're invisible in that list.
