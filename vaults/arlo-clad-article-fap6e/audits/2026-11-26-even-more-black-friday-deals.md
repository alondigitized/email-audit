# Even more Black Friday deals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth near-identical Black Friday send in two weeks. The creative is the same monochrome "BLACK FRIDAY SALE" hero and coral "SHOP NOW" pill that appeared on Nov 16 ("It's go time"), Nov 19 ("It's on") and Nov 21 ("Black Friday deals are live"). The new angle is "NEW STYLES ADDED" and "even more." The only visible change is a banner and one line of body copy, so a subscriber will probably see it as the same email again.
- The offer is clear but small. "Up to 40% off" appears once, in small white text on a black band under the hero, and the hero itself carries no number. The 8-tile category grid ("Shop Living Room," "Shop Bedroom," and so on) is clean and easy to scan. But it sends the reader to eight generic destinations with no hero product, price or review. The darkened tile overlays also make the photography look muddy.
- Verdict: a competent but fatigued send. It is clear and has no render bugs, but nothing in it justifies a fourth email on the same theme. The "new styles" news is buried and never shown.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and instantly readable, and the coral "SHOP NOW" pill stands out against the black.
- The 8-tile category grid is tidy and scannable, and each label is a clear verb plus a noun.
- "Up to 40% off" is stated in plain language and sounds honest.
- The footer has useful trust signals: the 30-day Satisfaction Guarantee, Help Center, and Shipping and Delivery.
- The render has no broken images, overlapping text or layout problems.

## What's weak

- The hero is the same as the Nov 16, 19 and 21 sends, so the email feels stale rather than "even more."
- "Up to 40%" is in small text below the hero, so the eye lands on "SALE" with no number.
- "NEW STYLES ADDED" promises news, but no new product is shown anywhere.
- The tile overlays are dark and the white labels sit over busy photos, which hurts legibility and the look of the furniture.
- The two small outlined footer buttons ("Explore what's new," "Shop best sellers") are weak CTAs. The black "Black Friday deals" line adds little beyond the earlier copy.
- There is no urgency or end date, and nothing shows reviews, pricing or financing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show the actual new arrivals. Use a hero or a 3-up of just-added products with "was/now" prices and one "up to 40% off" headline. This is the biggest lift because it delivers on "new styles."
- 2. Move the discount into the hero, for example "UP TO 40% OFF" at the largest size, and drop the generic "SALE" block.
- 3. Vary the creative from the earlier sends so it doesn't read as a fourth repeat, and add a real deadline such as "Ends Cyber Monday."
- 4. Lighten the tile overlays, or put the labels on a solid strip. Add a starting price to each tile ("Living Room from $X").
- 5. Add a review or bestseller proof point and a delivery-timing reassurance for holiday orders.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has no confirmed hard failures (0 issues, 4 warnings from QA). The source has malformed dark-mode CSS, three images with no `alt` attribute, and tracking redirects that hide the final destinations. The compliance and authentication headers could not be verified from this capture.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Destinations unverifiable:** Links and images use redirect wrappers, so UTM parameters and final landing URLs can't be checked from the source. The wrappers are `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/...~~/AAQVbhA~/...` and `links.email.article.com/s/eo/...`. Only 12 of 27 links were probed, and the QA output reports no broken links among them.
- **Mixed tracking domains:** The email uses the branded `links.email.article.com` alongside the generic `post.spmailtechnol.com`. The generic domain is a shared-reputation risk, and users may see it on hover.
- **Plain-text part:** 2416 of 3360 characters (72%) are URLs, so the long tracking URLs dominate the text version. QA flags this, and spam filters can penalize the ratio.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` (3 images, from QA):**
  - `…/BBZICyZpGCxNS1Zp_ZDFJJCnEpTmKwFPGD6fjnh603ZEzoNl8o304` (src on `post.spmailtechnol.com`)
  - `…/zEzVzLwrXOC_kQp_S0kEIwq0lsyeKCAa79fFR7dp19qVOfQ_k5J4I` (src on `post.spmailtechnol.com`)
  - `links.email.article.com/s/eo/j9iEhMkqcOfbuQMIJbPUiPwS979…` (src on `links.email.article.com`). The `/s/eo/` path suggests an open-tracking pixel, which should carry `alt=""`.
  - Content images without `alt` give screen-reader users nothing to read and show nothing when images are blocked.
- **Empty `<title></title>`:** Screen readers and some clients use the title.
- **Malformed dark-mode CSS (first `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block):**
  - `h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}` has a trailing comma, which makes the selector list invalid. Strict parsers drop the whole rule, so heading and paragraph colors won't invert.
  - `a{color:#fff;!important}` has a misplaced `!important`, so the declaration is invalid.
  - `line-height:auto!important` is not a valid value.
  - The block has one closing `}` fewer than it needs before `</style>`. Most parsers auto-close it, but the result is parser-dependent.
- **Positive:** `lang="en"`, the viewport meta and the `color-scheme` meta are present. The `[data-ogsc]` Outlook block is well-formed.
