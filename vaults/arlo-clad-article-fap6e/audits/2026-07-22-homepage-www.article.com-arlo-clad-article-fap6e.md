# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-07-22
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Article is running a full outdoor push right now, which makes sense for July, and honestly the wood close-up in the hero is doing a lot of heavy lifting — it's tactile and warm in a way that immediately reads "quality furniture brand." But I came here scoping sofas (I've been watching the Sven for three months), and this page sends me straight to the patio, not the living room. It looks like a premium DTC brand that knows its aesthetic; it just isn't reading my mind today.

## What's working

- **The wood close-up hero image** — whoever art-directed this understood that furniture people are texture-obsessed. That grain makes me want to touch the screen. It earns trust for the brand immediately.
- **"The great (looking) outdoors."** — the parenthetical is a nice copywriting move. It's self-aware, a little witty, millennial register. Doesn't feel like it was written by a 52-year-old VP of Marketing.
- **The free Interior Design Plan banner** — this is actually useful for me. I buy one big piece a year and I overthink it. A free stylist consult is exactly the kind of thing that tips a considered buyer over the line.
- **Search bar above the fold** — saves me when the hero isn't relevant. I can type "sofa" and pivot without hunting through nav.

## What's weak

- **Outdoor campaign, indoor shopper** — I'm not here for patio furniture. The entire hero and CTA are misaligned with what I'd actually buy on Article. No secondary pathway to sofas or living room above the fold.
- **Zero price signal** — I wait for the rare 10%-off promo. There's nothing here that tells me whether there's a sale on, what the shipping threshold is, or why today is the day to buy. No urgency, no hook.
- **No social proof** — Article has genuinely strong reviews and a cult following on Reddit and Twitter. None of that is visible here. Not a single star rating, badge, or "as seen in" signal above the fold.
- **The hamburger menu hides everything** — on mobile, all the category navigation is buried behind a tap. If the hero isn't for me, I have no visible shortcut to "Sofas" without extra taps.

## Recommendations

- **Add a secondary CTA strip below the hero** — something like a row of three category pills ("Sofas · Dining · Bedroom") so people who bounced off the outdoor push have an instant reroute.
- **Drop a shipping/promo strip above the hero** — even a simple "Free white-glove delivery on orders over $999" banner would stop someone like me from leaving. That's the info I'm hunting for before I even look at a product.
- **Swap the banner copy to a time-sensitive hook** — "Free Interior Design Plan — available through July 31" would give the same offer urgency that actually makes me act now versus bookmark-and-forget.
- **Add a trust badge cluster under the CTA** — Article's review count and delivery reputation are real differentiators. A "50,000+ 5-star reviews · Free returns" line under "SHOP OUTDOOR" would convert the fence-sitters.
