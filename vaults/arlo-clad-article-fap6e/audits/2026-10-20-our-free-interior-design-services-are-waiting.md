# Our free interior design services are waiting
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-structured service explainer. Article is selling its free interior design service instead of a product or a discount. A three-step story (Tell us about your space, Get your design plan, Shop the styles) carries the reader from the hero down to a repeated "Start your project" button. The rust and peach palette matches Article's warm, mid-century look, and the 3D room plans and Instagram-style room shots show what you get.
- The email is easy to follow and honest about the offer, which is "it's free." Its weakness is that it has no price hook, no deadline and no named product. After the 10-09 "Up to 50% off clearance styles" send and the 10-16 Melo launch, it's a change of pace, but it asks for the most effort from the reader: filling out a project brief. The same CTA three times in near-identical peach pills also makes it feel repetitive.
- Overall it's a solid, trustworthy send for someone furnishing a room, but it won't move anyone who isn't already planning a project.

## What's working

- The three "STEP ONE / TWO / THREE" labels with large serif headlines make the whole flow readable in a few seconds of scrolling.
- The hero shows swatches, a hand and a tablet with a floor plan, so the service looks real and concrete.
- "And the best part? It's free." is stated plainly in the dark brown band under the hero, so there's no buried fine print.
- The product-and-floor-plan image pairs in step two show what the design plan looks like. The step-three room photos carry @rusticwhiteinteriors and @marinarossa credits, which add some social proof.
- The "Shop worry free" testimonial (Ariana T., Austin, TX) and the 30-day returns link lower the risk of buying after the design step.

## What's weak

- No price offer, deadline or member benefit appears anywhere. "Are waiting" in the subject is the only urgency.
- The hero button says "LEARN MORE," while every other button says "START YOUR PROJECT." The weaker copy sits on the highest-attention button.
- The three peach "START YOUR PROJECT" pills are identical and sit on a peach background. The orange-on-peach contrast is soft, so the buttons don't stand out as much as they could.
- Step three, "Shop the styles," shows two room photos but no products, prices or links to specific items. After a service pitch it never turns into a shopping moment.
- The "Shop worry free" returns block and the footer links ("Explore what's new," "Shop best sellers") pull attention away from the single design-service goal.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a specific outcome in the hero, such as "Free 3D room plan in 48 hours." Change the hero button to match the rest: "Start your free project."
- 2. Make step three shoppable. Tag two or three products in the photos with names and prices, and link each to its product page.
- 3. Add a real reason to act now, such as a deadline or a perk for new projects, for example free shipping on the first design-plan order.
- 4. Differentiate the buttons. Use a solid dark or rust fill for the primary CTA and keep the peach pills only for secondary actions.
- 5. Move the testimonial and returns promise up, closer to step one, where hesitation about starting a project is highest. Cut one of the two footer shop buttons.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your room, designed for free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Share your style, get furniture layouts and 3D designs. No cost, no catch.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard ESP template (tracked links on `links.email.article.com` and `post.spmailtechnol.com`). QA found 0 hard failures. The header-based compliance and authentication warnings are most likely artifacts of the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed from the data. The confirmed issues are malformed dark-mode CSS, an empty `<title>`, and three images without alt text. The HTML source is truncated, so findings are limited to what was visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Probing covered 12 material links and skipped 18 (footer, utility and social links were deprioritized). The QA output reports no broken or redirecting links among the probed set. Skipped links are unverified.
- Image and link tracking uses two different hosts: `links.email.article.com` (branded) and `post.spmailtechnol.com` (ESP-owned, unbranded). Examples are `https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/Ai9kdwWlvESDpFJuTnPZXw~~/AAQVbhA~/...` and `https://links.email.article.com/s/eo/YTnlmsr8-...`. Serving images through the unbranded redirect domain is a minor reputation and consistency risk. A block on that domain would also break those images. Neither is confirmed as a failure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (3 images, QA-confirmed):**
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/Ai9kdwWlvESDpFJuTnPZXw~~/AAQVbhA~/sJM65b03...`
  - `src=https://links.email.article.com/s/eo/YTnlmsr8-...` (the QA labels it "25"). The `/s/eo/` path may be an open-tracking pixel. If so, an empty `alt=""` is the correct fix, not descriptive text.
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/PBpqpzxEdeT20SZukfcl0Q~~/AAQVbhA~/qB5LPfrv...`
  - With images blocked, or for screen-reader users, any of these that are content images will have no fallback.
- **Empty `<title></title>`** in `<head>`. Some clients and web-view versions use it, and accessibility tools flag it.
- **Malformed dark-mode CSS** in the first `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block:
  - `h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}` has a trailing comma, so parsers treat the selector list as invalid and drop the whole rule. Heading and paragraph colors won't be forced in native dark mode.
  - `a{color:#fff;!important}` has a stray `!important` outside a declaration. The `color:#fff` still applies, but without `!important`, so it can lose to inline styles.
  - That `@media` block closes only the last rule (`a .link{color:#fff!important}`) before `</style>`, with no closing brace for the `@media` itself. Browsers recover, but strict parsers may not.
  - The `[data-ogsc]` (Outlook.com dark mode) block is well-formed (`...}}</style>`), so it likely works, but it duplicates the broken rules.
- `lang="en"`, `color-scheme` meta and the viewport meta are present. No issues there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`) were visible in the truncated source, and the QA reported none.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA itself says AgentMail may not capture these headers, so this is unverified, not a confirmed violation. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both for senders at volume, so verify them against raw headers from a direct (non-relay) copy.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is expected via the relay and is not a confirmed defect. The From domain is `email.article.com`, so check alignment with the ESP's signing domain.
- The QA states an unsubscribe link was included in probing. The footer, physical address and unsubscribe markup are not in the truncated source, so they can't be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. Destination URLs are hidden behind tracking redirects in the visible source, and the QA does not report final landing URLs or UTM parameters. No mismatch is confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the dark-mode CSS: remove the trailing comma in `h1,h2,p,{`, change `a{color:#fff;!important}` to `a{color:#fff!important}`, and close the `@media` block.
2. Add `alt` text to the two content images and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel (if the `/s/eo/` image is one).
3. Add a meaningful `<title>`, for example the subject line.
4. Re-test headers on a direct send (not via the relay) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass and alignment.
5. Consider serving images through the branded `links.email.article.com` domain instead of `post.spmailtechnol.com`.
6. Follow the redirect chains on the skipped links and the primary CTAs to check landing URLs and UTM parameters.
