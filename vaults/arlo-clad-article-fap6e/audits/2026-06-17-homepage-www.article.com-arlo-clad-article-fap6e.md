# Homepage snapshot · www.article.com · 2026-06-17
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Article looks like the kind of brand I'd actually follow on Instagram — clean Scandinavian-ish aesthetic, unfussy photography, no-nonsense copy. Right now they're pushing outdoor lounge hard, which makes sense for June, but as someone who came here to research a new sofa, this sends me sideways from minute one. A solid brand signal, just pointed at the wrong aisle for me today.

## What's working

- **"Relax already." headline** — punchy and confident without being try-hard. Exactly the register I respond to. Doesn't over-explain.
- **The outdoor lounge photography** — the modular cream sofa, the concrete pedestals, the wood framing — this is legitimately good furniture photography. Even though it's outdoor, I stop to look because the design DNA matches what I care about.
- **Search bar front-and-center** — as someone who comes in with a specific mission (sofas, maybe the Sven), I'll use this immediately. It's not buried.
- **Top email banner is dismissible** — one tap on the X and it's gone. Not a modal hijack.
- **Seasonal coherence** — the subhead "The sun's not going to wait" pairs naturally with the June timing. This doesn't feel stale.

## What's weak

- **No offer above the fold** — I'm a wait-for-the-promo buyer. I check Article every few weeks for a 10%-off window. Nothing here tells me if there's a promo running. That's a bounce trigger.
- **Wrong category hero for returning shoppers** — anyone who's already bought indoor furniture from Article and came back for more gets pushed into outdoor immediately. No "explore living room" or "sofas" shortcut above the fold.
- **No trust anchors** — no review counts, no "free shipping on orders over $X," no returns mention. For a $1,500+ sofa purchase, that absence matters.
- **Navigation requires an extra tap** — hamburger menu means I can't see categories until I open it. If I want to browse sofas in one tap, I can't. The search bar compensates but it's still friction.
- **No people in the imagery** — I post furniture shots specifically because I like the "room as lifestyle" framing. But for a homepage, a lifestyle scene with a real human in the space would land harder emotionally.

## Recommendations

- **Add a promo strip or free-shipping callout** below the hero — even "Free shipping on orders over $999" changes the calculus for me immediately. I'm already thinking about whether my cart total qualifies.
- **Swap or add a secondary CTA** below the outdoor hero that routes to the living room / sofas category — something like "SHOP SOFAS" or "EXPLORE INDOOR" so returning visitors who know what they want aren't stranded.
- **Include at least one social-proof anchor above the fold** — a "★ 4.8 from 12,000 reviews" trust badge or a single pull quote costs nothing and changes the mood for high-consideration buyers like me.
- **Put a lifestyle human in the hero** — replace the empty-room outdoor shot with a scene where someone's actually using the sofa. The furniture alone is beautiful, but the aspirational hit doubles when I can see myself in the frame.
