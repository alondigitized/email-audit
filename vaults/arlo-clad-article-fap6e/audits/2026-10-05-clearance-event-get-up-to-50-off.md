# Clearance Event: Get up to 50% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, no-nonsense clearance blast, and it works. A saturated coral-pink gradient hero carries "Fall Clearance / Up to 50% off." in huge black type. The one-line body copy and a white "SHOP NOW" pill sit right under it. The eye lands on the offer first, and the subject and hero say the same thing.
- It's also a shift in cadence. Article's last four sends (09-15 "Home tour: A welcoming workspace", 09-18 "Storage solutions for every room", 09-23 "How to: Create an organized entryway", and 10-02 "Home tour: A space fit for fall") were all editorial with no discount. A subscriber who has watched that run gets a real price reason to click here, and this email earns more attention than the recent editorials did.
- The weakness is specificity. "Up to 50%" and "tons of styles" don't show any product, price or deadline. The four category tiles are muted, low-contrast photos with overlaid text, and none of them shows an actual markdown. The urgency is soft: "before they're gone for good" has no date. It's a solid promo, but a generic one.

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is excellent. "Up to 50% off." is the largest element, and the contrast of black on coral is strong.
- The subject, hero and banner all say the same thing, so the message is clear.
- The "SHOP NOW" pill is isolated and obvious, and it's visible without scrolling.
- The four tiles (Living Room, Bedroom, Decor, Outdoor) use "Shop + room" labels, which are clear verb-plus-noun CTAs, and they give browsers a quick way in.
- The footer trust row (Help Center, 30-day Satisfaction Guarantee, Shipping and Delivery) reduces purchase anxiety. The render has no visible bugs.

## What's weak

- The email shows no individual product, price or "was/now" figure, so "up to 50%" is unproven.
- There's no deadline or end date, so the urgency in "before they're gone for good" isn't credible.
- The tile photos are dimmed and grey, and the white overlay text is only moderately legible. None of the tiles says "up to X% off" or "clearance".
- The coral banner repeats the hero ("Clearance Event on now: Get up to 50% off") and adds nothing.
- The footer buttons ("Explore what's new", "Shop best sellers") pull attention away from clearance, which is the point of the email. There's no loyalty or member angle and no reviews or social proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace at least two tiles with real clearance products showing the was/now price and a clear "Shop Sofas" style CTA. Proof of the discount would lift clicks more than anything else.
- 2. Add a real deadline in the hero or the banner, such as "Ends Sunday". Alternatively, say something concrete like "While supplies last: 1,200+ styles marked down".
- 3. Put the discount on each category tile ("Living Room: up to 50% off"), and brighten the photos so the text reads cleanly.
- 4. Cut the redundant coral banner, or turn it into a countdown or free-shipping line. Drop the footer pills below the fold, or point them at clearance subcategories.
- 5. Add one social-proof line, such as a review count or a "best-seller now on sale" tag.

# Technical Audit: "Clearance Event: Get up to 50% off" (hello@email.article.com)

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has a sound responsive, dark-mode-aware structure, but it has malformed dark-mode CSS, an empty `<title>`, and three images without alt text. Most compliance, authentication and link-destination checks could not be verified. The HTML is truncated, and the QA probe covered only 1 of 12 material links.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probe coverage is minimal.** QA probed 1 of 12 material links and skipped 11. Destination status for the other 11 is unknown, and the QA output doesn't say which link was probed or what it returned.
- **Mixed tracking domains.** Two images are served through `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/...~~/AAQVbhA~/...`. Another uses `links.email.article.com/s/eo/...`. The first is a third-party, non-branded tracking host that doesn't match the sender domain `email.article.com`. This can hurt reputation and alignment, and it's a risk if that host is blocklisted by a filter.
- **Images are routed through redirect URLs.** The `post.spmailtechnol.com` image sources are tracker URLs rather than direct CDN paths. If the tracking host is blocked or slow, images may fail to load.
- No broken links are confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA-confirmed, 3 images):**
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/mhcR9BEgmC1XgZpu4Ltl9g~~/AAQVbhA~/hQqrfY8pqO7n32Xvlnphuz_...`
  - `src=https://links.email.article.com/s/eo/w66HPbOe9RL1ZNN2qqdKLWtS78KLDVNg...`
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/aBCrawqyD4klG8Tf2jYetA~~/AAQVbhA~/gWtyM_O838TF-...`
  
  With images blocked, or in a screen reader, these are unlabeled or announced by filename.
- **Empty `<title></title>`** in `<head>`. This affects the accessible name and web-view or archive rendering.
- **Invalid dark-mode CSS** (in the `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block):
  - `h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}` has a trailing comma. That makes the selector list invalid, so the whole rule is dropped. Headings and paragraphs would not be forced to white in `prefers-color-scheme` clients (Apple Mail, iOS). The `[data-ogsc]` equivalent is written correctly, so Outlook.com behaves differently.
  - `a{color:#fff;!important}` has a misplaced `!important` after the semicolon. The declaration is malformed, and the color is not forced.
  - `line-height:auto!important` on `.dark-img` is not a valid value and is ignored.
  - The `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark){` block is never closed. The following `<style>` block ends with a stray extra `}`. Most parsers recover, but Gmail is known to drop a whole `<style>` block on parse errors, so this is a risk.
- Positives: `lang="en"`, the viewport meta, the `color-scheme` metas, and Outlook conditional comments are all present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the subject or the visible HTML. The source is truncated, so this covers only the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found (QA WARN)** and **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found (QA WARN)**. QA itself says the AgentMail relay may strip these headers. This is unverified, not a confirmed defect. If the headers are genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements are not met.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (QA WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay, so this can't be assessed.
- The body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address are not visible in the truncated HTML, so neither can be verified. QA says an unsubscribe link is always included in probing, but it reports no result for it.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. The visible HTML shows no destination URLs or UTM parameters. Links are wrapped in redirect trackers (`links.email.article.com/s/eo/...`, `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/...`), and 11 of 12 links were not probed. No mismatch is confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add alt text** to the three flagged images. Use descriptive alt for content images and `alt=""` for decorative ones.
2. **Fix the dark-mode CSS:**
   - Change `h1,h2,p,{` to `h1,h2,p{`.
   - Change `a{color:#fff;!important}` to `a{color:#fff!important}`.
   - Remove `line-height:auto`.
   - Close the `@media` block and remove the stray `}` from the `[data-ogsc]` style block.
3. **Populate `<title>`** with the subject line or a meaningful string.
4. **Verify the headers** (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) against the raw message at the origin. The AgentMail relay is the likely reason they are missing.
5. **Check the tracking domain.** Branded tracking (`links.email.article.com`) is already in use for some links. Consolidating the images and links onto it, instead of `spmailtechnol.com`, would improve alignment.
6. **Improve QA coverage.** Re-run link probing with a higher cap so the unsubscribe link and the primary CTAs are probed and their final URLs and UTMs recorded. Use the untruncated HTML to confirm the footer address, the unsubscribe link and any merge tokens.
