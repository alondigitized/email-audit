# Up to 40% off ends Monday
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Article's deadline-reminder send for their Memorial Day Sale — the second promotional email in three days, following the May 19 "deals are in full swing" opener. It does exactly what a countdown email should: one clear message, hard deadline, shop-by-category grid. Clean layout, zero render bugs, tight subject line. The execution is disciplined.
- The problem is it's essentially the same email subscribers saw three days ago. Same green palette, same "UP TO 40% OFF" headline, same category tile approach. For a first-time opener this works perfectly well; for someone who already opened the May 19 send, there's no new hook — no "last chance," no featured product, no "we've added new items." A deadline email that looks identical to the announcement email leaves conversion on the table.
- This is a competent, safe send. For a subscriber ready to buy furniture, it's more than good enough. For a subscriber on the fence, it doesn't close.

## What's working

- **"ENDS MONDAY" urgency banner** is the first visual element — deadline is unmissable before the subject line even registers
- **Subject line is genuinely tight** at 25 chars: a specific discount plus a hard date, no waste
- **2×2 category tile grid** (Outdoor, Dining Room, Bedroom, All Sale) lets shoppers self-route without cluttering the hero with dozens of SKUs
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA** sits directly under the hero headline — no hunting required
- **Article's aesthetic stays consistent** — the Scandinavian-minimal palette and lifestyle photography match what the brand has trained subscribers to expect

## What's weak

- **No creative differentiation from the May 19 send** — a subscriber who opened that email will feel déjà vu; nothing signals this is a final-hours reminder vs. the original announcement
- **No specific product with a price** — "up to 40% off hundreds of styles" is abstract; a single hero item at "was $X, now $Y" would anchor desire immediately
- **Zero social proof** — no bestseller callout, no review count, no "X people are viewing this chair" — especially missing given this is a deadline push where herd behavior converts
- **Preview text likely repeats the subject** — the visible body copy ("Hurry — you don't want to miss up to 40% off ... only until Monday") hits the same "40% off" + "Monday" beats already in the subject line
- **Category tile photography is small and editorial** — individual furniture pieces aren't distinct enough to spark specific desire

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to one specific product at a named price.** The May 19 send used the generic sale banner; this reminder should do something it didn't — show a single aspirational piece (e.g., the Sven sofa: was $1,299, now $779) to give fence-sitters a concrete reason to act today rather than "maybe tomorrow."
- 2. **Reframe the hero copy as a genuine last-chance send.** Replace "MEMORIAL DAY SALE" with "FINAL 4 DAYS" or "SALE ENDS MONDAY" as the visual headliner — not buried in the green banner but as the primary typographic statement.
- 3. **Preheader should add information the subject doesn't contain.** Currently both channels say "40% off" + "Monday." The preheader slot should carry something new: free shipping, a specific category ("outdoor furniture included"), or a social proof hook.
- 4. **Add one bestseller or staff-pick module** between the hero and the category grid — a single "most-viewed this weekend" tile adds social proof and gives the email something the May 19 send didn't have.

- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off furniture — final hours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping through Monday — outdoor, dining, bedroom, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our most-viewed pieces this weekend are still in stock`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**CSS Error 1 — Trailing comma in dark mode selector (drops entire rule)**
```css
/* Inside @media(prefers-color-scheme:dark) */
h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}
```
The dangling comma before `{` is an invalid selector. CSS parsers that follow the spec will silently discard this entire rule, leaving `h1`, `h2`, and `p` text uncolored in dark mode on supporting clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Outlook iOS).

**CSS Error 2 — Semicolon before `!important` (drops the declaration)**
```css
a{color:#fff;!important}
```
The `;` terminates the declaration before `!important` is parsed. The `!important` token is then treated as an unknown at-rule and discarded. Link color override in dark mode will not apply.

**Minor:**
- `<title></title>` is empty. Several clients (Gmail web tab, Outlook preview pane) surface the title. Not a rendering blocker but a missed inbox-preview opportunity.
