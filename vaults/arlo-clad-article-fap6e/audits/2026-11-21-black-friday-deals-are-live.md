# Black Friday deals are live
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the Black Friday sale email for Nov 21, and it is the same creative as the Nov 16 "It's go time" and Nov 19 "It's on" sends. It has the same black "UP TO 40% OFF" band, the same monochrome "BLACK FRIDAY SALE" hero and the same coral "SHOP NOW" pill. The only new thing is the "live" framing in the subject. A subscriber who has seen this hero three times will not read it as news.
- The layout is clean and the offer is easy to find. There is one number, one button, and a tidy 2x4 grid of room tiles for browsing. The email never shows a product, a price, or an end date. "Only for a limited time" is the only urgency, and it has no deadline behind it.
- Judgment: competent but repetitive. It works as a reminder for someone already planning to buy furniture, and it gives no reason to act today.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 40% OFF" band and the oversized "SALE" headline put the offer first in the visual hierarchy.
- The coral "SHOP NOW" pill stands out clearly against the black and gray hero.
- The 2x4 room grid (Living Room, Bedroom, Dining Room, Entryway, Home Office, Outdoor, Decor, All Sale) lets a shopper pick their own path. The tile labels are verb plus noun.
- The 30-Day Satisfaction Guarantee appears in the body copy and again in the footer icon row, which lowers purchase risk.
- I saw no broken images, overlapping text or layout problems.

## What's weak

- The creative repeats the Nov 16 and Nov 19 sends almost exactly, so this is the fourth Black Friday email since Nov 8 and the offer hasn't escalated.
- "Up to 40%" is vague. The email doesn't say which categories reach 40% or what the typical discount is.
- There is no deadline. "Limited time" has no date or countdown, so nothing makes the reader act now.
- No product, price or review appears anywhere. The tiles are lifestyle crops with white text over dark overlays, so the "Shop Living Room" and "Shop Entryway" labels are hard to read.
- The hero is a dark gradient with no furniture in it, so it doesn't look like a furniture sale until you reach the tiles.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real deadline and a real escalation. Put an end date in the black band ("Up to 40% off · ends Monday 11:59pm PT") and give the Nov 21 send something the earlier ones didn't have, such as a deeper discount or a named doorbuster.
- 2. Replace the empty gradient hero with one hero product at a real price ("Isla sofa, $X, was $Y"), so the first screen shows what is on sale.
- 3. Show the discount depth on the tiles ("Living Room: up to 40% off") and put a lighter overlay or label chip behind the text so it is legible.
- 4. Add a line of social proof or a bestseller with star ratings above the footer.
- 5. Give the reader a reason to open this send rather than skip it as a repeat.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your room, up to 40% off. Today's doorbusters inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, beds and dining sets, plus free returns for 30 days.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale prices end Monday at 11:59pm PT. Start with living room.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with light/dark-mode support. The QA run found 3 images with missing or non-descriptive alt text and CSS errors in the dark-mode blocks. It could not confirm the unsubscribe headers or authentication, because the AgentMail relay did not capture them. The HTML is truncated, so findings below are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Mixed click-tracking domains.** Images and links go through two redirectors: `post.spmailtechnol.com/q/...~~/AAQVbhA~/...` and `links.email.article.com/s/eo/...`. A split like this can mean two systems are rewriting links. It adds redirect hops and gives two domains whose reputation and alignment can fail independently.
- **Opaque redirect URLs.** The destination URLs and any UTM parameters sit inside encoded redirect tokens, so they cannot be read from the source.
- **Partial link coverage.** Only 12 material links were probed and 16 were skipped. The 12 probed links produced no failures. The 16 skipped links (footer, utility, social) were not verified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 2,367 of 3,335 characters (about 71%) are URLs. This is a spam-filter risk. It comes from long tracking URLs inflating the text part.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Three images have missing or non-descriptive alt text.**
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/tP68-07KTSo4PSgtKsAiFQ~~/AAQVbhA~/Ez0dKE42...`
  - `src=https://links.email.article.com/s/eo/S7X2VOi3fnG1ESW4tbalgxofc6pLBVMjsV5gW4YOlC1...`. The QA flagged its alt value as `26`, so the alt is just a number.
  - `src=https://post.spmailtechnol.com/q/uPJwGVfrKm7Cudg7ihbSuA~~/AAQVbhA~/C-5zSxCqqLYpkBJXdRJaBSXxbox-fmVvCndWCBn1s8m2DW1Z4n...`
- **Empty `<title></title>`.** Some clients and screen readers use the title.
- **Malformed CSS in the first `@media(prefers-color-scheme:dark)` block:**
  - `h1,h2,p,{color:#fff!important}` has a trailing comma, which creates an empty selector. Browsers drop the whole rule, so headings and paragraphs don't get the dark-mode text color.
  - `a{color:#fff;!important}` has the `!important` in the wrong place. `color:#fff` still applies, but without `!important`, so it loses to any inline link color.
  - The `@media` block is never closed before `</style>`. Parsers recover from this by auto-closing the block.
  - `a .link` is a descendant selector. It probably should be `a.link`.
- **Stray `}` at the end of the `[data-ogsc]` style block.** It has an extra closing brace (`...{color:#fff!important}}`), and its rules are not inside a media query. Apart from the stray brace, this is the normal Outlook.com pattern.
- **Dark-mode risk.** Because of the dropped heading and paragraph rule, text may be wrong in clients that follow `prefers-color-scheme`. This is inferred from the CSS and not tested in a client.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** The QA tool says the AgentMail relay may have stripped it.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe can't be confirmed. Major mailbox providers expect this for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, again likely because of the relay.
- All three warnings are likely capture limitations and not confirmed sender defects. Verify against the original message headers. The unsubscribe link was included in the probe set, but no result was reported for it. The footer's physical address was not visible in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No confirmed issues. The destination URLs and UTMs are hidden behind the `spmailtechnol.com` and `links.email.article.com` redirectors, so landing-page alignment can't be checked from the source. The 12 probed links produced no failures.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive alt text to the 3 flagged images. Use `alt=""` for decorative ones, and replace the numeric alt `26`.
2. Fix the dark-mode CSS:
   - Remove the trailing comma in `h1,h2,p,{...}`.
   - Change `a{color:#fff;!important}` to `a{color:#fff!important}`.
   - Close the `@media` block.
   - Change `a .link` to `a.link`.
   - Remove the extra `}` after the `[data-ogsc]` rules.
3. Fill in `<title>`.
4. Check the raw message headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass with alignment.
5. Find out why two tracking domains are in use, and consolidate on one branded tracking domain if possible.
6. Shorten the plain-text part by removing tracking URLs or adding more readable text, to lower the URL ratio.
7. Probe the 16 skipped links and resolve the redirect chains to confirm UTMs and landing pages.
