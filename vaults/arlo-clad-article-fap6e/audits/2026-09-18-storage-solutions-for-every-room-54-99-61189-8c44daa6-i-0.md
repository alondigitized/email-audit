# Storage solutions for every room
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Article finally doing something other than pound the Labor Day discount drum — "Storage solutions for every room" is a clean, editorial, category-navigation email in the vein of the 2026-09-15 "Home tour" reset. No discount hero, no maroon-and-tan, no urgency banner. It's organized by use case (work-with-what-you've-got, set the scene, think small) with warm, well-styled lifestyle photography and clear section CTAs.
- The problem is there's no offer anywhere in this email — no price, no percentage off, no promo code. For a subscriber trained by six weeks of 40%-off blasts, this is a jarring value-prop switch with zero explanation of why. It reads more like a catalog page than a marketing email, which is fine as a change of pace but weak on urgency to click today versus browse later.
- Solid, tasteful, on-brand execution — but as a conversion vehicle it's soft, and the subject line undersells the visual quality inside.

## What's working

- Clear visual hierarchy: hero image, bold "Make some room." headline, single "Shop Storage" CTA above the fold.
- Consistent product photography style and warm, cohesive color palette (walnut, oak, cream) throughout.
- Logical room-by-room / need-by-need segmentation (desks, cabinets, wall shelves) makes scanning easy.
- Each section has its own labeled products and its own CTA button, so there are multiple clear paths to shop.
- "Shop Worry Free" trust module (30-day returns) near the footer adds credibility without cluttering the main flow.

## What's weak

- No offer, discount, or price visible anywhere in the email — pure browse-mode after weeks of price-led sends.
- Subject "Storage solutions for every room" is generic and undersells the actual editorial/lifestyle quality of the creative.
- No urgency or time-boundedness at all, which is fine tonally but does nothing to prompt an immediate click.
- Product names appear but no prices are shown under any product tile, adding friction for a comparison-shopper.
- Five back-to-back "Shop X" CTAs dilute the primary action — no single hero product is designated.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add at least one visible price point or "starting at $X" under the hero CTA — pure inspiration content converts worse than inspiration + price anchor.
- 2. Tighten the subject line to hint at the lifestyle angle, not just the category, to differentiate from a plain catalog email.
- 3. Add prices under each product tile (Leif Desk, Emera Cabinet, etc.) — shoppers comparing storage pieces want to see cost before clicking through.
- 4. Pick one hero SKU (e.g. the Wysen Cabinet) to feature above the fold with its own dedicated CTA, rather than four parallel section CTAs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Small space? Big storage ideas inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The desk-and-shelving combo that does it all`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wall-mounted, modular, or concealed — find the fit for your space`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New storage styles for every room, no floor space required`
