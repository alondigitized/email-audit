## Executive summary

- Sally Beauty again — I feel like I could draw this template from memory at this point. It's the "Appiversary" thing they hit me with a few days ago, still going, just now wrapped around a nail deal instead of the app-download push. The nail polish photo is honestly kind of pretty, but I do zero gel manicures at home and I'm not driving to Sally Beauty to buy dip powder between school drop-off and soccer practice.
- The 30% off nail essentials code is a real, specific offer, which is more than their last couple sends had. But it's buried under a giant "download the app" hero that has nothing to do with the subject line I opened for. Doesn't matter — swipe to archive, not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The subject line "Flawless nails, for less 💅" is at least concrete and on-topic with a real discount code (30NAILS) visible in the hero banner.
- The end date "Ends September 11" gives a credible, non-fake urgency window.
- Product tiles (Gel Pro polish, dip powder, nail glue, press-ons) are laid out cleanly with names, short descriptions, and individual "Shop now" links — easy to scan even though I'm not buying.

## What's weak

- The actual "Download the app" hero dominates the top of the email, not the nail offer — I have to scroll past app-download messaging to get to anything nail-related.
- Same red/pink Sally template, same rewards/points/Klarna footer stack I've seen in every one of their last five emails to me — nothing here feels new creatively.
- Nothing here speaks to me as a mom of two — no kid product, no family bundle, no reason this is relevant beyond "you're a woman who might paint her nails," which isn't really me these days.
- The rewards points tiers (2x/3x/4x) and Labor Day sale banner feel like a second, unrelated email got stapled onto the bottom of this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the nail offer, not the app download — put the 30% off code and product shots at the very top since that's what got me to open.
- 2. Cut the app-download hero entirely or shrink it to a small footer mention; it's competing with the actual offer for my attention.
- 3. Trim the redundant rewards/points/Labor Day blocks at the bottom — pick one message per email instead of stacking three campaigns.
- 4. If they want my clicks, show me something a busy parent could actually use in 5 minutes — press-on nails, not gel systems that need a lamp.
