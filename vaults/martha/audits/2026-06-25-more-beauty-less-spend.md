# More Beauty, Less Spend 💕
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- My first thought landing on this: Sally Beauty again. I've been counting, and this is roughly number seven since June 1 — I literally texted a friend "they just sent another one" two weeks ago as a running joke. That's the context this email walked into, and no creative can fully overcome that.
- Once I'm actually inside it, though? The deals are real. A full scrolling column of concrete percentage-off offers — 40% off It's 10 treatments, a BOGO on gel polish, hair color deals, a loyalty reward callout. For a mom who buys her own hair care when the kids are finally asleep and she remembers she ran out of something, there's genuine value here. It just doesn't earn the open the way it should.
- My call: skim, maybe tap the gel polish BOGO since I've needed to restock, then close. No forward — my friends have already heard me complain about Sally's send frequency this month.

## What's working

- The deal stack is real — 40% off It's 10 leave-in treatments caught my eye immediately, that's a product I actually reach for
- The loyalty reward ("Earn a $5 reward when you spend $50") reminded me I have an account and should be activating it
- Hero model looks like a woman in her 30s in summer mode — that's me, not a stock-photo 22-year-old
- Layout is clean on mobile; no hunting required, text is readable at glance size, tap targets feel comfortable
- Seasonal framing ("summer looks") matches what I'm actually thinking about right now

## What's weak

- "More Beauty, Less Spend" from the inbox tells me nothing — I already assume Sally has deals; I need to know *which* deal before I decide to open
- Clippers and trimmers near the bottom feel like they were copy-pasted from a different campaign entirely — that's not my drawer
- The $39.99 Brazilian Gloss Trio + Wax Warmer Bundle is a professional salon purchase; I'm not a stylist, I'm a mom with a bathroom shelf
- Seven sends in 25 days means each new email starts in a credibility hole; I'm aware I'm in a heavy-send stretch even when the content is decent
- Every single CTA says "Shop now" — by the fifth one they all blur together and I've lost the thread of which button does what

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the actual headline deal in the subject.** "40% Off It's 10 Leave-Ins + Free Gel Polish" makes me open. "More Beauty, Less Spend" makes me decide later.
- 2. **Give the hero one concrete offer.** The woman-with-great-hair image is doing brand work, but the first scroll shouldn't be blank of pricing — put the 40% off deal *in* the hero creative.
- 3. **Audit the product mix.** Cut clippers/trimmers or isolate them in a clearly labeled "Pro & Salon Picks" section. Don't make me scroll past men's grooming to find my gel polish.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTA copy per deal.** "Get 40% off," "Claim your free gel polish," "Shop BOGO hair color" — specific verbs make each section feel like its own moment.
- 5. **Signal cadence awareness.** "Your June savings lineup" or "Last chance before July" framing acknowledges that it's been a busy month and gives me a reason to care *now* rather than assume I'll get another one next week.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: `<meta>` tags missing `http-equiv` attribute**

Two meta declarations are syntactically incomplete:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, these tags are inert — charset declaration is not applied and the IE compatibility directive is ignored. Outlook on older Windows clients may fall back to Quirks mode.

**Issue: `Founders Grotesk` font referenced but never declared**

The global `a` rule sets:
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the stylesheet (only Open Sans variants are declared). All clients that don't have it installed locally will silently fall back to Helvetica. If this font is intentional, it needs a hosted `@font-face` declaration.

**Issue: `img { white-space: pre; }` is an unexpected rule**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` has no valid effect on replaced elements like `<img>` but can interfere with image rendering in some WebKit-based clients by preventing normal whitespace collapsing around the element. Should be removed.

**Issue: Stale Google Fonts version**

All six `@font-face` declarations pin Open Sans to `v17`:
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Current version is v40+. While Google Fonts versioned URLs remain accessible for backward compatibility, this indicates the template CSS has not been updated since approximately 2019 and may miss glyph coverage updates.

**Cannot assess:** `alt` attributes on `<img>` tags, color contrast ratios, and heading hierarchy — body content is truncated.
