# Time's running out on up to 40% off ⏰
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Another one. I actually laughed a little — Sally sent me "Shop deals up to 40% off" literally two days ago on June 23rd. Now it's "Time's running out on up to 40% off." Same offer, different subject line, 48 hours later. I feel like I'm watching the same commercial twice in one ad break.
- That said, the email itself is genuinely clean. The yellow hero banner, the "Up to 40% Off Online & In Store" headline, the free 2-hour delivery callout — it all scans fast. As someone who gets maybe 90 seconds to scroll before someone needs a snack, I appreciate a layout that doesn't make me work for it. The deals grid below shows real products with real percentage callouts: styling tools, fragrance, BOGO on hair care. I can see exactly what I'm getting.
- Would I click? Probably not — I already saw this sale on the 23rd and didn't bite. "Time's running out" isn't the nudge that changes that calculus. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read and move on? Yes.

## What's working

- The yellow hero grabs attention instantly — "Up to 40% Off Online & In Store" reads in one glance on mobile, no zooming required.
- The free 2-hour delivery callout is honestly more motivating to me than the % off — as a busy mom, "I can get this today without Amazon" matters.
- The "Don't miss out on Big Deals rewards & savings" section speaks to me by name — I am a rewards member and I notice when brands acknowledge it.
- The deals grid gives me specific numbers — 40% off styling tools, 20% off fragrance, BOGO 50% on hair care — rather than hiding behind "up to" vagueness the whole email.
- Layout is clean. No broken images, no overlapping text, clear visual hierarchy. It renders exactly right.

## What's weak

- This is functionally the same email they sent June 23rd with a new subject line. If I didn't act then, a "time's running out" re-skin 48 hours later isn't going to move me.
- No end date anywhere. "Time's running out" without a deadline is meaningless. I need "ends tonight" or "ends Sunday" to actually feel urgency.
- The $99.99 Infrared Dryer buried in "Online Only Offers" contradicts the value framing of the whole email. I came here for deals, not a triple-digit hair tool without context.
- Zero personalization — I'm a loyalty member and they still treat me like a cold stranger. Not even a "Hi Martha" at the top.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a hard deadline in the subject and the hero.** "Time's running out" is empty without a when. "Sale ends tonight at midnight" would actually trigger action.
- 2. **Stop re-sending the same offer 48 hours later to the full list.** If I didn't open or click on June 23rd, send me something genuinely different — a specific product pick, a "you haven't tried this yet" angle — not a subject line swap on the same email.
- 3. **Lead the hero with the most specific deal, not the widest range.** "40% off all styling tools" beats "up to 40% off" for my trust level.
- 4. **Swap "Shop Now" for a product-specific CTA.** A single "Best Deal Today" tile with one linked product would cut my friction in half compared to landing on a generic browse page.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free 2-hour delivery if you order before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hair tools, fragrance, BOGO hair care — pick yours before it's gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `img { white-space:pre; }` (high severity)**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```
`white-space:pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and can cause layout breaks in Outlook (all versions) and some Lotus Notes clients. It is likely a copy-paste artifact. Should be removed entirely.

**Issue 2 — Missing `http-equiv` on two meta tags (medium severity)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Both are missing the required `http-equiv` attribute. Without it, the charset declaration is advisory-only and the IE compatibility directive is ignored. Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Issue 3 — 'Founders Grotesk' has no `@font-face` declaration (medium severity)**
```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
The font is declared as primary for all anchor elements but has no corresponding `@font-face` block in the visible `<style>`. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica in every client. If the font is loaded via a linked stylesheet (not visible in the excerpt), confirm it survives ESP HTML sanitization.

**Issue 4 — Stale Google Fonts version (low severity)**
Six `@font-face` blocks reference `opensans/v17/` from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Current Open Sans is v40+. Google still serves legacy paths via redirect, but these can be slow or blocked. Most email clients (Gmail, Outlook) will ignore external font requests regardless, so this is low-impact but worth updating if the stylesheet is refreshed.

**Issue 5 — Mobile breakpoint at 499px (low severity)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)
```
Viewports 500–599px wide receive desktop layout. The conventional email breakpoint is 600px. Devices in the 500–599px range (some Android mid-range phones) will render the full desktop table. Flag for QA on affected device classes.

**Issue 6 — `font-display: swap` in `@font-face` (informational)**
`font-display: swap` has no effect in any current email client; it is a browser-only property. Not harmful, but adds dead CSS weight.
