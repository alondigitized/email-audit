# Everything they need for the first day and beyond
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — when I saw this in my inbox, the preview text made me audibly sigh. That same JSON schema dump, *again*. At this point I've lost count, but the 07-05 send was supposedly the "seventh straight" and we're now multiple sends past that. I almost swiped it away on reflex.
- But I opened it anyway because "first day" hit me right where I live — my daughter starts kindergarten this fall and my son is going into fourth grade. Good thing I did, because the actual email? Genuinely one of the best-built Back to School sends they've done. The BOGO is front and center, the age categories (Pre-School, Elementary) map exactly to my two kids, the light-up section has a sparkly pink shoe I could already picture on my daughter's feet. The Uniform category is perfect timing for my son's school.
- I clicked. I would not forward it to a friend because I'd be embarrassed to send them that preview text. But personally? This one earned the tap.

## What's working

- **BOGO offer is impossible to miss.** "Buy One, Get One 50% Off OR 50% Off a Single Pair" is the very first thing my eye lands on — that's smart banner placement for a budget-conscious mom buying for two kids at once.
- **Age categories are chef's kiss.** Pre-School (Tiny Kids) and Elementary (Big Kids) are exactly my two kids. I didn't have to do any mental math — Skechers did the segmentation work for me.
- **Light-up shoes section shows a pink sparkly shoe.** My five-year-old would have already grabbed my phone. That's the shoe she's going to want.
- **"Back to School Guide" framing.** The word "Guide" implies organization. As a mom with a mental checklist, that frames this email as useful, not just promotional.
- **Clean body layout, no broken images.** After a string of sends that had me bracing for chaos in the body, this one rendered beautifully.

## What's weak

- **That preview text. Again. Ten times.** At this point it's not a bug, it's a Skechers tradition. It actively suppresses my desire to open their emails because I've been trained to expect nothing useful before I get inside.
- **"ACE YOUR LOOK" hero CTA is aimed at the kid, not the parent doing the shopping.** I'm the one with the credit card. Talk to me.
- **Two Back to School emails in three days** (this and the 07-08 send). Both solid, but I'm starting to feel the cadence pressure. If a third drops this week I'm going to start ignoring the category entirely.
- **No deadline on the BOGO offer.** I want to know if I have three days or three weeks. Without that, I bookmark it mentally and forget about it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** I shouldn't have to say this for the tenth consecutive send. Whatever JSON-LD schema markup is bleeding into the preheader, it needs to be isolated from inbox clients. This is the single highest-leverage fix in this program right now — it's suppressing opens on otherwise strong emails.
- 2. **Put an expiration date on the BOGO.** Even "Offer ends Sunday" would make me act today instead of closing the tab and forgetting.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero CTA for the parent.** Change "ACE YOUR LOOK" to "SHOP THEIR FIRST DAY LOOK" or "BUILD THEIR BACK TO SCHOOL KIT" — something that speaks to me as the buyer, not my nine-year-old.
- 4. **Highlight Skechers Plus member pricing in the body.** I know I'm a member. The email doesn't seem to know or care. Even a one-liner ("Members, log in for your exclusive price") would make me feel like a known customer instead of a cold lead.
- 5. **Pace the Back to School cadence.** One strong BTS email per week through August is plenty. Two in three days starts feeling like the emails are trying to manufacture urgency instead of earning my attention.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pre-school through high school covered. Grab BOGO before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Light-ups for the little one, uniforms for the big kid — all BOGO 50% off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Back to School" Email (07-10-2026)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken links or critical rendering failures, but carries a systemic HTTP image-serving problem affecting 13+ assets and a third-party pixel loaded over plain HTTP. These issues will cause image suppression in security-conscious clients (Outlook, Apple Mail with "Block All Remote Content").

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No broken destination links detected.** 26 click-redirect links via `click.emails.skechers.com` were skipped from live probing (expected for tracking domains).

**Issues:**
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` — loaded without a protocol (`src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/..."`, no `http://` or `https://`). Protocol-relative URLs are deprecated; behavior is client-defined, typically treated as a broken relative path.
- Two `beacon.krxd.net` pixels + one `ink1000.com` pixel fire on open — three distinct third-party data-sync calls (Salesforce DMP, Return Path, ink1000). No issues per se, but worth confirming all are consented under applicable data agreements.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP images (13 assets):** All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets are served over `http://`, not `https://`. Apple Mail, Gmail (in some configurations), and Outlook with strict security policies will block these by default, resulting in blank image cells throughout the email body and footer.

Affected assets include:
- Skechers logo (`dde00662...png`)
- All footer icons: App Store, Google Play, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube
- One unnamed content image (`49468f73...png`)

**Missing alt text (2 images):**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — minor, but crawlers may flag it
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt; if blocked, renders as empty box with no fallback text

**CSS redundancy:** The breakpoint blocks at `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px` are duplicates of each other with identical rule sets. This adds ~2KB to the `<head>` with no additional behavior. Not a bug, but increases parse time marginally.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is applied globally via `* {}`. This prevents users from adjusting text size on iOS, which is an accessibility regression — `100%` is the correct value per WCAG 1.4.4.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source. Preheader text is hardcoded:

Members: stock up with BOGO 50% off on back-to-school essentials

The word "Members" implies personalization but there is no visible conditional block or merge field gating it — it would render identically for non-members if segment filtering at send time is not enforced upstream.

Campaign ID `MKG_US_NA_K_SEASONAL_FAMILY_EN_07102026` is consistent across the Krux impression pixel and the ink1000 pixel, indicating correct campaign tagging.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** Not verifiable from the truncated HTML — the footer containing the physical mailing address and unsubscribe link was cut off. No issues confirmed, none cleared.

**Authentication headers:** Not available from HTML source alone (requires email header inspection). Cannot assess DKIM/SPF/DMARC posture from this artifact.

**`no-reply@emails.skechers.com` sender:** Technically valid; no RFC violation. Confirm the domain has a proper MX or null MX record to avoid bounce-handling gaps.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The campaign ID `MKG_US_NA_K_SEASONAL_FAMILY_EN_07102026` appears in tracking pixels but **UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed** — all 26 click links are wrapped in `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects, which may or may not append UTMs before the final redirect. If UTMs are injected at the redirect layer, GA4/analytics continuity depends entirely on that redirect service functioning correctly. A redirect failure means zero attribution.

No evidence of UTM parameters directly embedded in the `<a href>` values visible in the truncated source.
