# The Whole Crew Needs Fresh Kicks
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I'm sitting at the kitchen counter while my son is in the backyard and my daughter is watching a show, and this lands from Skechers. The subject actually stopped my thumb — "The Whole Crew" is literally my life, and buying shoes for two growing kids is basically a quarterly ritual at this point. That subject line speaks directly to how I shop. Then I tap it open.
- The BOGO 50% off is right there in the hero and I won't pretend that doesn't get my attention. But here's the thing: I'm pretty sure Skechers sent me an almost-identical BOGO email on June 14th AND flagged the same deal on June 18th in the member email. This is the third time I've seen this offer in less than two weeks. At this point "buy one get one 50% off" is starting to feel like the regular price.
- The actual layout is clean — women's, men's, kids' sections all visible, and the kids' shoes look cute enough that I'd scroll down. But the "Street Staples" branding on the hero is doing nothing for me. I'm not hunting for street cred, I'm hunting for velcro and light-up soles. I'd probably tap the kids' CTA before closing out, but I wouldn't forward this one — it just doesn't feel fresh.

## What's working

- **"The Whole Crew" subject line is genuinely good** — it maps directly to how I think about buying shoes (everyone needs something, all at once, while there's a deal).
- **BOGO 50% in the hero is impossible to miss** — clear offer, above the fold, didn't have to hunt for it.
- **Three-section structure (Women / Men / Kids) is smart** — I can jump straight to Kids without hunting.
- **The kids' shoes in that section look light and wearable** — white with some color, approachable for a 5-year-old or 9-year-old, not trying too hard.
- **25% off apparel mentioned lower** — nice bonus, not screaming at me, just there if I want it.

## What's weak

- **Same BOGO deal again — third time in 12 days** — it's starting to feel like the price just *is* what it is now, which deflates the urgency.
- **"Street Staples" hero branding is not for me** — the hero model is a young dude in a cap; I'm a 34-year-old mom, not a streetwear shopper.
- **No Skechers Plus mention anywhere** — after "It's your month" and "Members Only" emails this month, dropping the member angle here feels like a step backward.
- **Preview text is JSON schema garbage again** — this is the fourth or fifth send in a row where the inbox preview is code, not copy. I'm a known subscriber and this is still broken.
- **No deadline on the BOGO** — if there's an expiration, it's not visible; that removes urgency and gives me an excuse to close out and come back "later" (which means never).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the preview text leak** — this JSON breaking in the inbox has been a multi-week problem now. Whatever schema.org markup is in the template needs to come after valid preheader copy, not before.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the BOGO** — "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" would get me to act now instead of saving it for later.
- 3. **Swap the hero visual for a family or kids-first image** — "The Whole Crew" subject promises a family feel; the hero delivers a lone young guy. That gap is jarring.
- 4. **Surface Skechers Plus member pricing or early access** — the last two emails made me feel like a member; this one treats me like a stranger.
- 5. **Add a kid-specific callout in the hero or sub-hero** — something like "Sizes for the whole family, including kids up to Y" would validate that I'm in the right place immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% Off — Shoes for the Whole Crew`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kids Shoes + Yours: Buy One Get One 50% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids' styles, women's picks — ends Sunday. Shop the BOGO now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New styles for the kids + 50% off the second pair. This weekend only.`

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
- Applied globally via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. Disables automatic font scaling on iOS Safari; small text will remain unreadably small without manual zoom (which `maximum-scale=1` also blocks).

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`**

**Duplicate media query blocks**
