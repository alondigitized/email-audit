# Just Dropped: Your Beauty Refresh ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so this is a Sally Beauty email and my first reaction is — oh, I actually do color my hair at home sometimes, so this isn't totally irrelevant to me. The "Gifts for Mom from under $25" section caught my eye immediately because hello, Mother's Day is right around the corner. There are a lot of deals stacked in here — BOGO 50%, buy two get one free, a Manic Panic bundle — but honestly it starts to feel like a clearance flyer after a while. Nothing in here feels like it was picked for *me* specifically. It's more like they threw every promo into one email and hoped something stuck. I'd probably skim it, maybe click the Mom gifts section, and then get distracted by my kids and close the app.

## What's working

- **"Gifts for Mom from under $25"** — This is smart timing. I'm a mom who will inevitably be browsing her phone this week, and that price ceiling ($25) is exactly where I shop for personal treats. It removes the mental friction of "is this too much?"
- **Deal variety** — BOGO, bundle pricing, buy-two-get-one all stack value visually. As a price-sensitive shopper I appreciate seeing the actual offer math laid out with a product image.
- **Manic Panic bundle at $21.99** — Clear price, product shown, deadline ("Only thru May 9"). That's how a deal module should look. Compact and complete.
- **Mobile layout** — Single column, readable without zooming. Tap targets on the CTA buttons look adequate. I'm not fighting to hit "Shop Now" with my thumb.

## What's weak

- **Too many competing promos** — By the time I get to "Buy one, get one 50% off Sauce Beauty" I've already forgotten what the hero was about. Four separate deal modules in one scroll is a lot. It feels like a deal dump, not a curated edit.
- **The hero is vague** — "New Deals Just Dropped — Hair Color & Beauty Must-Haves" with the subtitle "Great results at home, made to fit your routine and budget" is completely generic. Every beauty brand says this. It doesn't tell me *which* deals or *why now*.
- **The 5% off banner** — "5% off your next buy online, pick up in-store order" feels buried and honestly kind of weak as a top offer. 5%? For a mom with two kids stretching her budget that barely registers.
- **No personalization visible** — Nothing says my name, nothing references my past purchases, nothing acknowledges that I'm a loyalty member (if I am one). I'm treated like a stranger even though Sally presumably has purchase history on me.
- **Hair color product focus feels slightly off-target** — The BOGO hair color section shows purple and deep blue semi-permanent color. Fun for someone, but not my typical home color situation. Feels like inventory push more than curation for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one clear hero offer, not four.** Pick the strongest deal (probably the "Gifts for Mom under $25" given the timing) and build the whole email around it. Stack the secondary deals below as supporting content, not competing heroes.
- 2. **Make the Mother's Day angle explicit in the hero.** Swap the generic headline for something like "Treat Yourself This Mother's Day — Under $25." That's a hook I'd tap on immediately.
- 3. **Ditch or elevate the 5% banner.** 5% in-store pickup incentive is not compelling enough to be the first thing I see. Either make it 15–20% or replace it with a more relevant hook.
- 4. **Personalize at least the greeting or the product carousel.** Even "Hi Martha, your favorites are on sale" goes a long way on mobile where I have zero patience.
- 5. **Tighten the deal modules.** Three modules max per email. Each needs: product image, clear offer, price if possible, deadline. Remove the "Sauce Beauty" BOGO if you're already doing two other BOGOs — it cannibalizes attention.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed meta tags — two instances:**

```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Neither directive will function as intended. Some legacy clients will fail to detect UTF-8 encoding from the first tag, risking garbled characters in non-ASCII content (e.g., the `✨` subject emoji if mirrored in preheader text).

**Missing `@font-face` for primary brand font:**

```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
Six `@font-face` blocks load Open Sans variants; none load `Founders Grotesk`. All anchor text will silently fall back to Helvetica across every client.

**External font load from Google Fonts (v17 — outdated):**

```css
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...)
```
Open Sans is now at v29+. More critically, Gmail (web), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Yahoo Mail block external font requests entirely — fallback stack behavior should be explicitly tested and accepted before send.

**Invalid CSS on `img` elements:**

```css
img { white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space` is not a valid property for replaced elements (`<img>`). It will be ignored by all email clients but adds stylesheet noise and may confuse preprocessors or linters.

**Mobile `display:none` hiding risk:**

```css
.mobileHide { display: none !important; }
```
Gmail Android strips `<style>` blocks in some rendering modes, leaving `.mobileHide` elements fully visible regardless of screen width. If desktop-only content is hidden via this class, it will appear on Gmail Android.
