# Richer. Brighter. L'Oreal. ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Sally. Again. I counted — this is at least the seventh email from them in roughly ten days. I already had "More hair care, more savings" from two days ago sitting in my read pile, and here's another one. My thumb was halfway to "archive" before I even registered what it said.
- But here's the thing — the image actually stopped me for a second. That woman's hair is genuinely gorgeous. The warm golden-caramel, the "sunset shades, no salon required" copy — that landed. I'm a mom of two, I can't get to a salon, and the idea of getting that look from a box has some real pull. The L'Oreal Professionnel name also adds credibility; that's not the drugstore shelf stuff.
- That said, I kept waiting to see the deal. A discount, a dollar amount, a "buy X get Y" — something that makes me feel like opening this email gave me something. By the time I hit the product grid and only saw "Shop now" buttons and Klarna BNPL at the bottom, I'd already lost the moment. No meaningful offer means I'm not clicking today. I'd mark it as read, maybe come back when I have a coupon code.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is legitimately aspirational** — the model's hair is exactly the "summer color upgrade" the headline promises; no disconnect between copy and visual.
- **"No salon required" is smart copy for someone like me** — practical, time-saving, speaks to a mom who can't justify a $150 salon visit right now.
- **L'Oreal Professionnel branding adds a layer of trust** — this reads like pro-grade, not a random generic box dye.
- **Product grid is clean and scannable** — five products, clear names, product photos that actually show the shade result.
- **Mobile layout holds together** — nothing broken, text is readable, hierarchy flows top to bottom without me having to pinch-zoom.

## What's weak

- **No visible offer or discount in the body** — I'm price-sensitive; "Shop now" without a number does nothing for me. The top banner is too small to read clearly at mobile size and I'm not squinting for it.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — two days after "More hair care, more savings," this email has to work harder to earn my attention, and it doesn't.
- **"Shop now" CTAs are generic across all five products** — every button says the same thing; I have no sense of what's the hero product I should actually buy first.
- **Klarna BNPL at the bottom feels like a weak close** — if the products aren't expensive enough that I need installment payments, why lead me there? If they are, I need a price shown upfront.
- **Zero personalization** — I'm a known Sally customer and this reads like a cold blast to everyone on the list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a concrete offer in the email** — even "Save $5 on L'Oreal color kits" changes the math for me. Right now there's no reason to click today vs. next week vs. never.
- 2. **Pick one hero product with a linked PDP** — instead of five equal "Shop now" buttons, feature the Honey Blonde or a curated starter kit and make one CTA the clear champion.
- 3. **Rewrite the banner so it's readable on mobile** — whatever the top-of-email promo says, I can't read it; if that's the actual offer, it needs to move into the email body.
- 4. **Add a member-exclusive angle** — I'm a Sally loyalty member (or should feel treated as one); "Club member price" or "extra 10% for members" would make this feel like it was sent to me, not at me.
- 5. **Tighten the cadence or differentiate harder** — this email is too similar in tone to the last two. If you're going to send multiple times a week, each email needs a distinct hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `At-home color that looks salon-made — $X off L'Oreal`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sunset-hair look, no appointment needed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `5 shades to upgrade your summer — free shipping on orders $25+`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` attribute missing on two `<meta>` tags (high confidence):**

```html
<!-- As written — non-functional -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=edge" />

<!-- Correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv`, both tags are ignored by parsers. The charset declaration being inert is the more serious of the two — it removes explicit encoding enforcement in clients that don't auto-detect UTF-8.

**'Founders Grotesk' referenced but never declared:**

```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```

There is no `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` anywhere in the visible `<style>`. All anchor text will silently fall through to Helvetica. If this is intentional (local install only), it's not a bug, but if web delivery was intended, the declaration is missing.

**Open Sans pinned to `v17` (stale, minor):**

All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Current Open Sans on Google Fonts is v36+. Stale version URLs still resolve but may eventually be deprecated by Google.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` — dead rule:**

`white-space` has no defined effect on replaced elements (`<img>`). The rule is harmless but is dead CSS; likely a template artifact.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`:**

Standard email mobile breakpoint is 600px. Viewports between 500–600px (common on large phones and small tablets) will render the desktop layout. This may clip or reflow content on those devices.

**Alt text on images — cannot verify** from truncated source. Must be confirmed in full HTML.
