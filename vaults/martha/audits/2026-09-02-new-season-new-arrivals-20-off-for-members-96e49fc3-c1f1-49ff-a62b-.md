# New season. New arrivals. 20% off for members
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Hi, Martha" and my 1000 points right up top — fine, I'll give them that, it's consistent with every other Skechers email this week. But then it's the AI-generated model in the flannel courtyard shoot for New Arrivals, "New For Her," "New For Him," apparel bundles... and once again, nowhere — not once — is there a KIDS section above the fold. I've got a 5-year-old and a 9-year-old who both need new shoes for school, and Skechers has "KIDS" buried as a plain text link at the very bottom of the email, same weight as "CLOTHING" and "SALE."
- The 20% off for Plus members is a real, honest offer and I appreciate that it's specific, but it's tied to "regular priced styles" and I'm digging for the fine print already. Between this, "Your favorites, now in wide fit" (8/30), and "Labor Day savings start now" (9/1), it's the same 20% Plus discount dressed up in a new outfit every single day. I'm not opening a fourth version of this.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. Not forwarding it — there's nothing here for my kids, and that's the only reason I open a Skechers email in the first place.

## What's working

- "Hi, Martha" + 1000 points + REDEEM link at the top means someone's tracking my loyalty status, and that still lands.
- The 20% off banner is specific and visible immediately — no scrolling needed to see the offer.
- "SHOP NOW" and category CTAs are big, blue, tappable buttons — no tiny text links for the main asks.
- Hands Free Slip-ins and Wide Fit call-outs are genuinely useful product info for someone like me shopping for comfort/fit.

## What's weak

- No kids' shoes anywhere in the visual hierarchy — Kids is a plain text line at the bottom, same as every other catalog link, when it should be a hero section like "New For Her" and "New For Him."
- The hero model shot is labeled "AI-GENERATED MODEL" right on the image — I noticed that disclosure on the 8/27 email too and it makes the whole email feel less trustworthy, not more.
- "Complete the Look with Skechers Apparel" and the 40% off apparel banner is a lot of real estate for something I'm not here for on a shoe-shopping mission.
- This is basically the same 20%-off-Plus-members offer I already saw in "Labor Day savings start now" (9/1) and "Your favorites, now in wide fit" (8/30) — the cadence is exhausting.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a "New For Kids" section with actual product shots (velcro sneakers for my daughter, lace-up styles for my son) right after the hero — that's the single biggest miss and the reason I'd actually click.
- 2. Drop or shrink the AI-generated model hero, or at least don't caption it "AI-GENERATED MODEL" in bold white text — it undercuts trust.
- 3. Cut the apparel push down to one small banner instead of a full section — I came for shoes.
- 4. Vary the offer structure so it doesn't read as a repeat of the last three emails — bundle a kids-specific promo code instead of the same 20% Plus discount.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school shoes: 20% off for Plus members`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New kicks for the kids — 20% off ends 9/7`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Velcro, light-up, and lace-up styles for the whole family — plus 20% off for Plus members`

## Technical Audit

The email renders via a standard SFMC-hosted table layout with responsive breakpoints; the primary functional defect is a broken personalization token in the greeting, alongside several HTTP-only image assets and one missing beacon.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 37 tracking/click-redirect links present but skipped by automated probe (expected behavior for redirect-domain links; not a confirmed defect).
- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?GXAPGELSD77UBBHCVFIISVPV7U.100223&d=100223&bmt=0` — loads over HTTPS, no issue.
- Third-party pixels (Kantar/Krux `beacon.krxd.net`, ink1000.com) present for audience matching/ad tracking — functioning as tracking pixels, not flagged as broken.
- `ink1000.com` tracking pixel is missing its protocol scheme: `src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?..."` (no `http://`/`https://` prefix) — this will resolve as a relative path in most clients and fail to load, silently breaking that specific tracking/deliverability signal.

  - `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif`
