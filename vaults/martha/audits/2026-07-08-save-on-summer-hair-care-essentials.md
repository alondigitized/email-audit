# Save on summer hair care essentials 🔆
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Sally. Again. By my rough mental tally that's somewhere past eleven emails since June 1st, and I'm not even counting the back-to-back America25 sends from July 3rd and July 5th. The "flash sale" framing is starting to feel like background noise at this point — when every week is a flash sale, none of them feel urgent.
- That said, I didn't immediately swipe this one away. My daughter's hair has been an absolute frizzy disaster all summer, and "hair care essentials" landing in my inbox on a Tuesday when I have 15 minutes to kill isn't the worst timing. The red flash sale hero stopped my eye, the 20% off was right there, and "Ends July 10" actually means something to me — that's this week, not some vague "limited time."
- Would I click? Maybe — if what's in my cart is hair care and not hair color (which is excluded). Would I forward it? I'd text the deal to my sister if she's a member. Would I spend meaningful time in this email? Probably not. It lands in the "I'll get to it later" pile, where "later" is 50/50 between clicking and deleting.

## What's working

- **20% off hero is immediately readable** — big text, good contrast on red, yellow SHOP NOW button hard to miss even on a small screen.
- **"Ends July 10" is prominently placed** — a real date with a real deadline. Far better than the vague "limited time" language on some of their earlier sends.
- **Sally Rewards tier callout lands well** — seeing "4x points on $50+" in plain view makes me think about whether I'm close to a threshold. That math happens automatically.
- **"Picked for You" personalization** — even if I'm skeptical about how accurate it is, having a dedicated section that implies they looked at my history makes it feel less like a bulk blast.
- **Models reflect actual diversity** — the women in the "Your Beauty, Your Way" grid look like real people across different hair types. That matters more to me than I'd consciously admit.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is broken** — this is email eleven-plus since June 1. The flash sale framing has been used so many times it's lost all weight. I barely glanced at the subject before my thumb was already hovering over swipe-to-delete.
- **The exclusion is buried** — "Excludes hair color & clearance" is visible but small in the hero. Hair color is a massive category for Sally. If I came here specifically for that, I'd feel misled.
- **Preview text is wasted real estate** — "Sally Beauty Open for major savings" tells me nothing the sender name didn't already tell me. And whatever came after it in my inbox preview was garbled junk — I'm not going to pretend I noticed it in a good way.
- **No family or kid-friendly angle** — I buy products for my daughter's hair at Sally too. Nothing in this email acknowledges that I might be shopping for the whole household, not just myself.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the exclusion clearly in the hero** — put "excludes hair color + clearance" right under the headline in readable text, not fine print. The July 3rd and July 5th emails offered 25% sitewide via AMERICA25 — this offer is actually *weaker*, and if I'm reading closely I'll notice. Don't compound that by having it feel like bait-and-switch.
- 2. **Fix the preview text** — use those 40–50 characters to tell me the % off and the exact deadline before I even open. That is the one place you can still change my mind in the inbox view.
- 3. **Slow the cadence or differentiate the creative** — if you're going to send twice a week, the offers and visuals need to feel meaningfully different. Right now it's the same playbook at slightly varying discount levels.
- 4. **Add a family/kids section** — even a small "Shop kids' hair care" banner or a product row for gentle/kid-safe formulas would make me feel like they know who I am.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picks are 20% off — but only until July 10. Tap to see what's waiting.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<html>` missing `lang` attribute (confirmed)**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Should be `<html xmlns="..." lang="en">`. Required for screen readers and some spam filters.

**`<meta charset>` missing `http-equiv` (confirmed)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Correct form: `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />`. Without it, charset declaration is non-conformant and may be ignored by some parsers.

**`<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible">` malformed (confirmed)**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Missing `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`. Functionally inert but signals sloppy templating.

**`white-space:pre` on `img` (confirmed)**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```
`white-space:pre` on `img` elements is incorrect and can introduce unexpected spacing in Outlook and some WebKit clients. Should be removed.

**`-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic` obsolete (confirmed)**
IE-only property, unsupported in any current email client. Dead weight but harmless.

**Stale Google Fonts version (confirmed)**
All `@font-face` URLs reference `opensans/v17/`. Current Open Sans is v40+. Google Fonts serves these URLs via redirect, but a versioned URL that trails too far behind risks being dropped from their CDN. More critically: Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail block external CSS/font fetches — Open Sans will silently fall back to Helvetica in most inboxes.

**`Founders Grotesk` not loaded via `@font-face` (confirmed)**
```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
`Founders Grotesk` is a licensed typeface. There is no `@font-face` declaration for it in the visible CSS. Every email client will fall back to Helvetica for anchor elements.

**Mobile breakpoint at 499px (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```
Standard email breakpoint is 600px. Devices with viewport widths between 500–599px (common on mid-size Android phones) will render the desktop layout unmodified. Should be `max-width: 599px` or `max-width: 600px`.
