# Customer Approved Sneakers
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I grabbed my phone between refilling my son's water bottle and finding my daughter's missing sock, and this one slid into my inbox with — I kid you not — ANOTHER wall of JSON code in the preview. I have now seen that broken gibberish every single time Skechers sends me something this week. At this point I'm half-convinced it's intentional branding. It is not. It's embarrassing.
- Once I actually opened it, the bones are fine. The BOGO 50% off for Plus members is loud and right at the top, which I appreciate — I'm a deal-first shopper and that did catch my eye. The Arch Fit section with the star ratings felt trustworthy, and BOBS always registers with me. But here's the thing: I opened this email specifically hoping to find something for my kids before school supplies season hits, and the word "Kids" appears exactly once — as a teeny text link buried near the bottom, next to "Clothing" and "Sale." Zero product shots, zero styled photos of children's shoes. My son needs new sneakers. My daughter would lose her mind over a light-up something. This email treats me like I'm a single woman with one pair of feet to dress, which is the exact wrong assumption for a Saturday-morning Skechers shopper like me.
- I would not forward this. I might click the BOGO banner out of deal-habit, but I'd feel like I was fighting the email to find what I actually came for.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is the first thing my eye lands on** — bold, high-contrast banner, member benefit called out. That's the right offer in the right place for a price-sensitive repeat buyer.
- **Arch Fit section shows star ratings** — tiny but real social proof. That validates me when I'm on the fence about a comfort-focused shoe.
- **Clean mobile layout** — no overlapping text, no broken images in the email body itself, tap targets look reasonable on a phone screen.
- **"Extra 5% off for members" in the 40% off banner** — I'm a Plus member and this kind of call-out reminds me I'm getting something others aren't. Keeps me loyal.
- **Multiple category entry points** — Women, Men, Kids, New Arrivals text links near the bottom give me options even if the main content doesn't.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is broken JSON. Again. For the fourth time this week.** After seeing this in every single Skechers email since Monday, it's no longer a glitch — it's a trust leak. It signals that nobody is QA-ing these sends, which makes me wonder what else is sloppy.
- **Zero kids content in a visual email.** My primary shopping mission on Skechers.com is finding shoes for a 5-year-old and a 9-year-old. The email doesn't speak to that at all — no sneaker shots for kids, no "for the family" framing, nothing that says "hey mom, we see you."
- **"Customer Approved Sneakers" as a subject line is a missed opportunity.** There's a legit BOGO deal in here — why bury the lede? The subject sounds generic when there's a concrete offer to lead with.
- **No deadline on the offer.** "Up to 40% off" and "BOGO 50% off" with no end date feel more like a clearance section than a real promotion. I need a reason to act now rather than close the app and forget.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text, full stop.** This is the single highest-ROI fix. Pull the JSON schema out of the email header or wherever it's leaking. I've seen it five times this week from this brand — it's actively eroding inbox trust faster than any bad subject line.
- 2. **Add a kids section with real product imagery.** Even a 2-column row — "For Her (Girls)" and "For Him (Boys)" — would make me stop scrolling. Show me a light-up sneaker and a durable lace-up. I will click immediately.
- 3. **Move the offer into the subject line.** The BOGO deal is the hero; the subject line should say so.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% Off — Bestsellers for the Whole Family`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Members: Buy 1, Get 1 50% Off This Weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids, women's, and men's styles included — members save an extra 5%`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Plus member deal is live — shop Arch Fit, BOBS, and kids' picks`
- 4. **Put a real deadline on the promotion.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" or "This weekend only" gives me a reason to buy now instead of adding to my mental wishlist and never coming back.
- 5. **Segment the hero by shopping behavior.** I buy kids' shoes regularly — if they know that from my order history, show me a family-first hero, not a solo-lifestyle couple shot. Even "Bestsellers for the Whole Family" copy on the existing hero image would shift my perception.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — CDN domain `image.emails.skechers.com`:**
All product and UI images are served over HTTP, not HTTPS. Count: 13 confirmed HTTP `<img>` sources. Examples:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png
```
Gmail proxies these through its image cache, so they will load for Gmail recipients. Apple Mail (macOS/iOS) and Outlook.com will block mixed HTTP content; recipients on those clients see broken images. **Fix: update the CDN endpoint to HTTPS** — most ESPs support this via a simple domain config change.

**Missing alt text:**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — acceptable for a 1×1 pixel, but `alt=""` should be explicit.
- `49468f73-...png` — decorative or product image with no alt text. If it conveys information (product shot, promotional graphic), alt text is required for screen readers and image-off rendering.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Three overlapping media query blocks targeting `max-width: 375px`, `max-width: 480px`, and `max-width: 640px` with duplicated rule sets (`.hideme`, `.widthFull`, `.borderRight`, etc.). Duplication is not a rendering bug but increases parse weight and maintenance surface unnecessarily.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** Prevents user zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern for low-vision recipients; WCAG 1.4.4 recommends not restricting zoom. Not a deliverability issue but worth flagging if accessibility compliance is a goal.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:** Suppresses automatic font scaling on iOS Safari. Can cause sub-12px text to remain illegible on small screens.
