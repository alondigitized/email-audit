# Going... Going... Gone! 20% Off Ending Soon
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Sally, again. I just had "Time's running out on up to 40% off" in my inbox yesterday — June 25th — and now I'm looking at "Going... Going... Gone!" I've genuinely lost count at this point, but this has to be email number nine or ten from them since June 1. That cadence has conditioned me to treat Sally's emails the same way I treat smoke alarms in the break room: probably not an emergency.
- That said — and I say this grudgingly — 20% off *sitewide* is actually the most useful offer they've run all month. Most of June was "select" this and "select" that, with asterisks on everything. If the sitewide claim is real and today's the last day, there's a version of me who adds that Wella conditioner I've been putting off buying and hits checkout.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I happen to already know what I need. Would I forward it to a friend? No — Sally sent her the same email this morning.

## What's working

- **"20% off sitewide" is the strongest offer Sally has pushed all month.** After weeks of "select" category deals, "sitewide" actually registers as broader and more useful.
- **"Ends June 26" hits differently when it's literally today.** That's genuine urgency — not a fake timer, just a hard calendar date.
- **The sub-offers scan fast on mobile.** 40% off styling tools, BOGO Wella, 40% off ion hair care — I can read all four in two seconds. Good visual chunking.
- **"Earn more rewards" is a smart one-liner for loyalty members.** It reminds me that clicking earns points, not just saves me money today.
- **Free 2-hour delivery callout in green breaks up the layout nicely.** As a mom who'd rather not make a separate store run, that section actually gets my attention.

## What's weak

- **This is the second urgency-framing email in 48 hours.** "Time's running out" yesterday, "Going... Going... Gone!" today — I've been trained to disbelieve these deadlines.
- **The preheader is a copy of the subject, not a complement.** "Shop Big Deals before they're gone" tells me nothing the subject didn't already say. Zero new reason to open.
- **No personalization anywhere.** I've ordered from Sally enough times that "your Wella is on sale" would have actually stopped my thumb. Generic sitewide messaging treats me like a cold lead.
- **Asterisks on Wella\* and ion\* make me skeptical of everything.** If the 40%-off sub-offers have restrictions, I'm now quietly wondering what the sitewide 20% actually excludes.
- **Every CTA says "Shop Now" and drops me on the homepage.** I don't want to browse — I want a fast lane to hair care or styling. Generic entry points add friction for mobile shoppers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull in a personalized product hook.** "Your Wella Color Charm is 20% off today" or "Restock your favorites before midnight" — anything that proves Sally knows I'm a returning customer and not a first-time visitor.
- 2. **Collapse the urgency cycle.** Send one last-day email on the last day. Don't prime it with "time's running out" the day before — it burns the credibility you need for the close.
- 3. **Fix the preheader to do actual work.** Use it to surface a specific product category or a number the subject didn't mention, so the inbox preview reads as additive.
- 4. **Replace "Shop Now" with category CTAs.** "Shop Hair Care Deals," "Shop Styling Tools" — any specific verb + noun beats a generic entry point.
- 5. **Clarify the sitewide exclusions upfront.** If 20% really is broad with minimal carve-outs, just say "almost everything" or "no exclusions." That one phrase removes friction.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last Day: 20% Off Sitewide at Sally Beauty`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today Only — 20% Off Everything + 40% Off Styling Tools`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Styling tools, hair care, Wella BOGO — sale ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 40% off ion and Wella. No code needed — ends June 26.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `lang` attribute** on `<html>` element. Current: `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">`. Required: `lang="en"`. Affects screen readers and spam filter scoring.

- **Malformed `Content-Type` meta tag** — missing `http-equiv` attribute:
  ```html
  <!-- As sent -->
  <meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  <!-- Correct -->
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
  ```
  Charset declaration may be ignored by older Outlook/Exchange renderers without the `http-equiv`.

- **Malformed `IE=edge` meta tag** — same omission:
  ```html
  <!-- As sent -->
  <meta content="IE=edge" />
  <!-- Correct -->
  <meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
  ```

- **`img { white-space: pre; }`** — anomalous rule with no valid effect on `<img>` elements. Harmless but indicates a copy-paste error in the template CSS.

- **'Founders Grotesk' not declared** — `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a font with no corresponding `@font-face` block in the stylesheet. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica in every client. If this font is intentional, it must be loaded; if Helvetica is the intended fallback, the named font reference should be removed.

- **Google Fonts pinned to `v17`** — all six `@font-face` src URLs use `opensans/v17/`. Current version is v36+. Google maintains CDN backward compatibility, but the pin is stale and could break on future CDN restructuring.

- **Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`** — slightly below the industry-standard 600px. Devices with viewport widths 500–599px (e.g., some Android tablets in portrait) will receive desktop layout. Low risk, but worth aligning.
