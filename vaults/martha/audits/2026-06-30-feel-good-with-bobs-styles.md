# Feel Good With BOBS Styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- So I'm grabbing my phone during quiet time — kids are actually playing together for once — and this Skechers email slides in with "Feel Good With BOBS Styles." I have to be honest: before I even opened it, my inbox showed a wall of garbled tech code in the preview instead of, you know, actual words. That set the tone. When I got in, the email itself is pretty clean — dog photos, slip-on lifestyle sneakers, the whole BOBS charitable-giving angle — and the BOGO 50% off banner is right up top where it should be. But here's my problem: I opened this email because I need shoes for my kids, and this entire email is about adult women's lifestyle shoes. Cute shoes, sure, but that's not why I'm here.
- I want to like BOBS. The animal shelter donation story is sweet and I'd probably mention it to my sister if she were shoe shopping. But after a month of Skechers hitting my inbox every day or two — the June 25th "Prime Time Savings," the June 22nd price-tier email, another BOGO before that — I am exhausted. This is maybe the fourth BOGO deal in recent memory, and the offer that used to feel special is starting to feel like wallpaper.
- I'll mark it read. I won't click, I won't forward it. Maybe I'd come back to it if I had a reason to buy something for myself, which almost never happens before the kids' list is done.

## What's working

- **BOGO offer is impossible to miss** — the banner is the first thing my eyes go to, and the 50% off is crystal clear.
- **Email renders clean on mobile** — no broken images, text isn't overflowing, tap targets look reasonable.
- **The charitable angle is genuinely differentiated** — "Skechers has donated $66 million" and the Best Friends Animal Society callout gives the brand a real reason for me to feel okay spending money here, versus just another promo.
- **The LOVE!!!! social proof section** — five-star ratings, even in a compact block, builds trust quickly.
- **"Up to 40% Off" secondary offer** — two distinct savings signals in one email without feeling cluttered.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids content** — my whole shopping mission at Skechers is finding stuff for my 5-year-old and my 9-year-old. BOBS might have kids' styles; I have no idea because nothing in this email showed me.
- **The preview text is completely broken** — my inbox showed JSON schema markup code, not a preheader. It reads like a glitched email from a brand I've never heard of. That alone cost opens.
- **BOGO fatigue is real** — this is at minimum the third BOGO I've seen from Skechers this month. The June 22 "You Pick the Price" email was a breath of fresh air; going back to BOGO feels like a step backward.
- **The dog-mom lifestyle vibe doesn't match my life** — the hero image is a woman and a golden retriever. That's not me. I have two kids and zero dogs. Small thing, but it's one more signal that this email wasn't built with me in mind.
- **No Skechers Elite/rewards mention** — I'm loyalty-driven and price-sensitive; a member-exclusive angle would have pulled me in so much faster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately** — shipping broken JSON schema code to the inbox is an embarrassment. This should be the first thing any QA checklist catches. Write an actual preheader and test it in an inbox preview tool before sending.
- 2. **Add a kids BOBS section** — even a one-row module of kids' BOBS styles with "Shop Kids' BOBS" would transform this email for a large slice of the Skechers audience who shops for their families. If kids BOBS don't exist, create a crosslink to kids' comfort styles.
- 3. **Rotate off BOGO for one send** — try a members-only early access angle, or feature the charitable $66M story more prominently as the whole hook rather than burying it in body copy. "Every pair helps a shelter dog" as the subject would stop me cold.
- 4. **Give the offer a deadline** — "BOGO ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" would push me to decide instead of parking it.
- 5. **Lead with the mission, not the discount** — the BOBS charitable story is actually unique in Skechers' lineup. The current email puts BOGO first and buries the story. Flip it: lead with the feel-good mission, then reveal the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your BOGO 50% Off Helps Shelter Dogs Too`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy one, get the second half off — and help animals find homes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop BOBS this week and Skechers donates to Best Friends Animal Society.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no structural failures, but carries **13 HTTP image sources** that will be blocked by modern email clients enforcing HTTPS, and passes SHA-256-hashed subscriber PII to a third-party DMP via tracking beacons. The QA tool's summary line ("100% pass rate, 0 warnings") contradicts its own 14 `[WARN]` findings — that counter is bugged and should not be trusted.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP open-tracking pixel (Movable Ink)**
- `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy all images over HTTPS; this pixel will silently fail or be rewritten, inflating open-rate errors.

**Return Path pixel — missing protocol**
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499` — no `http://` or `https://` prefix. Protocol-relative URLs are unreliable in email rendering contexts and may resolve as relative paths rather than absolute URLs.

**Kraken Data (krxd.net) beacons — hashed PII leak**
- Beacon 1 passes `partner_uid=adf27fee67323d52536b18110294bf793c265b830082caba4b1a32664832189a` (64 hex chars = SHA-256 of subscriber email), confirmed by `sfmc_100007688_sha25` in the `partner` param.
- Beacon 2 passes `partner_uid=122277199` (raw SFMC subscriber ID).
- Both are zero-dimension images (`width="0" height="0"`) passed to a third-party DMP. This is a data-sharing disclosure obligation under CCPA/GDPR — confirm it is covered in the privacy policy and consent flow.

**26 click-tracking links**
- All routed through `click.emails.skechers.com` (SFMC). Destination UTM coverage cannot be verified from truncated source — see §6.
