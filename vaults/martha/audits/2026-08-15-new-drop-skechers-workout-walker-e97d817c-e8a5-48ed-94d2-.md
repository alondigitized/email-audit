- New shoe, same story. "Just In: Workout Walker" — okay, that's a genuinely new product launch, not a recycled promo banner, and I'll give it credit for that. ## What's working

- The hero product shot is clean — two colorways of the actual new shoe, easy to see the design.
- "Just In" gives it real news value, unlike the last few sends that were just re-skinned comfort claims.
- The 5-star review snippet ("Wearing all day long... comfortable to wear all day") is a nice trust cue, even if it's for the wrong audience.
- Sticky nav row at the bottom (Women/Men/Kids/Clothing/New Arrivals/Sale) at least makes Kids one tap away, even though it's the sixth thing I'd have to notice.

## What's weak

- No kids at all — no velcro sneaker for my daughter, no lace-up for my son, not even a family lifestyle shot to hint that Skechers makes kids' shoes.
- The 40% off banner is small, gray, and shoved near the very bottom, way after two full lifestyle scrolls — I'd never scroll that far on my phone.
- Preview text in my inbox is literally raw schema.org JSON code leaking through — that looks broken and unprofessional before I even open it.
- This is the fourth email in six days built on the same men's-jog/women's-walk template — I'm getting fatigued by the sameness even with a "new" shoe attached.

- 2. Move the 40% off deal up near the top, make it bold and specific (not just "up to"), so it's the first thing I see, not the last.
- 3. Fix the preview text — it's leaking raw JSON-LD code instead of real preview copy, which looks broken in-inbox.

- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?W6HTN52D5JHETKHSJUSACHH2RM.100224&d=100224&bmt=0` — HTTPS, fine.
