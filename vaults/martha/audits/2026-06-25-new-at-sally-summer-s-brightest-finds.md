# New at Sally: Summer's Brightest Finds ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- OK, so I opened this one because "New at Sally" felt like it might actually show me something I hadn't seen before — not another BOGO or last-call banner. After June's parade of deals emails from them, I'll admit a discovery email sounded almost refreshing. But then the very first thing I see is that red banner: "Big Deals are Coming!" Which... are they here or not? Because the rest of the email is entirely new-product showcase with zero pricing anywhere. That's a mixed signal right out of the gate and it mildly annoyed me.
- Once I got past the banner, the hero is clean and genuinely seasonal — summer vibes, a relatable-looking woman, nail art copy that made me think *oh I keep meaning to do my nails at home*. The product grid ("Don't Miss What's Trending") is stacked with stuff I vaguely recognize, and the Pride Month section is cute if a little corporate. But I hit the bottom of this email and felt kind of... nowhere? No price anchors, no "members save X," nothing telling me why today is the day to buy any of this.
- I'd mark this as read, not click. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing to send a friend ("hey look at this cool thing with no price attached" isn't a forward moment). If the "Big Deals" teaser resolves into an actual sale email in the next 48 hours and the new products in this email are on it, fine. But on its own, this is a look-book with nowhere to land.

## What's working

- **Hero image and headline land well.** "Keep summer bright and fresh" with a clean lifestyle photo — not cluttered, not stock-y. I stayed on it for a second.
- **Nail art callout in the hero subhead** ("one-stop nail art") caught me — that's actually my sneaky little impulse purchase category and they know it.
- **Subject line did its job.** "New at Sally" told me this wasn't another BOGO fire-drill, which meant I didn't pre-roll my eyes the way I have been lately.
- **Pride Month section** — it's small and in the middle but it felt genuine rather than slapped-on, which I notice.
- **Footer action icons** (Free Shipping, Easy Returns, Earn Points) — nice quick trust reinforcement, even if they're tiny.

## What's weak

- **"Big Deals are Coming!" banner with no actual deals.** The email immediately creates an expectation it never meets. That's a credibility ding, especially after all the discount-heavy sends this month.
- **Zero pricing anywhere in the product grid.** I can't impulse-buy something I can't price-check in 3 seconds on my phone while my kid's in the bath.
- **No member/loyalty hook in the body.** I have a Sally rewards account — if these new arrivals have early access or member pricing, SAY THAT. The "Earn Points" footer icon is doing all that work and it's basically invisible.
- **Blume section is buried.** That eye-product card at the bottom with the dark background actually looked kind of cool, but by the time I scrolled there I was already mentally out.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the 7th or 8th email from them since June 1. The June 5 "Brighten Up for Summer" send felt fresh; this one is starting to feel like wallpaper even when it's genuinely new content.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "Big Deals Are Coming" teaser or make it real.** Either drop a countdown + one sneak-peek price, or remove the banner entirely. A tease with zero payoff in the same email is just noise.
- 2. **Show at least anchor pricing on one hero product.** Even "Starting at $9.99" under the trending grid would give me something to act on.
- 3. **Add a loyalty member callout in the body.** Something like "Members earn double points on new arrivals this week" — this costs nothing and converts loyalists.
- 4. **Move the Blume feature up.** It's the most specific, ownable thing in the email (a named brand, a product category, in-store + online callout) and it's buried in the basement. That section would perform better as a secondary feature above the fold.
- 5. **Tighten the send cadence.** One more week of daily-ish sends and I'm hitting unsubscribe without thinking twice.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals + double points this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bold color, nail kits, and styling tools — shop what's new before it sells out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members earn double points on everything new in-store and online`

### 1. Technical Summary

Six web font URLs return 404, forcing system-font fallback across all clients. One third-party pixel fires over HTTP, which will be blocked by modern email clients enforcing mixed-content rules.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font 404s (6 broken URLs)** — All `@font-face` `src:` URLs reference versioned Google Fonts paths at `/v17/` which no longer resolve:

```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem6YaGs126MiZpBA-UFUK0Zdc1GAK6b.woff2
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/memnYaGs126MiZpBA-UFUKXGUdhrIqOxjaPX.woff2
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem8YaGs126MiZpBA-UFVZ0bf8pkAg.woff2
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem5YaGs126MiZpBA-UNirkOUuhpKKSTjw.woff2
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem5YaGs126MiZpBA-UN7rgOUuhpKKSTjw.woff2
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/mem5YaGs126MiZpBA-UN8rsOUuhpKKSTjw.woff2
```

Google Fonts has since moved to `/v40`+ paths. The `local()` fallback hints are present but rely on the recipient having the font installed locally; most won't. Result: email renders in Helvetica/system sans throughout.

**26 click-redirect links** — QA skipped HTTP probing on all tracking links (ESP click-wrap). Not confirmed broken, but destination URLs could not be verified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (8 images):**

- 4 Salesforce Evergage personalization tiles (`summary.png?userId=martha.st`) — zero alt text. If images are blocked, product recommendations are invisible with no fallback label.
- 1 Monetate/Punchh impression pixel (`pxl.mon-trk.com`) — no alt, expected for a pixel but should be `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen-reader noise.
- 1 VisualIQ impression pixel — contains unresolved `[CACHEBUSTER]` macro (see §4).
- 1 Dotomi/Conversant UCM pixel — no alt, HTTP source (see §5).

**`white-space: pre` on `img` global rule** — `img { white-space: pre; }` is set globally. This is a non-standard rule for `<img>` and has no effect, but it may cause unexpected layout if applied via inheritance in Outlook's rendering engine on table cells containing images.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved `[CACHEBUSTER]` macro** in VisualIQ pixel:

```
src: https://t.myvisualiq.net/impression_pixel?r=[CACHEBUSTER]&et=i&...
```

`[CACHEBUSTER]` was not replaced at send time. This is a literal string in the delivered HTML, meaning the pixel either fired with a static cache key (defeating its purpose) or is erroring silently. This breaks VisualIQ impression attribution for this send.

**`userId=martha.st`** in Evergage tile URLs — this appears to be a test/seed address that was not scrubbed from the production send template. If the Evergage API uses this to resolve product recommendations, all recipients may be receiving the same recommendations as the seed user rather than personalized tiles.

---

### 5. Compliance

**HTTP pixel (mixed content):**

```
src: http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2990&...
```

Non-HTTPS image source. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS resources. This pixel will not fire in the majority of the recipient base, breaking Dotomi/Conversant retargeting sync.

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — HTML is truncated so unsubscribe link and physical mailing address cannot be verified from source. Not flagged as absent, but should be confirmed in full HTML review.

**Authentication** — DKIM/SPF/DMARC headers not available in the provided payload (HTML-only). Sending domain `em.sallybeauty.com` should be verified as aligned with `sallybeauty.com` DMARC policy. Cannot confirm from source alone.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**26 tracking links** — UTM parameters could not be verified since QA skipped probing redirect chains. Destination landing pages and UTM attribution (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are unconfirmed. A manual spot-check of 2–3 CTA links is recommended.

**Evergage tile links** — if the `userId=martha.st` seed issue (§4) affects not just images but also click-through URLs, all recipients may land on product pages selected for the seed user, not themselves.
