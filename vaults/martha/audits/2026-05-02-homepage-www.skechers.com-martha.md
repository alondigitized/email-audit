# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the first thing I see is a solid BOGO deal front and center, which honestly made me stop scrolling — that's the kind of thing I actually care about. The hero is a celebrity lifestyle shot (Brooke Burke and Tony Romo, both looking very put-together) pushing Hands Free Slip-ins, which feels more "aspirational adult" than "mom with a 5-year-old and a 9-year-old who destroys shoes." Right now this homepage is squarely aimed at grown-ups who want effortless style, not me frantically trying to find a velcro sneaker before school starts.
- **5/10**

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF sticky banner** — That blue bar at the very top is the first thing I read, and it immediately made me think "okay, maybe I can get two pairs of kids' shoes for the price of one." That's exactly the math a mom like me does in her head.
- **Secondary promo strip ("25% OFF Apparel")** — Stacking two visible deals above the fold signals this is a value moment, not a dead browsing day. That keeps me engaged.
- **Mobile-first layout** — Clean header, easy-to-tap icons, hamburger menu that doesn't feel cluttered. Whoever designed this knows their customer is on a phone with one hand free.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins push** — For my 9-year-old who fights me every morning on laces, slip-ins are genuinely relevant. I just need them to show me kids' versions.

## What's weak

- **No kids' category visible above the fold** — This is a miss for me. I came here primarily for my daughter and son, and nothing on this screen signals "yes, we have great kids' shoes." I have to hunt.
- **Celebrity hero (Brooke Burke / Tony Romo) feels off-target** — They look great, but they read as a 45+ lifestyle brand moment, not something that makes me think "these are the shoes for my messy, active kids." I don't see myself here.
- **The Slip-ins headline is cut off** — The big blue "HANDS FREE Slip-ins" section is just starting to appear at the bottom of the screen, which means the actual product visual or CTA is below the fold. I'm not going to wait to see it.
- **No personalization signal** — Nothing says "shop for kids," "family bundles," or "rewards member prices" beyond the generic promo. It's all adults, all the time, right here.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Shop Kids" shortcut tile above the fold** — Even a small category chip strip (Women / Men / Kids) just below the header would let me self-select immediately and feel like the site knows I'm not shopping just for myself.
- **Swap the celebrity lifestyle hero for a family/slip-ins lifestyle shot** — Keep the Slip-ins push, but show a kid yanking on a slip-in before school. That image would stop me cold and make the product feel immediately useful.
- **Make the BOGO banner clickable with a landing page filtered to kids** — Right now "Applied at Cart details" is doing heavy lifting. A direct link to "BOGO on Kids' Styles" would convert me way faster.
- **Surface a loyalty / rewards hook in the secondary banner** — I'm already a rewards-program person. If that 25% off is stackable with points, tell me now — don't hide it in fine print.
