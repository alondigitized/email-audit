# Your Skechers Plus Monthly Summary is Here
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I actually kind of appreciate this one — it's doing something genuinely useful. Showing me my points balance, telling me I have a $5 reward waiting, and reminding me that the 2X points deal I saw earlier today is still live. That's real information I can act on. After all the vague "new styles!" emails, a concrete account summary feels refreshingly functional.
- But here's my frustration: same. broken. preview text. That JSON schema garbage is back — I called it out mentally in May 27th's email, again on the 28th, and now we're in June and Skechers is sending me two emails in one day and *both* of them have the same technical problem screaming at me from my inbox. That's embarrassing for a brand this size. I almost dismissed this without opening it because the preview looked like a bot had an accident.
- The product picks at the bottom feel like they were pulled for "a Skechers customer" not for *me* — I'm not seeing anything for my 5-year-old or my 9-year-old, and they're honestly who I'm shopping for most often. I'd mark this as read and maybe click to check my $5 reward, but I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **The $5 reward is front and center** — seeing a concrete, dollar-denominated reward I've already earned is genuinely motivating. That's not a vague "points" tease, that's real money.
- **1,000 points balance is displayed prominently** — the big number at the top of the email makes the account status immediately scannable, no digging required.
- **The 2X Member Appreciation Month banner connects to today's other email** — continuity across the two sends I got today actually reinforces the message rather than feeling repetitive.
- **"VIEW MY ACCOUNT" and "EARN POINTS NOW" are both clear, specific CTAs** — no "Discover More" vagueness here.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — the visual hierarchy is simple and I can read everything at a glance without zooming on my phone.

## What's weak

- **The JSON schema preview text is back — again.** I have now seen this broken preheader at least three times in the last week. This is a known, unfixed problem and at this point it reads as negligence, not a one-off glitch.
- **"We picked these just for you" shows adult sneakers** — no kids' shoes in the recommendations, and my two kids are who I'm actually shopping for. This personalization isn't personalized.
- **Two Skechers emails in one day** — the "Member Month is Here" email already hit my inbox today. Getting a second one the same day makes me feel marketed at, not valued.
- **"Points Earned: 0 / Points Spent: 0"** — if I'm seeing zeros in my monthly summary, that's a little deflating. A small nudge toward how I *could* earn points this month would be more motivating than showing me a blank scorecard.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the JSON preheader — this is the single most impactful fix.** Write real copy: something like "You've got $5 waiting — here's your June summary." This has been broken for weeks and it's killing open rates for an already-subscribed audience.
- 2. **Add at least one kids' shoe recommendation** — I've bought kids' Skechers before. If the algorithm knows I'm a Plus member, it should also know that I shop the Kids section. One kids' shoe card in the "We picked these just for you" row would make this feel like it's actually for *me*.
- 3. **Make the $5 reward CTA direct-to-cart, not account page** — "VIEW MY ACCOUNT" is a fine utility link, but I'd respond better to "Use Your $5 Reward → Shop Now." Reduce the steps between reward and purchase.
- 4. **When earned points and spent points are both 0, reframe it** — instead of showing zeros, show something like: "You've got 1,000 points — shop this month to keep the streak going." Reframe inactivity as opportunity.
- 5. **On same-day double-sends, stagger or combine** — the member appreciation promo and the monthly summary could have been one send, or sent 24 hours apart. Two in one day from the same brand pushes toward unsubscribe territory.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your $5 Reward Is Ready — June Points Summary`
- **Subject Alt B:** `1,000 Points + A Reward Waiting: Your June Recap`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `You've earned a $5 reward — plus 2X points all month. Here's where you stand.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$5 reward unlocked. 1,000 points in the bank. Don't leave it sitting there.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders with a stable table-based layout but ships with 15 HTTP image sources, a production asset served from a `devemails` subdomain, and two malformed `<meta>` tags. No blocking defects, but the mixed-content and dev-domain issues will cause silent image failures in secure email clients.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**24 click-redirect links** routed through `click.emails.skechers.com` — expected for SFMC; not flagged.

**3 Krux/Salesforce DMP pixels** fire on open, including one that exposes a raw hashed email and a subscriber ID in URL params:
```
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner_uid=adf27fee67...  (SHA-256 hash)
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner_uid=122277199     (raw subscriber ID)
```
Verify these are covered by your privacy policy and data-sharing disclosures.

**ink1000.com open pixel is HTTP-only** and missing `alt=""`:
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif
```
This will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail (MPP), and any client enforcing HTTPS.

**Return Path pixel has no protocol prefix** (bare hostname, no `https://`):
```
pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf...
```
Browsers resolve this as a relative URL; email clients may drop it entirely.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**15 image sources use `http://`** — all from `image.emails.skechers.com`. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, social icons (Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, YouTube), app store badges, footer icons, and one body image. Most secure email clients will block or show broken images for mixed-content sources.

**Dev/staging domain in production send:**
```
src="https://image.devemails.skechers.com/lib/fe33157075640674761d74/m/1/summary-tier-blue-angle.png"
```
The tier badge image is being served from `image.devemails.skechers.com` instead of the production `image.emails.skechers.com`. If that subdomain is not publicly accessible or rotates, this image will 404 in production inboxes.

**4 images missing `alt` text** (beyond the intentionally empty tracking pixels):
- `summary-tier-blue-angle.png` (tier status badge — functional content)
- `9010649e-...jpg` (product/hero image)
- `49468f73-...png` (body image)
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel — should have `alt=""` not omitted)

The tier badge is particularly important: screen readers get nothing for what is likely the primary value-prop element of this email.

**Two `<meta>` tags are missing `http-equiv`:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />           <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                             <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Outlook and legacy IE-engine clients may ignore charset and rendering directives.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import`:**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Mulish...');
```
`@import` in `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo. Mulish will silently fall back to system fonts across a large portion of the audience — confirm your fallback stack is acceptable.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoint rules** — identical rule sets defined for both `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px`. The 375px block is fully redundant since 640px subsumes it. Minor code bloat but no functional impact.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally** via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. This disables user-agent font scaling for accessibility users on mobile. Should be `100%`, not `none`.
