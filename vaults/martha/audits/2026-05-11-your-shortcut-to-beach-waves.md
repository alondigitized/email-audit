- I'm opening this on my couch on a Sunday afternoon while my daughter has fifteen minutes of iPad time and my son is theoretically doing homework. ## What's working

- **"Skip the salon. Keep the beach waves." is genuinely good copy.** Speaks directly to a busy woman who wants results without the time or money commitment. I felt that.
- **B2G1 with a hard deadline (May 22) is compelling.** I'm price-sensitive, so a real deal with a real expiration is much more actionable than "limited time" vagueness.
- **Two specific product categories visible (curling iron, waver).** Gives me something concrete to tap toward rather than a generic shop page.
- **The layout is clean and easy to skim on mobile.** Big hero, logical section breaks, readable type — I didn't have to zoom or squint once.
- **Klarna mention is a smart inclusion.** A $30+ curling iron is a small splurge for a mom watching every dollar, and installment framing makes it feel less like a splurge.

## What's weak

- **That model is not me or anyone I know at this stage of life.** She looks fresh out of college. A 34-year-old mom needs to see herself in the email, not a Pinterest fantasy.
- **Zero personalization.** I've shopped Sally Beauty before. My rewards balance, a past purchase reference, even a generic "Members earn more" badge — something. This could have been sent to my 65-year-old aunt and read exactly the same.
- **"Show me" is a weak CTA.** Show me what, exactly? "Shop Beach Wave Kits" or "Claim Your B2G1" would tell me where I'm landing and actually pull a tap out of me.
- **The B2G1 product selection is confusing.** Hair-building fibers for thinning hair and a purple toning mask for blondes don't belong in the same beach-waves pitch. It reads like clearance with a seasonal sticker slapped on it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the B2G1 in the subject line.** The deal is the strongest conversion signal. The "shortcut" framing is charming but the deal closes.
- 2. **Cast a more relatable model — or skip the model entirely and use a lifestyle shot.** A bathroom counter, a mom doing her hair while kids chaos happens in the background. Something I recognize.
- 3. **Add a loyalty/personalization hook in the hero.** Even "Your rewards + this deal = 🎉" would make me feel seen.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA:** "Shop B2G1 Hair Care" or "Build Your Beach Wave Kit" — verb + noun, destination clear.
- 5. **Curate the B2G1 products around one styling story.** If it's beach waves, show me wave sprays, texturizers, the waver itself. Pull the thinning-hair fiber out.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Two meta tags missing `http-equiv` attributes**

```html
<!-- as written — both are broken -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=edge" />

<!-- correct form -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, Outlook and legacy clients may not parse the charset declaration, risking character encoding corruption. Without `http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible"`, the IE/Outlook edge-mode hint is silently ignored.

**[MEDIUM] `@font-face` missing for 'Founders Grotesk'**

The `a` selector declares:
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` rule for `'Founders Grotesk'` exists anywhere in the provided CSS block. All anchor tags will render in Helvetica (or the system sans-serif fallback) in every client — including those that support web fonts — because the font is never loaded. If Founders Grotesk is the intended brand typeface for links, a hosted `@font-face` declaration or a fallback-only CSS change is needed.

**[MEDIUM] `img { white-space: pre; }`**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```

`white-space: pre` is non-standard on replaced elements like `<img>`. It has no defined effect on images per spec, but some WebKit-based clients (Apple Mail, some mobile clients) may insert unexpected whitespace around images. Remove or replace with `display: block` on image cells to suppress bottom-gap artifacts.

All six `@font-face` declarations reference `opensans/v17/` on `fonts.gstatic.com`. Current version is v29+. Google still serves v17 URLs, but they are a deprecated snapshot. No immediate rendering breakage, but worth updating to avoid a future silent failure.
