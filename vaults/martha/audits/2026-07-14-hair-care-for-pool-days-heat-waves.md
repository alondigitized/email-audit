# Hair care for pool days & heat waves 🌊
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Sally. Again. By my count this is at least the seventh email from them in two weeks — I still have "Summer hair starts with a freebie" from *yesterday* in my read pile — and honestly the subject got a tiny bit of a pass from me because pool season is genuinely on my mind right now. My kids have been in the water every weekend and my own hair is a crunchy disaster. So the topic lands. But when I opened it I immediately noticed: where's the deal? Yesterday was a freebie. Today is… just shopping? No code, no percentage, no "members get X." The creative is pretty and clean but the whole email feels like a plain editorial when I was primed for a follow-up offer.
- The cadence is also getting to me. "Your summer must-haves" (July 9), "Save on summer hair care essentials" (July 8), and now this one — that's three emails with basically the same summer-hair pitch inside of six days. I recognize the products but I'm not being given a new reason to click this time that I didn't have last week.
- Verdict: I'd scroll through on the couch for maybe 20 seconds, not click, and mark it read. Wouldn't forward to anyone.

## What's working

- **Seasonal relevance is dead-on.** Pool days and heat waves in mid-July — that's my actual life right now, and the "Swimmer's Shampoo / Swimmer's Conditioner" pairing is genuinely useful framing.
- **Visual layout is clean on mobile.** The hero model, the headline, the two-up product grid — nothing overlapped, nothing broken, easy to skim fast.
- **Model demographic matched me reasonably well.** She reads as a woman in her 30s with wavy hair — not a teenager, not a grandma. I didn't feel like this was aimed at someone else.
- **"JOIN SALLY!" loyalty badge is visible.** Signals there's a program, reminds me I should be a member if I'm not.
- **Subject length is mobile-friendly.** Short enough to read fully without truncation.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Yesterday had an actual freebie. Today has "Shop now." That's a downgrade in value that I notice instantly.
- **Preview text leaked garbage.** In my inbox the preheader read "Sally Beauty Shop your summer must-haves. ☀️" followed by a string of weird invisible characters. It looks like a technical glitch and it subtly erodes trust.
- **Every CTA just says "Shop now."** Six different "Shop now" links across the email — for shampoo, conditioner, heat foam, scalp oil, hair color, developer. None of them tell me *why* to click this one vs. any other product page.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Same summer hair story I've read from them three times this week. My eyes are glazing.
- **No price or product detail.** I can see product names but no sizes, no prices, no ratings. I have to click to get any information I'd actually make a decision on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete offer.** Even "members save 15%" or "free shipping on $30+" turns this from a catalog browse into a reason to act. Yesterday's freebie set an expectation — don't drop it cold.
- 2. **Rewrite every "Shop now" to something product-specific.** "Get the Swimmer's Shampoo" or "Protect my color" — anything that connects the click to a real outcome.
- 3. **Show a price or a star rating under each product.** One number. That's all it takes to give me a signal before I commit a tap.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** The hidden-character spacer trick is leaking into preview text in some clients. Cut it and write real copy.
- 5. **Give the preheader a distinct hook from the subject.** The subject says pool days — the preheader should tease *why* these specific products beat the generic drugstore stuff.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Chlorine-proof your hair this summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sally picks: hair that survives the pool`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Swimmer's shampoo, heat shield & bond repair — in one send.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The products I wish I had on the first hot weekend.`

## Technical Audit — Sally Beauty "Pool Days & Heat Waves" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard retail HTML email built on XHTML 1.0 Transitional with table-based layout and responsive media queries. Two structural meta tag defects and a missing `@font-face` declaration are the primary code-level issues; the rest of the body is truncated so link, compliance, and UTM findings are partial.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Partial assessment — HTML truncated before body links.**

- External font requests to `fonts.gstatic.com` (6 `@font-face` rules) will ping Google's servers at render time. Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection and Proton Mail both block or proxy these requests, causing Open Sans to silently fall back to system sans-serif. This is expected behavior but worth noting if render-consistency is a concern.
- Cannot confirm presence or correctness of click-tracking parameters (e.g., SendGrid/Salesforce MC link wrapping) without full source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta:**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv="Content-Type"`, this declaration is ignored by several email clients (Outlook 2007–2019, Lotus Notes). Risk: garbled multi-byte characters if any exist in the body.

**Issue 2 — Missing `http-equiv` on IE compatibility meta:**
```html
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
The attribute is a no-op as written.

**Issue 3 — `Founders Grotesk` referenced but never defined:**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
There is no `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` anywhere in the `<style>` block — only six Open Sans variants are defined. Every anchor element will render in the Helvetica fallback. If this font is hosted on a CDN, the `@font-face` rule is missing.

**Issue 4 — Dead CSS rule on `img`:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space` does not apply to replaced elements (`<img>`). The property has no effect here and should be removed to reduce noise.

**Issue 5 — Mobile breakpoint gap:**
The responsive block fires at `max-width: 499px`. Devices between 500–600px wide (some Android tablets, landscape phones) receive desktop layout with no responsive adjustments.
