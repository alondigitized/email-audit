# Summer Must-Haves Are Here
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Sally. Again. I see "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" in my inbox and I immediately think — didn't I just get "Your summer must-haves" from you, Sally, like seven days ago? I did. Same vibe, different email. At this point I know the Sally cadence well enough that the subject barely registers as new information.
- That said, I opened it. The email itself looks clean — big aspirational model shot, clear product grid, no broken images. It renders fine on mobile. But here's my issue: there is no actual offer in this email. No percentage off, no dollar threshold, no member exclusive. Just "Shop now" repeated about six times across product tiles. I'm a mom with a budget; "shop now" with no incentive is not a CTA, it's a suggestion. The Klarna mention at the bottom is a nice touch for bigger-ticket items like the curling iron, but it feels buried.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't forward it — there's nothing to share. If the banner at the top had a promo code or a real offer, I'd be clicking. Without one, this is just a lookbook.

## What's working

- Clean render — no broken images, no layout weirdness, reads top to bottom without friction on a phone screen
- The hero model is on-brand and aspirational for a woman in her 30s who actually cares about her hair
- Summer framing makes sense right now — heat protection spray and a curling iron are contextually relevant in July
- The product grid is easy to scan: name, image, "Shop now" — no clutter
- Klarna callout at the bottom is a smart inclusion for tools in the $40–80 range

## What's weak

- Subject "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" is nearly word-for-word identical to "Your summer must-haves" from July 9th — I noticed instantly, and it erodes trust in the brand's content calendar
- Zero offer in the hero — "Keep your summer style soft, shiny & hydrated" is a brand line, not an incentive; I'm price-sensitive and this email gives me no reason to act today
- No loyalty language anywhere — I'm clearly a known subscriber; treat me like one
- The banner at the very top looks promotional but is unreadable at mobile size — whatever deal is there, it's invisible
- "Shop now" as every single CTA is lazy; "Save on Silk Drops" or "Get the Waver" would give me something to grab onto

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with an actual offer** — a specific dollar-off threshold ("Spend $30, save $8") or a member exclusive in the hero would give me a reason to click immediately; right now this is a category browse, not a conversion email
- 2. **Fix the top banner legibility** — if there's a deal there (and it looks like there is), make it readable; a deal hidden behind tiny red text on mobile might as well not exist
- 3. **Rotate the subject line** — "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" is the same energy as last week's send; even a small pivot helps with fatigue
- 4. **Add loyalty acknowledgment** — one line ("As a Beauty Club member, you unlock free shipping on orders $25+") would remind me why I'm subscribed in the first place
- 5. **Pick a hero product** — the Infrared Curling Iron or the Deep Waver could anchor the whole email if given a price, a before/after, or a member price callout

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags missing `http-equiv` attribute (confirmed):**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />                             <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, these directives are non-functional. Outlook/IE rendering modes may not respect the IE edge instruction.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` — non-standard rule (confirmed):**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` elements has no visual effect but is non-standard and adds unnecessary noise; some aggressive email client CSS parsers may behave unexpectedly.

**`Founders Grotesk` not declared in `@font-face` (confirmed):**
```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the provided `<style>`. All link text will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif. If this is intentional (system font fallback only), it should be documented; if not, the `@font-face` declaration is missing.

**Open Sans loaded at version 17 (outdated):**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Current Open Sans on Google Fonts is v40+. Google has historically served old version paths indefinitely, but this is a latent risk if paths are deprecated. More practically, v17 lacks variable font support.

**Mobile breakpoint at 499px vs. industry standard 600px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)
```
Devices with viewport widths between 500–599px (common on Android) will receive the desktop layout. Standard email breakpoint is `max-width: 600px`.
