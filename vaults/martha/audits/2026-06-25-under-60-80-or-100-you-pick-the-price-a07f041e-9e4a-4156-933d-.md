# Under $60, $80, or $100, You Pick the Price
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, the price-tier layout is genuinely fresh — "You Pick the Price" is the most honest framing Skechers has used all month, and after back-to-back BOGO emails (the June 14 "Members Only" send is still in my head) I'm actually relieved to see something new. Three buckets, self-sort immediately, no mental math. "Your Budget" as hero copy is exactly how I actually think before I even open the app — I have a number, not a wish list.
- Here's the problem: this email does absolutely nothing for my kids. I'm a mom first when I'm browsing Skechers. My daughter needs sneakers before camp starts and my son destroys shoes on a two-month rotation. "Kids" appears once — in the secondary footer nav — and that's it. The whole body is women's adult styles. The $250 gift card block caught my eye for a second, but I genuinely couldn't tell if it was a sweepstakes, an earned reward, or just aspirational branding. I was still staring at it when I scrolled past it.
- I'd open this — the subject earns the tap — but I'd likely skip every hero CTA and hunt for the Kids link at the bottom. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- "Your Budget" in the hero is smart, direct, and lands for someone like me who mentally sets a cap before browsing.
- Three distinct price-tier blocks scan fast on a phone — I can find "under $60" in two seconds, which is where I start for the kids.
- Clean layout, no visual clutter — easy thumb navigation top to bottom.
- The BOGO banner at the top is still real money, even if I've seen it five times this month.
- Skechers Plus "Thank You" acknowledgment makes me feel like a known customer, not a cold email target.

## What's weak

- Kids shoes are completely invisible in the primary content — the only mention is buried in the secondary nav, and as someone who shops kids first, that's a dealbreaker for the main CTA.
- BOGO fatigue is setting in hard. Opening yet another email with that same banner at the top made me sigh before I even got to the new stuff.
- The $250 gift card block is confusing — is this a drawing I've been entered in? A spend-to-earn offer? A milestone reward? No explanation, just a big number and "Thank You."
- No deadline anywhere in the email. The price-tier concept is compelling but there's zero urgency — I'll tell myself I'll come back and I won't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a kids price-tier block.** Even one section — "Kids' Styles Under $50" with two or three styles — turns this into a family email. That's who I am.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** JSON schema is leaking into the preheader again — same issue from multiple prior sends. It's the first signal of "broken email" before I even decide to open.
- 3. **Let the price-tier concept lead; bury the BOGO.** The new framing deserves top billing. The BOGO can live below the hero or in the footer — it doesn't need to compete with the fresh idea.
- 4. **Clarify the gift card.** One sentence: "As a Plus member, you're entered to win" or "Spend $X to unlock" — just say what it is.
- 5. **Add a deadline.** "Valid through Sunday" or "This week only" is all it takes to make me act today.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Under $60/$80/$100" Email
**From:** no-reply@emails.skechers.com | **Date:** 2026-06-25

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, SFMC stack, table-based layout) but has a systemic HTTP-vs-HTTPS image delivery problem that will cause broken images in security-enforcing clients. One tracking pixel has a malformed URL that will never fire.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed Return Path pixel URL — will not fire**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
The `src` value is missing its protocol prefix entirely — not even `//`. Browsers and email clients treat this as a relative path. Validity/Return Path inbox monitoring data for this campaign is likely zero.

**HTTP tracking pixel on external domain**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=...
```
Plain HTTP from a third-party domain. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS resources; this pixel will not fire in those clients.

**26 click-redirect links** all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — expected for SFMC. QA skipped probing them, so destination URL health and UTM parameter presence are unverified (see Section 6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Systemic HTTP image delivery** — 11 of the confirmed image assets are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com` (not HTTPS). Affected assets include the Skechers logo, App Store/Google Play badges, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store, and all social icons. Gmail image proxy and Outlook.com will either block or proxy-rewrite these; Apple Mail on iOS 15+ blocks mixed content. Images will render broken or with an HTTPS-proxied version that may differ from the hosted original.

**Missing alt text on 2 images**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 open pixel) — low risk, decorative
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — unknown content; if this is a product/promo image, screen readers and image-blocked clients receive nothing

**Malformed `<meta>` tags** — two head tags are missing required attributes:
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />               <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                                 <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Most clients tolerate this, but it's technically invalid and may behave unexpectedly in older Outlook versions.

**Duplicate media query blocks** — breakpoint rules for `max-width: 480px` appear in a second `<style>` block below the first. The second block only partially re-declares rules from the first, creating two competing rule sources for the same breakpoint. Risk: specificity collisions if rules diverge in future sends.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** applied globally via `* {}` selector. This prevents iOS from reflowing small text, which can cause legibility issues at certain font sizes — `100%` is the safer value.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved `%%variable%%` or `{{token}}` placeholders visible in the truncated source. Recipient ID token appears correctly merged in the ink1000 pixel (`mi_u=8108404282`). Krux DMP pixels carry both SHA-256 hashed email and raw subscriber ID — both appear populated rather than literal token strings, suggesting correct merge at send time.

No issues found with personalization token rendering.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 destination links are wrapped in `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects — UTM parameters on final landing URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects. The campaign identifier `MKG_US_L60D_U_MULTI_ENGAGEDOPEN_EN_06222026` is present in two tracking pixels, which suggests campaign attribution is set up at the pixel level. Whether UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_campaign`, etc.) survive through the click-redirect to skechers.com cannot be confirmed from this source.

**Recommended check:** Resolve 2–3 sample click links and confirm `utm_source=email&utm_campaign=MKG_US_L60D...` (or equivalent) is appended to the final destination URL.
