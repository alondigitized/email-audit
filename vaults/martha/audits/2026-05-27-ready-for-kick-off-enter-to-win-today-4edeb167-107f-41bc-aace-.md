- 2. **Add a secondary offer lane for non-sweepstakes shoppers** — a simple "Shop while you enter" strip with an actual discount (even 15% off kids') captures people like me who won't enter a contest but are ready to buy.
- 3. **Put a kids' product module directly below the sweepstakes block** — "Gear up your little fan too" with velcro sneakers for girls and lace-ups for boys would make parents the hero of this send, not an afterthought.
- 4. **Show the actual prize** — the body copy says "jerseys to sneakers" but which sneakers? If there's a limited World Cup Skechers collab in that prize pack, show it. That visual would pull me in even if I'm not entering.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (high risk):** Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content images. 14 images are affected, including the Skechers logo, App Store/Google Play badges, social icons, and nav icons — all served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com/`:

**Missing alt text:** `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel) and `49468f73...png` have no `alt` attribute. The pixel omission is acceptable; the content image is not.

**Malformed meta tags:** Both charset and IE-compat directives are missing `http-equiv`:
```html
<!-- actual -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=Edge" />

<!-- correct -->
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=Edge" />
```
Outlook and older IE renderers may ignore these without the attribute.

**Duplicate media query blocks:** Rules for `.hideme`, `.widthFull`, `.width50percent`, etc. are defined identically at both `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px`. Not a rendering failure but inflates stylesheet size unnecessarily.

No other unresolved tokens (`%%`, `{{`, `[[`) were visible in the truncated source, but the greeting failure suggests the contact record or substitution step has a data gap worth auditing across the send cohort.
