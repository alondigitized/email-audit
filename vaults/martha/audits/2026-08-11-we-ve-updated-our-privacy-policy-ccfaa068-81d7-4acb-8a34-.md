# We’ve Updated Our Privacy Policy
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- Yeah, this is just a legal notice, not a marketing email — I opened it thinking maybe there was a deal buried under that weird preview text, and nope, it's just Skechers telling me they updated their Privacy Policy. No shoes, no offer, no kids' section, nothing for me to actually do here except maybe skim the bullet points out of mild curiosity about the "AI tools to analyze customer service interactions" bit, which honestly is a little unsettling, not exciting.
- I'm not mad at it — every retailer has to send these — but there's zero reason for me to click into anything besides maybe glancing at what data they're now collecting. Definitely not forwarding this to a friend, definitely not something I'd screenshot for the group chat. It's a "read in three seconds, archive" email.
- Compared to the back-to-school stuff they've been sending me all week (the 2-for-$100 bundle, the 25% off kids styles), this is just noise in between. Doesn't hurt my opinion of the brand, doesn't help it either — it's just compliance mail.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — no clutter, no broken images, easy to read without zooming.
- The bullet list of what changed is actually specific (AI customer service, ad tracking, field testing, appeal rights) rather than vague "we value your privacy" fluff.
- Customer support phone/email and footer info are all clearly laid out if I did need to reach someone.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to me as a shopper — no kids' shoes, no offer, no reason to open this over any other email in my inbox.
- The preview text is a garbled mess of raw links and template code ("Skechers(R) https://view.emails... web version https://www.skechers.com?src=template Hello, We have updated o") — that's the kind of thing that makes me think an email is broken or spam before I even open it.
- "Expanded analytics and advertising capabilities, including... post-purchase offers" reads like corporate-speak for "we're going to track you more," which isn't a great feeling to open with.
- No personalization at all — "Hello," not even my name, so it clearly reads as a stranger blast, not anything tailored to me as a member.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix that preview text — it's leaking raw URLs and boilerplate instead of real preheader copy, which is the single biggest thing hurting this email's credibility in the inbox.
- 2. Since this has to go out anyway, at least drop in "Hi Martha," at the top — costs nothing and makes it feel less like spam.
- 3. Consider a one-line reassurance near the AI/analytics bullets (e.g., "your payment info is never used to train these tools") — as a parent, that kind of language change makes me pause before I even read the actual point.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New AI, analytics, and privacy rights — here's what's changing.`
