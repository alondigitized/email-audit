# Make Her Mother’s Day ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I'm looking at this Sally Beauty Mother's Day email and my honest reaction is... it's pretty, it's timely, but it's not really *for* me in the way it thinks it is. I'm a mom who shops for her kids first, herself last, and when I do treat myself it's not usually a $60 flat iron on impulse. The "Make Her Mother's Day" subject line actually threw me — wait, am I supposed to be the buyer here? I'm the mom. That framing missed me before I even opened it. The 40% off is a real hook, and the layout is clean on my phone, but everything highlighted is styling tools, and I'd need to already be in the market for a flat iron to click.

## What's working

- The timing is genuinely good — six days before Mother's Day is the window where I might actually tell my husband "hey, look at this." The "Gifts for Mom from under $25" section lower in the email is the most relevant thing to me as a price-conscious shopper; that framing hits. The layout reads well on mobile — big clean sections, tap-able CTAs, nothing crowded. The 40% off is a real, specific number, not some vague "great value" nonsense. And I appreciate that the products shown are named specifically (Primo Titanium Flat Iron 1 Inch, XL Nano Ceramic Salon Flat Iron 1 1/2 Inch) rather than just generic "flat iron."

## What's weak

- **The "Make Her" framing is a real miss.** I opened this email. I am a mom. "Make Her Mother's Day" positions me as a gift-buyer for someone else's mom. If Sally Beauty has my email, they know I'm their customer — the subject should say "Make YOUR Mother's Day." That one word swap changes everything.
- **Four products, all styling tools.** The hero copy promises "hair, nails, fragrance & more she'll love every day" — but the email delivers four flat irons and curling irons. Where are the nail kits? The travel-sized fragrance sets? The under-$25 gifting promise in the bottom section has no products attached to it. I had to take it on faith.
- **No prices shown.** Not a single product has a price on it. I can't quickly gauge whether 40% off means something is now $18 or $58. For a price-sensitive shopper, I need that anchor before I decide if it's worth a click.
- **The preview text leads with "Sally Beauty"** — already the sender. That's wasted real estate. I'd rather see the actual offer in those first characters.
- **"Shop the love"** in the preheader is marketing-speak filler. It doesn't add information; it just takes up space where a price or deadline could live.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the subject:** "Make YOUR Mother's Day, [Name] ✨" or "Treat yourself, Mom — 40% off today" speaks to me as the recipient, not a third-party buyer.
- 2. **Show a price on the hero product.** Even one line — "Was $49.99, now $29.99" — gives me the quick scan I need before deciding to tap.
- 3. **Diversify the product grid.** Include one nail item and one fragrance/self-care item to deliver on the "all in one place" headline. Four flat irons in a row is a very narrow slice.
- 4. **Make the under-$25 section actionable.** Give it an actual product grid, not just a lifestyle shot and a line of copy. That's the section that most aligns with my budget.
- 5. **Surface a loyalty angle.** If Sally Beauty has a rewards program, show me what I'd earn or what member pricing looks like. That's what keeps me loyal to brands.
- 6. **Add an explicit deadline.** "40% off ends May 9" beats implied urgency from Mother's Day. Make it stick.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Malformed charset meta tag (confirmed)**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Missing the required `http-equiv="Content-Type"` attribute. The correct form is:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, this tag is ignored by conforming parsers. Encoding fallback behavior varies by client; risk is garbled special characters (accented names, currency symbols, the ✨ in the subject line).

**Issue 2 — `white-space: pre` on all `<img>` elements (confirmed)**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space` is not a valid property for replaced elements (`<img>`). While most email clients will discard it silently, Outlook on Windows (Word rendering engine) and some WebKit-based clients may interpret this unexpectedly, causing phantom whitespace gaps between stacked images. The `-ms-interpolation-mode` property is Outlook-specific and benign, but `white-space: pre` should be removed.

**Issue 3 — 'Founders Grotesk' font referenced but never declared (confirmed)**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
The `<head>` contains `@font-face` declarations only for **Open Sans** (6 variants via `fonts.gstatic.com`). There is no `@font-face` or `<link>` for 'Founders Grotesk'. All `<a>` tags silently fall back to Helvetica. If Founders Grotesk is the intended link style, either add its `@font-face` source or align the declaration with the loaded font (Open Sans).

**Issue 4 — External Google Fonts via `fonts.gstatic.com` (confirmed)**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...woff2
```
- **Version**: Open Sans v17 is loaded; the current version is v40+. Older versions may be purged from CDN cache at some point, though this is low near-term risk.
- **Client blocking**: Gmail (webmail and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and many corporate proxies block external font URLs. The fallback stack for Open Sans declarations is not visible in the excerpt — verify it is set to a safe system font (e.g., `Arial, sans-serif`).

**Issue 5 — Mobile breakpoint at 499px (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```
Industry standard is `max-width: 600px`. Viewports between 500–599px wide (common on Android devices at default zoom) will receive desktop layout rules, potentially causing horizontal overflow or text truncation.
