# Get Ahead of Back to School Shopping
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Oh wow — okay, so this one actually hit different. "Back to School" is basically a Bat-Signal for me right now. My son starts fourth grade in September and my daughter is going into kindergarten, so I'm actively thinking about shoes for both of them. I literally have a reminder in my phone that says "kids shoes before August." So when I opened this and saw that big BOGO 50% OFF hero right at the top — for a store I already have a rewards account with — I felt seen.
- But I have to say: before I even got to that good stuff, my inbox preview was another wall of JSON schema code from Skechers. That makes eight times in a row now. Eight. I'm so conditioned to it at this point that I've started mentally skimming it before I even register the subject line. That's a brand training me to ignore their preview text, which is genuinely impressive in the worst possible way.
- Inside the email, though? This is probably the most relevant thing they've sent me in a while. There's a Kids' section with what looks like a pink sneaker — perfect timing for my daughter — and the BOGO deal is front and center. I'd click. I'd probably click twice, once for each kid. I wouldn't forward it because my friends would see the same email from their own Skechers inboxes anyway.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is impossible to miss** — BOGO 50% off in massive bold yellow text lands immediately, no scrolling needed. That's the exact kind of deal that makes me stop mid-scroll.
- **"Back to School Preview" framing is perfectly timed** — July is exactly when I start thinking about fall shoes for the kids. This doesn't feel late or premature.
- **Skechers Plus membership is called out in the hero** — I have an account, so seeing Plus branding up top tells me this offer is for me specifically, not just any passerby.
- **Kids' section is present with a pink/purple shoe** — My daughter would lose her mind over that shoe. Seeing kids' footwear early in the scroll tells me they're thinking about families, not just adult runners.
- **Clean layout, no broken images** — After so many emails from this brand, I half-expected a visual mess, but the body renders completely fine. Good hierarchy, easy to tap.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is JSON garbage again — eight times in a row** — At this point it's practically a Skechers logo. I'm desensitized, but it still signals sloppiness before I even open the email.
- **No boys' shoe prominently featured** — The Kids' section shows what looks like a pink/purple sneaker. My 9-year-old son needs durable lace-ups in blue or black. He's invisible in this email.
- **No specific price points or product names** — I'm a researcher. Telling me "SHOP KIDS'" is fine, but showing me a $39.99 shoe or calling out "Skechers Uno" by name would stop me cold. Right now it's category browsing, not a deal.
- **No urgency deadline anywhere** — "Back to School Preview" implies early access, but without an end date I might bookmark this mentally and forget it entirely by tomorrow morning.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text leak — this is number eight.** I cannot stress this enough. Whatever JSON schema injection is running in their email template, it's been broken for at least two straight weeks of audits. This should be a P0 fix on the ESP side.
- 2. **Add a boy's sneaker to the Kids' section** — Families often have multiple kids. Show me a blue or black lace-up next to the pink one. I have two kids, not one.
- 3. **Put a "Ends Sunday" or specific date on the BOGO hero** — Give me a reason to click now instead of later. "Preview" implies limited time but doesn't commit to one.
- 4. **Swap "SHOP KIDS'" for something specific** — "Shop Back-to-School Shoes" or even "See All Kids' Picks" pulls harder than the generic verb.
- 5. **Surface one or two specific product names with prices** — Even just "$44.99 · Skechers Uno Kids'" beneath the category CTA gives me something to anchor on.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus members: buy one pair, get the second half off. Kids' styles included.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Get Ahead of Back to School Shopping"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken links or missing unsubscribe mechanisms, but carries a pervasive HTTP-over-HTTPS image loading problem affecting 10+ assets, plus two third-party pixel trackers that leak subscriber PII to Krux/Salesforce DMP without HTTPS protection.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links** all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard ESP wrapping, not flagged as broken.

**Three embedded tracking pixels of concern:**

- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif` (×2) — Krux DMP user-match pixel. Passes `partner_uid=122277199` (a plaintext subscriber ID) and a SHA-256 hashed email (`adf27fee...`). This is a third-party data sync; subscribers may not expect this.
- `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_ad_impression.gif` — Ad impression pixel with campaign ID `PRO_US_NA_U_SITEBUNDLE_FULL_EN_07082026` embedded in-email.
- `pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` — Inbox placement monitoring pixel (Return Path / Validity). Loads over **HTTP**, not HTTPS:
  ```
  src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79..."
  ```
  Missing protocol prefix entirely — will be treated as a relative URL and fail silently.
- `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — Additional open-tracking pixel over plain HTTP.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (10 confirmed, likely more in truncated HTML):**

All assets served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com` will be blocked by Outlook 2016+, Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection, and any client that enforces HTTPS-only mixed-content policy. Affected images:

| Asset | Alt text |
|---|---|
| `dde00662...png` | "Skechers" (logo) |
| `49468f73...png` | *(missing alt)* |
| `0ec56c9f...png` | "Available on the App Store" |
| `9bb508d0...png` | "Android App on Google Play" |
| `00100b23...png` | "Curbside Pickup" |
| `07cb60b5...png` | "AfterPay" |
| `fc08601a...png` | "Find A Store" |
| `b782e0aa...png` | "Instagram" |
| `a5c4cae0...png` | "Facebook" |
| `8c5738fc...png` | "YouTube" |

**Missing alt text** on two images: `o.gif` (open pixel — acceptable) and `49468f73...png` (content image — not acceptable; screen readers will announce the filename).

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is set globally. This prevents user agents from scaling text on mobile even when the user has accessibility font size settings enabled. Should be `100%`, not `none`.

**Duplicate media query blocks:** The `@media (max-width: 375px)` and `@media (max-width: 640px)` blocks are identical in content. The 375px block is entirely redundant and adds dead CSS weight.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe:** Not visible in the truncated source, but the presence of a legal/footer section is implied by the `.legal` CSS class. Cannot confirm unsubscribe link or physical mailing address without full HTML — these must be verified against the complete source.

**Authentication headers:** Not assessable from HTML alone; DKIM/SPF/DMARC must be checked against raw email headers. Sending domain is `emails.skechers.com` — a subdomain ESP setup which is standard, but alignment with `skechers.com` DMARC policy should be confirmed.

**Third-party DMP pixel (Krux):** The Krux user-match pixel transmits a hashed subscriber email and a numeric UID to a third party (`beacon.krxd.net`). If Skechers' privacy policy does not disclose this data sharing, it may create exposure under CCPA/state privacy laws given the California nexus of the brand.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `PRO_US_NA_U_SITEBUNDLE_FULL_EN_07082026` is present in the Krux impression pixel and the ink1000 open pixel, indicating internal campaign tagging exists. However, with 26 click links all redirected through `click.emails.skechers.com`, UTM parameter pass-through cannot be confirmed from the source alone — the redirect chain must be followed to verify that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` survive to the skechers.com landing page.
