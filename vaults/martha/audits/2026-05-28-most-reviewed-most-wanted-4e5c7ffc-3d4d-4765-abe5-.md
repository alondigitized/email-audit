# Most Reviewed & Most Wanted

- Oh look, the JSON is back. I genuinely don't understand how a brand this size keeps letting schema garbage run as the preview text — I flagged it in my head on May 21st, again on the 27th, and here we are on the 28th. ## What's working

- BOGO 50% off banner is the very first thing I see — no hunting, no fine-print excavation. That's the offer. Good execution.
- BOBS B Cute 2.0 in that dusty pink colorway is genuinely cute, and "easy on/off" in the review copy is a direct hit for a mom who wears practical shoes.
- Real customer quote under each product gives me actual social proof instead of generic marketing language.
- "SHOP BOBS," "SHOP ARCH FIT," "SHOP FITS" are real verb+noun CTAs — not "Discover" or "Explore."
- Email renders clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything stacks properly on mobile.

## What's weak

- The JSON schema preview text is back for the third time in eight days (May 21, May 27, now today). At this point it feels like nobody is watching the inbox experience at all.
- Zero kids product in the hero or featured sections — for an email claiming "Most Reviewed & Most Wanted," my most-wanted is a velcro sneaker my kindergartner can get on herself and a durable lace-up for my 9-year-old. Neither is here.
- The Arch Fit Arcade section reads as a men's shoe to me — gray, "nostalgia vibe," gym-adjacent styling. I scrolled past it instantly.
- The 25% Off Apparel block feels bolted on at the bottom like an afterthought. I came for shoes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text, permanently.** Whatever template or ESP setting is dumping JSON schema into the preheader field needs to be patched at the source. This is now a documented recurring failure and it's costing opens on otherwise decent emails.
- 2. **Feature at least one kids product in the body** — a light-up sneaker, a velcro style, anything. If this is a "Most Reviewed" curated list, kids shoes have to be in it. Drop the Arch Fit Arcade section for a family-focused spotlight.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the BOGO.** "Buy One, Get One 50% off on select styles" is compelling — "ends Sunday" would make me shop tonight instead of maybe-this-weekend.
- 4. **Cut or relocate the apparel block.** It dilutes the shoe focus and makes the email feel less curated.

## Technical Audit — SKECHERS "Most Reviewed & Most Wanted" (05/28/2026)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken personalization token produces a visibly malformed greeting in all sends. The remaining issues are infrastructure-level: a mix of HTTP and HTTPS image sources, missing alt attributes on several images, and a third-party pixel loaded over plain HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Third-party pixel over HTTP (broken in modern clients)**
- `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — loaded in a `<table>` outside the hidden preheader block; plain HTTP will be blocked or mixed-content-warned in Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail (since iOS 15), and most modern clients.
- Evidence: `<img src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=...">` — no `display:none` applied, renders as a visible 1×1.

**27 click-redirect links** — tracked via `click.emails.skechers.com`; domain-redirect pattern is expected. HTTP probing was skipped by QA; destination URLs were not validated for 4xx/5xx status or correct UTM passthrough.

**Open pixel** — `click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?...` correctly placed inside the `display:none` hidden block.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 13 confirmed**

All images hosted on `image.emails.skechers.com` use `http://` not `https://`. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, app store badges, footer icons (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, AfterPay, Curbside Pickup, Find A Store), and one unlabeled product image. Mixed-content rules will cause these to be blocked or load broken in secure email clients.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — Empty greeting token (confirmed broken experience)**

QA flags: `Greeting has empty name field (e.g. "Hi ,")`. The subscriber first-name merge field is resolving to an empty string rather than falling back to a default (e.g., "Hi there," or "Hi, Valued Customer,"). This renders as `Hi ,` with a dangling comma visible to recipients whose profile lacks a first name value.

Root cause is likely a missing fallback in the ESP merge tag, e.g. Salesforce Marketing Cloud syntax should be:
```
%%=v(@FirstName)=%% → %%[ IF EMPTY(@FirstName) THEN SET @FirstName = "there" ENDIF ]%%
```

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM / unsubscribe** — Truncated source prevents full verification of footer unsubscribe link, but the sending domain (`emails.skechers.com`) and physical address presence cannot be confirmed from the supplied excerpt. QA summary shows 83% pass rate with no explicit compliance failures flagged, so these are likely present in the untruncated footer.

**Authentication headers** — Not evaluable from HTML source alone; requires DMARC/DKIM/SPF header inspection on a delivered message. No issues confirmed or denied.

**Preheader** — Present and correctly hidden: `<div class="preheader" style="font-size:1px;display:none !important">BOGO 50% Off...</div>`. Text is visible in inbox preview as intended.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Campaign ID `MKG_US_NONCLICK_U_MULTI_NONPURCH_EN_05282026` appears in two tracking pixels (krxd.net impression pixel, ink1000 pixel). Whether this same campaign identifier propagates into UTM parameters on destination URLs (`skechers.com` product pages) could not be verified — the 27 click-redirect links were not probed. This is the primary open risk: if `utm_campaign` on landing pages differs from the pixel campaign ID, attribution will be split across two values in analytics.
