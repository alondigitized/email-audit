# Don’t Miss Your Beauty Refresh ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- **Persona:** Martha Stroll, 34, Millennial mom of two, mobile-first shopper
- OK so I'll be straight — I'm not a Sally Beauty person in the way I'm a Skechers person. My brain is usually on sneakers for the kids, not a hair color refresh for me. But I do exist as a 34-year-old woman with hair, and the Mother's Day angle in this email made me pause a half-second longer than I expected. The email is visually clean, loads fast (or looks like it did), and throws several real deals at me immediately. What kills it is that none of the CTAs give me a reason to click *specifically* — it's all "Shop now" everywhere — and I'm not a Manic Panic person, so the hero product bundle means nothing to me. The vague subject line didn't help either. If my husband were looking for a sub-$25 gift idea for me, this might actually be useful forwarded to him. But do I click for myself? Probably not today.

## What's working

- **The deals are real and readable.** Four distinct offer types in one email — a bundle price point, two multi-buy promotions, and a gift-under-$25 bucket. I can see the value prop without squinting.
- **Mother's Day hook is well-timed.** "Gifts for Mom under $25" is the smartest thing in this email. It's May 7th. That section speaks directly to a real occasion. A busy husband could screenshot that and buy something. That forwarding scenario is actually pretty likely from me.
- **Layout is clean on mobile.** Big tap areas, readable font, no weird sideways scrolling. Sally Beauty clearly knows its audience browses on a phone. That's appreciated.
- **Klarna notice is present.** I notice the installment info at the bottom — useful if a product catches my eye and runs over budget.

## What's weak

- **"Shop now" four times in a row.** Every single CTA says "Shop now." There's no specificity about what I'm shopping *for*. "Get the bundle," "Shop Mom gifts," "Try B2G1" — any of those would drive more intent. The vagueness makes my thumb lazy.
- **Manic Panic is the wrong hero for me.** I'm 34 with a morning routine built around efficiency. A 3-color lightning kit is not where I live. I'd bet good money that demographic segmentation could cut this differently for a core-customer audience vs. a casual Sally Beauty shopper.
- **The preview text leaks brand name first.** "Sally Beauty So many hair…" — the sender line already says Sally Beauty. Starting the preview with the brand name again wastes the one line of extra copy I actually read in my inbox before deciding to open.
- **No urgency anchor.** Mother's Day is literally two weekends away. I don't see a date, a countdown, or "while supplies last" anywhere that I can read. If it said "Order by May 10th for Mom Day delivery" I'd probably click right now.
- **Personalization is zero.** I'm Martha. This could have gone to literally anyone. No "Hi Martha," no "your favorites," no "because you bought X." I shop rewards programs specifically because they're supposed to know me — this doesn't feel like that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill generic CTAs.** Match button copy to the section it sits in. "Shop Mom gifts," "Get the bundle," "Try B2G1." Five extra words, much higher click intent.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the Mother's Day module.** "Order by Sunday, May 11" or similar. The holiday is the built-in urgency — use it explicitly.
- 3. **Move the preview text off the brand name.** Start with the offer: "Gifts for Mom under $25 + hair deals all week."
- 4. **Segment by purchase history.** If I've never bought hair dye, don't lead with Manic Panic. Lead with the care or styling products I actually buy.
- 5. **Surface loyalty/rewards if I have points.** "You have 340 points — here's what $25 gets you" would absolutely make me tap.
- 1. **Declare `Founders Grotesk` via `@font-face` or remove the reference** — the silent Helvetica fallback affects all link rendering and may be unintentional.
- 2. **Update Open Sans CDN paths from `v17` to the current version** or switch to a self-hosted copy to prevent future breakage.
- 3. **Raise the mobile breakpoint from 499px to 600px** to cover mid-range Android viewports.
- 4. **Audit full source for `alt` attributes** on all `<img>` tags before send.
- 5. **Confirm footer completeness** — physical address, unsubscribe link, and `List-Unsubscribe` headers are legally required and not verifiable from the provided excerpt.
- 6. **Verify UTM params on all body CTAs** once full source is available.
- 7. Remove `img { white-space: pre; }` — it has no semantic effect on images and risks side effects.
