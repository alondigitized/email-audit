# The sneakers you'll reach for every single day
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I'm in the school pickup line scrolling through my inbox, and this one from Skechers lands. The subject is actually pretty good — "reach for every single day" is literally how I shop for shoes, I need something I can wear from drop-off to the grocery store to the park. The "Effortless Casuals" hero copy inside confirms that. And yes, the BOGO 50% off banner is right at the top so I see the deal immediately — same BOGO they've been running since at least the June 18 "your month" send, but okay, it's a good offer.
- Here's where I lose interest fast: I open Skechers emails half the time because I'm trying to solve my kids' shoe situation — my 5-year-old needs velcro sneakers and my 9-year-old blows through soles every six weeks. This email has zero kids content in the main body. There's a tiny "KIDS" link buried in the bottom navigation, and that's it. The Men's section takes up almost as much space as the Women's section, so roughly half of what I'm looking at doesn't apply to me at all. The "Made to Move" athletic insert at the bottom also feels like a last-minute add from a different campaign.
- And that preview text — JSON schema code, again. I've seen it in at least four of their last six emails. It doesn't make me unsubscribe, but it makes me trust them a little less every time. I'll probably mark this read and come back to it "later," which means never. Mild click for myself if I had five minutes and was in a "treat myself" mood, no click for the kids.

## What's working

- "Effortless Casuals" + the comfort copy underneath the hero is the right pitch — go-anywhere, all-day wear is exactly what I need for myself
- BOGO 50% off is at the very top of the email, above any scroll, impossible to miss
- The female model is styled like a real person — casual blue jeans, relaxed top — not a glossy ad, actually looks like someone at my stage of life
- "$120 & UNDER" price label in the button row is exactly the kind of filter I want to see; it tells me immediately whether this is in my range
- The email body renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout weirdness

## What's weak

- No kids content anywhere in the main body — for a mom-shopper, this is the biggest miss in the entire send
- The JSON preview text is still broken, same issue I've mentally flagged on at least four consecutive Skechers emails this month — it now reads as a known engineering problem they haven't bothered to fix
- The Men's section eats significant real estate; for a woman shopping mostly for herself and her kids, that's wasted scroll
- No urgency or deadline on the BOGO — "limited time" is nowhere, so there's zero reason to act now vs. closing the tab
- Two competing offers (BOGO 50% off + 25% off apparel) dilute each other; I'm not sure which one to use or whether they stack

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a kids module — one row, four shoes.** Two for girls (velcro, color pop), two for boys (durable, lace-up). This would double my click motivation immediately and is the single highest-impact change for reaching a parent-shopper like me.
- 2. **Fix the preview text.** Six emails deep with broken JSON in the preheader. Whatever template or automation is injecting schema markup needs to be corrected before the next deploy — this is a real trust signal against them.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline to the BOGO.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" creates urgency. Right now I feel zero pressure to not just close the tab.
- 4. **Shrink the Men's section or reframe it as family.** Replace half of Men's real estate with kids, or rebrand the whole section as "For the whole crew" — a callback to the June 19 send that actually resonated with me.
- 5. **Consolidate the offers.** Pick BOGO or 25% off apparel as the hero offer — not both. Two offers creates decision fatigue and weakens each one.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Go-anywhere comfort under $120 — offer ends Sunday`

## Technical Audit — Skechers "sneakers you'll reach for every single day" (06/24/2026)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email deploys correctly across major clients at a structural level, but carries a broken tracking pixel (missing URL scheme), a widespread HTTP image problem across all hosted assets, and a misleading QA summary that masked 14 real warnings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken pixel — missing URL scheme**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0...">
```
No `https://` prefix. Email clients interpret this as a relative path; the Return Path deliverability pixel silently fails to fire every send. This is a live defect.

**HTTP tracking pixel**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_..._B
```
Plain HTTP. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com all proxy or block HTTP image requests. This third-party open/impression tracker is likely dark.

**26 click-tracking links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.emails.skechers.com` (HTTPS, correct). QA skipped HTTP probing on all 26 — no broken-destination check was performed.

**QA summary discrepancy**
The automated output states "100% pass rate, 0 issue(s), 0 warning(s)" in the summary line while the body lists 14 warnings. The pass rate reflects only the skipped link probes, not image or protocol health. Do not use that summary line as a health signal.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**All image assets served over HTTP**
Every image from `image.emails.skechers.com` uses `http://`, including the logo and all footer icons (App Store, Google Play, social icons, Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store). Evidence:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-...png
... (9 total)
```
Gmail and Yahoo block mixed-content images by default. Recipients on those clients see broken image placeholders across the entire footer.

**Missing alt text**
Two images lack `alt` attributes:
- `o.gif` (ink1000 pixel)
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` (unknown content image)

**Invalid `<meta>` attributes**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />        <!-- missing http-equiv -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                         <!-- missing http-equiv -->
```
Both tags omit `http-equiv`. Technically invalid; inconsequential in most clients but fails strict HTML validators.

**Desktop/mobile content toggle**
The template defaults `mobile-content: display:block` and hides `desktop-content` with `display:none`, then reverses at `min-width: 768px`. Outlook 2016–2021 ignores `@media` queries entirely and will render only the mobile layout regardless of screen size.
