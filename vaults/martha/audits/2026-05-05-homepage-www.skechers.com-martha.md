# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-05
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I was not expecting to feel seen like this. I open Skechers and the first thing I see is a mom kissing her little girl — both in pink sneakers — with "The Mother's Day Shop" in big script. I'm a mom of two, it's May, and they've got a BOGO 50% deal sitting right in the top banner. This feels like they knew I was coming. The vibe is warm, it's seasonal, and it's not trying too hard.
- **8/10**
- + Hero copy and imagery explicitly speak to my persona: "The Mother's Day Shop" with a mom-and-daughter image — that IS me
- + Concrete offer above fold: "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart" in the blue top banner, plus "25% OFF Apparel" in a secondary strip
- + Primary CTA visible: big "SHOP NOW" button in black, hard to miss
- + Visual hierarchy is clear: eye goes banner → hero image → copy → CTA in one clean sweep
- + No render bugs: layout looks clean, no broken images or overlapping text
- + Demographic signals match: a millennial mom with a young daughter is exactly who I am
- + Current campaign/season: Mother's Day is days away (it's May 5th), this is perfectly timed
- + Offer feels honest: "Applied at Cart" is transparent — no tricky code to fish for
- − No loyalty/rewards callout visible above the fold
- − Returning-shopper hook: "Recently Viewed" is barely peeking in at the very bottom edge — barely counts
- Score: 1 (base) + 7 confirmed = **8/10**

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF banner (top of page):** "Applied at Cart" means I don't even have to remember a code. As someone who buys shoes for three people (including myself, last), buying two pairs and saving 50% on the second is a real hook.
- **Mother's Day hero image:** A mom kissing her daughter who looks exactly my daughter's age, in matching pink sneakers — I actually paused on this. That's not an accident.
- **Dual-offer moment:** BOGO in the top banner AND "25% OFF Apparel" in the secondary strip tells me there are multiple deals in play. I feel like I'm landing on a value page, not just a brand page.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA clarity:** Big, black, full-width button. No ambiguity about what to do next.
- **Seasonal relevance:** Mother's Day framing in early May is spot-on. It doesn't feel like they forgot to swap out the banner from last month.

## What's weak

- **No kids category shortcut:** I'm here to buy shoes for my 5-year-old and 9-year-old. The only navigation visible is "MENU" — I have to go hunt. The hero serves the mom-gifting story, but nothing directly says "Kids" above the fold.
- **"The Mother's Day Shop" CTA is vague:** "SHOP NOW" doesn't tell me if I'm going to kids shoes, women's shoes, or a gift guide. I don't know what I'm tapping into.
- **No trust signals:** Zero reviews, no free-returns badge, no "X million happy customers" — nothing that gives me confidence before I click through.
- **Spring Apparel Sale secondary banner:** I'm here for shoes, not apparel. This banner competes for attention and dilutes the hero moment — and 25% off apparel from a shoe brand doesn't move me.
- **No loyalty/rewards visibility:** I'm exactly the type of shopper who signs up for rewards programs (I do the math, I accumulate points). Not mentioning it above the fold is a missed grab.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Shop Kids" shortcut below the hero:** One tap to Kids Boys and one to Kids Girls, right below the hero CTA — don't make me dig through MENU while my kids are fighting over the iPad.
- **Make the CTA more specific:** Change "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Mother's Day Styles" or even "Shop Matching Styles" — something that tells me what collection I'm about to land in.
- **Drop a loyalty nudge into the hero area:** A single line like "Skechers Plus members earn double points this weekend" is enough to stop me from buying as a guest.
- **Swap the apparel strip for a free-shipping threshold:** "Free shipping on orders over $50" would matter way more to me than 25% off apparel. I want to know what it costs to ship before I waste time building a cart.
