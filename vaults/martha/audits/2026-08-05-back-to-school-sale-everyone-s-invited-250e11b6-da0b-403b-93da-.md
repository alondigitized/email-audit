# Back to School Sale, Everyone's Invited
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Okay, the actual email body here is honestly one of the better back-to-school layouts Skechers has sent me — girls' section, boys' section, 25% off kids styles right up top, up to 40% off everything else. That's exactly the math I'm doing right now buying two pairs of shoes before school starts. My gut reaction the second it rendered was "finally, an easy scroll."
- But I almost never got to see any of that, because the preview text in my inbox was — again — a wall of raw JSON code. This is the third or fourth time this has happened from this exact sender (I flagged it hard on 7/22, 7/23, and again on 7/24), and at this point it stopped being a fluke and started being "does anyone at Skechers actually check what shows up on a phone screen." I open my inbox, see `{"@context": "http://schema.org/"...` next to the Skechers name, and my thumb is already halfway to swipe-delete before I remember this brand usually delivers.
- Bottom line: I'd open it because I know Skechers by now and I need kids' shoes this week, but I wouldn't forward it to a friend — not with garbage code sitting in the preview line making it look like a phishing email. Once inside, though, I'd click "Shop Boys" for my son's lace-ups without much hesitation.

## What's working

- The Girls/Boys split right under the hero is exactly how I shop — one for my daughter, one for my son, no digging through a general "kids" catalog.
- 25% off kids styles as the very first number I see is the right offer for a back-to-school email aimed at a mom like me.
- The boys' navy lace-up sneaker looks durable and school-appropriate — that's genuinely what I'd put my 9-year-old in.
- "Shop New Markdowns" with actual prices and strikethrough discounts ($100 → $74.99, $80 → $49.99) lets me price-compare without clicking through, which I always appreciate.
- Back-to-school icon row (globe, book, palette, flask) is a cute, on-theme touch that doesn't feel forced.

## What's weak

- The preview text is broken JSON schema code — again. Third time in two weeks from this sender, and it makes the email look hacked or spammy before I even open it.
- The girls' shoe is a pastel pink/blue running sneaker, not the velcro light-up sparkle style my actual 5-year-old wants — cute shoe, wrong kid stage.
- No loyalty/rewards mention anywhere, even though I know Skechers has a rewards program — feels like a missed chance to remind me I have points sitting there.
- No visible deadline or countdown for the sale in the main body — the date range is buried in the tiny legal footer, not up top where it'd actually push me to act today.
- Zero sizing or fit info on any hero product — for kids' shoes especially, I want to know if it runs true to size before I even click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the preview text — this is the single biggest lever. Every send with broken JSON in the preview is costing opens regardless of how good the body is. This needs to get escalated internally, not just noted again.
- 2. Swap the girls' hero shoe for a velcro or light-up style — my daughter is 5, not 10; the pastel running shoe reads more tween than kindergartner.
- 3. Put a real deadline banner near the top ("Ends 8/13") instead of leaving it in the footer fine print — urgency drives me to act same-day instead of "I'll come back to this."
- 4. Add a small rewards-member callout ("Members save an extra X%") since I know this program exists and it's currently invisible in this email.
- 5. Add "true to size" or a sizing note under the girls/boys hero shoes — sizing uncertainty is my #1 hesitation buying kids' shoes online.
