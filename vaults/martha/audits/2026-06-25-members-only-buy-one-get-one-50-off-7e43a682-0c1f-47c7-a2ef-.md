# Members Only: Buy One, Get One 50% Off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I'm at the kitchen table, kids are finally settled with a show, and I open this one because "Members Only" BOGO actually made me stop mid-scroll — that's real money when I need to buy shoes for two growing kids. The email itself is clean and loads fast on my phone, which I appreciate. The big hero graphic jumps right to the offer: BOGO 50% off (or 20% off one pair), and my eyes land there immediately. I see women's, men's, and kids' sections below, which matters because I'm always shopping for multiple people.
- But here's my honest reaction: the hero is a young woman in a sundress at what looks like a resort pool, and she's very clearly not me. I'm the one hauling sneakers in from a Target run, not posing at a luxury pool. That visual doesn't make me feel seen — it makes me feel like an afterthought. The kids section is genuinely there, but it's buried below two adult sections and the shoes shown look more athletic/adult-scale than the velcro light-up stuff I'm actually hunting for my daughter.
- I'd click the SHOP KIDS button because that BOGO deal is genuinely useful for me — buy one for my son, get half off for my daughter. I would not forward this to a friend because there's nothing shareable here, it's just a solid Skechers member promo. Mark it as a click-and-done.

## What's working

- **BOGO framing is immediately scannable** — "BUY ONE, GET ONE 50% OFF" in big bold type told me exactly what I'd save before I even fully registered the brand name.
- **Member exclusivity lands well** — "SKECHERS Plus MEMBERS" right under the logo made me feel like I earned this deal, not like I'm getting a blast to the whole list.
- **Three separate shop buttons** (Women's, Men's, Kids') mean I don't have to dig through a generic page — SHOP KIDS is right there.
- **Summer aesthetic is on-point for June** — the poolside/resort vibe works seasonally.
- **No render issues** — everything loaded clean, images are sharp, layout held together on mobile.

## What's weak

- **That preview text is broken again.** Seriously, Skechers has sent me this same JSON schema gibberish in the preview on multiple emails this month — June 12, June 11, June 7, June 6 all had it. At this point it's a pattern, not a glitch, and it makes me trust them a little less every time I see it.
- **The hero model reads 22, not 34.** Young woman solo at a pool — not a single signal that speaks to a mom with two kids in tow. I don't need to see someone exactly like me, but I'd appreciate someone I could actually picture myself as.
- **Kids section shows shoes that look adult-scale** — I don't see the velcro closures, light-up soles, or bold kids colors that would make me stop and think "oh that's perfect for my daughter."
- **No urgency with teeth** — "SHOP TODAY" is vague. When does BOGO end? Tomorrow? Next week? I'm much more likely to click now if I know this expires Sunday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is the single most damaging repeat issue.** Five of my last six Skechers emails have opened in my inbox with JSON schema as the first thing I read. Audit the template, add a proper preheader tag, QA before every send. This is costing them trust.
- 2. **Add a hard expiration date to the offer.** "BOGO 50% off — through Sunday" would push me to act right now instead of tabbing away.
- 3. **Surface one kids shoe in the hero or directly below it.** Even a small call-out like "Stock up for school — BOGO works on Kids' too" would make this email feel built for my household.
- 4. **Reorder the shop sections to Women's → Kids' → Men's**, or at least put Kids second. Moms like me are often the primary buyers for multiple family members and scan for kids first.
- 5. **Show the kids shoe details that matter** — velcro, machine washable, wide width — in the Kids section. One line of copy per shoe would go a long way.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers PLUS Members BOGO Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-layout structure with responsive CSS and Salesforce Marketing Cloud tracking infrastructure. The primary technical issues are pervasive use of HTTP image sources (11 assets) that will be blocked by modern email clients, a malformed Return Path pixel URL, and two missing `http-equiv` attributes on `<meta>` tags.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path pixel — missing protocol prefix (broken)**
```
src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499"
```
No `http://` or `https://` prefix. Email clients will resolve this as a relative URL and the pixel will silently fail, corrupting deliverability reputation data.

**ink1000 open pixel — HTTP**
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif"
```
Plain HTTP. Gmail and Outlook.com proxy image requests over HTTPS; a non-HTTPS origin will be blocked or proxied with a broken URL on some clients.

**28 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe**
All CTAs pass through `click.emails.skechers.com` — destination URLs and UTM parameter presence cannot be verified from source alone (see §6).

**QA summary vs. detail discrepancy**
The automated summary reports "100% pass rate, 0 warnings" while the detail log lists 14 `[WARN]` entries. The summary figure is unreliable and should not be cited in reporting.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags missing `http-equiv` attribute**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />       <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                         <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Both tags are non-functional as written. The charset declaration in particular affects character encoding fallback in older Outlook versions.

**11 images served over HTTP — will be blocked**

All `image.emails.skechers.com` asset URLs use `http://`. Affected images include the Skechers logo, App Store badge, Google Play badge, Curbside Pickup icon, Afterpay badge, Find A Store icon, and all social icons (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube). Gmail proxies these to HTTPS by rewriting URLs; Outlook desktop does not and will show broken images for recipients with "Download pictures" blocked.

**Two images missing `alt` text**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — minor; tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""`
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt text; renders as broken image placeholder if images are blocked

**Duplicate CSS media query blocks**
The `@media only screen and (max-width: 375px)` and `@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)` blocks are byte-for-byte identical. No functional breakage, but the 375px block is fully subsumed and adds ~1 KB of dead CSS.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
Applied via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. This suppresses iOS Mail's automatic font scaling for all elements, which can reduce readability on small screens without explicit font-size overrides for all text nodes.

### 5. Compliance

**Cannot fully verify from truncated source.** The provided HTML ends mid-table before the footer. CAN-SPAM requires: (1) physical mailing address, (2) functional unsubscribe mechanism, (3) non-deceptive subject/sender identity. Items 1 and 2 are typically in the footer — not visible here. The sender identity (`no-reply@emails.skechers.com`) and subject line are compliant on their face.

**Krux DMP pixels present**
```
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=adf27fee...
beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_subid&partner_uid=122277199
```
Two Krux user-match pixels transmit a hashed subscriber ID and a numeric subscriber ID to a third-party DMP. Depending on jurisdiction (CCPA, GDPR), cross-device matching of email-derived identifiers may require explicit consent disclosure. This is an existing architectural choice, not an acute bug, but worth flagging for privacy counsel review.
