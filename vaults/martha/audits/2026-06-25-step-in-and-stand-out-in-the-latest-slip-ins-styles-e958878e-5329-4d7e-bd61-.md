# Step in and Stand out in the latest Slip-Ins Styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I'm sitting on the couch during the brief window after dinner when both kids are doing their thing, and this one lands in my inbox. My first thought is the preview text — same JSON garbage that's been showing up from Skechers for months now. At this point it's almost a running joke. I opened it anyway because I'm a subscriber and BOGO always gets my attention, but that preview text would absolutely kill the open rate for any casual Skechers fan who isn't already loyal.
- Once I'm inside, the email is clean and renders fine. The BOGO banner is right at the top, which I appreciate. But here's the thing — this is the exact same BOGO 50% off offer I saw from them on June 14th. That was 11 days ago. And this whole email is essentially a Slip-Ins catalog for adults. There's a male model in the hero, a woman in workout gear in the second panel, no kids in sight until I get all the way down to the "KIDS" text link in the tiny category nav. I shop for my kids first and myself last, so an email that leads with a dude standing by a car and then never shows me a single kids' shoe? I'm marking it read and moving on.
- I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward this one. Nothing here that a friend couldn't find herself by going to the website.

## What's working

- **BOGO banner is the first thing my eye hits** — that's the right call. Discount-first hierarchy is exactly what I want when I open a promo email.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — after months of busted preview text, at least the body itself displays correctly top to bottom.
- **"FRESH & EASY" copy is readable and clear** — not brilliant, but it communicates the Slip-Ins ease-of-wear concept in two words without making me work for it.
- **Category nav at the bottom (WOMEN / MEN / KIDS)** — at least KIDS is there as an escape hatch if I scroll that far.
- **Afterpay and Klarna visible** — as a price-sensitive mom, seeing payment flexibility is a small but real trust signal.

## What's weak

- **Preview text is JSON schema garbage, again** — this is the fourth or fifth time I've seen this from Skechers. At this point it's a systemic failure they just haven't fixed, and it tanks opens.
- **The hero is a man** — I'm a 34-year-old mom. The first face I see is a guy standing by a car in jeans. It's not offensive, it just has nothing to do with me or why I open Skechers emails.
- **Zero kids content in the actual email body** — the whole email is Slip-Ins for adults. My daughter needs shoes. My son is about to start a new school year. There's not a single kids product visible until that tiny nav link.
- **This BOGO ran 11 days ago** — I got "Members Only: Buy One, Get One 50% Off" on June 14th. This is the same deal, without the "Members Only" framing that at least made it feel exclusive. At this frequency it stops feeling like an offer and starts feeling like wallpaper.
- **No urgency, no deadline** — the BOGO floats there with no expiration. "SHOP NOW" doesn't mean anything if I don't know how long I have.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text immediately** — this is the highest-leverage single change. JSON bleeding into the preheader has appeared in at least four sends this month. One engineer, one hour, would lift open rates across every future email. It's a trust erosion issue more than a deliverability one.
- 2. **Put at least one kids product in the hero or second panel** — I need to see a light-up sneaker, a velcro sandal, something for a 5-year-old or a 9-year-old. Even a "Shop Kids Slip-Ins" CTA next to the adult panels would change my calculus entirely.
- 3. **Rotate the hero model to a woman** — if the segment is mixed, fine, but leading with a male model in a women's-dominant email category misses the majority of your openers.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the BOGO** — even "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" transforms a passive glance into a calendar moment.
- 5. **Differentiate this send from June 14th** — same BOGO this fast should come with a reason: "Back by popular demand," a specific new color drop, something that justifies opening the same promotional email twice in two weeks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids + Adults: BOGO 50% Off Slip-Ins — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Slip-Ins just dropped (and BOGO is back)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Easy on, easy off — the style your whole family will actually wear.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New colors for kids and adults. Buy one, get one 50% off.`

The email's primary image CDN (`image.emails.skechers.com`) serves all assets over plain HTTP, creating a pervasive mixed-content risk that will break images in modern email clients. ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**QA tool skipped all 26 click links** — they resolve through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects, so destination URL integrity (UTM params, landing page alignment) cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Three third-party beacon pixels are embedded in the hidden tracking row:**

| Pixel | Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| User-match (SHA-256) | `beacon.krxd.net` | Salesforce DMP identity sync using hashed subscriber email |
| User-match (raw sub ID) | `beacon.krxd.net` | `partner_uid=122277199` — plaintext subscriber ID in URL |
| Ad impression | `beacon.krxd.net` | Fires campaign impression event on open |

The raw subscriber ID (`partner_uid=122277199`) is sent in plaintext to a third-party domain. This is standard SFMC/Krux retargeting practice but is a compliance surface under CCPA/GDPR.
