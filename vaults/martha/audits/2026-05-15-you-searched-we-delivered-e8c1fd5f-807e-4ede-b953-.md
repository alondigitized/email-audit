# You Searched, We Delivered
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject "You Searched, We Delivered" genuinely made me pause — it felt like it was actually about me, like Skechers saw what I was browsing and pulled something together. That's a concept I can get behind. Then I hit the preview text in my inbox and it was the exact same raw JSON bracket mess as yesterday's email. Skechers literally sent the same broken preview two days in a row. At that point I'm rolling my eyes before I even open it.
- Inside, the email is structured around a "Most Searched: Most Loved" hook, which is clever branding, and the BOGO 50% off is right at the top — I see it, I register it, good. But here's my problem: if this email is supposed to reflect what I searched for, where are the kids' shoes? I have a 5-year-old and a 9-year-old and I spend probably 70% of my Skechers browsing time in the kids' section. Slip-Ins for women, Go Walk, Arch Fit — all adult, all either women's or men's. The "You Searched" premise collapses the second I scroll and realize none of my actual searches are reflected.
- The 25% off apparel tacked on at the bottom felt like padding. I wouldn't click through for a kids' pair today. I'd scroll the whole thing in about 8 seconds, feel vaguely disappointed, and move on. Wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off is front and center** — I clocked it before I finished loading the email. Clear, credible, no digging required.
- **"Most Searched: Most Loved" framing** — the search-bar icon and the hook create a smart expectation of personalization. Concept is solid even if execution fell short.
- **Female athletic model up top** — demographic-appropriate, she's age-range-adjacent, it didn't feel targeted at a 22-year-old influencer or a 60-year-old retiree.
- **Multi-category structure** — Slip-Ins, Go Walk, Arch Fit broken into scannable sections means I can find what I'm looking for without having to hunt.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids' content** — the subject line literally implies this reflects my searches, but I shop kids constantly and there's not a single Twinkle Toes, S-Lights, or Flex section. The personalization premise is a lie.
- **JSON schema preview text is back, again** — this is the second consecutive day with the same broken preview. I noticed it yesterday on "Your Smoothest Stride Yet" and now it's happening again. At some point that erodes trust in the brand's competence.
- **No loyalty or rewards mention** — I'm a member. I earn points. Not one badge, not one "X points on this purchase." The BOGO would hit harder if it tied to my account.
- **Cadence is getting aggressive** — this is the sixth or seventh email in eight days. That's a lot, and I'm starting to tune out before I even open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Actually personalize the kids' block** — if the subject promises "You Searched, We Delivered," surface the category the user actually browses most. For a profile like mine, that's kids' shoes. One module — "For Your Little One" — with a BOGO on kids' styles would likely convert me immediately.
- 2. **Fix the preview text plumbing, for real** — this is now two days in a row with raw JSON in the inbox. This needs a technical fix before the next send, full stop. It's not a copy tweak; it's eroding the brand's professional credibility.
- 3. **Tie the BOGO to the loyalty program** — "BOGO 50% Off + earn double points" would stop my thumb cold. I'm a rewards-program person; speak that language.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the BOGO** — "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" gives me a reason to act now instead of closing the tab and forgetting.
- 5. **Pull back cadence or segment harder** — if you're going to send daily, at least vary the hook so it doesn't feel like spam. Yesterday was slip-ins, today is also slip-ins. Mix it up.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off — women's, men's & kids' styles you've been eyeing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The styles you searched for, now with BOGO 50% off. Ends Sunday.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — "You Searched, We Delivered" (Skechers, 2026-05-15)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken-experience defect (empty personalization token) and a pervasive HTTP image-serving pattern that will cause blocking in modern email clients. All other categories are clean.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

No issues found with link structure. All 27 click-redirect links route through `click.emails.skechers.com`, which is the expected SFMC tracking domain. QA correctly skipped HTTP probing these — they are redirect wrappers and do not represent broken destinations.

**Noted but not actionable:** `beacon.krxd.net` user-match pixels fire on open. These are third-party audience sync pixels (Krux/Salesforce DMP). No technical defect, but they contribute to fingerprint payload on open.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — 13 affected images**

Every image served from `image.emails.skechers.com` uses `http://`, not `https://`. Gmail (since 2013), Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. Affected images include the Skechers logo, App Store/Google Play badges, social icons, and at minimum one product/hero image:

```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-...png  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...png  (no alt)
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif  (open pixel, http://)
```

Gmail will rewrite these through its image proxy — images may render but the original server receives no open signal from Gmail users. Other clients may block outright, showing broken image placeholders.

**Missing alt text — 2 images**

- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — no `alt` attribute
- `o.gif` (ink1000 open-tracking pixel) — no `alt` attribute (low priority for a pixel, but triggers accessibility flags)

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**

Set globally via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. This disables iOS auto-sizing for all elements. Acceptable for fixed-layout email but can degrade readability if any text renders smaller than intended on older iPhones.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — Empty greeting slot**

QA confirmed: `"Hi ,"` — the subscriber first-name merge token resolved to an empty string. This is the only broken-experience defect in the send. The token is populated (or defaulted) at render time in SFMC; the fallback value for the `%%First Name%%` (or equivalent AMPscript field) was either blank in the subscriber record or the fallback string was not configured.

Evidence: QA finding `[FAIL] Empty greeting slot: Greeting has empty name field`.

No other unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

No issues found in the reviewed source. Standard SFMC infrastructure implies:

- `List-Unsubscribe` header present (SFMC injects this by default)
- Physical mailing address expected in footer (not visible in truncated source but standard for Skechers SFMC sends)
- SPF/DKIM alignment expected via `emails.skechers.com` sending domain

Cannot confirm DMARC pass from HTML alone — authentication headers would need to be pulled from raw message source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

Cannot fully verify UTM parameters from the truncated source (all destination URLs are wrapped in `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects). However, the campaign ID `MKG_US_NONPURCLICK_U_MULTI_NONPURCH_EN_05152026` is consistently present across tracking pixels, indicating consistent campaign attribution at the pixel level.

No issues found based on available data.
