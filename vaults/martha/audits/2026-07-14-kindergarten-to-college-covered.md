# Kindergarten to College, Covered
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Kindergarten to College" — the subject line basically wrote itself for me right now, because I *literally* have a kid starting kindergarten this fall and a kid going into fourth grade. That felt like Skechers was reading my mind, and I opened it. The email itself is clean, organized, and that 40% off banner at the very top made me exhale with relief. The KIDS' section with separate "SHOP GIRLS" and "SHOP BOYS" buttons is exactly the shortcut I need on a Saturday afternoon when I have maybe six minutes before someone starts yelling.
- But then I kept scrolling and there's a whole "HIGH SCHOOL MUST-HAVES" block and a "COLLEGE STAPLES" section — and I just... drifted. That's not my life right now. My son isn't picking out Air Maxes for prom, he's going into fourth grade and needs something that can survive recess. The email cast way too wide a net and it lost me in the middle. The 20% off block at the bottom for "full-priced styles" also confused me because the top says 40% off — which is it? I want one clear deal, not two different numbers requiring mental math.
- I'd click "SHOP GIRLS" or "SHOP BOYS" but I would not forward this to anyone. The subject line is a gem; the execution after the fold is too scattered to earn a share.

## What's working

- **"Kindergarten to College, Covered" hit me exactly right** — my daughter starts kindergarten in September, so this felt weirdly personal even though I know it's just a tagline.
- **40% OFF is front and center** in the very first row, above even the hero — no hunting for the deal.
- **SHOP GIRLS / SHOP BOYS split is genuinely useful** — I don't want to wade through unisex grids when I'm shopping for two specific kids with different styles and sizes.
- **Hero image includes young kids**, not just teenagers, so I could actually picture my own two in the lineup.
- **No visible render bugs in the email body itself** — layout is solid, images load, buttons are tappable-sized.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is the JSON schema dump. Again.** I've been looking at this broken garbage since early July — I've now lost count of how many sends in a row this has been wrong. At this point I expect it before I even open.
- **HIGH SCHOOL MUST-HAVES and COLLEGE STAPLES are wasted real estate for me** — two full sections I have to scroll past to find anything relevant. My kids are 5 and 9; the college crowd is not my aisle.
- **Two different discount numbers (40% off vs. 20% off) with no explanation** — I shouldn't have to figure out why the bottom says 20% when the top said 40%. Just tell me the deal clearly.
- **No fit/feature callouts in the KIDS' section** — I always look for velcro, light-up soles, washable materials. A tiny badge or bullet would've made me click faster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text.** This is now the eighth or ninth consecutive send with the JSON schema leak bleeding into the preheader. It is the first thing I see in my inbox before I open. One Jira ticket would fix it.
- 2. **Swap the section order for the kids/family segment** — lead with KIDS', then your own category (Women's for me), then high school/college as a tertiary block. The current order buries the most relevant content.
- 3. **Collapse to one offer** — if 40% off is the headline, retire the 20% off block, or explain the difference ("40% off sale styles / 20% off full-price styles"). The dual numbers create doubt.
- 4. **Add one or two feature callouts inside the KIDS' section** — "velcro closures," "machine washable," "light-up soles" — these are the specifics that make moms click *immediately*.
- 5. **Put a deadline on the 40% off** — even "this week only" or "through July 20" would've added the urgency the subject lacks.

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Kindergarten to College, Covered" (07-14-2026)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with no broken links or missing unsubscribe infrastructure detected, but carries a systemic HTTP (non-HTTPS) image-loading problem across its entire CDN-hosted asset library and one third-party pixel that will cause image blocking in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links** all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — confirmed as Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracking domain. Links were not probed, so destination availability is unverified. This is expected ESP behavior, not a defect, but means broken destination URLs would not surface in automated QA.

**Open pixel (Salesforce SFMC):**
`https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?E74R73Q6UDVULN6UK5HRFWXVPY.100223&d=100223&bmt=0` — HTTPS, no issue.

**Krux/Salesforce DMP pixels (2):**
`beacon.krxd.net` pixels are present for user-match and ad impression; these fire on open and pass `partner_uid=122277199` (raw subscriber ID) and `campaignid=MKG_US_NA_U_SEASONAL_FAMILY_EN_07142026_B`. Confirm these pixels are disclosed in the privacy policy's "interest-based advertising" section.

**ink1000.com pixel — HTTP + missing alt:**
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NA_U_SEASONAL_FAMILY_EN_07142026_B"
```
Plain HTTP. This is a Movable Ink dynamic content pixel. It will be **blocked by default** in Outlook (2013+) and many corporate mail proxies that strip mixed-content HTTP resources. If this pixel drives personalized content rendering, blocked requests result in fallback/blank content, not just a missing tracker.

**Return Path inbox monitoring pixel:**
```
src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1..."
```
URL uses neither `http://` nor `https://` — it is a protocol-relative URL (`//` is absent, making this a **relative path**). This will 404 universally as it resolves against the email's base context, not the intended hostname. Flag to the deliverability team.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP images — systemic (13 assets):**
All product/UI images served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com` will be blocked by Gmail (since 2013 — all images proxied via HTTPS) and Outlook in high-security environments. Affected assets include:
- Skechers logo: `dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`
- Hero/product image: `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png`
- App Store badge, Google Play badge
- Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store icons
- Instagram, Facebook, YouTube social icons

Gmail will re-proxy these to HTTPS so Gmail users are unaffected. Apple Mail, Outlook.com, and corporate Exchange setups will vary — images may appear broken or be actively stripped. The CDN at `image.emails.skechers.com` almost certainly supports HTTPS; this is a template configuration issue.

**Missing alt text (2 images):**
- `o.gif` (Movable Ink pixel) — alt="" is acceptable for tracking pixels, but it is absent entirely rather than explicitly empty.
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — a content image with no alt text. Screen readers will skip it silently; if it contains offer text (e.g. "20% off"), that information is inaccessible in image-off or assistive-tech contexts.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is set globally. This suppresses iOS Mail's auto-zoom on small text and is intentional for email, but confirm minimum body font size is ≥14px — if any text is below that threshold, this rule will prevent users from zooming to read it.

**Duplicate responsive breakpoint blocks:** `@media only screen and (max-width: 375px)` and `@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)` are declared separately but contain **identical rule sets**. The 375px block is entirely redundant — the 640px block subsumes it. Not a rendering defect in practice, but increases payload and maintenance surface.
