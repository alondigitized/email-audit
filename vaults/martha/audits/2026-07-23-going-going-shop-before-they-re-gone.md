# Going, going... shop before they're gone
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay, that preview text nearly made me swipe this into trash before I opened it. JSON code dumping into my inbox preview — I thought the email was corrupted. It looked like one of those phishing attempts my mom warns me about. When I actually opened it, the body was clean and organized, and that 25% off Skechers Plus banner was right there, which I appreciate because I'm a member and that's the first thing I look for.
- But here's my problem: I scrolled this entire email looking for my kids and found nothing. A KIDS link buried in the footer text navigation is not the same as showing me a velcro sneaker or a durable lace-up. Back-to-school is right around the corner. My nine-year-old has already wrecked two pairs this summer. Skechers knows I shop for kids — I have bought kids shoes from them before. Giving me three women's casual styles and calling it urgent doesn't land when I'm on a mission.
- Also, I got practically the same 25% off $100 offer yesterday. Fool me once. This feels like a resend with a snappier subject line, which is not urgency — that's cry-wolf behavior. I'd mark this read and move on.

## What's working

- The 25% off Skechers Plus members banner is bold and first in the visual stack — I saw it before anything else, and that's exactly where it should be.
- "SELLING OUT" as hero copy combined with "These styles are hot! Snag your size before they're gone" is a clean urgency one-two punch.
- The email body itself renders beautifully — three distinct shoe families, their own sections, individual CTAs per style. No clutter.
- The FLEXPADRILLE LO in blush is genuinely pretty and I noticed it — if I were shopping for myself today, that's my click.
- Skechers Plus callout signals that they see me as a member, not a stranger. That micro-recognition matters.

## What's weak

- The preview text is leaking JSON-LD structured markup — from my inbox this email looked broken before I even opened it, and that nearly killed the open.
- Not a single featured style is for kids. I'm a mom in late July — back-to-school is my entire shopping brain right now, and this email has no KIDS hero, no kids CTA, no velcro, no durable outsoles.
- I literally got the same 25% off $100+ Skechers Plus offer yesterday (July 22). Running it two days in a row with virtually identical terms trains me to ignore it, not act on it.
- "Going, going" is an urgency hook that needs a specific deadline to land — there's no end date anywhere, which makes the scarcity feel fake.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader right now.** The JSON bleed is the single most damaging technical error in this send — it tanks the open before anyone reads the subject. Zero-dollar fix, just needs someone to write an actual preheader string.
- 2. **Lead with or add a kids/back-to-school block.** One featured kids style — a velcro toddler sneaker or a school-durable boy's runner — with a dedicated "Shop Kids" CTA would have made this email relevant to my primary shopping mission.
- 3. **Put an actual deadline on the offer.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" turns "going, going" into real urgency. Without it, it reads like every other Skechers promo.
- 4. **Vary the offer day-over-day.** Yesterday was 25% off $100+. Today is 25% off $100+. Rotate the threshold, the discount, or the product focus — anything to avoid the "I can wait for this deal because it was here yesterday" reflex.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your kids' sizes are going fast — 25% off today, Plus members`
- **Subject Alt B:** `It's almost gone. Your size. Your 25% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `25% off $100+ with code SAVINGS — women's AND kids' styles going fast`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Snag the back-to-school styles (and yours) before your size disappears`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard SFMC/Salesforce Marketing Cloud stack with Krux/Salesforce DMP audience matching pixels. The primary infrastructure issues are pervasive HTTP (non-HTTPS) image assets and a single unprotected tracking pixel served over plain HTTP from a third-party domain.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links** all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard ESP wrapping, not auditable without live probing.

**Open pixel** (`open.aspx`) served via `click.emails.skechers.com` over HTTPS — no issue.

**Krux DMP pixels** (3 total via `beacon.krxd.net`):
- User-match pixel leaks hashed PII in the query string: `partner_uid=adf27fee67323d52536b18110294bf793c265b830082caba4b1a32664832189a` — this is a SHA-256 of subscriber data passed to a third-party data broker. Worth confirming this is covered by the privacy policy and consent model.
- Raw subscriber ID also exposed: `partner_uid=122277199`

**ink1000.com pixel** served over **plain HTTP**:
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NONPUR180_U_BEST_NONPURCH_EN_07232026
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block HTTP images. This pixel will fail to fire in most modern clients, making its measurement data unreliable.

**Return Path seed pixel** is missing the protocol:
```
pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499
```
The `src` attribute has no `http://` or `https://` prefix — this is a broken URL. The pixel will not load in any client.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**13 image assets served over HTTP** from `image.emails.skechers.com`. Gmail image proxy will rewrite these to HTTPS, but Outlook desktop (2016–2021) and some corporate environments block mixed-content images outright, causing broken image display for logo, hero images, app store badges, social icons, and utility icons (AfterPay, Curbside Pickup, Find A Store).

**2 images missing `alt` text**:
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracking pixel) — minor, but should be `alt=""`
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — content image with no alt; context unknown from truncated source, but if it carries product or offer information this is an accessibility gap

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is set globally via `* {}` selector. This prevents user font-size overrides in WebKit/Blink clients and can fail WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) for subscribers with visual accessibility needs.

**Duplicate media query breakpoints**: `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px` blocks are defined identically, then a third block at `max-width: 480px` in a second `<style>` tag partially overlaps them. Redundant but not breaking.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — suppresses pinch-zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern for low-vision users on iOS.
