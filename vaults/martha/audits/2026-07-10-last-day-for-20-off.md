# Last day for 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Sally, again. By my rough mental count this is somewhere around email twelve or thirteen from them since June 1st, and I had two in my inbox already this week — "Your summer must-haves" yesterday and "Save on summer hair care essentials" the day before. So when "Last day for 20% off" popped up this morning I had exactly one second of goodwill before the fatigue kicked back in.
- That said — the email itself, in isolation, is clean. "LAST DAY / 20% off / (almost) everything" hits instantly. The Sally Rewards multiplier block below the fold is smart because it actually pushes me to add one more thing to hit 3x or 4x earn. And I genuinely appreciate seeing "Excludes hair color & clearance" called out in the body before I've built a cart around things that won't qualify.
- I'd probably click. I've been putting off restocking on a styling product and a hard deadline helps me off the fence. But I wouldn't forward it to a friend — they've gotten the same Sally 20%-off email at least twice in the past two weeks and we've already joked about unsubscribing together.

## What's working

- **Instant hero read.** "LAST DAY" in big bold text above "20% off (almost) everything" — I read that in under two seconds flat, no decoding required.
- **Exclusions right in the body.** "Excludes hair color & clearance" is in the email, not buried in a tiny footer footnote. That earns trust.
- **Sally Rewards earn tiers are actually motivating.** 2x on $30+, 3x on $40+, 4x on $50+ — showing the ladder makes me do math in my head. That's working as intended.
- **Klarna is a quiet win.** Interest-free payments mentioned below the fold for $35+ orders. Useful for a budget-conscious household.
- **Rendered clean.** No broken images, no layout weirdness. That matters when I'm skimming on my phone between school drop-off and work.

## What's weak

- **Urgency has been cried-wolf'd into nothing.** June 26th was "Going... Going... Gone!" with 20% off. July 5th was "Last Call." Now July 10th is "Last day." Every week is the last day at Sally. I no longer believe the deadline is real.
- **Zero featured products.** The hero is 100% promotional scaffolding — a pink background, a number, a button. One hero product image (a specific styling tool, a nail kit, anything) would give me a visual reason to click beyond the discount alone.
- **"Shop now" is the laziest possible CTA.** It tells me nothing. Shop what? Where do I land? A category CTA ("Shop Hair Care" or "Shop Nails") would feel like Sally knows what I'm there for.
- **Preheader is a wasted inbox slot.** Leading with "Sally Beauty" before the actual copy is redundant — your sender name is right there. Then the rest is invisible spacer characters padding out the line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one real product in the hero.** Even a single product image with a name and original/sale price breaks the "generic sale" visual pattern that's made the last dozen emails feel identical.
- 2. **Make the CTA category-specific.** "Shop Styling Tools" or "Shop Nail Sale" beats "Shop now" for someone who comes in with intent and a short attention window.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** Drop the brand-name prefix and replace those invisible spacers with a tight secondary hook that earns real inbox real estate.
- 4. **Pull back on cadence.** This isn't one email's fault, but it's the highest-leverage fix. I'm a loyal Sally customer and I'm genuinely close to unsubscribing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 20% off your cart`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% off expires tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hair care, nails & styling tools — grab it before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends tonight. Stock up on your Sally staples.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`http-equiv` missing on two meta tags**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Both are missing the `http-equiv` attribute. Correct forms:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, these hints are ignored by parsers.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` is non-standard on images**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` has no defined effect on replaced elements like `<img>` and may cause unexpected layout behavior in WebKit-based renderers. Should be removed.

**'Founders Grotesk' has no `@font-face` declaration**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
Six `@font-face` blocks are declared for Open Sans, but none for Founders Grotesk. All anchor text will silently fall back to Helvetica. If this is an intentional web-safe fallback, it should be documented; if not, the `@font-face` block is missing.

**Open Sans loaded from Google Fonts CDN (v17 — outdated)**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
`v17` is a stale font version (current is v40+). More critically, Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo block external `@font-face` requests, so this font will not render for the majority of recipients regardless of version. The fallback stack should be verified as acceptable.

**Unsupported CSS declarations**
- `box-shadow` (`.boxShadow` class): not supported in Outlook or Gmail.
- `.filter-item:hover`: `:hover` pseudo-class is not supported in Gmail, Outlook, or Apple Mail on iOS. The hover state will never fire for most recipients.

Neither is a deliverability risk, but both are dead code in the majority of email clients.
