# From Arch Fit to Cozy Fit, Find Your Comfort Here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so Skechers has been on a roll lately with some genuinely good Back to School emails — the "Kindergarten to College" send on the 14th basically had my name on it — so I opened this one with some hope. What I got instead was a total pivot: a comfort-technology explainer email aimed squarely at adults. Cozy Fit, Arch Fit, Glide-Step, Slip-ins. Four sections, four adult CTAs, zero mention of my kids. The 40% off in the hero grabbed my eye, but the second I started scrolling and realized there wasn't a single kid's shoe, back-to-school mention, or even a throwaway "shop kids" banner, I basically checked out.
- The email itself isn't ugly — the layout is clean, the product photography reads well even on a small screen, and that SUMMER code at the bottom is clear. But I came in primed for more BTS content after a week of great sends, and instead I got a cheat sheet on adult comfort technology. It's like Skechers forgot who they've been talking to all week.
- I'd mark it as read and move on. Wouldn't click, definitely wouldn't forward it. If I want to shop for myself I'll go find that on my own — right now my mental bandwidth is on getting my kids set for school, and this email gave me nothing to work with there.

## What's working

- **40% OFF in the hero is immediate and legible** — I clocked the offer before anything else, which is exactly how it should work on mobile.
- **Four-section layout is scannable** — each comfort category gets its own photo, a short descriptor, and a dedicated CTA button. No confusion about what each one is.
- **SUMMER code with 20% off** is clearly visible at the bottom — easy to screenshot and use later if I do end up browsing for myself.
- **Hero couple looks like people my age** — not teenagers, not grandparents. That part of the demographic feel was right.
- **No layout breakage or broken images** — the email rendered cleanly on the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids content** — after five straight Back to School emails, completely dropping any mention of kids' shoes here feels like a whiplash. My primary reason to open a Skechers email right now is my daughter's kindergarten shoes and my son's sneakers.
- **That preview text is broken. Again.** A JSON schema dump showed up in my inbox as the preview — same exact issue that tripped up the July 10th send. This is now a pattern, and it makes Skechers look sloppy before I even open it.
- **No urgency anywhere** — the 40% off has no deadline attached, the SUMMER code has no expiration. I have no reason to click today versus next weekend.
- **Every CTA goes to a browse category, not a product** — "SHOP COZY FIT" is a browse page, not a specific shoe. When I'm on my phone with five minutes between school pickups, I want to land close to checkout, not start a discovery journey.
- **No loyalty callout** — after the July 12th email specifically called out Plus members with that SUMMER code block, this send feels generic. I'm a member and I got nothing that acknowledged that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is now urgent.** Two broken sends in six days means there's a systemic issue in how they're injecting structured data. Until it's fixed, every email is hobbled before it's even opened.
- 2. **Bring kids back into the email.** Even a single banner — "BTS picks for littles → SHOP KIDS" — would keep this relevant for me during peak shopping season. Adult comfort tech as the sole focus is the wrong call in mid-July.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the 40% off.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" would give me a real reason to click today. Right now it reads like a permanent feature.
- 4. **Swap at least one category section for a specific product with a price shown.** Show me the Arch Fit walking shoe at $65 (was $95), not just the category name. That's what makes me tap.
- 5. **Surface Plus member pricing explicitly.** Even one line — "Plus members save an extra 10%" — re-earns the loyalty I've already given them.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one broken tracking pixel (missing URL scheme), a systemic HTTP image serving issue across 12+ assets, and an accessibility-hostile viewport configuration. No unrendered merge tokens detected in the visible HTML.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path / Validity pixel — missing URL scheme (broken)**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" ...>
```
No `https://` prefix. Email clients interpret this as a relative path — the pixel fires to nowhere. Inbox placement monitoring via Validity is silently broken for this send.

**ink1000.com open pixel — HTTP**
```
src="http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif"
```
Plain HTTP. Gmail and Outlook.com proxy images over HTTPS; this pixel may report inflated/deflated opens depending on whether the proxy follows the redirect. Also flagged with missing `alt=""`.

**26 click-redirect links skipped**
All CTAs route through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard SFMC redirect. Cannot verify destination UTM params from source alone (see §6).

**Kruxd DMP pixels — subscriber ID exposure**
```
partner_uid=122277199   (raw subscriber ID)
partner_uid=adf27fee... (SHA-256 hash, likely of email address)
```
Both are sent to `beacon.krxd.net` (Salesforce DMP). The SHA-256 hash of a known email is reversible via rainbow tables — this may require disclosure depending on applicable privacy policy and CCPA data-sharing clauses.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image CDN — systemic (12 images affected)**

All content images are served from `http://image.emails.skechers.com`. Affected assets include the Skechers logo, social icons (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube), app store badges, and service icons (Curbside Pickup, AfterPay, Find A Store). Gmail and Outlook.com proxy these through their own HTTPS cache, masking the issue in most inboxes — but strict corporate proxies and some mobile clients will block or show broken images.

**Missing `http-equiv` on charset meta**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Should be:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
```
Without `http-equiv`, the charset declaration is non-functional in XHTML Transitional DOCTYPE; character encoding falls back to client default.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text).

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
Applied globally via `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`. Overrides system font scaling preferences — accessibility concern for users who rely on OS-level text size settings.

**Missing alt text on content image**
`49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` has no alt attribute. If this is a product or promotional image (not a decorative spacer), screen readers will skip it entirely.

**Duplicate CSS breakpoint blocks**
The `375px` and `640px` media query rules are declared twice (once each in the `<style>` block, then repeated). A separate `<style>` tag adds a third `480px` block with overlapping `.show` and `.mobile-hidden` rules. No functional breakage observed, but rule precedence is ambiguous and file weight is inflated.
