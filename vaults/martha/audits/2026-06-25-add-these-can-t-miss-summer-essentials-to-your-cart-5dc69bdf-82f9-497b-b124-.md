# Add These Can't-Miss Summer Essentials to Your Cart
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I picked up my phone while my son was doing homework and my daughter was (barely) occupied with LEGO, and this landed in my inbox from Skechers. My first reaction to the subject line is kind of a shrug — "Can't-Miss Summer Essentials" sounds like something every retailer sends in June, and the preview text was a wall of bracket gibberish again. Skechers did this in early June too and it made me feel like nobody's minding the store.
- Once I actually opened it, the layout is clean and the BOGO 50% off banner up top does its job — I see that instantly and my brain goes "okay, maybe." But then I scroll and it's jellies, canvas sneakers, sandals — all adult styles. There's a KIDS link buried way down in the footer, but not a single sneaker for my 9-year-old or light-up shoe for my 5-year-old in the actual email body. As someone who opens Skechers emails primarily to shop for my kids, I feel invisible in this email.
- I'd mark this as read and maybe tap through the KIDS footer link out of habit, but I wouldn't forward it to anyone. The BOGO would pull me back if I were shopping for myself, but this email didn't give me a reason to do anything urgently.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% off banner is front and center** — it's the first thing my eyes land on and it's clear and legible without zooming
- **Clean layout with no visual clutter** — categories are easy to scan, images are crisp and loaded correctly
- **Summer timing is on-point** — jellies and sandals feel genuinely relevant for late June, not stale
- **CTAs are specific and actionable** — "SHOP JELLIES," "SHOP CANVAS," "SHOP SANDALS" all have a verb + noun, which I appreciate over vague "Discover" language
- **The women's model in the hero is roughly my age** — not a 22-year-old influencer, which makes it feel slightly more like "me"

## What's weak

- **Broken preview text — again.** This is at least the third time since early June that Skechers has sent me an email with JSON schema leaking into the preheader. I remembered it from the June 6 and June 7 sends, and it's still not fixed. That's a trust eroder.
- **Zero kids content in the email body** — KIDS is a footer link, but my main reason for opening Skechers emails is to find shoes for my 5-year-old and 9-year-old, and this email completely ignores that
- **No loyalty mention** — I'm in the Skechers rewards program and there's nothing here acknowledging that, no member pricing or points callout
- **No deadline on the BOGO** — "buy one, get one 50% off" is great, but without an end date I don't feel any urgency to act now vs. next week

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preview text — this is a P0 issue.** Three broken preheaders in three weeks is a deliverability credibility problem. The JSON schema markup should never surface in inbox preview. This single fix would lift open rates across every future send.
- 2. **Add a kids module** — even one row with two to three kids' shoes between the sandals section and the footer would make me feel like this email was partially sent to me as a mom, not just as a woman. A "Shoes for the whole family" section with the BOGO callout would get me tapping.
- 3. **Surface loyalty rewards** — a small "earn points on this purchase" or "members save an extra X%" line near the BOGO banner would remind me I have a reason to buy from Skechers specifically vs. going to DSW.
- 4. **Add a BOGO end date** — even "this weekend only" or "ends Sunday" creates the urgency that currently isn't there.
- 5. **Subject line** — the current one is generic. It could name a category or at least the offer:
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% Off: Summer Sandals + Sneakers Are Ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Summer Shoes (+ the Kids' Too) — BOGO Inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jellies, canvas sneakers, everyday sandals — and something for the kids.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy one, get one 50% off. Stock up for the whole family before summer ends.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (14 assets):** All `image.emails.skechers.com` assets and the Movable Ink pixel use `http://`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365 all enforce HTTPS for remote images — these will be blocked or display broken image icons in security-conscious clients.

Affected assets include logo, app store badges, social icons, and product imagery:
```
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-...  (logo)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/49468f73-...       (missing alt)
http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/11/0ec56c9f-...       (App Store badge)
... (9 additional assets)
```

**Missing alt text:** `o.gif` (Movable Ink pixel) and `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` have no `alt` attribute. The pixel omission is acceptable practice; the product/content image is not.

**Dual media-query redundancy:** `.hideme`/`.hidden`/`.mobile-hidden` breakpoints are declared identically at both `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px`. No functional bug, but dead CSS weight.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is applied globally via `* {}`. This disables user-controlled text scaling and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).
