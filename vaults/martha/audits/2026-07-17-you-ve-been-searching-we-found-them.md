# You've Been Searching 🔍 We Found Them
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so "You've Been Searching 🔍 We Found Them" actually got me — because I *have* been searching. Constantly. For kindergarten shoes with velcro and something durable enough for a nine-year-old who destroys footwear. I opened this with genuine hope that Skechers had done the homework and pulled together what I was actually browsing.
- What I got instead: the Arch Fit / Cozy Fit / BOBS adult lifestyle roundup. Which — look, some of these recent sends have been great. That "Kindergarten to College" email on the 14th felt like it had my name on it. This one feels like the opposite: a subject that promises personalization, an email that delivers a generic popularity contest of adult women's comfort styles. The 40% off banner is clear, the 20% off member code (SUMMER) is real, and the email is at least clean and tap-friendly. But "Kids Clothing" appears as a three-word text link buried in a navigation strip after I've scrolled past three adult shoe categories, an exclusives module, and a member pricing block. That's not "we found what you were searching for." That's a footnote.
- I'll copy the SUMMER code into my notes app and mark this as read. Not clicking today — nothing here is for my kids.

## What's working

- The 40% off banner lands at the very top — I clocked it immediately even on a small screen.
- "SO POPULAR" + "Meet the styles shoppers search for most" is clean social proof framing that actually earns the subject line a little.
- The SUMMER member code is surfaced in the body — I don't have to hunt for it, which I appreciate as a Plus member.
- Layout is clean, no broken images, text is readable without zooming, tap targets feel right on mobile.
- That subject line hook genuinely worked on me — the 🔍 emoji did something; I opened faster than I should have.

## What's weak

- Subject promises personalized results; email delivers a generic adult-styles send — that gap is the biggest miss here.
- Zero kids shoe content in the hero or any featured section, despite Back to School being the most active shopping moment of my year right now.
- "Kids Clothing" lives as a tiny navigation link at the bottom — after Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, BOBS, Only at Skechers, and the member pricing module. If that's all I get, I'd rather have a separate email.
- No deadline on the 40% off — the urgency is implied but unconfirmed, so it's easy to tell myself I'll come back later (I won't).
- This is the seventh Skechers email in roughly eight days; the cadence is starting to blur together, especially when the payoff doesn't match the promise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the personalization real or drop the hook.** If the subject says "You've Been Searching," the hero should surface what I actually browsed — kids' sneakers, light-up soles, back-to-school sizing. Right now the subject is aspirational fiction. Either retarget with real behavioral data or swap to a subject that doesn't make a promise the body can't keep.
- 2. **Lead with kids content during Back to School season.** Split the hero — adult styles on one side, kids picks on the other. Or just send a separate email. I should not be the one hunting for the kids link.
- 3. **Put a deadline on the sale.** "Ends Sunday" or "through the weekend" turns a browsing moment into an action. Right now I have no reason to tap before naptime.
- 4. **Move the SUMMER code up one panel.** Member pricing is a loyalty retention signal — it should appear right after the hero, not two-thirds down.

- **Subject Alt B:** `We Found Them: 40% Off + Your Skechers+ Code Inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, BOBS — plus kids styles on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use code SUMMER for 20% off. Plus members get exclusive pricing.`

## Technical Audit — Skechers "You've Been Searching" (07/17/2026)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound but has two categories of confirmed infrastructure issues: all image assets are served over HTTP (not HTTPS), and the plain-text multipart alternative is absent entirely. Both are deliverability and rendering risks in modern inbox environments.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **26 click-redirect links** routed through `click.emails.skechers.com` — standard SFMC click-tracking, not probed. Verify redirect chain terminates at HTTPS landing pages and that all links are live.
- **Open pixel (ink1000.com)** served over HTTP: `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif` — see §3.
- **Krux/Salesforce DMP pixels** (`beacon.krxd.net`) are HTTPS and present in a hidden `display:none` table. The `partner_uid` value `122277199` appears to be a raw subscriber ID passed in cleartext to a third-party data broker — confirm this is consistent with Skechers' data-sharing disclosures.
- **Return Path seed pixel** (`pixel.app.returnpath.net`) uses a relative-style URL with no protocol prefix (`pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif...`) — this is missing `https://` and will likely fail to load.

- DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication headers are not included in the provided data — confirm via a live send header inspection (`Authentication-Results` header) that `emails.skechers.com` passes DMARC alignment.
