# Color confidence delivered 🧡
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Sally Beauty. Again. I opened this on a Sunday hunting for something for the kids' feet, and here's their confetti-dot header staring at me — same visual signature I've now seen basically every day this week. My honest gut reaction: "oh, this one." I don't color my own hair, so the whole email is just noise for my inbox.
- That said, if I squint and pretend I'm the target customer, it's actually one of their cleaner sends — a single clear model, a legible Buy 2 Get 1 Free offer up top, and a tidy row of specific products underneath instead of a wall of vague banners. But it doesn't change the fact that I'm not their customer and this is the umpteenth nearly-identical promo landing in the same week.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only to laugh with a friend about how often Sally Beauty shows up in my inbox lately. This one gets marked read and swiped past, same as the last several.

## What's working

- The Buy 2, Get 1 Free headline is big, bold, and impossible to miss — no digging required.
- One clean hero model with soft, glossy hair actually ties visually to "color with confidence" — feels intentional, not stock-photo random.
- Product cards below are specific (ColorCharm shades, ShineLuxe Top Coat) with individual "Shop now" links instead of one generic CTA.
- The Sally Rewards banner ($5 reward for shopping twice) is visually distinct and easy to spot if I were a member.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is remotely for me — no kids' shoes, no family content, not even adjacent to my actual weekend shopping mission.
- This is at least the eighth Sally Beauty email in two weeks (per the brand history) — my inbox thumb is on total autopilot for this sender now.
- Top banner is a cluttered stack of three different offers (in-store pickup, free delivery, 20% off sitewide) competing with the hero message right below it — visual hierarchy gets muddy fast.
- Four back-to-back product sections in a row feels like scrolling a catalog, not a curated pick, especially on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the send frequency — dial it back from near-daily to twice a week max; the fatigue is real and I suspect their unsubscribe rate is climbing.
- 2. Trim the top banner to one offer, not three — let the BOGO hero message be the single headline instead of competing with itself.
- 3. If they have any segmentation data showing a subscriber hasn't opened in weeks, suppress and try a re-engagement send instead of the same cadence.
- 4. Move the Rewards $5 banner higher — that's actually a stronger hook for a loyalty-driven shopper than another product card.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your salon-quality color, 3-for-2`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: earn a $5 Reward when you shop twice in August.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wella formulas, BOGO pricing, no salon required.`
