# Don't Miss 50% Off It's a 10 💖
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- My immediate reaction when this hit my inbox: *Sally. Again.* I've been keeping mental count all June and this is somewhere around email seven from them in less than four weeks. That "LAST CALL" banner is doing the crying-wolf thing — I know Sally's cadence, and there will be another "last call" next Tuesday. I almost swiped it away on autopilot.
- But I opened it because "It's a 10" actually means something to me. I keep the Miracle Leave-In in my bathroom. Seeing 50% off a specific brand I already use in the subject line was the thing that stopped my thumb. The email itself is clean and fast to scan — big offer at the top, four product tiles, done. No mystery about what they're selling.
- I'd probably click through to check whether my specific bottle qualifies under "select." If it does, I'm buying two. I would not forward this — it's a personal-use deal, not a "hey, you have to see this" moment. Mark as read after clicking or not clicking; I'm not dwelling on it.

## What's working

- **Offer is in the hero, no scrolling required**: "50% off select / It's a 10 leave-in treatments" is right there above the fold. I don't have to decode what this email is about.
- **Seasonal copy earned its place**: "Eliminate frizz & hold up in humidity" is exactly what I'm thinking about in June. That line felt written for right now, not recycled from March.
- **Sally Rewards block is a small but real win**: "Earn points while you shop for warm-weather hair care" speaks directly to me as a member. Points accumulation matters when I'm already price-sensitive.
- **Four product tiles are scannable**: Miracle Leave-In, Plus Keratin, Silk Express, Lite — I can see my options without a wall of text.
- **Free shipping on $35+ is visible**: The icon strip at the bottom gives me the shipping threshold without me having to wonder. That matters for a 50%-off stocking-up decision.

## What's weak

- **"LAST CALL" is meaningless after seven emails in June**: The urgency banner is crying wolf at this point. Compare this to the June 15 "More Beauty, Less Spend" send — same cadence, same vibe, same fake urgency. I'm numb.
- **"Select" with no qualifying product list**: The hero promises 50% off but doesn't tell me which SKUs count until I click. That one word — "select" — is a friction point I have to resolve before I'll commit to opening my wallet.
- **The Marta de Arcos / Pride Month section breaks the momentum**: I'm in "does my product qualify?" mode, and suddenly I'm looking at a vivid-hair influencer section with a completely different visual tone. I don't dislike it, but it stops my scroll dead.
- **Klarna on orders above $35 doesn't match the use case**: I'm buying a $14 leave-in at 50% = $7. Klarna for that is noise, not a feature. It clutters the footer for no reason at this price point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the qualifying items in the hero** — Add "Including Miracle Leave-In, Plus Keratin & more" right under the headline. That one line would remove the biggest reason I hesitate before clicking.
- 2. **Replace "LAST CALL" with an actual end date** — "Ends Sunday, June 29" is more credible than "LAST CALL" for the seventh time this month. Real date, real urgency.
- 3. **Dial back the send frequency** — One fewer email per week would make each send hit harder. The June 15 "More Beauty, Less Spend" was a better email than this one and I still didn't buy because I was fatigued. Cadence is the real conversion killer.
- 4. **Make the rewards tie-in transactional** — "Earn 2X points on leave-ins today only" beats the generic "earn points while you shop" block every time. Specificity converts.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two malformed `<meta>` tags:**

```html
<!-- Missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />

<!-- Missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
<meta content="IE=edge" />
```
Both tags are silently ignored by parsers — charset declaration and IE-edge mode are not applied. Correct form:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge" />
```

**Undeclared font `'Founders Grotesk'` applied to all `<a>` tags:**

```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
`Founders Grotesk` has no `@font-face` rule in this stylesheet. Every anchor tag will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif in all email clients. If this is intentional, the declaration is misleading; if not, the font is missing.

**`white-space: pre` on `<img>` elements:**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` has no effect on replaced elements (`img`) but is parsed and may trigger unexpected layout behavior in Outlook's Word rendering engine. Should be removed.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`:**
Standard email breakpoints target 600px. Devices with viewports 500–599px (common on Android) will receive desktop layout.

**Open Sans loaded at version `v17`:**
```
fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Current Google Fonts static URLs use v40+. v17 URLs remain live but reference a font file from ~2019. Not a breakage risk but flag for font QA if character rendering appears inconsistent.
