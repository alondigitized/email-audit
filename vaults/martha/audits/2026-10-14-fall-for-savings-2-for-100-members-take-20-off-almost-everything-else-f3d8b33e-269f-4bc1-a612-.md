# Fall for savings: 2 for $100 + members take 20% off almost everything else!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- My first thought was "another one?" This is my seventh Skechers email in about a week. The 10/14 "Final hours to save 25% off sitewide" send landed the same day, and now there's a different offer. I'm Skechers Plus, so the "Hi, Martha" and 1000 points strip at the top is a nice touch. The giant "2 FOR $100" is impossible to miss, and it's a deal I'd actually do the math on.
- Then I hit the shoes. There are three adult-looking sneakers in mauve, navy and blush, and the "kids" shoe is a quilted pink lace-up. That's not velcro, light-ups or sparkle for my 5-year-old, and nothing here is for my 9-year-old. The email also doesn't say whether the two pairs can be mixed across women's, men's and kids. That's the one question a mom shopping for two kids needs answered.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Kids'" if I'm on my phone this weekend, but I'd feel cautious. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably save it for the weekend and then check whether 2 for $100 beats the 25% off from yesterday.

## What's working

- The "2 FOR $100" hero is huge and clear. I get the deal in one second.
- "Hi, Martha" with the Member badge and 1000 points makes me feel like a known customer.
- The separate Women's, Men's and Kids' buttons are big and easy to tap on my phone.
- "MEMBERS TAKE 20% OFF" pays off my loyalty, and the "Valid thru 10/21" date gives a real deadline.
- Klarna and Afterpay plus the app download are useful for budgeting kid shoes.

## What's weak

- The shoes are all adult-style leather and quilted sneakers. Nothing says "5-year-old girl" or "9-year-old boy."
- It doesn't say whether I can mix kids' pairs. "Select styles. Exclusions apply" feels vague, and the real terms are in tiny print at the bottom.
- Two offers are stacked, 2 for $100 and 20% off "(almost) everything else." That's a lot to untangle, and it's different from yesterday's 25% sitewide. The cadence feels like constant promo whiplash.
- The 1000 points "enough for a $5 reward" nudge is cute, but it's small and not connected to the main offer.
- There are no prices, product names or reviews, so I can't tell whether the shoes are worth it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the Kids' section real. Show a velcro light-up girl's shoe and a durable lace-up boy's shoe, with "Mix & match for the whole family."
- 2. State the rules clearly near the hero: "Any 2 pairs, women's, men's or kids, mix freely."
- 3. Pick one headline offer and let the other be secondary. Don't send a "25% off" one day and "2 for $100" the next.
- 4. Name the products with a price, like "$50 each, 2 for $100," so I can see the savings.
- 5. Fold the points reminder into the offer ("Use your 1000 points on top").

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed broken-experience defect: an empty merge field renders the greeting as "Hi ,". The source also has a malformed tracking pixel, insecure `http://` image sources, ineffective meta tags, and a viewport setting that blocks zoom. The HTML was truncated and no headers were supplied, so the body, footer and link destinations could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 31 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe as click-redirect domains. Redirect resolution and destination status are unverified.
- **Malformed pixel URL:** `<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1db…">` has no scheme, so it resolves as a relative path and fails to load. That Return Path (Validity) pixel is likely used for deliverability and inbox-placement monitoring, so it is silently not reporting. QA did not flag this.
- **Insecure pixel:** `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=PRO_US_NA_U_SITEBUNDLE_FULL_EN_10142026` is plain HTTP, so it may be blocked or rewritten by clients' image proxies (QA WARN).
- **Pixel stack:** Five tracking images sit in a `display:none` wrapper: the SFMC open pixel (`click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx`), three `beacon.krxd.net` calls, and the Return Path pixel. The ink1000 pixel sits in a separate 1×1 table. Some clients suppress images in hidden containers, so open and attribution counts can undercount.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure image:** `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png` loads over HTTP and has no alt text (QA WARN ×2). The SFMC image host is on HTTP, so this image may be blocked or flagged as mixed content.
- **False positive:** QA reports `o.gif` as missing alt, but the source has `alt=""`. That is correct for a tracking pixel, so no fix is needed.
- **Ineffective meta tags:** `<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />` and `<meta content="IE=Edge" />` have no `http-equiv`, so neither declaration takes effect. Charset should still come from the Content-Type header.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Text resizing and focus:** `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` disables iOS text resizing. `a { outline: none }` removes the keyboard focus indicator.
- **Layout tables:** The visible top-level tables (e.g. `<table class="widthFull" width="640">`) lack `role="presentation"`.
- **Doctype:** XHTML 1.0 Transitional is acceptable.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **[FAIL] Empty greeting slot:** The greeting renders as "Hi ," because the name field is empty. The template has no fallback value for missing first names. This is a visible defect for any recipient without a first name on record.
- No unresolved raw tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) were visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not verifiable:** The footer, physical address, unsubscribe link and headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF/DKIM/DMARC results) were not in the supplied material, so no violation is confirmed.
- **Hashed identifiers in pixel URLs:** `beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=…_sha25&partner_uid=adf27fee…189a` carries a 64-hex value consistent with a SHA-256 hash, plus a subscriber ID (`partner_uid=122277199`). These go to a third-party DMP endpoint on every open. This is worth a privacy and consent check, since I can't confirm what the hash is derived from.
- **Sender address:** The sender is `no-reply@emails.skechers.com`. That is not a violation if a working opt-out exists, but confirm a monitored reply or contact path.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Not verifiable:** UTM parameters and landing URLs weren't visible in the truncated source, and all 31 links were skipped by the probe. No continuity issues are confirmed.
- The campaign ID `PRO_US_NA_U_SITEBUNDLE_FULL_EN_10142026` appears in the krxd and ink1000 pixels. Confirm the same ID reaches the landing pages through the redirect chain.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add a fallback to the name merge (e.g. `Hi there,` or drop the name) so "Hi ," cannot render.
2. Fix the Return Path pixel to `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=…`.
3. Switch `ink1000.com` and `image.emails.skechers.com` image sources to HTTPS. Add alt text to `dde00662-…png`, and keep `alt=""` on the pixels.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` and `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`. Restore a visible focus style on links. Add `role="presentation"` to layout tables.
5. Replace the bare `<meta content=…>` tags with `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">`.
6. Move the tracking pixels out of the `display:none` wrapper, for example into a visible 1×1 container, to avoid client suppression.
7. Review the hashed email and subscriber ID sent to `beacon.krxd.net` for privacy and consent.
8. Re-run the audit with the full HTML (footer and links) and raw headers, and probe the redirect chains. That would settle the unsubscribe, authentication and UTM questions.
