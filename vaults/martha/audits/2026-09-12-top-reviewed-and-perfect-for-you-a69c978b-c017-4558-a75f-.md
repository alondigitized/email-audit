# Top reviewed and perfect for you
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- Same 20% off, same 1000 points, same "Hi, Martha" as literally every email this week — I don't even need to read the fine print anymore, I know it's EXTRA20, valid through 9/14. This one's dressed up as review-driven ("5-Star Comfort," reviews under each shoe) which is a decent hook in theory, but every single shoe pictured is adult sizing — men's boots, women's slip-ins, a navy dad-sneaker, a beige comfort shoe. Not one kid shoe, not one hint of "KIDS" until it's buried as a plain text link way down in the "shop additional styles" list.
- This is a mark-as-read for me. I'm not the audience for "BOBS Skip Cute" or "Delson 3.0 - Roth" reviews from strangers — I came here hunting light-up shoes for my daughter and lace-ups that'll survive recess for my son, and this email just doesn't know I have kids. Not forwarding it, not clicking through the adult product blocks. If anything I'd tap the plain "KIDS" text link at the bottom out of habit, but that's me working around the email, not the email working for me.

## What's working

- The 20% off code (EXTRA20) is right up top in a clean navy banner, easy to spot at a glance.
- My 1000 points and rewards status are visible immediately — I do like being reminded I'm close to a $5 reward.
- The star ratings and real customer quotes under each shoe ("I have purchased 6 of this style and cannot recommend them enough!") add some trust/social proof I don't usually see from this brand.
- Clean layout, no rendering bugs, everything loads and lines up fine.

## What's weak

- Zero kids' products shown — no velcro sneakers, no light-up soles, nothing pink/sparkly for my daughter, nothing durable/blue for my son.
- "Kids" only shows up as a plain gray text link at the very bottom, same weight as "Clothing" and "New Arrivals" — no visual priority at all.
- The hero section and all three featured shoes are 100% adult product — this reads like a generic "top reviewed" blast, not anything personalized to what I actually shop for.
- I've seen this identical "Hi, Martha / 1000 points / 20% off" skeleton every day this week (yesterday's "Fall for cozy fits," the day before that, and the day before that) — the fatigue is real at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least one of the three "top reviewed" hero shoes for a top-reviewed kids' style — if you're going to lean on reviews as the hook, show me reviews from parents buying what I buy.
- 2. Move "Kids" out of the plain text list and give it its own visual block or button, same treatment as BOBS/Delson/Contour Foam got.
- 3. Vary the opening beat — I can recite "Hi, Martha / 1000 points / 20% off" from memory now; even swapping the layout order would help this feel like a different email.
- 4. If this truly is a "top reviewed across the board" email, at least segment it — show me the top-reviewed picks in categories I've actually browsed (kids), not a stranger's favorite dress boot.
