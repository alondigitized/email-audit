# Get a $5 reward for sharing your thoughts
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Oh, a survey email — not even a product push. First come first rewarded for $5 if I fill out a survey about Skechers Plus. Honestly my first reaction is "meh, fine, I'll take the $5 if I have two minutes," but nothing about this makes me want to open it urgently or forward it anywhere. It's clean and honest at least — no bait-and-switch, no fake urgency screaming at me.
- I'd probably leave it unread in my mental queue and maybe tap it later while the kids are watching something, mostly because 1000 points sitting there feels like free money I don't want to lose. Not something I'd ever forward to a friend though — there's zero personality or kid content here, it's just internal Skechers survey ops.

## What's working

- The $5 reward for a quick survey is a clear, honest, no-strings offer — I appreciate they're not hiding the catch.
- "Hi, Martha" and my 1000 points balance up top makes it feel a little personal, like they know I'm a member.
- "First come, first rewarded... once we hit 3,500 responses, that's a wrap" gives real, believable urgency instead of fake countdown clocks.
- The fallback line ("Didn't get to it in time? No worries...") is a nice, low-pressure touch — feels human.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is for my kids at all — no velcro sparkle shoes, no boys' sneakers, not even a photo of a kid. It's just navy blue text on a gradient banner.
- This isn't shopping content, it's a survey — as a mom squeezing in phone time on weekends, this isn't what I open Skechers emails for.
- The "SHOP ADDITIONAL STYLES" section below is just gray text links (Women/Men/Kids/Wide) with zero visuals — feels like a low-effort afterthought bolted onto a survey email.
- No product, no offer code, nothing time-bound beyond the vague "3,500 responses" cap — there's no real reason to act today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a small photo strip of kids' shoes near the survey CTA so even a non-shopping email still nudges toward my actual browsing habit — I'm scrolling for my kids first, always.
- 2. Give the survey itself a stronger hook — tell me what it's about (app experience? checkout? sizing?) so I know if it's worth my two minutes.
- 3. Turn "SHOP ADDITIONAL STYLES" into actual product tiles with images instead of plain gray text links — right now it looks broken/unfinished.
- 4. Consider pairing this with a small thank-you incentive tied to something I'd actually use, like "survey + kids' bundle promo code" in the same email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Quick survey, $5 reward — 2 minutes tops`
- **Subject Alt B:** `You're invited: share feedback, earn $5`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Be one of the first 3,500 members — reward yourself for your feedback.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 1000 points + a $5 survey reward are waiting, Martha.`
