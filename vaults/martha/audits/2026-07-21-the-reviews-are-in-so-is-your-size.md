# The Reviews Are In & So Is Your Size
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject got me curious — "The Reviews Are In & So Is Your Size" has this nice little promise of social proof plus availability, which is actually two things that move me. I'm a reviews-reader. I always check them before I buy, especially for the kids' shoes where fit is everything. So I opened it with genuine interest.
- Inside, the execution is solid enough. The "Most Talked About" hero is punchy, the three product spotlights with actual customer quotes and star ratings are a smart format, and the 20% off rewards block buried below the fold is useful if you scroll that far. But this is the *seventh* Skechers email in eight days — I counted, and yesterday they were screaming "Final Hours!" on the Summer Favorites Sale, which was allegedly ending. So today's send feels like the campaign is still alive despite what I was told last night, and that erodes trust just a little.
- The bigger miss for me: I'm here for my kids. My 5-year-old needs back-to-school velcro sneakers and my 9-year-old destroys shoes at a rate I do not want to think about. The featured shoes are all adult women's styles — Arch Fit, Cozy Fit, BOBS. Kids is a tiny text link at the bottom. I'd have scrolled right through this and maybe tapped the 20% off link, but I wouldn't call it a session-making email. Mark as read and circle back later, maybe.

## What's working

- **Reviews-as-hero format is genuinely smart.** Real customer quotes with star ratings under each product is the kind of social proof that actually stops my scroll — it feels less like a sales pitch and more like a friend texting me "hey these are good."
- **"Most Talked About" headline lands.** It creates mild FOMO without being obnoxious — I want to know what other people are buying.
- **20% off rewards block.** Even buried, I notice a discount code. Price-sensitive me clocked that.
- **Clean mobile layout.** No overlapping text, no broken images — everything is tap-able and readable without zooming. Skechers consistently delivers on this.
- **Category nav grid at the bottom.** Having KIDS listed as a shortcut at least gives me an exit ramp even if the hero didn't go there.

## What's weak

- **The preview text is complete garbage.** What I actually see next to the subject in my inbox is JSON schema markup — `[{ "@context": "http://schema.org/"...` — which is technical garbage that should never surface. That's the first impression, and it's broken.
- **Zero kids or family content in the featured zone.** The "Kindergarten to College" send on July 14th showed Skechers *knows* who I am. This one forgot entirely.
- **"Your size" promise doesn't pay off.** The subject teases availability, but the body doesn't surface size info, a size finder, or anything that resolves that hook. It feels like a trick.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Seven emails in eight days, including one that declared a sale was ending last night, and now a fresh send with no acknowledgment of that — my trust in the urgency signals is wearing thin.
- **Three hero products dilute attention.** I can't mentally commit to all three at once. One hero with a tight "most reviewed" badge would convert harder than a three-way split.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader — this is urgent.** Whatever is generating that JSON-LD schema markup as the first visible inbox text needs to be caught before send. This is the single biggest lift on open rate.
- 2. **Inject one kids/family block.** Even a single "Most Talked About — Kids" row with one shoe would have made this email feel like it knows me. Reference the July 14th "Kindergarten to College" energy.
- 3. **Make the 20% off visible in the hero.** If the offer exists, lead with it — don't bury it below three full product sections.
- 4. **Resolve the "your size" promise.** Add a size-finder CTA or a "sizes restocking" hook that pays off the subject's implicit availability claim.
- 5. **Cool the cadence.** Consider skipping a send or batching instead of 7-in-8-days — the urgency signals are losing credibility.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Real Reviews, Real Moms — See What's Hot This Week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Star ratings, real quotes, and your size is back in stock.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The shoes everyone's talking about — including one your kid will actually wear.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard SFMC table-based email with Krux DMP + Return Path + ink1000 pixel stack. Eleven images are served over plain HTTP and will be blocked or proxied by major clients; a Return Path pixel has a malformed (protocol-less) URL that will silently fail to fire.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Return Path pixel — broken URL (no protocol)**
```html
<img src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499" />
```
The `src` has no `http://` or `https://` scheme. Email clients treat this as a relative path; the pixel will never load. Should be `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=...`.

**ink1000 open tracker — HTTP only**
```
http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108404282&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NA_U_MULTI_ACQ_EN_07212026
```
Served over HTTP. Gmail and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS pixel loads, meaning open attribution for this source will undercount.

**26 click links unverifiable** — all route through `click.emails.skechers.com` redirects; UTM parameter integrity on destination URLs cannot be confirmed from source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images served over HTTP** — `image.emails.skechers.com` is used for all hosted assets including the Skechers logo, app store badges, social icons, and footer icons. Gmail HTTPS-proxies these (altering the URL), Outlook 365 may block them entirely behind corporate proxies. Full list from QA:
- `dde00662...png` (Skechers logo)
- `49468f73...png` (unknown — also missing alt)
- `0ec56c9f...png` (App Store badge)
- `9bb508d0...png` (Google Play badge)
- `00100b23...png` (Curbside Pickup)
- `07cb60b5...png` (AfterPay)
- `fc08601a...png` (Find A Store)
- `b782e0aa...png`, `a5c4cae0...png`, `8c5738fc...png` (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube)

**2 images missing `alt` text**
- `o.gif` (ink1000 tracker) — minor; tracking pixel, `alt=""` acceptable
- `49468f73-4651-4af3-bea2-61d1ae5db486.png` — unidentified content image; needs descriptive alt for screen readers and image-blocked clients

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents users from zooming on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Standard email practice but flagged as an accessibility regression.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*`** — disables OS-level text scaling on Safari/iOS for all elements. Use `100%` instead to allow system accessibility overrides to work.

**Missing `http-equiv` on two meta tags**
```html
<meta content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />          <!-- missing http-equiv="Content-Type" -->
<meta content="IE=Edge" />                            <!-- missing http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" -->
```
Without `http-equiv`, these declarations have no effect. The charset and IE compatibility directives are silently ignored.

**Duplicate media query blocks** — rules for `.hideme`, `.widthFull`, `.width50percent`, `.borderRight`, `.break`, `.legal`, `.responsive-td`, `.bottom-nav`, `.photo`, `.mobileFont16`, `.paddingBottom0`, `.mobile-pi`, `.mobile-img` are declared identically under both `max-width: 375px` and `max-width: 640px`. The 375px block is entirely redundant; it adds payload and parse overhead with no behavioral difference since 640px already covers it.
