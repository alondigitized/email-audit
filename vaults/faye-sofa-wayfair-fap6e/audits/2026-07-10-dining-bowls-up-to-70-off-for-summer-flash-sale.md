# DINING BOWLS ✨  UP TO 70% OFF for SUMMER FLASH SALE ✨ 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair leads the subject with a specific product category — dining bowls — and then promptly abandons it. Open the email and you get the exact same general-sale hero (SALE / ENDS TONIGHT / UP TO 70% OFF) that has appeared in every send this week. The dining category gets a passing mention in the mid-body category list ("dining: up to 60% OFF") but dining bowls are nowhere near the hero, and the product grid fills out with rugs, bar sets, outdoor furniture, and a basketball hoop. This is a structural repeat of the July 8 "BATH MATS You'll Love!!" send (scored 6/10) — specific subject, unrelated body.
- Outside of that core failure, the execution is competent. The ENDS TONIGHT urgency is credible (there is a visible timer/deadline banner), the discount ladder is clearly laid out, and the render is clean. Wayfair Rewards is surfaced at the bottom, free delivery threshold is called out, and the category discount grid is scannable. But "competent" is the ceiling when the subject hook doesn't connect to the body.
- The bigger problem is cadence. Counting brand history, this is at minimum the seventh Wayfair sale email in four days. "ENDS TONIGHT" loses all credibility when a new sale starts tomorrow.

## What's working

- **Hero urgency is concrete**: "ENDS TONIGHT" with what appears to be a visible countdown banner — not vague "limited time" language.
- **Discount ladder is scannable**: area rugs 70% OFF / entryway furniture 40% OFF / TV stands 42% OFF / dining 60% OFF — specific percentages, not "up to big savings."
- **Free delivery threshold visible**: "free & easy delivery over $35*" displayed as a clear banner, reduces friction anxiety.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, overlapping text, or empty placeholders visible anywhere in the email.
- **Wayfair Rewards surfaced**: Loyalty program is visible at the bottom footer section — good for repeat customers.

## What's weak

- **Dining bowls are the subject, not the email**: The hero is a generic SALE banner. A subscriber who opened for dining bowls sees rugs and bar furniture first — same bait-and-switch as the July 8 BATH MATS email.
- **"ENDS TONIGHT" credibility is shot**: This is the seventh-plus sale email in four days. If the sale ended yesterday, what ended it?
- **General product grid dilutes the category signal**: The "Your Home, These Picks" section includes a basketball hoop and outdoor furniture — dining bowls are invisible.
- **No dining bowls visible as a hero product**: Not a single bowl-specific product card is clearly featured in the above-the-fold hero area.
- **Subject line is over 50 characters and all-caps heavy**: Long subject with emoji stacking and ALL CAPS throughout reads as aggressive on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the body to the subject** — If dining bowls is the hook, make a dining bowl product card the hero image and link it directly to the dining bowls category page. The current email could swap in any category noun and the body would look identical. That's the problem.
- 2. **Let "ENDS TONIGHT" mean something** — Either enforce a real sale end (no new sale tomorrow) or drop deadline language entirely. The trust cost of false urgency compounds across sends.
- 3. **Cut the product grid to one category** — "Your Home, These Picks" spanning rugs, basketball hoops, and outdoor chairs makes this feel like a clearance dump, not a curated dining send.
- 4. **Trim subject line below 50 chars** — Remove one emoji and the redundant "for SUMMER" qualifier.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Web font with incomplete fallback stack** — `@font-face` loads `Sofia` from `secure.img.wfrcdn.com`. The only fallback specified is `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` (Outlook-only attribute). Gmail (web + Android), Yahoo, and AOL strip `@font-face` entirely and have no declared `font-family` fallback in the rule. If body text or headings inherit `Sofia` without a system-font stack (e.g., `font-family: "Sofia", Arial, sans-serif`), those clients render the browser/OS default, which may break layout metrics.

- **`.u-Title` font-size collapses to 32px on mobile for all title variants** — all five title classes (`--primary`, `--secondary`, `--tertiary`, `--white`, plain) override to the same 32px. If typographic hierarchy is load-bearing for scannability, this flattens it on mobile. Not a bug but a known limitation to document.

- **`min-width: 100vw` on `u ~ div`** — this is the standard "Blue Gmail" fix and is intentional, but it prevents Gmail Desktop from rendering the email narrower than the viewport. No issue unless the desktop layout has a hard max-width container (verify `#Container` width in full source).

- **`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` block** — iOS Safari-specific image inline fix is present. Correct usage.
