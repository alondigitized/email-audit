# MATTRESS TOPPERS AND PADS 🎉 Surprise 🎉 MEMORIAL DAY SURPLUS SALE 🎉 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Day nine of Wayfair's Memorial Day run and the formula is identical to every send before it. "MATTRESS TOPPERS AND PADS" in the subject drops you into the same broad algorithmic clearance grid — outdoor furniture, storage, rugs, kitchenware — with no mattress topper anywhere in the hero or above the fold. The subject-to-content mismatch is now the defining trait of this campaign and it has fully calcified into bait-and-switch.
- The email is visually competent: clean layout, patriotic "SURPLUS SALE" hero, visible discounts of 40–70% off, and a functional CTA stack. The "picked just for you" personalization hook is a nice touch. But the offer framing is diffuse — you're clicking to a clearance sale, not a mattress topper deal — and the subject line is a bloated 70+ characters crammed with ALL CAPS and three 🎉 emojis.
- The clearest comparison in recent history is the 05-26 "AREA RUGS for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡" send, which scored 7/10 precisely because the subject was concise and punchy. Today's subject does the opposite: it's longer, noisier, and the "Surprise" tease rings hollow when the "surprise" is just another clearance grid.

## What's working

- Memorial Day red/white/blue hero is on-brand and seasonally appropriate — the campaign visual coherence holds.
- Discount magnitude (up to 70% off) is prominently visible, clearly tiered (70/50/40%), and anchored near the top.
- "Picked just for you" personalization copy adds a small credibility signal to the grid.
- "Free & easy delivery over $335" threshold is surfaced mid-email — useful nudge before the product tiles.
- Email renders clean with no visible broken images, overlapping text, or layout breaks.

## What's weak

- Zero mattress toppers or pads are visible anywhere in the email — the subject promise goes entirely unfulfilled.
- Subject line is 70+ characters with ALL CAPS, three 🎉 emojis, and a meaningless "Surprise" — spam-signal overload that will get clipped on mobile.
- "Shop all sales" CTA is the least specific call-to-action possible; it could appear on any of the nine identical sends this week.
- No urgency mechanic inside the email body — "Memorial Day" is implied but no countdown, no end-date copy, no "ends tonight" hook.
- The big-name-brands section is a brand logo row with no actual product or price attached — it's filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put at least one actual mattress topper in the email.** If the subject names the category, one hero product with a real price and star rating should be above the fold. This single change closes the bait-and-switch gap and lifts click confidence.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters.** Drop the emojis, the redundant "PADS," and the "Surprise" tease. The 05-25 AREA RUGS send proved a cleaner, shorter subject outperforms the noise.
- 3. **Add a deadline inside the body.** "Ends Monday midnight" or a countdown banner converts the implied Memorial Day urgency into an actionable cue.
- 4. **Replace "Shop all sales" with a category-specific CTA.** "Shop Mattress Toppers" or "See All Bedding Deals" is a one-word change that lifts click intent.
- 5. **Retire or rotate the clearance grid.** Nine days of the same tile layout means engaged subscribers have already tuned it out.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom web font via `@font-face`:**
```css

  font-family: "Sofia";

```
- `@font-face` is stripped by Gmail (web and Android) and all Outlook versions. The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback handles Outlook correctly, but Gmail will silently fall back to the client default (usually sans-serif) — not Arial. - `.woff` only — no `.woff2` or `.ttf` fallback format declared. For Apple Mail / iOS Mail (which do support web fonts), `.woff2` is preferred for performance.
