#  🔔 PSA 🔔 UP TO 70% OFF is HEREEE 🔔
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's most discount-aggressive subject line in the recent barrage — triple bell emojis, all-caps, and "HEREEE" with three E's — and it lands squarely in spam-signal territory before the email is even opened. After the June 27 "STATEMENT HEADBOARDS THIS WAY →" send, which showed Wayfair can write editorial copy that earns attention, this is a significant regression in tone. "PSA" is Gen Z shorthand that doesn't fit a furniture retailer's core buyer, and the triple exclamation structure will trigger junk filters on several major clients.
- Inside, the body is the same rotating product-tile chassis Wayfair has deployed in every send this week — Fourth of July Clearance badges, a "picked just for you" module, discount callouts ranging from 40–70% off, and a free delivery bar. The personalized section is doing real work. But the rest is a dense grid with no hero moment, no single product that proves the 70% claim immediately visible, and no explicit deadline anywhere despite the holiday theme.
- The saving grace: the discounts are real, the timing is appropriate, and Wayfair's brand recognition carries the open rate on pure list inertia. But credibility is being spent faster than it's being earned across this week's cadence.

## What's working

- "Picked just for you" personalization module appears mid-email with star ratings — adds genuine credibility and breaks the grid monotony
- Discount badges (40–70% off) are large, readable, and appear above the fold
- Fourth of July Clearance framing gives the blast a reason-for-being, unlike "BIRDBATHS." or "AREA RUGS." earlier in the week
- "Free & easy delivery over $35" is a clean, visible secondary offer that reduces purchase friction
- Star ratings on product tiles provide lightweight social proof at the point of consideration

## What's weak

- "HEREEE" with three E's alongside three 🔔 emojis reads as spam bait and will be filtered or ignored by inbox-trained readers
- The body has no single hero product to substantiate the 70% claim — most visible badges are 40–50%, making "up to 70% off" feel misleading
- "PSA" in the subject line is informal slang that's brand-inconsistent for a home goods retailer
- No explicit sale deadline anywhere — "Fourth of July Clearance" implies July 4th but the email never says it
- This is the sixth-plus promotional blast from Wayfair in roughly a week; list fatigue is acute by this point

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject from scratch.** The "HEREEE" + triple-bell construction is the single biggest drag on this email. The June 27 headboard send proved Wayfair can write real editorial hooks — return to that register.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fourth of July Clearance: up to 70% off, ends July 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 70%-off list — furniture, rugs & lighting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free delivery on orders $35+ · no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Picked for you: 40–70% off through the long weekend`
- 2. **Lead with proof of the 70% claim.** Put one specific hero product at the maximum discount (name it, show the original price crossed out) directly below the nav bar. Right now the headline promise isn't visible in the body until the reader hunts for it.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** One line — "Sale ends July 4th at midnight" — converts browsers into buyers. The holiday hook is already there; the email just never closes the loop.
- 4. **Rest the cadence for two days before the next send.** Six near-identical templates in a week trains subscribers to tune out or unsubscribe. This clearance event deserves to feel like an event, not noise.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — fixed-height product name truncation without ellipsis:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name {
    word-break: break-all;
    overflow: hidden;
    display: block;
    height: 48px !important;
  }
}
```

`overflow: hidden` with a fixed `height` clips text silently — no `text-overflow: ellipsis` or `-webkit-line-clamp` to signal truncation. Long product names will be invisibly cut on mobile. `word-break: break-all` also breaks mid-word, which degrades readability.

- All `.u-Title` variants collapse to a single `32px` on mobile regardless of hierarchy — `--primary`, `--secondary`, `--tertiary`, `--white` share identical breakpoint rules with no differentiation.

**No issues found** in the media query structure itself or the mobile layout utility classes (`.u-Block`, `.u-HalfWidthBlock`, etc.) — these follow standard email responsive patterns.
