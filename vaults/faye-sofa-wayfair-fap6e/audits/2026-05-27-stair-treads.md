# STAIR TREADS.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Day ten or eleven of Wayfair's Memorial Day / surplus clearance run, and the pattern hasn't changed — but the subject line has. "STAIR TREADS." is the most stripped-back subject Wayfair has sent this entire campaign: no emoji, no percentage, no urgency, just a category name and a full stop. That minimalism is either a deliberate A/B test or creative exhaustion. The hero actually delivers on the subject — stair treads are shown at the top — which makes this better than several recent sends (see 05-25's "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR" that dumped you into gazebos and beverage coolers). The problem is everything below the fold: the same broad algorithmic grid of outdoor furniture, baskets, rugs, and bed products that has appeared in every email since May 19.
- The "Big Help Price Tags" and "Surplus Sale" banner messaging is vague at best. There's no stated percentage, no deadline, no specific price anchoring in the hero — just a sense that some things cost less than they used to. The subscriber who opened because they actually want stair treads will find one small hero module, then an unrelated scroll of home goods that has nothing to do with what was promised.
- The flat subject line is the most interesting creative choice here, but it's working against engagement: no offer, no urgency, no personalization, and it arrives after nine near-identical sends to the same list. The stair treads niche is narrow enough that most subscribers won't feel it's for them — and there's nothing in the subject to change their mind.

## What's working

- Hero actually shows stair treads — rare subject-to-hero alignment in this Memorial Day run.
- Subject is clean and short (13 chars) — no emoji noise, mobile-renders perfectly, doesn't feel like spam.
- "Shop Now" CTA is visible and unambiguous above the fold.
- Wayfair Rewards perks strip at the bottom reinforces member value for loyal subscribers.
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no visible layout bugs.

## What's weak

- "STAIR TREADS." with no offer is probably the flattest subject line Wayfair has sent all year — zero reason to open for anyone without an active stair tread project.
- The hero collapses fast: below the stair tread image, the algorithmic clearance grid returns and the stair tread thread is abandoned entirely.
- "Big Help Price Tags" is meaningless copy — no percentage, no anchor price, no deadline.
- Arriving two days after Memorial Day, the "Surplus Sale" framing feels like leftovers, not a compelling clearance event.
- No social proof (stars, review count, "X customers bought this") anywhere in the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the niche or don't name it.** If "STAIR TREADS" is the hook, the first scroll should be a tight stair tread shop — 6-8 SKUs with a price range and a "See all X styles" CTA — before widening to the clearance grid. Right now the hero is a tease with no payoff.
- 2. **Add a real number to the subject.** The full-stop minimalism is interesting, but without a price point it signals nothing. "Stair treads from $12" beats "STAIR TREADS." every time.
- 3. **Replace "Big Help Price Tags" with the actual sale mechanic.** "Up to 60% off" or "Starting at $14.99" gives subscribers a reason to click instead of an empty promise.
- 4. **Put a deadline on the Surplus Sale.** "Ends tonight" or "Clearance pricing through 5/28" would rescue the urgency that the subject completely lacks.
- 5. **Suppress this send to anyone who opened the last three emails and didn't click.** The cadence is exhausted — a disengaged segment is burning deliverability health.
