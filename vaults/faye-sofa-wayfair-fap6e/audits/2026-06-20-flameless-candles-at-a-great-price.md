# FLAMELESS CANDLES at a great price.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair is running it back. The same multi-category scatter chassis from the last six sends shows up here under a subject line promising "FLAMELESS CANDLES at a great price" — but the hero is a rewards strip, not a candle. The product grid shows pillows, patio furniture, and storage items. Flameless candles do not anchor the email visually. That's the defining problem: the subject made a product promise, and the body defaulted to the house template.
- The 5% Rewards strip is the most prominent above-the-fold element, which is consistent with Wayfair's recent push to embed loyalty into every send. That's fine strategy, but it means the actual flameless candles — the hook that got the open — are either buried or absent. Subscribers who opened expecting a candle deal will disengage quickly.
- Compared to the June 19 cat-tree send (3/10) that at least followed through with discounted cat trees, this one doesn't even have that basic discipline. The honest read: this is a loyalty-push email that borrowed a specific product subject line to inflate opens, without any intention of featuring that product.

## What's working

- **Sender recognition is instant.** The Wayfair logo and "editor@members.wayfair.com" from-address mean zero ambiguity in the inbox.
- **5% Rewards is concrete and trust-building.** "On every item, always" is a clean value prop that scans in one line.
- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breaks visible in the screenshot.
- **The outdoor patio and bedroom product grids include price labels,** giving visual price anchoring even without a sitewide sale.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is a lie the body doesn't keep.** "FLAMELESS CANDLES at a great price" is nowhere in the hero or any visible product slot.
- **Visual hierarchy is flat.** The rewards strip, a product grid, a "let's complete your space" section, and another product grid compete equally — the eye has nowhere to settle.
- **No time-bound urgency.** "Shop before they're gone" appears in the top banner but is generic filler; no end date, no countdown, no threshold.
- **ALL CAPS on the product noun signals discount-bin spam.** "FLAMELESS CANDLES" in all-caps reads like a closeout mailer, not a trusted home brand.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the seventh near-identical scatter-chassis send in the brand history window — the same outdoor furniture and pillow grids keep rotating through.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Feature flameless candles in the hero or don't use them in the subject.** Either build a candle-forward email (lifestyle shot, specific SKUs, price anchors) or write a subject that matches the multi-category scatter body. The gap between promise and delivery is the single biggest conversion killer here.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the "shop before they're gone" banner.** "Ends Sunday" or "Only 48 hours" costs nothing and makes the urgency credible.
- 3. **Collapse to one product section.** Rewards strip → one focused hero category (candles, or whatever is actually on sale) → one CTA. Remove the secondary "let's complete your space" section unless it's genuinely seasonally relevant.
- 4. **Drop the ALL CAPS product noun.** "Flameless candles at a great price." renders more premium than "FLAMELESS CANDLES at a great price."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Flameless candles — up to X% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The candle that never burns down (and it's on sale)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop our top-rated picks before stock runs out — plus earn 5% back on everything.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Flickering glow, zero fire risk. Prices this low won't last.`
