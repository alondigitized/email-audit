# DESKS 🤩 UP TO 70% OFF IS YOURS 🫵
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is yesterday's "HOT SUMMER SALE" email (which scored 8/10 in brand history) wearing a desk-specific mask. The subject line promises "DESKS 🤩 UP TO 70% OFF" — a concrete, category-specific hook — but the body delivers a full-home product dump: rugs, kitchen items, bathroom faucets, dining furniture, and more. The desk products appear in the first product row and then disappear entirely. Wayfair's brand history makes this pattern impossible to ignore: "WALL ART you'll love!!" failed the same test twice in one day on June 9, and now desks get the same treatment.
- The hero does its job — "70% OFF" is large, bold, and immediately visible. Individual product tiles show percent-off badges and star ratings, which gives the body some credibility. But the CTA is "shop all sales," not "shop desks," which completely surrenders the specificity the subject line earned. A subscriber who clicked because they need a desk is immediately deposited into a generic sale browse. That friction is avoidable and costly.
- Overall: a competent, high-volume promotional send that trades short-term subject-line clickbait for long-term trust erosion. Fine by the numbers; lazy by design.

## What's working

- **70% OFF hero is unmissable** — large, high-contrast, immediately anchors the value proposition above the fold.
- **Product tile badges** — every visible product carries a percentage-off label, reinforcing the sale narrative throughout.
- **Star ratings visible** on product tiles — social proof present without any extra copy needed.
- **"HOT SUMMER SALE" nav bar** — ties this send to the seasonal campaign, giving context and urgency at a glance.
- **Wayfair Rewards surfaced in footer** — loyalty touchpoint is present for members without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- **Subject promises desks; the email delivers a department store.** After the first product row, rugs, faucets, kitchen items, and dining sets dominate — desks are gone. Classic Wayfair subject-body bait, documented in the June 9 wall art audits.
- **"shop all sales" CTA abandons the desk hook entirely.** No "shop desks" button, no desk-specific landing page — the specificity investment in the subject is thrown away at the click.
- **Visual hierarchy collapses below the fold.** The hero is clean; below it, a wall of product grids with no editorial spine or category dividers. It reads as a data feed, not a curated send.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email.** "HOT SUMMER SALE" implies urgency but no end date, countdown, or "ends Sunday" copy is visible to close the loop.
- **"YOURS 🫵" personalization is hollow.** The pointing-finger emoji next to "IS YOURS" is a cosmetic gesture, not segmentation — nothing in the body confirms this is actually personalized to a desk-shopping customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the desk promise in the body.** Lead with a desk-only hero module — 3–4 specific desk picks with real product names, dimensions, and prices. Then expand to the broader sale *below* that section. The subject line earns trust; the body needs to honor it for at least one full scroll.
- 2. **Replace "shop all sales" with "shop desks on sale."** One word change routes clicked intent to the right category and converts the visit instead of dropping the user into browse mode.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends June 15" anywhere above the fold creates real urgency. The brand history shows Wayfair never does this — it's a consistent gap.
- 4. **Add editorial dividers between categories.** Label sections ("Desks & Home Office," "Rugs," "Kitchen") so the product dump feels curated rather than algorithmically vomited.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your desk deal: up to 70% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Desks from $X — Summer Sale, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Home office picks handpicked for you — free shipping over $35`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday: desks, chairs, and storage up to 70% off`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**3a. `word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name` (mobile)**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
}
```
`break-all` splits at any character, including mid-word. This can produce visually broken product names (e.g., "Mid-Centu\nry Desk"). The correct value for English content is `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word`.

**3b. Fixed pixel heights on mobile product cells**
```css
.ProductRec-name { height: 48px !important }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; ... }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; ... }
```
Hard-coded heights clip dynamic content. If product names or prices exceed the allotted box (e.g., long names at 13px, discount copy wrapping), text is hidden with no ellipsis — a silent truncation failure.

**3c. No `alt` attributes visible**
Image tags are outside the visible source window. Cannot confirm alt text presence. Flag for manual check — CAN-SPAM and accessibility best practice require descriptive `alt` on promotional images.

**3d. XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>
```
This is industry-standard for email and expected by Outlook. Not an issue.

**3e. Gmail / Outlook client hacks — correctly applied**
- `u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` — Gmail app dark mode fix, correct usage.
- `u + .Gmail .u-P--5` — Gmail-specific padding override, correct usage.
- `body[data-outlook-cycle]` — New Outlook stacking fix, correct usage.
