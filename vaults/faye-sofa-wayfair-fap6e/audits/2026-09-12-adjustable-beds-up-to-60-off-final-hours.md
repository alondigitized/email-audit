# ADJUSTABLE BEDS 🚩 UP TO 60% OFF 🚩 FINAL HOURS 🚩 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the cleanest render Wayfair has produced all week — no blank white cold open, no broken-image icon, the 60% OFF banner and the actual adjustable bed hero tile both load correctly right at the top. Compare that to 2026-09-12's "IF YOU WERE AN AREA RUG" (5.00/10) or 2026-09-11's "Our best gazebo deals" (4.00/10), both of which opened on a broken placeholder — this send finally executes the basics.
- The subject is a screaming, flag-emoji-bracketed, ALL-CAPS wall ("ADJUSTABLE BEDS 🚩 UP TO 60% OFF 🚩 FINAL HOURS 🚩") that reads more like a Facebook Marketplace listing than a retail newsletter, and the body doesn't fully deliver on it — the hero banner just says generic "WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT," and the grid underneath is a scattershot of grills, bathroom scales, ellipticals, and dressers with only the very first tile actually being an adjustable bed.
- Net: good render, weak targeting discipline. The one thing this brand's emails keep getting right is loud percentage-off banners; the thing they keep getting wrong is subject lines that overpromise a specific category and then bury it in an unrelated grid.

## What's working

- Full render with zero broken images or layout bugs — a real departure from this week's pattern of blank cold opens.
- The adjustable bed from the subject line is the actual first product shown, with a visible strikethrough price ($675.99 → $329.99) and a "Sale" tag.
- The purple 60% OFF banner is the clear visual anchor at the very top, above the fold.
- Star ratings are shown under every product tile, adding a layer of social proof.
- Secondary category bands (mattresses, bedding, area rugs, grills) give shoppers other entry points if beds aren't the draw.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "ADJUSTABLE BEDS" specifically, but the hero banner and 7 of 8 grid tiles have nothing to do with beds (grill, scale, elliptical, dresser, sink, chair, storage).
- Subject is ALL CAPS with three repeated 🚩 emoji — a textbook spam-filter trigger and a jarring inbox impression.
- "picked just for you" personalization copy sits above a grid that's clearly generic, undermining the promise.
- No dedicated adjustable-bed section or callout below the hero — it appears once and is never reinforced.
- Cadence problem: this is at least the sixth similar % -off blast from this brand this week (bidets, gazebos, pergolas, bedding, rugs, now beds).

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to drop the flag emoji and ALL CAPS, and make the hero banner actually say "Adjustable Beds" so subject and hero match — right now the promise is only kept by luck of tile order.
- 2. Rebuild the "picked just for you" grid so beds/mattresses dominate the first two rows, with the mixed-category grid moved further down as a secondary section.
- 3. Give the CTA button more specific copy than "SHOP NOW" — e.g. "Shop Adjustable Beds" — so it's obvious where the click leads.
- 4. Reduce blast frequency or vary the offer mechanic (bundle, free delivery) so this doesn't read as the same 60%-off template as the last five sends.

Standard Wayfair ESP template (Bee/Chamaileon-style responsive MJML-derived markup) with heavy tracking-pixel usage; automated QA passed 57% with 0 hard failures and 3 warning categories — mostly accessibility (missing alt text) and mixed-content image sources.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- All 12 probed material links carry consistent tracking params (`_emr`, `wfcs`, `_eml`, `source=batch`, `batchid`, `varid`, `csnid`, `brcid`, `skurectxid`, `event_rec_txid`, `refid=MKTEML_139890`, `emlid`, `maiid`) — internally consistent, no mismatched IDs observed.
- 6 of 12 probed links returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited) during automated probing, including the primary CTA "Get Exclusive Deals on the App" (`wayfairapp.onelink.me`) and "Shop Sale" (`wayfair.com/daily-sales`). QA flags these as inconclusive, not confirmed broken — recommend manual re-verification since 429s can mask genuine link rot.

- 20 open/impression-tracking pixels (`li.wayfair.com/imp?s=124126000` through `...019`) are served over **http://**, not https — see Rendering section.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 20+ `<img>` elements missing `alt` text, including all review-star rating images (`stars_9.png`, `stars_10.png`, `stars_11.png`), the main hero banner (`0909_warehouseclearout_skinny-endstonight.png`), the Affirm financing banner, and all 20 impression pixels (`li.wayfair.com/imp?...`). Screen readers will announce nothing or fall back to filename for these.
- 20 impression-tracking pixels load via `http://` (not `https://`) — e.g. `http://li.wayfair.com/imp?s=124126000&sz=2x1&m=false&p=8d7a85bb-7110-470c-a057-8c2546b42d19`. Mixed-content images are commonly blocked or flagged by mail clients (notably Outlook/Gmail proxy), which would silently drop these tracking beacons.
- Custom `@font-face` ("Sofia") declared with only a `.woff` source and `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback — no `.woff2`/system-font stack redundancy, but this is standard for Outlook-safe email CSS and not a defect.
- Responsive breakpoints use `@media (max-width: 480px)` and `(max-width: 320px)` with `!important` overrides — consistent with a maintained ESP template; no structural issues found in the truncated source.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No unresolved merge tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) or broken personalization fields found in the visible HTML or subject line. Subject line uses static flag emoji/copy, not personalization. No issues found.

- **List-Unsubscribe-Post** (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not detected — same caveat.
- **Authentication-Results** header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC outcome) not detected — again flagged as possibly a relay capture limitation, not necessarily a sender misconfiguration.
- An unsubscribe link is confirmed present in-body per QA note ("an unsubscribe link is always included"), so footer-level CAN-SPAM opt-out appears intact even though header-level one-click support is unverified.
- **Recommendation:** these three header warnings should be re-checked against raw MIME source (not the relay-normalized copy) before concluding non-compliance, since the QA tool explicitly flags relay-capture as the likely cause.
