# Open Box steals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- "Open Box steals" is Wayfair's most creative subject in the recent cadence — a welcome break from the category-cycling drip (dressers, desks, kitchen mats) that's dominated the last two weeks. The concept is genuinely compelling: open box inventory implies real discounts with urgency baked in. But the execution fumbles the landing. Instead of showcasing specific open box items with before/after pricing, the email delivers a generic 17-row category list that could have been slapped onto any Wayfair sale email sent this month.
- The hero is clean — lavender background, bold "open box deals" in dark purple, a small circular image of a green dutch oven — but the single product tease goes nowhere. There's no connection between that dutch oven and any row in the list below, and no specific product is ever surfaced. The entire email collapses into "SHOP ALL SALES," the same CTA the HOT SUMMER SALE email used on June 11.
- This is a good concept dressed in an existing template. The subject earns the open; the body doesn't earn the click.

## What's working

- Subject "Open Box steals" is the sharpest, most category-distinct hook Wayfair has sent since the June 10 tabletop editorial — it breaks the recent repetitive pattern.
- Hero typography is clean and instantly legible: "open box / deals" in oversize bold type lands the concept in under a second.
- Every category row pairs a name with a concrete percentage ("up to 70% OFF"), so there's a real number visible throughout the scroll.
- No render bugs — layout is tight, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- Wayfair Rewards callout in the footer rewards members with a brand benefit signal.

## What's weak

- 17 undifferentiated category rows with no hierarchy create decision paralysis — the eye has nothing to anchor on after the hero.
- One global "SHOP ALL SALES" CTA means every row funnels to the same destination; no category gets its own link.
- No urgency or inventory signal anywhere — "open box" inherently implies limited stock, but that tension is completely absent.
- The dutch oven in the hero is orphaned — it's the only product shown and never appears again, making it feel like a prop rather than a featured item.
- Subject says "steals," hero copy says "deals" — small inconsistency that undercuts the sharpness of the concept.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface 3-4 actual open box products with before/after pricing** — a $299 marked-down-to-$179 bar stool beats "Bar Stools | up to 70% OFF" every time. This single change would transform the email from a category directory into a deal showcase.
- 2. **Add an inventory scarcity signal** — "Limited quantities — once they're gone, they're gone" in the hero or as a banner below the CTA. Open box is the one Wayfair concept where urgency is 100% credible and should be exploited.
- 3. **Give the top 3 categories their own CTA buttons** (e.g., "Shop Area Rugs" at 70%, "Shop Bar Stools" at 70%) rather than one catch-all button. Cut the category list from 17 to 6.
- 4. **Make the hero image work harder** — swap the small circular dutch oven for a full-width lifestyle shot of an open box item with a price tag, or a split "was $X / now $Y" treatment.
- 5. **Reconcile "steals" vs "deals"** — pick one word and use it consistently across subject, preheader, and hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box: up to 70% off (limited stock)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `These open box prices won't last long`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real discounts on real inventory — bar stools, rugs, beds and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Once they're gone, they're gone — open box steals inside`
