# All under $29.99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- "All under $29.99" is the cleanest Wayfair send in this recent run — the $29.99 price cap is a genuine unifying concept, not a subject-line costume over an unrelated product dump. The hero lands the Memorial Day Clearance message immediately, and the section-by-section layout (outdoor → kitchen → vintage → storage → charm → deals of the day) follows a loose logic that holds up reasonably well as you scroll.
- The problem is cadence. This is the seventh Wayfair email in four days. The 2026-05-14 "backyard = hottest table in town" send was the gold standard — tight seasonal theme, promo code surfaced above the fold, specific category flow — and this email lacks all three of those differentiators. A price-cap email without a percentage-off anchor or a visible code reads as clearance-dump rather than curated value. The "deals of the day" blue armchair is a good specific product feature, but it's buried below five preceding sections.
- The email works as a volume play. It doesn't work as a targeted, feel-like-it-was-made-for-me send.

## What's working

- **Price cap is unambiguous.** "All under $29.99" in the hero sets clear expectations — no bait-and-switch risk, no buried fine print visible in the render.
- **Section-specific CTAs.** "Shop limited outdoor styles," "Shop chef-worthy picks," etc. beat the generic "Shop Now" and pull the click toward a relevant category rather than just the homepage.
- **Memorial Day hook is explicit.** "MEMORIAL DAY CLEARANCE" in the hero banner ties the send to the current moment — an improvement over the 2026-05-15 "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR at a great price" send that wore its subject line as a costume with no seasonal follow-through inside.
- **"Deals of the day" anchor.** The blue upholstered armchair featured as a specific, hero-sized product in the deals section is the one moment in the email that feels curated rather than dumped.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is eroding open intent.** Seven Wayfair emails in four days. Even a clean send gets skipped when the subscriber has trained themselves to ignore the sender.
- **No promo code.** The 5/14 "backyard = hottest table in town" send had `OUTDOOR10` surfaced at the top. This email has only the price cap — no additional incentive to click now vs. later.
- **Broad category spread dilutes the price story.** Outdoor chairs, kitchen cutting boards, vintage pots, storage bins, cheerful pillows, and a doormat are not a coherent value narrative. The $29.99 unifier helps but doesn't close the gap.
- **No visible deadline.** "Memorial Day Clearance" implies time pressure, but no explicit end date is shown. The urgency is implied, not stated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a countdown or explicit end date to the hero.** "Ends Monday" or "48 hours left" converts the implied Memorial Day urgency into a real reason to click today. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Surface a promo code.** The 5/14 email proved it works — bring back a code (e.g., `SAVE30`) to close the "why now" gap and give subscribers something to share or remember.
- 3. **Cut to three sections, not seven.** Pick outdoor, kitchen, and one wildcard. A tighter curation makes the "under $29.99" story feel like a gift guide, not a warehouse clearance rack.
- 4. **Let the "deals of the day" armchair lead.** A specific, photo-sized product with a visible price is more click-worthy than the abstract category tiles above it. Hero the deal; let the categories support it.
- 5. **Throttle send frequency.** Wayfair is cannibalizing its own open rates. Consolidate these daily sends into three per week maximum through the Memorial Day window.

- **Subject Alt B:** `29 things under $29.99 for Memorial Day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor chairs, kitchen finds, and more — all clearance priced for the long weekend`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Memorial Day deals end Monday. Here's what's still in stock.`
