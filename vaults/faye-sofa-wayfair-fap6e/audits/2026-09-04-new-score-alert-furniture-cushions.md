- The layout itself is also broken: there's a large blank white gap near the bottom of the email with a single stray blue dot floating in it, right before the footer icons. That's a visible render bug on top of the subject mismatch.
- Given the brand history of near-identical bait-and-switch sends this week, and a worse execution than the prior "cushions" email (5.00/10) because it drops the labeled product tile and adds a layout gap, this lands below that recent score.

## What's working

- The Labor Day 70% off banner is bold, high-contrast, and impossible to miss at the very top.
- Secondary CTAs ("Shop Sale," "New Arrivals") are clearly placed in the header for browsers who bail on the main narrative.
- The "cool down... on any budget" AC section has a clean hero image, clear category labels (window vs. portable), and matches late-summer seasonality.
- Footer loyalty/benefit icons (Rewards, Financing, Professional, Exclusive Brands) are visible and consistent with prior Wayfair sends.

## What's weak

- No section anywhere in the render is labeled "cushions" or shows a recognizable cushion product — the subject line is unsupported by the body.
- The email opens on doormats, not cushions, immediately breaking the subject's promise within the first scroll.
- There's a large blank white block with a stray blue dot near the bottom of the email — a visible layout/render bug.
- The body is an undifferentiated grid of 15+ unrelated categories (pergolas, sofas, fountains, gazebos, cabinets, wall art, recliners, playhouses) with no visual hierarchy pointing back to the subject's promise.
- Purple interstitial banners ("but wait, there's more...", "these," "SEE WHY") add clutter without connecting to any specific product story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject-to-hero mismatch first — either lead the hero banner with an actual labeled "Furniture Cushions" tile and price, or change the subject to match what's actually featured (doormats, since that's the first real content block).
- 2. Fix the blank white gap with the floating blue dot near the footer — that's a broken render any recipient would notice as a bug, not a stylistic quirk.
- 3. Cut the grid from 15+ categories down to 4-6 tied to a single theme (either all outdoor/patio or all "cushions & soft goods") so the eye has somewhere to land.
- 4. Drop the vague purple interstitials ("but wait, there's more...", "these") — they add scroll fatigue without adding information.
