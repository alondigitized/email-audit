# TUB FAUCETS ⭕ UP TO 70% OFF ⭕ ENDS TONIGHT ⭕ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair runs the same play it ran on BAKING SHEETS (5/10, July 4) and PATIO BAR STOOLS (7/10, July 6): lead the subject with a specific product noun — "TUB FAUCETS" — attach a credible 70% off claim, then deliver a generic multi-category discount grid with zero tub faucets anywhere in the body. The body opens with "picked just for you," shows area rugs, living room seating, bedding, accessories, flash deals on bunk beds and more rugs, and a product image grid of outdoor furniture and kitchen mats. A tub faucet is not visible anywhere from top to bottom.
- The urgency machinery is intact — "ENDS TONIGHT" appears in both the subject and the hero banner — and the email renders cleanly with Wayfair's standard layout. But that's table stakes. The core problem is a broken promise: anyone who opens for a tub faucet deal finds no such thing, and anyone who doesn't want area rugs (again) bounces immediately. This is the fourth or fifth consecutive send where the subject noun and the body content are completely disconnected.
- Verdict: competent execution of a fundamentally dishonest creative strategy. The 70% off banner and flash deals section do real work, but Wayfair is training its list to ignore its own subject lines.

## What's working

- **Urgency is consistent end-to-end** — "ENDS TONIGHT" appears in the subject, the top banner, and the flash deals section heading; the clock feels real.
- **70% off claim is prominent** — the yellow banner at the top of the body makes the offer instantly scannable without hunting.
- **Flash deals section adds genuine specificity** — a "24 HR ONLY" badge with distinct product tiles (bunk bed, rug) is a tier above the generic category grid above it.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no visible layout bugs; the multi-column grid holds together at email width.
- **Wayfair Rewards callout at the footer** — loyalty signal is present for members, even if buried.

## What's weak

- **No tub faucets anywhere in the body** — the subject noun is a lie; this is the same bait-and-switch as BAKING SHEETS and PATIO BAR STOOLS.
- **"Picked just for you" is doing zero work** — the products shown (rugs, seating, bedding) are indistinguishable from the last five Wayfair sends; no plausible personalization is visible.
- **Triple repeating ⭕ emoji in the subject** — looks spammy, not premium; erodes the credibility the urgency copy is trying to build.
- **ALL CAPS subject throughout** — combined with the emoji stack, this reads like a liquidation alert, not a curated deal.
- **Category discount percentages are vague** — "up to 60% OFF" on area rugs, seating, bedding, accessories means nothing without anchor prices; the flash deals section is the only place actual product specificity exists.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a tub faucet in the email.** One hero product tile — a faucet SKU with a from/to price — would convert the bait-and-switch into an honest send. Everything else can stay as editorial context.
- 2. **Drop the triple-emoji stacking** — one ⭕ before "ENDS TONIGHT" is enough; three reads as desperation.
- 3. **Lose ALL CAPS in the middle of the subject** — "Tub Faucets — up to 70% off, ends tonight" is more credible at a glance than the current shout.
- 4. **Replace "picked just for you" with the actual urgency frame** — "Savings end at midnight" or the specific sale name gives the opening section a reason to exist.
- 5. **Show a price anchor in the category tiles** — "from $19" or "was $89, now $44" makes the "up to 60%" claim land; without it, the discount feels abstract.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off tub faucets + more — tonight only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our best bath prices of the summer. Deal ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Faucet styles from $29 — plus rugs, seating, bedding on sale.`
