# 🔴 CLEARANCE ⚪ UP TO 70% OFF 🔵
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's Memorial Day Clearance blast — a high-volume catalog dump that throws every discount they have at the wall. The top two tiles (50% and 70% off) land cleanly with big readable numbers, and the Memorial Day Doorbuster section adds a coherent secondary narrative. But the email loses discipline fast: by the third scroll, you're drowning in a grid of near-identical discount tiles with no editorial spine tying them together.
- The content is real and well-rendered, no broken images or obvious display failures. Wayfair's brand is strong enough to carry a lot of clutter, and clearance events genuinely perform for home goods shoppers. But this is a mass-spray, not a targeted send — the "picked just for you" row is the only nod at personalization, and it competes for attention with a dozen other modules.
- Judgment: competent execution of a lazy strategy. It will convert the already-motivated buyer who came looking for Memorial Day deals. It will do almost nothing to activate the fence-sitter.

## What's working

- **The 70% OFF number is the biggest thing on screen** — it lands immediately and is the right anchor for a clearance event.
- **Memorial Day Doorbuster framing** gives the mid-email section a distinct identity and urgency; the dark background makes it visually pop against the white sections above and below.
- **"Picked just for you" with visible star ratings** is the one personalization signal in the whole email, and it's placed early enough to feel intentional.
- **No render failures** — every product image loaded, text isn't overlapping, layout holds together at this render width.
- **Free delivery threshold ($35)** is called out at the bottom, which reduces a common checkout objection.

## What's weak

- **The subject line is pure spam bait** — ALL CAPS plus three flag emojis plus a percentage figure stacks every cargo-cult urgency trick at once; spam filters and jaded subscribers see through this immediately.
- **Too many competing CTAs** — "savings >", "unique furniture >", "shop all sales", app download, social links — there's no primary call-to-action, just a buffet of equal-weight links.
- **Visual hierarchy collapses below the fold** — after the hero tiles, every section is roughly the same height, weight, and color treatment, producing visual monotony.
- **No credible deadline** — "Memorial Day" implies urgency but no explicit end date is visible in the body tiles; the small banner text at top is easy to miss.
- **"Buy one, get 15% off" feels out of place** — a BOGO-adjacent offer sandwiched between 50% and 70% off banners looks weak and dilutes the clearance narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the flag emojis and ALL CAPS in the subject** — replace with a single specific hook (see alts below). This is the single highest-leverage change; the current line is aggressively filter-bait.
- 2. **Surface one explicit end date in the hero** — "Ends Monday" or "Ends 05/26" in the top tile removes ambiguity and adds real urgency without manufactured pressure.
- 3. **Collapse the middle sections** — cut half the doorbuster tiles and replace with 2–3 editorially chosen products from the "picked for you" pool; let the personalization engine do its job.
- 4. **Rename the CTAs to specific verb + noun** — "Shop Bedroom Clearance" beats "savings >" every time. Pick the top 3 categories and make each CTA earn its click.
- 5. **Remove the "buy one, get 15% off" module** — it undercuts the clearance energy; if it must stay, move it below the fold as a secondary offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off: Memorial Day Clearance starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance weekend — 70% off ends Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, outdoor — everything's marked down before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Doorbuster deals inside. Free delivery on orders $35+.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Fixed-height containers on mobile — confirmed issue:**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { height: 48px !important }  /* + word-break: break-all */
  .ProductRec-price { height: 40px; vertical-align: top }
  .ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; vertical-align: top }
  .ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }
}
```

These hard pixel heights will clip product names, prices, or copy if the text is longer than the box or if system font scaling is active (accessibility/large-text users). `word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name` also breaks mid-word regardless of whitespace, which degrades legibility.

**Font loading — minor issue:**

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```

Only `.woff` is declared — no `.woff2` source. Modern clients will download the larger .woff file unnecessarily. `.woff2` should be listed first with `.woff` as fallback. This is a performance issue, not a rendering failure.

**CSS block size:** The `<head>` CSS block is very large. Gmail clips messages at ~102 KB total; if the full email approaches that threshold, the CSS block will be partially dropped and mobile layout will break.
