#  ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ UP TO 70% OFF ⭐ ⭐ ⭐
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's laziest subject line of the streak: "⭐ ⭐ ⭐ UP TO 70% OFF ⭐ ⭐ ⭐" — five stars and a percent figure, nothing else. The niche-noun bait they've been running (BIRD BATHS, OTTOMANS, OUTDOOR RUGS) is at least memorable; this is a warehouse-clearance flyer masquerading as a curated email. Inside, the creative is a dense product grid with paired discount badges (40% OFF, 50% OFF, 70% OFF) scattered across bedroom, outdoor, and home categories — no single hero product, no anchoring story.
- The free & easy delivery threshold ("over $35") and the Wayfair Rewards loyalty callout appear mid-scroll, which is a functional signal, but everything above the fold is a compressed montage of product tiles that blurs together. The Fourth of July sale framing is at least seasonally appropriate, but it's buried in a header bar rather than featured as the visual centerpiece.
- Compared to the June 28 "LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99" send — which at least landed a concrete $99 anchor — this email gives you nothing to grab onto. It's a generic percent-off blast that could have come from any furniture retailer on any day of the year.

## What's working

- **Fourth of July / summer clearance context** is visible in the top header bar, giving the discount pile a seasonal reason-to-exist.
- **Multiple discount tiers** (40%, 50%, 70% OFF badges) are legible on individual product tiles — the price logic is clear even at thumbnail scale.
- **Free delivery threshold** ("free & easy delivery over $35") is surfaced mid-email as a friction-reducer.
- **App download CTA** at the bottom adds a secondary conversion path for high-intent browsers.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is content-free** — five star emojis plus "UP TO 70% OFF" tells you nothing about what's on sale; it reads like spam bait.
- **No hero product or category** — the layout opens immediately into a multi-column grid with no single anchor image, so the eye has nowhere to land.
- **"UP TO" framing undercuts itself** — the highest discount is the least likely to apply to anything the recipient actually wants; it's the oldest trick in retail and most subscribers know it.
- **Loyalty/Rewards mention is generic** — "earn $1 toward every $40 spent" is buried mid-scroll with no personalized balance or tier callout.
- **Dense tile compression** makes this indistinguishable from the prior five Wayfair sends — same badge style, same grid, same purple CTA buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the subject line entirely.** "UP TO 70% OFF" with star emojis is the single biggest drag on this email. Pick a concrete category or price anchor the way the June 28 rug send did.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fourth of July: 70% off bedroom & outdoor — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 4th of July deal: beds, sofas, rugs up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free delivery over $35 + earn Rewards on every order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bedroom, outdoor, living room — pick your deal before it ends`
- 2. **Add a single hero block above the grid.** One full-width image of the single deepest-discounted item (say, the 70%-off item visible in the lower grid) with its price, the crossed-out original, and a direct CTA. The grid can follow below.
- 3. **Make the deadline credible and visible.** "Fourth of July Clearance" implies July 4th is the end date — show it. A countdown or explicit "ends July 4" badge near the top would convert the vague urgency into something actionable.
- 4. **Personalize the Rewards callout.** "You have $X in Rewards" beats "earn $1 per $40" for any logged-in member — it turns a passive loyalty mention into a spend trigger.
- 5. **Reduce star emoji count.** Three stars on each side reads as spam-signaling. One star or none, and let the offer speak.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Font format gap**: `@font-face` loads only `.woff`. No `.woff2` (25–30% smaller, supported by all modern clients) and no `.eot` (Outlook desktop). Fallback `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` is present, so Outlook degrades gracefully, but modern Apple Mail / iOS Mail clients will download a larger file unnecessarily.

- **Fixed-pixel heights on mobile content containers** (confirmed in CSS):
  - `.ProductRec-name`: `height: 48px !important` with `word-break: break-all` — long product names will be clipped; `break-all` also produces mid-word breaks that damage readability.
  - `.ProductRec-stars`, `.ProductRec-shippingCopy`: `height: 16px` — if star images fail to load, the row collapses to 16px with no visible fallback.
  - `.ProductRec-price`: `height: 40px`, `.ProductRec-salesCopy`: `height: 28px` — dynamic pricing/copy that exceeds these heights will be clipped on mobile.

- **Single responsive breakpoint**: Only `480px` and `320px` media queries are defined. No tablet breakpoint (600–768px range). On iPad/Android tablets the email renders at desktop width, which may produce very small tap targets.

- **No `alt` text visibility** in truncated source — cannot confirm images have `alt` attributes. Required for clients with images-off default (Outlook, corporate Gmail).

**No issues found:** Gmail (`u + .Gmail`) and Outlook (`body[data-outlook-cycle]`) client hacks are present and syntactically correct.
