# POTTING BENCHES & TABLES 🟥 MEMORIAL DAY ⬜  DEALS, DEALS, DEALS 🟦 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair hits the same wall for the third time this Memorial Day run. The subject promises "POTTING BENCHES & TABLES" — a specific, shoppable category — and the email delivers outdoor furniture, bedroom furniture, curtains, and grills. Zero potting benches. Not one. This is the exact same bait executed on May 21 ("POTTING BENCHES & TABLES at a great price." → patio hero) and May 23 ("KNIFE SETS 🙌 UP TO 70% OFF" → zero knives). At this point it reads less like a mistake and more like a systemic process failure where subject lines and content templates are assembled independently.
- The body itself is competent but generic — the "picked just for you" framing, the four-category tile row, the doorbuster section, the personalized picks grid. It's the identical Memorial Day clearance scaffold Wayfair has used all week. The `free & easy delivery over $35` callout and the doorbuster CTA are the strongest in-body execution moments; everything else blends into the blur of prior sends.
- The verdict: a passable clearance email attached to a lying subject line. That's not a design problem, it's a trust problem.

## What's working

- **Delivery offer is prominent.** "Free & easy delivery over $35" with a bold "shop all sales" CTA appears mid-scroll and is the clearest action in the email.
- **Category tiles are scannable.** The four-tile row (outdoor furniture, bedroom furniture, curtains & drapes, grills & more) gives a fast visual menu — useful for a subscriber still deciding what to shop.
- **"Doorbuster deals" framing adds urgency.** "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals" is a distinct value tier that breaks the page monotony.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum — email displays without layout issues.

## What's weak

- **Zero potting benches or tables in the email.** The subject's entire category hook evaporates on open. A subscriber who clicked expecting garden furniture will bounce immediately.
- **"DEALS, DEALS, DEALS" in the subject is a spam-filter magnet and looks cheap.** Three repeated words is a textbook spam signal and undercuts the brand.
- **"Picked just for you" is unearned.** Outdoor furniture, bedroom furniture, curtains, and grills in one swipe isn't personalization — it's the entire home catalogue.
- **No potting/garden product visible anywhere, even in the "Your Home, These Picks" grid.** If the subject was intentional A/B targeting, the email segment wasn't matched to the creative.
- **Fifth Memorial Day send in three days.** Cadence fatigue is real; this send has no differentiator from Monday's or Tuesday's clearance dump.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the content — or match the content to the subject.** Either drop the potting bench hook entirely and write an honest subject, or build an actual potting bench / garden furniture module and wire it to the subject. The bait-switch is eroding trust send by send.
- 2. **Kill "DEALS, DEALS, DEALS."** Replace with the actual discount figure or a specific deadline. "Save up to 65% — sale ends Monday" does more work and doesn't look like a phishing attempt.
- 3. **Add a garden/outdoor category module** if you're going to keep targeting this audience. Even a single product tile tied to the subject promise would redeem the open.
- 4. **Give the "Your Home, These Picks" grid a reason to exist.** Show the discount on each tile, not just an image and a title.
- 5. **Throttle the Memorial Day cadence.** Five sends in three days with no meaningful differentiation trains subscribers to auto-delete.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 65% off potting benches, tables & outdoor — sale ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Potting benches from $XX — Memorial Day pricing, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free delivery on orders over $35 — shop the full outdoor clearance now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Garden furniture, patio tables & more — deepest discounts of the year`
