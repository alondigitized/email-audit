# Popular Picks
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- This is another Wayfair catch-all, and it is the least focused of the week. The subject "Popular Picks" promises a curated list. The render opens with a purple "the must-shop steals" banner, and "Popular Picks »" is just the first tile in a 2x2 grid. That grid also repeats "On sale now: pergolas," which was the subject of the 11-13 send. There is no single hero offer. The email is a long scroll of category tiles with no price or percent-off in the top third.
- The only concrete offer is "get 5% rewards... on every item, always," and that is a standing member perk. The real deal, "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" with the Sealy badge, is buried near the bottom. Below the first screen the email runs through "fresh-picked favorites," a shed block ("total sellouts"), and about ten more category tiles. Someone who isn't already shopping for a shed or a bench has no reason to keep scrolling.
- Verdict: a competent template with no point of view. It would open on brand recognition alone and get a weak click. It sits at the bottom of the 5-6 range.

## What's working

- The Wayfair wordmark and purple banner make the sender instantly recognizable.
- The Rewards block ("get 5% rewards... on every item, always") is the one clear member benefit, with a visible "Take a look →" link.
- Star ratings under the "fresh-picked favorites" products add some social proof.
- The shed block ("total sellouts" with a five-star badge) is the strongest single hero in the email, with a clear "Shop store-it-all sheds" button.
- The footer is clean, with an app-download module and a QR code.

## What's weak

- No hero offer appears above the fold. Nothing in the first screen shows a price, a percent off, or a deadline.
- "Popular Picks" is only one tile among many. The subject over-promises, as in the 11-14 "TABLE LAMPS" and "Area Rugs" sends.
- The "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" banner is at the very bottom, where almost nobody will see it. It should be the lead.
- The pergolas and gazebos repeat from earlier in the week (11-12 "Pergolas for less," 11-13 "On sale now: pergolas"), so cadence feels stale.
- The grids mix very different categories (kitchen sinks, Christmas trees, desk, shoe racks) with no theme or reason to click one over another.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" banner to the top and make it the hero, with a single button such as "Shop Black Friday deals."
- 2. Make the subject and the hero match. If the subject is "Popular Picks," the top of the email should be a single bestseller grid with prices.
- 3. Cut the tile count to six or eight and group them by theme, such as hosting, bedroom or outdoor.
- 4. Add prices or "from $X" labels to the tiles. Only a few currently show one ("Under $30," "Under $25," "Under $199").
- 5. Retire pergolas for a week and rotate in fresher categories.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Early Black Friday: up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our top-rated picks, from $30`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bestsellers in bedding, rugs and decor, plus 5% back on every order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sheds, sinks and trees are selling out. See what's still in stock.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has no confirmed link breakage, but it has widespread missing alt text, 20 non-HTTPS tracking pixels, and unverifiable compliance headers. The headers are likely a relay capture gap rather than a Wayfair defect.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **429 responses are probe artifacts, not broken links.** 8 links returned 429 (e.g. `https://www.wayfair.com/daily-sales?_emr=…&sltid=2`, `…/sheds-c89567.html`, `…/popular-picks-se49538.html?eid=356237`, the `wayfairapp.onelink.me/2420802157` app link ×2). Wayfair rate-limited the checker, so these are neither pass nor fail.
- **Coverage is partial.** 12 links were probed and 60 skipped. 4 more were unprobed after the 70s budget:
  - `…/on-sale-now%3a-pergolas--se82161.htm`
  - `…/gazebos-%26-pergolas-from-%24100-se8…`
  - `…/kitchen-mats-under-%2430-se10022.htm`
  - `…/wayfair-rewards?…`
  - The percent-encoded `%3a`, `%26` and `%24` in these URLs are worth a manual check.
- **20 non-HTTPS impression pixels.** `http://li.wayfair.com/imp?s=124126000…124126019&sz=2x1&m=false&p=23908a14-…` (20 pixels, sequential `s=` values) uses plain HTTP. QA flags "Non-HTTPS source may be blocked". Clients that block mixed content will drop these, and impression counts will be undercounted.
- **Open pixel.** `https://t.wayfair.com/b.php?x=…` is HTTPS but has no alt text. It is not a defect on its own, but it is a separate tracker from the 20 `li.wayfair.com` pixels.
- **Non-HTTPS app fallback.** The onelink URL carries `af_web_dp=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.wayfair.com%2Fthe-wayfair-app`, so desktop users get an HTTP URL first and depend on a redirect.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Unconfirmed, not failing.** `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found, and `Authentication-Results` was missing. QA notes that the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so SPF, DKIM, DMARC and one-click unsubscribe cannot be assessed from this data. Do not treat them as Wayfair failures without the raw headers.
- **Footer content is unverified.** The unsubscribe link is always probed per QA, but no result is listed. The HTML was truncated before the footer, so the unsubscribe link and the physical postal address could not be verified.
- The From address is `editor@members.wayfair.com`, a subdomain consistent with the brand. No mismatch was observed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No `utm_*` parameters on any observed link.** Wayfair uses proprietary params instead: `source=graymatter_batch`, `batchid=15`, `varid=5`, `csnid=e15faabd-…`, `sltid`, `refid=MKTEML_121690`, `emlid=101`, `maiid=16755`, `mdlid`. Standard analytics will not attribute these as campaign traffic unless Wayfair maps them. This is a limitation for outside auditors, not a defect.
- **Header banner and text link go to the homepage.** `wfus_batchheaderrevamp_event_top_3_purple` and the "Wayfair" logo link to `https://www.wayfair.com/?…sltid=5`, not the event page.
- **"Popular Picks" lands on the event.** It links to `/daily-sales/filters/popular-picks-se49538.html?eid=356237&unifiedeventid=356237`. This matches the subject, and the URL was rate-limited, not failed.
- **Duplicate destinations.** Several tiles share `/daily-sales` (`sltid=2` and `sltid=4`) with different `_eml`, which is fine for per-slot tracking.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve the `li.wayfair.com/imp` pixels over HTTPS. This also fixes the HTTP `af_web_dp` fallback in the onelink URL.
2. Add descriptive alt text to every linked image (`blankBanner`, the header banner, the sheds tile, the doors tile). Add `alt=""` to the pixels and to purely decorative images.
3. Give the star-rating images alt text such as "4.5 out of 5 stars", and put the loyalty disclaimer in live text or give its image alt.
4. Re-probe the 8 rate-limited links and the 4 unprobed links with throttling or a longer budget before calling them valid.
5. Capture raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from a direct send, not the relay, to settle the compliance items.
6. Check that the production template does not reference `_test` asset names.
7. Treat the 57% pass rate with caution. It is driven mostly by alt-text warnings on tracking pixels, which inflate the count.
