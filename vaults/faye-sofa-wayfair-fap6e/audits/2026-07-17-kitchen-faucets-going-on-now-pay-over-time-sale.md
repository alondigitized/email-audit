# KITCHEN FAUCETS 🚩 Going on NOW: *PAY-OVER-TIME SALE* 🚩 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This email reprises Wayfair's pay-over-time Affirm play — the same pitch the brand ran on July 15 with "🔔 PAY-OVER-TIME SALE WITH AFFIRM 🔔" — but this time lashed to "KITCHEN FAUCETS" as a category hook. That's a marginal structural improvement: the July 15 Affirm send floated free of any specific product, while this one puts actual kitchen faucet images in the hero. The body still collapses into the full Wayfair catalog within two scroll-lengths: area rugs at 70% off, curtains at 60% off, outdoor fireplaces at 48%, entryway furniture at 44%. None of those are kitchen plumbing.
- The 🚩🚩 emoji choice is quietly disqualifying. Red flag emojis carry internet-culture connotations of "warning sign." In a retail context Wayfair almost certainly means a sale banner or pennant — but savvy subscribers will read double-🚩 as the brand accidentally self-tagging its own bait-and-switch. Combined with ALL CAPS and *asterisks* around PAY-OVER-TIME SALE, the subject reads spam-adjacent before anyone opens it.
- This email cannot escape its cadence context. It arrives the day after the third consecutive "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" send (July 14, 15, and 16). Wayfair's inbox slot is burned — a subscriber who saw three kitchen mats emails in three days and now gets a kitchen faucets email is almost certainly tuning out the sender name entirely, not reading the subject.

## What's working

- Kitchen faucets genuinely appear in the hero product tiles — more honest than the July 14–16 KITCHEN MATS trio, where the body body completely abandoned the subject.
- Affirm 0% APR is surfaced at the very top of the email, giving the pay-over-time claim real visual weight rather than burying it in fine print.
- Render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, clear grid layout throughout.
- Wayfair Rewards, Pay Over Time, and Exclusive Brands get dedicated footer callouts that reinforce brand value props for anyone who scrolls.
- The subject packs a category (kitchen faucets) AND a financial mechanism (pay-over-time) into one line — more information-dense than Wayfair's typical single-noun subject.

## What's weak

- The category deal tiles — rugs 70% off, curtains 60% off, outdoor fireplaces 48%, entryway furniture 44% — snap the kitchen faucets promise in the first content block past the hero.
- Double 🚩 emojis read as internet sarcasm, not a sale flag; they're the first thing a subscriber registers after the sender name.
- The subject is ~54 characters and will truncate mid-phrase on nearly every mobile client — most recipients see "KITCHEN FAUCETS 🚩 Going on NOW: *PAY" and nothing more.
- *Asterisks* around PAY-OVER-TIME SALE look like escaped plain-text formatting or over-engineered emphasis — either way, spam-adjacent.
- Three "KITCHEN MATS under $30" sends in three consecutive days immediately preceding this email mean that Wayfair's credibility as a signal vs. noise sender is at its lowest point of the month.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the subject — pick kitchen faucets OR pay-over-time, not both.** Combining a product category with a payment mechanism splits attention and blows the character budget. Run two separate, tighter sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kitchen faucet deals — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `0% APR on kitchen upgrades with Affirm`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pay over time on faucets, sinks & fixtures. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No interest for 12 months. Your faucet shortlist is waiting.`
- 2. **Replace the category deals block with kitchen/plumbing fixtures.** If the subject says kitchen faucets, every product tile from hero to fold should be faucets, sinks, or fixtures — not rugs and outdoor heaters. The mismatch is the core credibility problem.
- 3. **Kill the 🚩 emojis.** No replacement needed, or swap for 🏷️ if an emoji is required. Red flag emojis are too loaded in current internet culture to deploy unironically in a discount email.
- 4. **Add a real deadline.** "Going on NOW" is noise. "Ends Sunday, July 20 at midnight" gives subscribers a reason to act. Without it the offer reads as permanently available.
- 5. **Force a 48-hour inbox gap after any three-email run.** After the July 14–16 triple "KITCHEN MATS" sends, any Wayfair promo arriving July 17 is radioactive. The cadence damage is the real suppressor of this email's performance, and no subject line fix can overcome it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Web font will not render in most clients.**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff)
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
Web fonts are blocked in Gmail (all platforms), Outlook Windows, Yahoo Mail, and Samsung Mail — the majority of commercial email volume. The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback handles Outlook but is non-standard (should be inside `font-face` descriptor as `mso-generic-font-family`). Confirm all typography degrades acceptably to the system font stack.

**`<head>` `<style>` block stripped by Gmail.**
All responsive rules (`@media`, `.u-FullWidth`, `.u-HalfWidthBlock`, etc.) are in a `<head>` `<style>` block. Gmail web strips `<head>` styles entirely. The Gmail-specific hack (`u + .Gmail`) is present and correct but only patches a narrow subset of rules. Product grid, button padding, and font-size overrides at mobile widths will not apply for Gmail webmail users.

**`word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name`:**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
```
This breaks words at any character boundary, not just word boundaries. Can produce visually broken product names (e.g., "Kitchen Fauce-ts"). Prefer `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word`.

**`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` in media query:**
iOS-only inline image override. Valid client-hack pattern; no issue beyond noting it targets only iOS Safari.

- Color contrast ratios for promotional text
- Table `role="presentation"` on layout tables
