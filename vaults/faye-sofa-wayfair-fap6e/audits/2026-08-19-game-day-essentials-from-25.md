# GAME DAY ESSENTIALS FROM $25 >>>
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This one actually lands. Subject promises "GAME DAY ESSENTIALS FROM $25" and the hero delivers instantly — "tailgate HQ / Shop game-day essentials" with a "picks from $25" badge sitting right on top of a grill, cooler, and folding chair styled outdoors on grass. That's a coherent, on-brief hero, which is rarer than it should be in this brand's recent history — compare to 2026-08-17's "OTTOMANS" send that showed zero ottomans.
- Below the hero, the email holds the tailgate theme all the way down: grills from $50, portable seating from $45, coolers from $50, tailgating games from $20 (cornhole/ping-pong/paddle sets). Every module's product photography actually matches its header. The "trending on social — tailgate grills" module is a nice touch that reinforces relevance rather than distracting from it.
- Where it falls apart is the back half: after "the game-day shop" CTA banner, it dumps into "more styles to scoop up" with pergolas, slipcovers, recliners, lamps, and Adirondack chairs — a generic clearance grab-bag that has nothing to do with game day and dilutes the focus the top two-thirds worked to build. Still, since the hero and the first four content modules do the job, this is a solidly above-average send for this brand.

## What's working

- Hero copy and badge ("tailgate HQ," "picks from $25") directly mirror the subject line's promise and price point.
- Every themed module (grills, seating, coolers, games) shows product photography that actually matches its label — no bait-and-switch.
- Clear price anchors on each section header ($50, $45, $50, $20) give a scannable "what will this cost me" signal.
- Purple CTA pills ("Get cookout-ready," "Sip and chill," "Play, watch, cheer") are playful, on-theme, and readable.
- Consistent purple/white grid layout with no visible render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The bottom third ("more styles to scoop up": pergolas, slipcovers, recliners, lamps, Adirondack chairs) is generic clearance filler that has zero connection to tailgating and dilutes the on-brief momentum built up top.
- "trending on social" module credits "@thedailybrandy" but shows no actual social proof (likes, comments, follower count) — it's just a repurposed product photo with a hashtag-style label.
- No explicit deadline or urgency cue anywhere (no "this weekend," no countdown) despite this being a clearly time-sensitive, event-driven send.
- Top nav bar splits attention with "Shop Sale" and "New Arrivals" links competing with the tailgate theme before the reader even reaches the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or drastically shrink the "more styles to scoop up" section — it undoes the focus the rest of the email built; if Wayfair wants to cross-sell, do it in one small module, not five.
- 2. Add a real urgency cue tied to an actual game/date (e.g., "Order by Thursday for Sunday kickoff") since tailgating is inherently calendar-driven and nothing here signals a deadline.
- 3. Make the "trending on social" module earn its placement with a real proof point (follower count, "10K shares") or drop the social framing entirely.
- 4. Move "coolers" or "grills" — the highest-intent categories for a tailgate shopper — up before "portable seating" to front-load the strongest purchase intent.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grills, coolers, chairs & games — all tailgate-ready`
