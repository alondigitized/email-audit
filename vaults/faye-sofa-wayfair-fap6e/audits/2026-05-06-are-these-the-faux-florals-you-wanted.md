# Are these the FAUX FLORALS you wanted?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a browse-retargeting email from Wayfair centered entirely on faux floral rugs and mats. The subject line mimics a behavioral trigger ("Are these the FAUX FLORALS you wanted?"), implying the recipient browsed this category. The email delivers on that promise structurally — it leads with a strong lifestyle hero shot of an outdoor floral rug, then stacks two product-recommendation grids. The seasonal fit (spring florals) is solid. The weakness is execution: the grids are dense, specific pricing or discounts aren't legibly surfaced in the hero, and CTA copy is too small to confirm specificity. Functionally clean, strategically decent, but the offer doesn't punch hard enough to earn a click on its own.

## What's working

- **Behavioral trigger framing.** "Are these the FAUX FLORALS you wanted?" correctly mimics a browse-abandonment dynamic — it creates the impression the email was assembled around the recipient's actual intent, not a blast list.
- **Hero image.** The lifestyle shot of the outdoor rug on a deck is aspirational and seasonally on-point. Florals on a patio reads immediately as spring.
- **Category consistency.** Every visible product tile matches the stated theme — faux floral rugs and mats. No off-category drift dilutes the message.
- **App promotion placement.** The "Get Exclusive Deals on the App" block is separated cleanly at the bottom, not interrupting the shopping flow.

## What's weak

- **No surfaced price or discount in the hero.** "The must-shop deals" is vague. A reader who doesn't scroll will leave without knowing if there's an actual offer or just curation.
- **Product grid density.** Two stacked grids of small thumbnails create visual noise. Individual products don't get room to breathe, and the "we think you'll ♥ these" module reads as generic recommendation filler rather than curated intent.
- **CTA copy unreadable at reasonable scale.** The button labels under each product tile are too small to confirm they're specific (verb + noun) vs. vague ("Shop Now").
- **Subject line uses gratuitous ALL CAPS.** "FAUX FLORALS" in caps is a mild spam-trigger pattern that some inbox filters and readers interpret negatively. It also softens the behavioral-trigger tone with a shoutier register.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific discount to the hero.** Even "Up to 40% off faux florals" as an overlay or subhead would convert browsers into clickers. Right now the deal is implied, not stated.
- 2. **Reduce the grid to a curated top-3 or top-4.** Fewer, larger product tiles with visible price + rating would outperform 12+ thumbnail tiles where nothing stands out.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to drop ALL CAPS.** Replace "FAUX FLORALS" with title case. Consider adding urgency: "Are these the faux florals you wanted? Deals end soon."
- 4. **Surface a deadline.** If there's a sale end date, make it visible in the hero — "Sale ends Sunday" converts hesitant browsers far better than "must-shop."
- 5. **Test a single hero CTA** ("Shop Faux Floral Rugs") above the fold before the grid, rather than relying on buried product-tile buttons.
