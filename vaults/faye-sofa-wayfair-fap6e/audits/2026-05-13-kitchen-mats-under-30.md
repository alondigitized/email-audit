# KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic Wayfair product-discovery email doing the genre competently but not memorably. The subject line makes a specific price promise — kitchen mats under $30 — and the email opens with a bright lifestyle hero and a dense product grid. That's the right structure. The problem is that the body never explicitly delivers on the "$30" promise: no prices are legible on the product tiles, no callout banner reinforces the ceiling. A shopper who opened expecting a clearance-rack moment will scroll a lifestyle photo essay instead.
- The lower half introduces two separate goals — a product recommendation carousel ("we think you'll ♥ these") and an app-download pitch — which splits the email's attention at exactly the moment the shopper is deciding whether to click. Neither CTA is specific enough to pull a decision.
- Wayfair's brand recognition saves this from being a waste of a send, but the execution leaves the subject's best asset — that $30 ceiling — stranded.

## What's working

- **Subject delivers a scannable value prop.** "Under $30" is a concrete price anchor that clears the inbox noise; the emoji adds softness without going spammy.
- **Hero image is clean and on-category** — colorful, well-lit mat in a real kitchen context, immediately confirms the product type.
- **Grid layout gives variety** — multiple product shots give the shopper options to browse without a single click.
- **Wayfair brand recognition** — logo is prominent, the color palette is instantly recognizable.

## What's weak

- **The "$30" price point disappears inside the email.** Product tiles show no visible pricing, so the hook from the subject goes unconfirmed and trust erodes.
- **Hero copy is vague.** "The most shop-ready" is category-speak that means nothing; it should be doing the work of the subject line — restating the offer.
- **App-download CTA hijacks the bottom third.** This is a secondary conversion goal that pulls attention away from the purchase flow.
- **No urgency signal anywhere in the body.** No "today only," no countdown, no "limited inventory" — the offer could be evergreen or expired, the email doesn't say.
- **"We think you'll ♥ these" section is generic.** Without names, prices, or reasons, it reads like filler padding.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pin the price in the hero.** Replace "The most shop-ready" with something like "Kitchen mats • All under $30" so the subject promise is echoed the instant the email opens. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Add visible prices (or at least the $30 badge) on every product tile.** The offer is the whole email — every product needs to reinforce it.
- 3. **Cut or move the app-download block.** If it must live here, push it below the fold and after the main CTA row, not as a competing call-to-action.
- 4. **Add a real deadline.** Even "prices good through Sunday" adds urgency that the email currently has zero of.
- 5. **Give the recommendation section specificity.** At minimum, show a product name and "$X" on each tile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kitchen mats under $30 — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kitchen upgrade: mats from $12`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Striped, solid, and patterned — all under $30. Free shipping too.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dozens of styles, every one under $30. Ends Sunday.`
