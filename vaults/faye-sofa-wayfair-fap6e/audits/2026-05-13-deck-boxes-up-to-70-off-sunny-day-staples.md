# DECK BOXES 🏖️ UP TO 70% OFF sunny-day staples 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent Memorial Day clearance blast from Wayfair — well-rendered, on-season, and stuffed with product tiles. The problem is right at the top: the subject line promises deck boxes specifically, but the email body delivers a sprawling clearance across fire pits, hot tubs, outdoor furniture, textiles, and more. That mismatch is the defining weakness. Shoppers who opened for outdoor storage will land on a generic sale page.
- The "picked just for you" personalization hook and the Memorial Day timing both do real work. The discount ladder (up to 90% off in places, BOGO 15% off, free delivery threshold shown) gives browsers plenty of reasons to stay. But this email is built for the impulse scroller, not the intent-driven buyer the subject line attracted.
- **Verdict:** Solid, forgettable, slightly bait-y. Executes the standard Wayfair clearance format well, but the subject/body mismatch is a trust tax that compounds over time with this audience.

- **"Picked just for you" hero** signals segmentation and makes the email feel less like a broadcast blast.
- **Memorial Day timing is exactly right** — outdoor/deck products in mid-May land with natural seasonal urgency.

- **Free delivery threshold shown** ($35) — removing that friction point inline reduces a common exit reason.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no layout breaks, no overlapping copy visible anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body mismatch is the main problem** — "DECK BOXES" in the subject is a product-specific promise; the email is a category-wide clearance. High-intent deck box shoppers arrive, don't see a curated deck box section, and bounce.
- **ALL CAPS subject ("DECK BOXES," "UP TO 70% OFF")** pattern-matches to promotional spam even when the content is legitimate — aggressive at inbox glance.
- **"Shop all sales" CTA is maximally generic** — sends a deck-box searcher straight to the top of the funnel instead of the relevant category.
- **No visible deadline** — Memorial Day implies urgency, but no "ends Monday 5/26" or countdown makes it easy to defer indefinitely.
- **No social proof on product tiles** — no star ratings, review counts, or "bestseller" badges visible anywhere in the grid.

- 1. **Align subject to body (or body to subject)** — either rewrite the subject to reflect the actual email scope ("Memorial Day clearance: outdoor, storage & more") or curate a deck-box-focused section prominently in the hero. This is the single highest-leverage fix.
- 2. **Add a concrete end date** — "Sale ends Monday, May 26" in the hero banner converts the implied holiday urgency into an actual deadline.
- 3. **Replace the generic CTA** — "Shop Outdoor Storage" or "See Deck Boxes" honors the subject promise and reduces friction for the most-targeted segment.
- 4. **De-caps the subject line** — title case or sentence case reads less aggressively at inbox.
- 5. **Add star ratings/review counts** to the top two or three featured product tiles — a "4,200+ reviews" badge on one hero item provides the social proof this format is missing.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom web font — partial client support**

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff)
       format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```

- `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` is set correctly for Outlook fallback.
- Gmail (Android/web), Yahoo, and AOL will ignore `@font-face` and silently fall back to the device default sans-serif — **not Arial**. If the design depends on Sofia for proportional spacing or headline sizing, layout may shift in those clients.
- Only a `.woff` source is declared; no `.woff2`. Most modern email clients that do honor web fonts prefer `.woff2` for download size. Low severity, but worth noting.

**`word-break: break-all` on product names**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; display: block; height: 48px !important }
}
```

`word-break: break-all` is aggressive — it breaks at any character boundary, not word boundary. On mobile, single-word product names will be mid-word split if they overflow. `word-break: break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the safer choice.

**Fixed pixel heights on dynamic content blocks**

Multiple product and event cells use `height: XX px !important`:
```css
.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; vertical-align: top }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; vertical-align: top }
.u-HalfWidthBlock .EventBlock-name { height: 48px !important }
```

If any product name, price string, or shipping copy exceeds the fixed height (e.g., long sale price like "Was $249.99, Now $74.99"), the overflow is clipped. This is a known pattern in Wayfair's system, but worth flagging if any A/B or personalized content can produce longer strings.
