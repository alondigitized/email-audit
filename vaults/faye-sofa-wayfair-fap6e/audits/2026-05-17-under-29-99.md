# under $29.99
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a day-two repeat of the same value proposition Wayfair already sent yesterday — "All under $29.99" (scored 7/10) has become "under $29.99" with a near-identical product structure underneath. The hero lands cleanly: "$29.99" cap is front and center, "Your Favs from $20" is a nice sub-hook, and the layout is tidy. But any subscriber who opened yesterday's send is getting the same pitch 24 hours later with different product thumbnails swapped in, which is a cadence-fatigue problem Wayfair keeps running into this week.
- The email cascades through five loosely connected sections — cleaning, metallic modern, outdoor/garden, deals of the day, and a rug footer — held together only by the price cap. That's more than yesterday's send managed, but the through-line weakens with every scroll. The 70% off teaser at the bottom is a buried prize that should be up top.
- Bottom line: a competent execution of a concept that already fired yesterday. The price promise still works; the fatigue doesn't.

## What's working

- The "all under $29.99" hero is unambiguous — price cap reads instantly, no decoding required.
- "Your Favs from $20" as a sub-headline hints at curation and creates a value floor beneath the cap.
- Layout is clean — no broken images, overlapping text, or visual bugs visible.
- The 70% off callout near the bottom acts as a value escalator for anyone who scrolled through.
- App download CTA at footer is a reasonable secondary goal that doesn't compete with the primary offer.

## What's weak

- This is functionally the same email as yesterday's "All under $29.99" send — near-identical concept, same subject structure, sent back-to-back on consecutive days. Cadence fatigue is real.
- "Shop now" appears as the CTA copy across every section — no specificity, no pull.
- Five product categories (silverware → cleaning → metallic modern → garden → rugs) feel like a product dump held together by a price sticker.
- No urgency mechanism — nothing signals when the $29.99 cap expires or why today beats tomorrow.
- No social proof, reviews, or bestseller badges visible anywhere in the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send the same concept two days running.** Yesterday's "All under $29.99" already used this exact hook. Either skip a day or pivot the angle hard — "Today's $29.99 Picks" with a "refreshes daily" mechanic would at least justify the re-send.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends midnight" or "Today only" in the hero turns a browse into a decision.
- 3. **Rewrite section CTAs to be category-specific.** "Shop Cleaning Picks" / "Shop Garden Finds" / "Shop Metallic Modern" — each drives category intent, not just homepage traffic.
- 4. **Surface the 70% off earlier.** That callout is buried below four product sections; it's the most compelling number in the email and it's invisible on first open.
- 5. **Tighten the category count.** Pick three strong categories, not five thin ones, so each section has room to breathe and feels curated.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: everything under $29.99`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh $29.99 picks — new today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kitchen, cleaning, garden finds — all capped. Ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Silverware, metallic decor, garden gear — all under $29.99 right now.`
