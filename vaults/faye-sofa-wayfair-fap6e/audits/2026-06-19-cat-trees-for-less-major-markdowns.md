# CAT TREES for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- The subject line — "CAT TREES for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡" — is one of the most specific hooks Wayfair has sent in this entire recent stretch. It names a product, implies a price drop, and implies inventory (markdown = finite supply). That's a rare trifecta for this brand. Then the email body opens with "The must-shop deals" — the most generic headline in retail — and delivers a multi-category product scatter: rugs, textiles, outdoor furniture, a hammock, an umbrella. No cat trees visible anywhere in the render.
- This is the same bait-and-switch Wayfair executed on June 17 with the "DORM ROOM DEALS" subject and June 17 again with "Area Rugs from our Exclusive Brands" — and both times the body was the identical scatter chassis. At this point it's a pattern, not an accident. The trust cost compounds every time a subscriber opens expecting the thing that was promised.
- The "we think you'll ♥ these" algorithmic picks section is the one honest section — at least it's trying to personalize. But it can't rescue an email that broke its contract with the reader in the first scroll.

## What's weak

- **Subject-body mismatch — again**: Cat trees promised; rugs, outdoor furniture, and textiles delivered. No cat trees visible in the rendered email.
- **Hero headline is dead weight**: "The must-shop deals" is the same content-free header Wayfair defaults to on every scatter send. It resets any anticipation the subject built.
- **No visible primary CTA**: There's no prominent, labeled button driving to a cat tree category or collection — just the grid, which sends the click everywhere and nowhere.
- **No time-boundary in body**: The subject implies urgency ("markdowns") but nothing in the visible email — no countdown, no "ends [date]," no inventory signal — makes that credible.
- **Third consecutive week of the same chassis**: June 15, June 18, and now June 19 all deliver this identical multi-block scatter. A subscriber noticing the pattern is a subscriber starting to route-to-trash.

- 1. **Put cat trees in the email.** If the subject says cat trees, the first product block must be cat trees. One hero cat tree with a price, a "before" markup, a savings call-out, and a "Shop Cat Trees" button. Everything else is secondary.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero headline to echo the subject**: "Major markdowns on cat trees. Today only." The subject created the contract; the body has to honor it immediately.
- 3. **Add a time boundary**: "*Offer ends [date]* — while supplies last" directly under the hero. The subject implies scarcity; the body never closes the loop.
- 4. **Swap the scatter grid for a 3-product hero module**: Three specific cat trees, each with star ratings, one key spec (height / number of tiers), and current price. That's a click, not a browse.
