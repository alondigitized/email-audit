# ❗ UP TO 70% OFF ❗ 2 DAYS ONLY ❗
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a high-volume, discount-first promotional blast built entirely around a 48-hour sale event. The email leads with a clean urgency frame (48 HR / 70% OFF), then cascades into an extensive grid of category-discount tiles, a personalized product module, a BNPL nudge via Affirm, and a footer app-download play. The structure is competent but the execution is exhausting — every module screams the same thing (percentage off, percentage off, percentage off), and by tile six the discount fatigue is real. The "Picked Just For You" section is the lone bright spot for engagement, but it's buried mid-scroll. The email is long enough to be Gmail-clipped, which cuts off the tail-end CTAs and the Affirm offer for a meaningful slice of openers.
- Gets clicks from bargain hunters already primed to buy. Does nothing to build brand affinity or differentiate Wayfair from any other home-goods retailer running a 48-hour sale this weekend.

## What's working

- **48-hour countdown frame** is immediately legible — the "48 HR" lock-up in the hero translates urgency without needing to read a word of body copy.
- **70% OFF as the lead number** is a credible, attention-grabbing anchor; positioning it prominently above the fold is correct.
- **"Picked Just For You" module** is the most engaging section in the email — product tiles with visible star ratings and product names give shoppers a reason to linger rather than skim.
- **Free delivery threshold ($35+)** is surfaced mid-email, which can reduce cart abandonment anxiety at the decision point.
- **Category breadth** (furniture, lighting, storage, bath, office) means different shoppers have multiple relevant entry points.

## What's weak

- **Discount fatigue is severe.** The email is essentially: tile of % off, tile of % off, repeat for 12+ modules. There's no narrative arc, no product story, no reason to feel anything except mildly overwhelmed.
- **Product imagery is small and interchangeable.** At email width, the category tiles show tiny cropped product shots that are hard to distinguish. A single hero product with context (a styled room, a specific item) would do more work than eight thumbnail tiles.
- **The Affirm offer is buried** near the bottom of a very long email, after most readers have scrolled out. A BNPL nudge belongs above the fold or adjacent to higher-ticket categories.
- **No category hierarchy or editorial curation.** 40% off storage lands with the same visual weight as 70% off furniture. Nothing signals "this is the deal of the event."
- **Email length will trigger Gmail clipping**, cutting off whatever is past the ~102KB mark — almost certainly the bottom half of this email for a large portion of openers.
- **App download CTA at the bottom** is boilerplate and will be invisible to anyone who didn't scroll the full length.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the tile count by half.** Pick the 4-5 categories with the deepest discounts or highest margin and give each one more visual space. Fewer, bigger bets beat a wall of small ones.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product or room scene**, not just a logo + percentage. Give shoppers something to desire before you give them a discount.
- 3. **Elevate the Affirm offer to a dedicated mid-email placement**, tied specifically to a high-ticket category (sectionals, mattresses, outdoor furniture). BNPL messaging only converts if it's adjacent to a relevant, high-price item.
- 4. **Separate the "Picked Just For You" module** and move it up — it's the only section that feels personal, and it's the most likely to drive qualified clicks.
- 5. **Shorten the email** to avoid Gmail clipping. If the full grid must ship, split it into two sends: a hero flash and a browse-by-category follow-up.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@font-face` — WOFF only, no WOFF2:**
```css
src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
mso-font-alt: "Arial"
```
WOFF2 is absent. More critically, Gmail (Web, Android, iOS), Apple Mail iOS, and Outlook all suppress `@font-face`. The only declared fallback is the Outlook-specific `mso-font-alt`; there is no CSS `font-family` fallback stack in the excerpt, meaning non-Outlook clients that block the font get the browser/client default, not a controlled fallback.

**Fixed-height text containers at mobile (potential clipping):**
```css
.ProductRec-name { height: 48px !important }
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px; vertical-align: top }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px; vertical-align: top }
```
Fixed pixel heights clip overflowing text on small or large-type accessibility settings.

**`word-break: break-all` on product names:**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; }
```
`break-all` splits mid-word at any character boundary. Correct value is `overflow-wrap: break-word` (or `word-break: break-word`), which only breaks when the whole word doesn't fit.

**Missing `lang` attribute:** No `lang="en"` visible on the `<html>` element. Required for screen reader language detection and WCAG 3.1.1.
