# Open Box steals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- "Open Box Deals" is the most strategically sound subject line Wayfair has sent in the past two weeks of audits. It breaks the niche-noun-ALL-CAPS template entirely and promises something genuinely different: discounted returned/open-box inventory, a hook that appeals to bargain hunters on a different emotional register than "UP TO 70% OFF" blanket claims. The lavender hero with an illustrated Dutch oven landing on the word "deals" creates a light, inviting visual — cleaner than most of what Wayfair has sent lately.
- The problem is the body collapses into the same category-list filler every other Wayfair email delivers. Sixteen rows of "Category | up to X% OFF" with no product thumbnails, no specific item, no anchor product — the open-box premise evaporates the moment you scroll past the hero. A subscriber who opened expecting curated "like-new" steals gets a generic sale nav bar instead.
- Still, this is the best Wayfair creative in the recent run. The subject earns the open; the body just doesn't deliver on it.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the standout of the recent streak** — "Open Box steals" is lowercase, specific, and conveys a distinct product type (clearance/returned goods), not a generic "UP TO X% OFF" shout.
- **Hero visual is clean and on-brand** — lavender palette, bold "open box deals" wordmark, and a single lifestyle product (Dutch oven) create strong visual hierarchy.
- **Percentage ranges per category are concrete** — "up to 70% OFF" against "Area Rugs" gives a tangible anchor even in list form.
- **Loyalty + financing utility blocks** at the bottom (Wayfair Rewards, Flexible Financing, Professional, Exclusive Brands) reinforce membership value without cluttering the main offer.
- **No render bugs** — text is crisp, images load, no overlapping elements visible.

## What's weak

- **The open-box premise disappears after the hero** — the category list is identical to every other Wayfair promotional email; there's no featured open-box product, no "only X left" inventory urgency, nothing that makes this feel like a special clearance event.
- **Zero product imagery in the body** — sixteen rows of plain text links with discount percentages is the lowest-engagement layout Wayfair deploys; no thumbnail = no desire.
- **No time-bound urgency** — open-box inventory is inherently scarce, but the email doesn't say "while supplies last" prominently or set any deadline.
- **"SHOP ALL SALES" CTA is too generic** — it undercuts the "open box" premise and sends the click to the broader sale, not a dedicated open-box landing page.
- **Category list has no hierarchy** — Area Rugs through Decorative Accents are displayed at equal weight; there's no "featured steal of the day" to anchor attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one hero open-box product above the list.** A real item — e.g., "KitchenAid Stand Mixer, like new — $199 (was $399), only 3 left" — would validate the open-box promise and drive urgency that the category list cannot.
- 2. **Change the primary CTA to "Shop Open Box"**, not "SHOP ALL SALES." The current CTA dilutes the email's entire premise.
- 3. **Add scarcity language** — "Quantities are limited and sell fast" or a timestamp ("Inventory refreshed daily") gives the open-box format its natural urgency hook.
- 4. **Collapse the category list to 5-6 top categories with product thumbnails**, rather than 16 text-only rows. More scroll depth ≠ more clicks.
- 5. **Preheader should reinforce the "like-new" angle**, not just trail off.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Like-new prices on open-box furniture & décor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Open box: up to 70% off returns & display models`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Items in like-new condition — inventory limited, ships fast`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Returned & display pieces at a fraction of retail — while they last`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Web font: WOFF only, no WOFF2.** The `@font-face` block declares a single `woff` source. Gmail and most Android mail clients ignore web fonts entirely; the CSS provides no explicit CSS fallback stack (only `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` for Outlook). Any client that can load fonts but prefers WOFF2 will also miss it. Impact: Sofia brand font silently drops to system default across a large client share.

- **`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` Gmail hack** is present. This is intentional (prevents Gmail clipping), but it can cause horizontal overflow on narrow viewports if any table cell's min-width pushes past the screen width. Confirm with Gmail mobile rendering test.

- **Dual breakpoint strategy (480px and 320px)** — the 320px breakpoint only targets `Footer-link` font-size. If the design ships any other elements that need 320px adjustments, they are unaddressed.

- **`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` iOS Safari hack** for half-width block images — this is a valid workaround but scoped to iOS only; Android WebView email clients with similar stacking issues would not be covered.

- **Alt text on images** — not visible in the truncated source. Flag for QA team to verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, especially product images which carry price and description context.
