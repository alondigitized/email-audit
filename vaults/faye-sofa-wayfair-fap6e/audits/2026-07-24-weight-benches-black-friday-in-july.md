# WEIGHT BENCHES 🎉 🥳 BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY 🎉 🥳 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair sends a fifth email in roughly 36 hours, this time leading with "WEIGHT BENCHES" in the subject — a specific fitness-equipment promise. Open the email and there are no weight benches. There is no fitness section. There is a "picked just for you" personalisation header, a doorbuster strip with percentage-off callouts for bedding, TV stands, and seating, a generic "shop all" button, and then the same rug and home-decor grid that has appeared in nearly every Wayfair audit in recent brand history. The July 22 "END TABLES 🚩" send pulled the same stunt; so did the July 23 ornaments email. At this point the bait-and-switch is a defined Wayfair creative pattern, not a one-off mistake.
- The doorbuster section — yellow background, bold percentage callouts — is the one legible value proposition in the body, and it actually works visually. But it says nothing about weight benches, and a subscriber who opened on the strength of that subject will feel misled before they scroll past the hero. The Affirm financing badge and the Wayfair Rewards strip at the bottom add credible purchase infrastructure, but they can't rescue an email where the body never honours the subject.
- The verdict is simple: another high-cadence, bait-and-switch Wayfair send. Better than the ornaments email (which promised 80% off and showed none of it), weaker than the July 20 teaser that at least kept its promise in the body.

## What's working

- **Doorbuster strip is visually distinct** — yellow background with percentage-off callouts creates a clear focal point amid a dense layout.
- **Concrete percentage offers are visible** — "up to 50% / 60% / 70% off" category callouts give shoppers an actual number to act on.
- **Affirm badge and loyalty footer add purchase credibility** — financing option and Wayfair Rewards visible for members who care about those signals.
- **"Black Friday in July" event branding is consistent** — the campaign frame is legible and matches the banner at the top of the email.

## What's weak

- **No weight benches anywhere in the body** — the subject promise is broken within the first scroll; this is bait-and-switch.
- **"Shop all" CTA is the weakest possible button copy** — a subscriber interested in weight benches has no direct path to them.
- **Five Wayfair emails in ~36 hours** — cadence fatigue is real, and this send does nothing to justify another slot in the inbox.
- **"Picked just for you" personalisation header sits above what appears to be generic home goods** — if this truly were personalised, weight benches would be in the product grid.
- **Emoji spam in subject** — four celebration emoji flanking the copy add no signal and read as desperation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put weight benches in the email.** This is non-negotiable. If the subject promises a product category, the hero image, the first product tile, and the primary CTA must all reflect it. Anything less destroys trust.
- 2. **Replace "Shop all" with "Shop Weight Benches"** — or whatever the actual category is. A specific CTA doubles click intent for people who came because of the subject.
- 3. **Cut the send cadence.** Five emails in 36 hours is a churn driver. Either consolidate BFIJ into one or two sends per day or segment so heavy emailers get fewer touches.
- 4. **Lose two of the four celebration emoji** — one 🎉 reads festive; four reads unhinged.
- 5. **Surface a price anchor in the subject** — "up to 70% off" would outperform the vague "Black Friday in July" framing for click intent.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday in July: fitness & home, up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Adjustable benches, dumbbells, racks — doorbuster prices through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Black Friday in July picks are ready — shop before they sell out`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**Web font fallback is declared but font will silently drop in most clients.**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff);
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
`@font-face` in email is ignored by Gmail (all surfaces), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. The `mso-font-alt` covers Outlook only. Gmail web/app will silently fall through to the system sans-serif — not Arial — unless Arial is explicitly declared in a `font-family` stack on each element. If brand typography depends on Sofia rendering, this is a gap on the highest-volume clients.

**Breakpoints present and structurally correct:** 480px primary, 320px secondary. Standard pattern; no issues.

**iOS-specific `@supports` hack present:**
```css
@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none) {
  .u-HalfWidthBlock img { display: inline !important; vertical-align: top !important; }
}
```
This is a known workaround for WKWebView image stacking — expected in Wayfair templates.

**Gmail `u + div` hack present** (`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }`): correct approach for Gmail app clipping prevention.
