# Open Box steals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "Open Box Deals" is genuinely the most differentiated Wayfair send in their recent barrage — the concept (customer returns = your discount) is a real value story with inherent scarcity logic baked in. The tagline "Their return = your chance to save big" earns its place; it explains the premise in one line without resorting to the usual FLASH SALE alarm bells. That's meaningful given how many all-caps urgency frames Wayfair has burned through this past week.
- But the execution immediately collapses into the same 17-category scroll wall that defined the June 23 "FOUR-DAY FLASH SALE," the June 25 "MAILBOXES," and nearly every other send in this run. The best hook in the email — that open box inventory is finite, and returns sell out — is completely absent from the body. No countdown, no "X items left," no "this week only." The natural urgency of the open box concept is abandoned the moment you scroll past the hero.
- The net result is a strong idea wearing a tired template. This should score higher than it does; the template is what holds it back.

## What's working

- The hero concept — "Their return = your chance to save big" — quickly communicates *why* this sale is different from every other sale, which is rare for Wayfair.
- Three visually distinct product types in the hero image (accent chair, glassware, stand mixer) hint at real breadth without feeling like a cluttered grid.
- Per-category discount percentages (up to 70% on Area Rugs and Bar Stools, 65% on Dining Room Sets) are specific enough to be scannable and credible.
- Clean, no-bug render — every image loads, no overlapping text, layout holds on mobile width.

## What's weak

- The same 17-category list format that appeared in the June 22 COFFEE MAKERS send, the June 23 FLASH SALE, and the June 25 MAILBOXES send — the chassis is exhausted and subscribers have trained to scroll past it.
- Zero urgency in the body despite open box being the one category where "while supplies last" is genuinely true and not a marketing cliché.
- "SHOP ALL SALES" CTA is disconnected from the open box concept; "SHOP OPEN BOX" would have tied the thread.
- Subject "Open Box steals" is all lowercase — fine aesthetically, but there's no hook beyond the concept name. No discount range, no category hint, no urgency signal.
- No social proof, no ratings, no "X units left" — the email skips every conversion signal that works for deal-driven categories.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Inject scarcity into the body.** Add a single line under the hero: "Once it's gone, it's gone — quantities are limited to what customers returned." This is true, it's distinctive, and it does the urgency work without a fake countdown timer.
- 2. **Rename the CTA.** Change "SHOP ALL SALES" to "SHOP OPEN BOX DEALS" — keeps the theme consistent and reduces the sense that this is just another generic sale page.
- 3. **Cut the category list by half.** Pick the 6–8 highest-discount categories, remove the rest. Depth > breadth. The current 17-row list trains the eye to tune out.
- 4. **Add a featured product.** One specific open box item ("This $689 chair came back — now $290. Grab it before someone else does.") would anchor the email's value story and give people something concrete to click.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to signal the discount range:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box: up to 70% off furniture + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Someone returned it. Now it's yours for less.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited quantities — these deals disappear when returns run out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Area rugs, dining sets, bar stools — all open box, all marked down`
