# The chicken coop everyone LOVES. See why →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair finally breaks the subject-line bait-and-switch pattern — at least partially. "The chicken coop everyone LOVES" is a genuine creative choice: leading with social proof on a single specific product instead of another "UP TO 70% OFF EVERYTHING" blast. The hero image follows through; you see an actual chicken coop. That's a first in this July run.
- The problem is what happens next. After a single hero, the email reverts to the exact same multi-section general-catalog format that's appeared in every send since July 7 — rugs, bedding, open box deals, welcome mats, small-space storage, app download. The "people love this" story evaporates immediately. A chicken coop is also a niche product that skews rural/suburban homesteader; most of Wayfair's home-decor subscriber base has no use for one, so the subject earns curious opens that the body cannot convert.
- Compared to the July 12 "TRASH CANS & RECYCLING" send (which had the same bait-and-switch problem), this one is cleaner — the hero at least delivers on what was promised. But the email's ambition stops at the fold.

## What's working

- Hero image and headline are aligned — the chicken coop actually appears, a meaningful improvement over the July 12 trash-can send where the body showed zero trash cans.
- "People love this chicken coop" is a story-first hook; social proof in headline copy is a change from generic sale headers.
- The 48-hour countdown timer in the top bar anchors urgency without clutter.
- Visual hierarchy is clean — the eye lands on the hero coop image immediately, no render bugs visible.
- Layout is mobile-readable with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- After the hero, the email pivots to the identical multi-section general catalog used in every July send — rugs, bedding, welcome mats, storage — with no further mention of the chicken coop.
- A chicken coop is a niche, outdoor-specific product; the subject earns curious opens from an audience unlikely to buy one, burning trust on the mismatch.
- "People love" and "everyone LOVES" are thin social proof — no review counts, star ratings, or customer quotes are visible to substantiate the claim.
- No visible member pricing or loyalty benefit, despite this coming from the members.wayfair.com sender domain.
- The product sections below the fold are the same visual format repeated 4+ times with no creative variation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the narrative or don't start it.** If the chicken coop is the hook, build the email around it: show it styled in a backyard, include a customer quote with a star rating, link the CTA directly to the product page. Right now the story ends at row one and the email becomes indistinguishable from every other July send.
- 2. **Add real social proof to the hero.** "4.8★ from 2,300 reviewers" next to "people love this" would make the claim land. The copy claims it without supporting it.
- 3. **Surface the member benefit.** The send comes from `members.wayfair.com` — there should be a visible member-exclusive badge or early-access callout to justify that targeting.
- 4. **Cut two catalog sections.** Open box, rugs, welcome mats, small-space storage, AND app download is too much for one send; it reads as a brand that doesn't know what it wants to say.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"2,300+ reviews and counting. This coop delivers."`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"The backyard upgrade everyone's talking about →"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Rated 4.8 stars — and it ships free. See the build."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Our most-loved outdoor find this summer. Plus 70% off everything else."`
