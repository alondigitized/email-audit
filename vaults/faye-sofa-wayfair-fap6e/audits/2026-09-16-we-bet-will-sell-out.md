## What's working

- Top banner ("EPIC FALL SALE — 70% OFF") is visible immediately with no scroll, unlike several recent sends that opened on blank space.
- Star ratings under each product thumbnail add a thin layer of social proof throughout the grid.
- The "let's get lounging" yellow-chaise section breaks up the grid monotony with a genuine lifestyle hero shot.
- Clean top nav (Shop Sale / New Arrivals) gives an easy top-of-email exit if nothing below grabs attention.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "will sell out" but nothing in the body shows stock counts, "almost gone" badges, or any scarcity signal on a specific product — the urgency is manufactured, not shown.
- No hero product or category is elevated as *the* thing that will sell out; it's a dozen unrelated categories (fans, toilets, coffee makers, sheds) competing for attention.
- Most product tiles show no price or percentage-off at all, just a title and a ">" link — the 70% off banner is disconnected from the actual grid below it.
- A stray blue dot / render artifact sits in white space near "the bedroom edit" section, echoing the broken-image problems flagged in the 2026-09-16 recliners send.
- "Find your fave" and "style" are filler section headers that add no information and slow the scroll toward anything actionable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one or two "selling fast" SKUs and lead the email with them, tied directly to the subject line — show a stock indicator or "X left" callout so "will sell out" is backed by something visible.
- 2. Add visible prices/percentages to at least the first 3-4 product tiles instead of leaving the 70% banner as the only number in the email.
- 3. Fix the stray blue-dot artifact near "the bedroom edit" — it reads as a broken image and undercuts trust.
- 4. Cut "style" and "Find your fave" filler headers; replace with category labels that carry information (e.g. "Under $200", "Ships this week").
- 5. Vary the subject/hero pairing — this is essentially the same 70% OFF fall-sale creative sent 2026-09-15 with a new subject slapped on; repeat sends with reskinned urgency claims will fatigue subscribers fast.
