# RUGS *sale*. Instant savings, endless options!️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair opens strong with "the must-shop steals" — a hero that actually delivers rugs, which is a small mercy given the July subject-line-to-body bait-and-switch pattern we've seen all month (the July 10 "DINING BOWLS" send, score 8/10, did the same general-sale pivot). The 7% back credit card block anchors a real value proposition for members. But the moment you hit "but wait, there's more..." the email abandons its rug mandate and dumps what appears to be a full home goods clearance shelf: bedding, pillows, lamps, decorative art, a dresser. The subject promised a rug sale. The body delivers a sitewide SKU dump.
- The subject line "RUGS *sale*. Instant savings, endless options!️" has a more fundamental problem than just the asterisks: "instant savings, endless options" is dead filler copy that adds zero information. Worse, this is Wayfair's fifth or sixth rug-themed email in July. The cumulative cadence fatigue is now the single biggest drag on open rate — a subscriber reading their inbox has already been down this road four times this month and knows exactly what they'll get.
- Net read: a clean render with a coherent hero, undermined by copy rot in the subject and structural bait-and-switch in the body. Mid-tier Wayfair, consistent with July's pattern.

## What's working

- **Hero follows through on the promise.** "The must-shop steals" hero visually features rug products — the first Wayfair July rug send that doesn't immediately pivot to a generic banner in the above-fold section.
- **7% back block is visually distinct.** The purple credit card section breaks up the layout and surfaces a concrete member benefit with a "Learn More" CTA.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no layout overflow, no lorem ipsum — the email displays correctly.
- **Subject length is mobile-safe.** ~47 characters keeps it readable on a truncated inbox preview.

## What's weak

- **"But wait, there's more..." kills the rug focus.** The product grid below the fold is a sitewide dump — bedding, pillows, artwork, furniture — with no organizing principle or connection to the rug promise.
- **Subject asterisks are a liability.** `*sale*` renders literally in most email clients; it reads as a markdown artifact or editorial note, not emphasis. It erodes trust without adding interest.
- **"Endless options" is filler.** The second clause of the subject contributes nothing — it's the email equivalent of "we have lots of stuff."
- **No clear percentage discount in the hero.** The 7% back is for a credit card sign-up, not a product sale. A shopper who came for a rug deal finds no "X% off" before they scroll.
- **Cadence is the elephant in the room.** This is at minimum the fifth July send with rugs in the subject. Even a well-executed email gets tuned out at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "endless options" entirely — replace with the actual discount or a category hook.** The subject should mirror what's in the hero. If there's a price floor or a lead product, say it.
- 2. **Cut or reframe the "but wait, there's more..." section.** Either keep the email rug-only, or rename it to something that earns the pivot ("Also on sale this week"). Right now it signals that the subject line was bait.
- 3. **Drop the asterisks in the subject.** They don't render as bold; they look like a formatting error.
- 4. **Give the 7% back block a rug-specific hook.** "Earn 7% back on your next rug purchase" converts better than a generic credit card pitch that could have been dropped into any email.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** No subject line fix lifts this if subscribers have already seen four nearly identical sends this month. One rug email per week max.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off rugs this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Area rugs, outdoor rugs, runners — all on sale. Free shipping on orders $35+.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Budget-friendly picks starting at $29 — shop the must-have steals before they're gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Subject line trailing character — confirmed issue:**
- Subject: `RUGS *sale*. Instant savings, endless options!️`
- The `️` following `!` is a lone **U+FE0F (Variation Selector-16)**. VS-16 is a modifier that must follow a base emoji codepoint; standalone, it renders as an invisible zero-width character or box in some clients (Yahoo Mail, older Android). It should be removed or preceded by a valid base emoji (e.g., `🏷️`).

**`<style>` block in `<head>` — partial risk:**
- Gmail (non-Google Apps) strips `<head>` styles. The code includes a Gmail-aware hack (`u + .Gmail .u-P--5`) confirming awareness, but classes like `.u-MobileHide` / `.u-MobileShow` and all responsive breakpoints depend on this block surviving. If stripped, mobile layout collapses to desktop-width.
- **Evidence:** `.u-MobileHide { display: none !important }` — this rule is only in the `<style>` block; no inline fallback visible.

**No `<meta name="viewport">` confirmed** — truncation prevents verification. Required for iOS Mail correct initial scale.
