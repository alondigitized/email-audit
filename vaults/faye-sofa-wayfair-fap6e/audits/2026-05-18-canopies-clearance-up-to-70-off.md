# CANOPIES 🟥 CLEARANCE ⬜ UP TO 70% OFF 🟦 
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's third "UP TO 70% OFF" subject line in three days — pet gates on 5/16, "under $29.99" on 5/17, now canopies on 5/18. The formula is fully legible at this point: pick a seasonal product noun, bolt on patriotic block-color emojis (squares this time, circles last time), claim 70% off, deploy. To be fair, the canopy IS the first hero product in the email — so the subject isn't pure bait-and-switch — but the body abandons it almost immediately for treadmills, sewing machines, dining furniture, storage organization, and rugs. By the third tile, "canopies clearance" has evaporated.
- The Memorial Day hook still carries weight — "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals" breaks the scroll with genuine seasonal context — and the render is clean throughout. But "picked just for you" rings hollow when the hero products below span fitness equipment to home sewing. There's no coherent through-line in this email.
- The bigger problem is cadence. This is the sixth Wayfair send in four days from this audit run. Subscribers who've been paying attention are now pattern-matching the formula. Each iteration dilutes the next one, and the 70% OFF anchor is losing credibility through repetition.

## What's working

- **Canopy as lead product is correct.** The first hero thumbnail IS a canopy/gazebo tent, so the subject-to-open experience isn't a complete betrayal.
- **Memorial Day banner adds genuine urgency.** The "MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals" strip mid-email gives the clearance framing a credible time peg.
- **10% OFF MEMBERSHIP visibility.** The loyalty bar is prominent and gives members a layered reason to click that non-members don't have.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, star ratings render on hero products — technically solid.
- **Up to 70% OFF is immediately visible** in the hero without scrolling.

## What's weak

- **The canopy focus evaporates after one tile.** Treadmills and sewing machines as co-hero products dilute the outdoor/seasonal story immediately.
- **"Picked just for you" is pure boilerplate.** Fitness equipment and home sewing machines in the same "personalized" hero section signals algorithmic noise, not curation.
- **Same 70% OFF claim for the third time in three days.** The May 16 pet gates send used the same language; subscribers who opened both are discounting it.
- **Subject ALL CAPS + block emojis = spam-adjacent.** The 🟥⬜🟦 square pattern mirrors the 🔴⚪🔵 patriotic circles from May 15 — the disguise is thin.
- **No canopy-specific offers or product details.** No size specs, no coverage area, no color options — nothing that would make a canopy-intender click past the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to canopies for at least 60% of the body.** If the subject leads with a specific product, the first three full content rows should be that product — sizes, dimensions, color variants, a curated "complete your outdoor space" cross-sell. Kill the sewing machine and treadmill.
- 2. **Surface a canopy-specific promo code.** The May 14 "OUTDOOR10" send ($10 off $100+) was the strongest email in this audit run specifically because it had a named code. Give canopy buyers a reason to act today vs. waiting.
- 3. **Break the 70% OFF repetition cadence.** On the third consecutive send using this claim, consider a dollar-off anchor ("canopies from $39") or a compare-at price — something that feels different.
- 4. **Add a specific Memorial Day end date.** "Ends Monday" or "Last chance — Memorial Day weekend only" in the hero would transform implied urgency into actionable urgency.
- 5. **Replace "picked just for you" with a category-specific header** like "Shade your space before summer" — it earns the personalization signal rather than just claiming it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Canopy clearance — up to 70% off, this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your backyard needs shade. Canopies from $39.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Gazebos, pergolas & shade sails — all Memorial Day priced.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find the right size before they sell out this weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Custom web font (`Sofia`) will not render in most clients.** `@font-face` in email is supported only by Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail. Gmail (all variants), Outlook (Windows), and Yahoo strip or ignore it. The `mso-font-alt: Arial` fallback is set, but no CSS `font-family` stack with a safe fallback (e.g., `"Sofia", Arial, sans-serif`) is visible in the truncated block — confirm this is set on body/text elements.

**Subject line emoji risk.** `🟥 ⬜ 🟦` (Unicode colored squares, U+1F7E5/U+2B1C/U+1F7E6) render correctly in Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 2019+, but display as tofu (`□`) in Outlook 2007–2016 on Windows. No fallback subject variant is possible at the HTML level, but worth noting for ESP-side subject-line testing.

**Breakpoint coverage gap.** Media queries target `max-width: 480px` and `max-width: 320px` only. No `min-width` (desktop-up) queries visible. This is intentional for email (desktop inherits base styles), but verify the base layout is explicitly table-based with fixed widths for Outlook compatibility.
