# On sale now: pergolas
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- Subject says "On sale now: pergolas" and, for once, the hero delivers exactly that — a nice lifestyle shot of a pergola over a patio seating area with the caption "On sale now: pergolas »" directly underneath. That's the most honest subject-to-hero match this brand has sent in the last week of audits. But the pergola has no price, no percentage, no size options visible — just a link — and by the second row the email has already moved on to privacy screens, area rugs, living room seating, and a "moody dark" cast-iron pot, none of which relate to pergolas or even outdoor living broadly.
- This is the same "Sales You *Almost* Missed" grab-bag template seen across 2026-09-14 through 2026-09-18 sends, just re-headlined. The structure hasn't changed: one on-brand hero, then a wall of unrelated categories (nursery, dressers, vacuums, mattresses, bar carts, grills, laundry hampers). Anyone opening for the pergola deal scrolls past a dozen irrelevant tiles to find nothing more about pergolas.
- Judgement: solid subject/hero pairing drags down into the same recycled catalog blast. Worth opening, not worth reading past the fold.

## What's working

- Hero image and headline caption ("On sale now: pergolas »") directly match the subject line — no bait-and-switch at the top.
- "LAST CALL" and "This Week's Best Deals, Picked for You" banner gives a clean, legible entry point.
- Visible percentages on several tiles (70% off area rugs, 60% off seating, 50% off mattresses, 40% off nursery/grills) give concrete offers to latch onto.
- Star ratings and "X Recently Purchased" counts on the mid-section grid add real social proof.
- No broken images, overlapping text, or lorem ipsum — the render is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No price or discount percentage anywhere near the pergola hero — the one product the subject promised is the one tile without an offer.
- The email abandons the pergola/outdoor theme almost immediately in favor of an unrelated 25+ tile catalog dump (nursery, dressers, vacuums, laundry hampers).
- "Sales You *Almost* Missed" section has nothing to do with pergolas or outdoor living, undercutting the subject's focus.
- This is functionally the same layout as the 2026-09-16 "BLACK OUTDOOR PRIVACY SCREENS" and 2026-09-18 "Sectionals from $200" sends — same purple grid, same recycled tile categories, just reshuffled.

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the pergola hero an actual offer — a percentage or dollar figure — so the one thing the subject promises has a reason to click.
- 2. Follow the hero with 2-3 more outdoor/patio tiles (patio heaters, outdoor privacy screens are already in the email — move them up front) before pivoting to unrelated categories, so the email honors its subject for more than one screen's worth of scroll.
- 3. Cut or relabel "Sales You *Almost* Missed" — it's an unrelated catalog dump; either theme it to outdoor living or move it further down and stop treating it as a headline section.
- 4. Vary the template structure across sends — three sends this week have used this identical purple-grid layout with reshuffled tiles, which trains subscribers to skim past it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your outdoor upgrade starts here: pergolas + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shade, seating, and rugs — all marked down this week`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard Wayfair batch-marketing template (Sailthru/ESP-style tokenized links) with widespread missing alt text and multiple insecure tracking pixels; no fatal rendering or compliance blockers identified from available data.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 of 70 total links were probed (58 skipped per click-likelihood prioritization); of those probed, 7 returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited by origin, not confirmed broken) and 4 were left unprobed due to a 70s time budget — none confirmed as dead links.
- 20 tracking pixels (`http://li.wayfair.com/imp?s=124126000` through `...019`) load over plain HTTP rather than HTTPS, flagged by QA as "Image uses http://" — non-HTTPS resources may be blocked by mail clients enforcing mixed-content/TLS policies, silently degrading open-tracking accuracy.
- Standard multi-parameter tracking scheme present on all sampled links (`_emr`, `_eml`, `wfcs`, `csnid`, `varid`, `batchid`, `sm`, `refid=MKTEML_122338`, `emlid=101`, `maiid=16211`) — consistent across links, no malformed or truncated parameters observed.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 8+ content images missing `alt` attributes, including the primary hero banner (`wfus_0331_twbdpfy_2024_email_banner-gif.gif`), two SKU recommendation callout images, the "shop all picks" image, and multiple `stars_10.png`/`stars_11.png` rating-star images — screen readers will announce these as unlabeled, and no fallback text renders if images are blocked.
- All 20 open-tracking pixel images (`li.wayfair.com/imp?...`) also lack `alt` text (expected/acceptable for 1x1 tracking pixels, not a real accessibility concern on its own, but flagged since combined with HTTP delivery above).
- Media queries and Outlook/Gmail-specific CSS hacks (`u + .Gmail`, `body[data-outlook-cycle]`, `@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)`) are present, indicating deliberate cross-client compatibility handling for Outlook, Gmail, and iOS — no issues found in the visible markup.
- Custom web font (`Sofia`) declared via `@font-face` with `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback for Outlook — correctly implemented fallback pattern, no issue.
