# 🥳 🙌 🛒 Memorial Day DEALS 🥳 🙌 🛒
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Six emojis. Two words. Zero category, zero offer, zero reason to care. "🥳 🙌 🛒 Memorial Day DEALS 🥳 🙌 🛒" is Wayfair's most content-free subject line of this entire Memorial Day run — and there has been stiff competition. Compare to May 21's "🔴 CLEARANCE ⚪ MEMORIAL DAY 🔵," which at least had the dignity of one flag-themed aesthetic. This one reads like someone set their subject-line generator to MINIMUM and hit send.
- Inside the email, the picture is considerably better. The body is structurally competent: a clear 40%/50% OFF hero, a named doorbuster (Adirondack chairs, 25% off buy-one), a "picked just for you" personalized grid, visible star ratings on product tiles, and a clean app CTA at the bottom. Content, category, and season are aligned for once — no mismatched knife sets or phantom chandeliers.
- The problem is that the subject line has already lost the subscriber. After six Memorial Day sends in as many days, Wayfair needed something that stopped the thumb. "Memorial Day DEALS" bookended by emoji triplets is the inbox equivalent of a shrug.

## What's working

- **Content matches the subject** — "Memorial Day DEALS" is so vague it can't mismatch; outdoor furniture, home goods, and big-name brands all qualify. A low bar cleared, but notable given this brand's week-long record of subject/body mismatches.
- **Named doorbuster is specific** — "Buy one Adirondack chair, get 25% OFF" is the sharpest single offer in the email and gives the scroll a landing point.
- **Star ratings on product tiles** — social proof is visible without the reader having to hunt for it.
- **App CTA is distinct** — the bottom exclusive-deals-on-app block is visually separated and doesn't compete with the primary sale flow.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, text is legible, no broken layout sections.

## What's weak

- **Subject is pure emoji noise** — six stacked emojis with "DEALS" is a hard spam signal and carries zero product or offer information.
- **Cadence is burned** — this is at minimum the seventh Memorial Day send this week; subscriber fatigue is real and the subject line does nothing to cut through it.
- **No time-bound deadline anywhere visible** — "Memorial Day" implies a weekend end-date, but the email never says "ends Sunday" or "ends Monday." The urgency is implied, not stated.
- **Multiple competing section headers** — "picked just for you," "doorbuster deals," and "big-name brands" compete for attention rather than building a single narrative.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "shop all sales" is the primary action text; for an email with a named doorbuster, that's a missed specific CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the subject with a single doorbuster anchor** — the Adirondack chair offer is the most specific, seasonal, and clickable thing in this email. Lead with it. The generic "DEALS" subject throws away the best hook in the body.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Adirondack chairs: buy one, get 25% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day doorbuster: outdoor furniture up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 40% off big-name brands — sale ends Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picked-for-you deals are inside, ending soon.`
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Sale ends Monday, May 26" somewhere in the hero converts "Memorial Day" urgency from implied to actionable. This one change lifts click urgency without redesigning anything.
- 3. **Change the primary CTA to match the doorbuster** — swap "shop all sales" for "Shop Adirondack Deals" or "Shop Outdoor" on the doorbuster tile specifically; keep "shop all sales" as a secondary link below.
- 4. **Drop two of the three competing section headers** — "picked just for you" + one other section is enough. Three named zones with different promises dilute the read.
- 5. **Kill the emoji stacking** — one seasonal emoji (🏖️ or 🇺🇸) maximum. Six emojis across the subject line triggers spam filters and reads as low-quality regardless of what's inside.
