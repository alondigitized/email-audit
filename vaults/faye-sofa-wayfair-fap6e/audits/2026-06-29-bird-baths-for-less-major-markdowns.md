# BIRD BATHS for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair's "BIRD BATHS for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡" lands as a warmed-over version of the same niche-noun ALL-CAPS template the brand has been cycling through for weeks — "MAILBOXES," "AREA RUGS.," and now this. The mechanic is the same: provocative single-category subject, then an email that doesn't follow through on that promise. Open the email and you're greeted with a generic "score styles you ♥" hero collage, then — bafflingly — a "picked just for you" section dominated by area rugs. Bird bath content finally appears in a third section labelled "your future forest are here," well below the fold.
- The bones here are fine: loyalty rewards are called out clearly, the 20% off offer is present, and the layout renders without obvious bugs. But there's a disconnect between what the subject line promises and what the email delivers at the top. A subscriber who opened expecting bird bath markdowns gets area rugs first — that's a broken expectation that cools click intent fast.
- This email is also the second bird bath send in Wayfair's recent barrage (the prior "BIRDBATHS." send from the brand history already burned the format's novelty). The *MAJOR* asterisks and ALL-CAPS escalation read less like urgency and more like a brand out of ideas for differentiation. Serviceable, but not earning any goodwill.

## What's working

- **Loyalty callout is visible and early** — "get 5% rewards… on every item, always" appears in its own band near the top, which is a clear membership benefit signal.
- **20% off offer is present** — a concrete discount number is visible in the lower body, which satisfies the minimum bar for a promo email.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breaks visible in the screenshot.
- **Seasonal fit** — the outdoor/garden angle is appropriate timing for early July.

## What's weak

- **Subject promises bird baths; hero delivers a generic style collage** — the subject set a specific expectation that the hero completely fails to meet.
- **"Picked just for you" features area rugs, not bird baths** — the personalized section pushes the subject's product category even further down, undermining cohesion.
- **Bird bath content is buried in the third main section** — a subscriber on mobile has to scroll past two unrelated modules before reaching what the subject advertised.
- **ALL-CAPS + asterisks + arrow emoji is spam-signal territory** — the subject line "BIRD BATHS for less. *MAJOR* markdowns ➡" stacks multiple inbox-hygiene red flags simultaneously.
- **Repeats the niche-noun gambit on a category already sent** — per brand history, "BIRDBATHS." already ran; this send doesn't offer a meaningfully different angle or deeper discount.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with the bird bath, not a generic collage** — if the subject promises "BIRD BATHS for less," the first visual should be a bird bath. One strong hero image with a dollar-anchored headline ("Bird baths from $39") would close the expectation gap immediately.
- 2. **Pull the bird bath product grid above "picked just for you"** — or at minimum, remove the rug module from this send so the content matches the subject throughout.
- 3. **Swap ALL-CAPS + asterisks for a cleaner hook** — the spam-signal overload (caps, asterisks, arrow) costs deliverability goodwill. A concrete price anchor does more work.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the "20% off" offer** — "Ends Sunday" or "This weekend only" converts a vague markdown into genuine urgency.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 20% off bird baths (and the rest of your garden)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stone, ceramic, and solar styles. Outdoor refresh starts here.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on orders $35+ — sale ends Sunday.`
