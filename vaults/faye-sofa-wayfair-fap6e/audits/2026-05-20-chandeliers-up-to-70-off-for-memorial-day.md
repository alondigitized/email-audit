# CHANDELIERS ✨ Up to 70% OFF for MEMORIAL DAY ✨ 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- "CHANDELIERS" is the entire premise of this subject line, and the email delivers zero chandeliers. The hero opens on an outdoor pergola structure, the category tiles are curtains & drapes, storage & organization, and dining furniture, and the doorbuster grid shows patio sets and gym equipment. This is the same kitchen-sink clearance template Wayfair has been firing daily for a week — only the product noun bolted onto the subject line changes. That bait-and-switch pattern was obvious when it happened on KITCHEN MATS (5/18), LOW-PILE RUGS (5/19), and BEDDING SETS (5/19). With chandeliers it is especially egregious because lighting is a considered, room-defining purchase — a subscriber who clicks expecting chandelier options and lands on outdoor furniture is not browsing further.
- The execution itself is competent: Memorial Day urgency is clear, the BOGO dining call-out and "up to 80% off" storage banner are credible anchors, and the layout renders cleanly. But none of that matters when the category promise evaporates the moment the email opens. Wayfair has now sent roughly eight emails in four days using the same formula. The canopy email on 5/18 earned its 8/10 because it actually delivered on the subject; this one cannot make that claim.
- Clear judgement: a mechanically decent clearance mailer wearing a false label. The chandelier subject will drive opens from a specific intent segment and immediately disappoint them.

## What's working

- "Up to 80% OFF" storage banner and BOGO dining callout are concrete, credible percentage offers.
- Memorial Day Exclusive "doorbuster deals" section adds urgency framing beyond the generic clearance copy.
- "free & easy delivery over $35*" CTA module is prominent and repeats on every Wayfair send — reliable trust signal for existing subscribers.
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible.
- Wayfair Rewards block at the bottom surfaces loyalty value for members.

## What's weak

- No chandelier anywhere in the email — the subject noun is a complete fabrication relative to the body content.
- Hero image is an outdoor pergola, which signals outdoor furniture, not lighting — wrong category from the first pixel.
- "shop all sales" CTA is maximally generic; there is no chandelier or lighting-specific link to rescue the intent.
- This is the eighth+ Memorial Day clearance email in four days from Wayfair — the "exclusive doorbuster" framing has lost all credibility given the cadence.
- "picked just for you" personalization language appears above a completely non-personalized department dump.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Actually send chandeliers.** Filter the product grid to lighting — chandeliers, pendants, flush mounts. If the warehouse has Memorial Day stock on lighting, this email could be genuinely useful to a specific subscriber segment. If it doesn't, don't use the subject noun.
- 2. **Replace the hero image.** Swap the outdoor pergola for a dramatic lit chandelier in a dining or entryway setting. The subject primes visual expectation; the hero instantly breaks it.
- 3. **Make the CTA category-specific.** "Shop Chandeliers — Up to 70% OFF" beats "shop all sales" for the subscriber who opened for lighting.
- 4. **Drop the "picked just for you" header** unless personalization is actually firing. A generic clearance grid wearing a personalization label reads as condescending to anyone who notices.
- 5. **Thin the cadence.** Eight emails in four days during one holiday weekend is a suppression risk. Batch the remaining Memorial Day sends or skip a day.
