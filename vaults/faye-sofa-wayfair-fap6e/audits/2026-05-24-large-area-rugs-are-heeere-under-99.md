# LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's best subject line of the entire Memorial Day run. "LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99" gives the subscriber exactly what they need to decide — category and price — in 38 characters. After a week of emoji soup, mystery hashtags, and the infamous "#kitchenmats," a legible subject with a concrete price anchor feels like a breakthrough.
- The email doesn't hold up the bargain. The top zone does show rug imagery, which is more than Wayfair managed in, say, the "KNIFE SETS" send (zero knife sets, May 23). But within two scrolls, the email has pivoted to an "Open Box Deals" section and a "not just for coffee." coffee-table feature, and the bottom half is the same scattered home-goods grid Wayfair has been recycling since at least May 21. The $99 price point — the email's entire reason to open — never reappears in the body as a shoppable anchor.
- The "Open Box Deals" callout is the one genuine differentiator. It's clearly branded, visually distinct, and offers a value framing Wayfair hasn't leaned on in this Memorial Day run. It's just in the wrong email. If it ran under a subject line that promised it, it would land much harder.

## What's working

- Subject line is Wayfair's strongest of the Memorial Day run — specific category, specific price point, under 50 chars
- Top scroll zone anchors on rug-adjacent imagery, partially honoring the subject promise
- "Open Box Deals" is a genuine creative differentiator, visually distinct with its own styling
- "not just for coffee." is punchy section copy — actual brand voice, not placeholder

## What's weak

- The $99 price point disappears after the subject — it's never repeated in a shoppable hero grid with visible prices
- Email pivots to Open Box and coffee tables by the second content block, diluting the rug promise fast
- No deadline or end-date visible anywhere — "Memorial Day" implies urgency but the email doesn't close it
- Bottom product grid is the same mixed-category dump from at least four prior sends this week
- No rug sizing, style, or fit detail visible — the highest-friction decision for a rug shopper is "what size," and it goes unaddressed

- 3. **Move or cut the coffee table section** — it undercuts the rug narrative; put it in its own dedicated send
- 4. **Repeat "$99" on the first CTA button copy** — "Shop Rugs Under $99" outperforms "Shop Now" every time
- 5. **Add size-navigation tiles** (5×8 / 8×10 / Runners) — removes the #1 friction point for rug shoppers
