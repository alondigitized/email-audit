# SERVING BOWLS 🔔 Memorial Day Clearance is ON 🔔 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair sends a Memorial Day clearance blast with a hero "up to 70% OFF" and a product grid spanning furniture, rugs, and outdoor lanterns. The structure is familiar and competent — the discount is front and center, the category breakdown is scannable, and the delivery threshold is called out. Nothing is broken in the render.
- The problem is the subject line. "SERVING BOWLS" is shouted in ALL CAPS as the hook, but serving bowls are nowhere to be found as a featured product, category header, or visible CTA inside the email. A recipient who opened specifically looking for serving bowl deals will scan for three seconds, find rugs and lanterns, and bounce. That's a direct credibility tax on every future send — subscribers learn that the subject line is just bait for "here's our full clearance."
- This is a well-executed broadcast that undermines itself with a subject/content mismatch. The bones are solid; the subject line is the single biggest failure here.

## What's working

- **70% OFF hero is impossible to miss** — the discount is the first thing your eye lands on after the logo.
- **Category breakdowns with inline discounts** (furniture 65%, rugs 70%, outdoor 60%) give quick-scanners a reason to keep reading.
- **Loyalty spend-to-save offer visible** ("Spend $300, save $50") adds a second hook for existing members.
- **Free & easy delivery threshold** ($35+) is clearly surfaced — removes a common friction objection before the click.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible.

## What's weak

- **Serving bowls are not in the email.** The subject line shouts a specific product category that has no visible presence in the body — not in the hero, not in a dedicated section, not even in the product grid thumbnails.
- **"Shop all sales" is the weakest possible CTA copy** — sends users to the broadest possible destination with no category anchoring.
- **ALL CAPS + double bell emoji in subject** signals promotional aggression; spam filters and cynical subscribers treat this as a yellow flag.
- **Product thumbnails are too small to drive desire** — the grid at the top and the "Your Home, These Picks" section feel like visual noise rather than curated picks.
- **No urgency deadline visible** — "Memorial Day Clearance is ON" implies a time limit but no end date or countdown is shown in the body.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Either feature serving bowls or change the subject.** This is the single highest-leverage fix. If the segment was built on serving-bowl browse behavior, add a visible serving bowls section or banner. If not, drop the mismatch entirely.
- 2. **Replace "shop all sales" with a category-specific CTA** ("Shop Serving Bowls," "Shop Outdoor," etc.) tied to the strongest performing category.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — "Sale ends Monday, May 26" in the body converts the implied urgency into something actionable.
- 4. **Drop ALL CAPS from the subject line** and reduce to one emoji maximum.
- 5. **Consolidate the product grids** — two separate "picks" grids plus three category rows makes the email read as a bulk send rather than a curated recommendation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day Clearance: up to 70% off serving bowls & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Serving bowls, rugs & more — Memorial Day prices now live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, and kitchen picks — sale ends May 26`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free delivery on orders $35+ · Ends Memorial Day`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name`**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; display: block; height: 48px !important }
```
`break-all` splits words at arbitrary character boundaries. For product names this produces visually broken text (e.g. "Kitch-enAid"). `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word` is the correct fix for overflow containment without mid-word splits.

**Custom web font loaded via `@font-face`**
```
src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff)
```
`woff` only (no `woff2`, no `woff` + `woff2` stack). Web fonts render in Apple Mail and iOS Mail; Outlook (all versions), Gmail (web + Android), and Yahoo silently fall back to `mso-font-alt: "Arial"`. This is expected behavior for email, but if the design assumes Sofia renders universally, that assumption is incorrect in the majority of B2C email clients by market share.
