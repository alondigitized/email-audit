# TRASH CANS & RECYCLING ⚫ UP TO 80% OFF ⚫ BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY ⚫ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's eighth-plus email in this same Black Friday in July run, and it commits the series' signature error more cleanly than any prior send: the subject promises "TRASH CANS & RECYCLING" and the email contains zero trash cans, zero recycling bins, and zero mention of either category anywhere in its roughly 30 tiles of content. Not in the hero, not in the "picked just for you" grid, not in the four discount-category banners (rugs, bedding, TV stands, cooling), not in the "Your Home, These Picks" footer grid. Compare this to 2026-07-24's "Drying Racks" send, which at least surfaced drying racks twice in its footer — this one delivers nothing the subject promised.
- What's actually inside is a generic BFIJ dump: a Bedsure bedding hero, storage furniture, a hanging egg chair, cookware, lanterns, a wine fridge, and repeat filler modules ("Our Favorite Area Rugs," "Kitchen Mats Under $30," "On Sale Now: Bed Frames") that have shown up verbatim in prior sends this week. The execution quality (clean render, clear pricing, visible ratings, purple brand consistency) is fine in isolation, but the subject-to-body gap is now the defining trait of this campaign, and a subscriber who opens for trash cans gets nothing to click.
- Judgement: solid production values wasted on a subject line the email has no intention of honoring — and this is the worst version of that pattern yet, since the promised category doesn't even make a token cameo.

## What's working

- Hero banner leads with the offer immediately — "80% OFF, Everything Ships Free" is visible without scrolling.
- Clean render throughout: no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- Was/Now pricing and star ratings are visible on nearly every product tile, giving concrete purchase signals.
- Consistent purple/white Wayfair branding and a clear "shop all sales" CTA button.
- Loyalty/value props (Wayfair Rewards, Pay Over Time, Wayfair Professional) are surfaced near the footer.

## What's weak

- The subject's entire premise — trash cans and recycling — never appears anywhere in the email; this is a hard bait-and-switch, not a soft mismatch.
- Subject line is 64 characters and stacked with ALL CAPS phrases plus three black-circle emoji dividers, reading as spammy rather than concrete.
- "Black Friday in July" as a campaign banner is now stale — per brand history this is at least the eighth near-identical send in roughly a week, so the urgency no longer reads as credible.
- The hero product (Bedsure bedding) has nothing to do with the subject or with most of the grid below it, so there's no single coherent "pick one thing and sell it" moment.
- Footer "Your Home, These Picks" grid repeats modules (drying racks, area rugs, kitchen mats) seen in prior sends this week, reinforcing fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the core defect first: either write a subject that matches the body's actual contents (storage/bedding/outdoor), or build an email that actually contains trash cans and recycling bins if that's the intended category push. This single fix would do more for trust and click-through than any copy tweak.
- 2. Cut the emoji-and-caps subject formatting — it's read as spam-adjacent for several sends running and no longer differentiates.
- 3. Replace "Black Friday in July" as the dominant banner with a category-specific hero when the subject promises a category — consistency between promise and payload is the fix, not more of the same banner.
- 4. Slow the cadence or vary the module set — reusing "Our Favorite Area Rugs" / "Kitchen Mats Under $30" across multiple sends this week signals a content-recycling problem to attentive subscribers.
- 5. Give the CTA buttons specific copy ("Shop Storage Deals," "Shop Bedding") instead of generic "Shop Now" / "shop all sales."
