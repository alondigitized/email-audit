# BEDDING SETS ➡️ Memorial Day CLEARANCE ➡️ Home UPGRADES ➡️ 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's seventh Memorial Day clearance email in four days, and the formula is completely legible now: bolt a specific category noun ("BEDDING SETS") onto the subject line, then send the same all-department clearance dump underneath. The hero leads with a "picked just for you" personalization header and a 70% OFF banner — which is fine — but within two scrolls you're looking at Adirondack chairs, brand-name vanities, outdoor yard shots, and a full pizza spread that has no business being in a bedding email. The bedding the subject promised appears at the very bottom, in the "Your Home, These Picks" carousel, small and easily missed.
- The email isn't broken. The 70% OFF anchor is visible, the Memorial Day hook is timely, and the brand-name deals section (Costway, Allen+Roth, Glacier Bay) adds some credibility. But Wayfair is now running these so fast — three emails on 5/18 alone — that cadence fatigue is real. The subject line uses three ➡️ arrows as separators between three different value propositions, which reads like a spam dashboard rather than a coherent pitch.
- Verdict: competent execution on a structurally broken brief. Promising bedding and delivering everything but bedding is not a targeting strategy.

## What's working

- **70% OFF is immediately visible** in the hero — no scroll required, no ambiguity.
- **"MEMORIAL DAY EXCLUSIVE doorbuster deals"** banner lands strong and makes the sale feel time-bounded.
- **BOGO Adirondack chair offer** (buy one, get one 25% OFF) is specific and seasonal — the most concrete, actionable offer in the email.
- **Brand-name deals section** (Costway 65% off, Allen+Roth 45%, Glacier Bay 45%) adds perceived legitimacy beyond generic clearance language.
- **"Free & easy delivery over $35"** is clearly called out and removes a common objection.

## What's weak

- **Bedding — the hero noun in the subject — is buried at the very bottom** in a small "Your Home, These Picks" carousel, below furniture, outdoor gear, and a pizza photo.
- **A pizza image appears mid-scroll**, which is jarring and incoherent in a home goods clearance send — it breaks visual trust with no obvious explanation.
- **Three competing value propositions in the subject** ("BEDDING SETS ➡️ Memorial Day CLEARANCE ➡️ Home UPGRADES ➡️") means no single hook lands; the arrows read as spam formatting.
- **"Picked just for you"** is a generic label, not actual personalization — no name, no category-level signal that matches the subject's bedding promise.
- **Cadence is at saturation.** This is the 7th email in four days; the 5/19 "LOW-PILE RUGS" email already went out today, making this a double-send day.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restructure the email to actually deliver bedding.** If "BEDDING SETS" is in the subject, the hero image, the first product grid, and the primary CTA should all be bedding. Move Adirondack chairs and vanities to a secondary section or a separate send.
- 2. **Kill the pizza image or replace it with outdoor dining/entertaining context.** If it's meant to evoke outdoor summer entertaining, it needs furniture or table settings in frame — a standalone pizza reads as a wrong-asset mistake.
- 3. **Collapse the subject to one value prop.** Pick the strongest single angle: the specific category or the clearance discount, not both plus a third catch-all.
- 4. **Slow the cadence.** Two sends in one day (rugs + this) is the point where unsubscribes accelerate. Batch Memorial Day sends to one per day maximum.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Web font loaded as WOFF only, no WOFF2:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Sofia";
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
  mso-font-alt: "Arial"
}
```
- No `woff2` source listed. WOFF2 offers ~30% better compression; its absence increases load time in clients that do render external fonts.
- Most email clients (Gmail, Outlook) block `@font-face` entirely, so `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` is the Outlook fallback — but there is no generic `font-family` stack fallback in the CSS for non-Outlook clients that block web fonts (e.g., Gmail Android). The fallback chain should be `"Sofia", Arial, sans-serif` declared on body/container elements.

**Issue — `.ProductRec-name` uses `word-break: break-all` on mobile:**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; display: block; height: 48px !important }
```
`break-all` breaks mid-word at any character boundary, which can produce unreadable product names. `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word` is the safer choice.

**Minor — Single `.woff` font request is blocked by default in most email clients.** The network fetch will fail silently and fall through to system fonts, which is acceptable — but the request itself adds latency in clients that do attempt to load it (Apple Mail, some iOS Mail).
