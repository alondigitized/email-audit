# KNIFE SETS 🙌 UP TO 70% OFF 🌞 Prep for sunny season 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair has done it again. "KNIFE SETS 🙌 UP TO 70% OFF" is the subject line, and the email contains zero knife sets. Not one. The actual content is the identical Memorial Day clearance dump the brand has been cycling since May 19: "picked just for you" hero tiles (recliner, shelving), doorbuster BOGO deals on Adirondack chairs, ceiling fans, power recliners, and rugs. The gap between subject promise and email delivery is now at its widest of the entire Memorial Day run.
- What makes this send uniquely egregious is that knife sets are a highly specific kitchen category — a shopper who opens expecting a curated knife-block deal is going to bounce the moment they see patio furniture. Compare that to May 21's "POTTING BENCHES & TABLES" misfire, where at least the subject and the actual outdoor content were in the same general universe (garden). Knife sets and Adirondack chairs share nothing.
- The underlying email itself is competent — clean render, multiple concrete offers, a working CTA — but it's wasted on a subject line that torpedoes trust before the first scroll.

## What's working

- Multiple concrete discount signals are visible: UP TO 75% OFF, BOGO 15% OFF, Adirondack chair 25% OFF — easy to skim.
- "Doorbuster deals" section creates a secondary urgency layer that the hero row lacks.
- Free delivery threshold ($35) is clearly surfaced mid-email, reducing purchase friction.
- Visual render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping elements, no lorem ipsum.
- Star ratings visible under product tiles provide baseline social proof without requiring any scrolling.

## What's weak

- Not a single knife set appears anywhere in the email — this is pure bait-and-switch on the subject line's core promise.
- "Prep for sunny season" tacked after the knife sets line makes zero tonal sense; knives aren't a seasonal outdoor purchase.
- The "picked just for you" hero section feels generic — a recliner and shelving unit do not say "personalized."
- Doorbuster deal for "buy one Adirondack chair, save 25% OFF" is a weak BOGO structure — one chair at 25% off is just a discount, not a compelling buy-one-get-one.
- This is Wayfair's eighth Memorial Day send in four days; the cadence is exhausted and the formula is completely visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the actual email.** The single highest-leverage fix. If Wayfair is running a knife sets sale, build that email. If this is the standard Memorial Day dump, don't put "KNIFE SETS" in the subject. The trust erosion from repeated mismatches will eventually crater open rates.
- 2. **Give the hero one job.** The "picked just for you" section should feature two or three products with a unifying reason — not a recliner next to shelving. Lead with the doorbuster if that's the strongest offer.
- 3. **Cut the emoji stack.** 🙌 + 🌞 in the same subject line alongside ALL CAPS reads as low-quality promotional spam. One emoji max, tied to a specific product or mood, not both.
- 4. **Put a hard deadline on the doorbuster.** "Doorbuster deals" implies fleeting availability; adding "ends Monday midnight" makes it credible.
