# 📣 WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT 📣 PRICE DROPS 📣
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair is running the same warehouse-clearout playbook it's been on for at least a week, and this send is the loudest version yet — three megaphone emojis in the subject, all-caps everything, no specific product or deadline. The hero lands with a legitimate 60% OFF hook, and the "picked just for you" grid does add a personalization layer that slightly differentiates this from the June 1 resend of the outdoor-rugs email. But the email's fundamental problem is identical to every other Wayfair send this month: it promises a curated warehouse event and delivers a broad algorithmic category grid with no narrative thread.
- The "Tuesday, September" label visible on one of the bottom product tiles is a genuine content bug — that date has no business appearing in a June email and signals either a stale sale tile or a broken content-population step. It won't kill the campaign, but it undermines the clearout-urgency framing.
- The judgment: a structurally adequate sale email executed with too many spam signals in the subject and zero editorial restraint in the body. It's the fourth or fifth "clearout" framing this week, and even loyal subscribers are going to start scrolling past.

## What's working

- **60% OFF is front-and-center** — the hero badge is the first thing the eye lands on and it's a genuinely strong discount claim.
- **"Picked just for you" header** — adding a personalization layer to the grid is the right instinct; star ratings on individual tiles add lightweight social proof.
- **Seasonal category mix is relevant** — patio umbrellas, outdoor furniture, grills, and garden are all appropriate for early June, making the content feel timely even if the framing is lazy.
- **Clean render overall** — images load, layout holds, no overlapping text or broken sections (aside from the date anomaly below).

## What's weak

- **Triple-📣 all-caps subject is a spam signal** — three megaphone emojis plus full all-caps is the exact inbox pattern that trained subscribers to stop opening.
- **"Tuesday, September" label on a product tile** — a date that is months in the future (or from a prior campaign) appearing in a June email is a content-population bug; it undercuts the clearout urgency.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email** — "Warehouse Clearout" implies limited time but there is no end date, no countdown, nothing to motivate now-vs-later.
- **No single hero product or category** — the grid is algorithmically broad; May 30's "TABLE LAMPS ⌛ UP TO 60% OFF ends June 3" email showed what happens when Wayfair commits to one category and a real deadline — it scored 8/10.
- **Cadence fatigue** — this is at minimum the third clearout-themed send in seven days. The brand is training subscribers to ignore the word "clearout."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one category and name a deadline** — the May 30 lamp email proved this works. "60% OFF Outdoor Furniture — ends Tuesday" beats "WAREHOUSE CLEAROUT" every time. One focused email outperforms five broad ones.
- 2. **Fix the "Tuesday, September" content bug** — audit whatever feed or sale-tile template is populating that date label before the next send.
- 3. **Cut to one emoji max in the subject** — replace the triple-📣 with a single emoji or none; the all-caps already supplies urgency.
- 4. **Add a hard deadline to the hero** — even "this week only" is better than no time signal.
- 5. **Surface the best deal specifically** — the 60% OFF badge in the hero doesn't say what category. "60% OFF Patio Furniture" is worth a click; "60% OFF" is worth a shrug.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Warehouse Clearout: up to 60% off outdoor furniture`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off ends soon — patio, grills & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your summer upgrade is in here — deep cuts on the categories you've been browsing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearout prices on outdoor furniture, grills, mattresses and more — while stock lasts`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Flagged issues:**

- **WOFF-only font delivery**: `@font-face` loads only `sofia_reg.woff` — no WOFF2 variant. WOFF2 is ~30% smaller and supported by all modern clients that support web fonts in email. `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback for Outlook is correctly set.

- **Fixed pixel heights on mobile product cells** create content-clipping risk:
  - `.ProductRec-name { height: 48px }` — long product names will clip silently
  - `.ProductRec-price { height: 40px }` — sale price + strikethrough may overflow
  - `.ProductRec-stars, .ProductRec-shippingCopy { height: 16px }` — tight; icon rendering varies across clients

- **`word-break: break-all` on `.ProductRec-name`**: This is aggressive — it will break mid-word regardless of word boundaries. `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word` is safer for product copy.

- **Duplicate mobile media query blocks**: `.u-Button`, `.u-Button--primary`, `.u-Button--transactional`, and `.u-Button--alternate` each declare identical `padding: 16px 0 !important` in separate `@media` blocks — four blocks for the same rule. Minor CSS bloat; negligible deliverability impact but increases template payload.
