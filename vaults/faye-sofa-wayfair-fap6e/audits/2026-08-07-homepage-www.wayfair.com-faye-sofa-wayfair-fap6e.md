# Homepage snapshot · www.wayfair.com · 2026-08-07
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is very "we know you love a deadline" energy — I open the app and I'm immediately hit with a giant purple "ENDS TONIGHT" card stacked on top of a really cute yellow-and-blue striped patio scene. It reads like classic Wayfair: rewards-gated flash sale, big percentage-off badge, urgency copy doing the heavy lifting. Right now it's pushing outdoor furniture, not the mid-century living room stuff I actually browse for hours, so it caught my eye but it's not quite fishing in my pond.

## What's working

- The "ENDS TONIGHT" card — I'm a flash-sale girl, this is exactly the kind of countdown pressure that gets me to tap before I overthink it.
- The "up to 50% OFF" badge stacked right on the hero image — clean, unmissable, no scrolling needed.
- "Wayfair Verified" checkmark badges on the product tiles below (the black chair, the rocking chairs) — that little trust signal matters when I'm about to sink money into furniture I can't sit in first.
- The heart/save icons on every tile — perfect for my "browse for hours, decide later" habit.

## What's weak

- The hero is 100% outdoor patio furniture — croquet mallets, yellow bistro chairs, striped rug. I'm shopping living-room and starter-home basics, not backyard sets, so this doesn't pull me in personally.
- No people anywhere in the shot — just product and props. A little sterile for a "picture it in my house" browser like me.
- "Up to 50% off" with no visible category or fine print — I've been burned before by "up to" deals that turn out to be one clearance rug at that price.
- No star ratings or review counts visible on the product tiles, and reviews are basically my whole decision-making process.

## Recommendations

- Swap the hero rotation to lead with a living-room or mid-century vignette for shoppers whose browse history skews that way, keep outdoor as slide 2/3/4.
- Add a star rating + review count under each "Wayfair Verified" tile — even just "4.7★ (2,300)" would do a lot of trust-building work above the fold.
- Put a specific category or dollar cap next to "up to 50% off" (e.g. "up to 50% off outdoor") so it doesn't read like classic bait pricing.
- A recently-viewed or "Picked for you" rail right under the hero would hook returning shoppers like me faster than a generic "Wayfair Verified" category rail.
