# Open Box Deals are here 📦
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a category directory dressed up as an email. The hero — mismatched furniture pieces (an orange armchair, a green lamp, a mustard pillow, red oven mitts) crammed under a bold "Open Box Deals" wordmark — sets up the idea that these are clearance/liquidation items, but then the email pivots into a wall of 17 identical purple category buttons (Wall Art, Area Rugs, Bar Stools, Bedding...) that could belong to literally any Wayfair sale. There's no product photography, no specific SKUs, nothing that actually says "these are open-box/returned items" beyond the headline graphic.
- Compare this to 2026-07-30's "$15" send, which echoed its subject hook directly in the hero and scored 9/10 for that discipline — this email does the opposite. It promises a specific, interesting concept (open box = deep, unusual discounts on real inventory) and then delivers the exact same generic "up to X% OFF" category-grid template Wayfair has run all week, just relabeled. The hero image itself is also a legibility problem: five unrelated objects overlapping in a small frame reads as visual noise, not "here's what's in stock."
- Judgment: this is a weak, low-effort send that undersells its own premise. Open-box deals are inherently more clickable than generic clearance because shoppers assume steeper savings and are curious what's actually available — this email never shows them anything.

## What's working

- The "Open Box Deals" headline is bold, high-contrast red-on-cream and reads clearly even at a glance.
- Percent-off figures are specific and go up to 70% (Area Rugs, Bar Stools, Lighting), which is a genuinely strong discount depth.
- Categories are legible with clear "up to X% OFF" labeling on every single button — no ambiguity about what's on sale where.
- No broken images or rendering errors — unlike the 2026-07-31 outdoor privacy screens send, everything here actually loads.
- Footer perks (Wayfair Rewards, Pay Over Time, Professional, Exclusive Brands) and app-download QR block are present and clean.

## What's weak

- Zero actual product photography of open-box items — the hero collage (armchair, lamp, pillow, oven mitts) is generic stock-looking furniture, not identifiably "returned/open-box" merchandise.
- 17 nearly-identical purple category buttons in a row is an enormous, monotonous scroll with no visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to look first.
- The email never explains what "open box" means or why it's a differentiated deal (no "like-new," "inspected," "limited quantity" messaging) — it's just a rebrand of a standard clearance layout.
- No urgency or scarcity signal anywhere, despite open-box inventory being inherently limited/one-off — a missed and easy win.
- The hero image composition itself is cluttered — mitts, pillow, chair, and lamp overlap awkwardly with no clear focal product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic category grid with 4-6 actual open-box product tiles (photo, was/now price, "1 left" or "Open Box – Like New" badge) — this is the single highest-impact fix since the entire premise of the email is unshown.
- 2. Add scarcity/urgency copy ("Limited quantities — once it's gone, it's gone") since open-box stock is inherently non-repeatable, unlike normal clearance.
- 3. Cut the button list from 17 to the top 6-8 highest-discount categories to reduce scroll fatigue and sharpen hierarchy.
- 4. Clean up the hero collage — pick 2-3 cohesive items instead of 5 competing objects.
- 5. Add a preview/preheader with real copy — currently blank/junk, a wasted opportunity to reinforce "open box" scarcity before the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box Deals: up to 70% off, while it lasts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Returned. Inspected. Up to 70% off today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited open-box inventory — rugs, lighting, and more up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One-of-a-kind savings on like-new furniture, while supplies last`
