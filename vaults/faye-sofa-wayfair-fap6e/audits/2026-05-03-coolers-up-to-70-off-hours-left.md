# COOLERS ⏱️  UP TO 70% OFF ⏱️ HOURS LEFT ⏱️ 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- A classic Wayfair multi-module sale blast. The urgency framing in the subject is aggressive (triple clock emojis, ALL CAPS), and the email delivers a legitimately deep discount promise — but almost nothing inside is specifically about coolers. The body pivots immediately to a broad home furnishings recommendation grid: sofas, outdoor canopy tents, shelving, rugs, curtains, wall art. The subject-to-body mismatch is the single biggest liability here. Everything else is competent Wayfair execution — clear discounts, personalized product picks, app upsell, Rewards section — but the experience feels like a bait-and-click rather than a focused send.

## What's working

- **Discount specificity by category.** The horizontal category bars ("rugs + runners | up to 70% OFF", "home office furniture | up to 60% OFF") are a clean UX pattern — they let a skimmer self-select in under a second.
- **"Picked just for you" module.** Personalized recommendation grid with star ratings visible on product cards adds social proof and relevance signal without requiring scrolling.
- **Free delivery threshold visible.** "Free & easy delivery over $35" banner removes a common checkout objection early.
- **Multi-surface coverage.** Wayfair Rewards, Flexible Financing, Wayfair Professional, and app-exclusive deals are all present — every buyer type has a hook.
- **Urgency is reinforced inside.** The "ENDS TONIGHT" banner at the top of the body matches the subject's "HOURS LEFT" — the clock isn't just a subject line tease.

## What's weak

- **Subject-body mismatch.** The subject says "COOLERS" in bold first position. The email body contains no visible cooler product, no cooler category bar, no cooler hero image. This is a credibility-eroding bait: if you opened for coolers, you immediately feel misled.
- **Hero image is not cooler-related.** The hero shows a blue outdoor canopy/gazebo structure. It's outdoor-adjacent, but not the product category promised.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "Shop all sales" is a homepage redirect masquerading as a CTA. It does not fulfill the specific promise made in the subject.
- **Module bloat dilutes urgency.** The time-pressure framing in the subject ("HOURS LEFT") dissipates across six-plus content modules. By the time you reach the app promo, the urgency has evaporated.
- **Product detail is invisible at thumbnail scale.** The recommended product cards are small enough that sizing, material, and price are unreadable — you're clicking blind.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the cooler promise.** If "coolers" is the hook, the first visible module above the fold should be a cooler hero or a "Shop Coolers — Up to 70% Off" category link. Right now the subject sells a product the body doesn't stock.
- 2. **Add a cooler-specific CTA.** Replace or supplement "Shop all sales" with "Shop Coolers" pointing directly to the filtered category page.
- 3. **Tighten the subject line.** Drop two of the three clock emojis and move the specific offer ahead of the product noun: "Up to 70% off coolers — ends tonight" is cleaner, less spammy, and more credible.
- 4. **Surface price on product cards.** Even small-format pricing (was/now) would lift click confidence on the recommendation grid.
- 5. **Cut the bottom modules or push them below the fold.** Wayfair Rewards, Flexible Financing, and the app promo are evergreen — they don't need to compete with a time-sensitive flash sale.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font — WOFF only, no WOFF2:**
```css
src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
```
WOFF2 offers ~30% smaller file size. Modern email clients that support web fonts also support WOFF2. The `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` fallback is correctly set, so Outlook degrades gracefully — this is a performance observation, not a breakage.

**`word-break: break-all` on product names (mobile):**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; overflow: hidden; height: 48px !important }
```
`break-all` will split mid-word on any character, which can produce broken hyphenation in product names. `overflow-wrap: break-word` or `word-break: break-word` is less aggressive and preferable.

**Fixed pixel heights on dynamic content blocks (mobile):**
```css
.ProductRec-price { height: 40px }
.ProductRec-salesCopy { height: 28px }
.u-HalfWidthBlock .EventBlock-name { height: 48px !important }
```
Fixed heights clip content if price strings or event names exceed the reserved space. Risk increases with sale price + strikethrough stacked layouts.
