# ⏰ 72 hours only: UP TO 60% OFF ⏰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair's "72-HR CLEAROUT" is their strongest subject line in this entire June run — specific timing, a hard percentage, and credible urgency. But the body is the same multi-category scatter chassis they've been mailing every single day since at least June 17. The June 18 "Popular Picks" send scored 7/10 on body execution despite a terrible subject; this email inverts that — a great subject dragging around the same unfocused grid payload.
- The "Picked just for you" personalization block with visible star ratings is the brightest spot in the body. Everything below it is category tile soup: eight different discount buckets, none of them given room to breathe or a single hero product to anchor intent.
- The 72-hour window is real urgency — Wayfair doesn't always back that up with visible countdowns, but the subject does the heavy lifting. The problem is they've sent two emails on June 20 already (the flameless candles send earlier today). By the second email in a day, even a strong subject starts reading as background noise.

## What's working

- **"72-HR CLEAROUT" hero** lands immediately — the time constraint is the clearest single message Wayfair has communicated all month.
- **Category-level discount specificity at the top** (50% / 30% / 40% / 45% across four rooms) gives scanning shoppers a fast routing signal.
- **"Picked just for you" product grid** with visible star ratings is a genuine personalization signal — breaks from the purely editorial tone.
- **Afterpay financing mention** is smart for higher-ticket furniture clicks; lowers perceived barrier at consideration.
- **No render bugs** — the grid renders cleanly across sections; no broken images or layout drift visible.

## What's weak

- **Same scatter chassis, again** — this is structurally identical to the June 18 Outdoor Rugs send, the June 17 Dorm Deals send, and the June 20 Flameless Candles send from earlier today. Urgency subject + grid body is now a recognizable pattern, which kills urgency.
- **"UP TO 60% OFF" is ceiling, not floor** — the top-row categories show 30% and 40%, so the hero claim overpromises what most shoppers will find.
- **No single hero product** — eight category tiles with no clear winner means the eye wanders and no one item earns the click.
- **"Shop all sales" CTA** is the weakest possible button copy for a 72-hour clearout — it reads like a permanent link, not a deadline-driven action.
- **Two emails in one day** — this is the second Wayfair send on June 20; cadence fatigue is real and erodes open rates on the second touch regardless of subject quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product or category** — the 60% OFF badge is the loudest discount in the email; surface a single hero item (e.g., a specific sectional or bed frame at 60%) with a direct product link above the fold. The grid can live below it.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA copy** — "Shop 72-Hour Deals" or "Claim My 60% Off" turns a generic navigation link into a deadline action.
- 3. **Resolve the "UP TO" promise** — either lead with the category where 60% actually applies or drop the claim to the true median (40–45%) so it feels earned rather than bait-and-switch.
- 4. **Add a visible countdown or badge** — "Ends Sunday 11:59 PM" somewhere in the hero area would make "72 hours" feel tangible rather than marketing copy.
- 5. **Cut the second same-day send** or differentiate it by category (e.g., "the bedding-only clearout") so subscribers don't see two structurally identical emails before noon.
- **Subject Alt A:** `72 hrs: 60% off [specific category] ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearout ends Sunday — your shortlist inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your picked items are marked down — 72 hours to grab them.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% off bedding, 50% off outdoor — clock's running.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `word-break: break-all` on product names (mobile):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; ... }
}
```
`break-all` splits words at arbitrary character positions (e.g., "Chair" → "Cha-ir"). `break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` is the correct value for wrapping long product names without mid-word breaks. This will visually mangle product titles on iOS/Android.

**Issue — Web font with no broad fallback stack:**
```css
@font-face { font-family: "Sofia"; src: url(...sofia_reg.woff); mso-font-alt: "Arial" }
```
`mso-font-alt` handles Outlook only. Gmail (webmail + Android), Samsung Mail, and other clients that strip `<style>` will silently fall back to the user-agent default — not "Arial". The font stack on elements using `font-family: Sofia` should explicitly declare: `"Sofia", Arial, sans-serif` to guarantee a safe fallback everywhere.
