# KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- For once, Wayfair's subject line has a real receipt: "KITCHEN MATS under $30 ✨" and the very first tile under "the must-shop steals" is, in fact, a kitchen mat, priced under $30, image included. Compare that to the brand's 2026-07-25 "CRIBS" and "TRASH CANS & RECYCLING" sends, which named a product the email barely contained — this one delivers on the promise, at least at the top.
- But delivering the mat and *featuring* the mat aren't the same thing. The header banner — which is what actually gets seen first — makes zero mention of kitchen mats; it's the same generic "80% OFF BLACK FRIDAY IN JULY ENDS TOMORROW" banner this brand has now run across five-plus emails this week (dumbbells, trampolines, trash cans, cribs, and now this). The kitchen mat itself gets a single small tile before the email sprawls into 20+ unrelated categories — pool tables, birdbaths, ping-pong tables, window units, drying racks.
- Net: this is the most honest subject-to-content match Wayfair has sent in this run, but the execution still buries the hero instead of building around it. Judgement: middling — accurate, not compelling.

## What's working

- The "Kitchen Mats Under $30" tile is the literal first item in the first content grid, with a matching product photo.
- The "get 5% rewards on every item, always" banner is a clean visual break with real lifestyle photography.
- No render bugs — clean grid, no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- Consistent purple/white Wayfair brand system carried through every section, including the footer and app-download block.

## What's weak

- The top banner ("80% OFF Black Friday in July Ends Tomorrow") doesn't mention kitchen mats at all — a scanning reader sees generic sale copy, not the subject's promise.
- Kitchen mats get one small, equally-weighted tile among roughly 24 tiles spanning pool tables, birdbaths, bread pans, and air conditioners — no visual priority for the thing that got someone to open the email.
- The banner treatment and "ends tomorrow" urgency language is a near-verbatim repeat of what this brand ran on 2026-07-25 and 2026-07-26 sends — cadence fatigue is real at this point.
- No actual price, discount %, or close-up shown for the mats beyond the generic "Under $30" label — no reason to believe this is a deal versus the everyday price.

## Recommendations

- 1. Promote the kitchen mat to the hero banner spot with a real photo and specific price — right now the banner and the subject-matching content are disconnected.
- 2. Trim the tile wall from ~24 items to 6-8 genuinely adjacent categories (rugs, mats, slipcovers) instead of dumping pool tables and air conditioners into a "kitchen mats" email.
- 3. Put an actual discount on the mat tile itself ("$24.99, was $59.99") instead of the vague "Under $30."
- 4. Retire the "Black Friday in July Ends Tomorrow" banner — it's been reused across too many sends this week and undercuts its own urgency.
