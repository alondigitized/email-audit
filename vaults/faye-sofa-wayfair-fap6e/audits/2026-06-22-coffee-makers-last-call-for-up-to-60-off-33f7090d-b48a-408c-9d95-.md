# COFFEE MAKERS 📢 LAST CALL for UP TO 60% OFF ❗ ❗ ❗ 
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair pulls a classic bait-and-switch: the subject line screams "COFFEE MAKERS" in all caps, but open the email and you land on the same multi-category scatter chassis they've been mailing all week — mattresses, area rugs, home office furniture, bedding. Not a single coffee maker is visible in the hero, the category bands, or the "Your Home, These Picks" section. The "ENDS TONIGHT" red banner at the top provides genuine urgency, but the body doesn't earn it — it's the same grid of dozens of unrelated SKUs they've been running since June 19.
- This is also at minimum the seventh Wayfair email in four days. The June 20 send already promised "UP TO 60% OFF" with a 72-hour clock, and now the same discount reappears as a "LAST CALL" two days later. Any subscriber paying attention knows the sale never really ends.
- The email is competently rendered and brand-consistent. The failing is entirely strategic: the subject line is a traffic-farming lie, and the body has no conviction about anything.

## What's working

- **Red hero banner commands attention immediately** — "ENDS TONIGHT" reads as a credible, scannable close-out signal.
- **Category percentage bands are clean and specific** — "area rugs | up to 80% OFF" is concrete and easy to parse at a glance.
- **Wayfair Rewards and app CTA** at the footer give loyalty users a reason to stay in the ecosystem.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken gaps or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Subject says coffee makers; email has zero coffee makers.** This is a trust-eroding bait-and-switch that a repeat subscriber will notice immediately.
- **Same scatter chassis for the seventh consecutive send** — no editorial point of view, just a grid of everything on sale.
- **Subject line is spam-signal-heavy:** ALL CAPS, three ❗ emoji stacked, and a 📢 — all hallmarks of a message that trains inbox filters and subscribers alike to tune out.
- **"LAST CALL for UP TO 60% OFF" is the same offer as the June 20 "72 hours only: UP TO 60% OFF"** — the urgency is now provably hollow to anyone who got both emails.
- **No social proof, no product detail, no sizing/specs anywhere** — nothing to justify a click beyond the generic discount promise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the body to the subject.** If you're going to headline coffee makers, dedicate the hero to coffee makers — three to five specific models, prices before/after, visible savings. The scatter chassis actively punishes a specific subject line.
- 2. **Drop the spam-signal stack.** One urgency signal (either "LAST CALL" or ❗ or the 📢 — not all three) is enough. ALL CAPS on the category name is fine; ALL CAPS on every word is noise.
- 3. **Retire the "ENDS TONIGHT" urgency if the sale continues tomorrow.** Subscribers who got the June 20 "72 hours only" email and are now getting a "LAST CALL" two days later have learned that deadlines are decorative.
- 4. **Consolidate cadence.** Seven emails in four days is aggressive to the point of list fatigue. Wayfair's June run would land harder as three tight, category-specific sends rather than daily blasts of the same chassis.
