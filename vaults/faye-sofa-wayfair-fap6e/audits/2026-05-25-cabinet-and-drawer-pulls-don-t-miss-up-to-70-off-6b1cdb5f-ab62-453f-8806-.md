# CABINET AND DRAWER PULLS 🔴 ⚪ 🔵 DON’T MISS UP TO 70% OFF 🔴 ⚪ 🔵 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Day seven of Wayfair's Memorial Day run, and the subject-to-content mismatch is now fully ritual. "CABINET AND DRAWER PULLS" in the subject delivers a broad, algorithmic clearance grid — gazebos, beverage coolers, outdoor chairs, rugs, bathroom hardware, kitchen items — with one tiny image that might be a pull in the top-left corner. At this point it reads less like a targeting mistake and more like a template where someone types a random SKU category and ships it.
- The "picked just for you" framing and the "70% OFF" banner at the top are the email's two strongest assets, and they're squandered by a grid that contradicts both. If personalization is real, it isn't showing here. If 70% off on cabinet pulls is real, the subscriber has no way to find it without hunting.
- This is structurally the same email Wayfair sent on 5/24 with "POTTING BENCHES & TABLES" and 5/25 with "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR" — same layout, same flash-deals block, same footer. The category in the subject is a decoy; the email is always the same clearance carousel.

## What's working

- **70% off banner is immediately visible** — the discount claim lands above the fold and sets clear expectations.
- **Flash deals block adds urgency** — labeled percentage-off tiles (40% OFF, 15% OFF, etc.) give the mid-email section concrete scannability.
- **Free delivery threshold is stated** — "$35+" is visible and removes a common purchase friction point.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds at mobile width.

## What's weak

- **Subject category is a fiction** — "CABINET AND DRAWER PULLS" is nowhere prominent in the email body; the grid is the same generic clearance template used all week.
- **Subject line is 60+ characters with six emoji** — blows through the 50-char mobile threshold and trips spam-signal patterns simultaneously.
- **No credible deadline** — "DON'T MISS" is not a date; it floats with no anchor, making urgency feel reflexive rather than real.
- **"Picked just for you" feels hollow** — the product grid beneath it is clearly category-agnostic clearance, not personalized selection.
- **Sixth nearly-identical email this week** — cadence damage is real; a subscriber who opened the 5/24 sends already knows what to expect and is already tuning out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match subject to hero, or pick a real category and own it.** The 5/24 "LARGE AREA RUGS are heeere! under $99" (scored 7/10) worked because the email delivered on the subject. Either commit to cabinet/drawer pulls with a real pull-focused grid, or use a subject that matches the actual contents.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 chars and drop the emoji tricolor.** The red-white-blue emoji stack reads as partisan noise in a home goods context and eats valuable preview characters.
- 3. **Add a real deadline.** "Ends midnight Tuesday" outperforms "DON'T MISS" in every click test. Memorial Day is over on 5/26 — use the actual date.
- 4. **Rest the cadence.** Seven emails in six days for the same event is subscriber fatigue territory. One final send with a genuine "last call + specific lowest prices" frame would outperform this.
