The rendered email contains exactly one wall-decor item — a "WELCOME" sign tucked in the middle of a long scroll — surrounded by area rugs, a garden bench, patio umbrellas, outdoor pillows, and the usual Memorial Day grid dump.

There is no outdoor wall decor hero, no price anchor for the category named in the subject, and no reason to believe the subject was written by anyone who looked at the creative. - The clearance banner is clean, on-brand, and immediately reads as Memorial Day.
- Product grid is well-organized with prices visible beneath each tile.

- A bench, umbrellas, and patio pillows are at least seasonally appropriate.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "OUTDOOR WALL DECOR" — the email delivers a single welcome sign and a flood of rugs/furniture.
- Fifth near-identical Memorial Day send in a week; subscriber fatigue is real.
- No price anchor in the hero for the category named in the subject.
- "at a great price" is the vaguest possible offer phrasing — no %, no $, no urgency.
- Hero is interchangeable with every other Wayfair Memorial Day email this week.

- 1. **Match the envelope to the box.** If the subject says outdoor wall decor, the hero must be a wall-decor product with a price. Otherwise rename the subject to the actual category leading the email (looks like outdoor rugs again).
- 2. Replace "at a great price" with a concrete anchor: "from $19" or "up to 60% off."
- 3. Cut the Memorial Day bunting hero — it's been running five days and is now invisible.
- 4. Add a single hero product tile above the grid so the eye lands somewhere specific.
- 5. Throttle cadence — five Memorial Day blasts in a week is training subscribers to ignore the sender name.
