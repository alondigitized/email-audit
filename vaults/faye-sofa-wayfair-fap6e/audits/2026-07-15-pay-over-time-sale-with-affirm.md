# 🔔 PAY-OVER-TIME SALE WITH AFFIRM 🔔 
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Wayfair's Affirm pay-over-time pitch, and it falls into the exact trap the brand keeps repeating: the subject promises a focused financial offer (0% APR with Affirm), but the body immediately dilutes that into yet another full-catalog clearance scroll — 70% off major appliances, 50% off mattresses, 40% off everything imaginable, and a "Picked Just for You" grid. The Affirm angle disappears into the visual noise faster than you can read the hero banner.
- There's a real idea buried in here — using a financing hook to push larger-ticket items — but Wayfair executes it by slapping an Affirm logo in the top bar and calling it a day. Everything beneath is indistinguishable from the July 12 trash cans send, the July 10 dining bowls send, and every other full-catalog clearance blast this month. The subscriber already knows this playbook; surprise and urgency are fully depleted.
- This is the weakest concept-to-execution gap of the July batch. At least the July 13 "chicken coop" send committed to a single product. This one commits to nothing.

## What's working

- The top bar hero with the Affirm 0% APR callout and the Wayfair logo is immediately visible and legible — the financing hook at least lands above the fold.
- "Picked just for you" personalization block is a genuine attempt at segmentation and breaks the monotony of the category grid.
- Discount percentages are large, bolded, and scannable — a subscriber can assess value at a glance without reading body copy.
- The "Shop Sale" and "New Arrivals" navigation tabs at the very top provide quick escape valves for deal-seekers.

## What's weak

- The Affirm financing narrative evaporates after the top bar — not a single product in the grid is called out as "ideal for pay-over-time" (i.e., big-ticket items). The copy doesn't connect financing to a reason-to-buy.
- Full-catalog grid with 10+ category blocks (appliances, mattresses, rugs, outdoor, storage, patio, kitchen, bakeware, bath…) creates decision paralysis — there is no hero product, no anchor purchase.
- "Picked just for you" grid shows rugs, a kitchen sink, a dresser, and a bookcase side-by-side — the personalization signal is incoherent and undermines trust in the algorithm.
- Subject line is ALL CAPS with two 🔔 bell emojis and reads as pure inbox spam noise, continuing the July pattern of overloaded subjects (see: July 12's trash cans 🔔⏱️🔔⏱️).
- No visible deadline or urgency tied to the Affirm offer — "sale" with no end date is not a credible motivator.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the Affirm narrative or drop it.** Feature 3–5 high-ticket items ($300+) that actually benefit from pay-over-time — a sofa, a mattress, a major appliance — and show the monthly payment breakdown next to each price. That's the email. The clearance grid belongs in a separate send.
- 2. **Kill the emoji bells in the subject.** One emoji max, functional not decorative. Replace the subject with a concrete benefit statement.
- 3. **Add an Affirm offer deadline.** "0% APR through Sunday" is a real urgency trigger; "PAY-OVER-TIME SALE" is not.
- 4. **Tighten the "Picked for you" block.** Surface only same-category items to make the personalization feel real — three coherent items beat six random ones.
- 5. **Reduce category blocks to 4–5.** Cut the bakeware, patio, and storage sections; keep major appliances, mattresses, furniture, and rugs — categories where financing is actually relevant.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font format: woff only, no woff2**
```css
@font-face {
  src: url(https://secure.img.wfrcdn.com/st4/stores/common/fonts/sofia/sofia_reg.woff) format("woff");
}
```
No `woff2` source declared. Modern clients (Gmail app, Apple Mail, iOS Mail) support woff2 and it's ~30% smaller. Fallback degrades to `mso-font-alt: "Arial"` in Outlook — acceptable, but woff2 should be added.

**Aggressive word-break on product names**
```css
.ProductRec-name { word-break: break-all; }
```
`break-all` breaks mid-word on any character. Should be `word-break: break-word` or `overflow-wrap: break-word` to avoid visually fragmented product names on mobile.

**Duplicate media query blocks**
The `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` rule is split into at least two separate blocks in `<head>` — one for button classes, one for layout utilities. Some clients (older Android Gmail) parse only the first matching media block. These should be consolidated into a single `@media` block to avoid rule-drop.

**No visible `<meta charset>` or viewport `<meta>` in truncated head**
Standard email practice includes `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8" />`. Cannot confirm presence without full `<head>` — verify in full source.

**`u ~ div { min-width: 100vw }` Gmail hack present**
This is a known Gmail width fix; its presence is intentional and correct.
