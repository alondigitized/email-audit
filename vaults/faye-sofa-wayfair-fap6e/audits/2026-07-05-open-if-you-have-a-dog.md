# OPEN IF YOU HAVE A DOG >>>
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Wayfair breaks its own recent pattern with a subject line that actually does something new: audience qualification over product-noun bait. "OPEN IF YOU HAVE A DOG >>>" earns opens not by naming a SKU, but by screening for identity — a smarter hook than seven consecutive "KITCHEN MATS" or "BAKING SHEETS" blasts. The hero delivers on that promise cleanly: "pup approved." with a dog lounging on furniture, "#dogsofwayfair" social tie-in, and a pet-product carousel with visible price anchors.
- Then Wayfair does what Wayfair does. Past the first two product rows, the email falls off a cliff into the same multi-category July 4th clearance grid that has been running all week — rugs, shelving, lanterns, bedroom sets, outdoor furniture, none of it remotely dog-adjacent. The "more styles to scoop up 🐾" button with its lone paw emoji is a fig leaf, not a bridge. A subscriber who opened because they have a dog is now being sold a linen duvet.
- The CTA copy compounds the damage: "shop before they go gone" is a visible grammatical error. It reads as sloppy rather than intentionally casual. The email earns its opening with a genuinely good subject, then squanders it with the same bait-and-switch infrastructure underneath. Better than the kitchen mats spam cycle, not as clean as the July 2 wall-mounted shelves send.

## What's working

- Subject line pivots from product-noun bait to persona-qualification — "OPEN IF YOU HAVE A DOG >>>" is the freshest Wayfair subject in this streak by a wide margin.
- Hero section is immediately coherent: dog image, "pup approved." headline, "#dogsofwayfair" hashtag, all on-brief.
- Pet product carousel shows price anchors ("starting at $14.49", "starting at $9.49") — specific enough to set expectation.
- The July 4th 70% OFF banner uses patriotic red/white/blue stripes, making the sale feel timely rather than evergreen filler.

## What's weak

- Classic Wayfair bait-and-switch: after two pet-product rows, the email collapses into the generic July 4th clearance grid (rugs, lanterns, shelving, bedroom sets) with no dog products in sight.
- "shop before they go gone" is a visible grammatical error — "they go gone" is broken copy that undermines the brand's credibility at the moment of conversion.
- ">>>" in the subject reads as spam-adjacent; it signals desperation rather than urgency.
- No credible deadline anywhere — "before they go gone" is vague and doesn't motivate same-session action.
- The "pace your space" section header has zero connection to the dog persona that drove the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the dog vertical end-to-end.** Replace the generic home goods grid with curated pet furniture, crates, outdoor pet gear, and feeding stations. The subject promise costs nothing to keep — the generic grid is a product-catalog copy-paste that breaks trust.
- 2. **Fix the CTA copy.** "Shop before they go gone" has to go. "Shop the pup picks" or "Claim your dog's deal" are both clean, on-brand, and grammatically correct.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends July 6" turns a vague offer into an action signal.
- 4. **Drop the ">>>" from the subject.** The persona-qualification hook is strong enough without spam arrows.
- 5. **Strengthen the price anchor in the hero.** Move the "starting at $14.49" signal up into the hero section rather than burying it in the carousel row.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<head>` CSS will be stripped by Gmail web client.**
The entire responsive stylesheet is declared inside `<style type="text/css">` in `<head>`. Gmail (web, not app) strips `<head>` and `<style>` blocks entirely. This means `.u-FullWidth`, `.u-MobileHide`, `.u-MobileShow`, and all 480px breakpoint rules are silently dropped for Gmail web users. Inline styles must duplicate layout-critical rules for Gmail compatibility.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag.**
The opening tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers and accessibility validators will flag this; it also affects some spam filters that check for locale signals.

**480px breakpoint is below current standard.**
Mobile media query targets `max-width: 480px`. Most modern email clients and guides use `600px` as the primary breakpoint; 480px misses a wide band of small-tablet and large-phone viewports (481–599px) that will receive the desktop layout.

**`@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` block present** — this is a known iOS Mail rendering workaround for half-width image stacking. Not a bug, but confirms the template targets iOS-specific quirks.
