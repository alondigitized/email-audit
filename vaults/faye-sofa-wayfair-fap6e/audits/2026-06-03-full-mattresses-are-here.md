# FULL MATTRESSES. ARE. HERE.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- "FULL MATTRESSES. ARE. HERE." is a punchy subject line that promises editorial conviction — mattresses, front and center, take it or leave it. Open the email and that conviction evaporates. The hero isn't mattresses. It's the same "shop before they're gone / Warehouse Clearout" banner Wayfair has been cycling all week, followed immediately by the same "Get It Now. Pay Later" BNPL pitch that hijacked the bath mats email on June 2. Mattresses eventually show up in a product grid under "your home. Your style." — grid item three or four, sharing real estate with bedroom sets and assorted unrelated SKUs.
- This is the bath mats playbook on repeat: write a subject that implies a curated, category-focused send, then deliver a clearance-dump with a BNPL upsell stapled to the top. The stylistic trick (FULL MATTRESSES. ARE. HERE. — the staccato periods) lands well in the inbox preview. It does not survive contact with the email itself.
- The lower half pivots to outdoor furniture and string lights under "let's complete your space." A mattress email that ends with patio products is not a mattress email.

## What's working

- **Subject line cut-through**: The period-separated syntax is unusual enough to stop a thumb mid-scroll; 28 characters clears the mobile truncation threshold easily.
- **No render bugs**: The layout reads cleanly — no broken images, overlapping elements, or obvious glitches visible.
- **"Shop before they're gone" urgency**: The hero's scarcity framing at least gives a reason to click now rather than later.
- **Bedroom product grid exists**: Mattress/bed imagery does appear in the email, so the subject isn't a total lie — just a generous interpretation of the content.

## What's weak

- **BNPL as hero CTA again**: "Get It Now. Pay Later / Learn More" is a financing pitch, not an offer. It adds friction (click to learn what you're even getting) instead of removing it. This is the second send this week where BNPL leads above the primary category.
- **Subject-to-body mismatch**: The subject promises a mattress moment; the email delivers warehouse clearout. Same failure mode as the June 2 bath mats email (4/10).
- **Outdoor furniture in a mattress email**: "Let's complete your space" with string lights and patio sets belongs in a different send. It dilutes whatever mattress intent remained.
- **No price signal anywhere**: No % off, no dollar anchor, no "from $X" — just BNPL and a vague clearance framing. Mattress buyers want a number.
- **"Learn More" CTA**: The worst possible button copy for a high-consideration category. Mattresses are one of the highest-AOV decisions in home goods. "Shop Full Mattresses" or "See Prices" would do real work here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the hero actually about mattresses** — a single hero mattress image, a price anchor (even "from $199"), and a direct "Shop Full Mattresses" button. Cut the BNPL section or move it below the fold.
- 2. **Drop the outdoor section entirely from this send** — if it's a mattress email, make it a mattress email. One category, one CTA, one editorial POV.
- 3. **Add a price or offer signal** — "Full mattresses from $149" or "Up to 40% off full sizes" in the hero copy. Without a number, clearance is meaningless.
- 4. **Replace "Learn More" with action copy** — "Shop Mattresses," "See All Full Sizes," or "Find My Mattress."
- 5. **Break the BNPL habit at the hero** — this is the third email this week where the above-fold CTA is a financing pitch rather than a product or offer CTA.
