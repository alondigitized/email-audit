# Homepage snapshot · bookshop.org · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is my people — a platform literally built to funnel money to independent bookshops — and the $47.6M raised counter hits immediately and hard. That said, the hero book (Coco and Stephen, Together Forever, a cat-and-bunny story by Beth Stern) is firmly in the "gift table at a Barnes & Noble" lane, not the hand-sold literary fiction I expect from indie culture. The cookie consent modal is eating the bottom third of my screen before I've even decided I want to be here.

## What's working

- **The raised-funds counter ($47,613,147.23 Raised For Local Bookstores)** — this is the entire reason I choose bookshop.org over Amazon, and it's right there, prominent and specific. That number updating in real time is exactly the signal that justifies my loyalty.
- **The Mental Health Awareness Month 15% off banner** — specific cause, specific discount, seasonal relevance. That's the kind of mission-aligned promo that fits the brand.
- **The 20% OFF badge on the hero** — layered with the 15% banner, it signals real savings without feeling discount-bin.
- **The search bar** — front and center, right after the logo. As someone who usually comes in with a specific title or author, I can get to my book in one tap.

## What's weak

- **The hero book choice (Coco and Stephen, Together Forever)** — a kitten-and-bunny friendship story by a celebrity's wife is not what converts the person who specifically picked bookshop.org *over* Amazon to support indie culture. This reads like Barnes & Noble's "new nonfiction gifts" table, not a curated indie recommendation.
- **The cookie consent modal** — "Accept" and "Deny Non-Essential" are the two most visible interactive elements on my screen. Before I've browsed a single thing, I'm being asked to make a privacy decision. The stacked buttons eat the bottom 25% of the viewport.
- **No visible shop CTA** — there's no "Browse Books," "Shop Now," or anything that functions as an actual entry point to the catalog. The modal buried whatever lived below the hero.
- **No new-arrivals or "what's trending in fiction" rail** — above the fold it's one featured book, and it's not mine.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the hero with an indie-press frontlist pick or a curated staff-picks editorial moment** — lean into what bookshop.org *is*: a curator, not a celebrity gift shop. A "Staff Pick of the Week" framing would immediately signal identity to someone like me.
- **Move the cookie consent to a slim bottom bar** (not a full blocking modal) so the actual page can be read and engaged with before I'm gated by a privacy prompt.
- **Add a visible "your bookshop earned X" or local-shop spotlight module** — Bea literally tracks her favorite shop's earnings percentage. Surface that dashboard hook above the fold and I'm converting to a logged-in session immediately.
- **Add a literary fiction or "new this week" quick-link strip** under the search bar — one row of genre chips ("Literary Fiction · Indie Press · Memoir · Poetry") would take me off the hero and into my actual category in one tap.
