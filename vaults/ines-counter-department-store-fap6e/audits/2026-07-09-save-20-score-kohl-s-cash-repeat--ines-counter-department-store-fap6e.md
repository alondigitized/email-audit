# Save 20%  ✅ Score Kohl's Cash ✅ Repeat 🔁
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Forty-eight. That's my new count, and Kohl's shows up today with *20% off* — a step down from the 30% they were screaming at me yesterday in two separate emails, complete with "ENDS TODAY" and "HOURS LEFT" panic. The "Repeat 🔁" emoji in the subject is either the most honest thing Kohl's has ever sent me or the most tone-deaf — they're announcing the rerun before I even open the door. I genuinely don't know whether to respect the transparency or resent the implied shrug.
- Inside, I get a home goods and back-to-school mash-up, and I'm standing there as a Nordstrom Beauty counter regular wondering where the relevance is. Not a single beauty product. No women's fashion. Just outdoor furniture at 50%, kitchen appliances at 40%, and a parade of preteens going back to school. The one thing that keeps me from completely dismissing this is the Kohl's Cash barcode in the footer — I don't spend a dollar on any card that doesn't send value back to me, and that coupon is real money. But it's buried. Compared to what Neiman Marcus just did — a clean, confident "1,000+ new arrivals" editorial with zero discounting and total composure — this email feels like a clearance rack that caught fire.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. Maybe revisit the Kohl's Cash balance later.

## What's working

- **Kohl's Cash barcode is present and scannable** — for a loyalty-driven shopper, this is the most concrete value in the email, and I'm glad it's there even if it's buried in the footer.
- **"Savings & Rewards Member" badge at the top** acknowledges I'm a known customer, not a cold prospect — a small signal but Macy's often skips this on their blast emails too.
- **"Make guests linger. Sorry in advance."** — the wittiest line in the email. It doesn't shout at me; it winks. More of this, please.
- **Clean render, no broken images** — every section loaded properly, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy. Execution is solid.
- **Back-to-school category navigation** (Girls / Juniors / Boys / Young Men / Shoes / Backpacks) is genuinely well-organized if that's your mission.

## What's weak

- **20% after 30%** — yesterday Kohl's sent me two separate "ENDS TODAY / HOURS LEFT" emails at 30% off. Today's offer is worse. That's not a deal; that's a post-sale markdown pretending to be a new event. Sephora ran a week of Stacking Weekend urgency and then pivoted to an editorial — they didn't circle back with a lesser number.
- **No beauty whatsoever** — I am a beauty counter regular. There is no beauty module, no skincare, no fragrance. Nothing. Even a small "earn Kohl's Cash on beauty" banner would have landed differently.
- **"Repeat 🔁" in the subject line** — telegraphing that you're running a repeat promo is the wrong kind of honesty. Neiman Marcus never tells me it's recycling creative.
- **Back-to-school kids section feels like a different email** — models are preteens and early teens. I'm 47. This content isn't for me, and the section takes up significant scroll real estate.
- **Offer clutter** — Outdoor 50%, Kitchen 40%, Bedding 40%, Back to School... this reads like three separate campaigns duct-taped together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote Kohl's Cash to the hero, not the footer** — for your Rewards members, that's the real hook. Lead with it.
- 2. **Add a beauty module** — even a single banner linking to Kohl's beauty would have earned a click from me. This was a missed opportunity on a day when Sephora's inbox presence was already strong.
- 3. **Stop broadcasting the offer downgrade** — segment the list. People who engaged on the 30% emails shouldn't get a 20% follow-up to the same inbox thread; that trains them to wait for the floor.
- 4. **Pick one editorial focus per send** — Home OR Back to School. Not both. Macy's does this better when they build a seasonal hero; this feels like a SKU dump.
- 5. **Retire "Repeat 🔁" forever** — if you have to tell me it's a repeat, the offer has lost its occasion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn Kohl's Cash this weekend — 20% off now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Kohl's Cash is waiting. Shop home & more.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop outdoor, kitchen, back to school and earn $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Home must-haves, back to school, and Kohl's Cash on everything — savings already started.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard ESP-driven HTML 4.01 Transitional template with VML Outlook fallbacks and responsive media queries. Core rendering structure is functional, but compliance headers are missing from the relayed copy and alt text is absent on all tracking/content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe.** All click links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com` redirect chains — not probeable without live session cookies. No broken destination URLs confirmed, but cannot rule out dead links in this send.

Three distinct pixel/tracking systems fire on open:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — CoherentPath behavioral pixel
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/.../o.gif` — Marigold (Sailthru) open pixel
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — secondary ESP open tracker
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager DMP sync

**No issue with multiples per se**, but the CoherentPath pixel `img` is missing `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` (both present on the Marigold pixel — inconsistent treatment).

**Malformed tag confirmed in source:**
```html
</custom>
```
immediately after the `click.s.kohls.com` open pixel `img` — this is an unclosed/invalid custom element that will produce a parse warning in strict renderers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on all 9 flagged images** — includes both tracking pixels and what appear to be content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, etc. served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`). The Marigold open pixel correctly uses `alt="" aria-hidden="true"`; that pattern is not applied consistently.

**`<title>` is empty** (`<title></title>`). Screen readers and some preview panes surface this; it should be set to match the subject line or a sanitized equivalent.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**Google Fonts loaded three times** — once via `<link>` and twice via `@import` inside a `<style>` block for both Roboto and Montserrat. Redundant; adds parse overhead in clients that support web fonts.

**`meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`** — this suppresses referrer on outbound clicks, which will cause UTM params to be invisible to any server-side referrer parsing on Kohl's landing pages (though query-string UTMs are unaffected if present in the click URLs themselves).
