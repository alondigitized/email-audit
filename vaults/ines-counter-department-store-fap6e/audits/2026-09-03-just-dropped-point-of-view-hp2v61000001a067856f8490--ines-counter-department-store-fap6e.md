# Just dropped: point of view.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one lost me almost immediately. "point of view" as a hero — lowercase, no offer, no product name I recognize — and I had to scroll a full screen before I even understood I was looking at a new brand launch. I don't shop by "founder's pov," I shop by "does this work and is it worth my money," and this email buries that under skincare-influencer language — "snatch it," "own your POV" — that reads more like a 25-year-old TikTok skincare girlie than someone who's been buying Estée Lauder at the counter since I had a landline.
- The one thing that did catch my eye was the top banner — "50% off Select Beauty" with PHILR, Urban Decay, and The INKEY List called out, "TODAY ONLY." That's the third time this week Sephora has run that exact banner (9/1, 8/31, 8/29 by my count), so at this point it's wallpaper, not urgency. Compare that to 8/31's "Take a closer look" send, which actually led with $5 off 250 points and 4x points on top brands — that felt like they knew I was a Beauty Insider member. This one doesn't mention my tier once until the very bottom footer, as an afterthought logo strip.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only to make fun of "snatch it" with my sister. This reads like a designer got to run wild on a product-launch mood board and nobody asked "would Ines actually buy this."

## What's working

- The 50% off banner up top is a real, specific offer and at least gives me a reason to keep scrolling past the fold.
- Product photography is clean and consistent — none of that overlapping/broken-image mess I've seen in cheaper sends.
- The "Beauty Insider" tier strip at the bottom (Insider / VIB / Rouge) is present, so they haven't completely forgotten I'm a member — it's just buried.
- Testimonial quotes ("my skin looks visibly smoother, firmer") add a little trust, more than the usual grid-of-tiles approach.

## What's weak

- The whole "point of view / prep it / snatch it / wear it" concept is unclear branding for a beauty veteran — I still don't know what "POV" actually is as a product line by the time I'd normally stop scrolling.
- No loyalty points value tied to this launch at all — Neiman's and even 8/31's Sephora send led with points math; this one doesn't mention points until the generic footer.
- It's a wall of near-identical product tiles — six or seven SKUs in a row with tiny captions — genuinely hard to scan on a phone without a lot of scrolling and zooming.
- The offer up top (50% off select beauty) has nothing to do with the "point of view" launch below it — two unrelated campaigns stapled together, which fractures the hierarchy.
- Slang like "snatch it" and "own your POV" doesn't read like Sephora talking to me, a Nordstrom/Saks regular — it reads like they built this for a Gen Z audience and mass-mailed it anyway.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with either the discount OR the launch — not both stacked with no connective tissue. If it's a launch email, make the launch the hero and drop the 50%-off banner, or vice versa.
- 2. Cut the slang. "Snatch it," "prep it," "wear it" — swap for something that signals expertise, like Neiman's editorial fashion-magazine tone I called out on 9/2's send.
- 3. Surface points/tier value near the top, not buried in the footer — even a one-liner like "Rouge members earn 2x points on POV" would pull me in.
- 4. Trim the product grid to 3-4 hero SKUs with bigger images and real benefit copy, not six tiny tiles in a row.
- 5. Tie the "50% off" banner to a specific, relevant category for me (skincare/prestige) instead of a generic three-brand callout.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New at Sephora: the skincare line insiders are asking about`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Rouge perks + a new skincare launch, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `4-step routine, dermatologist-tested — plus 50% off select beauty ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn 2x points on the newest prestige skincare drop`
