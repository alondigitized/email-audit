# Up to 85% off clearance STARTS TODAY ➡️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Sixty-two. That's where I am with Kohl's since Memorial Day, and I almost didn't open this one — until I noticed "85% off" rather than the 40% they've been running on rotation all week. That number got my attention. But the moment I'm actually inside the email, my interest evaporates pretty fast: home decor, outdoor patio furniture, activewear, shoes, and then — I blinked — *Amazon device deals*? In a clearance email? I'm 47, I shop beauty counters at Nordstrom and Saks, and somehow Kohl's thinks I want a Fire TV Stick between the clearance banners. There's no beauty in here. No fragrance, no skincare, no cosmetics. Just camping-pillow adjacent content and a credit card pitch. The Kohl's Cash callout near the bottom is the only thing that genuinely speaks to how I shop — I will not open my wallet without points back — but by the time I get there, I've already checked out emotionally.
- Would I click? On the clearance categories they're actually showing? No. Home patio refresh is not on my list. Would I forward this to a friend? Maybe to my friend Janet who redoes her deck every summer. Not to anyone in my beauty circle.
- Mark as read.

## What's working

- **The 85% off hero catches the eye immediately** — it's a genuine escalation from the 40% cadence all week, and the red/pink clearance banner is visually dominant. I noticed the step-up.
- **Kohl's Cash is present** — earning $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent is exactly the kind of loyalty signal I look for before I decide if a trip is worth making. It's visible near the footer.
- **Rewards-exclusive 15% extra on clearance is a real hook** — "Kohl's Rewards Exclusives: take an extra 15% on top of clearance prices" is compelling if the clearance inventory includes anything I want.
- **CTAs are specific and labeled** — "Shop Home," "Shop Active Sale," "Shop Now" are all verb + noun. Nothing vague like "Discover More."
- **Clearance opening feels fresh** — after four consecutive 40%-off-ends-today sends, pivoting to a *new* clearance event launching today is at least a structural change that feels earned.

## What's weak

- **Beauty is completely absent** — I am a beauty counter regular and there is not a single skincare, cosmetics, fragrance, or haircare mention anywhere in this email. Sephora ran 4X points on all hair care this same week and spoke directly to what I actually spend on. Kohl's doesn't even try.
- **Amazon Device Deals is a bewildering editorial pivot** — I'm in a clearance email and suddenly there's an Echo device. This reads like a co-marketing insert that got dropped in without any thought for recipient fit. Neiman Marcus would never.
- **Credit card pitch mid-email is a momentum killer** — "The card that puts you first" placed between the activewear section and the wallet coupons breaks whatever little shopping intent was building. Macy's at least earns its card pitch by confirming an offer first.
- **Demographic signals are off** — outdoor patio pillows and activewear models don't signal "beauty counter regular, 47, Gen X." The styling feels suburban clearance event, not edited lifestyle.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is brand email number 62-ish since Memorial Day. The open was earned by the subject-line upgrade to 85%, not by any trust they've built. One more week of daily sends and I'm unsubscribing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a beauty and fragrance clearance section** — Kohl's stocks Bliss, Yes To, and drugstore-adjacent beauty that clears heavily. Put even one skincare or cosmetics module in here and the email becomes relevant to me.
- 2. **Remove or bury the Amazon Device Deals** — this belongs in a dedicated tech email sent to a tech-interested segment, not dropped into a clearance fashion-and-home send.
- 3. **Move the Kohl's Cash and Rewards callout above the fold** — the best loyalty hook is buried at the bottom. Put "earn Kohl's Cash + Rewards members save an extra 15%" in the hero sub-copy and it changes the value proposition immediately.
- 4. **Trim to three sections max** — Home, Women's, and a loyalty hook. The current email has six competing categories fighting for attention, which means none of them land.
- 5. **Kill the credit card pitch for non-cardholders** — if I already have the card, show me my balance. If I don't, a clearance email is not the moment to recruit me.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rewards exclusives inside — plus earn Kohl's Cash on every dollar`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New clearance event starts today — shop your categories before they're gone`

### 5. Compliance

**Physical address — FAIL.** No physical mailing address detected in the HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address. If it exists below the truncation point, this should be verified against the full source; if absent, this is a violation.

**List-Unsubscribe header — WARN.** Header not captured by AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm presence. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaint rates.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — WARN.** Same capture gap. Without this header, Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe button does not activate, which is now a bulk sender requirement for >5k/day Google recipients.

**Authentication-Results — WARN.** SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown due to relay. Sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (ESP subdomain); DMARC alignment should be verified against `kohls.com` parent domain out-of-band.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present** — correct practice to suppress search indexing of web-view versions and suppress referrer leakage.
