# These EPIC DEALS are off the chart 📈
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again. "EPIC DEALS" screaming in that same white-on-red banner I've now seen — what, five, six times this week? Sept 21, 23, 24, 25, and now this. The bag hero is at least a new prop instead of the handbag or fleece pile, but functionally this is the identical tile wall of 50% here, 30% there, toys, flannels, ghosts. I scroll and scroll and nothing says "you, a 47-year-old woman who buys skincare at the counter" — it's a general store circular dressed up as urgency.
- Compare this to Neiman's boot roundup I looked at — that one at least had an editorial hero shot that looked considered, even if thin on offer. This has ten offers and zero point of view. And Macy's "Last Chance" triggered email, dinged for costume-urgency, at least had ONE clear ask. This has fifteen.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding. I'm marking it read and moving on — same as I did the last four times this week.

## What's working

- The tote-bag hero photo is at least a fresh image, not the same handbag or fleece stack recycled from Tuesday.
- The 3x Rewards banner near the top is a real loyalty signal — I do have a Kohl's Card, so seeing my multiplier called out is the one thing that felt like it was for me.
- Category nav bar (Home/Women/Men/Kids) up top is a decent wayfinding gesture for someone who wants to skip straight to a section.
- Individual product prices ($21.99 bras, $24.99 flannels) are specific, not vague "up to" fluff, which I appreciate more than Sephora's flowery Hispanic Heritage Month copy that buried its actual deals.

## What's weak

- This is the same red "EPIC DEALS" stacked-tile format I flagged on the 21st, 23rd, 24th, and 25th — nothing about the structure has evolved, just the SKUs.
- Zero targeting toward me: kids' clothing, toy LEGO branding, men's flannels, pet costumes — I'm a beauty-counter woman, not a family bulk shopper, and Kohl's has my purchase history to know that.
- Visual hierarchy is chaos — by the fourth "EPIC DEAL" red tile my eye has stopped registering any of them as urgent, they're wallpaper.
- The "Must-haves too good to ghost" Halloween tchotchke section (skulls, jack-o-lanterns, a stuffed dog in a costume) reads cheap and cluttered, nothing like the white-space restraint Neiman Marcus uses for its editorial sends.
- No beauty, no fragrance, no "for you" personalization anywhere in an email this long — Sephora at least stays in its lane category-wise even when its editorial framing misses.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this from fifteen offers to three, max, and lead with the one most relevant to the recipient's purchase history — if I'm a repeat apparel/home shopper, open with FLX clothing or the bedding deal, not toys and flannels I'll never buy.
- 2. Kill the repeating red tile format entirely — I've now seen it five times in a week and it's lost all urgency. Rotate creative structure at least every few sends.
- 3. Segment this list — a beauty/apparel shopper like me shouldn't see the identical toy-and-flannel grid a parent of toddlers gets.
- 4. Move the 3x Rewards / Kohl's Cash callout higher and bigger — that's the one genuine loyalty hook in the whole email and it's buried under the hero.
- 5. Trim the Halloween tchotchke grid — it looks like clutter, not a curated seasonal moment.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 3x Rewards through Sunday, plus your top picks inside`
