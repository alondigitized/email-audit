# A point of Pride
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Two emails from Neiman Marcus today. Two. The gift card send this morning at least had a hook — up to $1,250 back, clear call to action, felt like it was trying to earn my attention. This one walks in right behind it wearing a beautiful pink dress and absolutely nothing to say except "we care about Pride Month." And you know what? I believe them. The editorial is gorgeous. The cause is real. But I'm a loyalty-card-carrying Gen X woman who cross-shops between NM, Saks, and Nordstrom, and when I open an NM email I'm still — even in June — asking myself: *what's in this for me?* The answer here is: browse our curated edit. That's it. No offer, no event, no points tie-in.
- The hero model in the hot pink dress stopped my scroll. "Pride and Joy" is a smart headline — genuine wordplay that earns its moment. But after that? Two category CTAs and some product thumbnails that don't go anywhere specific. I'd click "Shop LGBTQ+ Owned & Founded Brands" out of genuine curiosity, but I wouldn't forward this to a friend because there's nothing actionable to share. It's more of a brand statement than an email. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **The hero image earns its real estate.** The bright pink maxi dress is editorial, joyful, and distinctly NM-coded — a level above what Macy's or Kohl's would lead with.
- **"Pride and Joy" lands.** The headline has actual wit. It's not cringe corporate rainbow-washing; it feels considered.
- **Cadence shift is noticed and appreciated.** After weeks of Designer Sale red heroes and 50% off hammers, this visual reset registers. I noticed it the same way I noticed the Dad's Day pivot on June 2.
- **Clean layout, no render issues.** Hierarchy from image → headline → CTAs reads without effort. No broken images, no overlapping text.
- **"Shop LGBTQ+ Owned & Founded Brands" CTA is specific.** It tells me exactly what I'm clicking into, which is more than the vague "Discover" buttons I've seen elsewhere.

## What's weak

- **No offer. At all.** For a loyalty-driven shopper, a values email with zero transaction hook is a missed layup. Even Nordstrom ties their give-back campaigns to loyalty point events.
- **Two emails in one day is too many.** The morning gift card email was the better send. This one should have been tomorrow or Thursday — the doubling up dilutes both.
- **The product thumbnails go nowhere.** The sequined dress and the gold bracelet at the bottom are teasers without context — no price, no "shop this look," no link specificity. Saks would never leave product that pretty unanchored.
- **No loyalty signal anywhere.** Not a single mention of InCircle, points, or member access. For a card-carrying NM shopper, that absence is felt.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a transaction layer.** Tie the Pride edit to a loyalty bonus — double InCircle points on purchases from LGBTQ+ owned brands through June 30. Values + reward = email that gets forwarded.
- 2. **One email per day, not two.** Push this to June 4. The gift card email owns today; let this own tomorrow.
- 3. **Anchor the product thumbnails.** Name the items, link to the PDP. The sequined piece is beautiful — make me want to own it.
- 4. **Give "Shop Designer Collections" a purpose.** This second CTA is vague and competes with the first. Replace it with something specific: "Shop the Pride Edit — New Arrivals."
- 5. **The subject is too cute for discovery.** Anyone not already thinking about Pride will scroll past "A point of Pride" without a second thought. Make the subject earn the open from the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our Pride edit: LGBTQ+ brands worth knowing`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Pride Month picks from brands we love`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated styles from LGBTQ+ owned and founded names — plus double points this month`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh arrivals supporting Pride — shop the edit now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** (`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`) blocks user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Apple Mail and some webmail clients still honor this value.

**Three commented-out viewport tags remain in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are debugging/legacy test artifacts. They add noise, slightly increase parse weight, and risk being accidentally uncommented in future edits.

**CSS checkbox hamburger nav** — the interactive mobile nav uses the `:checked` pseudo-class on a hidden `<input>` to toggle visibility. Support in email clients is narrow (only some iOS Mail / Apple Mail versions). Falls back to a hidden static menu elsewhere — functional degradation is graceful but should be documented.

**Two separate `<style>` blocks** — one inline, one via a second `<style>` tag after `</head>`. Gmail strips the second block in some render paths. Consolidate into one `<style>` in `<head>`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Ambiguous `@user` scenario** — the comment block reads:

```
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)
...
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

The SHA256 hash assignment is commented out, meaning the Lookup path is active. However, the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" note without an explicit active-scenario marker makes future template edits error-prone. If `@user` should be the hashed email for downstream pixel/personalization calls, this is a live logic defect.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are declared via `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)` — if the CRM feed row is missing for a subscriber, both silently resolve to empty string. No fallback or error-branch is visible in the truncated source. Downstream use of these in link parameters would produce malformed tracking URLs (e.g., `&uEm=&cmdid=`).
