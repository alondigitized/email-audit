# Open for our bag edit
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- A Sephora "bag edit" landed in my inbox this morning and my first reaction was: Charlotte Tilbury. That got my attention — CT is my counter brand, full stop. When I see Pillow Talk and the dreamy editorial treatment, I'm at least curious. But the subject line "Open for our bag edit" is the kind of copy that belongs on a Pinterest board, not in my inbox on a Sunday. My bag? Their bag? Am I shopping or reading a Vogue column?
- The free mascara GWP in the top banner is doing the heavy lifting here — that's the only concrete reason to click. But Sephora buried the spend threshold, so I have no idea if I qualify. Compare that to what Neiman Marcus sent me recently — "Just for you: 25% off one item" — that email knew exactly what it was offering and to whom. This one is hedging with editorial language when it should be selling. The visual is polished, Charlotte Tilbury is aspirational (if skewing younger than my face), and the product assortment is broad enough to find something. But I've gotten four Sephora emails in the last four days, and none of them have remembered that I'm a 47-year-old Insider member with a very specific basket.
- I'd click to the GWP banner out of habit — that's Sephora conditioning me, not good email marketing. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **Charlotte Tilbury as the editorial anchor** — CT is a counter brand with genuine prestige; putting it front-and-center earns immediate credibility with me
- **Free mascara GWP is visible before I scroll** — the top banner is doing exactly what it should: leading with the incentive
- **"WHAT'S IN OUR BAG?" is clean and legible** — big type, no clutter, visually I knew where to look
- **No render bugs that I can see** — layout holds, images loaded, nothing broken
- **Sephora Insider sender name lands correctly** — I know this is my account email, not a cold blast

## What's weak

- **"Open for our bag edit" subject line tells me nothing** — whose bag, what edit, what's in it for me? Compare this to the July 16 Sephora send — "Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase" — that one led with the number; this one leads with an aesthetic vibe
- **Hero model reads young** — blonde, luminous, feels mid-30s at most; I'm 47 and I have money to spend, Sephora, meet me where I am
- **GWP spend threshold is invisible** — "Free Charlotte Tilbury Mascara / SHOP TO REDEEM" without the qualifying amount feels like a bait I have to click through to understand
- **Below-the-fold product sprawl** — Chanel, Em Jay, L'Occitane, an oil-balancing hydration banner, Best of Lip — this is a lookbook, not a pitch; Macy's gets criticized for overload and Sephora is doing the same thing here just with better photography
- **No points language anywhere** — yesterday I had 4X on hair care; today, nothing. That's a missed beat. I organize my life around Insider points.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the GWP** — the mascara is the hook, not the bag metaphor. Swap the subject to lead with the offer.
- 2. **Show the spend threshold in the banner** — "Free mascara with $75+ purchase" is honest and motivating; "shop to redeem" is vague and friction-inducing
- 3. **Age up the hero model or show the product on a surface** — Charlotte Tilbury's own brand does this beautifully; a flatlay or a hand hold works for Gen X shoppers who've aged out of the influencer-selfie frame
- 4. **Cut two sections below the fold** — oil-balancing hydration and Best of Lip compete with the Charlotte Tilbury story; let one hero breathe
- 5. **Add an Insider point callout** — even "Earn Insider points on everything" gives me a reason to click that aligns with how I actually shop

- **Subject Alt B:** `Charlotte Tilbury just landed. Grab the free mascara.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Zoom restriction — confirmed issue.**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) prohibits suppressing user-initiated zoom. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly — confirmed issue.**

```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

This blanket rule overrides OS-level text scaling for visually impaired users across all block and inline elements. Standard practice targets only specific containers (e.g., `body`, `.wrapper`), not `a`, `span`, `strong`.

**`robots` meta value malformed — minor.**

```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```

`no index` (space-separated) is not a valid directive; the recognized value is `noindex`. Email clients ignore this tag, but if the email is hosted for browser viewing (view-in-browser link), web crawlers would index it. Correct to `content="noindex,nofollow"`.
