# The Roundup: Tantalizing Totes
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Oh, this one's just handbags — no discount math to do, no gift-card tier table like yesterday's send. And honestly, that's fine by me. This is a straight-up designer editorial: leopard-print YSL tote up top, then a clean six-across grid of Balenciaga, Saint Laurent, Christian Louboutin, Bottega Veneta, Zimmermann. No crimson banner screaming at me, no "LAST CALL," no calligraphic S doing its umpteenth curtain call — just quiet confidence that I know these names and want to look at bags.
- That said — there's zero price anywhere on this email. Not under one bag. For a $2,000+ leopard-calf-hair YSL tote, I get wanting to keep it aspirational, but I still want a number before I click through, especially on my phone during a commute. Compare this to Kohl's intimates sale I looked at this week — ugly layout, but at least it told me the price. Neiman's is betting I'll click on desire alone.
- Would I click? Probably yes, on the Saint Laurent quilted tote — that burgundy is gorgeous and I have a weakness for quilting. Would I forward it? Maybe to my sister who's shopping for a fall bag, but I'd caption it "no prices, of course" because that's very on-brand for this house.

## What's working

- The hero leopard YSL tote is genuinely striking — stops my thumb mid-scroll, no small feat.
- Editorial framing ("The Roundup: Tantalizing Totes") feels like a stylist picked these, not an algorithm — that's the Neiman's tone I actually subscribe for, same energy as the Aug 17 "1,000+ new pre-fall arrivals" send I liked.
- Six-bag grid is clean, evenly cropped, labeled by designer name only — no clutter, no badges competing for attention.
- Two clear CTAs up top ("Shop Saint Laurent" / "Shop Totes") give me a fast lane if I already know what I want.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere. Not one. I shouldn't have to leave the email to find out if we're talking $800 or $8,000.
- No loyalty callout — I'm an Neiman's InCircle-type shopper by nature, and there's nothing here acknowledging tier points on a designer bag purchase, which is exactly the moment I'd want to see it.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" and "Download the app" icons at the bottom are sandwiched together and read like an afterthought — Saks does personal-shopper access with more warmth and visual weight than this two-icon row.
- Zimmermann raffia bag is the odd one out stylistically next to five structured leather totes — feels like a merchandising afterthought at the bottom right.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price (or "from $X") under each bag in the grid — this is the single biggest lift. I don't need a sale price, just a number to decide if it's worth the click.
- 2. Surface InCircle points or tier benefit on at least the hero image — "Earn X points on this Saint Laurent tote" would turn browsing into buying for someone like me.
- 3. Move "Match with a personal style advisor" up near the hero, not buried in the footer icon row — that's a real differentiator against Macy's and Kohl's and it's wasted down there.
- 4. Swap the Zimmermann raffia for a sixth structured tote, or split into two separate roundups (structured vs. woven) so the grid feels curated rather than padded to six.
