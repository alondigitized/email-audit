# Enjoy 20% off home, shoes & styles for the crew 🛍️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Tenth day. Tenth day of "Friends & Family 20%" and honestly I could recite the blue starburst layout in my sleep at this point — same coupon graphic, same "Ends August 30" urgency line that's now actually true instead of evergreen filler, which I guess counts as progress. I skimmed past the grid of category tiles (Men, Girls, Boys, Shoes, Home) without stopping, then the back-to-school block, then landed — finally — on something with a little personality: the "Picked with you in mind" product grid with the Ninja Slushi machine and the Serta mattress topper. That's the first moment all email that felt like an actual merchandising decision instead of a coupon wrapper.
- Compare this to Sephora's daily deal blast, which at least leads with a percentage-off hero on actual beauty product instead of a lifestyle model in a gray tee. Or Neiman Marcus's "Tailored for any occasion" send, which was a real editorial moment — moody styling, one look, one story. Kohl's is still playing the volume game: cram every department into one email and hope something sticks. It works a little better than the Denim Deal Days send from two days ago because there's genuine product variety here, but it's not enough to make me open my wallet.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Absolutely not — there's nothing here that says "you specifically" to me, a 47-year-old who shops the beauty counter, not the Kohl's home aisle. I'll mark it read and move on, same as every other day this week.

## What's working

- The "Picked with you in mind" product tiles with real prices ($2.99–$9.99 towels, $349.99 Ninja Slushi, $137.99–$209.99 mattress topper) finally gave me something concrete to evaluate instead of another banner.
- Kohl's Rewards status and 5% earn rate sit right at the top — at least they're reminding me I'm a member, unlike Sephora's "0 points" nag that's now hit five sends in eleven days.
- The coupon code and barcode block near the bottom is genuinely useful if I were actually going in-store, which fits how I shop — I do like to redeem at the register.
- "Ends August 30" is a real, near-term deadline this time, not the vague evergreen urgency Kohl's has leaned on all week.

## What's weak

- Same blue starburst "Friends & Family 20%" hero for the tenth straight day — I'm not even reading it anymore, it's wallpaper, exactly like I said about the 8/28 slow cooker email.
- The hero model and the "Our love language? Extra savings" family-photo grid (Women, Men, Girls, Boys, Shoes, Home) reads like a Kohl's circular, not a beauty-counter regular's inbox — nothing here says "you," it says "everyone."
- Two separate CTA blocks (Back to School shop button, So-Back Sweepstakes enter button) before I ever hit a product I'd actually buy — that's a lot of scrolling for someone who wants my Amanda jeans or my Ninja machine up front.
- No beauty, skincare, or fragrance content at all — for a shopper who cross-shops Nordstrom and Saks counters, this reads like it was written for a completely different customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "Picked with you in mind" product grid up near the hero — that's the only block with real merchandising signal, and it's currently buried two-thirds down the email.
- 2. Retire the blue starburst hero graphic. Ten days of identical creative trains me to skip it entirely; even a color swap would reset attention.
- 3. Segment by purchase history — if I've never bought a slow cooker or a Ninja machine from Kohl's, don't lead a general send with kitchen appliances. Sephora at least tries to segment by category even if it botches the loyalty messaging.
- 4. Cut one of the two mid-email CTA blocks (Back to School or Sweepstakes) — pick the one with better conversion data and drop the other to shorten the scroll to checkout.
- 5. Add a "Your Kohl's Cash balance" line next to the Rewards status header — Macy's does confirmation-email urgency better than this, but neither brand shows me real account state, and that's the one thing that would make me feel like a known customer instead of a mailing list entry.
