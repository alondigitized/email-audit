- The personal style advisor and app tiles at the bottom are the right kind of service cue for someone who likes human help.
- The text is large and readable on a phone, and the buttons are wide, easy tap targets.

## What's weak

- "In boutiques" with no product page. Is it online or not? I can't tell.
- The three buttons ("See more ready-to-wear / accessories / shoes") are identical outlined boxes with pale grey text. "See more" of what? I haven't seen any product yet, only one look.
- There's no price, no named piece, and no sizing or availability cue. The model's red hair flower is the only color, and I can't find out what it is.

## 1. Technical Summary

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 17 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the QA probe (no HTTP check), so their destinations and redirect health are **unverified**. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- The plain-text part is 3199 of 3690 characters URLs (about 87%). Long tracking URLs in the text alternative can hurt readability and spam scoring. It is a real deliverability signal, though minor.
- The truncated source shows no unresolved or broken `href`s, so no broken links are confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern.
- `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't select the correct language.
- `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. It doesn't describe the email, which is the "CHANEL Cruise 2026/27" message.
- An HTML comment (`<!--REQUIRED variables…`) sits before the `<!DOCTYPE>`. In older Outlook/IE-based rendering engines, anything before the doctype can trigger quirks mode. Move the doctype first or strip the comment.
- The mobile CSS uses exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=hide]`. These stop matching if an element carries more than one class (for example `class="hide foo"`). Use `*[class~=hide]` or single-class discipline.
- The hamburger nav depends on `:checked` sibling selectors and `display:none` fallbacks (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). Gmail and Outlook don't support this. The source shows no confirmed non-interactive fallback in the truncated portion.
- Alt text, image dimensions and contrast can't be assessed because the body is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The template header comment was left in the sent HTML. It lists the required variables and the instruction `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`. It exposes internal data-extension and field names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`, `@base_refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`. It also shows a leftover instruction, `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. This is a template-hygiene and information-disclosure issue. Anyone can read it with View Source.
- In the visible portion there are no unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `@var` tokens in rendered content. Only the comment contains them.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- QA flags the `List-Unsubscribe` header as not found (WARN). The QA note says the relay may not capture it, so this is **unconfirmed**.
- The `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058 one-click) is also not found. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements make this a risk if it is truly absent. Verify in the raw ESP send.
- The `Authentication-Results` header is absent because of the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice. Alignment still needs checking at the source.
- The footer (physical address and unsubscribe link) is outside the truncated HTML, so CAN-SPAM body requirements can't be assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM or landing-page data is visible in the truncated source. The template references a `@refid` built from `@base_refid`, which suggests tracking goes through a campaign ref ID rather than UTMs. This is unverified.
- The 17 redirect links were not followed, so landing-page alignment with the CHANEL Cruise subject can't be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the ESP template comment (the `REQUIRED variables` block and the `SET @user` line) from the production output. Place the `<!DOCTYPE>` first.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a descriptive `<title>`, such as the subject line.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` in the raw ESP message, and verify SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment outside the relay.
5. Shorten the URLs in the plain-text part, or reduce the number of links in it.
6. Switch the mobile attribute selectors to `~=` and confirm a fallback for the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook.
7. Re-run QA with the click-redirect domains whitelisted (or resolved) so destinations and UTM/ref parameters can be validated.
8. Re-audit with the full, untruncated HTML to cover alt text, footer compliance and link targets.
