# 1,000+ new luxury arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Six Neiman Marcus emails in four days. I'm now officially tracking. And here's the thing — this one lands on the same day as the Designer Sale email I already opened this morning, the one screaming "Up to 50% Off." So I go from a 50%-off signal to a full-price new arrivals grid within hours, and the cognitive whiplash is real. These two emails should not share a calendar date.
- That said, the email itself is clean. Neiman Marcus logo, black hero, tasteful grid — they know their lane. And unlike Macy's, which keeps dressing up confirmations and triggered emails in urgency clothing they haven't earned, this one is honest: it's a merchandising email, it looks like a merchandising email, no tricks. I respect that. But honest-and-forgettable is still forgettable.
- There's no offer here. No InCircle point multiplier, no gift-with-purchase threshold, no event invitation — nothing that gives me a reason to click *today* instead of *eventually*. The Beauty Event email from June 8 had a $125 GWP threshold I practically memorized. This one gives me twelve product thumbnails and a "Shop Women's" button. I'll mark it read and come back if I remember.

## What's working

- **The brand grid earns its keep.** Versace, L'Agence, Givenchy, Fendi, Dries Van Noten, Akris Punto — those names read well to me at 47. This isn't Kohl's throwing a shopping cart emoji at me; these are brands that mean something.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Black hero first, product grid second, footer last. My eye lands exactly where Neiman's wants it.
- **Hero copy is honest.** "Brand New Styles Just Landed" is direct and correct. I don't feel baited.
- **Dual-gender CTAs.** I sometimes shop men's gifts, and the clean split to "Shop All Men's New Arrivals" is a small courtesy that adds up.
- **No render bugs.** Every product image loaded, no overlapping text, no broken layout. After the cadence sins, at least the execution is sound.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** I don't reach for my card at Neiman's without a reason — a GWP threshold, an event, triple points, *something*. Sephora would have surfaced a Beauty Insider perk right in the hero. This email gives me nothing to act on.
- **Same-day send as the 50%-off Designer Sale email is a messaging mistake.** Why would I pay full price for anything today when your other email this morning promised deep discounts?
- **"Shop Women's" as a CTA is barely a CTA.** Neiman's is a 7-floor store; that's a department, not a destination. Sephora opened cleaner than this — their "STARTS TODAY: up to 50% off select beauty" email put product and benefit in the same breath.
- **Sixth email in four days.** I'm a loyal customer, not a subscriber to be carpet-bombed. The June 8 Beauty Event set a high watermark; by now, the signal is drowning in noise.
- **No InCircle mention anywhere.** I'm almost certainly an InCircle member. A loyalty hook — even a small "Earn double points on new arrivals through Sunday" — would have given me a click reason.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Layer in a loyalty incentive.** "Earn triple InCircle points on new arrivals through Sunday" in the hero subhead costs Neiman's almost nothing and gives me a concrete reason to click today. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Break the same-day send collision.** If a 50%-off Designer Sale goes out at 9am, the full-price new arrivals should go out tomorrow. Mixed pricing signals in one day train customers to wait for the sale.
- 3. **Sharpen the hero to one or two lead products.** The CHANEL Métiers d'Art email from June 10 stopped my scroll because it had a story. Lead with "New from Fendi and Dries Van Noten" and link directly to those items, not a 1,000-product category page.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence.** After the June 8 Beauty Event, I was engaged. Six emails later, I'm fatigued. The Beauty Event email worked precisely because it felt curated and earned.
- 5. **Replace the generic CTA with brand-specific or category-specific language.** "Shop New Givenchy," "Explore the Fendi Drop," or even "Shop Women's New Arrivals by Brand" tells me what I'm getting on the other side.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "1,000+ new luxury arrivals"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with multi-table CRM lookups for personalization. Four QA warnings were raised; none are hard failures, but two compliance headers are absent from relay capture and one personalization variable has an unresolved dual-definition that is a live send risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 26 tracking links unverified | QA skipped all click-redirect domains — link integrity (404s, redirect chains, destination domains) is unknown |
| Plain-text version is 90.5% URLs | 5,270 of 5,820 chars are URLs; no readable prose — high spam-filter risk and an inaccessible fallback for plain-text clients |

The plain-text ratio is the most actionable issue here: major filtering systems (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) penalize plain-text bodies that are near-entirely machine-generated URL strings.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead code in `<head>`** — three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags shipped to production:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
No functional impact, but indicates an unclean template with debugging artifacts.

**Zoom prevention — WCAG 1.4.4 violation** — the active viewport tag uses `maximum-scale=1`:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
This blocks user-initiated zoom in mobile browsers and violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Fragmented responsive breakpoints** — responsive overrides are split across two separate `<style>` blocks targeting three different breakpoints (480px, 520px, 600px), increasing specificity conflict risk. No functional bugs confirmed from the truncated source, but this is a maintenance hazard.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved dual-scenario on `@user`** — the template header contains an explicit instruction ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW") and two competing definitions:

```
// Active (apparent): @user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
/* Commented out:    SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */
```

If the wrong scenario is live, `@user` either resolves to an EMAIL_ID integer or a SHA-256 hash — downstream URL params built on `@user` would silently pass the wrong value to analytics or site personalization endpoints. This is a live send risk if not audited before deployment.

**Null-value risk on `@uEm` and `@cmdid`** — both are looked up from `NM_CRM_Feed`:
```
@uEm  = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm",   "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
If a subscriber has no row in `NM_CRM_Feed` (e.g., a recent acquisition or non-CRM address), both return empty strings. Any URL built with these tokens will emit `&uEm=&cmdid=`, which breaks analytics attribution and may cause site-side errors depending on how these params are consumed.

**No-op CONCAT** — `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` performs no transformation. This is either a vestigial pattern or an incomplete template (e.g., a suffix was removed but the CONCAT wrapper was not cleaned up). Should be simplified to `@refid = @base_refid` or documented.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Header | Status | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` | **Not captured** (QA WARN) | Gmail, iOS Mail, and Yahoo use this for native unsubscribe UI; absence increases spam complaint rate |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **Not captured** (QA WARN) | Google and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders above the 5k/day threshold |
| `Authentication-Results` | **Not captured** (QA WARN) | SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unverifiable through the AgentMail relay |

**Note on header capture**: All three missing headers may be present at the MTA level and stripped by the AgentMail relay before delivery. The SFMC platform injects `List-Unsubscribe` by default. Verify against the raw SMTP headers at the receiving MTA, not the relay-captured version, before treating these as confirmed absences.

**DMARC alignment risk** — the envelope From is `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). DMARC alignment requires that `e.neimanmarcus.com` is explicitly covered by the `neimanmarcus.com` DMARC policy (relaxed alignment) or has its own `_dmarc.e.neimanmarcus.com` record. Without `Authentication-Results` capture, this cannot be confirmed.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Full assessment blocked — all 26 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the QA prober, so destination URLs, UTM parameter presence, and landing page alignment cannot be verified from this audit.

Structural risk identified: the null-value scenarios on `@uEm` and `@cmdid` (section 4 above) would silently corrupt click-tracking parameters for any subscriber absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, producing analytics gaps or broken personalization on the landing page.
