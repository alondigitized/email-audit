# 1,000+ new arrivals to perfect your individual style
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Eleven. That's where I am with Neiman Marcus since July 2nd, and today they walk in with a fresh-face new arrivals email — no sale, no Last Call, no countdown. After a solid week of percentage-off conditioning, the editorial pivot almost felt like a deep breath. The hero is restrained and confident: "Brand New Styles Just Landed" on a black ground, the kind of clean authority you'd expect from a Saks window display. And the brand lineup they're showing me — Ferragamo, Chloé, Fendi, Dolce&Gabbana — is genuinely my tier. These are the labels I price-check at the beauty counter before drifting over to the handbag floor.
- But here's my problem: they've split this email between women's and men's, and I spent half the scroll time looking at Amiri shorts and Tom Ford dress shirts I have zero use for. No InCircle acknowledgment. No price signals. No event invitation. Just a very pretty catalog that says "things exist, go find them." After ten back-to-back urgency emails, I came in primed to buy and they handed me a mood board.
- I'd mark this read and maybe revisit the Ferragamo bag if I had time — but that's my brand loyalty doing the lifting, not this email. I wouldn't forward it to a friend; there's no hook to share. A quiet "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" click is the ceiling here.

## What's working

- **Hero earns the pause.** Black ground, clean white type, zero exclamation points — after a week of Last Call hysteria, this actually registers. It reads closer to Saks editorial than Macy's doorbuster, which is exactly the brand position Neiman Marcus should be defending.
- **Brand roster is legitimately compelling.** Ferragamo, Chloé, Dolce&Gabbana, Fendi, Dries Van Noten, McQueen in one grid — this is the kind of designer density that justifies a Neiman Marcus relationship. Macy's could not assemble this lineup and Sephora's "Summer's hottest beauty" this week felt thin by comparison.
- **CTA sequencing is logical.** "Shop Women's" / "Shop Men's" in the hero, followed by a "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" anchor below the women's grid — the structure is clear and doesn't fight itself.
- **The render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy. After watching Sephora's stacking-weekend emails get increasingly chaotic in layout, that baseline discipline matters.

## What's weak

- **Eleven emails in seven days has compounded the debt.** Even a well-executed editorial send gets discounted when I've been flooded. I opened this on diminishing goodwill — Neiman Marcus has spent the week training me to expect discounts, and this email doesn't explain why I should shift gears to full-price browsing.
- **Men's content is real estate I didn't ask for.** I'm shopping for myself. Balenciaga sunglasses and Amiri denim shorts aren't relevant, and giving them equal visual weight buries the women's story that actually speaks to me.
- **Zero InCircle acknowledgment.** Not a single line about points, early access, or member privileges. Macy's at least waves the Star Rewards flag before asking me to scroll. Treating me like a cold prospect on email eleven is a relationship miscalculation.
- **No price visibility whatsoever.** Jumping from ten "up to 70% off" emails to a full-price browse with no price anchoring is a jarring ask. Even one visible price point — on the Ferragamo bag, on the Chloé trousers — gives me a reason to lean in or self-select out. Right now I'm browsing blind.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the send: women-only version.** Kill the men's grid entirely and use that space for a second women's brand row or a featured "InCircle Early Access" callout. That single change is the highest-leverage move; it makes this email feel curated for me instead of divided between me and someone else.
- 2. **Add one InCircle touchpoint.** Even a single line — "InCircle members: points post automatically on all new arrivals" — shifts the tone from anonymous storefront to valued-customer privilege. I've earned that acknowledgment.
- 3. **Give the Ferragamo tote a hero moment.** It's the most eye-catching piece in the women's grid — a full-width hero with a direct product link and one sentence of copy would give me a real reason to click, not just a reason to browse. Sephora at least spotlights one SKU; Neiman Marcus should be doing that with a $1,200 bag.
- 4. **Add a light truthful urgency signal.** Not a fake countdown — something like "New this week" or "Selling fast in the first 24 hours" on a featured piece creates a reason to act now instead of saving the tab and forgetting it.
- 5. **Surface at least one price.** Show me the Chloé trousers are $595 and I either say "okay" or I come back during the next Last Call. Right now I can't make any decision at all.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus — "1,000+ new arrivals" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript and delivered from `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Critical issues: development template comments are shipped in delivered HTML exposing internal data extension names and field mappings; one-click unsubscribe headers required by Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender rules are absent or uncaptured; and the plain-text body is 87% URLs, a known spam-filter trigger.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links unverified** — QA probe skipped all click-redirect links (`e.neimanmarcus.com` ESP domain). Destination URL integrity and UTM parameter presence are unconfirmed.

**`@refid` is a no-op** — The template defines `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")`. Concatenating an empty string produces an identical value to `@base_refid` and indicates an incomplete merge token or leftover placeholder that was never parameterized.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` have no null guards** — Both are resolved via `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)`. Subscribers absent from the CRM feed will silently resolve to empty strings, breaking any tracking parameter that depends on these values without any error signal.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** — Active viewport tag:
```
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
This prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Three commented-out viewport tags** — Non-functional, but indicate the template carries iteration history from production sends:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**iOS 9 animation hack present** — The `-webkit-animation: bugfix` keyframe loop targeting iOS 9 hamburger menus is dead code (iOS 9 = 2015) but carries no functional risk.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor; no rendering impact.

**Alt text** — Cannot audit fully due to source truncation.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Template comments shipped in delivered HTML** — The following block appears verbatim in the email source received by recipients:

```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE) @base_refid (from Campaign Calendar)
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This exposes: data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), internal field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), and a dead-code alternate scenario for `@user`.

**Dual `@user` resolution scenarios are unresolved** — One scenario sets `@user` via a lookup; the other (commented out) sets it to `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. A comment reading "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" is present in the delivered source, indicating the template was not finalized before send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA WARN. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this header for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. Its absence (or relay non-capture) risks inbox placement degradation. Needs raw SMTP-level verification — the AgentMail relay may have dropped it.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured** — QA WARN. One-click unsubscribe is required by the same Google/Yahoo 2024 mandate. Cannot distinguish between genuinely missing and relay-dropped without SMTP header inspection.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC: unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured via relay. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain; alignment with `neimanmarcus.com` (the visible brand domain) cannot be confirmed from available data.

**Plain-text body: 87% URLs** — QA flagged 5,289 of 6,099 plain-text characters as URLs. Spam filters penalize this pattern; a plain-text part this URL-dense provides no meaningful fallback and can reduce deliverability scores.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — Not visible in truncated source; cannot audit.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable** — All 26 tracking links were skipped. Cannot confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` are appended to destination URLs. If the ESP click-redirect does not preserve UTMs, GA4 will attribute clicks as direct traffic.

**`@refid` / `@cmdid` are the primary attribution tokens** — NM appears to use proprietary `refid`/`cmdid` parameters rather than standard UTMs. If `@cmdid` resolves empty (due to missing CRM feed entry), that subscriber's session arrives at the site with a broken CRM attribution token and no fallback.

---

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|

| High | Confirm SPF/DKIM pass and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` via full header inspection |
