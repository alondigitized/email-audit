# Today only: 20% off home 🤩 New summer picks await ☀️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Forty-four. That's roughly where I've lost count of Kohl's in my inbox since Memorial Day, and this one shows up with two different promises crammed into one subject line — home sale AND new summer picks — like it couldn't decide which pitch it wanted to make. I open it and the first thing my eye lands on is not the home section at all; it's a "New Arrivals" fashion spread of breezy dresses and men's shorts. The "Today Only" home-discount banner is there at the top, but the email immediately pivots into fashion, then Nike activewear, then clearance, then a loyalty discount at the very bottom. It's four emails wearing one trenchcoat.
- To be fair, there's real content here — concrete percentages, recognizable brands, a seasonal hook. The 4th of July email two days ago (the one that actually stopped my thumb) understood that a single clean narrative beats a catalog dump. This one did not get the memo. Where that send felt like Kohl's made a choice, this one feels like the product team had a Tuesday meeting and every department got a slot.
- I would not click. I would definitely not forward it. I'd file it under "there's probably a real deal in here somewhere but I'm not doing the archaeology."

## What's working

- **"Today Only" urgency at the top** — the banner is the first thing I see and it does communicate a real deadline, which is more than the last two "HOURS LEFT" sends gave me.
- **Clean navigation row** — Decor, Bedding, Bath, Kitchen & Dining is a fast scannable way to route me if I actually came in for the home offer. Sephora does this well with their category pills; Kohl's learned something.
- **The extra 20% loyalty note at the bottom** — burying it aside, the stacked-savings mechanic (clearance + extra 20%) is genuinely interesting if you're already shopping Kohl's. I notice it. I just wish it led.
- **Seasonal imagery** — the summer-dress models and the outdoor loungewear actually feel like July, which is more than the last batch of "20% off sitewide" sends that used generic photography.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-email mismatch** — the subject promises a home sale, but the hero visual is a fashion spread. That bait-and-switch registers immediately and it damages trust before I've scrolled an inch. Neiman Marcus would never open with accessories copy and then show me luggage.
- **Four competing offers, zero hierarchy** — home 20%, Nike 50%, clearance 70%, loyalty extra 20%. Macy's does this too and it's the thing I like least about them. Sephora sends one number and makes it feel like an event; this feels like a clearance rack.
- **Nike gets a better hero than the headlined home sale** — that full-width Nike activation block is actually well-designed, which makes the supposed top-line offer feel like a footnote. Priorities are inverted.
- **Still no beauty** — I know Kohl's carries beauty brands. Not once in this run of sends have I seen a targeted hook for someone like me. Sephora sent me a personalized 30% off my exact loyalty tier two days ago; I'm still thinking about that email. This one I'll forget in 90 seconds.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is what, number 44? The fact that I'm still counting means the damage is done. "Today only" lands differently when "today" has happened every day for six weeks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and commit.** If the subject is the home sale, open with the home sale — a gorgeous styled shot of a summer outdoor patio or a fresh-linen bedroom, not a fashion spread. The mismatch between subject and body is the single biggest repair.
- 2. **Move the loyalty stacking note to the hero.** "Today only: your extra 20% stacks with clearance" is a better first sentence than anything currently above the fold. That's the reason a loyalty member opens this email.
- 3. **Cut the Nike section or make it a postscript.** A Nike 50% block that outdesigns the titular offer is a product-prioritization problem. If Nike is a supporting offer, it should look like one.
- 4. **Add one beauty or personal care hook.** Even one product callout — a fragrance, a skincare brand they carry — tells me I'm seen. Right now I'm just a body.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** This isn't a single-email fix, but going from daily to three-times-a-week would make "Today Only" feel true again.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members only: stack 20% on top of clearance — today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in for summer + your 20% is live right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Home, fashion, Nike — one extra 20% covers everything.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearance already up to 70%. Your member discount stacks.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Today only: 20% off home" (2026-07-06)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-based HTML mailer with three separate pixel trackers and 22 click-redirect links. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, and accessibility gaps are widespread across tracking and content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Three distinct open-pixel systems are firing simultaneously:**

- `https://click.chp.kohls.com/o/6ca9786a-...` — CoherentPath behavioral tracker
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif?mi_u=604230016` — Marigold Engage (Sailthru) open pixel
- `https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?ZI6FPHUZG3TELE7LL45K2...` — secondary ESP open tracker (likely Braze or Cheetah)

Triple-counting opens means engagement metrics in any single platform are unreliable by default.

**22 click links skipped** by the HTTP probe — all route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect chains. No broken links were confirmed, but redirect depth and final destination health are unverified.

**Malformed HTML tag** in the pixel block:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag is invalid and orphaned — no matching opening tag. Most clients will ignore it, but it is a parse error that could trip strict validators or certain rendering engines.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA scan. All are either tracking pixels or content images served from `mi.kohls.com`. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (they do, per QA flags, so the issue is the *absence* of the attribute entirely vs. an empty string — these render as broken image text in some clients when images are blocked).

Specific flagged srcs:
- `o.gif` (open pixel) — no `alt`
- `event` (Adobe Audience Manager pixel at `kohls.demdex.net`) — no `alt`
- 6 content/rp images — no `alt`, meaning image-off renders are completely blank for those product areas

**`<title>` tag is empty** (`<title></title>`). Screen readers announce the tab/window title; an empty title is a WCAG 2.4.2 failure.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag disables pinch-to-zoom — a known WCAG 1.4.4 violation and accessibility barrier on mobile.

**`max-height: auto`** in the responsive CSS is not a valid value (`auto` is not accepted for `max-height`). Browsers silently ignore it; no functional breakage, but dead code.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) visible in the truncated source. The `mi_u=604230016` parameter in Marigold pixel URLs is a resolved subscriber ID, confirming personalization tokens processed correctly at send time.

No issues found.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Physical address:** Not present in the truncated HTML source. QA confirms no address detected. CAN-SPAM §7(a)(4) requires a valid postal address. If it appears below the truncation point, this is a non-issue — but it must be verified in the full render.

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers:** Not captured by the AgentMail relay. These are required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024) for senders at Kohl's volume. The relay gap means status is unknown — not a confirmed absence, but must be verified at the MTA level.

**RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`):** Same relay gap. Non-compliance at this send volume would affect Gmail placement.

**Authentication-Results header:** Not present via relay — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unverifiable from this capture. Sending domain `s.kohls.com` should be checked directly.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All 22 click links route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect chains. UTM parameters on final landing page URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain — the HTTP probe skipped all 22.

The `mi_ecmp=1016725_202676` and `email_name=260706_DG_Email_Monday` parameters on the Marigold image URLs are campaign-tagged, indicating the campaign ID and date are wired correctly into the tracking layer. Whether the same campaign parameters survive through to `kohls.com` product pages is unverified.
