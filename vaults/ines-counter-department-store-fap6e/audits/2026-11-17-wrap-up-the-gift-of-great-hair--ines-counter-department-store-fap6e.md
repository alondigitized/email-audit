## Executive summary

- Tenth Sephora email in about a week, and I could feel it. The hero is a Dior Mini freebie, but the subject is about hair gifts. So the top and the bottom of this email argue with each other. Then it keeps going: hair gift sets, Spot It. Shop It., Jo Malone, Cyber Week, free sample bags, a holiday gift guide. It's a catalog with a long legal block at the bottom.

That tells me what I'm looking at. But I'm a counter regular. I'd rather be told what my Beauty Insider points get me than be handed six hair sets with the offer buried in the fine print.

- The Dior Mini "Get a DIOR Mini" block at the top gives me a concrete reward and a "Shop to redeem" button.
- The product grid shows set names, prices and the "was/now" markdown on the Fenty set. That's the kind of detail I want.
- The "Only at Sephora" and "Limited edition" tags give me a reason to buy here instead of at Nordstrom.
- The "Hi, Rosie" greeting at the top treats me as a known member.

## What's weak

- The Dior hero and the hair subject don't match. I opened for hair and the first thing I see is a fragrance freebie.
- It's too many modules. Hair, Jo Malone, Cyber Week, sample bags and a gift guide all compete, and no single CTA wins.
- The legal text at the bottom is a wall. The Dior offer has conditions, but the screen only says "Exclusions/terms apply."
- The product prices are small, and the grid is hard to read on a phone without zooming.
- Nothing speaks to my tier. I can't tell what Rouge or VIB status gets me here.

- 1. Pick one story. Either lead with the hair gifts and move the Dior freebie lower, or retitle the email around the Dior Mini.
- 2. Show Beauty Insider points or tier pricing on the hair sets. That's what would make me click.
- 3. Cut the bottom modules (Jo Malone, Cyber Week, sample bags) and send them separately. Competitors like Neiman Marcus keep one clear offer per send.
