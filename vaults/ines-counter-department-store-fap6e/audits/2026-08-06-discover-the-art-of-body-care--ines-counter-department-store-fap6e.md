My eye actually landed somewhere good for once: "Pick a FREE Body-Care Trial Size" up top with a real mechanic (from $40 order, redeem at checkout) is the kind of concrete offer I want, and it's above the fold before the mood copy even kicks in.
- Then it's the usual Sephora scroll — Wash, Hydrate, Stay Fresh, Exfoliate, each with four-ish products, CLEAN badges, "Exclusive at Sephora" tags. It's organized better than the "hot right now" grab-bag emails, I'll give it that — at least there's a taxonomy instead of just vibes. ## What's working

- The free trial size offer up top is a real, specific mechanic ($40 minimum, redeem at checkout) — that's rarer than Sephora usually manages.
- CLEAN badges and ingredient callouts (Cashmere Wood, Vanilla, Bergamot; Amber, White Musk, Sandal) are the kind of product detail I actually read before buying fragrance-adjacent body stuff.
- The section headers (Wash, Hydrate, Stay Fresh, Exfoliate) give this some structure — better organized than the "So hot right now" scroll-dump from two days ago.
- The +500 Beauty Insider bonus points challenge is on-brand loyalty language, which I recognize from the Rouge program.

## What's weak

- Eight-plus product rows before I get to anything resembling urgency or a second offer — this is Kohl's-level overload territory, just with prettier product photography.
- No pricing hierarchy — L'Occitane at $30, Sol de Janeiro at $32, minis bundle unclear — I have to hunt for numbers instead of them being sold to me.
- The "Body-Care Minis" set near the bottom shows "22 BLOOMS" over the products with no price visible in the screenshot — that's a miss, I want to know what a bundle costs before I scroll further.
- The Rihanna "Fenty" callout and "App Early Access" block at the very bottom feel tacked on, unrelated to body care, diluting a scroll that was already too long.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the +500 bonus points challenge up near the free trial size offer — two real hooks stacked at the top beats one hook followed by seven screens of product grid.
- 2. Cut the product count by half. Neiman Marcus proved this week that one clean "EXTRA 40% OFF" beat two days of horse-and-linen mood boards — Sephora's mood-board-then-catalog approach has the same problem, just with skincare instead of resort wear.
- 3. Put a price on the Body-Care Minis bundle in the image itself, not just "22" — I'm not going to click through to find out.
