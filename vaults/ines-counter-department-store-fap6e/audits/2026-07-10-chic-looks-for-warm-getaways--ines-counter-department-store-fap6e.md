# Chic looks for warm getaways
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. I've counted thirteen Neiman Marcus emails since July 2nd, and today they walk in without a sale sign, without a countdown, without a single percentage point — just "Sea-Breezy Style" and a model on a beach in a maxi skirt that I genuinely covet. I'll give them this: the editorial is clean, the photography is sharp, and after a week of being Last-Called into submission, the absence of urgency feels almost meditative.
- But here's the trap. They spent eight days training me to expect a discount. My hand is literally conditioned to look for a percentage before I click. A.L.C. sets and Michael Kors Collection blazers are exactly my register — I would have clicked *yesterday* if yesterday's email had told me they were on sale. Today the same brands show up with no offer attached and I feel cheated by a pattern I didn't even realize I'd internalized. That's not Neiman Marcus's fault entirely, but it is their send strategy's fault.
- I'd mark this read, maybe tap the A.L.C. sets section out of genuine interest, and close without buying. I wouldn't forward — Sephora's July 8th summer editorial at least surprised me with something I hadn't seen; this one feels like a screensaver version of a luxury resort brochure.

## What's working

- Hero photography earns its place — the beach model in the flowing two-piece is aspirational without being age-irrelevant; this reads closer to Saks editorial than Macy's promotional overload
- "Effortless dressing doesn't get much easier than one-and-done sets from favorite designers" is genuinely good copy — it's the sentence I would say to a friend when I hand her the set off the rack
- Specific brand callouts (A.L.C., Michael Kors Collection) give the email a curated spine; this is the opposite of Kohl's blasting "fill your cart" at me for the 48th time since Memorial Day
- "Match with a personal style advisor" surfaced at the bottom is a differentiator NM actually owns — it's a soft loyalty signal even without the InCircle badge next to it
- Visual hierarchy flows without friction: hero → headline → CTAs → sets editorial → product grid → app prompt; my eye never got lost

## What's weak

- No offer, no pricing, no sale bridge — after nine days of Last Call conditioning, the complete absence of even a "shop the sale" footnote is a missed layering opportunity; Nordstrom would have stitched the editorial and the clearance tab together without breaking the visual tone
- InCircle is invisible — I am a points collector and they treated me like a stranger; even Sephora's weakest editorial (the July 8th "Summer's hottest beauty" that scored a 6) at least gestured toward Insider tier
- The "We think you'll like" trio loses the thread: a jewelry accessories brand alongside a Michael Kors Collection blazer and an A.L.C. jacket disrupts the luxury register the hero was carefully building
- The second CTA pill under the hero is unreadable at mobile scale — if it's a brand name, I can't make it out, and an illegible button is a dead button
- Thirteen emails in eight days has primed me for high-alert filtering mode; a palate-cleanser editorial is the right call strategically, but the goodwill is diluted by sheer volume

## Recommendations

- 1. **Bridge editorial to the sale** — one sentence below the hero: "Resort favorites from A.L.C. and more — shop Last Call styles included." Give me the beauty of the photography AND the economic permission I've been conditioned to expect. This is not a contradiction; Nordstrom does it seamlessly.
- 2. **Surface InCircle before the footer** — a single tasteful line: "InCircle members enjoy complimentary shipping on these styles." That is the difference between browsing and buying for someone like me.
- 3. **Clean up the product grid** — keep A.L.C. and Michael Kors Collection; replace the accessories brand with a third sets or cover-up option that stays in the resort fashion lane the hero established.
- 4. **Fix the second hero CTA** — make it legible or replace it with a category label that reads at thumbnail size on mobile.
- 5. **Cadence correction** — this editorial would land significantly better as email 5 in 8 days, not email 13. The palate cleanser needs some palate.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Effortless dressing for wherever you're headed — shop sets, cover-ups & more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sea-breezy style picks — InCircle members ship free`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Chic looks for warm getaways"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) table-layout send with a CSS hamburger nav. Two compliance headers are absent from the relay capture, and the plain-text part is almost entirely URLs — both are active deliverability risks for bulk-sender Gmail/Yahoo enforcement.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**30 tracking links unprobed** — all click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP probe. No broken-destination confirmation is possible; this is a coverage gap, not a confirmed bug.

**Plain-text body is 89% URLs** (`4148 / 4678 chars`). This exceeds the 70% warning threshold by a wide margin. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY`, Barracuda, Proofpoint) penalize plain-text parts that are link-dumps. The plain-text part should mirror readable body copy with links inline.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unquoted CSS attribute selectors** — the responsive stylesheet uses `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, etc. (no quotes around the value). Strict HTML parsers and some email clients treat these as invalid; the correct form is `*[class="contenttable"]`. If these selectors fail silently, mobile layouts will not apply.

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** — three alternates are present as HTML comments. This is dev artifact clutter; it does not affect rendering but increases template maintenance risk (wrong variant could be uncommented during a future edit).

**Hamburger nav relies on CSS checkbox toggle** — the `:checked~` sibling selector pattern used for the mobile nav has near-zero support in email clients (Outlook, Apple Mail, Samsung Mail all ignore it). The fallback is a permanently hidden nav on mobile, which is the acceptable degraded state, but the dead CSS adds ~2 KB of payload.

**iOS9 webkit animation bugfix** — `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding:0; } to { padding:0; } }` was a 2014-era workaround for an iOS Safari rendering bug. iOS9 is end-of-life; this code is dead weight but harmless.

**Alt text not verifiable** — the source is truncated before image tags appear. Alt text presence on all `<img>` elements should be confirmed in the full template, particularly for image-blocking environments (Outlook desktop default).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable scenario is ambiguous.** The template comment reads:

```
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

The `/* */` notation inside an HTML comment is not standard AMPscript syntax — it will not execute. The intent appears to be a scenario toggle (subscriber lookup vs. SHA-256 hash), but neither branch is visibly activated in the captured source. If the actual deployed template has the same structure, `@user` resolves as empty/null, which would break any tracking or personalization parameter that depends on it.

**All variable declarations are in HTML comments** — `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` are declared in comment blocks. This is normal SFMC AMPscript pattern (comments used as documentation headers), but the actual `%%[SET ...]%%` or `%%[VAR ...]%%` blocks must exist elsewhere in the template. Confirm they are present in the full source and not inadvertently inside comments.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are looked up from `NM_CRM_Feed`. If a subscriber has no matching record in that feed, both return empty string — verify downstream usage handles null gracefully (e.g., conditional UTM param inclusion).

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA: `[WARN]`). Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Missing it risks inbox demotion or filtering by both providers.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA: `[WARN]`, RFC 8058). One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` under the same Gmail/Yahoo policy. Its absence means Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button in the UI won't function automatically.

**Caveat:** both headers may be injected by the SFMC or ESP layer and simply not captured by the AgentMail relay. Confirm presence on a raw SMTP-level header dump (e.g., Gmail "Show original") before treating as a confirmed send-side bug.

**`Authentication-Results` not found** (QA: `[WARN]`). SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown from this capture. Same relay-capture caveat applies — verify via Gmail "Show original" to confirm DKIM signature from `e.neimanmarcus.com` is present and passing.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer (not visible in truncated source) — confirm both are present in the full render.
