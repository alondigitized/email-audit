# Don’t miss out on a $50–$500 gift card
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Neiman Marcus was in my inbox yesterday with the exact same offer — "Earn a $50–$500 gift card with your purchase today." Today it's back as "Don't miss out," which is the marketing equivalent of nudging me with your elbow while I'm standing right next to you. I know. You told me yesterday.
- That said, the gift card earn structure genuinely speaks to how I shop. The tiered table is clean, the hero is front and center, and Jimmy Choo and Zimmermann in the same scroll? That combination works on me more than I care to admit. This email isn't broken — it's just redundant. The cadence fatigue from fifteen-plus NM sends since July 2nd is the real drag.
- I'd click it — grudgingly, because it's the right offer for how I actually shop. I would not forward it to a friend because I'd have to explain that she already received this yesterday and will probably receive it again tomorrow.

## What's working

- **Tiered gift card table is the hero.** $500 → $50, $1,000 → $125, all the way up — scannable in three seconds, no mental math required. Neiman Marcus knows I'm doing math on this.
- **Brand mix earns trust.** Jimmy Choo, Ulla Johnson, Zimmermann, FRAME, Veronica Beard, Theory — that's not a random grab bag. That's a considered edit that signals someone in the building knows their customer.
- **"Get summer-ready" landing copy is appropriately light.** It doesn't oversell. After the gift card table does the heavy lifting, a one-line seasonal reminder is exactly the right amount of copy.
- **"LIMITED TIME" badge doesn't feel hysterical.** Compared to Macy's running "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" on a triggered email last week, this is admirably restrained.
- **No render issues.** Clean layout, images load, text is legible. Basic table stakes, but Sephora's "Obsessed? Same." email from earlier this month had nothing to look at either, so at least this one shows merchandise.

## What's weak

- **This is yesterday's email with a different subject.** "Don't miss out on a $50–$500 gift card" is the fear-of-missing-out remix of "Earn a $50–$500 gift card with your purchase today." The offer is identical. The creative is identical. Same code AJKMC, same brands, same table. Kohl's runs the same 20% + Kohl's Cash template what feels like every other day; I'd hoped Neiman Marcus was better than that.
- **Six CTAs is an editorial collapse.** SHOP WOMEN'S and SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING are two buttons doing the same job right next to each other. SHOP MEN'S CLOTHING in a women's-coded email is noise. SHOP HOME is a non sequitur. Pick three and commit.
- **Fine print is a wall.** I can see a dense block of promotional terms at the bottom. I know every gift card promo has exclusions — I've been around long enough — but the sheer volume of it makes the offer feel like it comes with an asterisk the size of a billboard. Saks would surface the key restriction in a single clean line and let me feel respected.
- **No personalization.** Fifteen emails in eleven days and not one of them uses my name or references what I actually browse. By now Nordstrom would know I'm not shopping wedding dresses (see: July 11th NM email — still recovering from that one).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rest the cadence.** Two gift card emails in two days is the fastest way to make me feel like a number, not a customer. Send this Monday, and let yesterday's version breathe for at least 48 hours.
- 2. **Cut CTAs to three.** SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING, SHOP SHOES, SHOP HANDBAGS. Those are my three. Drop MEN'S and HOME entirely; they dilute the offer and signal that this wasn't built for me.
- 3. **Surface one fine-print headline.** "Valid on full-price styles across women's, shoes, and handbags" — one sentence in light text above the fine print block. Let me shop with confidence instead of squinting at legalese.
- 4. **Add a hard date to "LIMITED TIME."** "Offer ends July 15" takes up nine characters and converts anxiety into action. Without it, I'm not sure whether I have two hours or two weeks.
- 5. **Personalize the hero subhead.** "Get summer-ready and earn a gift card with your purchase" is fine. "Ines, your summer edit earns up to $500 back" is a reason to keep my loyalty.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your gift card window closes soon — $50 to $500 back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Earn up to $500 back on the summer styles you're already eyeing`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends July 15. Spend $500 or more on Jimmy Choo, FRAME, Zimmermann and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four tiers. One code: AJKMC. Offer valid through this weekend only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy table-based layout but has three notable technical risks: an AMPscript variable scenario block that appears to have been left in an ambiguous state (the `@user` SHA-256 assignment is double-commented out), missing compliance headers not captured by the relay, and 26 unverified tracking links.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe.** All click-tracking URLs were excluded from validation. Broken redirect chains (e.g., expired campaign tokens, wrong environment slug in the base URL) would be undetected. No direct evidence of breakage, but no confirmation of health either.

**No issues found** with link syntax visible in the truncated source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta blocks user zoom.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
This prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Two alternative viewport tags are present but commented out — one of them (`user-scalable=no`) would be worse; the `width=device-width` variant without `maximum-scale` would be correct.

**Three conflicting viewport meta tags in source.** Two are commented out, but the commented-out variants (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29` and `width=1024`) signal unresolved responsive strategy debt. Not breaking, but increases maintenance risk.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack with `position: absolute`.** This pattern has known non-rendering in Outlook desktop and Outlook.com. If any subscriber opens on Outlook, the mobile nav degrades silently with no fallback visible in the truncated source.

**`p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` is a global reset.** This forces center-alignment on all paragraph elements, which will override any inline `text-align` on `<p>` tags throughout the email body in clients that load `<style>` blocks.

**No `role="presentation"` or `aria-hidden="true"` visible on layout tables.** Screen readers will attempt to interpret structural layout tables as data tables.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA-256 assignment is double-commented and likely unset.**

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This line uses both an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`) and an AMPscript block comment (`/* */`), making it inert. The block header says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" but no active scenario block is visible. If `@user` is referenced anywhere in the email body or tracking URLs for identity stitching or personalization, it will resolve as empty/null.

**Internal data model is exposed in HTML comment block.** The required-variables comment at the top leaks:
- Table names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`
- Field names: `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`

This is visible to anyone who views source on the email. Not exploitable on its own, but provides reconnaissance into the Salesforce Marketing Cloud data model.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` sourced from `NM_CRM_Feed` via lookup.** If a subscriber's email is not present in `NM_CRM_Feed`, these lookups return empty string — verify that downstream uses of these variables have null-guards (e.g., `IIF(EMPTY(@uEm), ...)`) or the sends will silently produce malformed tracking parameters.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured.**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policy (enforced February 2024 for >5k/day senders). Its absence — even if only undetected at the relay layer — should be confirmed against raw message headers before the next send.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured (RFC 8058).**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method variant) is required by Gmail/Yahoo policy for bulk senders. If not present, Gmail will show a slower multi-step unsubscribe flow and may down-rank deliverability.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown.**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (subdomain). Cannot confirm SPF pass or DKIM signature validity from available data.
