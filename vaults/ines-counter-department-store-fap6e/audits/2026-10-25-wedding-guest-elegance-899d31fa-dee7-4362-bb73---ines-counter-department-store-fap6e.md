# Wedding-guest elegance
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- I like the pictures and nothing else. The black strapless gown on the gold chairs is a beautiful hero. But this is the fifth Neiman's email I've opened since the 23rd, and it's another lookbook with no number in it. On the 24th they gave me "$50 gift card for every $200 spent." This morning they gave me "up to 30% off." This one gives me "Take Your Wows Seriously" and two outlined buttons.
- The second half is a man in a tuxedo jacket with a jeweled lapel, under the line "Jilt the fashion mainstays and opt for something cool and unconventional instead." I'm a 47-year-old woman shopping for a wedding-guest dress, so that section is wasted on me. I'd skim it, not click, and not forward it. I might come back if I had a wedding on the calendar, but I'd go to the site and not use this email.

## What's working

- The hero photo is lovely. A sleek black gown with a white sweetheart neckline and a rose at the hip looks like something I'd actually wear.
- The layout is clean and uncluttered, with plenty of white space. Sephora's top banners this week were busier, and this opens cleaner than that.
- "Shop Wedding" and "Shop Dresses" are plain, readable buttons that go where they say.
- The "Match with a personal style advisor" footer is a real service-tier signal. That's the Neiman's way of saying they'll look after me.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no price, and no deadline. Last week's Fall Style Sale emails at least gave me a number to react to.
- The second half is a men's tuxedo, with "Shop Men's Wedding" as a small gray underlined link. That feels like a different email glued onto mine.
- The buttons are thin gray text on white outlines. They're polite, but they don't pull my eye.
- There are no designer names, prices, or product details. I can't tell who made the dress, so I have nothing to click on.
- Nothing mentions InCircle or points. I won't spend without points back, and Macy's at least dangles a member perk in its subject lines.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real product and price under the hero, such as "The Jason Wu gown, $1,295." Then link the hero to that dress and not to a category page.
- 2. Add one offer line, either InCircle points or the sale percentage. Without a number, it's a magazine page.
- 3. Replace the men's block with a women's guest-dress edit, or segment the send so the men's block only goes to men.
- 4. Make the buttons solid black with white text and name the category: "Shop Wedding-Guest Dresses."
- 5. Slow the cadence. Five lookbooks in four days is a lot, and Kohl's has taught me what that fatigue feels like.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the template comment sits before `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC ...>`. Content ahead of the doctype can trigger quirks mode in legacy IE-based renderers.
- **Hamburger nav relies on a checkbox hack:** `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` need `:checked` sibling support. Gmail and some other clients don't support it, and the fallback can't be seen in the truncated source. Treat it as a risk, not a confirmed defect.
- **Brittle class selectors:** the rules use exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They fail if any element carries a second class.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space.
- **Alt text and tap targets:** I can't assess these because the body markup is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in rendered content in the visible source.
- The leaked comment shows the personalization inputs. These include `@email`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and a `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` scenario. Whether hashed identifiers (`uEm`, `cmdid`) end up in link query strings can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
