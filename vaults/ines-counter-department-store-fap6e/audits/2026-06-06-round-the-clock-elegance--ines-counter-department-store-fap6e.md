# Round-the-clock elegance
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- I'm going to say this plainly: this is the fifth or sixth Neiman Marcus email I've opened in what feels like seventy-two hours, and I'm running low on goodwill. The one that landed two days ago — the gift card event, concrete tiers up to $1,250 back — earned that attention. This one shows up behind it like a beautiful but empty-handed houseguest. "Day-to-Night Elegance" is a genuinely lovely editorial premise. The hero shot of that camel coat, the three pieces in the grid — it all looks expensive and considered. I appreciate that Neiman Marcus isn't screaming at me the way Kohl's does with its perpetual 85%-off countdown clock. But there is not a single offer, not a price, not an InCircle nod anywhere in this email. I cross-shop Nordstrom and Saks constantly, and both of those brands know how to wrap aspiration around an incentive.
- The thing that actually stopped me mid-scroll: there's a "Shop Men's New Arrivals" callout tucked into the right-hand module of what is clearly a women's styling email. Not a subtle nod to gifting. Not "for the person you're traveling with." Just... men's arrivals. That's a segmentation miss that makes me feel like Neiman Marcus doesn't actually know who I am.
- I'll scroll. I won't click. I certainly won't forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- **The "Day-to-Night Elegance" headline lands.** It's the kind of copy that respects me as an adult — no exclamation points, no emojis, no countdown timer. A sentence that implies lifestyle, not desperation.
- **The three-product grid is visually clean.** The black feathered skirt, the layered beige blouse, and the white ruffle top photograph beautifully together and actually tell a coherent story.
- **Navigation bar up top is uncluttered** — WOMEN, MEN, SHOES, SALE — and I can find the sale tab if I want it.
- **The styling copy is useful.** "Pair with white trousers during the day and a slim skirt and sandals for night" is specific editorial advice I'd find in a magazine. That's the right register for NM.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** Not a percentage. Not a dollar off. Not even an InCircle point multiplier. After the June 4th gift card event email, which gave me concrete tiers and a real reason to open my wallet, this feels like a non sequitur.
- **The men's "New Outlook" / "Shop Men's New Arrivals" module.** In a women's fashion email. I am not shopping for a man right now. This is exactly the kind of segmentation sloppiness that makes me feel like a database record, not a customer.
- **Subject line is doing nothing.** "Round-the-clock elegance" is 24 characters of aspiration with no hook. Sephora's "LAST DAY for 10% off" at least tells me *why* to open. This tells me nothing.
- **No pricing anywhere.** I can see three products and I have no idea if they're $150 or $1,500. For a brand I cross-shop against Saks and Nordstrom, that's a friction point — I need context before I click.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is send number five or six this week. Even a beautiful email can't survive that inbox density without an offer attached.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a reason to click — any reason.** Even "complimentary shipping today" or "InCircle double points on tops through Sunday" would transform this from a look-book into a transaction. The June 4 gift card email proved NM knows how to do this.
- 2. **Kill the men's module or replace it with a cross-sell that makes sense** — a "complete the look" shoe or bag recommendation, not men's arrivals.
- 3. **Show prices.** Even a "from $295" under each grid item anchors the scroll and signals transparency. Macy's always anchors its hero offers even when the number hurts.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence.** Five emails in three days is eroding the open equity the gift card send built. One fewer blast this week would have made this one land harder.
- 5. **Sharpen the subject line** — tie it to a product or a mini-offer:
- **Subject Alt A:** `The piece that works from desk to dinner`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 pieces. Morning meeting to midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wear the silk blouse both ways — no outfit change needed. Shop now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The feathered skirt, the ruffle top, the look that earns a second glance.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Round-the-clock elegance"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard SFMC-rendered HTML blast with a partially-commented AMPscript variable block and several template hygiene issues. Four QA warnings — all compliance or deliverability-class — were raised; none caused outright failures but two are material for inbox placement at scale.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links present, none probed.**
All click URLs are wrapped in redirect domains and were skipped by the HTTP checker. No broken-link verification was possible. This is expected for ESP-tracked mail, but the absence of probe data means dead-link detection requires a separate pass against the live redirect targets.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs (3,170 of 3,686 chars).**
QA finding: `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`. The plain-text alternative is almost certainly auto-generated from tracked URLs rather than meaningful text. Spam filters that weight plain-text content (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_ONLY`, Barracuda heuristics) will penalize this. It also renders as unusable in plain-text mail clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport meta tags — template cruft.**
Three alternate viewport directives remain in the `<head>` as HTML comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate the template was never cleaned after breakpoint testing. One live viewport tag is active and correct; the others should be removed to reduce parser ambiguity and template debt.

**`<title>` tag has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but visible in browser preview panes.

**Responsive CSS uses `*[class=...]` attribute selectors exclusively.**
Example: `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`. This pattern is intentional for Outlook compatibility but is fragile — it matches only elements whose `class` attribute is exactly that single token. Any element with multiple classes (`class="contenttable foo"`) will not match. Confirm no multi-class elements in the full HTML rely on these selectors.

**Hamburger nav is implemented via CSS checkbox hack.** No `<input type="checkbox">` is visible in the truncated source. If the checkbox is missing or mis-IDed in the full HTML, the mobile nav will be permanently hidden. Needs verification against the full source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable assignment is double-commented and will not execute.**

The AMPscript block reads:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The outer `<!-- -->` is an HTML comment; the inner `/* */` is an AMPscript comment. SFMC strips the AMPscript comment first, leaving `<!-- -->` which the HTML parser then drops entirely. **`@user` is never set.** If any downstream link or personalization token references `@user`, it will render as empty string or throw an AMPscript runtime error.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" is an unresolved manual selector.**
The comment block lists two scenarios for setting `@user` and explicitly instructs the operator to choose one. This is error-prone — if the sender forgot to uncomment the intended block, the wrong identity token ships. There is no guard or default.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.**
Concatenating with an empty string adds no value. If the intent was to append a suffix (e.g., a variant code), it was omitted.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent.**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Without it, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button in the UI is unavailable, which increases "Report Spam" rates as the user's only exit. CAN-SPAM does not require this header, but omitting it is a deliverability risk for Gmail Postmaster reputation.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058).**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. One-click unsubscribe (Gmail's machine-readable POST method) requires this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Google's bulk sender policy (enforced Feb 2024) mandates one-click unsubscribe for senders above the threshold. If NM is above 5,000 Gmail sends/day, this is a policy violation, not just a best-practice gap.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown.**
QA finding: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. The AgentMail relay did not surface authentication results. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain ESP envelope; its DKIM alignment with `neimanmarcus.com` (header From domain) should be confirmed via a direct DMARC lookup or MX Toolbox. Cannot confirm pass/fail from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

36 tracked links were not probed, so UTM parameter verification is not possible from this audit. The following should be confirmed in a live-link pass:

- All CTA links carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and ideally `utm_content` (per-module).
- `@refid`/`@base_refid` are appended to destination URLs as expected (see token issue above — if `@refid` is empty, any URL built from it will have a blank segment).
- Redirect chain from the tracked click URL terminates at the correct product/category page and does not cycle through an additional redirect that drops UTM params.
