# Up to 70% off clearance & closeout items right this way ➡️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is, by my rough count, somewhere around email number 35 from Kohl's this June. I know because I stopped counting around the 28th. So when "right this way ➡️" landed in my inbox — two days after "HOURS LEFT ⏰," one day after "Ends today," and right next to some Deal Days send from the same morning — I had to actively remind myself to click it open at all.
- Once I did: the email is competently executed. The 4th of July clearance angle is timely, the 70% hero lands immediately, and the Kohl's Cash banner is right below the nav where I can see it. Kohl's knows how to stack a promotional email. The problem isn't craft — it's that none of this is *for me.* I'm the woman who opened Neiman Marcus's Prada summer essentials send this same week and actually exhaled. The gap between those two emails isn't just price point; it's how the brand imagines the person on the other end.
- I'll mark this read without clicking. I won't forward it. And if July looks anything like June on the Kohl's cadence, I'm going to seriously consider the unsubscribe button — which would be a real shame, because I do buy at Kohl's. Just not this version of Kohl's.

## What's working

- **70% is a real number, immediately visible.** The clearance hero makes the offer legible before I even process the rest of the email — better than Macy's "confirm now for extra 25%" where the actual discount hid behind a confirmation interstitial.
- **Kohl's Cash earns real estate.** The "$5 Kohl's Cash" banner sits just below the navigation bar. I see it. I care about it. Points-back is the only reason I opened this at all.
- **The 4th of July timing is right.** Stars, stripes, clearance — that's exactly where the calendar is. It doesn't feel recycled the way the Deal Days parade felt by June 27.
- **Levi's gets its own named section.** A branded anchor mid-email gives me something to latch onto. Levi's isn't embarrassing to buy on sale.
- **Clean navigation bar.** Women / Men / Kids up top, self-selectable. The email isn't fighting itself structurally.

## What's weak

- **"CLEARANCE" in block caps is the hero.** Not a product. Not a brand. Not a lifestyle image. The word *clearance.* That is the opening move. Neiman Marcus led with Prada photography and zero shouting. This leads with the functional equivalent of a going-out-of-business sign.
- **This is email 35 in June from Kohl's.** The cadence has kneecapped every urgency signal in this subject line. "Right this way ➡️" after a month of "HOURS LEFT," "Ends today," and "better hurry 🏃‍♀️" is the boy who cried wolf in promotional clothing. I'm numb.
- **No beauty, zero.** I'm a beauty counter regular. Kohl's carries Clinique, The Ordinary, and plenty more. Not one skincare, fragrance, or cosmetics product appears in this email sent to me. That's a segmentation failure, not a category gap.
- **Models and aesthetic skew young and mass-market.** The women's section shows women who look mid-to-late twenties in denim cutoffs and patriotic swimwear. I'm 47. I don't need to see my exact reflection — but I need to see my *life* somewhere in here, and I don't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by past behavior.** If I've clicked beauty or women's apparel before, lead with that. A "beauty clearance: up to 70% off" hero block for someone like me would lap this generic swimwear opener every time. Sephora would never send me a July 4th email and bury the skincare.
- 2. **Lead with a named brand or product, not the word CLEARANCE.** "Levi's. Clinique. Up to 70% off." does the same job but treats me like an adult. Make the clearance feel curated, not liquidated.
- 3. **Add a real deadline.** "Through July 4th" or "while sizes last" gives the ➡️ arrow somewhere to point. Without a date, urgency is theater.
- 4. **Cut the send frequency in half starting July 1.** One strong deal email beats five identical ones. The Sephora "last chance" send I got this week worked partly because the cadence hadn't completely burned me out on them yet.
- 5. **Unpack the Kohl's Cash mechanics.** I see "$5 Kohl's Cash" but no earn thresholds. If you want me to justify a clearance haul on points, tell me what I need to spend.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's Clearance Email (2026-06-29)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a commercial blast but has a malformed HTML tag, 13 images missing alt text, and compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, physical address) that are either absent or unverifiable through the relay. Authentication status cannot be confirmed.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed HTML in open-pixel container**
The ExactTarget/SFMC open-tracking pixel block contains a stray closing tag with no matching opener:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?HSEBHYL2KURUVOLTKBJZGEGNNU.60266..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is invalid and will generate a parse error in strict renderers. Low breakage risk in practice, but should be removed.

**Three separate open-tracking pixels**
The email fires three distinct open/impression pixels on load:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/98735bda-...` (Coherent Path)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (MessageGears/mi)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?HSEBHYL2...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager — 0×0, no alt)

Triple-firing is unusual and could inflate open-rate counts across platforms if not deduplicated upstream.

**22 click-tracking links unverifiable**
All CTA links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com`. HTTP probing was skipped. UTM parameters and final destination URLs cannot be confirmed from source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 failure (Resize Text, Level AA) and will trigger accessibility flags in audits. Standard practice is to omit `user-scalable` or set it to `yes`.

**13 images missing `alt` text** (per QA)
Tracking pixels (1×1, 0×0) account for 4 of these — acceptable to leave blank or set `alt=""` explicitly and `aria-hidden="true"`, which two of the four already do. The remaining flagged images are content images via `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/...` and `images.am.kohls.com/is/image/kohlsprod/...`:
- `54952180_4_2x` (product image — no alt)
- `54991917_3s_2x` (product image — no alt)
- Multiple `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` rendition images — no alt

Product images with no alt text fail screen readers and will render as broken descriptions in image-off environments.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Outlook, Apple Mail) surface the title in previews or accessibility trees. Should be set to the email subject or a short descriptor.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA: WARN)
Not detected by the relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface one-click unsubscribe UI. If absent at the MTA level (not stripped by AgentMail), this will suppress native unsubscribe prompts and may trigger spam classification on high-volume sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA: WARN)
RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe is required by Google and Yahoo bulk sender policies (>5k/day). Absence is a deliverability risk.

**Physical address not detected** (QA: WARN)
HTML source is truncated — address may exist below the fold. If genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM §5 violation (15 USC 7704). Needs manual confirmation against the full rendered footer.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
Referrer suppression is intentional for privacy (standard practice), but means inbound analytics on kohls.com will show no email referrer for image loads. Not a bug, but worth noting for analytics teams.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTAs pass through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com` redirect chains — final destination URLs and UTM parameters are not visible in source and could not be probed. The following cannot be verified:
- Whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended at redirect time
- Whether the `email_name=260629_DG_Email_Monday` campaign code is passed through to site analytics
- Landing page existence and relevance to offer copy

The `mi_ecmp=1016160_2026629` parameter in rendition image URLs suggests MessageGears campaign tracking, but this only covers opens/image loads, not click-through attribution.

**Recommendation:** manually spot-check 2–3 CTA redirect URLs to confirm UTM chain is intact before reporting email-attributed revenue.
