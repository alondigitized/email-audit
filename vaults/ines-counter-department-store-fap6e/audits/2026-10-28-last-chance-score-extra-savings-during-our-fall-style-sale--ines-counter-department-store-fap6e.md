# Last chance: Score extra savings during our Fall Style Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the cleanest Neiman's email I've opened this week. After the "Earn a $50 gift card" reminder yesterday, I expected more gift-card talk, but this one is a straight sale. "UP TO 30% OFF" is in big red serif on a blush hero, with one black SHOP NOW button. I know the offer in about two seconds, and it doesn't shout at me.
- It stops being useful below the hero. The nine-product grid is all designer ready-to-wear and handbags, with no prices and no markdowns. "Up to 30%" on a Balmain bag is a different proposition from 30% on a Polo knit, and the email won't tell me which. The fine print says "select regular-price items" and "does not apply to beauty or fragrance." I buy at the beauty counter, so I'm excluded from the one thing that brings me in the door.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already in the market for a fall boot or a bag. Forward it? No. Mark it read? Probably, and I'd come back if the deadline got closer. The subject says "Last chance," but the fine print shows the sale ends tomorrow night, 10/29 at 10 PM CT. That date should be in the email body, not buried at the bottom.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "FALL STYLE SALE / UP TO 30% OFF" is the biggest thing on the page, and the red numerals pull my eye straight to it.
- It's quieter than Macy's. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email shouted at me; this one stays calm and reads as a premium store.
- The brand names under each product (Stuart Weitzman, Cinq à Sept, Canali) tell me it's real designer stock, not a clearance dump.
- The category buttons at the bottom (Handbags, Women's Shoes, Jewelry) let me skip to what I want.
- The personal style advisor and app tiles feel like a service I might use.

## What's weak

- The urgency is missing from the body. "Last chance" is in the subject, but the hero only says "LIMITED-TIME SAVINGS." The real deadline is in 8-point type at the bottom.
- There are no prices, no "now" figures and no percentages on any product. Sephora at least shows me a specific item and a reason, and Kohl's puts a coupon graphic in front of me. Here I have no idea what I'd save.
- "Exclusions Apply" under the button, plus "does not apply to beauty or fragrance," reads as a catch. I'm a beauty regular, so nothing here speaks to me.
- There's no loyalty or card angle. I won't use a card without points, and nothing says whether I earn anything on sale items. The gift card from yesterday's send is gone.
- The "extra 15% off for up to 65% off" line is only in the fine print. That's the best part of the offer, and it's hidden.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends tomorrow, 10/29." Right now the subject promises urgency the body doesn't deliver.
- 2. Show a real markdown on at least a few products, with the was and now prices. Pull the "extra 15% off, up to 65% off" line out of the fine print and up into the hero.
- 3. Add one line about points or the gift card from yesterday's email. Tell me if sale items earn.
- 4. Add a beauty or fragrance tile with a note that it's excluded, or send me a different email. Don't let me find the exclusion on my own.
- 5. Make the buttons specific, like "Shop boots & bags up to 30% off" instead of SHOP NOW.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Template comment left in the production HTML:** it sits before the DOCTYPE. It begins `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`, followed by `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
  - It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It shows the template was not stripped of authoring instructions.
  - A comment before the DOCTYPE can also push some renderers into quirks mode.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) on supporting clients.
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang` attribute. Screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. It doesn't describe the message.
- **Checkbox-hack mobile nav:** the CSS uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked`. This pattern doesn't work in Gmail (web/app) or Outlook desktop. The fallback isn't visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm it fails.
- **Legacy and fragile CSS:**
  - Exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=hide]` stop matching if an element gets a second class.
  - `body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s }` runs a permanent animation just to work around an iOS 9 bug.
  - These are low severity.
- **Alt text and image sizing:** not assessable. The `<img>` markup is cut off.
