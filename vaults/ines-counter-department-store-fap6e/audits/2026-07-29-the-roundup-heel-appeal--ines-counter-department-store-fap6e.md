# The Roundup: Heel Appeal
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Now this is what I open Neiman Marcus emails hoping to see — no siren-red "70% OFF LAST CALL" banner, no spend-and-save chart doing math homework for me, just a single gorgeous polka-dot mule sitting on a garden step like an editorial spread. It's a designer roundup, plain and simple: Valentino, Roger Vivier, Louboutin, Alexandre Birman, Manolo Blahnik, Malone Souliers, six pairs, six names I recognize on sight. That's the tier-program clarity I actually want — this is full-price, no-apologies designer access, and it reads like it.
- That said, "roundup" is doing a lot of work here with zero pricing anywhere on the page. Not one dollar sign, not a "from $X," nothing. I love a heel as much as the next woman who's spent a small fortune at the Louboutin counter, but I'm not clicking six product shots blind to find out which of these breaks four figures. Compare this to the July 29th "Extra 25% off Last Call" send from the same brand — that one told me exactly what I was getting and I clicked. This one just shows me pretty shoes and trusts me to go find out.
- Would I click? Probably, out of pure curiosity about the Malone Souliers polka-dot mule since it's the hero image twice over. Would I forward it? Maybe to my friend who's shoe-obsessed, purely as inspiration — not as a "let's shop this together" moment, because there's no offer to rally around.

## What's working

- The hero shot — that polka-dot raffia mule on the stone step with ivy behind it — looks like it belongs in a magazine, not an inbox. Genuinely stopped my scroll.
- Six recognizable designer names laid out cleanly in a grid, each with a real product photo and no clutter around it — this is the opposite of the Macy's "confirm now" scramble I sat through last week.
- "Shop Heels" and "Shop Garden Party Heels" as two distinct CTA buttons gives me an actual choice instead of one vague "Shop Now."
- No fine print, no exclusions, no "some restrictions apply" — refreshing after the Kohl's barcode-and-Rewards-bar routine I keep seeing.
- Consistent white space and serif type throughout — feels like Neiman Marcus, not a generic department store template.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing on a page full of luxury shoes is a real miss for someone like me who cross-shops Saks and Nordstrom on price before I commit — I want at least a "starting at $X" cue.
- "The Roundup" as a recurring section name means nothing to me if I haven't seen it before — no context for what this franchise is or why I should care it's back.
- Nothing here acknowledges I'm a Neiman Marcus regular — no InCircle points mention, no "you've earned X toward your next reward," nothing that says they know who I am the way Sephora's Insider badge does (even when Sephora fumbles the "0 points" greeting).
- "Garden Party Heels" as a button label is cute but undefined — garden party where, when, what does that even mean for a shoe silhouette I'm choosing in late July.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or price range under at least the hero shoe, and ideally under each of the six products — a designer roundup without a number is a lookbook, not a marketing email, and it costs me a reason to click through.
- 2. Tie this to InCircle status somewhere in the header or footer — even a small "Earn points on every pair" line would make me feel like a member and not a stranger.
- 3. Give "Garden Party Heels" a concrete hook — is this a June wedding-guest capsule, a specific in-store event? Right now it's a label without a story.
- 4. Add a line of social proof or styling context under the hero — "as seen on," a stylist quote, anything that earns the real estate that pure product shots don't.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New designer heels: Valentino, Louboutin & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your heel edit, starting at $395`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six standout styles for your next event, InCircle points included`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Manolo Blahnik to Roger Vivier — this week's heel roundup`
