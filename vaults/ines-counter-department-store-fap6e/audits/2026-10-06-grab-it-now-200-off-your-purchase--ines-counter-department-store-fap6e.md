# Grab it now: $200 off your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction was relief, then a raised eyebrow. Neiman's is giving me a real offer with a spend ladder: spend $200 and save $50, up to $1,000+ and save $200. The lavender hero is clean and doesn't shout. But I got the tier table, 15% off beauty, JUST4U and "Last chance" all last week. Now there's a fresh code, NMCIRCLE, and a new promo that overlaps with 3x points. I'm tracking the cadence, and Neiman's is training me to wait for the next one.
- The email is polite. It's a quiet serif headline, a table I can read without squinting, and a grid of designers I'd actually shop: Mac Duggal, Tod's, Strathberry, rag & bone. It's better mannered than Kohl's, whose Deal Days siren emoji sends were all noise by the sixth one. It also isn't Sephora's two-sales-at-once "Earn Points Faster" pile-up. The "up to" framing and the long fine print take a little shine off it, though. Nobody says "up to $200 off" on a $1,000 spend and feels generous.
- Would I click? Yes, if I already had a purchase planned. The Triple Points line for InCircle members is what I care about, since I don't swipe a card without points back. I'd forward it to a friend only if she was buying a gown. For me, it's a bookmark-for-the-event email, not a forward.

## What's working

- The spend-and-save table is instantly readable. $200/$50 through $1,000+/$200 needs no decoding, and it's the kind of clarity Macy's "confirm now" email never gives me.
- The code, NMCIRCLE, sits right under the headline, so I don't have to hunt for it in the fine print.
- The black band "Plus, InCircle Members earn Triple Points" puts my loyalty benefit right under the offer. That's what I look for.
- The designer grid has named brands in a clean 3×3 layout. Tod's, Jimmy Choo and Mac Duggal read as a department-store beauty-and-fashion shopper's list, not a clearance rack.
- The category buttons at the bottom make it easy to jump straight to shoes or handbags.

## What's weak

- The offer is "Up to $200 off" and "Exclusions Apply" is printed right in the hero. That's the buried fine print I notice, and it reads as a hedge.
- Two nearly identical promos in a week is a lot: this one and last week's 15% off beauty. The 15% text-signup block at the bottom is a third discount ask in the same email, and it undercuts the dollar-off ladder.
- Nothing in here is beauty, my main category. It's all clothing, shoes and bags, so it doesn't speak to me as a counter regular.
- The fine print is a dense wall in tiny type. I can't read the Triple Points rules on my phone without zooming.
- The product images aren't linked to prices or specific offers. Nine designers and no sense of what $200 off actually buys.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the InCircle Triple Points offer, or pair it with the dollar-off table in the hero so members see the double benefit at once. That's the benefit that actually moves me.
- 2. Drop "Exclusions Apply" from the hero. Put one short plain line there and link to the full terms.
- 3. Add a beauty tile or a row of fragrance and skincare, so a counter regular like me has a reason to click.
- 4. Cut the 15% text-signup block, or move it to a different send. It competes with the main offer.
- 5. Space out the sends. If you've mailed me the tier table, JUST4U, 15% off beauty and now this, give me a quiet week.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your InCircle Triple Points event starts today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $200, save $50. Spend $1,000, save $200. Code NMCIRCLE.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Triple Points on designer favorites, in stores and online through 10/12.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source has a few confirmed code-level problems: internal template comments leaked into the delivered HTML, a viewport meta tag that blocks zoom, and a missing `lang` attribute. Link, compliance and authentication checks are mostly unverifiable. The HTML is truncated, 33 tracking links were not probed, and the relay appears to have stripped the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable:** the QA run skipped HTTP probing of 33 tracking links ("Tracking/click-redirect domains — skipped HTTP probe"), so redirect health and final destinations are unconfirmed.
- The 62% pass rate with 0 issues mostly reflects skipped and unknown checks, not verified-good links.
- The truncated source shows no `href` values, so I can't assess link structure, broken anchors or missing alt-wrapped links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on supporting clients. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language declared:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation profile.
- **Brittle mobile selectors:** the media query uses exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching if any element carries a second class. Gmail has also historically been inconsistent with attribute selectors. I can't confirm the failure in the truncated markup.
- **Checkbox hamburger menu:** the nav depends on the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` hack inside `@media (max-width:600px)`. It only works in clients that support `:checked` (Apple Mail, iOS Mail and some others). I can't see the fallback markup, so whether the nav is reachable elsewhere is unverified.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the leaked comment block (see §4) comes first. Standards-mode rendering in legacy Outlook/IE-engine clients can be affected. This is low severity.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and isn't descriptive. This is minor.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template notes shipped in production HTML:** the source begins with `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) ... @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->` and `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. This exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`). It also shows an unresolved "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction, which suggests the template wasn't cleaned before send. The lookup syntax has an unbalanced parenthesis (`@email))`), but it sits in a comment and isn't executed.
- **No visible unrendered tokens:** no `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `@var` placeholders appear in the rendered portions I can see.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Warning, unconfirmed:** `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture them, so this may be a capture artifact. It can't be called a sender defect without the raw headers. If the headers really are absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements are not met.
- **Warning, unknown:** `Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
- **Not verifiable:** the truncated source doesn't show the in-body unsubscribe link or the physical postal address.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. The visible source has no destination URLs, and the 33 tracking links weren't resolved. The `@refid` / `@base_refid` variables in the leaked comment suggest campaign IDs are appended to links, but that's unconfirmed. If `@uEm` (a hashed email, per the SHA256 note) is appended to link URLs, it would put an identifier in query strings.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip all template and developer comments (the `REQUIRED variables` and `SET @user` blocks) at send time. This stops the DE schema leaking and keeps the DOCTYPE first.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Make the mobile CSS robust: use `[class~=…]` or plain class selectors, and confirm the nav has a fallback in clients that don't support `:checked`.
5. Capture the raw headers from the original send, not the relay copy. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are present and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with tracking redirects resolved. That would let you check final URLs, UTM or refid consistency, the unsubscribe link, the postal address, and whether hashed identifiers appear in URLs.
