# Time to treat yourself to up to 60% off top designer styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus doing what it does best — clean execution, prestige hero, no emoji chaos. The red "DESIGNER SALE / UP TO / 60% OFF" block is legible the second the email loads, and the brand grid underneath — Jacquemus, Chiara Boni La Petite Robe, Valentino Garavani, Chloe — is a genuinely accurate read on who I am. Someone pulled this product selection and knew exactly what a 47-year-old NM customer actually buys.
- My problem: this is a billboard, not a conversation. Two days ago, that "up to $200 off your next purchase" email felt like Neiman Marcus knew my account existed. This one? I'm a stranger again. No InCircle acknowledgment, no points reminder, nothing that signals I'm a known customer rather than recipient number 847,000 on a sale blast. And for a brand where I actually spend money, that gap stings a little more each time they do it.
- I'd tap through to women's shoes or the handbag section — the Valentino tile alone could do it. But I'm browsing, not buying. I wouldn't forward this; I'd forward the one with a specific product I actually want a friend's opinion on.

## What's working

- **The red hero is perfect hierarchy.** "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO / 60% OFF / SHOP SALE" — nothing to decode, nothing buried. Eye goes straight there.
- **Brand curation is legitimately right for me.** Chiara Boni La Petite Robe is practically designed for a 47-year-old who shows up to events. Stella McCartney, Chloe, Valentino Garavani — this isn't the Kohl's product grid, and I notice that.
- **Category buttons at the bottom are smart segmentation.** "SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES," "SHOP HANDBAGS," "SHOP DRESSES" get me to a fast lane without wading through a 400-item event page. That's genuine UX consideration.
- **"Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA.** I don't discover it the hard way at checkout. That's respecting my time.
- **Zero render issues.** Clean product tiles, readable typography, no broken images, no overlapping text. Renders like they care.

## What's weak

- **No InCircle mention anywhere.** I refuse to transact without points back — that's not negotiable. The June 22 "$200 off" email at least felt tied to something personal. This treats me like a stranger to the program.
- **No prices on product tiles.** I can see the Jacquemus bag and the Chloe sandals. I have no idea where they sit in the "up to 60% off" range. I shouldn't have to click blind to find out if something is actually in reach.
- **"Treat yourself" framing is weak for this brand.** That's Macy's language — they used it on the last "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email. NM should assume I've already decided to treat myself. The question is which piece. Frame the event, not the permission.
- **Cadence is becoming a problem.** This is roughly the ninth email since June 15. Sephora made their "chapter closing" email feel like an actual deadline worth acting on. At this volume, NM is training me to wait for the next send instead of acting on this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add InCircle framing to the hero or a banner above it.** Even one line — "InCircle members earn points on every sale item" — makes this feel targeted. That single change lifts both open and click for the customer segment actually worth retaining.
- 2. **Show starting prices on the product grid.** "From $285" under the Chiara Boni dress lets me pre-qualify from the email. Sephora shows original and sale pricing on every product tile in their sale sends; NM is behind on this.
- 3. **Give "before they're gone" a specific signal.** If the Valentino sandals are in 4 sizes, say 4 sizes. If the sale ends Sunday, say Sunday. Vague scarcity is the most ignored copy in retail email.
- 4. **Suppress this send for openers who didn't convert on June 22.** I saw the "$200 off" email. Sending me a blunter version of the same event two days later burns goodwill without adding new information.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Up to 60% off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) XHTML table-based email with AMPscript personalization. QA captured 4 warnings and no hard failures; primary concerns are an unresolvable `@user` variable, missing unsubscribe headers at the relay layer, and a plain-text body that is nearly all URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**QA finding:** 25 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped — HTTP probing was not performed, so broken destination URLs cannot be ruled out.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs** (5,225 of 6,001 chars). A plain-text part composed almost entirely of raw redirect URLs damages spam filter scoring and is unreadable in plain-text clients. This suggests the plain-text version was auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking wrappers.

No issues found with link count or structural href attributes from the truncated source alone, but the 25 unprobed redirects are a gap in QA coverage.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` tags** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are dead code. They indicate iterative debugging that was never cleaned up; they pose no active risk but are clutter that can confuse future template edits.

**Hamburger nav uses the CSS checkbox hack** (`navbar-checkbox:checked`) — this is functional in current iOS Mail but is not supported in Outlook or Gmail web, where the hamburger is silently hidden. Acceptable if fallback layout is verified, but worth noting as a client-compatibility assumption.

**`<title>` contains a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, no functional impact.

**Two separate `<style>` blocks** in `<head>` (one for general responsive rules, one for 3-product/2x2 grid breakpoints at `480px`/`520px`). Functional but non-standard; some older clients process only the first `<style>` block.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: `@user` assignment is commented out.**

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

The comment block also says `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`, implying `@user` is set via one of two conditional paths. If neither path is active in the deployed version, `@user` will be an empty string wherever it is interpolated into links (e.g., identity stitching or personalization query params). This cannot be confirmed from the truncated source alone, but the commented-out `SET` is a red flag that should be verified against the deployed template.

**Variables declared in comments only** — `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` are all listed in a header comment as required. If any lookup fails silently (e.g., `NM_CRM_Feed` returns no row for a given address), these default to empty string in SFMC AMPscript without raising a send-time error. No null-guard (`IIF(EMPTY(@uEm), ...)`) is visible in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Header | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` | **Not captured** | QA: "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay" |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **Not found** | QA: one-click unsubscribe may not be supported |
| `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM) | **Not found** | QA: "Expected via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM status unknown" |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are likely a relay capture artifact rather than a true send-time omission — SFMC appends these headers by default for commercial sends. However, this **must be verified against raw message headers** on a delivered copy. Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate both headers; absence at delivery would be a compliance violation.

SPF/DKIM status is unverifiable from this audit. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a SFMC-managed subdomain and is expected to have valid DKIM alignment, but the relay gap means this audit cannot confirm it.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 25 click-redirect links were skipped by the prober, so UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be verified programmatically. The `@refid` variable (constructed as `CONCAT(@base_refid, "")`) is the likely UTM campaign value appended to destination URLs — if `@base_refid` is empty or the `@user` hash resolves to blank, tracking attribution will be broken for those recipients.

No direct evidence of UTM stripping or redirect loops, but the unprobed links are a coverage gap.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P1 — Verify `@user` assignment is live in the deployed template.** The commented-out `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` line must not be the only path. Confirm the active scenario block executes before send. Test with a seeded address that exists in `NM_CRM_Feed`.

**P1 — Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` headers on a delivered message.** Pull raw headers from a test inbox delivery (not the relay copy). Yahoo/Gmail bulk sender compliance requires these; an audit gap here is not the same as a pass.

**P2 — Rebuild plain-text body.** 87% URL content will degrade spam filter scoring. Strip tracking wrapper URLs from the plain-text alternative and use readable destination text with bare domain references.

**P2 — Probe the 25 redirect links.** Re-run QA with redirect-following enabled or manually spot-check 5–6 destination URLs to confirm they resolve, carry UTM params, and land on the correct pages.

**P3 — Add AMPscript null-guards for CRM lookups.** Wrap `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, and `@emailid` in `IIF(EMPTY(...), "fallback", ...)` to prevent silent blank-string interpolation in link params.

**P3 — Remove commented-out viewport tags.** Three stale `<meta name="viewport">` variants in `<head>` are dead code; remove to reduce template maintenance risk.
