# Labor Day Sale + 15% off home is calling your name 📞
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's doing the thing Kohl's does best — burying me in category tiles until I forget what I opened the email for. It's LABOR DAY at the very top with a $10-off-$25 stacked on a $5-Kohl's-Cash-per-$25 offer, and then it just... keeps going. Women, Kids, Home, jeans, dresses, home decor, furniture, bras, handbags, clearance, a mattress topper, a Ninja blender — I counted at least ten separate category blocks before I hit the barcode coupons at the bottom.
- The home stuff is genuinely fine — that 15%-off-home coupon layered on top of a bath towel deal and a nightstand shot is the one moment that made me pause, since I do actually shop Kohl's for home basics when I'm not at the counter. But this isn't a "home" email despite what the subject promised me — "home is calling your name" gets one section out of fifteen, sandwiched between jeans and a Ninja blender ad. Compare that to yesterday's actual Martha Stewart home email, which at least had a real hook. This one just uses "home" as clickbait for a general clearance blowout.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I'm already annoyed at needing bath towels. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, it's just an inventory dump with a coupon at the end.

## What's working

- The dual offer at top ($10 off $25 + $5 Kohl's Cash per $25) is clear and I understand it in two seconds.
- The home section — nightstand, throws, towels — actually looks like a home email, briefly.
- Barcode coupons at the bottom are a nice touch for someone like me who still likes clipping/scanning in-store.
- "Picked with you in mind" — I've seen Kohl's use this before and it's still a cheap personalization gesture, but at least it's consistent with prior sends.

## What's weak

- The subject promised "home" and I got everything BUT home for most of the email — jeans, dresses, bras, handbags, a Ninja drink maker.
- Visual hierarchy collapses after the first two offers — it's just tile after tile with no clear "start here."
- The "20% off Bras for women" tile next to "40% off Handbags" feels like a random grab-bag, not a curated home sale.
- Clearance banner mid-email ("Save up to 85%") competes directly with the Labor Day offer for my attention — I don't know which deal is the deal.
- This is the same sprawling-department-store-circular problem I flagged in the 8/31 clearance email (6/10) — Kohl's still hasn't learned to pick a lane.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. If the subject says "home," lead with home — nightstand, towels, throws, decor — and push apparel/jeans/bras into a separate send. Neiman's cleaned up their palette after weeks of banner overload; Kohl's needs the same discipline.
- 2. Move the 15%-off-home coupon to the top, right under the Labor Day banner, since that's the actual hook in the subject line.
- 3. Drop either the Clearance block or the "Picked with you in mind" block — having both plus five category sales is overload, not choice.
- 4. Give me one hero home image (styled room, not just a nightstand on white) — Sephora's "point of view" launch failed by being too vague, but Kohl's home section fails the opposite way, too generic and catalog-like.
