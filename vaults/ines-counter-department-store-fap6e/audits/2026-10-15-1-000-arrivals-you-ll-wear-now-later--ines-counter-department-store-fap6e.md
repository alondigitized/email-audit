# 1,000+ arrivals you’ll wear now & later
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's I like. After three gift-card sends in three days, and the 10/14 "First Call: Up to 40% off" tomato-red block, a black panel that says "Brand New Styles Just Landed" with no dollar ladder felt like a salesperson who waited for me to finish my coffee. There's no offer and no countdown, so nobody is shouting at me.
- The catch is that it asks me to do all the work. Twelve products, no prices, no points, and no mention of the gift-card event I got pitched this morning. I do my beauty and designer shopping where I'm rewarded for it, and this email doesn't tell me whether I'd be rewarded here. It's a pleasant lookbook, but I wouldn't forward it.
- I'd click once, probably on the Jimmy Choo or the Polo Ralph Lauren dress, and then bounce if the page didn't show me anything about points.

## What's working

- The black hero with "Brand New Styles Just Landed" is calm and readable. It's much quieter than the tomato-red "First Call" block from 10/14.
- Named designers under every product (Issey Miyake, Jimmy Choo, TOM FORD, L'AGENCE) work as designer access for me. I scan for names, not product descriptions.
- The grid is clean and evenly spaced, with white space around each item. Nothing overlaps and no images are broken.
- Women's comes first, with its own "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" button, and men's is a separate block. That's easy to skip through.
- The footer has a personal style advisor link, which is the one service cue in the email.

## What's weak

- There's no price, offer, or points mention anywhere. I can't tell if this is worth my time before I click.
- Nothing links to the gift-card promo from this morning. The two emails feel like different companies.
- The hero's "Shop Women's" and "Shop Men's" links are small, thin, underlined text on black. They're hard to tap on a phone and look like an afterthought.
- Half the email is men's. It's a fair split for a general list, but it doesn't read as made for me, and I'm scrolling past six menswear products.
- There's no tier or loyalty signal at all, and I'm not sure I'm even recognized as a member.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one line of loyalty or offer copy under the hero, such as my points status or the gift-card progress, so the email ties to the week's campaign.
- 2. Make the hero CTAs real buttons. They're tiny underlined links now.
- 3. Add prices, or at least "From $X," under the designer names.
- 4. Use a women's-first or personalized sort for people who only click women's.
- 5. Pick one hero product instead of twelve equal tiles.

# Technical Audit: "1,000+ arrivals you'll wear now & later"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a checkbox-hamburger mobile nav. It leaves internal ESP template comments in production source, and it blocks pinch-zoom. The automated header warnings (4 warnings, 0 failures) come from the AgentMail relay and are not confirmed defects. The source was truncated, so body links, footer and images were not reviewed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 30 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so redirect chains, destinations and link health are unverified.
- The plain-text part is 5726 of 6548 characters (about 87%) raw URLs. This is a URL-heavy text alternative, probably the long tracked URLs printed in full. It can raise spam-filter scores and is poor to read in text-only clients.
- No broken or malformed links were found in the visible source. The truncation prevents checking the rest.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language declared:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Checkbox hamburger nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked` rules rely on `:checked` support. Gmail (web and app) strips this, so the menu is likely non-functional there. The `.contentInteractive{display:none !important}` rule hides the interactive content by default.
- **Fragile selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar use exact-match attribute selectors. They fail silently if an element carries more than one class. `*[class].show` also declares `width` twice (`auto !important`, then `278px`).
- **Legacy and global rules:** `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` are legacy Outlook.com hacks. The global `p { margin:0 auto; text-align:center }` can override inline alignment inherited from content blocks.
- **Minor:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space. The DTD URL uses `https://www.w3.org/...`, while the canonical system identifier is `http://`. This is harmless but non-standard.
- **Not verifiable:** image `alt` text and contrast, because the body was truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The opening comment block ships in the delivered HTML. It exposes internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), the lookup logic, and the unresolved instructions `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This is a template hygiene and information-disclosure issue, not a rendering break.
- The variable definitions contain unbalanced parentheses, for example `@user Lookup(...))`. This is inside a comment, so it has no effect on rendering.
- No unresolved visible tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) were seen in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` was not found. The QA note says the relay may not capture it, so this is **unconfirmed**.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) was not found, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it for senders of this volume, so it should be verified against the raw headers at the sending ESP.
- `Authentication-Results` was missing, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The `From:` domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a sending subdomain, so check its records directly.
- The visible source doesn't show the footer, so the body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not confirmed either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTM parameters and landing destinations couldn't be assessed. The 30 tracked links weren't resolved and the product links fall in the truncated section.
- The template comment references `@refid` / `@base_refid`, which suggests campaign IDs are carried in the URLs. That is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC template comment block (DE names, field names, scenario instructions) before send, for example with a build or pre-send lint step.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Add a fallback for the `:checked` hamburger nav in clients that strip it (Gmail). A visible static nav or a link to the site would work.
5. Verify the raw `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` headers, along with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, at the ESP or in a seed-inbox test. Don't rely on the relay output.
6. Use a shorter or cleaner plain-text alternative, for example by shortening tracked URLs or limiting link density.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and resolve the tracked links, so the footer, `alt` text, body unsubscribe link and UTM parameters can be checked.
