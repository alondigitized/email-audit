# Summer’s chicest tops
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Seven Neiman Marcus emails since June 15 — I had already counted before I even thought about tapping. After that "$200 off" send two days ago (the one that actually made me open, browse the Designer Sale, and almost pull the trigger on a Valentino piece), "Summer's chicest tops" slides into my inbox with pure editorial energy and zero offer language in the subject. That's a hard follow.
- The peachy hero is genuinely lovely — the color story is cohesive, the model is elegant, and "Peachy Keen" as a headline is the brand being charming rather than screaming at me. The three product cards land well because they name actual designers — Brunello Cucinelli and Dorothee Schumacher are not names I need translated. That's Neiman Marcus treating me like a customer, not a zip code.
- And then the email splits personality on me. Below that clean editorial moment sits the Designer Sale Private Event banner — "UP TO 40% OFF select designer styles" — slapped on like someone in the marketing meeting said "throw the sale in there too." Which is it? Aspirational curation or urgency sale? I'd close this email without clicking the tops section, maybe tap the sale banner if I hadn't already clicked through twice this week. No forward.

## What's working

- The peachy color story is cohesive and genuinely seasonal — the hero earns its real estate and doesn't feel like a stock photo grab
- Calling out Brunello Cucinelli and Dorothee Schumacher by name signals NM is talking to someone who already knows these designers, not introducing them
- "These chic tops deserve front-of-closet status" is editorial copy that sounds like the brand, not a promo alarm
- The Designer Sale Private Event banner delivers a concrete offer clearly — "UP TO 40% OFF," a "SHOP NOW" button, no fine print acrobatics required to find the number
- Clean render, zero visible bugs — at this send volume, a pixel-perfect execution is genuinely appreciated

## What's weak

- No InCircle mention, no points reminder anywhere — I'm a card-carrying NM customer and this email treats me like a first-time visitor who found the site via Google
- Two campaigns fighting in one scroll — the editorial tops and the designer sale are two separate stories, and Sephora's category sends feel sharper precisely because they commit to one story per email
- The strapless feathered/draped hero top reads more late-20s Instagram than 47-year-old professional who also shops that Brunello Cucinelli card below — the demographic signal is off for me specifically
- The 40% off offer is buried below the hero image and three product thumbnails — by the time I reach it I've already formed an opinion about the email
- Copyright notice reads "© 2024" at the footer — a small detail, but it's the kind of inattention that chips at the "we know you" positioning this brand tries to project

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story.** Either send the tops editorial or send the designer sale push — the split personality means neither gets full attention. If the sale is ending, lead with that and pull tops in as supporting product.
- 2. **InCircle it.** A single line — "Your InCircle points apply" or "Members earn double through Sunday" — is the difference between me clicking through to NM and opening the Nordstrom tab instead. Macy's at least flashes my Star status in their triggered sends; NM has the more premium program and doesn't bother.
- 3. **Move the offer into or near the hero.** If the Designer Sale is the commercial reason for this send, a "up to 40% off, including these styles" subhead under the peachy hero would let the email do double duty without the jarring context switch.
- 4. **Reconsider the hero styling for the core customer.** The strapless feathered/draped top is beautiful but it's an influencer shot. The Louboutin email from June 19 landed better on me because the styling felt like it belonged to someone my age.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with multiple unresolved variable conflicts and commented-out dead code indicating template drift. Compliance posture is weak: both `List-Unsubscribe` headers are absent, and SPF/DKIM authentication status is unverifiable via the relay.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part is 78.8% URLs (3,311 / 4,200 chars)**
QA flag: `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`. A machine-generated plain-text part consisting almost entirely of redirect URLs is a strong spam-filter signal. Gmail's bulk sender heuristics and SpamAssassin both penalize this pattern.

**35 tracking/redirect links skipped**
All click-tracking links passed through a redirect domain; destinations and UTM integrity could not be verified. This is a blind spot — broken destination links or missing UTM params would not surface in this audit.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating `@base_refid` with an empty string produces the same value. If a campaign suffix (e.g., a variant code) was intended, it was never populated. Any downstream reporting keyed on `@refid` will be indistinguishable from `@base_refid`.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple conflicting viewport meta tags (commented-out)**

```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
This is template drift — dead code that signals the template has been patched repeatedly without cleanup. No rendering defect today, but a maintenance liability.

**iOS 9 hamburger keyframe hack still present**
```css

@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```

Comment explicitly labels this "Hamburguer fix IOS9." iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2019. This is inert but contributes to CSS payload bloat.

**Two responsive breakpoints with overlapping selectors**
The template contains two separate `<style>` blocks with media queries at `max-width: 600px` (first block) and `max-width: 480px` / `max-width: 520px` (second block). The `*[class=...]` attribute selector pattern used throughout is a workaround for older Outlook/Gmail clients that strip `class`-based selectors — functional but fragile. No confirmed rendering defect, but specificity collisions are possible on edge clients.

If the active AMPscript block (outside the truncated HTML) uses the hashed variant without the lookup, `@user` will be a SHA-256 hash; if it uses the lookup, it will be a database EMAIL_ID. **`@emailid` and `@user` appear to be the same lookup**

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` variable assignments lack `SET` keyword in comment definitions**

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. RFC 2369 requires this for bulk senders. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender policy mandates `List-Unsubscribe` for senders of >5,000 msgs/day to Gmail/Yahoo addresses. Its absence increases the probability of Gmail placing this in the Spam folder or suppressing inbox delivery.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`. One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is required by the same Google/Yahoo 2024 policy. Without it, list-managed unsubscribes via the Gmail UI will not work, leading to higher spam complaint rates.

**Both headers may be present upstream but stripped by the AgentMail relay**
QA notes: "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay." This should be verified by inspecting raw headers directly from an inbox (e.g., Gmail "Show original"), not the relay capture. If NM's ESP (Salesforce MC) is injecting these headers correctly and the relay is stripping them, this is a monitoring gap, not a sending defect.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status for `e.neimanmarcus.com` is unknown in this capture. Same relay caveat applies — verify at the inbox level.

**Unsubscribe link in body**
CAN-SPAM requires a functional opt-out mechanism in the body. The source was truncated before the footer; presence of a working unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from the provided HTML.
