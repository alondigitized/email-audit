# Top-shelf beauty, right this way 👉🏽
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventeenth Sephora email I've had in about a week, and the subject only half matches what I'm looking at. "Top-shelf beauty" is a nice promise. But the real news is the black "Savings Event" banner at the top, and it says "COMING SOON." It's a 30% off badge for Sephora Collection, a 10% off badge for Insiders, and the dates are 11/3–11/9. So I'm being teased with a sale I can't use yet. I'd have been fine with it if the email just told me to come back on the 3rd.
- Below the banner is a big navy "TOP SHELF" hero with a Shop Now button, then a Jo Malone-style hair tool image and "Limited-Edition Shark Beauty." After that comes a product grid with Sol de Janeiro, Paula's Choice, Chris McMillan, Biossance, Jo Malone, Rihanna's Fenty and Givenchy. The brand mix isn't bad. But the hero and the shark-themed block compete with each other, and I can't tell which one is the story. Nordstrom would give me one clear lead.
- Would I click? Maybe for the Jo Malone cologne or the Givenchy. Would I forward it? No. I'd keep it as a reminder for 11/3, and only because I have to plan when to spend my points.

## What's working

- The "Coming Soon" savings banner is useful. It tells me the 30% and 10% dates (11/3–11/9), so I can hold off on a purchase.
- The product grid has recognizable names like Jo Malone London, Fenty Beauty and Givenchy. The "New + Only at Sephora" tags help me pick out what's exclusive.
- The Insider tier is called out right in the savings badge. That's clearer than Macy's, where I have to hunt for who gets what.
- The holiday gift guide strip at the bottom is a handy shortcut, and it's split into skincare, makeup, fragrance and hair.

## What's weak

- The hero says "Top Shelf" and "Shop Now," but the biggest offer is for next week. The page asks me to shop now while the promo says come back later, which is confusing.
- The savings banner has a "Build Your Basket" button, and it doesn't say what I'm building toward. It's a vague CTA on a sale that hasn't started.
- Too many sections. I counted the Top Shelf hero, the Shark Beauty block, Discover Your Next Indulgence, Spot It. Shop It., the Seth Rogen gift block, and the Gift Guide. That's the Macy's overload I notice, and Neiman Marcus's Fall Style Sale email was much cleaner about its single deadline.
- The Seth Rogen "Anyone can gift beauty" block is a random celebrity moment. It doesn't speak to me as a counter regular who buys at brand events.
- The fine print is a dense wall at the bottom. The offer exclusions are buried, and I can't tell if 30% applies to anything beyond Sephora Collection.
- This is the fourth send in a row with the same patchwork layout. Compare the "THIS JUST IN" email on 10/28 and "These gifts = 🌶️🌶️🌶️" on 10/26.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Lead with either the 11/3 savings event or the top-shelf product edit, not both. If the sale is the news, put the dates and tier percentages in the hero.
- 2. Change the "Build Your Basket" button to a concrete verb, like "Save my spot" or "Add to my Loves list."
- 3. Cut the Seth Rogen and shark blocks, and keep one gift section.
- 4. Move the exclusions out of the footer wall and put a one-line summary under the badges.
- 5. Space out the cadence. Seventeen emails in a week trains me to ignore you, while Kohl's at least repeated one clear offer.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Fixed width:** the outer wrapper is `<table width="700" … style="min-width:700px; width:700px">`. The `min-width` forces horizontal scroll or scaling on viewports narrower than 700px. The `@media (max-width:480px)` rules only adjust fonts and image sizes, and don't override the table width.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Malformed CSS:** an `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size {…} <![endif]-->` conditional comment sits inside the `@media` block in `<style>`. Outlook won't parse it as intended, and some clients may drop the rule that follows it.
- **Invalid robots value:** `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` should be `noindex`. This is harmless in email.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Sephora</title>` is generic. This is minor.
- **Structured data:** the JSON-LD block has `"discountCode": ""` and an empty `PromotionCard` type. Most clients ignore it, and Gmail may reject incomplete markup.
- **Plain-text part:** the QA check reports a text version of 0 chars. This may be a relay-capture artifact, but as captured the email is multipart-less for text-only clients and spam filters.
