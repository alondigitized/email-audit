# LAST CHANCE 🥺 Get up to 50% off select beauty—plus earn 500 bonus points with $75 min. spend.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- I saw this subject line and immediately thought: didn't I get this four days ago? The May 22 send was the same core offer — 50% off select beauty, 500 bonus points, $75 minimum — and it earned some goodwill from me because Sephora finally dropped the "Rosie" mess and just led with the deal. Now they've slapped "LAST CHANCE 🥺" on top of it and called it a new email. The pleading-face emoji is doing a lot of work that the offer itself shouldn't need. If the deal is real, it doesn't have to beg.
- Inside, there's actually a lot here that works for me. I don't buy without points, so "Stacking Weekend" is exactly my language — stack the discount, stack the Insider points, walk away feeling like I gamed it. The rhode limited-edition drop is current and interesting, the barcode at the bottom means I can flash this at a counter in-store, and for the first time in weeks there's no "Rosie" anywhere. The Beauty Insider visibility at the bottom confirms I'm a known member.
- But the email goes on forever. By the time I hit the Auto-Replenish pitch, the New Color Collection, the store locator, and the "We Belong to Something Beautiful" footer, I've completely lost the thread. Compare this to the clean, focused Neiman Marcus "new arrivals" structure — they stack too many emails, but each one has a clear job. This one is trying to do six jobs at once. I'll click, but only because my cart has been sitting since the 22nd and this email reminded me the clock is real tonight.

## What's working

- **The stacking mechanic is front and center.** "50% off + 500 bonus points" — that's exactly how I justify a big Sephora run to myself. Sephora knows points are the price of admission with me.
- **No "Rosie."** After three consecutive personalization misfires (the last being the May 20 "10% off ends soon, Rosie"), the absence of a wrong name is a genuine win. It reads like a message to a subscriber, not a merge-field mistake.
- **The rhode drop is timely.** Limited-edition Hailey Bieber collab showing up in a last-chance email makes the send feel alive. Nordstrom does this well — they anchor promotional emails in a current product moment. This does too.
- **The in-store barcode.** I actually shop brand events in person. Being able to use this at the counter is practical in a way most promo emails aren't.
- **Beauty Insider confirmation in the footer.** I can see my loyalty program reflected back at me. That's a small but real trust signal.

## What's weak

- **"LAST CHANCE 🥺" on a recycled campaign.** Sephora sent me essentially this same offer on May 22. The pleading emoji doesn't manufacture urgency that four days of delay already diluted. Kohl's has been pulling the same "LAST CHANCE" move eleven days running — I don't expect Sephora to do the same.
- **The subject line is trying to carry everything.** 90-plus characters, the full offer spelled out, the emoji, the urgency — on my phone this truncates badly and the 🥺 is the loudest signal that survives.
- **The email is extremely long.** Auto-Replenish in the middle, New Color Collection, store locator, footer loyalty section — by the fourth module I've stopped shopping and started scrolling to find the exit. Macy's packs in this much content too, but they at least anchor each section to a tighter CTA.
- **Generic "SHOP NOW" across multiple blocks.** I can't tell from a single button whether I'm landing on the sale page, the must-haves grid, or the homepage. Make it "Shop 50% Off Beauty" and I know where I'm going.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to the essentials.** The current subject tries to carry the entire offer. It doesn't need to. Let the preheader do half the work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 50% off + 500 bonus points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stacking Weekend ends at midnight — up to 50% off`
- 2. **Write a real preheader.** Right now the subject is so long it's eating into preheader real estate. The first text from the email body is probably leaking in.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Must-haves, the rhode drop, and your $75 stack — midnight deadline.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Beauty Insider points max out tonight. 50% off ends at midnight.`
- 3. **Pull one hero product into the subject or hero image.** A brand name — Charlotte Tilbury, Tatcha, Drunk Elephant — anchors the "select beauty" claim and makes 50% feel real, not hedged.
- 4. **Drop the 🥺.** If the offer expires tonight, that's urgency enough. The pleading emoji makes it feel like a Snapchat, not a Sephora.
- 5. **Move Auto-Replenish to a standalone send or footer module.** It breaks the shopping momentum mid-scroll, right when I've just been warmed up by the must-haves grid.
