# Get 20% off  + find home deals☀️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-six. That is where I am in my Kohl's count since Memorial Day, and this one lands mid-morning with what is now a completely predictable formula: 20% off with a coupon, earn Kohl's Cash, shop the same seasonal headers they have been rotating since spring. The subject teased home deals, and the email delivered — OUTDOOR and INDOOR headers dominate the scroll, with a coffee maker, some white bedding, and what looks like patio items all competing for my attention at once.
- The part that stings for me personally: I am a beauty person. I shop the Sephora counter inside Kohl's when I am already there for something else. But in an email this long, Beauty gets a tiny circular thumbnail icon so far down the page it is practically a footnote. The category grid near the bottom — Women, Men, Kids, Home, Beauty, Shoes — looks like a site nav, not a curated offer. That is not a beauty send. That is a clearinghouse.
- I will mark this read. I will not click. I will not forward it. If Sephora's own inbox-level targeting (their "Just Your Type" send earlier this week) set the bar, Kohl's is not even in the same sport.

## What's working

- **Kohl's Cash is front and center** — "Plus, earn Kohl's Cash" sits directly below the 20% hero, which is the one thing that makes me not unsubscribe entirely. Points back matter to me and they know it.
- **The 20% off offer is unambiguous** — no hunting for the number, no "up to" qualifier obscuring the headline. That is cleaner than Macy's "extra 25% off" confirmation chain from last week, which buried the mechanics.
- **OUTDOOR / INDOOR structural split** — I appreciate that they tried to organize the content. The section labels are bold and navigable, which is more discipline than I expected given how much product they crammed in.
- **No render bugs I can see** — the layout holds, images load, text isn't overlapping. Clean execution on a technically complex multi-section email.

## What's weak

- **Beauty is an afterthought** — the category gets one tiny circle icon in a six-item grid near the bottom. Neiman Marcus just sent me an entire email organized around showpiece bags. This one can't give me a single beauty callout above the fold?
- **This is the same email I opened yesterday, and the day before** — the 20% + Kohl's Cash formula has not changed in two months. At this point the "Summer picks" language reads like wallpaper. Cadence fatigue is real.
- **The "guests linger" headline is doing too much** — "Save on must-haves that make guests linger (Bonus: advance.)" — what does "advance" mean in parentheses? It reads like a truncated merge token or a placeholder that never got resolved. Confusing copy on the first section headline.
- **Six "Shop Now" buttons with no hierarchy** — every section gets the same grey Shop Now button. There is no primary CTA. Everything is equal priority, which means nothing is priority.
- **Home-only skew for what is supposed to be a multi-category store** — I shop Kohl's for more than throw pillows. The category grid at the bottom feels like an afterthought, not personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface a beauty offer above the fold** — even one beauty product (a skincare brand, a fragrance, the Sephora-at-Kohl's angle) in the hero or an early secondary block would make this relevant to me. Sephora does persona-led targeting; Kohl's is leaving that customer on the table.
- 2. **Kill three of the six Shop Now buttons** — pick one primary destination this send. A coffee maker OR summer bedding OR patio — not all three. One clear offer, one clear CTA.
- 3. **Fix the "(Bonus: advance.)" truncation** — whatever that is, it is broken copy. Cut it or finish the sentence.
- 4. **Rotate the formula** — the 20% + Kohl's Cash structure has earned a rest. Even a "Member Exclusive Early Access" framing (like Nordstrom does before its Anniversary Sale) would feel like news.
- 5. **Add a deadline** — at minimum a "through Sunday" line so the coupon feels time-bounded and worth acting on now rather than later (or never).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Kohl's Cash window: 20% off ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Members get 20% off + Kohl's Cash — use it this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop home, beauty, and more before the offer expires.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 you spend — this weekend only.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Get 20% off + find home deals" (2026-07-15)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard HTML 4.01 Transitional table-based layout with multi-vendor tracking infrastructure (CoherentPath, Message ID, Adobe Audience Manager/Demdex). No critical rendering blockers found, but several accessibility and compliance gaps are confirmed by the QA scan.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped** — all CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` (CoherentPath) and `click.s.kohls.com`, which return redirects. Link destinations and HTTP status codes could not be verified by the probe.

**Three separate open-tracking pixels** are present in the `<body>`:
- `https://click.chp.kohls.com/o/8c6983dc-...` (CoherentPath open pixel)
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (MessageID open pixel)
- `https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?YKC3...` (a third open tracker, domain `s.kohls.com`)

Three redundant open-tracking calls is abnormal and suggests uncoordinated ESP/CDP stacking. This increases page weight and complicates attribution deduplication.

**Invalid HTML around pixel 3:** The open pixel div closes with `</custom>` instead of `</div>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
This is a malformed closing tag. Most email clients will recover, but it is a markup error that could cause rendering issues in stricter parsers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA scan. Affected images include content images (the `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` series) that appear to be personalized/dynamic product images, not just tracking pixels. Screen readers will skip these entirely. The three tracking pixel `<img>` tags (which should have `alt=""`) also lack it — they will generate screen reader noise in some clients.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** disables pinch-to-zoom:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is an accessibility violation for users with low vision.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` provides no context for assistive technologies or email client tab labels.

**Google Fonts loaded three times** — via two `<link>` tags and a duplicate `@import` inside `<style>`. Only one method is needed; the redundancy adds latency in clients that honor external CSS.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No unresolved merge tokens detected** in the truncated source. Dynamic content appears to be pre-rendered server-side via the `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` image URLs, which carry `mi_u=604230016` (user ID) and `mi_ecmp=1017626_2026715` (campaign ID) as query params — consistent with MessageID's personalized image rendering approach.

One image URL contains a blank token: `mi_kc_redm_end_dte=` and `mi_kc_event_pe` with no value — these appear to be optional suppression/event parameters that are conditionally populated. If these control conditional content and are blank unintentionally, it warrants a check against the template logic.

---

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM — physical address not detected.** The QA scan found no mailing address in the HTML excerpt provided. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. Either it is present below the truncation point or it is missing — the ESP delivery team should confirm.

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** from captured headers. RFC 2369 compliance requires `List-Unsubscribe` at the SMTP/header level. Its absence may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not forwarding headers, but it cannot be confirmed without raw SMTP headers.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent** (RFC 8058). Gmail and Apple Mail enforce one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders; missing this header can trigger inbox suppression or force Gmail to synthesize its own unsubscribe link.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is present. This suppresses the HTTP Referer header on outbound clicks, which will break any referrer-based analytics on Kohl's landing pages that are not covered by UTM params.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />`** — correct for email; prevents bot indexing if the email is web-viewed.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Authentication-Results header not captured** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. If DKIM is failing, link click-through rates will be suppressed by spam filters before the UTM question is even relevant.

**UTM parameter coverage cannot be confirmed** because all 22 CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` redirects, which were not probed. CoherentPath typically appends UTM params at redirect time rather than in the raw HTML source. If the redirect chain is broken or misconfigured, sessions will land on kohls.com without campaign attribution — undetectable until GA4 source/medium reports show `(direct)` spikes on the send date.

**`no-referrer` meta (see §5)** compounds this: if UTM params are ever missing from a redirect, the referrer fallback is also suppressed, leaving the session completely unattributed.
