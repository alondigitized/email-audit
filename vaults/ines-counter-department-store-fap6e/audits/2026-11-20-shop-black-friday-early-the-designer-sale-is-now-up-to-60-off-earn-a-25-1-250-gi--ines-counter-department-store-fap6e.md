# Shop Black Friday early: The Designer Sale is now up to 60% off + Earn a $25–$1,250 gift card (includes beauty & sale styles)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- Three offers are stacked in one black box with a red border: "up to 60% off" on the Designer Sale, a "$25–$1,250 gift card," and "up to 30% off select regular-price styles." That's three different numbers fighting for the same spot. I read it twice and still couldn't say what I'd actually get. It's loud and boxy, and it's the opposite of the quiet Gorski and Dolce&Gabbana emails I said I'd keep on 11/18 and 11/19.
- The product grid underneath is nice. Parfums de Marly, a Dolce&Gabbana bag, Jimmy Choo boots, Chloe. But nothing has a price, a percent or a "was/now." It's a pretty lineup with no reason to click on any one item. The fine print is the bigger problem. It's a wall of text, and the gift card tiers ($100 gets you a $25 card, up to $5,000+ for $1,250) only appear down there. The hero never tells me what I'd have to spend.
- I'd open it, skim it, and probably not click. I wouldn't forward it. I'd come back only if I were already planning a Chloe bag purchase and wanted to check the gift card math.

## What's working

- The black, red and white hero is bold, and the "Designer Sale" block is the first thing my eye lands on.
- The designer grid has real names (Chloe, Tom Ford, Jimmy Choo, McQueen). That's the access I shop Neiman's for.
- "Includes beauty & sale styles" matters to me. Beauty is usually excluded, so seeing it called out is a plus.
- The category buttons (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Beauty) are large, easy to tap on a phone and clearly labeled.
- Personal style advisor and store-finder links at the bottom feel like counter service.

## What's weak

- Three offers in one hero is too much. Sephora Insider's Dyson email at least led with one idea, even if it didn't deliver on it.
- The gift card range "$25–$1,250" tells me nothing until I decode the spend tiers in the fine print.
- "Limited time only. Exclusions apply." sits in tiny gray type under the button. That reads like hiding something, not like a counter associate being straight with me.
- Product tiles have no pricing or discount, so I can't tell what's in the sale. Macy's at least puts a percentage on the page.
- This is the same Designer Sale push I've been getting all week. I'm well past tired of it.
- The disclaimer block is about a third of the email, in small type that's hard to read on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer, the Designer Sale, and make the gift card a clearly secondary strip. Put the tiers right there: "Spend $250, get $50."
- 2. Put sale prices or "up to X% off" tags on the product tiles.
- 3. Move the deadline, 11/22 at 10 PM CT, into the hero as a plain line instead of burying it in the fine print.
- 4. Cut the fine print and link to full terms.
- 5. Space out the sends. Six in four days teaches me to ignore them.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a hamburger-nav CSS hack and responsive media queries. It has three confirmed code-level issues: leaked ESP/AMPscript scaffolding, no `lang` attribute, and a viewport that disables zoom. Header-level compliance and authentication could not be verified, because the relay did not capture those headers. The source was truncated, so body links and footer markup were not inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **29 tracking links not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and UTM handling are unverified.
- **Identifiers likely appended to URLs.** The leaked comment lists `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and `@user` (with the option `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`). These are probably appended to links, but no link markup is visible in the truncated source, so this is unconfirmed.
- No broken links are confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `lang`.** The root element is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers can't select the correct language profile.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Fragile responsive selectors.** The rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching if an element carries more than one class, and clients that rewrite class names will break them. Legacy `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` hacks are also present.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav.** The CSS uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. This pattern isn't keyboard-accessible, and Gmail and most non-WebKit clients strip or ignore it. The nav markup wasn't in the truncated source, so whether a non-CSS fallback exists is unconfirmed.
- **Weak `<title>`.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has trailing whitespace.
- **Stale commented-out viewport variants** (`width=1024`, `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`) add dead weight.
- The inspected portion shows no other defects. Image `alt` text and `role="presentation"` on layout tables can't be assessed from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template scaffolding left in the delivered HTML.** The first node is a comment: `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE) @base_refid … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …) … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`.
  - It shows the build instructions weren't stripped.
  - It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It sits before the `<!DOCTYPE>`, which can trigger quirks-mode behavior in some legacy renderers.
- No unresolved merge tokens were found in rendered content within the visible portion. The only raw tokens are inside the comment.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Headers not verified.** The QA tool reported no `List-Unsubscribe`, no `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) and no `Authentication-Results`.
  - The tool itself attributes these to AgentMail relay capture, so they are inconclusive rather than confirmed sender defects.
  - They account for the 62% pass rate.
- The unsubscribe link and physical address live in the footer, which was truncated. CAN-SPAM body requirements are unverified.
- The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment can't be assessed without the original headers.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. No landing URLs were in the visible source, and all 29 tracked links were skipped by the QA tool. UTM presence, parameter consistency and landing-page matching for the Designer Sale and gift-card offer are all unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the AMPscript/variable documentation comment (and the commented-out viewport tags) in the production render. Move that documentation into the template repo.
2. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang="en"`) to `<html>`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Switch responsive selectors to `[class~=...]` or plain `.class` selectors, and confirm a non-CSS-dependent nav fallback for Gmail and Outlook.
5. Re-run QA against raw `.eml` source or a seed inbox that preserves headers. That would verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC).
6. Resolve a sample of the 29 tracking links, following redirects to the final URL, to check status codes, UTM parameters and landing-page alignment.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to cover the footer, `alt` attributes and link markup.
