# Fashion Finds: Matching Sets
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- No offer, no price, no percentage sign anywhere on this thing — and my first reaction is "wait, is this even a promotional email or did I get an editorial mailer by mistake?" That model in the rust-orange slip dress on a rocking chair is genuinely gorgeous, editorial-quality, feels like a Net-a-Porter lookbook page — but I'm 47, I don't have unlimited time to admire lighting, I want to know what this costs before I click anything.
- This is the same move Neiman Marcus pulled on 2026-07-30 with "The New Edit: 1,000+ new designer styles" — pretty grid, zero pricing, straight-up lookbook energy — except this time they at least swapped the black-and-white slab for a moody outdoor shoot. Compare that to the very next day, "Today only: 15% off in the Neiman Marcus app," where they at least had a number to hang my attention on. This "Fashion Finds" send has none of that. Sephora's "FREE foundation and concealer sample set" send from around the same window put a concrete, honest offer front and center with zero scavenger hunt — this email doesn't even try.
- Would I click? Only out of curiosity about the linen striped set from A.L.C., and only because I already trust the brand. Forward to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, it's just a nice photo. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The hero image is beautifully shot — soft natural light, relaxed pose, feels aspirational without being try-hard.
- "Matching Sets" as a trend call-out is smart for late July — coordinated separates are genuinely what I'd be shopping for right now for end-of-summer dinners.
- Clean typographic hierarchy up top — "FASHION FINDS" in that serif slab reads immediately as Neiman Marcus's editorial voice, consistent with their brand.
- Product grid is tidy and scannable — six items, designer names underneath (Cinq à Sept, L'Agence, A.L.C.), no clutter.
- Two clear CTA buttons right under the hero ("Shop Matching Sets," "Shop L'Agence") so I'm not hunting for where to click.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere. Not on the hero, not under a single product tile. I have no idea if these matching sets are $200 or $2,000.
- No loyalty/InCircle callout at all — I'm supposedly a valued customer and there's nothing here acknowledging tier status or points, unlike the personal style advisor nudge buried way down at the bottom which at least gestures at "known customer" but arrives too late.
- This is the second lookbook-style, no-offer send from Neiman Marcus in two days (following "The New Edit" on 7/30) — that's a cadence problem, not a one-off.
- The bottom third of the email is just a wall of utility links (Shop Contemporary, Shop Clothing, Shop Shoes, Shop Handbags, Shop Men's, Shop Kids') that has nothing to do with "matching sets" — feels like padding to hit a template length.
- Subject line "Fashion Finds: Matching Sets" reads like a magazine headline, not something from a store I actually shop at for a reason (price, event, sale).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a price or "from $X" under at least the hero look.** This is the single biggest lift possible — I can't evaluate desire without a number, and right now this reads like window shopping, not a targeted send.
- 2. **Cut the bottom department-link wall.** If I wanted to browse Kids' or Men's I'd go to the site — this dilutes the matching-sets theme the whole email is built around.
- 3. **Add an InCircle callout or points-earning line** near the CTA buttons — I don't use a card without points back, and Neiman Marcus knows that about its own program, so surface it.
- 4. **Trim the grid to 4 items and give me one "Shop the full edit" link** — six near-identical crops without prices is more scroll than payoff.
- 5. **A/B test subject lines that actually promise something concrete**, since "Fashion Finds" alone gives me zero reason to open over the dozen other lookbooks in my inbox.
