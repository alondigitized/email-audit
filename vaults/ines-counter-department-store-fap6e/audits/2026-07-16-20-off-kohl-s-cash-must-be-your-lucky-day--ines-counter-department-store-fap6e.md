# 20% off + Kohl's Cash?  Must be your lucky day 🤑
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Here we are again. Email fifty-seven from Kohl's since Memorial Day — I've been counting — and it arrives with the exact same formula I've opened since before the Fourth of July: 20% off, Kohl's Cash, summer hero imagery, a scattered product grid. The "lucky day" framing in the subject line even carries the same 🤑 emoji they ran yesterday, which tells me nobody in that marketing department is reading their own sent folder.
- That said, Kohl's Cash is real money to me and I won't pretend otherwise. The Beauty Insider callout in the lower third is the one thing here that almost earns my click — 10% off a $100+ beauty purchase stacks nicely if I'm already restocking Neutrogena or a Tarte foundation. But they buried it below a lifestyle photo, a full clearance banner, and a five-category nav. By the time I scroll to the beauty section, I've mentally already left.
- I'd mark this read. I would not forward it. If I'm already near a Kohl's, I might screenshot the barcode at the bottom — but this email does not make me drive there.

## What's working

- The 20% off hero banner is the first thing my eye hits — red background, clean percentage, dates visible without squinting. No hunting for the number.
- Kohl's Cash is called out right there in the hero, which is the one loyalty mechanic that keeps me subscribed despite the daily volume.
- The "Light and breezy season" theme is seasonally coherent — the color palette, the linen-adjacent fabrics, the copy all read as July.
- The Beauty Insider 10% off $100+ block is a real, specific incentive — not a vague "save big" promise. That specificity is exactly what Sephora does well, and I was glad to see it here at all.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, the barcode at the bottom is crisp and scannable.

## What's weak

- The 🤑 emoji ran yesterday (July 15, "Save 20% + get Kohl's Cash to top it off"). Running the same emoji two days in a row is not a strategy, it's an oversight. Sephora's "Just Your Type" campaign from earlier this month at least changed the creative between sends; Kohl's is copy-pasting the same skeleton.
- The hero headline is "Light and breezy season," but the anchor products shown are a man's floral pink shirt and a pair of olive wide-leg trousers — not what I came here for. This email doesn't know what it's selling or to whom.
- Beauty is buried below apparel, clearance, and a full category navigation before I reach the one offer that actually matches my shopping identity. Macy's, for all its "LAST CHANCE" urgency theater, at least puts the beauty angle in the subject line when targeting me.
- The "UP TO 70%" clearance banner mid-email completely breaks the "light and breezy" tone. It reads like two briefs collided. Clearance energy and summer-refresh energy do not mix.
- "Must be your lucky day" has casino slot-machine copy, not loyalty-program warmth. I don't want to feel like I hit a jackpot. I want to feel like a preferred customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the beauty offer.** Move the Beauty Insider 10% off block immediately below the 20% off banner — or make it the hero entirely on a beauty-segmented send. Neiman Marcus pivoted their entire creative to a single category (bags) in their last send; Kohl's can at least bump beauty above clearance in the hierarchy.
- 2. **Retire the 🤑 emoji for at least a week.** Swap in something that signals value without the lottery-ticket connotation. Cadence fatigue compounds when the emoji is identical send-over-send.
- 3. **Segment the list.** Apparel buyers and beauty buyers are not in the same mindset. A targeted beauty send — subject line leading with the 10% off, hero image featuring a product shelf or model in a beauty moment — would lift conversion on both splits.
- 4. **Quantify the Kohl's Cash earn.** "Earn $X per $Y spent, redeemable [start]–[end]" converts better than a vague "Plus earn Kohl's Cash!" I want to know exactly what I'm accumulating.
- 5. **Move clearance to the footer.** Secondary offer, secondary position — don't let it cannibalize the summer-refresh narrative at mid-scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Kohl's Cash window is open — plus 20% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Beauty Insider: 10% off $100+ (and 20% off the rest)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn Kohl's Cash through Sunday. Stack it on top of 20% off — don't leave it sitting.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `10% off your next beauty haul and 20% off everything else. Both, today.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "20% off + Kohl's Cash" (2026-07-16)

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard promotional ESP send using Coherent Path (chp.kohls.com) for click-tracking and MessageGears (mi.kohls.com) for pixel/open tracking. No structural HTML errors detected, but three compliance gaps and a pervasive alt-text omission pattern warrant attention.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped by HTTP probe** — all routed through `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears) and `click.chp.kohls.com` (Coherent Path). Expected behavior; no broken link signal, but destination URLs cannot be verified without live probe.

**Three distinct tracking pixel stacks active simultaneously:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — Coherent Path open pixel (1×1)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/.../o.gif` — MessageGears open pixel (1×1)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager segment sync (0×0)

No direct issue, but the Demdex pixel (`d_sid=13245196`) fires a third-party cross-site tracking request on open. Under strict CSP or privacy-hardened clients this may be silently blocked without fallback.

**`<custom>` tag on line:**
```html
</custom>
```
Orphaned closing tag immediately after the open-pixel `<img>`. Not valid HTML 4.01 Transitional (the declared DOCTYPE). Benign in practice but signals template assembly artifact.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on all tracking/pixel images** — 11 flagged by QA:

| Image | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `fc29b776...` (chp.kohls.com) | Coherent Path open pixel |
| `o.gif` (mi.kohls.com) | MessageGears open pixel |
| `event` (kohls.demdex.net) | AAM segment pixel |
| `2a5adc5a...`, `f441d45d...`, `c2f81e9a...`, `415c8a18...` | Content render-proof images with `email_name=260716_DG_Email_Thursda` |
| `69494b48...`, `78308d1d...`, `916d6fa0...` | Additional mi.kohls.com assets |

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly (currently absent) to satisfy screen readers and WCAG 2.1. The content images (`email_name=260716_DG_Email_Thursda`) are missing descriptive alt text, which degrades the experience when images are blocked.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-zoom on mobile:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Common in legacy email templates but worth flagging.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>`. Screen readers announce the window title; leaving it blank is an accessibility gap and forces clients to display the raw subject line or nothing.

**Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import`** for Roboto and Montserrat — redundant double-fetch. The `<link>` tags are already inside the `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` guard, then `@import` repeats them in the same block. This doubles font requests on non-Outlook clients.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected** — QA relay did not capture this header. CAN-SPAM does not mandate the header, but Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements require it for senders above 5K/day. Kohl's volume almost certainly exceeds this threshold. Absence could trigger inbox demotion.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not detected** — One-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. If this header is absent at the MTA level (not merely undetected by the relay), this is a deliverability compliance gap.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (subdomain). Cannot confirm alignment without raw headers. Note: the `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` tag is correctly set, which prevents link click referrer leakage.

**Physical address** — not visible in truncated source; must appear per CAN-SPAM §7(a). Assumed present in the footer (below truncation point) — cannot confirm from provided source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters cannot be verified** — all 22 outbound links route through `click.s.kohls.com` redirects. UTM attribution depends entirely on what the ESP appends post-redirect. No UTM parameters are visible in the raw HTML href values, which is expected for click-tracked ESP links but means there's no fallback if the redirect chain breaks.

**`email_name=260716_DG_Email_Thursda`** (truncated at 30 chars) appears in multiple render-proof pixel URLs. This is an internal campaign tag, not a UTM param, but its truncation suggests a field length issue in the pixel assembly template.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** is present — correct, prevents email mirrors from being indexed.
