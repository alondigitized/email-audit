## Executive summary

- Pretty, quiet, and no offer. After four days of Designer Sale shouting ("up to 50% off", "40% off" with codes, "$650 off") and this morning's "Buy More, Earn More" gift card send, I'll take the calm. But it's a catalog. It tells me gifts exist and gives me no reason to act today.
- The first thing I see is a man in a tuxedo next to a convertible. I'm a 47-year-old woman, and the first screen isn't for me. The "Her" half sits far below the fold. ## What's working

- The black wordmark, thin nav, and cream and burgundy headline block look like Neiman's. It reads as restrained, not pushy.
- The red dress and shopping bags photo is the best image in the email. It's the gift story in one frame.
- Named designers under each product (Zegna, Kiton, Christian Louboutin, Lyma, Jimmy Choo, Acqua di Parma) tell me this is real designer access.
- The Lyma and Acqua di Parma tiles are the only beauty and fragrance cues. They're the closest thing to my category.
- Nothing is shouted. After Neiman's own run of sale emails, this is a relief.

## What's weak

- There's no offer. I see no percent, no dollar amount, no shipping promise, no deadline. "Wrap up their wishlists" is a mood, not a reason.
- The hero is a man, then a section called "Gifts for Him" first. The women's gifts only start about halfway down.
- Nothing mentions InCircle, points, or gift wrap. I won't spend on a card without points back, and this email never says whether I'd earn any.
- The links are small underlined text ("Shop Gifts for Him", "Shop Holiday Decor"). They're hard to tap on a phone and don't look like buttons.
- The products are tiny and unpriced, and none of them link anywhere specific that I can see. The fragrance and lip color are barely legible.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one real reason to buy above the fold: free gift wrap and shipping, or a points event. Neiman's already ran a spend-and-get gift card send this morning, so tie this email to it or drop it.
- 2. Lead with gifts for her, or split the hero in two. Right now I have to scroll past the tuxedo to find my section.
- 3. Add prices, and make each tile a link to that product. Show Lyma and Acqua di Parma bigger and add a beauty gift row.
- 4. Turn the underlined text into real buttons: "Shop gifts for her", "Shop gifts under $100".
- 5. Add a visible ship-by date for holiday delivery. That's the credible urgency a gift shopper responds to.

# Technical Audit: "Make holiday dreams come true" (Neiman Marcus)

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a standard XHTML table-based template from Salesforce Marketing Cloud. It has an exposed internal template comment, a zoom-blocking viewport tag, and no `lang` attribute. The HTML source is truncated and the QA headers were not captured, so the compliance, link and UTM checks are unverified rather than passed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- The QA run found 17 tracking/click-redirect links and skipped the HTTP probe on all of them. Their status codes, redirect chains and final destinations are **unverified**.
- The plain-text part is 3199/3706 characters (~86%) URLs. This is the QA warning. It is most likely long tracking URLs inflating the text part, which is a spam-score risk. Shortening the redirect URLs or adding more descriptive plain-text copy would help.
- No broken links are confirmed in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Exposed template internals:** The delivered HTML begins with a comment containing ESP build notes. It names the Data Extensions `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`. It also names the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm` and `cmdid`, and the variables `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid` and `@cmdid`. It also contains the line `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This is a data-schema disclosure to every recipient. These comments should be stripped at send time.
- **Comment before the DOCTYPE:** The comment sits ahead of `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN"...>`. Content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some older clients.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on some mobile clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`, so screen readers cannot pick the right language.
- **Generic `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and does not describe the email.
- **Mobile selectors:** The mobile rules use exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`. They stop matching if an element gets more than one class. Nothing in the visible source shows this happening.
- **Hamburger nav:** The checkbox-hack nav (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`) relies on CSS support that is partial in Gmail and Outlook. Fallback behavior cannot be verified from the truncated source.
- **Images:** Alt text and image dimensions are not visible in the truncated source, so they are unverified.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unrendered merge tokens are visible in the rendered portion. The only variable syntax found is inside the HTML comment above.
- The rest of the body was truncated, so token resolution is unverified.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **QA WARN:** The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not found.
- **QA WARN:** The `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058 one-click) was not found.
- **QA WARN:** The `Authentication-Results` header was not found, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown.
- QA notes that the AgentMail relay may strip these headers. They are therefore **not confirmed missing**. They should be checked against the raw original message.
- The unsubscribe link and physical address are not visible in the truncated source, so they are unverified.
- The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain, which is normal for ESP sending.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The destinations of all 17 links are masked by tracking redirects. UTM parameters and landing-page alignment cannot be assessed from the available data.
- The template does carry a `@refid`/`@base_refid` campaign-reference convention in its comment. The resulting query parameters are not visible.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the leading ESP comment block, which exposes DE names, field names and variable logic, from the production send.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and improve the `<title>` text.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` in the raw message headers. If they are missing at the origin, add them, since Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
5. Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment from the original message, since the relay hid `Authentication-Results`.
6. Resolve the 17 tracking redirects with a following HTTP probe, and check for 200 status, no redirect loops, and UTM/`refid` persistence on the landing pages.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to cover the footer, alt text, unsubscribe link and merge tokens.
