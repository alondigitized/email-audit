# Summer's in full swing: 1,000+ new arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five emails in. And after four consecutive days of LAST CALL clearance and 70%-off subject lines — plus a same-day "just for you: 25% off one item" that actually landed well this morning — Neiman Marcus is now sending me a completely separate new-arrivals blast. Same day. That's two from NM today, and my first instinct is to check if my inbox filter is broken.
- That said, I have to give them credit for the pivot: this email is calm. Black hero, white type, clean product grid. No SCREAMING. After all the urgency theater from the past week, "Brand New Styles Just Landed" almost feels luxurious by contrast. The women's brand lineup is genuinely good — Louboutin, Chiara Boni, Isabel Marant, Givenchy, STAUD, Alice + Olivia — these are the brands I'd stop at the counter for. That's doing a lot of work.
- But half this email is men's clothing. Half. I don't shop men's at Neiman's. And there's not a single mention of InCircle, no pricing, no styling context, no reason to click now versus next week. Compare this to Sephora's "bag edit" send — they led with Charlotte Tilbury, gave it editorial weight, and made me feel like someone curated it specifically for my habits. This NM email is a product catalog formatted as an email. I'll note the Louboutins for my next in-store trip, and I'll mark this as read.

## What's working

- **The brand roster is legitimately strong for my demo.** Louboutin, Isabel Marant, Givenchy, Chiara Boni — this is my language. They got the merchandise right.
- **The hero is clean and confident.** Black background, white serif type, no promotional clutter. After the clearance-blast visual noise this week, this reads like someone hit reset.
- **Cadence pivot registers as deliberate.** Switching from LAST CALL urgency to a clean new-arrivals editorial feels intentional — I notice it, and it earns a little goodwill.
- **"Shop Women's" and "Shop Men's" CTA split is logically clear.** At least they acknowledged there are two audiences.

## What's weak

- **Half the email is men's.** I'm a 47-year-old woman who shops beauty and women's RTW. Burberry plaid shirts and Amiri sneakers are dead weight to me.
- **Zero InCircle signal anywhere.** I refuse to use a card without points back, and NM has my card on file. Not one mention of InCircle, member-early-access, or points — a glaring miss for the brand's highest-value customers.
- **No editorial voice.** Twelve product images, twelve brand names, two buttons. No "why these, why now." Sephora's bag edit explained why each pick mattered this season. This is just a grid.
- **Same-day send after the "25% off one item" email.** One Neiman Marcus email today was already enough. Two in one day signals poor list coordination — and the personalized discount email was the better one to stand on.
- **No pricing, no product details, no urgency.** Just arrived, but there's no reason to act. "Quantities limited"? "Pre-sell before it's gone"? Nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the men's section entirely, or segment.** This should be a clean women's new-arrivals send for me. Men's belongs in a separate email or at the very bottom with minimal real estate. Cutting it in half instantly makes the email feel more targeted.
- 2. **Lead with a specific hero arrival, not a generic banner.** "Christian Louboutin just landed" would have me clicking before I even read the rest. The 1,000+ number is overwhelming; a single marquee name is seductive.
- 3. **Add the InCircle hook.** Even a one-line footer callout: "InCircle members get early access to new arrivals" or "Earn points on everything that just landed." It costs nothing to say and means everything to the cardholder.
- 4. **Give the product grid some editorial scaffolding.** One sentence per brand grouping: "Back in stock in time for August travel" or "The bag edit Neiman's stylists are carrying right now." The Sephora model works because it explains intent — steal it.
- 5. **Coordinate send cadence with the team.** A "25% off one item" personal discount and a new-arrivals blast on the same day from the same brand is a coordination failure. The discount email performs better; let it breathe.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer's in full swing: 1,000+ new arrivals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) build with standard table-based layout and responsive CSS. No critical blockers identified, but four QA warnings — covering one-click unsubscribe compliance, deliverability authentication, and tracking link opacity — warrant attention before the next send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped (HTTP probe not executed)**
All click-redirect links were skipped by the AgentMail relay during QA, meaning destination URLs and redirect chain integrity were not validated. Any broken or misconfigured click-redirect would be invisible in this run.

**Plain-text body is 94% URLs**
`5,289 / 6,126` characters in the plain-text alternative are URLs. This is a deliverability red flag — spam filters (particularly Gmail and Proofpoint) penalize plain-text parts that are nearly all links with no readable content. The plain-text version should mirror the prose content of the HTML, not be auto-generated from raw URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport meta tags**
Three deprecated `<meta name="viewport">` variants are present as HTML comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but represent dead code accumulation. No functional issue, but they should be removed to reduce template maintenance risk.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor — some preview pane renderers surface the `<title>` value. Strip the trailing space.

**Duplicate media query breakpoints**
Responsive rules are split across two `<style>` blocks: one at `max-width: 600px` and one at `max-device-width: 480px / 520px`. This is intentional (three-up product grid behavior), but the two-block pattern increases parse order sensitivity in older clients (Outlook Android, Samsung Mail). No confirmed rendering breakage, but consolidation reduces risk.

**No `alt` text evidence in truncated source**
The source was truncated before image tags were visible. Alt text coverage on product images cannot be confirmed from the provided excerpt — requires full source review.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable comment left exposed**
The template opens with a multi-line AMPscript variable declaration block in an HTML comment:
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated" ...) @uEm ... @cmdid ... -->
```
This comment is rendered verbatim in the HTML source delivered to the inbox. It leaks internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`), and lookup logic to any recipient who views source. It should be removed from the sent template — keep it only in the template editor as a documentation block if needed.

**Commented-out SHA256 hashing line**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This suggests a previously active hashed email signal was disabled. If `@user` is used downstream for identity resolution or targeting (e.g., paid media pixel matching), the current un-hashed value could be a PII handling concern depending on how the variable is surfaced.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
QA warns this header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM does not mandate the header, but major inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo, Apple Mail) use it to surface the unsubscribe button in the UI. Its absence increases the likelihood of spam-button clicks as the substitute action.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024) depends on this header. If the header is genuinely absent — not just missed by the relay — this is a compliance gap that can affect deliverability to both providers. Verify against the raw SMTP headers on a seed address send; the relay capture may be incomplete.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com` uses a dedicated ESP subdomain (`e.neimanmarcus.com`), which should have DKIM configured. Confirm DKIM signing is active on this sending domain and that SPF includes the ESP's sending IPs. Without this confirmation the 43% QA pass rate is not interpretable.
