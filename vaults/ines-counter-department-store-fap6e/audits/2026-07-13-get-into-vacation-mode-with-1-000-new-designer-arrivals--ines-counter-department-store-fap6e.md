# Get into vacation mode with 1,000+ new designer arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Sixteen emails in thirteen days from Neiman Marcus, and today they pivot completely away from the gift card pitch they were running at me yesterday — twice — and land in my inbox with a runway-style new arrivals spread. No offer. No urgency. No InCircle mention. Just nine product images and two CTAs pointing me at "all women's new arrivals," as if I forgot where the website lives.
- The brands are genuinely right for me — Tom Ford, Prada, Bottega Veneta, Saint Laurent — so I didn't delete it immediately. But the hero says "Brand New Styles Just Landed" and that's the most generic phrase in retail email. Sephora sent me the same energy last week ("🎉 New products have arrived") and I gave them grief for it too; at least they had a product benefit. This email has none.
- I won't click. I won't forward it. I'll mark it read and maybe pause to wonder whether that Alaia dress is something I should look up — but I won't be doing it from this email.

## What's working

- **Brand caliber is spot-on.** Tom Ford, Prada, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta — these are exactly the brands I'd walk through at Neiman's on a Saturday afternoon. No fast-fashion noise.
- **Layout is genuinely clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. For a nine-product grid that's easy to mangle, the execution is tidy.
- **Hero CTA split is functional.** "Shop Women's" vs. "Shop Men's" at least gives me a clear first tap — I'm not hunting for a button.
- **Season read is correct.** "Vacation mode" in mid-July is appropriate. At least they're not sending me a Valentine's Day palette.

## What's weak

- **Half the email is men's.** I am a 47-year-old woman. The Ralph Lauren Purple Label blazer, the Montblanc belt, the Ferragamo loafers — all real brands, all not for me. Splitting screen real estate 50/50 by gender in what feels like a women's send is a waste of my scroll.
- **Zero offer.** Yesterday's email was a $50–$500 gift card earn. Today it's full-price new arrivals with no price context whatsoever. After the cadence of offers I've received since July 2nd, this feels like a bait-and-switch in reverse.
- **No InCircle recognition.** I'm a loyalty-card holder. Macy's at least reminds me of my extra 25% when I open their emails. Neiman Marcus treats me like a stranger every single send.
- **"1,000+ new arrivals" is abstract.** That number sounds impressive until I realize it means I have to do all the curation work myself. Give me three hero items that are genuinely curated for someone like me — not a grid of nine things that feel pulled at random.
- **Cadence exhaustion is real.** Email sixteen in thirteen days, and this one brings nothing new to justify the open. Kohl's does this too — I've received fifty-plus since Memorial Day — and it makes me tune out even the sends that matter.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the men's section entirely, or banish it to a single text link at the bottom.** Sending a women-first email that's 50% men's styling is an editorial error. Segment by demonstrated shopping behavior — if I haven't browsed men's in two years, don't give it half my screen.
- 2. **Anchor to a single curated hero product, not a nine-item grid.** Pick the Alaia dress or the Bottega piece. Tell me something specific: why it's arriving now, why it matters this season, maybe a line about limited sizing. That's what a good personal stylist does — and Neiman Marcus is supposed to be that brand.
- 3. **Acknowledge the loyalty relationship.** One line: "As an InCircle member, you have early access" — or even just "you're seeing this first." That changes the entire emotional register of this email.
- 4. **Reconnect to the gift card offer.** Yesterday you told me I could earn a $50–$500 gift card. If that's still live, a single line here — "your gift card window is still open" — turns a new arrivals browse into a conversion moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tom Ford, Prada, Bottega — just landed for vacation`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals that actually go somewhere this summer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated for your July — 9 pieces worth knowing about.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your gift card earn window is still open. Spend it on these.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Vacation Mode / 1,000+ New Arrivals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with table-based layout and a CSS hamburger nav. The 43% QA pass rate is driven entirely by infrastructure/header gaps introduced at the AgentMail relay layer — the HTML itself is structurally sound but carries several dead-code artifacts and a high-risk merge token pattern.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

| Severity | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| WARN | 26 click-redirect links not probed | QA: `26 tracking link(s) skipped — Tracking/click-redirect domains` |
| WARN | Plain-text version is 87% URLs | QA: `5289/6108 chars are URLs` — significant spam-filter signal |
| INFO | Redirect domain not confirmed resolvable | All 26 links skipped; no HTTP 200/301 confirmed |

The plain-text body URL ratio (87%) is well above the 70% threshold that triggers spam filter penalties in Gmail and Outlook. Plain-text should contain human-readable content with links interspersed, not a dump of raw tracking URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead code / commented-out markup** — three `<meta name="viewport">` variants left in `<head>` as HTML comments. This is debugging residue:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
No rendering impact, but increases payload size and signals poor template hygiene.

**iOS 9 hamburger animation hack** — the `-webkit-animation: bugfix` keyframe loop is present in the mobile stylesheet. iOS 9 is effectively extinct (0.01% market share); this is dead weight that runs on every mobile render.

**`<title>` has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but visible in browser tab previews for webmail clients.

**Hamburger nav via CSS `:checked`** — the interactive mobile nav relies on hidden `<input type="checkbox">` toggled by labels. This pattern has known failure modes in Outlook Mobile, Yahoo Mail app, and Samsung Mail. No fallback for clients that suppress `:checked` pseudo-class behavior is visible in the truncated source.

**Alt text** — cannot confirm from truncated source; recommend verifying all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes, particularly product images.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: `@user` SHA256 hash is commented out.**

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

The comment block at the top instructs implementers to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" — but `@user` is commented out with `/* */` syntax inside an HTML comment, meaning it does not execute. If any downstream link or personalization block references `@user` for identity resolution or audience targeting (common in NM's behavioral retargeting links), it will render as an empty string or AMPscript error.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.** Concatenating with an empty string literal is vestigial code from a prior template version where a suffix was appended. Should either be `SET @refid = @base_refid` or removed.

**`@uEm` lookup passes email address into URLs.** The `uEm` field from `NM_CRM_Feed` is typically the raw or encoded email address embedded in click URLs for identity stitching. This creates PII exposure via HTTP Referer headers when the subscriber navigates away from the landing page. Confirm the value is hashed/encoded before URL injection.

### 5. Compliance

| Severity | Header | Status |
|---|---|---|
| WARN | `List-Unsubscribe` | Not found — AgentMail relay not capturing |
| WARN | `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | Not found — one-click unsubscribe unconfirmed |
| WARN | `Authentication-Results` | Not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unknown |

The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` warnings are likely a relay artifact — NM/SFMC virtually always injects these. However, **absence at the capture layer means the audit trail is incomplete.** Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate both headers for >5k/day senders; if headers are genuinely absent at delivery, this is a deliverability compliance failure.

Authentication-Results absence prevents confirming DKIM signing by `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Given the sending domain is a dedicated ESP subdomain, DKIM should be verifiable — the gap is in relay capture, not necessarily in the email itself. Flag for relay configuration review.

Footer unsubscribe link is expected to be present (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3)) but cannot be confirmed from truncated source.
