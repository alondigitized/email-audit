# Designer Sale update: new styles added up to 60% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Ten days of Neiman Marcus in my inbox, and I've been keeping score. The June 24 "Your $50–$500 gift card is here" email made me feel like a VIP. This one? This one feels like the sale-floor PA announcement that comes on right after the nice moment. The red hero is gorgeous — I'll give them that. Clean, premium, instant. "Up to 60% off" on Jacquemus, Valentino, Stella McCartney, and Chiara Boni La Petite Robe is legitimately good news if it's real. But I scroll past the hero and there isn't a single price anywhere on the page. Not one "was $895, now $358." Just bags and blazers floating in white space, and eight CTA buttons that somehow include two for men's. I'm 47. I don't need men's shoes.
- I'd click — the brand lineup is too good to ignore completely — but I'd click with one eyebrow raised. The "Exclusions Apply" line in tiny text under the hero button is exactly the kind of thing that turns a great sale into a frustrating afternoon. I've been burned by that exact qualifier at Macy's and Saks enough times to know what it usually means. No InCircle mention. No "as a member, you have early access." Nothing that signals Neiman Marcus knows who I am after a decade of being a customer.
- I would not forward this to a friend. I'd save it for myself and check handbags first.

## What's working

- **The red hero is arresting.** "The Designer Sale — Up to 60% Off" in Neiman's clean serif on a deep red panel is the strongest visual hierarchy I've seen in this brand's recent run. My eye landed exactly where it should.
- **Brand roster earns the scroll.** Jacquemus, Chiara Boni La Petite Robe, Stella McCartney, Valentino Garavani — these are the names that make me stop. That's Saks-caliber brand curation, and seeing them all at sale pricing in one email is genuinely compelling.
- **The category CTAs at the bottom are actually useful.** "SHOP HANDBAGS," "SHOP DRESSES," "SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES" let me navigate straight to what matters instead of wading through everything. That's one thing Macy's never figures out — their CTAs funnel you to a cluttered homepage every time.
- **No render bugs.** The email loads clean, imagery is sharp, text is readable without pinching my screen. Basic bar, but this run of NM emails has been consistent on execution.
- **"Save on these designer styles before they're gone"** lands the right note of quiet urgency without feeling like a Kohl's "WOW 🎉" energy explosion.

## What's weak

- **Not a single price anywhere.** Eight products, zero price tags. After "up to 60% off," I want to see "Was $1,200 / Now $480" next to the Valentino mules. Without it, "up to 60%" could mean one clearance blouse and six items at 10%.
- **"Exclusions Apply" is doing a lot of heavy lifting.** That small-text footnote under the SHOP SALE button immediately makes the 60% claim feel conditional. I've been through this at Macy's LAST CHANCE emails — confirm, click, find the exclusion list stretches to designer, contemporary, and anything I actually want.
- **Two men's CTAs in an email aimed at me.** "SHOP MEN'S" and "SHOP MEN'S SHOES" buttons take up real estate in a six-button grid that should be mine entirely. Sephora would never dilute a women's beauty email with men's grooming CTAs mid-grid.
- **No InCircle recognition at all.** This is my fifth Neiman Marcus email in less than three days, and not one of them has acknowledged that I'm a loyalty member. The June 24 gift card email at least felt personal with "Your." This one treats me like a cold prospect.
- **The cadence is getting loud.** This is email five since June 24. I noticed. I'm starting to tune them the same way I tune out Kohl's at thirty-one emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show at least three actual prices next to the hero products.** "Jacquemus Le Chiquito — Was $650, now $325" is the single change that makes this email convert instead of browse. The 60% claim needs a receipt.
- 2. **Personalize the hero or subhead for InCircle members.** Even "Member Early Access" in a small badge above the hero would change the tenor of this entire email. I want to feel like I got here first.
- 3. **Cut the men's CTAs from the main grid.** Move them to a collapsed footer row or drop them entirely from this send. Run a segmented version.
- 4. **Swap "before they're gone" for a hard date.** "Offer ends Sunday" is more credible than soft scarcity language. After ten days of promos, I've learned NM's "before they're gone" often means "until the next email."
- 5. **Add one beauty or fragrance product** to the grid. I'm a beauty-counter regular. Tom Ford, La Mer, or even a fragrance bundle at sale pricing would pull me down the page further than another blazer.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Designer Sale Update" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The send infrastructure uses Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with an ESP relay (sender domain `e.neimanmarcus.com`); the AgentMail capture layer missed critical headers (authentication, unsubscribe), making deliverability posture unverifiable from this sample. The plain-text body is nearly entirely URLs, which is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links unverifiable.**
All click-through links are wrapped by a redirect domain and were skipped during HTTP probing. Link rot, broken redirect chains, or misconfigured UTM parameters on any of the 26 links cannot be confirmed from this capture.

**No issues confirmed**, but the redirect chain opacity is a blind spot — any broken links would go undetected until click-through.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport meta tags left in production HTML.**
Three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` variants are present as HTML comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are debug/iteration artifacts. They don't affect rendering but bloat payload and expose template history.

**iOS9 hamburger animation workaround still active.**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
```
This is a ~2015-era hack for a version of iOS with <0.1% market share. Dead weight.

**CSS-only interactive hamburger nav (`:checked` pattern) has narrow client support.**
The `.mobileCont:checked~table` logic only fires in Apple Mail / iOS Mail. Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo silently ignore it. The fallback degrades to a static state, which is acceptable, but the interactive intent is lost for the majority of mobile clients.

**Duplicate breakpoint declarations** at both `max-device-width: 480px` and `max-width: 480px` (and again at 520px) — redundant but harmless.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable declarations are exposed in HTML comment output.**
The opening block renders verbatim in the delivered HTML source:
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ... -->
```
This exposes data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`), and lookup logic to any recipient who views source.

**`@user` variable has ambiguous scenario selection.**
The comment block says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" and the active/inactive path for `@user` is:
```
<!-- /* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
It is not clear from the HTML alone which scenario was activated at send time. If the wrong branch was used, `@user` may be unset (`%%user%%` would render as an empty string or literal token in links).

**`@user` assignment in comments appears malformed** — the comment reads `@user Lookup(...)` without an `=`, unlike the other variables (`@emailid (from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup...)`). If this comment is a copy of actual AMPscript that was ever live, the missing `=` would have caused a runtime error.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: status unknown.**
QA warning: header not captured by AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm presence or absence at delivery. Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements mandate this header.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not found.**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for senders above 5,000/day threshold. If absent at the MTA level (not just missing from relay capture), this is a compliance gap — Yahoo began enforcement in February 2024.

**`Authentication-Results` header: absent.**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be determined from this capture. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain; alignment between envelope `From`, header `From`, and DKIM `d=` value is unverifiable.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs by character count** (5,268 / 6,036 chars per QA). A plain-text version composed almost entirely of click-redirect URLs with no readable prose is a known spam filter signal and degrades the experience for text-only clients.

---

Cannot assess UTM parameter presence or landing page alignment — all 26 links were redirect-wrapped and not probed. The sending infrastructure routes clicks through `e.neimanmarcus.com`, which should append UTM params before forwarding. This is unverifiable from the capture.

No broken pixel or missing tracking image issues were flagged.
