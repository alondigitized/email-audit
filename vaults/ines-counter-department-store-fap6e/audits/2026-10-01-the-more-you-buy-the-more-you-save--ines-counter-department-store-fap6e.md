# The more you buy, the more you save
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the first Neiman's email in a week that made me do math. The tiered table up top is clear enough: spend $150, save $30, and so on up to $650 off at $2,000. The 20% at the first tier is fine. The tiers get less generous as you move up in dollars, which is not the "more you buy, the more you save" the subject promises. I'd call it honest but not exciting.
- The rest is the usual Neiman's quiet. Nine products in a grid, designer names only, no prices and no shouting. That restraint is why I keep opening them, and Kohl's would have put three red banners across the top of this. But the products feel random: a gown, a tux blazer, a loafer and a corduroy jacket. Nothing says beauty or points to me specifically. Beauty is also excluded in the fine print, which is a real miss for someone like me.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already shopping for a coat. Would I forward it? No. It's a fair offer, but it isn't a reason to leave my beauty counter loyalty.

## What's working

- The spend-and-save table is easy to read. I can see my tier at a glance, with no hunting for the math.
- The promo code sits right under the headline with a plain black "Shop Now" button. That is a clear, calm CTA.
- The gold hero is understated. It's far less frantic than Kohl's "EXTRA" sends this week.
- The product grid carries real designer names (Akris punto, Tod's, Jimmy Choo, Staud), which fits designer-access shopping.
- "Ends 10/4/26 at 10:00 PM CT" in the fine print is a specific, credible deadline.

## What's weak

- Beauty and fragrance are excluded in the fine print. I only learn that at the bottom, after I've been sold on "up to $650 off."
- The "up to $650" headline is a stretch. Most people will land at the $30 or $100 tier.
- There are no prices and no loyalty cues. As someone who won't use a card without points, I see no InCircle mention anywhere. Macy's at least tied its send to a confirm-now deal.
- The grid is scattershot, with eveningwear next to loafers and nothing for beauty. Sephora Daily Deals at least gives a reason for each tile.
- "Claim your 15% off" for a text signup feels like a different brand voice, and it sits on a black block that undercuts the calm.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the exclusions (beauty, fragrance, jewelry) near the table, not in 7-point type at the bottom. Honesty up front earns more trust than a bigger headline.
- 2. Add a member line such as "InCircle members: earn points on top." That would make me feel known.
- 3. Segment the grid by what the recipient buys. Beauty buyers should see something else, or a different send altogether.
- 4. Show prices or "from $" on a few tiles so the offer connects to real items.
- 5. Replace the "Shop Women's/Men's/Home" buttons with category links that match the grid.

## Technical Audit

- **Plain-text part is 79% URLs** (5441 of 6884 chars). This is consistent with long redirect-wrapped URLs inlined in the text alternative. It is a spam-filter and readability risk (QA WARN).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` (or `xml:lang`), so screen readers can't pick the right language.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. These only match when the class attribute is exactly that value. Any element with multiple classes will silently skip the mobile override. I can't confirm this from the truncated markup.
- **Checkbox hamburger nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~` pattern is not supported in Gmail and several other clients. A fallback is not visible in the excerpt.
- **Minor:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has trailing whitespace and doesn't match the subject line.
- Image alt text and image-to-text ratio can't be assessed because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens are visible in the rendered portion of the source.
- **Template logic leaked into delivered HTML.** The pre-`<!DOCTYPE>` comment (`REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid … @uEm … @cmdid`) ships to recipients. It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`). It also contains a note, `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`, that the template author left unresolved. The comment text also has unbalanced parentheses in the `Lookup(...))` expressions. Nothing in the comment executes, but a draft-level configuration note reached production.
- Because the comment says to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO", the sender may not have finalized which `@user` scenario the template uses. Check that the body doesn't contain a raw `@user` or `%%` token (not visible in the excerpt).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
