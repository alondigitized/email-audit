# Special offer: Free shipping on your $150+ online order
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Free shipping on $150 with a code — fine, I guess, but this is the least interesting hook Neiman's has sent me this month. Compare it to yesterday's triple-points-on-Brunello-Cucinelli email or Wednesday's tiered gift-card table — those made me do math I *wanted* to do. This one's just "here's a shipping fee we won't charge you," and then reads the fine print says InCircle members already get free standard shipping with no code needed — so as a member, this email is basically for other people.
- The nine-item product grid underneath is a decent mixed bag — I clocked the Guerlain Abeille Royale cream and the Dolce&Gabbana red cardholder immediately — but there's no story tying them together, no "new arrivals" or "we picked these for you" framing. It's just merchandise soup under a shipping banner.
- I wouldn't click. I might scroll past the product tiles out of habit since Guerlain caught my eye, but I'm not entering a code I don't need. Not forwarding this one — nothing here is a "look at this" moment the way the YSL tote roundup was.

## What's working

- The lavender-blue hero is clean and on-brand, easy to read at a glance, no zooming needed.
- Product grid photography is crisp and consistent — no broken images, no placeholder gaps.
- Guerlain Abeille Royale in the grid is a legitimate beauty-counter draw for me personally.
- Category shop buttons (Women's, Men's, Shoes, Beauty, Handbags, Home) are big, tappable, easy to hit on a phone.

## What's weak

- I'm an InCircle member and the fine print tells me my free shipping doesn't need this code at all — so the entire hero exists to talk past me, not to me. Sephora does this too (three straight emails with my points balance stuck at zero) — brands pushing offers that don't reflect my actual account status.
- No loyalty tier callout anywhere in the visible design — the member benefit is buried in 8-point legal text at the bottom, not treated as a headline the way it should be for an InCircle regular.
- Nine products with zero curatorial logic — no "new in," no occasion, no size/fit info visible on any of them. It's a wall of merchandise, not a look.
- "Exclusions Apply" sitting right under the CTA is a small buzzkill — doesn't build trust the way a clean, honest offer would.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with InCircle status instead of a shipping code — something like "Members ship free, always" would actually acknowledge who I am instead of making me read fine print to find out I'm exempt.
- 2. Give the product grid a theme — "Fall arrivals," "Your Beauty Edit," anything — instead of nine unrelated SKUs with no connective tissue.
- 3. Move the loyalty language out of the footer legalese and into a visible badge near the hero.
- 4. Drop or shrink "Exclusions Apply" — or at least don't put it directly under the button.
