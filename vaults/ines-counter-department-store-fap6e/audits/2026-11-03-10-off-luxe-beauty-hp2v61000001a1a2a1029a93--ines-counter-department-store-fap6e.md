# 💎 10% OFF LUXE BEAUTY 💎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This one is better than the last few. After a week of Sephora subject lines that didn't match their bodies, "Luxe Beauty" and a body headed "Treat Yourself to Luxe Makeup" actually line up. I can see Armani, Prada, Dior, YSL, Gucci and Tom Ford, grouped by price: under $40, under $60, under $70. That's how I shop at the counter. I pick a price I'm comfortable with and then pick the brand.
- What I can't find is the 10% off the subject promises. The only offer I see near the top is free same-day delivery with code SPEEDY on $75, ending 11/9. The price tiers are real prices, not a discount. For a subject that leads with "10% OFF," that's a bait-and-switch. Neiman Marcus handled this better with "25% off": one black box, one number, one button, no hunting.
- I'd click, but only on the Armani or Dior tiles, and only after I'd confirmed the discount applies to those brands. I wouldn't forward it. It's the twenty-fourth Sephora email this week and it doesn't feel special.

## What's working

- The "Luxe Under $40 / $60 / $70" structure shows me designer names at prices I can see. Nobody has to guess what "luxe" means.
- The product photography is clean. The Armani, Dior and YSL compacts look like prestige counter items, not Kohl's-style clutter.
- Specific product names and prices sit under each tile, e.g. Dior Backstage Glow Maximizer Palette $55 and Prada Touch Cream-to-Powder Blur $42. I get the shade and finish detail I care about.
- The "Savings Event" banner with "stacks with other offers" is a respectful cue for a points-conscious shopper.
- The Sephora Collection credit card and the in-store barcode at the bottom reward the customer who actually shops at the event.

## What's weak

- The subject says 10% off and I can't see 10% off in the hero. The fine print mentions it, and that's too buried.
- Free delivery with SPEEDY sits above the hero and competes with it. It's a second offer before I've even reached the first.
- Same-day delivery needs a $75 spend. That's a threshold, not a gift, and it's hard to read in the small box.
- "Splurge Now" is the only button in the hero, and it goes to a general page. "Splurge" isn't a respectful word for a 47-year-old with a mortgage.
- The bottom half turns into a Macy's-style pile-up: holiday hits, hair sets, $50 and under gifts, a gift card banner, the card offer, and a barcode. That's too many messages after a clean top.
- The legal text is a wall of tiny type. I can't read it without zooming.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the discount in the hero. If it's 10% off, the first thing I see should be "10% off luxe beauty, Nov 3–9," with the code and the eligible brands.
- 2. Link each tier to its own product page, and make each CTA specific. "Shop luxe under $60" beats "Splurge Now."
- 3. Cut the bottom third to one message. Pick between the gift guide, the hair sets and the gift card.
- 4. Move the SPEEDY delivery offer below the hero, or make it a one-line strip.
- 5. Say plainly who gets what: Insider tier, Rouge, points multipliers. That's the tier clarity I look for.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Alt text:** The QA warning on `o.gif` looks like a false positive. The source has `alt="" aria-hidden="true"`, which is correct for a decorative pixel. The preheader pixel (`app.sephora.com/O/…`) has no `alt`. It is hidden with `display:none`, so the impact is minimal, but adding `alt=""` would be cleaner.
- **Malformed CSS:** The `<style>` block has `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size {…}<![endif]-->` inside the `@media only screen and (max-width:480px)` block. Conditional comments are not valid there. Browsers will probably treat `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size` and `<![endif]--> .mobileHeaderTierRouge` as invalid selectors and drop those rules. This would break mobile sizing for `.mobileHeaderTierRouge` (and the intended MSO `img.mso-size` rule). This is inferred from CSS parsing rules, not tested in a client.
- **Fixed width:** The wrapper table has `width:700px; min-width:700px`. The only mobile overrides are the font and image classes in the media query. The layout may force horizontal scrolling on narrow clients that ignore those overrides.
- **Viewport meta:** `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Robots meta:** `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` is invalid. The valid token is `noindex`.
- **Structured data:** The JSON-LD has `"discountCode": ""` and `@type: "PromotionCard"`, which isn't a schema.org type. Most clients strip `<script>` tags anyway, so this is low severity.
- **Plain-text part:** The text version is 0 chars (QA FAIL). Multipart/alternative has no usable text part, which hurts accessibility and deliverability.
