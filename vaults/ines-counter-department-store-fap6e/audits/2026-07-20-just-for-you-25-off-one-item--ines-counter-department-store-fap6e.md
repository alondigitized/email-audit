# Just for you: 25% off one item
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-five. That's where I am with Neiman Marcus this July, and after four consecutive days of LAST CALL banners and 70%-off clearance blasts, "Just for you: 25% off one item" actually made me stop scrolling for a second. The creative pivot is the right instinct — clean hero, aspirational brand grid (Miu Miu, Jimmy Choo, Manolo, Fendi), a real code, and none of that liquidation-bin energy the last three sends were radiating. If the July 18th "Up to 70% off new sale arrivals" email was NM at its most frantic, this one is trying to remember that it's Neiman Marcus.
- But here's where it loses me entirely: no InCircle mention. Anywhere. I've opened twenty-five emails this month. I have a membership. Points are not a bonus for me — they're a precondition for clicking buy. Sephora surfaced my multiplier in the subject line on that 4X hair care send last week. Macy's at least gestures at Star Rewards on confirmation emails. NM gives me a promo code — JULY25-1DGZ-RRLPET — that I have to manually type at checkout, on my phone, while I'm trying to remember whether the Ulla Johnson piece I want is in stock in an 8. That's not respect. That's homework.
- I'd read it, screenshot the code, and mark it as read. Maybe come back to it mid-week if I'm already on NM for a specific piece. Not clicking right now. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- Hero offer reads in under two seconds — "25% OFF one regular-price item" + SHOP NOW button is exactly where the eye lands first; no hunt required.
- The product grid earns its real estate: Miu Miu, Chloe, Ferragamo, Oscar de la Renta, Jimmy Choo, Fendi, Veronica Beard, Ulla Johnson, Manolo Blahnik — this is the NM I actually want a discount at, not the clearance rack version from the last four sends.
- "LIMITED TIME · EXCLUSIVE OFFER" framing is calm urgency, not a shriek — a genuine step up from the ALL-CAPS LAST CALL energy of the July 18th and 19th emails.
- Category shop buttons (Women's Clothing, Women's Shoes, Handbags, Accessories) give me a practical exit ramp when I know exactly what I want.
- No render bugs, no broken images, clean layout top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No InCircle mention anywhere — for a brand that sent me 25 emails this month, not surfacing loyalty status is a miss that Sephora would never make on a percent-off offer.
- Subject and preheader both open with "Just for you" — inbox shows the redundancy immediately, and one of those slots is now doing nothing.
- The promo code requires manual entry at checkout; no auto-apply link means I screenshot it and forget it, which is how conversions die.
- Coming directly after a week of 70%-off LAST CALL offers, 25% off regular price feels like a value step backward — the sequencing makes this harder to sell than it would be in isolation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface InCircle status in the body** — one line: "Your InCircle points apply automatically." Removes my single biggest hesitation and Sephora already knows to do this.
- 2. **Break the subject/preheader sameness** — both can't open with "Just for you." The preheader is 49 wasted characters right now.
- 3. **Make the code auto-apply** — wire the SHOP NOW button to a URL that pre-loads the discount. Manual code entry at mobile checkout is where this conversion dies.
- 4. **Anchor the deadline** — "LIMITED TIME" is not actionable; "Offer ends Wednesday at midnight" is.
- 5. **Link at least one brand tile directly to a product page** — Manolo Blahnik → a specific heel, not the Manolo category. That's the click that turns a browse into a buy.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — "Just for you: 25% off one item" (Neiman Marcus)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript and uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with a CSS checkbox-hack hamburger nav. The primary technical concerns are exposed AMPscript variable declarations in raw HTML comments, missing RFC-required unsubscribe headers, unverifiable authentication chain, and a plain-text body that is >70% tracking URLs — a known spam-filter trigger.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is URL-saturated**
QA flagged that 5,268 of 7,435 chars (71%) in the plain-text part are URLs. Major spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY`, Gmail) penalize plain-text alternatives that are predominantly links rather than readable content. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking parameters.

**26 tracking links unverifiable**
All 26 click-redirect links were skipped during HTTP probing (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar redirect domain). No broken-link confirmation is possible from this audit, but any redirect-chain failures will produce dead landing pages silently.

**`CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` pattern**
Evidence in the AMPscript comment block:
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string is a no-op and suggests a template where a suffix token was removed without cleanup. If `@base_refid` is null/empty, `@refid` silently resolves to an empty string, which would corrupt any URL that appends `%%=v(@refid)=%%` as a query parameter.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out viewport meta tags in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead configuration debt. No functional impact, but these should be removed — the presence of `width=320, initial-scale=0.29` in particular indicates an ancient mobile-scaling workaround that was never cleaned up.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but some clients expose the `<title>` value in tab/window labels.

**Hamburger nav has no ARIA instrumentation**
The CSS checkbox hack (`navbar-checkbox:checked`) produces no accessible toggle state. Screen readers receive no `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, or `role="navigation"` signals. The `<input type="checkbox">` controlling the menu is not labeled. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 4.1.2 failure on supporting clients (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail).

**iOS9 `-webkit-animation: bugfix` present**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
```
iOS9 is EOL (2016). This pattern is harmless but adds parse overhead and flags as unusual in some content analysis tools.

**Alt text unverifiable from truncated source**
Image `alt` attributes could not be audited. Given the promotional nature of the email, missing or empty `alt` on CTA images would be a WCAG failure and degrades rendering when images are blocked.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable declarations are exposed in rendered HTML**

The entire variable setup block is wrapped in an HTML comment (`<!-- ... -->`), not inside an AMPscript `%%[ ]%%` block. This means the comment is delivered verbatim to every recipient's inbox:

```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) 
    @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
    @cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) -->
```

This exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), and the lookup architecture to anyone who views source. It is not a direct data leak of PII, but it is an information disclosure risk and violates basic operational security hygiene for a retailer of this profile.

**`@user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` is doubly commented out**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
An AMPscript comment (`/* */`) inside an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`). The intent was likely to make this line inactive, but since the outer block is already an HTML comment, the `@user` variable is never set by AMPscript either way. If any downstream personalization block references `%%=v(@user)=%%`, it will render as an empty string rather than a SHA-256 hash.

**`@emailid` has dual-source fallback with no error handling**
```
@emailid (from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New)
```
If the DE lookup fails and the fallback also returns null, `@emailid` is empty. Any URL using this value for analytics identity stitching (e.g., `&eid=%%=v(@emailid)=%%`) will send a blank parameter silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
QA finding: header absent from relay capture. RFC 2369 compliance requires this header for bulk mail. Gmail and Yahoo Mail use it to surface the "Unsubscribe" button in the client chrome. Its absence increases the likelihood of user-initiated spam complaints over the native unsubscribe flow.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected**
QA finding: RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not present. As of February 2024, Google and Yahoo require this for bulk senders (>5k/day). Neiman Marcus at this volume almost certainly qualifies. If the headers are present upstream but dropped by the AgentMail relay, this should be confirmed against raw SMTP logs — but cannot be cleared from available data.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
QA finding: `Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (SFMC subdomain). Without a confirmed `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass`, deliverability risk cannot be assessed. Verify via direct SMTP header capture or a seed-based inbox test (e.g., Mail-Tester, GlockApps).

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from truncated source. Standard SFMC templates include a footer with physical mailing address — confirm it is present and not conditionally hidden by a display:none class on mobile.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All 26 click links were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM tagging (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) cannot be confirmed present on landing page URLs. Given the `@refid`/`@base_refid` structure, campaign attribution likely rides a proprietary `refid` parameter rather than standard UTMs — confirm this aligns with the analytics platform (GA4 vs. internal BI).

**Offer continuity unverifiable from truncated source**
The subject promises "25% off one item." Whether the landing page surfaces a matching offer code, pre-applies the discount, or requires coupon entry cannot be confirmed without probing the actual redirect destinations.
