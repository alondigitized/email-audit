# 50% off select Sephora Collection lip faves 🪩
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one actually opens where the subject promised — a real 50% off banner on Sephora Collection lip picks for National Lip Day, "Today only." No bait-and-switch this time, which is a relief after last Sunday's "up to 50%" that turned out to be 30% off Charlotte Tilbury. But the good behavior lasts exactly one screen. By the second scroll I'm looking at two brand-new fragrance launches I've never heard of, then a nine-item new-arrivals grid that's mostly blush, body butter, hair conditioner, and scalp toner — maybe two actual lip products in the whole block. If the subject says "lip faves," show me lip faves, not a grand tour of the whole store.
- This is also, by my count, the fifth or sixth Sephora email this week, and that's exactly the fatigue problem I flagged with the sunscreen blast on the 24th. Sephora keeps having good ideas for one banner and then padding the rest of the email with unrelated merchandising. Compare that to Neiman Marcus, who at least repeats the SAME offer structure across sends so I know what I'm getting even when I'm annoyed by the repetition — Sephora repeats the cadence but not the content, so I never build a mental model of what's coming.
- Would I click? Maybe, just for that top banner — the 50% off is real and I do buy Sephora Collection lip stuff. Would I scroll past the fragrance detour and the product grid? No. Forward to a friend? No, there's nothing here worth sharing, it's a standard deals email. Read and move on.

## What's working

- The hero banner delivers exactly what the subject promised — 50% off, Sephora Collection, lip, National Lip Day, no mismatch like last week.
- "SHOP NOW" button is right under the offer, first thing my eye hits, no hunting required.
- The Insider/VIB/Rouge tier bar at the bottom is a nice touch — as a member, I like seeing where I stand without digging for it.
- 4X points on Patrick Ta is a legible loyalty hook, and I do actually care about points multipliers — that's the one thing that would make me pause before deleting.

## What's weak

- The lip offer gets one banner, then it's fragrance launches (RUHVEDA, INFLUIXIOUS — brands I don't know) and a grid that's barely lip-related at all. Subject says lip, email delivers everything but.
- No product-level pricing tied to the 50% off — I see a collage of lip products up top but nothing in the grid below actually marked down, so I can't tell what's really on sale versus just "new."
- This is at least the fifth Sephora email in my inbox this week. Kohl's moved into my guest room with their coupon spam, and now Sephora's doing the same thing with a rotating cast of themes.
- Zero lifestyle or model imagery anywhere — it's all flat-lay product shots. Fine for a deals email, but it means there's nothing here that says "this is for a 47-year-old regular," it's a generic catalog page.

## Recommendations

- 1. Keep the lip focus through the whole email — if the subject and hero promise lip faves, the next section should be more lip, not a fragrance launch and an unrelated new-arrivals wall. That single fix would do more than anything else here.
- 2. Put the 50% off price directly on the lip products in the grid (strikethrough original price, sale price) so I can see the deal at the product level, not just in banner copy.
- 3. Cut this to one send this week instead of layering it onto five others — Neiman Marcus's tiered spend table is repetitive but at least consistent; Sephora's constant theme-switching just reads as noise.
- 4. Move the Insider/VIB/Rouge tier bar and points hook higher — that's the part of the email that actually respects me as a repeat customer, don't bury it at the bottom.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your lip faves, half off — National Lip Day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 4X points on Patrick Ta through 7/29`
