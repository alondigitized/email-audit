# From your feed to your cart 🤩
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a Sephora Daily Deals send, and I didn't get a clear reason to open it. The top says "Up to 50% off Hair Faves" with a "TODAY ONLY" badge. Then the email switches to "SOCIAL STARS" and a Cult Gaia fragrance feature. After that come a product grid, a Klarna/Afterpay/PayPal block, a value-size hair care tile, and Korean skincare. Five stories are competing in one email. The subject line says "feed to cart," which is the social-media angle, but the deal at the top is hair. Nothing in the subject prepared me for that.
- I've been getting the hair and fragrance beats from Sephora all week. The 9/23 "Hair Daily Deals" email opened with the offer and nothing else, and it scored 8. This one is the same offer, buried in a longer email and less focused. Two days ago the Living Proof send made me scroll past other offers to find the 40% off. This one does the same thing in a different order.
- Would I click? Maybe the hair banner, since I have a Rouge-tier habit, but I'd probably skim and move on. I wouldn't forward it. Nothing here says "you," and nothing shows my Beauty Insider points.

## What's working

- The top banner is clear. "Up to 50% off Hair Faves" with "TODAY ONLY" and a single Shop Now button is easy to read.
- The free same-day delivery line with code DELIVERED and the 10/7 deadline is a concrete perk.
- The Cult Gaia block lists shade names and prices ($48–$228), so I can judge whether it's worth my time without clicking.
- The "Only at Sephora," "Clean," and "Limited Edition" tags in the grid help me pick out what's exclusive. Versace, Tom Ford, and Jo Malone in the mix make it feel like it's speaking to a department-store shopper.
- The Insider/VIB/Rouge tier bar is at the bottom, so the loyalty program is at least present.

## What's weak

- There are too many stories. Hair, Cult Gaia, a new-arrivals grid, pay-later, value-size hair, and Korean skincare all fight for attention. Macy's can be loud, but its emails at least keep one offer on top.
- The subject doesn't match the content. "From your feed to your cart" points to social-trending products, but the strongest offer is hair at up to 50% off.
- Klarna, Afterpay, and PayPal take up a full tile. I don't want to split payments, and I won't use a card that doesn't earn points. That space is wasted on me.
- The loyalty message sits at the very bottom. There's no points multiplier, no tier status, and nothing that treats me as a member. "Rewards Bazaar" is a link, not a benefit.
- The grid tiles have small product names and prices with no reviews or stars. It looks like a catalog page, not a curated pick.
- The legal fine print is a long block at the bottom in tiny type. I can't read it without zooming, and "up to 50%" with several asterisks doesn't feel fully honest.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut to one job. Lead with the hair deal, add a short loyalty line ("Rouge: 2x points on hair"), and drop or move the pay-later tile and the Korean skincare block. A focused email works better. Neiman Marcus's "Festive Heels" send was one hero and six products with no noise, and it scored 6 with far less going on.
- 2. Make the subject describe the offer. If the offer is hair, say hair. If it's Cult Gaia, say that.
- 3. Put a member benefit in the hero, such as points or a tier perk, so I can see what's in it for me. Ulta would bury the offer, but Sephora can do better than this.
- 4. Add ratings to the product grid and pick 4 to 6 items, not 8 or more.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off hair, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cult Gaia just landed + 50% off hair`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free same-day delivery with code DELIVERED. Ends 10/7.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus new Cult Gaia fragrances and Rouge points on hair.`
