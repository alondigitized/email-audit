# Don't miss out on up to $200 off...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "Neiman's again, same $200 ladder." This is the sixth offer email in five days. I got "Grab it now: $200 off your purchase" on 10/6, "You could save $50-$200" on 10/7, "Up to $200 off + 3x InCircle points" on 10/8, and "Up to $200 off + 3x Points? Yes, please..." yesterday. Today's hero is the same lilac block with the same NMCIRCLE code and the same spend-and-save table. The only new thing is the "Don't miss out" nag, which at least has a real deadline: 10/12 at 10 PM CT.
- The email itself is clean and the offer is easy to read. Spend $200 and save $50, spend $1,000 and save $200, with the code right there. The triple points strip sits underneath. A designer grid follows with no prices and no beauty anywhere. I shop the counter, so I looked for beauty and found a gown, heels and a leather jacket. Nothing here tells me whether my usual purchases even count.
- Would I click? Only if I already had a $500 or $1,000 purchase planned. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read. I'd also keep it around until 10/12 in case I decide to cash in the points multiplier.

## What's working

- The spend-and-save table is the clearest part. I can see in two seconds what $500 gets me ($100 off).
- The black "Plus, InCircle Members earn Triple Points" strip gives the loyalty angle its own line. Kohl's buried its Kohl's Cash below the offers, so this is better.
- The code is bold and right in the hero, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The nine-up designer grid (Ramy Brook, rag & bone, Jimmy Choo, Tod's) feels like the Neiman's I like. It's far calmer than Macy's, which screamed "LAST CHANCE" at me.
- Nothing is broken. Images load, text doesn't overlap, and the lilac "Shop Women's..." buttons are big enough for a thumb.

## What's weak

- It's the sixth near-identical offer in five days, and my 10/9 note called the gown editorial the only send I actually liked. Frequency is making the "limited time" claim feel false.
- The grid has no prices and no beauty. Sephora Insider at least led with a points hero on a named brand, and I could tell where my money would go.
- All nine products are different designers and there's no hero item. It reads as a catalog dump, not an edit.
- The fine print is a wall of tiny bold text. I only learn there that sale and reduced-price items don't qualify, and that points don't apply to "beauty salons" and other store services. That's the sort of surprise that makes me distrust a points offer.
- "Don't miss out" is a bit shouty. I'd rather be told what I'd miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop re-sending the same ladder daily. Send it once, then a reminder on the last day with only the deadline and the points as the new information.
- 2. Put a beauty row in the grid or the shop buttons. A beauty-counter regular needs "Shop Beauty" and a clear statement of whether it counts toward the spend tiers.
- 3. Show prices or "from" prices on the grid, so I can mentally clear the $500 threshold.
- 4. Move a one-line version of the exclusions (sale items out, beauty salons out) up under the table.
- 5. Pick one hero product instead of nine equal tiles.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Sunday: $200 off + 3x InCircle points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `48 hours left: up to $200 off, code NMCIRCLE`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $1,000, save $200, and earn triple points through 10/12 at 10 PM CT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last two days for InCircle triple points on designer, shoes and handbags.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a standard SFMC-built, table-based email with a media-query and checkbox-hack mobile nav. It has two confirmed code-level defects: leftover internal template comments and a zoom-blocking viewport tag. The source is truncated and the QA run has 3 warnings and 0 failures, so footer, link and header checks are only partly verifiable.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking:** 32 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe as click-redirect domains. Destinations, redirect health and UTM handling are unverified, but this is not a confirmed defect.
- **Link bodies not visible:** The truncated source shows no `href` values, so I can't audit link-level problems.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation. If the attribute is set elsewhere, it isn't visible in the source provided.
- **Invalid font stack:** `.disclaimerText` uses `Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. The quoted `'sans-serif'` is read as a font name, not the generic fallback, so the stack has no true generic fallback.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** Mobile rules such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` match only when `class` is exactly that value. Any element that gets a second class loses the responsive rule.
- **Checkbox nav limited to some clients:** The hamburger menu uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. This works in Apple Mail and iOS, but Gmail and Outlook strip or ignore `:checked` and the `<input>`, so those clients depend on whatever fallback exists. I can't see the fallback in the truncated source.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is non-descriptive and has trailing whitespace. This is minor.
- **Not assessable:** Image `alt` text and `role="presentation"` on layout tables fall in the truncated portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template comments shipped in the delivered HTML:** The first comment block exposes:
  - SFMC Data Extension names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`.
  - Field names: `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`.
  - Variable names: `@email`, `@refid`, `@base_refid`.
  - The author instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" and `/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */`.
- **Impact:** This leaks CRM schema details and the email-hashing approach to anyone who views source. It also shows the template was not cleaned before send. Remove the comments or compile them out at send time.
- **Leftover commented-out code:** There are three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants (`user-scalable=no`, `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`). These are dead code but harmless.
- **No unrendered tokens:** No unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens appear in the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` missing:** The header was not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed as a sender-side defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` missing:** The RFC 8058 header was not found, so one-click unsubscribe support is unverified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it for high-volume senders, so check the raw headers at the source.
- **`Authentication-Results` missing:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA note attributes this to the relay.
- **Footer unverified:** The physical address and unsubscribe link fall outside the truncated source. The QA run recorded no failures.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Cannot be assessed:** The 32 tracking links were not resolved, so UTM parameters and landing-page alignment with the "up to $200 off" subject are unconfirmed.
- **Likely tracking mechanism:** The `@refid` / `@base_refid` variables in the comment suggest campaign attribution runs through a reference ID, not UTMs. That is an inference and not verified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC variable and Data Extension comments (and the dead viewport comments) from the production send, using AMPscript-safe comment syntax or a pre-send minify step.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag to restore pinch-zoom.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a descriptive `<title>`.
4. Change `'sans-serif'` to unquoted `sans-serif` in `.disclaimerText`.
5. Use `*[class~=…]` or `.class` selectors for the mobile rules, and confirm the nav has a non-`:checked` fallback for Gmail and Outlook.
6. Check the raw message headers at the source (not through the relay) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment).
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with resolved tracking redirects, to check UTMs, final landing URLs, `alt` text and the footer.
