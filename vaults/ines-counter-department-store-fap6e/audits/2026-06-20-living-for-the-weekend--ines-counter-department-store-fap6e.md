# Living for the weekend?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Well. After six Neiman Marcus emails in five days — including that genuinely solid "up to $200 off" send from two days ago that made me click through to the designer sale — this is what lands in my inbox on a Friday morning: "Easy Going." A lifestyle storytelling email. No offer. No event. No percentage. Just a model in a denim jacket and two soft-serve CTAs asking me to "Shop Weekend" or "Shop Contemporary."
- I get it — NM needs to mix in brand-building alongside the promo cadence. But the timing feels off. They had me engaged with the designer sale. I was in buying mode. This email pumps the brakes and asks me to browse casual contemporary separates I wasn't thinking about. And as someone who primarily crosses paths with NM at the beauty counter and the designer event floor, an email about "lightweight signatures and crisp core essentials" reads like it got routed to my inbox by mistake.
- I marked it as read. I would not forward it to a friend. If someone specifically asked me what NM was doing in the casual space right now, I'd remember seeing *something*, but I couldn't tell you a single product that was in it.

## What's working

- **Clean render, clean layout.** Nothing broken, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum — this email is professionally executed even if the strategy misses me. Macy's has sent me emails that look like someone sat on the design file; this isn't that.
- **Hero image is genuinely pleasant.** The photography has an editorial quality — city street, real-woman energy, not a studio mannequin. That's NM's aesthetic doing its job.
- **Brand recognition carries the open.** The Neiman Marcus logo and nav bar set my expectation immediately. I know where I am.
- **Product grid is tasteful.** RAG & BONE, AGOLDE, R13 — these are brands I respect. The six-product layout below the hero gives me something to look at without being the chaos Sephora served me in their "beauty wins" email with seven modules stacked wall to wall.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Zero. After "up to 60% off" two days ago, this is a full-price lifestyle email with no hook. That's a gear shift I wasn't invited to make.
- **Two CTAs competing with each other.** "Shop Weekend" and "Shop Contemporary" side by side — I literally couldn't tell you which one I'd click. Split attention is lost attention. One clear CTA would've focused me.
- **Not my category.** I'm a beauty-counter customer. I also buy designer shoes and the occasional event piece. "Casual contemporary separates" is not where my NM wallet opens. Kohl's at least knew I was in last-minute-gift mode when they sent the Father's Day email. NM doesn't seem to know who I am this week.
- **The copy is filler.** "The new casual is all about remixing soft, lightweight signatures and crisp core essentials." I read that sentence twice and retained nothing. It's the marketing equivalent of a white noise machine.
- **No loyalty signal anywhere.** I have an account. I have purchase history. Nothing in this email acknowledges that — no member pricing, no circle loyalty note, nothing. The Saks equivalent would at least whisper something about my status.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one CTA and give it a destination.** "Shop the Weekend Edit" pointing to a curated collection page beats two vague buttons that split the click.
- 2. **Add a price anchor or promo layer.** Even "complimentary shipping on this edit" is something. Full-price, no hook, after a sale cycle? The email needs a reason to act now.
- 3. **Acknowledge the sale-to-lifestyle shift explicitly.** Something like "Sale's still on — but here's what's new at full price" keeps me oriented. Right now it just ignores the context.
- 4. **Rewrite the body copy as a specific outfit recommendation.** "Three ways to wear the lightweight stripe" beats a generic brand statement every time.
- 5. **Add a loyalty or account callout** — even a footer line saying "InCircle members: shop early access to new arrivals" would make me feel seen.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard SFMC-rendered, table-based HTML send with no blocking bugs, but carries four QA warnings worth actioning: missing compliance headers, unverifiable tracking links, and an authentication gap introduced by the AgentMail relay. One AMPscript scenario-selection comment is exposed verbatim in the delivered HTML.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links unprobed**
All click-redirect links (via `e.neimanmarcus.com` click-tracking domain) were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URLs, HTTP status codes, and redirect chain depth are unconfirmed. If any destination is a 404 or expired campaign page, it would not surface in this audit.

**Plain-text body is 85% URLs**
QA reports 2,965 of 3,481 plain-text characters are URLs. A near-empty plain-text alternative that is mostly machine-generated link strings is a spam filter signal (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY` and similar rules). The plain-text should be a human-readable summary, not a URL dump.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) and is flagged by accessibility linters. Remove `maximum-scale=1` — it does not meaningfully protect layout in modern email clients.

**Three commented-out viewport variants in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code. No rendering impact, but indicates iterative patching without cleanup. Should be removed before the template is reused.

**CSS checkbox-hack hamburger nav**
The mobile nav uses `:checked` pseudo-class toggling (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`). This is not supported in Gmail, Outlook.com, or Yahoo Mail webmail. Users on those clients will see either a broken hamburger or a permanently collapsed nav — no fallback visible-nav is apparent in the truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Scenario-selection comment delivered to inbox**
The email opens with a multi-line AMPscript documentation block in an HTML comment, including this unresolved marker:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The block also includes: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`. This comment was not stripped before send. While HTML comments are invisible to recipients, they are readable in "View Source" and expose the internal variable wiring, including CRM lookup table names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`). This is an information-disclosure risk for a phishing-aware recipient or security researcher.

**No-op `CONCAT` in `@refid`**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
This concatenates an empty string — it is functionally identical to `@refid = @base_refid`. Likely a vestigial suffix placeholder (e.g., originally `CONCAT(@base_refid,"_v2")`). Not a bug, but a code smell that could silently break if the pattern is copied and the suffix is mistakenly treated as meaningful.

**CRM lookup failure modes not visible**
`@uEm` and `@cmdid` are pulled from `NM_CRM_Feed` via lookup. If a subscriber is absent from that DE, both variables return null. The truncated source does not show null-guards or fallback values for these tokens. If either is used in a link URL, a null value could produce a broken tracking parameter for unmatched subscribers.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header missing**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found
```
The `List-Unsubscribe` header is required for bulk senders under Google/Yahoo's 2024 sender requirements (>5k/day threshold). Its absence — even if caused by the AgentMail relay stripping it — means Gmail may suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and flag the send as non-compliant. This needs to be confirmed against the raw SMTP headers at the MTA level, not just as relayed.

**RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe absent**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found
```
`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` is the companion header required for Gmail/Yahoo one-click compliance. Without it, even a present `List-Unsubscribe` header will not trigger the one-click UI — users get a mailto fallback at best.

**SPF/DKIM authentication unknown**
```
[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found
```
The relay did not surface `Authentication-Results`. SPF and DKIM pass/fail for `e.neimanmarcus.com` cannot be confirmed from this capture. This is a relay artifact, not necessarily a sending failure — but it must be verified at the MTA level before treating the email as authenticated.

All CTAs route through `e.neimanmarcus.com` click-tracking redirects. UTM parameter presence, landing page alignment, and destination HTTP status are unconfirmed. There is no way to assert continuity without probing the redirect chain.
