# Don’t miss out on an extra 20% off top designers
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Here we are again. This is Neiman Marcus's third "extra 20% off the Designer Sale" email in as many days, and even though I've conditioned myself to recognize that red hero at a glance — I practically know it by heart at this point — my thumb was already moving toward archive before I'd consciously decided anything. That's not a great place for a brand to be.
- To be fair: the email itself is well-constructed. The brands in the grid are exactly right for someone like me — Zimmermann, Jacquemus, FRAME, Veronica Beard, A.L.C. These aren't mystery names; these are the labels I compare across NM, Nordstrom, and Saks on any given weekend. The extra 20% is real money when you're looking at a Zimmermann dress that's already marked down. But when you've been in my inbox with this same offer since June 12 — first it was "limited time," then it was an "upgrade," now it's "don't miss out" — the urgency language has been completely hollowed out. It reminds me of Kohl's, where the 20% never truly ends and I've learned to wait rather than act.
- I won't click today. I didn't click on the June 12 send or the June 13 send. There's nothing new here to change that calculus.

## What's working

- The red hero with "EXTRA 20% OFF" is visually unmissable and communicates value in under two seconds — NM has genuinely nailed this template.
- The brand grid — Zimmermann, Jacquemus, Veronica Beard, A.L.C., Cinq à Sept — is exactly the right shortlist for a 47-year-old woman who cross-shops Saks and Nordstrom for these exact labels.
- "SHOP SALE" button is clean, prominent, and not buried below a wall of copy.
- The email renders perfectly — no broken images, no overlapping text, clean sans-serif typography throughout.
- The category shortcuts at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Dresses) give me routing options without forcing me into a search bar.

## What's weak

- This is the third "extra 20% off" send in three days. "Don't miss out" lands as noise when the offer has no visible end date and has now appeared in my inbox on June 12, June 13, and June 14.
- No InCircle mention anywhere. I refuse to buy without points back, and knowing whether this purchase earns InCircle credit — or whether points stack on top of the extra 20% — is the actual lever for my buy decision.
- No beauty content whatsoever. I'm the customer who walks the NM beauty counter first. One Chanel or La Mer banner and I'm clicking without hesitation.
- The "Exclusions Apply" disclaimer sits in the smallest possible type below the hero. At this point I'm actively suspicious of what's excluded, because every one of these sends has buried that somewhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a hard end date on the offer.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" turns this from ambient inbox clutter into a genuine decision trigger. Sephora has been doing this better than NM all spring — their sale countdowns create actual urgency; NM's "don't miss out" doesn't.
- 2. **Add an InCircle member callout** — one line is enough: "InCircle members earn points on every qualifying purchase." That's often the difference between clicking and waiting for the weekend trunk show.
- 3. **Add one beauty brand.** A secondary banner — La Mer, Chanel Beauty, Tom Ford — tells me NM knows I shop the whole store. Macy's does this better in their mixed-category sends, even if the overall execution is messier.
- 4. **Rotate the creative.** Three identical red hero emails is not a drip campaign, it's a copy-paste error. Show a Zimmermann look on a model my age. Give me a close-up of that Jacquemus bag. The brand grid is fine but it goes static fast when I'm seeing it for the third consecutive morning.
- 5. **Lead the subject line with something specific** instead of generic FOMO copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Jacquemus, FRAME — extra 20% off, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your extra 20% ends tonight — top designers, sale prices`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Veronica Beard. A.L.C. Cinq à Sept. Plus earn InCircle points on every qualifying purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Zimmermann, Jacquemus, and 6 more — extra 20% off sale styles. Ends tonight.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Extra 20% off top designers"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound HTML with standard ESP boilerplate, but the QA relay failed to capture authentication and compliance headers, leaving SPF/DKIM status unknown. The plain-text part is nearly entirely URLs, creating a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirect URLs routed through `e.neimanmarcus.com` or equivalent click-tracking domain were not probed. As a result, destination URL validity, redirect chain depth, and UTM parameter presence are unverified for those links.

**Plain-text part is 87% URLs** (5,225 of 6,016 chars). Spam filters — particularly Gmail and Proofpoint — penalize plain-text alternatives that are overwhelmingly URL content with minimal human-readable text. This is a confirmed finding from QA.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags** are present in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate incomplete cleanup from template versioning. No functional rendering risk, but they increase payload size marginally.

**Dual breakpoint media queries at both 480px and 520px** in a second `<style>` block (`.ImgSize`, `.ImgSize2x2`) are layered on top of the primary 600px breakpoint query in the first `<style>` block. This fragmentation increases the chance of conflicting rules on mid-size viewports. No confirmed breakage, but worth consolidating.

**No `role="presentation"` on layout tables** — standard for table-based email layout; not flagged as a hard failure but reduces screen reader hygiene.

**`<title>` tag contains a trailing space**: `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor but reflects template hygiene.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Variable block exposed in HTML comment at top of source:**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ... -->
```
This comment is present in the delivered HTML. It discloses internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`), and lookup logic to any recipient who views source. This is an **information disclosure issue** — not a rendering bug, but a data architecture exposure.

**`@user` assignment appears incomplete:**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256_EMAIL */-->
```
This line is commented out in the delivered source. If `@user` is used in any link parameter for personalization or suppression, its value depends on the uncommented lookup above — the relationship between the two is ambiguous without seeing the full AMPscript block.

**No visible unfired merge tokens** in the truncated source — no `%%variable%%` or `[[variable]]` literals found unresolved.

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay — cannot confirm presence |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | Not found — one-click unsubscribe may not be supported |
| `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM) | **WARN** | Not captured by relay — deliverability authentication status unknown |
| Physical address in footer | Unverified | Source truncated before footer — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM §7 compliance |

The absence of `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is notable: **Gmail and Yahoo Mail require RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe** for bulk senders (≥5k/day). If NM qualifies as a bulk sender on these platforms (almost certainly yes), missing this header risks placement in spam or bulk folders.

The missing `Authentication-Results` is an artifact of the relay capture method, not necessarily a sending failure — but it means DKIM/SPF pass cannot be confirmed from this audit.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 tracked links passed through a click-redirect domain and were not probed. **UTM parameter presence on destination URLs is unverified.** Given the `@refid` and `@base_refid` variables defined in the template comment, it appears campaign attribution is handled via a custom `refid` parameter rather than standard `utm_campaign` / `utm_source`. Whether those parameters survive the redirect chain to the landing page is unconfirmed.
