# Rag & Bone’s fall inspiration
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- This one is quiet, and I liked it more than I expected to. One tall image, a model in a navy military-band jacket with an oversized white bow collar and slim black boots on stone stairs. Then "Uniform Dressing" and two buttons. It reads like a page from a magazine, not a flyer. After the forest-green Friends & Family creative Neiman's sent three times this week, this is a relief.
- It also gives me almost nothing to act on. I don't see a price, a percentage, a product name, or a points or loyalty mention. I'm a woman who won't put a card down without points back, so an email with no reason to buy today is easy to admire and then close. The jacket looks great, but I can't tell if it's Rag & Bone or what it costs, and nothing in it is clickable to that specific piece as far as I can see.
- I'd read it and mark it as read. I wouldn't forward it. If I were already in the mood for a fall blazer I might tap "Shop Rag & Bone," but nothing here creates that mood.

## What's working

- The restraint. There's no shouting and no countdown clock. It treats me like a grown woman with taste, and Saks does that more often than Macy's does.
- The hero is a full-length shot on real architecture, and the whole outfit is visible. The navy jacket and black boots are something I'd wear.
- "Uniform Dressing" is a smart fall idea for my age group. I want a work wardrobe I don't have to think about.
- The layout is clean. The eye goes straight to the photo, then the headline, then the buttons.
- The two outlined buttons are easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no price and no member benefit. Compare Macy's "extra 25% off" email, which was pushy but at least told me what I'd save.
- The copy never says what the jacket is or who makes it. The subject says Rag & Bone, but the body doesn't mention it until a button label.
- "Shop Rag & Bone" and "Shop Contemporary" are generic. Neither points at the jacket I'm looking at.
- The bottom half is app download, style advisor, store finder and account links. It's tidy, but it's filler. The style advisor line is the only thing that feels like a real service, and it's buried.
- The buttons are pale grey text on white, and the thin type is hard to read.
- It's a fourth Neiman's email in a week, and the second in two days that leans on editorial mood without a hook. On the 27th it was Bottega Veneta and on the 28th it's "Between-season style additions." They blur together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to act. Name the jacket, show a price, and link the hero image and a button straight to it. Even "Shop the jacket, $495" would beat "Shop Contemporary."
- 2. Give me a loyalty line. If InCircle points or a bonus-points event applies, say so under the headline. I shop where I get points back, and Sephora does this better with its point multipliers right up top.
- 3. Turn "Match with a personal style advisor" into the main call to action for this theme. A uniform wardrobe is exactly what an advisor helps with.
- 4. Add two or three more product tiles below the hero, such as the jacket, a trouser and a boot, each with a name and price. Ulta would have done this, and for once I'd want the extra rows.
- 5. Darken the button text and make the labels specific.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three pieces to build a work-week wardrobe, plus a stylist to help.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn points on Rag & Bone this week. Shop the jacket and the boot.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Missing language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`, which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Checkbox-hack nav:** the mobile menu uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` with `display:none` defaults. Gmail app, Outlook and some webmail clients don't support `:checked`, so the nav is inert or hidden there. Whether a fallback exists can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Fragile selectors:** the media queries use exact-match `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. These stop matching if an element carries more than one class, and Gmail's class handling is unreliable. Any element with a combined class would not get its responsive rule.
- **Legacy CSS:** `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` are legacy, and `-webkit-animation: bugfix` is an iOS9 workaround. All of it is dead weight.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. This is minor.
- **Unverified:** image alt text and image-to-text ratio can't be assessed because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
