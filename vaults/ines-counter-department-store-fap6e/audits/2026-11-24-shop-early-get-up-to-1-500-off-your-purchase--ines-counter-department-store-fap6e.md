# Shop early & get up to $1,500 off your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the black box with the red border again. I've had that panel from Neiman's since 11/19, and today it's back for the fourth or fifth time. This version is at least honest. It has one number, a spend-and-save table, and a code. It's cleaner than this morning's "3 ways to save" stack, which tried to cram three offers into the same panel. The Designer Sale email from earlier today is already sitting in my inbox, so this one feels like a second helping.
- I did read the table, because it tells me the real deal. $250 saves $50, $500 saves $125, and $5,000 saves $1,500. The headline says "$1,500 off" in huge type, but the typical saving is 20 to 25 percent. The big number is the top tier, and most people won't get there. I don't love that, but the table is right there, not buried. The fine print is the part that bothers me: sale and reduced-price merchandise don't qualify. That means it doesn't stack with the Designer Sale I was just emailed about.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I were already planning a La Mer or Maison Francis Kurkdjian purchase. Beauty is explicitly included, which is rare. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and wait for the Last Call.

## What's working

- "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" is the line that speaks to me. Sephora and Ulta never make me wonder whether beauty is excluded. Nordstrom and Saks often do.
- The spend-and-save table is clear and easy to read. Macy's would have buried this in a coupon and made me confirm to get it.
- The code THANKFUL is visible, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The product grid mixes beauty (La Mer, Maison Francis Kurkdjian) with clothing, shoes and bags. It looks like a department store, not a clearance rack.
- The dress and the shoes look like they're aimed at someone my age. The edit feels adult.

## What's weak

- The headline promises $1,500 off, but you need to spend $5,000 for it. The real offer is 20 to 25 percent, and the hero oversells it.
- Nothing in the email mentions Neiman Marcus' InCircle program or points. I won't use a card that doesn't earn points, and there's no sign of whether points stack or are excluded. For a loyalty-minded shopper that's a miss.
- The sender name "Neiman's Black Friday" is a campaign, not the brand. It also arrives right after the Designer Sale send, and the cadence is wearing on me.
- The grid items have no prices and no links I can tell apart. They're just brand names under the photos. Beauty gets two products out of nine.
- The "Exclusions Apply" line in the hero is small and low-contrast gray on black. I can barely read it at this size.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show a realistic headline: "Save up to 25% on regular-price styles, including Beauty." Keep the table, but lead with the typical savings, not the top tier.
- 2. Add one line on InCircle points and whether they earn on top of the discount.
- 3. Make the exclusions legible. If the Designer Sale items don't qualify, say so up top.
- 4. Add a beauty-specific tile, such as "Shop La Mer," with a price.
- 5. Space out the sends. Two near-identical Black Friday panels in one day is too much.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Code THANKFUL: up to 25% off, beauty too`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $250, save $50. Includes beauty and fragrance. Ends 11/28.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Content before DOCTYPE:** a large HTML comment (the ESP variable block) comes before `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Some clients and parsers treat content before the DOCTYPE as a trigger for quirks mode.
- **Checkbox hamburger menu:** the mobile nav uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table`. These checkbox-hack patterns aren't supported in Gmail and several Outlook clients. The visible source doesn't show a fallback.
- **Other items:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space. The commented-out viewport variants (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`) are dead code.
