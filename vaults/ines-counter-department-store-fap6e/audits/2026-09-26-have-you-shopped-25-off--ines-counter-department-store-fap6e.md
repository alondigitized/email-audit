# Have you shopped 25% off?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Have you shopped 25% off?" is the reminder nudge for the exact same Friends & Family event I already logged twice this week — the 24th's "Reminder: $50 gift card for every $200" and now this. Same forest-green banner, same "FRIENDS & FAMILY" wordmark treatment, just swapped the gift-card mechanic for the straight 25% off + double points pitch. It's competent — this brand rarely embarrasses itself the way Kohl's does — but I'm three sends deep into the same event now and my patience for repetition is not infinite.
- The nine-tile grid is actually well-curated for someone like me: D&G, A.L.C., Theia, Cult Gaia, Gianvito Rossi, Brunello Cucinelli — that's a real mix of the dress-up pieces I'd wear to a fall dinner and the day-to-day polish (the A.L.C. trench, the Cult Gaia bag) I'd actually buy. InCircle double points called out is the one line that makes me feel like a known customer rather than a cold-blast recipient — Neiman's does that consistently better than Macy's ever does with its Star Rewards mumbo jumbo.
- Would I click? Mildly — the Cult Gaia bag or the A.L.C. trench, maybe. Forward to a friend? No, this is a "have you seen this" nudge, not a "look at this" moment — that was the D&G editorial two days ago. This one gets skimmed and archived.

## What's working

- The InCircle "Double Points" callout in the black bar is real loyalty language, not generic filler — that's the beauty-counter-regular hook that keeps me reading.
- Product grid mixes women's and men's across a real range of luxury names — D&G, Cult Gaia, Brunello Cucinelli — so it doesn't feel like a dumping ground.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA instead of being buried in six paragraphs of fine print below — I respect that even if the fine print itself is still a wall.
- Clean single-column layout, plenty of white space — no Sephora-style tile-wall overload, no Kohl's red-box fatigue.

## What's weak

- This is the third Friends & Family email in under 48 hours (9/24 reminder, 9/25 gift-card version, now this) — the subject "Have you shopped 25% off?" practically admits fatigue is the strategy.
- No hero model, no editorial shot — just typography on a green field. After the gorgeous D&G lace shot two days ago, this feels like the lazy version of the same event.
- "select styles" next to 25% off is the honesty tax — I don't know what's actually included until I click, and the fine print below confirms sale/reduced-price and beauty/fragrance are excluded anyway.
- Nine product tiles with zero prices — at my level I don't need prices splashed everywhere, but a "from $X" on at least the hero pieces would help me decide whether to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "reminder" framing entirely for this cadence — if I'm getting a third touch on the same event, lead with something new (a specific price point, a single hero product, or genuinely fresh imagery) instead of "have you shopped yet?"
- 2. Bring back an editorial hero shot like the D&G lace campaign — typography-only banners are the weakest asset this brand has when a real model shot is sitting right there in the archive.
- 3. Add a countdown or explicit "ends 9/30" in the hero, not just buried in the fine print — Nordstrom does urgency better because it's visible above the fold.
- 4. Reorder the product grid to lead with the item most likely to convert (the Cult Gaia bag or Gianvito Rossi mules) rather than alphabetical-by-brand.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 25% off ends Wednesday — shop before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Cult Gaia bag you saved, 25% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, InCircle members earn Double Points through 9/30.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals from Theia, A.L.C. and Brunello Cucinelli — 25% off.`
