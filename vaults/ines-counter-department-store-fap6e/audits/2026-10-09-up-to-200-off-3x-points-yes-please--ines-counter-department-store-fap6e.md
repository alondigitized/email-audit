# Up to $200 off + 3x Points? Yes, please...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Neiman's offer email I've had in four days, and I recognized it before I opened it. The offer is the same ladder I got on 10/6 ("Grab it now: $200 off your purchase") and 10/7 ("You could save $50–$200"). It uses the same lavender block and the same NMCIRCLE code. On 10/8 I got "Up to $200 off + 3x InCircle points" and then the quiet "Prep for the cold months." Today's subject is that 10/8 offer with "Yes, please..." added. The extra words don't change the offer, and "Yes, please..." sounds like it's talking for me.
- The email itself is clean. The ladder is easy to read, and the black bar saying InCircle members earn Triple Points is what I look for. The nine-product grid is a pretty row of designer pieces: Ramy Brook, rag & bone, Jimmy Choo, Tod's. But there are no prices and no beauty anywhere, and I'm a counter regular. I'd shop this only if I already had a $500 outerwear purchase planned, and I might. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and wait for the next quiet one.
- Compared with the others, this is tidier than the Kohl's "Extra 20% off + $20 off your $100+ purchase 🎉" send, which stacks deals. Sephora Insider's "FREE Dr. Groot trial size with your min. spend 💫" hid its minimum. Neiman's puts every threshold in the open, so it's the more honest of the two. The Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email shouted at me, and Neiman's doesn't. The gap is frequency and sameness, not tone.

## What's working

- The spend ladder ($200→$50, $500→$100, $750→$150, $1,000+→$200) is readable at a glance. I can do the math in my head, which Sephora's "min. spend" didn't let me do.
- The black bar, "Plus, InCircle Members earn Triple Points," sits right under the hero. That's the tier signal I'm looking for, and it isn't buried.
- The tone is calm. It says "Limited time" with no countdown clock and no all-caps shouting, so it treats me like an adult.
- The black SHOP NOW button on lavender is easy to find, and the tap targets are big enough for my thumb.
- The nine-brand grid reads as a curated designer edit, with named brands under each image. That feels like Neiman's and not a Macy's wall of product.

## What's weak

- I've seen this offer, this lavender block and this code four times this week. It's wearing out the InCircle goodwill.
- The fine print carries the real deadline (ends 10/12 at 10:00 PM CT). The hero only says "Limited time," so the urgency isn't credible where I'd read it.
- The products have no prices, and "Exclusions Apply" has no link. I can't tell whether the Jimmy Choo bag or the Tod's boot even qualifies.
- There's no beauty in it at all. Neiman's has a counter I'd shop, and this is all apparel and shoes. There's no sign it knows I'm a repeat customer.
- The terms are a wall of tiny type: bonus points not earned on beauty services, and Bergdorf Goodman excluded. It reads like an escape hatch, not a perk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending the same offer five days running. Rotate one hero a week, and include beauty or a gift-with-purchase for InCircle members.
- 2. Put the end date in the hero ("Ends Monday 10/12") and link "Exclusions" to a plain-language list.
- 3. Show prices and tag the pieces that qualify. A tier-aware line like "You're 120 points from your next reward" would do more than a generic bar.
- 4. Make the CTA specific ("Shop outerwear," "Shop boots") and tie it to the season.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Monday: up to $200 off + 3x InCircle points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 3x points weekend: leather jackets & boots`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $500, save $100 with code NMCIRCLE. Ends 10/12 at 10 PM CT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Triple InCircle points on outerwear, boots and handbags through Monday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML has no confirmed breakage, but the source was truncated and the QA run couldn't verify headers or tracking links. The one concrete finding is that internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) personalization documentation shipped in the delivered HTML.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** the QA check skipped 32 tracking links because they use click-redirect domains, so the QA data doesn't confirm redirect resolution or destinations. This is an observation about coverage, not a defect.
- The truncated source doesn't show any `href` values, so I can't flag specific malformed or broken URLs.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the long `<!--REQUIRED variables: ...-->` comment sits ahead of `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Content before the DOCTYPE can push some older clients (e.g., legacy Outlook/IE-based engines) into quirks mode.
- **Leftover code:** three commented-out viewport metas (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`, and one with `user-scalable=no`) remain in the source.
- **Hamburger menu:** the mobile nav uses the checkbox hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.hamburger` / `.menu`) inside an `@media (max-width: 600px)` block. Clients that strip `:checked` or `<input>` elements, including Gmail, will drop the interactive menu. Whether a static fallback exists isn't visible in the truncated source.
- **Class selectors:** the mobile rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`. These fail on any element carrying more than one class. I can't confirm from the truncated source whether any element does.
- **`<title>`:** `Neiman Marcus ` has a trailing space and no campaign-specific text. This is minor.
- Alt text, image dimensions, and the HTML text-to-image ratio can't be assessed from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal documentation exposed:** the opening comment ships SFMC variable documentation to every recipient's inbox source. Examples are `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)` and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`. This leaks data-extension names and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), plus the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option. It doesn't render, but it is information disclosure and clutter.
- **Comment syntax:** the comment contains unbalanced parentheses (`@user Lookup(...))`), which suggests it was pasted and not maintained.
- No unresolved visible tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) appear in the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found:** the QA check says the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. This is unconfirmed and may be a capture artifact. Verify against the raw message from the sender.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found:** same caveat. One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules, so it is worth checking.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found:** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The QA tool attributes this to the relay.
- The visible unsubscribe link and the physical address aren't in the truncated source, so they can't be confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No issues confirmed. Destination URLs and UTM parameters aren't visible in the truncated source, and the tracking links weren't probed. The subject's "Up to $200 off + 3x Points" offer can't be matched to a landing page from the available data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC `REQUIRED variables` comment from the production template. Use a build step that removes `<!-- -->` blocks, or move the notes into template metadata.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and delete the dead commented viewport tags.
3. Move any comment content below the DOCTYPE, or remove it.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` plus SPF/DKIM/DMARC in the original message headers, since the relay may have hidden them.
5. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with tracking links resolved, so UTM params, unsubscribe link, footer address, and alt text can be checked.
6. Provide a non-`:checked` fallback for the hamburger nav, e.g. a plain link row, for clients that don't support it.
