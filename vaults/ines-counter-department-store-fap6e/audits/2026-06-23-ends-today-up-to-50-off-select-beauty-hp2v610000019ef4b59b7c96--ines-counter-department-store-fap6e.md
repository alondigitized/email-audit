# ENDS TODAY: up to 50% off select beauty 🫣
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- *"Ends today"* after *"chapter is closing"* yesterday — I see what Sephora is doing, and the campaign logic is at least coherent this time. The June 22 email told me the sale was wrapping up, this one delivers the final-day push. Fine. But they handed me the exact same "Major Summer Deals" hero for what feels like the fourth consecutive email, and this one still has the catalog-overload problem I flagged on June 20 — I counted at least six distinct modules before hitting the footer, ranging from the core sale to a text-message opt-in to a "Just Arrived" section that has nothing to do with urgency.
- The 50% off offer IS real enough that I'd pause. Fifty percent at Sephora with Beauty Insider points stacking is actually a reason to open my wallet. But the "select beauty" qualifier is doing a lot of work, and past Sephora "up to" sale emails have taught me that the hero percentage applies to maybe three brands I don't care about. So my hand goes to the mouse and then hesitates.
- I'd click through but only to the sale page, not because this email earned the click — because the discount is large enough to overcome the noise. I would not forward this to a friend. I'd screenshot the offer percentage and text it instead.

## What's working

- **Offer size stops the scroll.** 50% off is a number big enough to override my fatigue with this campaign — and at Sephora that can genuinely mean something on a La Mer or Tatcha item.
- **Subject-to-content alignment is better than June 22.** Last time "ENDS TODAY"-level urgency pointed me at an app-download push. This one actually loads the sale. That's a low bar, but Sephora cleared it.
- **Beauty Insiders Get More module is present.** I noticed it mid-email — bonus points callout, same-day delivery perk. As someone who tracks points obsessively, seeing that the loyalty layer is active matters.
- **The 25% off tier below the hero creates a fallback offer.** If the 50% items are garbage, there's a second reason to stay in the email. That two-tier structure is smart.

## What's weak

- **Recycled hero for the fourth time this cycle.** "Major Summer Deals" was the June 20 hero, the June 22 hero, and now this. Neiman Marcus would have swapped creative for the final-day push — a countdown, a different color treatment, *something* to signal the stakes are higher today.
- **Catalog overload.** "Hear about the Important Stuff First" (text opt-in), "Spot it. Shop it.," "Just Arrived" — these modules have no business being in an urgency email. Strip them. They dilute the one thing I need to feel: *the clock is running.*
- **"Select beauty" does real damage to trust.** I've been here before. The 50% number in the subject brings me in, and the "select" on the body copy reminds me that my actual wishlist items are probably excluded. Macy's does the same "LAST CHANCE" bait-and-switch, and I've learned to read these with skepticism.
- **🫣 emoji reads younger than me.** The peek-eye is TikTok vocabulary. It's not offensive, but it's not *me*, and it signals Sephora is not thinking about which segment is actually opening this email at 7am on a Thursday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four of the six modules.** Keep: hero sale offer, Beauty Insiders loyalty callout, one product carousel of the actual 50%-off items. Drop: text opt-in, "Spot it. Shop it.," "Just Arrived." If it's truly the last day, treat it like the last day.
- 2. **Swap the hero creative.** New color, new image, a visible countdown timer. Signal that something changed since yesterday — because right now this looks like a resend of June 22 with the subject swapped.
- 3. **Name at least two specific brands at 50% off in the email body.** Show me that NARS or Drunk Elephant is in the sale and I'm clicking immediately. "Select beauty" gives me nothing to act on.
- 4. **Drop the peek emoji, tighten the subject for the audience.** The offer is strong enough on its own without a gimmick emoji that skews to a younger demographic than me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends today: 50% off Tatcha, NARS, and more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours — Beauty Insider sale ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Beauty Insider points still stack on top.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These are the 50%-off brands worth grabbing before midnight.`


This suppresses pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android, an accessibility barrier for low-vision users. WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of function. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Aggressive `text-size-adjust: none` on all elements:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;

}
```
Applied globally rather than scoped to the outer wrapper. Disabling text-size-adjust on `body` or `div` can override OS-level accessibility font scaling. Standard practice is to apply only to `body` or the outermost wrapper table.
