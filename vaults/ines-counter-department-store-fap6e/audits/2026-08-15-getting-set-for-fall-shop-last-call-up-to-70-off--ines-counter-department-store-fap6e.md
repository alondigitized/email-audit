# Getting set for fall? Shop Last Call (up to 70% off)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- There it is again — that crimson block. This is the fourth time this week I've had Neiman's shout "LAST CALL" at me in that same shouty red, and now they've stacked "EXTRA 25% OFF" on top of "UP TO 70% OFF" like more numbers make it more convincing. I get it, the sale is real, but at some point the escalating percentages start to read like static.
- Once I get past the banner, though, this is actually one of their better merchandising grids — Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Theia, real designer names with real product shots, not vague lifestyle imagery. That's the part that would make me scroll. But nine products laid out with zero pricing, zero fabric or fit detail, and a maze of six red "SHOP" buttons at the bottom is a lot of homework before I'd click anything.
- Compared to Saks, who at least varies their hero art between sends, this is Neiman's running the identical crimson-banner playbook I already saw on 8/13 ("Have you shopped our Last Call Sale?") — same red, same "LAST CALL," just a slightly bigger number this time. I'd scroll past the banner to look at the Balmain jacket and the Manolo mules, but I wouldn't click. This one gets skimmed and archived, not forwarded.

## What's working

- The designer name grid — Isabel Marant, Balmain, Bugatchi, Manolo Blahnik — is genuine catnip for someone who cross-shops Saks and Nordstrom; I recognize every one of these labels.
- Clean product photography, consistent white/gray backgrounds, nothing broken or overlapping.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the button instead of buried in six-point fine print at the very bottom — I appreciate that Neiman's doesn't hide it entirely, even if it's small.
- Fine print at the footer spells out the actual mechanics (25-70% off, savings reflected at checkout, no adjustments for prior purchases, ends 9/7) — that's more honest than most.

## What's weak

- The crimson "LAST CALL" banner is the exact beat Neiman's ran on 8/13 and the exact color/tone they've used for their designer-percentage banners on 8/11 and 8/12 — I'm fatigued on it before I even scroll.
- Zero prices anywhere. Not on the hero, not under a single one of the nine products. I can't tell if "up to 70% off" means the Balmain jacket is $400 or $1,400.
- No member/loyalty callout at all — I'm a "refuses a card without points back" shopper, and there's nothing here about InCircle points, tier status, or double-points days on this sale. Compare that to Kohl's, who at least always shows my rewards-member status bar even when the offer itself is stale.
- Six identical red "SHOP" buttons at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Men's, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, Dresses) is visual overload — it's the same "everything, everywhere" trap Macy's falls into, just in Neiman's red instead of Macy's red.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "starting at" figure under at least the hero products — "Balmain jacket, now $XXX" does more work than another red banner.
- 2. Retire the crimson Last Call banner for a few sends — I've now seen it 8/13 and 8/15 this week alone; alternate with the cleaner black-block "new arrivals" treatment they used on 8/13's other send.
- 3. Add an InCircle points or status line somewhere near the top — even a small "Earn double points on Last Call today" would make this feel like it's talking to a member, not a stranger.
- 4. Cut the six-button CTA wall to two or three — Women's Sale and Shoes Sale, not every department restated.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Last Call favorites: extra 25% off starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Isabel Marant — up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 25% off select styles, ends 9/7 at 10pm CT`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `InCircle members: your Last Call picks are here`
