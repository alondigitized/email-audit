# Reminder: Get up to $250 off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Third time this exact tiered box has landed in my inbox — 8/26, 8/27, and now this "reminder." At least this send finally shows me product instead of just a code and a math table, so the mango-yellow banner up top with SHOPFALL and the $400/$1,000 tiers feels almost like set dressing for the actual point: nine pieces below it, and this is a genuinely nice edit — Mac Duggal and Badgley Mischka gowns, rag & bone denim, a Jacquemus basket bag, Jimmy Choo platforms. That's a real merchandising effort, more than Neiman's has bothered with in the last two sends combined.
- But it's still a reminder email for an offer I've now seen three days running, and the fine print at the bottom kills my appetite before I even scroll that far: beauty and fragrance excluded, no stacking, Bergdorf Goodman excluded, employees excluded. For someone like me who counter-hops for beauty specifically, "does not apply to beauty, fragrance" sitting in that legal block is the brand quietly telling me this offer isn't really for my favorite category. Sephora would never bury that — they'd have led with what's included. I'm not clicking SHOP NOW again; I already made my call on this promo two days ago. Not forwarding, not deleting either — just marking it read and moving on.

## What's working

- The product grid is the best thing Neiman's has shown me all week — real brand names (Jimmy Choo, Amiri, Tod's) with clean, consistent studio shots, not just another discount box.
- "LIMITED TIME" and "Ends 9/3/26" give this an actual deadline, unlike some of the vaguer Neiman's sends I've seen.
- The tier table (spend $400/save $100, spend $1,000/save $250) is at least legible and not buried — I can do that math in two seconds.
- Style-advisor and app CTAs at the bottom nod to the personal-shopper relationship Neiman's built its name on, even if it's a small gesture.

## What's weak

- This is the third consecutive day I've gotten this identical spend/save structure — Neiman's own history shows 8/26 and 8/27 ran it too, and calling this one a "reminder" just admits the fatigue rather than fixing it.
- Beauty and fragrance are explicitly excluded in the fine print — as someone whose whole persona is counter-hopping, that's the one line that tells me this email wasn't really built with me in mind.
- No loyalty/InCircle tier language anywhere — Saks Fifth Avenue at least gestures at "exclusive access" tone for its cardholders; this reads like it could've gone to anyone with an email address.
- "Exclusions Apply" with zero detail up top, then a wall of fine print at the very bottom — that's the buried-conditions move I already flagged on Macy's confirm-now email, just with more patience required to scroll to it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with what's actually new — swap the hero for the product grid (or a hybrid) so the "1,000+ new styles" energy from 8/27 carries into this send instead of repeating the same discount box for a third day.
- 2. Call out beauty/fragrance exclusions in plain type near the offer, not four screens down in legal copy — I'd rather know upfront than feel misled after I've engaged.
- 3. Add one line of InCircle loyalty language ("InCircle members save an extra X" or point-earning mention) — right now this could be any department store's generic promo.
- 4. Retire "reminder" framing for a promo repeated three days straight — it just calls attention to the fatigue instead of masking it.
