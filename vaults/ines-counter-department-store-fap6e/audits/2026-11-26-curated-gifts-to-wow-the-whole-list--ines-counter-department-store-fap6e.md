# Curated gifts to wow the whole list
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a Neiman's email that doesn't shout at me. After a week of the black box with the red border, including the three-offer "$1,500 off + extra 25% off + Designer Sale" send that landed this morning, this one is just a white page and a gift edit. It's the closest thing to a counter experience I've had from them since the Saint Laurent roundup on 11/25. I'd open it for that reason alone.
- But a beauty-counter person can't act on it. There's no offer, no deadline and no points or tier mention. It's Black Friday week, and the same inbox has been telling me about $1,500 off. A gift guide with zero price anchoring, and nothing saying whether my Jo Malone hand cream counts toward anything, is pretty to look at and hard to use. The product labels are also tiny, and I'd be squinting on my phone.
- Would I click? Maybe the Jo Malone or the Guerlain lip oil, out of curiosity. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read, and keep the calm layout in mind for next time.

## What's working

- Calm, editorial layout. After the black-box sends it reads as respect for the customer. Sephora's sixteenth email in two weeks is nowhere near this restrained.
- "Grand Gestures" is a clear, elegant hero. The line "Make every gift unforgettable, big or small" fits the season without shouting.
- The numbered 01–10 product edit is easy to scan. Jo Malone and Guerlain are the two beauty items, and they're the ones I noticed first.
- The designer mix (Bottega Veneta, Louboutin, Polo Ralph Lauren) signals the access I expect from Neiman's.
- The header nav (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) is clean and tappable.

## What's weak

- There's no offer anywhere. Macy's sends me "extra 25% off" with a deadline, and Kohl's has Kohl's Cash. Here I get nothing on a Black Friday week.
- No loyalty or points signal. I won't put a card down without points back, and there's no InCircle mention to tell me whether these gifts earn anything.
- The product captions are very small, and there are no prices. I'm guessing at what a Bottega clutch costs against a $38 hand cream.
- The only CTAs are two small underlined text links, "Shop All Gifts" and "Shop Gifts for Kids." There's no real button, so the tap targets are weak.
- Only two of the ten items are beauty. The sunglasses block ("Road Ready," IC Berlin) is off-theme for a gifting email and reads like filler.
- The kid's accordion toy and the boy's outfit are scattered among adult items, so the gift guide feels unfocused for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one clear line of benefit under the hero, such as "Earn bonus InCircle points on gifts through Sunday," plus a real button. Right now there's no reason to act today.
- 2. Add prices to the captions and make the text larger.
- 3. Split the edit by recipient or budget (Beauty & fragrance, Under $100, Under $250). I'd shop "Beauty gifts" immediately.
- 4. Cut the sunglasses block or move it below a gifting-specific module.
- 5. Replace the underlined links with proper buttons.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gifts worth giving: beauty, bags & more from $38`
- **Subject Alt B:** `10 gifts we'd wrap first, plus bonus points`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jo Malone, Guerlain and more. Earn bonus points through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our editors' picks by price, with free gift wrap on every order.`

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Curated gifts to wow the whole list"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML Transitional email with media-query responsive CSS. The source is truncated, so body-level checks (links, images, alt text, footer) could not be confirmed. The QA run produced 0 hard failures and 4 warnings. Three of those warnings come from capture limits of the AgentMail relay, not from confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 17 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their resolution, redirect chains and destinations are unverified.
- The plain-text part is 3199/3707 characters URLs (86%, above the 70% threshold). This is consistent with long tracked URLs in the text alternative. It can raise spam-filter scores, and it hurts readability for text-only clients.
- I can't assess individual `href` values, because the truncated source ends inside `<style>`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on clients that honor it. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language declared:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **CSS-only hamburger menu:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` pattern, with `.menu` set to `display:none` and `visibility:hidden`, only works where `:checked` is supported. Gmail and Outlook don't support it. Whether a fallback nav exists is outside the visible source, so verify it.
- **Brittle selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`-style exact-match attribute selectors fail if a class list ever contains more than one class.
- `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is non-descriptive. This is minor.
- Alt text, image dimensions and the dark-mode handling are not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The delivered HTML opens with a developer comment block listing internal AMPscript variables and Salesforce Marketing Cloud Data Extension names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, and the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`. It also includes the placeholder `/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */`.
- This is an information leak of internal data-schema names to every recipient. The comment is also left unresolved ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"), which suggests the template was not cleaned before send.
- No unrendered `%%…%%` or `@var` tokens are visible in the displayed portion outside comments.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are not found. The QA note says the relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. If they are genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (one-click unsubscribe) are not met. Check the raw message at the sending ESP.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` are unknown. This is a relay limitation, not evidence of failure.
- The visible HTML does not show the footer, so the physical postal address and the unsubscribe link can't be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The 17 tracked links were not probed, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters are visible in the truncated source. The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the header comment suggest campaign tracking is carried through the redirects, but I can't confirm that from the evidence.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the developer comment block** (variables, DE names, scenario notes) from the production send. Use AMPscript comments (`%%[ /* */ ]%%`) so they are removed at render time.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. **Verify the raw headers** at the ESP or a seed inbox: `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment.
4. **Probe the 17 tracked links** with a redirect-following check, and confirm that final URLs land on valid pages with consistent UTM or refid parameters.
5. **Shorten the plain-text part.** Reduce the tracked-URL weight, or trim the text alternative.
6. **Test the hamburger nav fallback** in Gmail and Outlook, where the checkbox hack is unsupported.
7. **Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML** to cover alt text, the footer and the link inventory.
