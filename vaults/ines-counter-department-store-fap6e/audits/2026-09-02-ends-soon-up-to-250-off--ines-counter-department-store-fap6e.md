# Ends soon: up to $250 off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Oh, it's the mango banner. Again. This is Neiman's — same brand that put me through the entire $250-off tiered saga starting 8/26, then gave me a nice breather with the crossbody roundup on 8/30 and the "1,000+ new arrivals" grid on 8/31, and even redeemed themselves yesterday with the Cleo Wade editorial. Now we're right back to "SPEND $400 / SAVE $100, SPEND $1,000 / SAVE $250" — the exact same math table, same pale-yellow block, just wearing an "ends soon" trench coat this time.
- Here's the thing though — the product grid underneath actually saves it a little. Akris punto trousers, a Chiara Boni gown, Jimmy Choo tote, rag & bone velvet flats — that's a real edit, not filler, and it reads like something a Saks or a Bergdorf's would curate for someone like me. But I've now seen this banner four times in a week and a half, and fatigue is fatigue no matter how nice the shoes are.
- I'm not clicking on the banner itself — I've mentally filed "SHOPFALL" as a code I already know. I might scroll to the product grid out of habit since Jimmy Choo caught my eye, but I'm not forwarding this to anyone. My friends already know Neiman's runs this sale on a loop.

## What's working

- The product grid is genuinely well-curated — Akris punto, Chiara Boni, Jimmy Choo, rag & bone all read as "counter regular" appropriate, not mass-market filler.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the offer instead of being buried in six-point fine print — that's more honest than most of what lands in my inbox.
- Clean nav bar and logo at top — no clutter, no banner ad stacking, unlike some of the Kohl's sends I've seen with the Martha Stewart cross-promo crammed in.
- The tiered spend table is at least legible this time — two columns, no squinting required.

## What's weak

- This is the same tiered banner and code (SHOPFALL) I already saw on 9/1's "Limited time left to get up to $250 off" — nothing here differentiates it beyond swapping "ends soon" language in.
- "Treat yourself and save on styles you love" is generic filler copy sitting right after a big yellow banner — it does nothing to bridge me into the product grid.
- Beauty and fragrance are explicitly excluded per the fine print — for a counter regular like me, that's the one category I actually shop, and it's the one they carved out.
- No loyalty/InCircle callout anywhere on this send — Neiman's knows I'm a member, and there's zero acknowledgment of points or tier status, which feels like a missed "known customer" moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire the mango banner for a cycle. Sephora's Marc Jacobs send led with "Free Gift" as the hook instead of a math table — Neiman's could lead with the product edit (that gown, those Jimmy Choos) and drop the offer into a smaller strip below it. The grid is the actual selling point here.
- 2. Surface InCircle status or points-back language somewhere near the offer — I refuse to use a card without points back, and this email doesn't even try to remind me what I'm earning.
- 3. If beauty/fragrance is excluded, say so plainly near the top instead of line four of the fine print — don't let me find out at checkout.
- 4. Vary the creative format between sends — three near-identical tiered banners in eight days reads as "the same email," even when the code or headline text changes.
