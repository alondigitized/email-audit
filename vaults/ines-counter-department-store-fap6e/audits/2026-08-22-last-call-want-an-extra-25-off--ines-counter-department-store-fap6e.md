# Last Call: Want an extra 25% off?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one I'd actually open my wallet for. The red "EXTRA 25% OFF... LAST CALL" banner is doing exactly what it should — huge, unmissable, one clean number, one clean CTA. And then the grid underneath actually shows me real designer names: Balmain, Dries Van Noten, Manolo Blahnik, Zimmermann. That's the Neiman's I subscribe for — not another points-math worksheet.
- That said, my brain immediately does the subtraction Neiman's is hoping I won't: "up to 70% off" then another 25% off *that* — so what am I actually paying? The fine print at the bottom answers it ("savings of 25-70% off regular prices... interim markdowns may have been taken") but that's after I've scrolled past six product tiles and eight red CTA buttons. Compare this to yesterday's triple-points gift-card email, which put its math right in the hero — this one buries its math at the footer.
- Would I click? Yes, on the Zimmermann skirt or the Manolo mules specifically — those are impulse-worthy. Would I forward it? Maybe to my sister who's also a Last Call regular, but I wouldn't send it to a friend as "you have to see this," it's still just a sale email, well-executed but not surprising.

## What's working

- The hero is unambiguous — 25% off, "Last Call" branding front and center, one white "SHOP SALE" button that pops against the red.
- Product grid is genuinely aspirational for me — Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Dries Van Noten read as real designer access, not clearance junk.
- Deadline is specific and real ("Ends 9/7/26 at 10:00 PM CT") — not vague "hurry" language, that's honest urgency.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" — that's a nice touch that Nordstrom does well too and Neiman's is finally leaning into.

## What's weak

- The stacked math (up to 70% off + extra 25% off) forces me to do arithmetic in my head or dig to the footer — Nordstrom Anniversary emails just tell me the final price.
- Six near-identical red "SHOP [category]" buttons in a grid at the bottom is overkill — feels like Macy's-style button spam, not the restrained Neiman's tone I liked in Wednesday's "Easy Does It" email.
- No loyalty/InCircle point mention at all here, which is odd given how hard yesterday's Triple Points email leaned on it — inconsistent member treatment week to week.
- "Exclusions Apply" sitting right under the CTA is a small deflate — I know it's necessary, but it's the first thing my eye catches after the button.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number near the hero, not just percentages — even "styles from $XX" would stop the mental math and match how Saks does it on final sale.
- 2. Cut the six-button "SHOP [category]" grid down to two or three — Zimmermann/Balmain/Manolo browsers don't need a "Shop Kids" button in their face.
- 3. Reintroduce InCircle points language somewhere small, even a footer line — after yesterday's Triple Points send, silence here reads like the loyalty program only shows up when it's convenient for them.
- 4. Move "Ends 9/7/26" up near the hero, not just the footer fine print — it's a good deadline, don't waste it.
