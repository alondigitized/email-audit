- Crown Affair is exactly the right brand to feature — it's cult-level, counter-adjacent, and the founder-story angle gives it credibility I can't dismiss.

- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero headline, pull quotes, then CTA. Macy's beauty stack could never pull this off — they'd throw 40 products at me with no editorial spine.
- "3 Makeup Tips, 1 Brilliant Artist" is structured well — numbered, specific, not a wall of product thumbnails.
- Beauty Insider tier badge visible at the footer — even a visual reminder that I have standing with this brand matters to me.

## What's weak

- ALL CAPS subject is Sephora raising its voice at me in the hallway. After calling me "Rosie" last week, the relationship is already slightly bruised — this doesn't help rebuild it.
- Zero offer, zero member benefit, zero event signal. I'm a loyal customer who just got misnamed. A gesture — a gift-with-purchase, early access, anything — would go a long way here.
- The "ICYMI" section is buried in small type near the bottom and I almost missed it entirely. If there's a launch or a happening in there, it deserves a real headline.
- The preheader ("Hot drops, the latest news, insider expertise, and more") is entirely generic — Neiman Marcus ran a more specific preheader on their welcome-brochure email, and that email disappointed me. This preheader is doing even less work.

- 2. **Add one tier-specific hook.** Even one line: "As a ROUGE member, get first access to the Crown Affair routine" turns a read into a click. The tier badge at the footer is decoration right now, not action.

- 4. **Drop one specific Crown Affair SKU with a price.** The editorial earns the sell; let the sell follow the content. Don't make me go hunting.
- 5. **Verify the makeup-tips CTA destination.** "SHE SPILLS MORE HERE+" is the best CTA copy in the email — make sure it goes to a shoppable tips page, not Sephora's homepage.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `robots` meta value has a whitespace typo:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Correct value is `noindex` (no space). Functionally harmless in email clients (they don't crawl), but sloppy and would break if the document were ever indexed by a webmail proxy.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Preventing all user-initiated text scaling is an accessibility failure. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires text to be resizable up to 200% without loss of content. Using `none` (rather than `100%`) is the more aggressive form and breaks browser-level text scaling in webmail contexts.

**Issue — no `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
`lang="en"` is absent. Screen readers and translation engines rely on this. CAN-SPAM doesn't require it, but WCAG 3.1.1 does.

**Cannot verify** — `alt` attributes on `<img>` tags are not in the visible portion of the source.
