# The Roundup: Summer Sandals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails from Neiman Marcus since July 2nd — sale, sale, sale, bigger sale, last day — and today they serve me a sandal editorial with zero offers. I'll give them this: the woven raffia hero shot is genuinely gorgeous, and the "Summer Sandals" roundup format is a palate cleanser after that week of oversized typographic "S/ale" blocks. But I'm standing at the counter the morning after the "Last Day for 60% Off" email, and now you want to show me a curated edit with no prices, no percentage, no nothing? The whiplash is real.
- The layout is clean and editorial in a way that actually suits Neiman Marcus — it feels like a page torn from their print catalogue, which is not a bad thing. The "Shop My NM" secondary CTA caught my eye; that's the kind of member touch that makes me feel like a known customer rather than a mass-blast recipient. But without a single price point, offer tier, or even a brand callout I can read from this screenshot, this email is doing brand work when my purchase intent is still warm from yesterday's deadline pressure.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. I might pull it up tonight on my laptop to actually browse the edit — the curation looks elevated — but I'm not clicking right now. I would not forward it; there's nothing urgent enough to share.

## What's working

- **Hero product is aspirational and seasonally perfect.** That woven raffia sandal with the floral backdrop looks like something I'd spot at Saks during a July trunk show — it earns the open.
- **"The Roundup" format signals curation, not clearance.** After a week of sale blasts, this editorial framing actually repositions NM back toward its premium lane.
- **"Shop My NM" CTA is a quiet loyalty signal.** Two buttons — one generic ("Shop Sandals"), one personal ("Shop My NM") — tells me they remember I'm a member. Sephora and Macy's rarely give me a personalized path this visible this early in the email.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs.** Layout holds, images load, logo is crisp. Basic, but after a week of busy sale creative, the whitespace feels intentional.
- **Product grid looks genuinely varied.** I can spot a flat, a stiletto, a platform, a strappy evening option — real range without looking like a clearance dump.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer after a week of escalating sale urgency.** Yesterday was "Last day for up to 60% off." Today: nothing. Kohl's at least bakes a "save 30%" into every editorial roundup. This transition feels unplanned.
- **Product names and prices are unreadable at mobile size.** I can see images but I can't identify the brands in the grid without zooming — and that's a tap-and-squint interaction that Gen X women don't tolerate for long.
- **The cadence fatigue undercuts everything.** This is email nine in six days. Even a beautiful editorial starts to feel like noise at that volume. Sephora managed their Stacking Weekend arc in five emails with clear escalation logic; NM's had no arc — just a sale that suddenly ended, replaced by a roundup.
- **No social proof anywhere.** Even a simple "bestseller" or "back in stock" badge would give me confidence. Sephora's "2X points" callout does more urgency work in four characters than this whole email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a member-only offer hook.** Even "NM Card members get complimentary shipping on this edit" — one line above the first CTA — converts this from a browse email to a buy email. I refuse to use a card without points back; remind me my card works here.
- 2. **Surface at least one brand name and price in the body.** Callout the pink strappy sandal (if that's Amina Muaddi, say it) with a price. Don't make me click to find out what I'm looking at.
- 3. **Acknowledge the transition from sale season.** A single line — "The sale's wrapped; here's what we're reaching for next" — reframes the pivot as intentional instead of tone-deaf.
- 4. **Pull back on send cadence for the next 48 hours.** The inbox is full; rest my attention so the next email actually gets opened.
- 5. **Add a "Styled by your advisor" callout near "Shop My NM."** That CTA is doing real personalization work but it reads like a button label, not a benefit.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Roundup: Summer Sandals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript template with standard table-based layout and a CSS checkbox-hack hamburger nav. Four QA warnings surfaced: missing one-click unsubscribe headers (a Gmail compliance issue since Feb 2024), unverified authentication, a URL-saturated plain-text body, and 36 unprobed tracking links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links unprobed**
All outbound links pass through a click-redirect domain and were skipped during HTTP probing. Final destination URLs, redirect chain depth, and landing page validity are unverified for this send.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs**
QA measured 3,138 of 3,647 plain-text characters as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `HTTPS_HTTP_MISMATCH`, Postini URL ratio rules) penalize plain-text parts that are nearly entirely links with no substantive prose. This degrades inbox placement, especially at non-Gmail providers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead commented-out viewport tags**
Three `<meta name="viewport">` variants are commented out immediately below the active tag:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but signal uncommitted template cleanup. No functional impact.

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Visible in browser-tab previews on WebKit-based clients and in some preview pane subject lines. Minor.

**Hamburger nav (CSS checkbox hack) has near-zero email client support**
The `.mobileCont:checked~table` selector drives mobile interactive state. This pattern works only in Apple Mail and a small subset of webmail clients. Gmail (Android/iOS/web), Outlook (all versions), and Samsung Mail will silently fall back — confirm the fallback state is the correct non-interactive layout, not a blank/hidden content block. The `contentInteractive` / `nocontentInteractive` display swap is the risk area:
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=nocontentInteractive] { display: none !important; }
```
If `nocontentInteractive` content is navigation or CTAs, they may be hidden by default in non-supporting clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block is inside an HTML comment**
The variable declarations at the top of the document — `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` — are wrapped in `<!-- -->`. SFMC executes AMPscript inside `%%[ ]%%` blocks, not HTML comments. If the intent was a documentation header, that is fine — but the actual `SET` and `Lookup()` calls must exist in proper AMPscript blocks elsewhere in the template. Confirm the live template contains executing AMPscript, not just this comment header.

**`@user` assignment is double-commented**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This is a JavaScript-style comment inside an HTML comment. It will never execute. If `@user` (the SHA-256 hashed email) is used downstream for personalization or analytics passthrough in any link parameter, it will render as an empty string or literal `@user` token at send time.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" note left in deployed template**
The comment instructs the template author to pick a variable-population scenario. If multiple scenarios exist in the template body (e.g., CRM-matched vs. fallback subscriber lookup), and none was removed before send, both branches may execute and conflict. Cannot verify without the full template body.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. Gmail requires `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5k/day to Gmail) and has enforced one-click unsubscribe since February 2024. Missing or relay-stripped headers risk Gmail surfacing its own unsubscribe UI or suppressing inbox placement.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. This header is the mechanism Gmail and Apple Mail use for one-click machine-initiated unsubscribes. Without it, the `List-Unsubscribe` header (if present) requires the user to open an email client to complete the flow, which Gmail no longer accepts as compliant for bulk senders.

**Authentication-Results header not captured**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown for this send as captured through the AgentMail relay. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain — confirm DKIM signing is active on that subdomain and that the `d=` value in the DKIM signature aligns with the From domain at the organizational level (`neimanmarcus.com`).

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not assessable from the truncated HTML. Standard NM footer is expected to contain it — flag for manual confirmation against the full render.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Tracking link destinations unverifiable**
All 36 outbound links are click-redirect URLs. UTM parameter presence (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`), `@refid`/`@base_refid` passthrough, and final landing page relevance to "Summer Sandals" content are unconfirmed. One broken redirect in a hero CTA would silently drop conversion.

**`@refid` construction**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
The second CONCAT argument is an empty string — this is a no-op. If the intent was to append a suffix (e.g., email variant tag), the value was not filled in. If `@refid` is appended to link URLs for campaign attribution, this is a build defect.
