# Style Lessons from a Tastemaker
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Okay, now this one I actually stopped and looked at. No urgency banner, no percentage-off code, no exclusion wall — just a stylist, a mood, and an actual outfit I could picture myself wearing to a fall dinner. Jessica de Ruiter's quote "I like the mix of classic and undone" is a real point of view, and that black slip dress with the shoulder wrap in the top hero photo is genuinely covetable on a woman my age, not a 23-year-old.
- That said, my eye did wander — who is Jessica de Ruiter and why should I trust her over, say, an in-house Neiman's stylist I might already know from the store? And there's zero dollar signal anywhere in this email, which after four straight days of Neiman's hammering me with 25%-off codes (September 13th and 14th, twice) feels like a jarring tonal whiplash. I'd click "Shop the Look" out of pure curiosity about that grey Stella McCartney cardigan with the fur cuffs, but I wouldn't forward this to a friend — it's nice, not "you need to see this" nice.

## What's working

- The hero image is a woman my actual demographic — laugh lines, real body, not a 19-year-old in her mother's clothes. That's rare and I noticed it immediately.
- The shoppable flat-lay at the bottom (Hermès lipstick, Saint Laurent sunglasses, Jil Sander bag, McQueen skirt, Saint Laurent pumps) is smart — it's a full capsule wardrobe, not just one dress, and every item is labeled so I know exactly what I'm looking at.
- No fake urgency, no "ends today" screaming — after two days of that exact tactic from this same brand, the calm register is a relief.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" footer CTA is a nice Neiman's-specific touch — that's the kind of white-glove service Saks and Macy's don't really offer me at this level.

## What's weak

- There is no price anywhere. Not on the cardigan, not on the bag, not even a "shop the look starting at $X." I have to click through blind, and at Neiman's price points that's a real barrier for me.
- "Shop the Story" and "Shop the Look" are both underlined text links, easy to miss on a phone — no actual button. Sephora and Macy's at least give me a colored button to tap.
- The stylist framing needed one more line — a credential or a "NM contributing stylist" tag would've done it. Right now it reads like an influencer I don't know.
- Given I got two nearly-identical 25%-off emails from this same brand on September 13th and 14th, this beautiful, offer-free send right after feels like the marketing team isn't talking to itself — pick a lane.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "shop from $X" under at least the hero look — I'm not going to click through five product pages just to find out the cardigan is $895.
- 2. Turn "Shop the Look" into an actual button, not underlined text — my thumb needs a bigger, clearer target.
- 3. Add one credibility line under Jessica de Ruiter's name — stylist credentials or "as seen in" — so I trust the recommendation the way I'd trust Sephora's staff-pick call-outs.
- 4. Space this out from the discount-code emails — sequence editorial content and percentage-off offers so they don't collide within 48 hours.
