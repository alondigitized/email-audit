# Oooh, it’s 50% off select beauty ❤️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject in my inbox says "Oooh, it's 50% off select beauty ❤️" — and then I open it and I'm staring at "The Hair Styling Awards," a whole hair-product bake-off with a hairstylist headshot and 12 "outstanding performers" broken into Sleekness & Shine, Definition, and Volume categories. The 50% off beauty offer I actually opened this for is buried nine screens down, wedged between a shampoo discount and the loyalty footer. That's a bait-and-switch, plain and simple, and it's the kind of thing that makes me trust a sender less every time it happens.
- This is also the sixth Sephora send in less than a week hitting my inbox — I've tracked "Stacking Weekend" through 9/4, 9/5 (twice), 9/6, 9/7, and 9/8's "LAST CHANCE" scream-fest, and now this one on 9/9 which at least changes the creative but still can't resist tacking the same 50%-off starburst onto the bottom like a rider on a bill. Compare that to Neiman Marcus's "Buy More, Earn More" send, which led with the actual offer and a clear gift-card ladder — I knew in three seconds what I'd get. Here I have to scroll past a hairstylist's resume to find out what the subject line promised me.
- Would I click? Only if I scroll all the way down, which most people commuting on their phone won't do. Would I forward it? No — maybe to a friend who's into a specific hair treatment, but not as a deal to shop together. I'm marking this one read and moving on, mildly annoyed that the subject oversold what's actually a hair-awards catalog.

## What's working

- The hairstylist "meet the judge" section (headshot, name, quote) gives this a magazine-editorial feel I don't usually get from a mass blast — that's a step up from Kohl's generic "fall faves" grid.
- Product cards are clean — price, star ratings, and a green "Clean at Sephora" badge are all legible without pinching to zoom.
- The "5 for $10" hair trial size banner up top is a genuinely low-commitment, specific offer — that's the kind of thing that gets a Gen X shopper to try something new without risk.
- Loyalty footer (Insider tier bar, 500 bonus points call-out, barcode for in-store) is present and clear, same muscle Sephora always flexes well.

## What's weak

- The subject promised beauty discount, the hero delivered a hair-awards catalog — that mismatch is the single biggest miss here, and it's worse than anything I dinged Macy's "LAST CHANCE" confirmation email for, because at least that one's urgency matched its content.
- The actual 50% off select beauty offer is nine screens deep — by the time I get there I've scrolled through three "outstanding performer" categories and a hairstyling quiz CTA. Ulta would bury an offer, sure, but not usually this far.
- No sizing, ingredient callouts, or "why this made the list" copy beyond a one-line review quote per product — for a hair category, I want to know curl type or hair texture fit, and it's just not there.
- This is my sixth Sephora email in six days and the offer underneath is the exact same 50%-off-plus-500-points mechanic I've now seen five times — the hair awards wrapper is new, but the payload is stale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick a lane: either send this as "The Hair Styling Awards" with a hair-focused subject, or send it as "50% off beauty" with that offer up top. Don't promise one and deliver the other — that's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Move the 50% off beauty banner into the first screen if that's genuinely what you want me opening for — right now it's an afterthought below a full awards program.
- 3. Add one line of real product detail per "outstanding performer" (hair type, hold level, heat-protectant claim) — a star rating and a quote isn't enough for me to trust a hair product blind.
- 4. Vary the closing offer creative — I've now seen this same red 50%-off starburst five times this week; even a new color treatment would help it not feel like the same email recycled.
