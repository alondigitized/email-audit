# The Designer Sale favorites are selling fast!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- After what feels like the eighteenth email from Neiman Marcus in two weeks, "The Designer Sale favorites are selling fast!" rolls in — and honestly, this is one of the cleaner sends they've done this whole stretch. The red hero pops, "UP TO 50% OFF" is right there in your face, and the brand lineup in the product grid is actually worth looking at: Zimmermann, Valentino Garavani, Burberry. After that bewildering "Summer suiting for refined moments" editorial on May 27 (which landed the morning after "Last chance: 25% off ends tonight"), and then the two-in-one-day situation on May 28, a sale email with real brands and a real number is a relief.
- That said, I'm numb. "Selling fast" and "LIMITED TIME" after two straight weeks of near-daily sends reads like ambient noise. When Sephora doubles up in a day, at least the offer is ending — there's a credible event. This feels like NM just... keeps going. In red. Forever. And then there's the kicker at the bottom: a "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF — Sign up" block inviting me to join the mailing list. I am *on* the mailing list. I am *living proof* I am on the mailing list.
- Would I click? Yes — SHOP HANDBAGS, probably, because Valentino Garavani at half price is genuinely interesting. Would I forward it? No. This is table stakes for anyone with an NM account.

## What's working

- The red hero with "UP TO 50% OFF" in large white type is impossible to miss — visual hierarchy does exactly what it should, eye goes straight to the number.
- Brand mix is calibrated right for me: Zimmermann, Valentino Garavani, Burberry are names I'll actually act on during a sale, not filler.
- Separate category CTAs — "SHOP HANDBAGS," "SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES," "SHOP DRESSES" — mean I don't have to wade through menswear to find my lane.
- "Exclusions Apply" is acknowledged up front under the hero, not buried in six-point footnotes.
- No render issues: images load, text is legible, layout holds cleanly on the screen.

## What's weak

- Not a single price anywhere in the product grid. It's a sale email featuring a Zimmermann dress and a Valentino bag — I need the crossed-out original and the sale price. Sephora puts before/after pricing directly on the product card; NM shows me the item and asks me to click on faith.
- The "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF — Sign up" block at the bottom is a newsletter welcome popup that got lost in an email template. I'm already subscribed, obviously.
- No loyalty acknowledgment at all for an existing customer. Saks Circle surfaces my tier before the offer copy; here I get a subscriber recruitment pitch.
- Cadence fatigue has genuinely eroded the urgency language. After the May 27 "Last chance" and two sends on May 28, "selling fast!" is sonic wallpaper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put prices on the product cards.** Even two or three items with original + sale price would do more than any copy rewrite — "Was $1,290, Now $645" under that Zimmermann dress would get me to tap immediately.
- 2. **Swap the newsletter signup block for a loyalty member hook.** If I have NM InCircle status (and at this spend level, I might), surface it: "Your member early access ends Sunday" or "You've unlocked an extra 10% on top — here's how to stack it."
- 3. **Cut the product grid in half and add pricing.** Six items with no prices is a catalog preview. Three hero items with real sale pricing, breathing room, and a clear CTA each will convert better.
- 4. **Rest a day.** One day of inbox silence would make the next sale email hit twice as hard.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your sale picks won't last: NM Designer Sale, up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Zimmermann dresses, Valentino bags, Burberry — prices cut now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited time: the 50% off designers worth the click.`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable — unresolved dual-path ambiguity (elevated risk).**

The template comment block declares:
```
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
Immediately below, a second scenario is commented out:
```ampscript
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The block header reads *"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"*, indicating this is a two-path template where the developer must activate exactly one assignment. Currently, `@user` resolves to `EMAIL_ID` (a numeric/opaque subscriber key). If any link or tracking pixel passes `@user` as a URL parameter for identity resolution, it is emitting an internal subscriber ID instead of the SHA-256 hash — which is the more privacy-safe and analytics-standard value. Conversely, if the downstream analytics system expects a hash, all attributed events for this send will be unresolvable.

**`@emailid` redundancy.** `@emailid` is declared with an identical Lookup to `@user`:
```
@emailid (from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New)
```
Two variables resolving to the same value from the same table is a copy-paste artifact. Confirm whether `@emailid` is actually used distinctly or is a dead variable.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** look correctly sourced from `NM_CRM_Feed` and are the expected CRM identity fields. No issue found.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not detected (warning).**
> QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`

Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button. Its absence means those clients will not show one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints. SFMC can inject this header at send time — verify it is enabled in the send definition or account-level settings, not just the template.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not detected (warning).**
> QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`

Required for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe compliance (Google's February 2024 bulk sender mandate). A list of >5k recipients/day without this header risks inbox placement demotion. This is separate from the in-email unsubscribe link.

**`Authentication-Results` — not captured.**
> QA finding: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found: SPF/DKIM status unknown`

The AgentMail relay did not forward authentication headers. SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status for `e.neimanmarcus.com` cannot be confirmed from this capture. Validate directly via a seed send to a Gmail address and inspect raw headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — not visible in the truncated HTML fragment provided; assumed present in the footer based on standard NM template patterns, but not confirmed by this audit.
