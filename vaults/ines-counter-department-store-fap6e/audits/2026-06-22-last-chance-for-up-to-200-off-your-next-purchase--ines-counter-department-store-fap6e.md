# Last chance for up to $200 off your next purchase!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Two days ago I opened that "$200 off" subject line — the June 20 one that is still sitting in my cart consideration pile — and now Neiman Marcus is back in my inbox calling it a last chance. I'm not annoyed. Actually, the thing that stopped me this time is the InCircle Triple Points callout below the spend tiers, because that's new information stacked on top of the dollar offer. Points are the whole reason I carry that card. A 3× earn on top of $50–$200 back is a genuinely useful stack if I was going to buy anyway.
- What I am tired of is "last chance" with no actual clock on it. The code is JUNE. Does that expire tonight? June 30? I shouldn't need to open a browser and check — that detail belongs in the email. And this is my seventh-plus NM send since June 15; I counted before I clicked. The fatigue is real, even when the offer is real.
- I'd click through. I would not forward — a friend would need too much context about InCircle mechanics and spend tiers that I'd have to explain, and that's friction I'm not creating.

## What's working

- InCircle Triple Points line below the tiers is the genuine upgrade vs. the June 20 original send — it gave me a reason to open this one and not just scroll past
- Spend tiers are laid out in a clean table ($35 off at $100 / up to $200 at $1,000+) — I can see exactly where I'd land without hunting through fine print; compare this to Macy's "LAST CHANCE" email that buried its confirmation mechanics in legalese
- "SHOP THE SALE" CTA is above the fold, unambiguous, and uses the code JUNE which is simple to remember
- The "Match with in-personal style advisor" link at the bottom actually resonates with me — I'm an in-store buyer and that's a touchpoint I'd use at the counter
- Brand names in the grid — Jimmy Choo, Stuart Weitzman, Cinq à Sept, STAUD — are all in my tier; this doesn't feel like a Kohl's clearance push

## What's weak

- "Last chance" without an actual expiration date is urgency theatre — a specific date or "expires tonight at midnight" would convert me faster
- Stuart Weitzman appears twice in the brand grid, which reads like a filler slot, not a curated edit
- Six category buttons at the bottom (WOMEN'S CLOTHING, MEN'S, SHOES, JEWELRY, HANDBAGS, HOME) dilute a send that should have one job: get me to the sale
- Zero beauty in this grid — I'm InCircle, NM knows my beauty spend, and yet not a single fragrance or skincare card; Sephora's chaotic BEACH CLUB email at least knew it was a beauty brand talking to a beauty customer
- The app download strip reads like a corporate checklist item, not something designed for me

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real deadline in the email** — "Code JUNE expires June 22 at midnight" or "expires June 30" — "last chance" without a date is the single biggest fix available
- 2. **Cut four of the six category CTAs** — keep SHOP THE SALE plus one category (SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES works; Jimmy Choo is right there in the grid)
- 3. **Add one beauty SKU** — a La Mer or Sisley or Kilian card that says NM knows I'm not just a shoes buyer; even a single "earn Triple Points on beauty" callout would do it
- 4. **Pull the app strip** or drop it to below the legal text; it's visual noise in a sale email
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `JUNE expires tonight — Triple Points + $200 off`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Your InCircle bonus runs out with JUNE — last call`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 3× points and save up to $200. Code expires at midnight.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Tiers start at $100 — pick your spend level before JUNE is gone.`

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block is comment-only in the provided source.** The top of the email contains a required-variables comment listing:

```
@email, @base_refid, @refid, @user, @emailid, @uEm, @cmdid
```

The comment also notes: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` followed by a commented-out line:
```amPscript
/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */
```

**Issue:** `@user` appears to be toggled between a CRM lookup (`Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`) and a SHA-256 hash assignment, but the active scenario is not visible in the truncated source. If the wrong scenario is live, `@user` could be an unhashed email passed to downstream pixels — a potential PII exposure in tracking parameters.

**Recommendation:** Confirm which `@user` scenario is active before send. If `@user` is passed in any URL parameter, it must be the hashed value.

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | One-click unsubscribe not confirmed |
| SPF/DKIM authentication | **WARN** | `Authentication-Results` header absent from relay capture |

**`List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post`:** These are required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024 for senders >5K/day). Their absence from relay capture does not confirm they are missing from actual delivery — the AgentMail relay may strip or not forward these headers. **This must be verified against a raw `.eml` received at a Gmail or Yahoo inbox**, not inferred from relay metadata.

**SPF/DKIM:** Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Authentication status is unknown from relay capture. DMARC pass requires SPF or DKIM alignment on this subdomain. If NM uses SFMC's shared sending infrastructure without a custom DKIM domain, DKIM alignment may fail on strict DMARC policies.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not verifiable from truncated source, but typically present in NM footer. Confirm it appears above the `</body>` tag.
