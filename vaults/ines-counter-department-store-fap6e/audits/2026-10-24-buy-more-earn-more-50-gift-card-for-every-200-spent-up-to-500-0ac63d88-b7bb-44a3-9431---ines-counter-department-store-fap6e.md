# Buy More, Earn More: $50 gift card for every $200 spent (up to $500)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This one has a number, and I'm glad. After a week of Neiman's lookbooks, including yesterday's David Yurman email, this blush hero says "$50 GIFT CARD for every $200 spent" in a big red and black serif. I can read the offer in two seconds. It's a bigger-ticket version of yesterday's "Fall Style Sale: up to 30% off".
- The question I always ask is whether I'd rather have this than 30% off. A $50 card on $200 is 25% back, but it's a future credit. It arrives within 8 weeks and it expires. Reading the fine print at the bottom, I see that returns get subtracted and can forfeit the card. I buy and return at the brand event, so that matters to me. It is a points-back offer, which I like, but it's mostly Neiman's money to keep if I'm careless. I'd click it, and I'd read the terms before I put a card down. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: "$50 GIFT CARD / FOR EVERY $200 SPENT," with the code FALLNM and a black SHOP NOW button right under it.
- The "$500 maximum reward" line sits under the button, not buried in the legal text, so the cap feels honest.
- The nine-designer grid (Oscar de la Renta, Burberry, Zegna, Tom Ford, Chloe) reads as grown-up designer access. Nothing in it is aimed at a twenty-year-old.
- The category buttons for women's clothing, shoes, jewelry, handbags and home let me go straight to my category.
- The layout is cleaner than Macy's, which stacks four offers on top of each other. Here it's one idea.

## What's weak

- The "Excludes beauty" line is in small type under the button. That's my category, and it's the thing I buy from a counter. Sephora at least tells me up front what's in the promo.
- The terms block is a wall of tiny bullets: tiers, the 8-week delay, the return clawback, and designers excluded without being named. A brand that respects me wouldn't make me decode this.
- The "Claim your 15% off" text-list block comes after the gift card offer. It's a second offer with a different mechanism, and it dilutes the first. It's also useless to me as someone who already gets these emails.
- Nothing shows my tier or points status. For a counter regular who won't use a card without points back, a stranger and I get the same email.
- The product tiles have no prices and no reason to pick one over another. They're just a catalog again, as with the 10/22 "1,000+ new arrivals" email. The nine tiles are also the only things I can tap, apart from the buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the excluded designers and say what "select" means above the fold, so I'm not left reading the legal block to find out whether my item counts.
- 2. Put a worked example in the hero: "Spend $400, get $100 back."
- 3. Drop the 15% sign-up block, or send it only to non-subscribers.
- 4. Add prices to the product tiles and one line about the return policy.
- 5. Show a loyalty line for members, such as "InCircle members: here's what this stacks with."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code FALLNM. Up to $500 back. Beauty excluded, designer exclusions apply.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Buy More, Earn More: $50 gift card for every $200 spent (up to $500)"

The HTML source is truncated inside the `<style>` block, so I could only audit the head, CSS and a leading comment. Body markup, links, footer and alt text are unverified.

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a legacy table-based SFMC/ExactTarget template. It leaks internal AMPscript and data-extension notes in an HTML comment, disables pinch-zoom, and has no `lang` attribute. The three QA warnings (missing `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`) are probably artifacts of the AgentMail relay and are unconfirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- QA skipped 30 tracking links because they use click-redirect domains, so none were probed. Link validity, redirect chains and final destinations are unverified.
- No `href` values appear in the truncated source, so I can't check link counts, `http` vs `https`, or the footer links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Generic title.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't match the subject or offer.
- **Hamburger menu.** The mobile nav relies on the checkbox hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`, `.hamburger`, `.close`). It isn't supported in Gmail or Outlook clients, and it is hard to reach by keyboard or screen reader. I can't see whether a visible fallback nav exists.
- **Brittle class selectors.** Exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` only match when `class` holds a single token. If an element carries multiple classes, the mobile rules silently won't apply.
- **Legacy hacks.** `.ReadMsgBody`, the `-webkit-animation: bugfix` iOS9 keyframes and `*[class].show { width:auto … width:278px }` (a duplicate `width` declaration) add weight and aren't harmful by themselves.
- **Global paragraph rule.** `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` forces centering on every `<p>`, which risks unintended alignment in body copy.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE.** The leading comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE …>`. This can trigger quirks mode in some older clients (IE-based and legacy webmail).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template internals leaked.** The delivered HTML starts with `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid … @uEm … @cmdid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->` and `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. Recipients never see the comments, but anyone viewing source gets internal DE names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`). The comment also shows an unresolved authoring choice ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO"). It should be stripped at send time.
- No visible unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `@var`, `{{ }}`) in the rendered portion. Whether `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid` resolved inside link query strings can't be confirmed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **WARN, `List-Unsubscribe` not found.** QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly stripping it. Unconfirmed, not a verified defect.
- **WARN, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** Same caveat. One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders, so check the raw message at the origin.
- **WARN, `Authentication-Results` missing.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`, so check alignment with the original headers.
- The visible-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated section and could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Not assessable. No destination URLs are visible, and the 30 tracking links were not followed, so UTM or `refid` parameters and landing-page alignment with the "$50 per $200 (up to $500)" offer can't be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip AMPscript and authoring comments (`<!--REQUIRED variables … -->`) from production output. This removes the internal DE names and fixes the pre-DOCTYPE comment.
2. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`, and set a descriptive `<title>`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Provide a non-checkbox fallback nav for Gmail and Outlook, and make sure the hamburger is keyboard accessible.
5. Replace exact `[class=…]` selectors with `[class~=…]` or `[class*=…]`.
6. Re-run the header checks against the original message, not the relay copy. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML with a resolver for the click-redirect domain, so UTM/`refid` parameters, final URLs, the unsubscribe link and the postal address can be verified.
