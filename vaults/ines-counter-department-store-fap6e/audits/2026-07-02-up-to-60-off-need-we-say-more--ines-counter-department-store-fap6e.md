# Up to 60% off. Need we say more?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Two Neiman Marcus emails in one day. That's what I'm working with. This morning they sent me a 40% off note that I scored a 6 — it felt slightly stale compared to the genuinely editorial "S ale" typographic hero that landed 7/1 and earned an 8 from me. Now it's the same afternoon and the offer has jumped to 60%. I'm not mad at the number. I'm a little unsettled by the implication that 40% wasn't enough to move inventory by lunch.
- That said, the email itself is cleaner than its predecessor. The red hero is bold without being desperate — and that's a meaningful distinction at this price point. When I compare it to Sephora's "⭐ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐" emoji bomb that I reviewed recently, NM looks like a grown-up. The product curation is genuinely in my lane: Chiara Boni, Veronica Beard, Valentino sandals. Somebody thought about a woman in her 40s who actually shops the counter, not just clicks through to homepage. That earns real credit.
- What it doesn't do is talk to me as a member. Not a word about InCircle. I refuse to spend four figures at Neiman Marcus without my points tracking — that's not negotiable — and this email acts like that relationship doesn't exist. I'd click to see what's actually in the sale, but I'd land skeptical and start checking exclusions immediately.

## What's working

- **The red hero lands** — "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" in stacked white type is confident and editorial without resorting to the !!!! energy that Kohl's leans on. The typeface does the work.
- **Brand curation is legitimately for me** — Chiara Boni La Petite Robe, Veronica Beard, Valentino, Stella McCartney, Jacquemus. This isn't a clearance dump; someone selected designers a 47-year-old who shops Nordstrom and Saks would actually recognize and want.
- **Sub-category CTAs let me self-select** — "SHOP HANDBAGS," "SHOP DRESSES," "SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES" at the bottom mean I don't have to fight a mega-sale page. That friction reduction matters.
- **No render bugs at all** — every image loaded, layout is clean, nothing overlapping. Worth saying out loud because Macy's can't always manage it on mobile.
- **Preview text does what the subject skipped** — "Save on these designer styles before they're gone" introduces the urgency that "Need we say more?" decided to play coy about.

## What's weak

- **InCircle is invisible** — This is the single biggest miss. I don't drop meaningful money at NM without knowing my points are tracking. Sephora, even on its noisiest Stacking Weekend blast, still tells me what tier I'm in and what I'm stacking toward. NM goes silent.
- **No prices on the product tiles** — Six designers, zero dollar amounts. "60% off" means nothing to me without an anchor: "Was $895 → Now $358." Without that, "up to" starts sounding like fine print.
- **"Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA** — In small italics, immediately below the "SHOP SALE" button. I see it. I always see it. At this price point, that two-word disclaimer has burned me before, and it makes me slow down rather than click through.
- **Two question-format subjects in one day** — "Need a summer refresh?" this morning, "Need we say more?" now. The rhetorical formula stops feeling confident and starts feeling like a tic. The 7/1 "Surprise" send didn't need to ask me anything — it just showed up with the offer and looked good.
- **Men's CTAs are irrelevant noise** — "SHOP MEN'S" and "SHOP MEN'S SHOES" in the same footer grid as my categories. If NM knows I'm a women's-only shopper (and they should), these rows shouldn't exist in my version.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put prices on the product tiles** — Even "from $X" or a was/now pair on two or three tiles would make the 60% feel real. Right now the number floats in the hero without landing anywhere tangible.
- 2. **Lead with InCircle** — A single line in the header — "InCircle members: earn points on every sale piece" — turns this from a one-time price email into a loyalty moment. That's the difference between a customer who clicks and one who stores.
- 3. **Segment out the men's CTAs** — If I'm on a women's list, the men's rows shouldn't render. This is a segmentation failure that Macy's makes regularly; NM should be doing it better.
- 4. **Add a real deadline to the hero** — "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 6" in the red block would make today's click feel necessary rather than optional. "Before they're gone" is soft urgency.
- 5. **Vary the subject formula** — Three question-format subjects in three days and the cadence registers as anxiety, not confidence.

- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off the designers you actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop before your size disappears — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `InCircle members earn points on every sale piece`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Up to 60% off" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard SFMC-deployed table-based HTML build with no hard blocking issues, but carries four QA warnings — two compliance gaps (unsubscribe headers), one deliverability signal (plain-text URL density), and one authentication blind spot — plus two AMPscript template hygiene risks in the variable block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped (unverifiable)**
All click-redirect domains were excluded from the HTTP probe. Link health (broken links, redirect chains, destination 4xx/5xx) cannot be confirmed for any CTA in this send.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs**
QA: `5225/5993 chars are URLs`. A valid plain-text alternative should have readable prose mirroring the HTML content. A near-bare URL dump is a known spam-filter trigger and provides no fallback for text-only clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Missing `lang="en"` prevents screen readers from selecting the correct voice/language profile. Required for WCAG 2.1 Level A.

**Hamburger nav relies on CSS `:checked` + `transition`**
The mobile nav uses a checkbox-state hack with CSS transitions. Neither the checkbox state trick nor `transition` is supported in Outlook (any version) or older Gmail in-app clients. Users on those clients will receive a broken or permanently-hidden nav — no functional fallback is present.

**Multiple commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags**
Three alternate viewport declarations are left in comments inside `<head>`. These are inert but indicate prior iteration that was never cleaned up — a maintenance/readability risk if a future editor uncomments the wrong one.

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but subject line preview tools may surface this space in some clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Ambiguous `@user` assignment (scenario selection not enforced)**
The variable block opens with:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
That line is HTML-commented out. The active code block sets `@user` via `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)` — meaning `@user` receives an internal `EMAIL_ID` integer, not a SHA256 hash. If downstream link parameters or personalization blocks expect a hashed email in `@user`, they will receive the wrong value type with no runtime error.

**`@refid` is a no-op CONCAT**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string is a no-op. This is either a placeholder left from a template and the real suffix was never added, or the variable was intended to carry a modifier that was stripped. If `@refid` is used in tracking URLs, it is functionally identical to `@base_refid`.

**`@emailid` fallback path unclear**
The comment says "from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New" but no conditional (`IIF`/`IF`) is shown in the truncated source. If the DE column is null and no conditional guards the lookup, this could result in empty `emailid` parameters in tracking URLs.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found**
QA: `Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay`. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button. Its absence (or relay stripping) means subscribers on those clients lose one-click opt-out at the inbox level — increasing friction and spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
Required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Missing this header means one-click unsubscribe via POST is not supported, which is a deliverability compliance gap for a sender of Neiman Marcus's volume.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown**
QA: `Authentication-Results header not found`. The AgentMail relay did not surface authentication results — cannot confirm SPF pass, DKIM signature validity, or DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com`. This is a monitoring blind spot, not a confirmed failure.

**Physical address and body unsubscribe link**
Not verifiable from truncated HTML — these must be confirmed present in the footer per CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) and §5(a)(3).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All 26 tracking links were skipped. Cannot confirm whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are correctly appended, or whether `@refid`/`@cmdid` are being passed as expected query params.

**`@uEm` (encoded email) in URLs**
The `NM_CRM_Feed` lookup for `uEm` suggests personalized URL parameters for site-side identity stitching. If the CRM feed row is absent for a subscriber, `@uEm` will be empty — resulting in unstitched sessions and broken personalization on the landing page with no visible error.
