# 💝 MAKEUP SETS: HERE & NOWHERE ELSE 💝
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- Sephora again, and I'll give them this one: it's a real product email, not the magazine page I got on 10/12 ("The Chat," the "I HAVE PATRICK" send). The shouty subject is the only part that feels like a stranger wrote it. Inside, it's a clean column of exclusive makeup sets. Each has a brand name, a price and a "value" figure, and they include Dior, NARS, YSL, Charlotte Tilbury and Westman Atelier. That's my kind of counter.
- I'd still skim it more than study it. It's very long, and the first thing I see is a Fragrance Mini banner, not the makeup hero the subject promised. Every set also repeats the same "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA • LIMITED EDITION" tag, so after the third one it stops meaning anything. This is the fifth or sixth Sephora send in about a week. I'd probably click through to the NARS or Charlotte Tilbury sets and skip the rest. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The sets are named by brand, with a price and a "$ value" callout, for example NARS at $62 against a $130 value. I can do the math without clicking.
- The brand mix is department-store counter, not drugstore: Dior, YSL, NARS, Charlotte Tilbury. That tells me someone understood who I am.
- "Beauty Insiders get free shipping" sits right above the headline. A member benefit that respects me is what I look for.
- "Shop Makeup Sets" is a clear verb-plus-noun button, and it matches the subject.
- The Beauty Insider tier bar near the bottom shows where I stand. Nordstrom and Saks don't show me that this plainly.

## What's weak

- The "Get a Fragrance Mini" banner on top competes with the makeup hero. I came for sets, and the first thing I see is a redemption offer for something else.
- Every product carries an identical "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA • LIMITED EDITION" label. When everything is exclusive, nothing is.
- The subject has all caps, a colon and two heart emojis. It reads like a flash-sale blast, not something I'd get from a beauty counter I trust.
- There's no deadline anywhere. "Limited edition" has no date on it, so I feel no reason to act today.
- It runs long, and the bottom half drifts into Klarna and Afterpay logos, "Mini sets for the holidays" and "$35 and under skincare." That's Macy's-style overload on top of a sets email.
- The "value" numbers are the brand's own claim. I'd want to check them before trusting them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the makeup hero first and move the Fragrance Mini banner below the sets. The subject makes a promise, and the opening should keep it.
- 2. Cut the repeated badge. Use it once in the hero, then give each set one real differentiator, such as shade range or "set includes 3 full-size items."
- 3. Add a real deadline and a member angle, like "Insiders: free shipping through Sunday." Neiman Marcus at least ties its gift-card ladder to a spend threshold, and Kohl's puts "Ends Oct. 18" right in the top banner.
- 4. Trim the footer clutter and keep one secondary module.
- 5. Drop the all-caps treatment. Sephora's own "Beauty for literally everybody" send on 10/11 was calmer and read better.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Images with no `alt` attribute (3, confirmed by QA):**
  - `2021_Navs_Header_06.jpg` (`images.harmony.epsilon.com/.../2021_Navs_Header_06`). This is a navigation header image. With images blocked, screen-reader users and image-blocked clients lose the nav labels.
  - `o.gif` (`mi.sephora.com`) and the `app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF...` image are tracking pixels. They should carry `alt=""` so assistive tech skips them. This is low severity.
- **Viewport blocks zoom.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` disables pinch-zoom. That is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern on clients that honor the tag.
- **Text resizing is disabled.** The CSS sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` and `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` on `div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td`. This prevents user-driven text scaling in iOS Mail and similar clients.
- **Invalid meta value.** `<meta name="robots" content="no index" />` should be `noindex`. It is harmless in email but incorrect.
- **Plain-text part missing (QA FAIL).** The text version is 0 characters. The email has no non-HTML fallback for text-only clients or for spam filters that weigh multipart/alternative.
- **Outlook support is present.** The VML and `o:` namespaces and `mso-` rules are in place, with no issues.
