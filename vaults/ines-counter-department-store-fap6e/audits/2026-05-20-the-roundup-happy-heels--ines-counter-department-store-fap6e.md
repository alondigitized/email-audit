# The Roundup: Happy Heels
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Nine. That is the number I am now carrying into every Neiman Marcus subject line I see, and it is doing real violence to my willingness to engage. This one lands as a clean editorial lookbook — genuinely pretty, correct brands, the right category for me — and I am still struggling to feel anything about it because my goodwill account is completely overdrawn. The InCircle email from earlier today, the Prada collection send, the Designer Sale with The Row and Louboutin, now this: they have turned their list into a firehose and this email is collateral damage from that decision.
- The creative itself is restrained in a way that actually suits me. I am 47 and I respond to curation, not chaos. Six shoes, designer names I recognize on sight, clean white background, no screaming. That is the right register for Neiman Marcus. The trouble is that there is no reason to act. No event, no offer, no price, no InCircle hook. It is a magazine spread that has been misfiled as a promotional email.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I might glance at the Louboutin and the Manolo and then go back to my inbox, mildly resentful that I wasted the swipe.

## What's working

- The hero shoe — blush pink pointed-toe mule — is the right aspirational opener for someone who shops the Neiman Marcus shoe floor; I knew immediately this was curated for me, not a general sale blast.
- The six-product grid is smart editorial curation: Dolce&Gabbana, Louboutin, Saint Laurent, Manolo Blahnik — those are names I recognize in a glance, and seeing them together signals taste level without having to say a word.
- Visual hierarchy is genuinely clean; my eye moves from hero to headline to the two CTAs to the product grid without any friction.
- "Shop Heels" and "Shop Must-Have Shoes" as paired CTAs actually mirrors how I browse — sometimes I want the full floor, sometimes I want the edited list. That split is thoughtful.
- No render bugs anywhere I can see — white space is intentional, not broken.

## What's weak

- Not a single price point anywhere in the email. Nordstrom would show me the $895 beneath the Louboutin; Saks would show me a sale price if one existed. I cannot evaluate a purchase with zero price signal.
- No InCircle mention at all — for a brand that sent me a loyalty email earlier today, the complete absence of a points or member angle here is a missed connection. They know I am a customer and they are not using it.
- Two nearly identical CTAs ("Shop Heels" / "Shop Must-Have Shoes") is wasted real estate — they resolve to essentially the same intent and neither one is specific enough to create urgency for a particular shoe.
- The cadence is the loudest problem: nine emails in approximately 48 hours. Kohl's is sending six in eleven days and that already feels like a lot. This is a different category of overreach.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price or occasion anchor under each product** — even a "from $X" or "new for spring" tag would give me a reason to click rather than admire. Nordstrom does this in their shoe roundups consistently and it converts me because I can pre-qualify before I leave my inbox.
- 2. **Merge the two CTAs into one that is specific** — "Shop the Edit" or "See All Six" with a count creates focus. The double button reads as indecision.
- 3. **Add a thin InCircle callout** — a one-line footer "Earn InCircle points on every pair" is all it takes to remind me there is a reason to buy here rather than cross-shop Saks.
- 4. **Pull the cadence back to one email per day maximum** — this is a product and strategy note, not a creative one, but it is the single thing that would make every email they send land with more authority. Macy's sends me confirmation urgency at least with a clear trigger rationale; Neiman Marcus is sending editorial for its own sake at a pace that destroys editorial credibility.
- 5. **Surface one hero with a price** — Sephora personalizes imperfectly (my last three emails were addressed to someone named Rosie), but they always tell me the discount before I click. Neiman Marcus has the better product and the worse email economics. Fix that asymmetry.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Happy Heels: 6 pairs worth the splurge`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals: Louboutin, Manolo & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dolce, Saint Laurent, Manolo — shop the edit before your size is gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn InCircle points on every pair. Shop the heels roundup now.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Roundup: Happy Heels"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) with AMPscript personalization and a CSS-only hamburger nav. No critical rendering blockers detected, but four deliverability/compliance warnings from QA require attention before future sends — particularly the missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers and unverifiable authentication status.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** All click-redirect URLs routed through a tracking domain (consistent with SFMC `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) — the probe correctly skipped these rather than flagging false positives. Cannot confirm destination URLs or UTM hygiene without resolving the redirect chain.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs** (3,520 of 4,042 chars). This is a spam-filter signal. The plain-text alternative is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML with no human-readable copy — a pattern flagged by Gmail and SpamAssassin rules. The plain-text version should be a hand-crafted summary of the email content.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack** (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`). This pattern works in Apple Mail but silently degrades in Gmail, Outlook, and most webmail clients — users on those clients never see the nav. No fallback is present in the source.

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` alternatives** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Stale dead code; no rendering impact, but indicates template debt.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` loop on `body`** targets an iOS 9 hamburger menu bug that no longer exists on supported iOS versions. Benign but unnecessary.

**No `alt` text visible in the truncated source** for product images — cannot confirm coverage. Image-heavy heels roundup emails are high-risk for blocked-image clients if `alt` is absent.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.** Concatenating with an empty string string literal produces the same value as `@base_refid` alone. This is almost certainly a template placeholder (`""` should be a module-suffix string like `"_A"` or `"_hero"`). If `@refid` is used in tracking parameters, all modules will share an identical refid, breaking module-level click attribution.

**Dual-scenario user-identity logic is partially commented out:**
```amscript
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The SHA-256 path is commented out but the comment block containing it is still present as plain HTML comment — meaning the instruction text ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW") is visible in View Source to any recipient who inspects the email. The live path appears to be the `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` lookup. Confirm only one scenario is active and strip the other from production sends.

**Three CRM lookups per send** (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed` twice) will add SFMC query overhead at send time. If the sendable DE already carries `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, and `cmdid`, the lookups should be replaced with DE column references.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA WARN). This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. Its absence from the relay-captured headers suggests either the AgentMail relay is stripping it or SFMC is not injecting it. Verify SFMC "SafeUnsubscribe" or custom header injection is configured on this send definition.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA WARN). Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (the Gmail/Yahoo requirement) is not supported. Gmail will show a manual unsubscribe flow instead, increasing spam complaint probability.

**Authentication-Results header absent** (QA WARN). SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers — this is likely an AgentMail relay artifact, not a real authentication failure. Confirm SPF alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` and DKIM signing is active on the SFMC sending domain. DMARC policy for `neimanmarcus.com` should also be `p=quarantine` or `p=reject`.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated HTML; assumed present in the footer. Confirm.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot resolve destination URLs from tracking redirects without unwinding the click chain. Key risk: if `@refid` is broken (see §4), UTM `campaign` or `content` parameters derived from it will be wrong or uniform across all links, collapsing module-level attribution in analytics.

No UTM parameter structure is visible in the source — confirm that SFMC link wrapping or AMPscript URL construction appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before the tracking redirect layer.
