# Need a summer refresh? Shop up to 70% off L’Agence, Manolo Blahnik & more
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Twelve. I've opened twelve Neiman Marcus emails since July 2nd, and this one — with its deep-red "LAST CALL" hero and an extra 25% stacked on top of the up-to-70%-off they've been running since last week — lands with diminishing returns. I know the campaign by heart at this point. They used the same mechanic yesterday ("Last Call starts now"), the same language the day before, and now they've sweetened the pot with an extra quarter off. Fine. The deepened offer got my attention, and L'Agence at 70% off is genuinely the kind of thing I'd wear to a client lunch and never return. But I am not opening this email with the same energy I had on July 2nd.
- What kept me from immediately swiping away: the product grid is actually well-curated. Akris, Stella McCartney, Versace, Oscar de la Renta — these are my brands, my aesthetic, the things I'd try on during a Nordstrom anniversary event or browse at the Saks Last Call section. The email looks clean on mobile. The visual hierarchy goes straight to the offer, no hunting.
- What annoyed me: Manolo Blahnik is front and center in the subject line, and then I open the email and see Stella McCartney platform sandals, not a single Manolos image in sight. That feels like classic bait-and-drop, and I've been around long enough to notice it. I'd click to the shoes section anyway — but I don't love being led in under false pretenses. I'd mark this as read. I might click the L'Agence or Oscar de la Renta swatch. I would not forward it; I've already sent the July 8th version to my sister.

## What's working

- **The deepened offer is the correct move at this stage of the campaign.** Extra 25% on top of 70% is how you get a long-time subscriber who's been ignoring your "Last Call" for four days to actually click.
- **Product grid is authentically my taste.** Akris, L'Agence, Oscar de la Renta, Versace — no fast-fashion filler; this reads like someone actually pulled upscale designer inventory.
- **Red hero with clean white typography lands instantly.** No hunting for the offer; it's the first thing my eye hits, exactly where it should be on a 47-year-old's phone screen at breakfast.
- **Category buttons at the bottom (SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING, SHOP HANDBAGS, SHOP SHOES) give me a direct lane** if the main sale grid doesn't have my size — that's how I actually shop a sale.
- **No render bugs.** Macy's broke three images in their last "LAST CHANCE" email; this is clean.

## What's weak

- **Manolo Blahnik in the subject, no Manolo in the body.** The product grid has Stella McCartney sandals, a SHOP SHOES button, and no Manolo imagery. That's bait I was old enough to see coming.
- **Zero InCircle love.** I'm a high-tier member and this email treats me like a cold prospect who just wandered in off the street. Saks Fifth Avenue's comparable sale emails surface my tier consistently; Neiman Marcus goes cold here.
- **No beauty whatsoever.** I am a beauty-counter regular. This brand knows I buy La Mer, Augustinus Bader, and Sisley at their counters. A single beauty slot in this grid — a perfume, a skincare set on sale — would've made this feel like they know me.
- **"Exclusions Apply" in micro-type under the hero.** After twelve sends in nine days, trust is thin; every fine print asterisk registers louder than it should.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Kohl's sends me forty-eight emails since Memorial Day and I expect it from them. Neiman Marcus at this volume undermines the luxury positioning I came here for. Sephora just ran a multi-send Stacking Weekend sequence and still felt more intentional than this last-week barrage.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one beauty product in the grid.** A single slot — La Mer Concentrate, a Tom Ford fragrance, anything from the beauty floor at 30% off — reminds me why I shop Neiman Marcus instead of Nordstrom Rack.
- 2. **Feature the Manolo Blahnik you promised me.** If you lead with the brand in the subject, the first image in the shoe row should be that stiletto. This is basic editorial follow-through.
- 3. **Surface my InCircle status somewhere above the fold.** Even a one-line "As an InCircle member, your extra 25% stacks automatically" would make me feel seen instead of farmed.
- 4. **Edit the subject below 50 characters on mobile.** The current line truncates badly on iPhone.
- 5. **Replace the generic question opener with the specific offer.** "Need a summer refresh?" feels like a soft tease before a 70%-off sale — lead with the punch.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus Sale Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) that violate current bulk-sender requirements from Google and Yahoo, plus an ambiguous AMPscript variable scenario selector that creates a latent personalization data risk. All other technical signals are minor.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is URL-saturated**
QA flag: 5,225 of 6,058 plain-text characters are URLs (86%). Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Postini) penalize plain-text parts where the URL-to-content ratio exceeds ~70%. This increases the probability of bulk/spam folder placement.

**26 tracking links not probed**
All click-redirect URLs (likely `clicks.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) were skipped during HTTP validation. Broken redirect chains, expired short-links, or mis-parameterized redirects cannot be ruled out without direct probing of those domains. This should be resolved by running link checks against the actual redirect endpoints.

**No issues confirmed in link destinations** — destination URL correctness is unverified due to the skipped probes above.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS checkbox hack is non-functional in most clients**
The hamburger nav uses `:checked` pseudo-class toggling (`.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive]`). Gmail (web and app), Outlook, and Samsung Mail strip `<input type="checkbox">` elements and do not support `:checked` in email. The interactive content layer will silently collapse to its default `display:none` state in the majority of mobile clients. This is a functional defect, not a visual one — users on affected clients get a broken nav with no fallback.

**Duplicate and conflicting viewport meta tags**
Three `<meta name="viewport">` tags appear in `<head>` — one active, two commented out. While the comments are harmless, the presence of competing values (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29` and `width=1024`) suggests ad-hoc patching. If a comment is accidentally removed during template editing, the live viewport directive changes.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
`<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, but some clients surface the title as the notification preview or tab label.

**Alt text unverifiable from truncated source**
The HTML was cut off before `<img>` tags appear in body content. Alt attribute presence cannot be confirmed. If images are decorative-only (no alt or `alt=""`), screen reader users receive no content.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable scenario is ambiguous and risky**

The AMPscript preamble establishes `@user` via a subscriber lookup (returning an `EMAIL_ID` integer):
```
@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
Then immediately below, inside a block comment, is an alternative assignment:
```ampscript
/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */
```
The developer comment says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" but provides no explicit conditional logic — the switch is done by commenting/uncommenting lines manually. If a deployment template ships with the wrong scenario active, `@user` will contain a raw numeric `EMAIL_ID` where a SHA-256 hashed email is expected (or vice versa). Any link that passes `@user` as a URL parameter (e.g., for identity stitching on-site) would expose the wrong data type, potentially leaking a raw CRM identifier in click logs.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op**
Concatenating an empty string adds no transformation. If this is intentional padding for a string-type cast, it should be documented. If it's a vestige of a removed suffix, the expression can be simplified to `@refid = @base_refid`.

**`@emailid` defined redundantly**
The comment defines `@emailid` with a conditional note ("from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup") but the AMPscript shown uses only `Lookup` with no `IIF`/`IF` guard. If the sendable DE always carries `EMAIL_ID`, the unconditional lookup fires an unnecessary data extension query on every send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — CRITICAL
QA finding: `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this header for senders at ≥5,000 messages/day. Missing it risks deliverability enforcement (deferral or filtering) from both providers. Required format:
```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:unsub@e.neimanmarcus.com>, <https://...unsubscribe-url...>
```

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — CRITICAL
QA finding: RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not detected. Gmail's bulk sender policy requires this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe` so users can unsubscribe via the Gmail UI without visiting an external page. Required format:
```
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**Note:** Both headers may be present in the actual SMTP envelope but not captured by the AgentMail relay — this should be verified against raw message headers before treating as a definitive production gap.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not found in captured data. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain ESP envelope; DKIM alignment between the `From:` domain and the signing domain cannot be confirmed from available data. Verify DKIM `d=` tag matches `neimanmarcus.com` for RFC 5322 alignment.

**Physical address and opt-out link**
Cannot confirm from truncated HTML — assume present given NM's scale, but should be confirmed in full source review.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
With 26 tracking links skipped, UTM attribution on click-through URLs (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) cannot be confirmed. Given the `@refid`/`@base_refid` pattern, campaign attribution is likely appended server-side at click time, but this should be spot-checked against one resolved redirect URL to confirm parameters survive the redirect chain.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` identity stitching**
Two CRM fields (`uEm` = likely hashed email, `cmdid` = likely campaign or consumer ID) are looked up and presumably appended to landing page URLs for identity resolution. If the `NM_CRM_Feed` lookup returns null (e.g., for newly acquired addresses not yet in the CRM feed), these parameters will be empty strings in the URL — verify that the landing page handles missing identity parameters gracefully rather than throwing a client-side error.
