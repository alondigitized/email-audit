# Summer’s hottest beauty ☀️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- After six days of Sephora hammering Stacking Weekend into my inbox — the 🚨 flare gun on July 7th, the "ends tomorrow" panic on the 6th, the reminders on the 5th and 3rd — I open this and find... a multi-brand editorial? No sale. No points. Just "Summer's Hottest." I'll admit there's something almost refreshing about that pivot. I'm not being screamed at. But I'm also not being sold to in any way that makes sense for who I am as a Sephora Insider who actually buys.
- The layout itself is clean and organized — brand-by-brand sections with clear product imagery, a good grid feel, a few green checkmark badges that read like editor's picks. The Ordinary, MAC, Fenty, NARS, Sol de Janeiro, Violette_fr — these are real brands I care about. But it's a long scroll with no through-line. No anchor. No reason to click *now* rather than eventually maybe.
- I'd mark this as read, not click. I'd forward it to no one. If Sephora had tied any one of these picks to a loyalty offer — even a "Insider-only early access" nudge — I'd have clicked before I finished my coffee.

## What's working

- **Brand roster is credible** — The Ordinary, NARS, Fenty, Sol de Janeiro are all brands I actively shop. Showing me these names earns a second look.
- **Green checkmark badges** on select products feel like editor validation, not just inventory dumping. That's a nice editorial touch.
- **Clean visual hierarchy per section** — each brand block reads as its own module, easy to scan on mobile without my glasses.
- **No screaming** — after the 🚨 subject from July 7th, this email doesn't shout. That alone is a point in its favor.
- **Seasonal hook is real** — "Summer's Hottest" in early July is timely. Compared to Kohl's running that hourglass "ENDS TODAY" energy for six weeks straight, Sephora at least sounds like they checked a calendar.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer — none.** No %, no $, no free shipping threshold, nothing. After days of "30% off + 2X points," I'm trained to expect value. This feels like bait-and-switch in the other direction.
- **No loyalty signal anywhere visible.** I'm a Sephora Insider. I refuse to spend money without points. Neiman Marcus's Last Call email — scored 8/10 this week — at least surfaces the tier benefit. This email treats me like a stranger.
- **Too many brands, no hero.** Eight brands, a vacation collage at the bottom — it reads like Macy's-style overload, not Sephora's usually tighter editorial voice. I don't know what they *most* want me to buy.
- **"Summer's Hottest" is vague.** Neiman told me "up to 70% off" in five words. This subject gives me nothing I can act on before I even open.
- **Cadence whiplash.** Six emails in eight days, and the tone just shifted from urgency red-alert to leisurely discovery. I'm not calibrated for this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one loyalty hook in the hero.** Even "Earn points on every pick" or "2X points, Insiders" in the subhead turns this editorial into a reason to click today rather than next week.
- 2. **Pick a hero brand and lead with it.** The Ordinary Korean SPF is teased at the top — lean into that. Make it the one thing this email is *about* and let the rest be supporting cast.
- 3. **Give me a reason now vs. later.** "New arrivals" is not a call to action. "New this week — available now in-store and online" with a credible availability signal would move me.
- 4. **Trim the scroll.** Three to four brands, not eight. MAC, Fenty, Sol de Janeiro, NARS — that's a cohesive summer edit. Judy Bacalau and The Curlies feel like filler on this send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: The Ordinary Korean SPF + summer must-haves`
- **Subject Alt B:** `MAC, NARS, Fenty — your summer edit is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn Insider points on every single one`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Just landed — shop before it sells out`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Change to `minimum-scale=1` or remove `maximum-scale` entirely.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Blanket suppression of OS-level text scaling. Conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4. Acceptable for table/td to prevent Outlook reflow issues, but should be scoped away from `body`, `div`, `span`, `a`.

**Duplicate `@media` rule blocks**
The `.appear` class selector is defined three times inside the same `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` block:
```css
/* block 1 */
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block !important; width: 100% !important; ... }
/* block 2 — identical */
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
/* block 3 — identical to block 2 */
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
```
No functional bug (last rule wins, and they are identical), but it inflates stylesheet byte size unnecessarily.

**`robots` meta value has a space**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a valid directive — the correct token is `noindex`. Email clients ignore this tag, so there is no functional impact, but it indicates a template error.
