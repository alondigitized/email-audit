# Last Call: *Extra* 40% off sale styles starts now (& ends soon)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- Well, finally — a Neiman's email that just tells me the number. After two days of horse-and-linen mood boards and beach-stroll "Sand & Style" scrolls with zero pricing, this one opens with "EXTRA 40% OFF" in letters the size of my thumb, followed by "up to 75% off." That's the kind of math I actually stop scrolling for.
- The designer lineup underneath is doing real work for someone like me — Zimmermann, Akris, Veronica Beard, Stella McCartney, Fendi — that's a roster I recognize from actually walking the floor at Nordstrom and Saks, not some fast-fashion filler. But then it falls apart the same way every Neiman's Last Call email does: nine products, zero prices. I can't tell if that Fendi sunglasses "sale" is $180 or $380, and I'm not clicking through nine times to find out.
- Would I click? Probably, on the SHOP SALE button up top, because the offer itself is real and time-bound. Would I forward it to a friend? No — I'd forward the Sephora 4X points email before I'd forward this. This one's fine, not share-worthy.

## What's working

- The hero doesn't waste my time — 40% off, up to 75% off, "LAST CALL" branding I already know means real clearance, not a teaser.
- "Exclusions Apply" is small but at least it's stated up front near the CTA, not hidden three screens down like Macy's tends to do.
- The designer names in the product grid — Zimmermann, Akris, Cinq à Sept, Veronica Beard — read like my actual closet, not a 25-year-old's.
- Clean layout, one column of category buttons, nothing overlapping or broken — a relief after how busy Kohl's sends have been getting lately.
- A real deadline in the fine print (Ends 8/9/26 at 10PM CT) — that's more concrete than the vague "ends soon" up top.

## What's weak

- Not a single price on nine products. I have to click through blind to find out if the Stella McCartney bag is a steal or still $1,200 after 40% off.
- No InCircle loyalty mention anywhere — I've shopped Neiman's events for years and there's zero acknowledgment I might already be a member.
- That black "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF — join our text list" box sitting right after nine shop-by-category buttons is a swerve; I'm mid-sale-browsing and suddenly being pitched to hand over my phone number.
- This is the third distinct Neiman's send in my inbox this week — after "Field Notes," "Sand & Style," and two separate gift-card-math emails — and I'm starting to feel like I'm on a daily drip, not a curated list.
- No model, no styling shot, just flat-lay product tiles — fine for clarity, but it makes the email feel more like a catalog page than an invitation to an event, which is usually Neiman's strength over Macy's.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or percentage under every product tile — even just "40% off" repeated per item would close the gap between "I'm curious" and "I'm clicking." This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Cut the text-list opt-in box out of a sale email entirely, or move it to the very bottom near the footer where it won't compete with the shop-by-category CTAs.
- 3. Add one line acknowledging InCircle members — even "Earn double points on sale styles" would do more for me than a generic text-signup pitch.
- 4. Dial back the cadence — bundle "Last Call" and the gift-card promotion into one send instead of firing separate emails on back-to-back days; I'm cross-shopping three other stores and this is edging into inbox fatigue.
- 5. Swap one flat-lay for a single styled shot — even a small one — so this doesn't read as pure catalog next to how Neiman's "Field Notes" send actually looked like an event.
