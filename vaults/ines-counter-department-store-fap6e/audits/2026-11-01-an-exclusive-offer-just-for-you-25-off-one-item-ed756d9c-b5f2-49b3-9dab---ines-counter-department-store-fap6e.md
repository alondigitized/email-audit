# An exclusive offer just for you: 25% off one item
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident email, and for once Neiman's isn't shouting. A black box, "25% OFF," one code. After last week's gift-card and Fall Style Sale pushes, this is the first send that feels addressed to me. "Just for you," a unique code and a single-use offer read like a private note, not a blast.
- The fine print is where it falls apart. Beauty and fragrance are excluded, and I'm a beauty-counter regular. So are Chanel, Dior, Gucci, Hermès, Moncler, Prada and Saint Laurent, and the list runs on. The hero says "one regular-price item" and then the footer takes back most of what I'd want to buy. The offer ends 11/3 at 10 PM CT, and nothing above the fold says so. That's two days of runway I only find by reading the legal paragraph.
- I'd click, but only to test whether the item I want is eligible. I wouldn't forward it. Sephora's savings event email at least lets me use the discount on the thing I came for.

## What's working

- "25% OFF" is huge, white on black, and it's the first thing I see. There's no hunting for the offer.
- The unique code sits in its own cream band right under the hero. I can copy it without opening anything.
- "An exclusive offer just for you" and "one regular-price item" feel personal. It's much calmer than Kohl's seventh "20% off + $10 off" send since 10/26.
- The nine-tile grid (Veronica Beard, Miu Miu, Chloe, Ferragamo, Zegna and others) shows designer names. I know that wardrobe. The zebra coat is a nice echo of Friday's Veronica Beard email.
- The six category buttons are big, dark, and easy to tap with a thumb.

## What's weak

- The exclusions are buried in tiny type at the bottom. Beauty, fragrance, Chanel, Dior and Hermès are all out. I'd call that a bait-and-switch feeling, and it's the opposite of respect.
- The only deadline is in the fine print. "For a limited time" is vague. Macy's "LAST CHANCE" email at least puts urgency where I can see it.
- The product tiles aren't labeled with eligibility or price. I can't tell whether the Veronica Beard coat or the Miu Miu shoe actually qualifies. Some brands pictured may be excluded.
- There's nothing about my InCircle status or points. I won't use a card without points back, and this doesn't tell me whether the offer earns them.
- "Shop Now" goes to a general destination. The six category buttons below add choice but not direction.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the end date into the hero, e.g. "Ends Nov 3," and put a short "Not valid on beauty, fragrance, or select designers" line next to "Exclusions apply."
- 2. Show only eligible items in the grid, with a regular price on each. Add a "Shop eligible beauty-free favorites" button.
- 3. Add one InCircle line saying whether the purchase earns points.
- 4. Make "Shop Now" say what it does, e.g. "Shop 25% off."

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables user zoom on iOS and some Android clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern.
- **Missing language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` (or `xml:lang`), which hurts screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Template comments before the DOCTYPE:** the document opens with a large HTML comment, then `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`, before `<!DOCTYPE>`. Content before the DOCTYPE can push some legacy engines into quirks mode.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the subject. It shows up in web-view and some client tab or preview contexts.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and similar are exact-match attribute selectors. They stop matching if a second class is ever added to those elements.
- **Hamburger menu:** the nav uses a checkbox `:checked` hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). Gmail and most Outlook clients don't support it, so the nav needs a non-interactive fallback. I can't verify one from the truncated source.
- **Stylesheet issues:**
  - `.show` declares `width` twice (`auto !important` and `278px`).
  - The second `<style>` block has no `type` attribute, which is harmless.
- **Alt text and image dimensions:** not assessable, because the truncated source ends before the body.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template internals exposed:** the leaked comment lists variable names (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`). It also lists internal Data Extension names and lookups: `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)` and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)`. The `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` instruction is also visible, so the template scaffolding shipped with the send.
