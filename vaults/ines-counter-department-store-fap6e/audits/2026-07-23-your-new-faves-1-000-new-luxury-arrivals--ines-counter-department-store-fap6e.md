# Your new faves: 1,000+ new luxury arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-nine. That's where Neiman Marcus and I are this July, and walking into this one the morning after a "Tick. Tock. 70% off Last Call" email is a particular kind of tonal whiplash. One day they're practically begging me to clear their markdown racks; the next they're dropping a black-on-white editorial grid like I'm supposed to forget the sale even happened. I actually don't hate the pivot — editorial new arrivals beats another countdown clock — but the execution feels like it was built for the press release, not for me.
- What I see is a clean, cool grid of luxury names I know: Saint Laurent, Dries Van Noten, Toteme, Loewe. That lineup is right for a customer like me. But roughly half this email is men's product — JW Anderson denim, Kiton suiting, BVLGARI frames — and I'm not shopping for a husband. No price context, no loyalty signal, no beauty whatsoever (I'm a counter regular — this is my whole thing), and a CTA that drops me into a category page with 1,000+ items to sort through. On a Wednesday morning before work, I'm clicking close, not shopping.
- Mark as read. No forward.

## What's working

- **Brand lineup earns the email.** Saint Laurent, Dries Van Noten, Toteme, Loewe in a single grid — that's a legitimately strong new arrivals story and Neiman Marcus is one of the few places that can tell it.
- **Black hero is calm, not shouty.** After four days of markdown urgency, the restraint is noticeable. Saks rarely pulls this off without feeling cold; Neiman Marcus lands it.
- **Clean render, zero junk.** No broken images, no overlapping type, no lorem ipsum. Macy's Last Chance email this week had formatting friction I had to scroll past — this doesn't.
- **CTAs are labeled and visible.** "Shop Women's" and "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" are clearly differentiated from "Shop Men's." Simple but right.

## What's weak

- **Half the email is men's product.** I don't need Ann Paris plaids or Kiton blazers. A women's-first segmented version of this email exists somewhere; I didn't get it.
- **No beauty, not one product.** I spend real money at the Neiman Marcus beauty counter. Not a single skincare launch, fragrance, or makeup new arrival in this send. Sephora's "Summer Juice Bar" email last week did a better job of tying product novelty to me specifically — and Sephora scored a 6 from me.
- **No prices, no context.** I'm looking at a Saint Laurent bag thumbnail and a Dries Van Noten shoe with no signal of what I'm walking into. Even Kohl's — Kohl's — showed me instant Kohl's Cash and a rewards multiplier. Neiman gives me nothing about what membership means here.
- **"1,000+ arrivals" is the wrong number.** That's not inspiration, that's a catalog I don't have time for. Give me five hero pieces I need to see now.
- **Cadence is eating the email.** Twenty-nine emails in July. This one could have been good. At this volume, I'm pre-conditioned to skim.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the men's grid entirely.** If I've never browsed menswear, I shouldn't be seeing Kiton suiting taking up half my screen. This is a solved problem — Nordstrom does it routinely.
- 2. **Add one beauty hero.** A new Saint Laurent fragrance, a La Mer skincare launch, anything. A single beauty callout between the women's and men's grids would have stopped my scroll.
- 3. **Show at least one price — or a price range.** "Arriving from $X" anchors the editorial. Without it, the email reads like a mood board, not a shopping moment.
- 4. **Cut "1,000+ arrivals" and name 3-5 hero pieces in the subject.** Specificity beats volume.
- 5. **Surface loyalty/rewards context.** I refuse to use a card without points back. A line like "InCircle members: points on every new arrival" costs nothing and earns clicks.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a well-structured SFMC AMPscript template with responsive CSS, standard email-client compatibility resets, and a CSS-only hamburger nav. QA yields a 43% pass rate driven by four warnings — none are send-blockers, but two compliance gaps (List-Unsubscribe headers) carry regulatory weight, and the plain-text body is nearly all URLs, which is a deliverability liability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs (5,289 / 6,086 chars)**
Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) penalize plain-text alternatives that are URL-dense with no readable prose. This ratio suggests the plain-text version was auto-generated directly from the HTML without stripping tracked redirect URLs.

**26 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All 26 tracked links route through a redirect domain; the HTTP probe was skipped, so broken destinations cannot be confirmed or ruled out. These must be spot-checked manually or via an ESP link-validation pre-send tool.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces the same value as `@base_refid`. If a campaign-specific suffix was intended (e.g., `CONCAT(@base_refid,"_NL")`), it was omitted. This would cause all links to carry the bare base ref ID, making sub-campaign attribution impossible.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out viewport meta tags are dead code**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These don't affect rendering but indicate copy-paste drift across template versions. The active meta tag is correct; the dead variants should be removed.

**CSS-only hamburger nav has limited client support**
The `:checked` pseudo-selector pattern used for mobile nav interaction is not supported in Gmail (web or Android), Outlook 2013–2019, or Samsung Mail. In unsupported clients the nav renders as a static collapsed state with no way to expand it — acceptable as a progressive enhancement only if a fallback static nav is present. Not confirmed in the truncated source.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but some inbox preview systems surface the `<title>` value; the trailing space is a lint issue.

**No issues found** with core compatibility resets: `mso-table-lspace/rspace`, `.ExternalClass` line-height fix, `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` iOS link suppression, and `*[class=X]` attribute selectors (correct pattern for Gmail CSS class stripping) are all present and correct.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` scenario selection is ambiguous**

The template comment instructs the sender to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO":
```
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The SHA256 scenario is doubly-commented (inside `<!-- -->` wrapping `/* */`). If `@user` is intended to carry a hashed email for downstream personalization or pixel matching, this line is inert. Whichever scenario is correct, the choice should be enforced in the live AMPscript block, not left as a comment reminder.

**`@cmdid` missing assignment operator in documentation**
```
@cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
The comment block omits `=`. If the live AMPscript mirrors this typo, it is a syntax error that would cause AMPscript to error out or return a null value silently depending on error handling settings. Needs verification against the actual script body.

**No null/fallback guards visible for Lookup calls**
Three Lookup calls target `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`. If a subscriber's email is absent from either DE (e.g., new opt-in not yet synced), all dependent variables (`@user`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`) return empty strings. No `IIF` or default-value fallback is present in the visible portion. Silent nulls here would corrupt tracking links and any personalized content blocks that depend on these values.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header unconfirmed** `[QA WARN]`
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe` (and one-click `List-Unsubscribe-Post` per RFC 8058) for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. SFMC should be injecting these automatically; the gap is likely a relay-capture artifact, but must be verified by inspecting raw headers on a seed-address send.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) unconfirmed** `[QA WARN]`
Same caveat. Without this header, Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe UI is unavailable. Gmail may suppress the email or downrank it for non-compliance.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** `[QA WARN]`
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via this relay path. Given that the sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (a dedicated ESP subdomain), SPF and DKIM are almost certainly configured — but should be confirmed via a MXToolbox or mail-tester.com seed send on the next deployment.

**CAN-SPAM:** Unsubscribe mechanism and physical address are standard in NM templates; no issues identified in visible source. Footer content was truncated and not fully auditable here.
