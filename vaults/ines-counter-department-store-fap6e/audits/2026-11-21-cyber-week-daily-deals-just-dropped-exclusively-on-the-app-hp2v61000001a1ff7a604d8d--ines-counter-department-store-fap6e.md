# Cyber Week daily deals just dropped exclusively on the app 👀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the eleventh Sephora email I've seen in about ten days, and this one is at least trying to tell me something. It opens on a purple sequin hero with a big "30% OFF Benefit Cosmetics." That's a number I can read in half a second. Then I notice the whole thing is "exclusively on the app." I shop at the counter and at the brand event. I don't download an app so a retailer can ping me.
- Under the hero is a calendar of deals, 11/22 through 11/30. I like that one. It tells me what's coming and lets me plan, which is more respect than Kohl's gave me with its eighth "ends today" this week. But then the email keeps going: gift cards, a 30% tile, Kérastase, a gift guide, a credit card pitch and a texts pitch. Past the hero it turns into a catalog again, the same complaint I had on 11/14 and 11/17. I might click Benefit if I need mascara. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The 30% off Benefit hero is huge and clear. I know the offer before I've thought about it.
- The 11/22 to 11/30 deal calendar tells me which brands are coming and when. I can plan around it, and that's respectful.
- The brand names in the calendar, like Laura Mercier, Fenty and Kiehl's, match a beauty-counter shopper.
- The "Hi, Rosie" greeting and the free shipping line at the bottom feel like a known customer, even if the tiny greeting is easy to miss.
- The purple palette is cohesive and holiday-appropriate without being garish.

## What's weak

- "Exclusively on the app" shuts out how I actually shop. An offer that only works through a download feels like a toll, not a perk.
- The free sample bag banner at the top competes with the Benefit hero. That's two heroes before I've scrolled, and the fine print on the BAGITFIRST code, with its $95 minimum, is tiny and hard to read.
- After the calendar, the second half repeats itself. "Up to 30% off" appears again and the Kérastase gift sets come in alongside it. This is the Macy's-style overload I dislike.
- The legal block at the bottom is a wall of microscopic text. The sample bag terms (limit one, while supplies last) feel buried. The offer isn't clearly honest.
- The pitch for the Sephora credit card and texts is the "refuses to use a card without points" moment. It pushes a card at me when I already have my own points habits.
- Sephora at Kohl's keeps showing up in the footer. I find that odd for someone who cross-shops Nordstrom and Saks, and it dilutes the premium feel.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer. Make the Benefit 30% the only hero and move the sample bag lower, or send it as its own email.
- 2. Say upfront what the app is for, or give a web equivalent. If the 30% really is app only, tell me in the subject line, and let the web shopper in on at least one day.
- 3. Cut everything after the deal calendar except one CTA. The gift guide, Kérastase, gift cards, the card pitch and the texts pitch can wait.
- 4. Pull the sample bag terms into a readable line. Say the minimum spend in plain type.
- 5. Show a loyalty hook. Tell me what Beauty Insider tier I am and what I earn. I'd click that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Benefit 30% off today, app only 💜`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Cyber Week calendar: 30% Benefit, then Fenty on 11/27`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a free sample bag with $95. See every brand and day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One day only on the app. Here's the schedule through 11/30.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Fixed width:** The wrapper is `<table width="700" style="min-width:700px; width:700px">`. The `min-width:700px` forces horizontal scrolling or shrink-to-fit on clients that ignore the `max-width:480px` media query.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Invalid markup in `<style>`:** An `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size {…} <![endif]-->` block sits inside the `@media` rule. Conditional comments aren't valid inside CSS, so the `.mso-size` rule is likely ignored in Outlook.
- **Invalid robots value:** `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` should be `noindex`. This has little practical effect in email.
- **Missing language and roles:** `<html>` has no `lang` attribute, and the layout tables have no `role="presentation"`. Screen readers will announce them as data tables.
- **Plain-text part missing:** The QA check reports a text version of 0 chars. This is a `[FAIL]` for deliverability and for text-only or accessibility clients.
- **JSON-LD block:** The `<script type='application/ld+json'>` block includes `"@type": "DiscountOffer", "discountCode": ""`. The empty value is invalid structured data, and most clients strip `<script>` anyway.
