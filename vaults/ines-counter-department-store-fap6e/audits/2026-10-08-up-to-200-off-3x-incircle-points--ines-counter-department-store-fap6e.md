# Up to $200 off + 3x InCircle points
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Neiman's offer email in three days, and my gut says "I already know this one." On 10/6 I got "Grab it now: $200 off your purchase," on 10/7 "You could save $50–$200," and now "Up to $200 off" with the same lavender block, the same NMCIRCLE code and the same spend ladder. The only new thing is 3x InCircle points, and it's buried in a black strip under the hero. I'm a points person, so that strip should have been the headline.
- The email itself is tidy and doesn't shout. Neiman's is calmer than Kohl's, which has sent me about a dozen Deal Days emails since 10/4. But a quiet design doesn't make up for sending the same offer on repeat. I'd open it, scan the ladder, and probably not click today. I wouldn't forward it. Burberry on 10/7 was the Neiman's I actually like.

## What's working

- The spend ladder is clear: $200 gets $50, $500 gets $100, $750 gets $150, $1,000+ gets $200. I can do that math in two seconds.
- The lavender hero with a single black SHOP NOW button is easy to read and doesn't scream.
- The designer lineup (Alice + Olivia, rag & bone, Mac Duggal, Strathberry, Jimmy Choo, Tod's) feels like what I'd actually shop. The brand names under each product help.
- The code, the deadline (10/12 at 10:00 PM CT) and the points terms are all printed in full. Nothing is hidden, even if it's long.
- The category buttons, with Handbags and Women's Shoes among them, let me jump straight to what I want.

## What's weak

- The 3x InCircle points are an afterthought. "Plus, InCircle Members earn Triple Points" sits in a dark strip with "See terms & conditions below." Sephora puts "2X INSIDER" in giant red letters at the top, and that's how a points offer should look.
- It's the same offer as 10/6 and 10/7. "Limited time" feels hollow when I've seen it three times this week.
- The product grid has no prices, no "was/now" and no sale tags. I can't tell which items qualify. The fine print says sale and reduced-price items don't, so I'm guessing.
- The fine print is a wall of dense type. The exclusions (fur, jewelry, handbags and shoes can't earn bonus points, no gift cards) aren't something I want to find after I've already built a cart.
- The category buttons are lavender on lavender-gray with thin text. They're readable but low contrast, and they don't feel like buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the 3x points in the hero next to the dollar offer. Something like "$200 off + 3x points," with the points in the headline instead of a footnote.
- 2. Space out the sends. One offer email a week, with editorial in between like the Burberry and Prada sends.
- 3. Show the qualifying price on at least a few products so I know the offer applies.
- 4. Pull the exclusions that matter, like handbags, shoes and jewelry not earning bonus points, up into a short line near the offer.
- 5. Make the category buttons darker so they read as taps.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Triple points + up to $200 off, ends 10/12`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $200, save $50. Spend $1,000, save $200. Code NMCIRCLE.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Alice + Olivia, Jimmy Choo, Tod's and more. InCircle earns triple.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a hamburger-menu CSS hack. It has one confirmed code-level problem: internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) developer comments were left in the delivered HTML. The three QA warnings come from the AgentMail relay and don't show a defect in the email. The source was truncated, so body-level checks (links, alt text, footer) couldn't be done.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** QA skipped 32 tracking links because they are on click-redirect domains and were not probed. Their destinations, redirect health and UTM handling are unconfirmed.
- **Internal tracking variables exposed:** The leading comment references `@refid`, `@base_refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid`. This indicates the links carry per-recipient identifiers, but the truncated source doesn't show the final link format.
- No broken or malformed links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` caps scaling at 1, which blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern.
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang` attribute. Screen readers have to guess the language.
- **Weak title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic, has a trailing space, and doesn't reflect the subject line.
- **Checkbox hamburger menu:** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked` rules rely on the `:checked` hack. Gmail and several Outlook clients don't support it. Check that the nav still works there, or falls back to something usable.
- **Class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]` and similar rules use exact-match attribute selectors. They stop matching if an element ever gets more than one class.
- **Dead code:** The source has several commented-out viewport tags (`user-scalable=no`, `width=320`, `width=1024`). They have no effect but add weight.
- **Not assessable:** Image alt text, the preheader and the footer text are outside the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template internals shipped to recipients:** The first lines of the delivered HTML are an AMPscript developer comment. It exposes these internal names:
  - Data extensions: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`
  - Fields: `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm` and `cmdid`
  - The scenario note `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`
- **Why it matters:** This is a template-hygiene failure and information disclosure. The comment is invisible to readers but readable in view-source and to any scraper. It also suggests a scenario-selection step was never finalized.
- No unrendered visible tokens (such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}`) were found in the provided excerpt.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both headers were not found. QA says the relay may not capture them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a violation. Check the original message in a direct inbox.
- **Authentication-Results:** The header is missing, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. QA expects this through the relay. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain, which is normal for this setup, but alignment couldn't be tested.
- **Footer:** The physical address and visible unsubscribe link are in the truncated portion and couldn't be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No landing URLs or UTM parameters appear in the provided excerpt, and the tracking links were skipped. Continuity can't be assessed from this data.
- The `@refid` and `@base_refid` variables point to campaign-calendar-based attribution. Whether it ends up as a query parameter or only in the redirect is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the SFMC developer comment block** (the `REQUIRED variables` and `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` comment) from the rendered output. Use AMPscript-side `/* */` comments, which SFMC removes before send, instead of HTML comments.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta and delete the commented-out viewport variants.
3. **Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"`** to `<html>`, and use a descriptive `<title>`, ideally matching the subject.
4. **Test the hamburger nav** in Gmail (web and app) and Outlook. Make sure the nav links are reachable without the `:checked` hack.
5. **Re-run the audit** with the full HTML source, and resolve a sample of the 32 tracking links to check their destinations and UTM or `refid` parameters.
6. **Check the headers on a direct inbox copy.** Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC results. Treat the relay-related warnings as pending until then.
