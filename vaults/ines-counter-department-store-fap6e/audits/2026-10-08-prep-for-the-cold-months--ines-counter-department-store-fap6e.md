# Prep for the cold months
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is the quiet Neiman's again, and after four days of dollar-off ladders I'm relieved. There's no code, no countdown and no InCircle math. I got "Up to $200 off + 3x InCircle points" earlier today, so a plain outerwear editorial lands like someone lowered the volume. It looks pretty. The vested model in the wide burgundy trouser is the kind of styling I'd screenshot.
- It's also thin. "Vested Interest" is a clever headline, but nothing tells me what to buy or why today. The second half is a man's green leather bomber, and I'm not shopping for him. I'd read it, appreciate the restraint, and probably not click. Forwarding it to a friend? Only if she were shopping for a man's jacket.

## What's working

- The hero photo is gorgeous. The vest over sheer leopard with wide trousers feels like a real stylist built it.
- "Vested Interest" is witty and sets a fall mood without shouting.
- The two outlined buttons, "Shop Outerwear" and "Shop Designer Collections," are easy to see and easy to tap.
- After the Burberry send on 10/7 and the Prada send on 10/6, this follows the same tasteful pattern. Neiman's is better when it isn't pushing a discount.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" is a real service signal. Macy's never gives me that.

## What's weak

- I can't find any offer, and no InCircle points or member benefit appear anywhere. I don't use a card without points back, so a quiet send that ignores my tier does nothing for me.
- The second half is menswear, and the only text link there is "Shop Men's Outerwear." That's a miss for me.
- The lower banner copy ("Look cool, stay warm...") is generic and could be any retailer's. It names no brand, price or piece.
- The buttons are thin gray outlines with light type. They're polite but timid, and the small gray text is hard to read on a phone.
- Today's earlier "$200 off + 3x points" email makes this one feel like it was sent from a different brand.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the designers. Caption the hero with the brand and price so I know what I'm looking at. Sephora Insider at least tells me what I'm getting, even when it hides the minimum spend.
- 2. Make the second panel women's, or let me choose. Show a woman's coat first, then the men's coat.
- 3. Add one line of loyalty. Something like "InCircle members: 2x points on outerwear" would turn an editorial into a reason to buy this week. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" is loud, but at least it gives me a reason.
- 4. Make the buttons solid and bigger, with specific copy such as "Shop women's coats."
- 5. Pace the sends. This is the fifth Neiman's email this week, so either merge today's two or hold one.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**

All four are relay or capture limitations rather than confirmed defects in the email, and the truncation prevents checking links, images and footer.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- QA skipped 17 tracking links (click-redirect domains) from HTTP probing, so their destinations are unverified.
- The "Plain-text is >70% URLs" warning (3199 of 3707 chars) points to tracked URLs in the text part. This is a deliverability signal, not a broken link.
- No broken or malformed links can be confirmed, because the truncated source doesn't show the `<a href>` values.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- The mobile CSS uses attribute selectors like `*[class=contenttable]`. These match only a class attribute that is exactly that value, so an element with extra classes won't match. This is fragile but not a confirmed failure.
- The hamburger nav depends on `:checked` checkbox and `~` sibling selectors (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). Gmail app and Outlook strip or ignore these, so the nav likely won't work there. This is inferred from the CSS and not tested.
- `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and carries no subject context. It is minor.
- `maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta blocks pinch-zoom in some clients, which is an accessibility concern.
- Alt text, `lang` and `role="presentation"` can't be assessed because the source is truncated.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- The top of the source has an HTML comment holding internal SFMC/AMPscript setup notes. It lists `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, the `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` data extension names, and the instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW". It ships to recipients and exposes internal data-model names. That is an information leak, though low severity.
- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the truncated source.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- QA reports the `List-Unsubscribe` header not found and the `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not found. Both warnings say the AgentMail relay may have stripped them. They are unconfirmed, and the sender may well include them. If they are really absent, that would conflict with Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- `Authentication-Results` was not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The cause is the relay, so this is a gap in the test setup and not an email defect. The sender domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated subdomain.
- The physical address and the in-body unsubscribe link are in the truncated footer, so they can't be verified.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- No UTM parameters or landing URLs can be confirmed from the visible source. The `@refid` and `@base_refid` variables suggest campaign tracking through a refid parameter and not UTMs.
- QA skipped probing of the 17 tracking links, so landing page alignment is unverified.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Remove the internal SFMC variable and data-extension comment block from the production template before send.
2. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` in the raw message at the source (not through the relay). If missing, add them.
3. Capture `Authentication-Results` from the original delivery, or check DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` directly.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta to allow zoom.
5. Add a plain-text part with readable copy and fewer raw tracking URLs.
