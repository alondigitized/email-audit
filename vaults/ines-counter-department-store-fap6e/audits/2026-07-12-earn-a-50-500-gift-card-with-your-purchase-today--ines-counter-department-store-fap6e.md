# Earn a $50–$500 gift card with your purchase today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Okay. After fourteen Neiman Marcus emails since July 2nd — "Last Call," "70% off," a wedding dress out of nowhere — this one finally speaks my language. Earn a gift card for spending money I was probably going to spend anyway? That's not a sale. That's points. That's my actual love language. I noticed immediately that the spend/earn tiers are right there in the hero — no digging, no asterisk hunt, just: spend $500, get $50 back. Clean.
- What gives me pause is the fine print situation. There's a wall of promotional legalese at the bottom that would take me three minutes to decode on my phone while waiting for an Uber. Sephora Insider has trained me to expect exclusion clauses, so I know to look — and when I see a block of text that dense, I assume the headline offer is more complicated than it appears. That erodes some of the goodwill the hero built.
- I'd mark this read and probably come back to it before my next NM purchase this weekend. I would not forward it — gift card promotions feel private, like a deal you keep to yourself. Click? Yes, but only once I've mentally committed to buying something anyway. This email is the push, not the reason.

## What's working

- **The earn table is the hero, and it earns it.** Tiers laid out cleanly — spend $500/$1,000/$3,000/$11,000+, earn $50/$125/$250/$500. I can do that math before I even scroll.
- **Code JASNM is front and center.** No hunting. No "find your code in your account." It's right there, which is exactly how Macy's should have handled their confirmation email from last week instead of making me click through to confirm.
- **Designer brands in the product grid pull weight.** Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, FRAME, Veronica Beard, Theory — these are names I actually shop. This isn't Kohl's sending me a striped sundress on a 20%-off coupon I've seen four days in a row.
- **"Match with a personal style advisor" in the footer is a quiet trust signal.** NM earns this — it doesn't feel like a gimmick when it's them saying it.

## What's weak

- **Zero beauty in this email.** I'm a counter regular. I've spent meaningful money on La Mer and Chanel at NM. There's not a single SKU, brand badge, or even a "shop beauty" CTA. The gift card mechanic would push me toward a skincare restock fast if they'd connected the dots.
- **The fine print block at the bottom is a red flag I can read from here.** All I can tell is that it's long, small, and full of exclusion language. That undercuts an offer that was doing so well up top.
- **"Limited Time" without a date is vague.** "Today" is in the subject. The email body says "limited time." Those two signals don't match. Is this a one-day offer or a week? I've been burned before.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email fifteen (or close) from NM since July 2nd. Even a great mechanic lands flat when I'm on my third coffee and already a little numb to the stream.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard end date.** "Earn through July 15" beats "limited time" every time. Credible urgency, not vague pressure.
- 2. **Add a beauty-specific CTA.** "SHOP BEAUTY" should sit alongside "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING." A single button change that captures the customer segment most likely to spend $500 in one trip.
- 3. **Soften the fine print visually.** Consider a collapsible "full terms" link instead of a 200-word paragraph at the bottom. Sephora Insider does this well — the offer headline is trusted because the asterisk isn't terrifying.
- 4. **Let the spend tiers breathe a little more on mobile.** The table is doing a lot of work and at small sizes the earn amounts can blur together.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $500, earn $50 back — gift card offer ends Tuesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next NM purchase just got a $50–$500 bonus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four tiers. Use code JASNM. Ends July 15.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jimmy Choo, FRAME, Zimmermann — earn as you shop this weekend.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus Gift Card Email

### 1. Technical Summary

SFMC AMPscript template rendered as a table-based HTML email with responsive CSS breakpoints. QA passed at 57% with 3 warnings — all compliance/deliverability headers — and 26 tracking links unverifiable; no hard errors.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** Click-redirect domains (presumably `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) were not HTTP-probed, so destination URL integrity, UTM parameter presence, and redirect chain health are unconfirmed.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` in URL parameters (risk, not confirmed bug).** The AMPscript comment block declares `@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)` and `@cmdid = Lookup(...)`. If either resolves to empty string on a subscriber with no CRM Feed match, links containing these tokens will append blank/malformed query params — no visible breakage but dirty analytics and potential 404s depending on landing page validation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in live viewport tag — accessibility violation.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents browser zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Users with low vision cannot zoom.

**Three commented-out viewport tags — template debt.**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Non-functional but indicates unresolved template experimentation. The `user-scalable=no` variant in the first comment is an identical accessibility violation — if uncommented it would replace the live tag with the same problem.

**Duplicate media query breakpoints.** Two separate `<style>` blocks define responsive rules: one at `max-width: 600px` and a second at `max-device-width: 480px` / `max-width: 480px` / `max-device-width: 520px` / `max-width: 520px`. `max-device-width` is deprecated. No functional breakage in current clients, but introduces maintenance risk.

**No `alt` text visible in truncated source for product images.** Could not confirm — HTML is truncated before image blocks. Should be verified against full source.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` scenario selector left as comment — template hygiene issue.**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The comment block header says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," indicating `@user` is conditionally set to either a CRM lookup or a SHA-256 hash. The chosen path is not visible in the truncated source, but leaving the unchosen scenario as an inline code comment exposes implementation detail (hashing strategy, data extension name `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`) to anyone who views source. No data leakage at send time, but poor template hygiene.

**`@emailid` fallback lookup.** Comment documents: `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)` as fallback if `@emailid` not in sendable DE. If a subscriber is not in `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `@emailid` resolves to empty string. Downstream impacts depend on whether `@emailid` appears in link params or suppression logic.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed** (QA WARN). AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If absent at delivery, Gmail and Apple Mail will not show the native one-click unsubscribe UI, and Yahoo/Google bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) may not be met for high-volume sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed** (QA WARN). Required for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe to function. SFMC supports this header natively — if missing, it suggests the relay is stripping headers or the sending configuration omits it.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN). SPF and DKIM pass/fail status unverifiable through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment from this capture. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — should be verified directly against MX/DNS or a full header capture from a live inbox.

No physical postal address or unsubscribe link was visible in the truncated source — these are CAN-SPAM requirements. Cannot confirm presence; must be verified in full HTML.
