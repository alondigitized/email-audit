# Last chance for up to 40% off select regular-price & sale styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the same "Sale" typographic hero. The same oversized decorative "S" with "ale" in italics beneath it. I gave Neiman Marcus real credit for this creative on Thursday when they sent "Have you shopped these new sale styles?" — the proportions were bold, the hierarchy worked, and it felt like a campaign identity. That was four emails ago. At this point they've taken a good piece of furniture and moved it to every room in the house hoping I won't notice it's the same couch.
- What actually earns this email a second look is the product grid below the fold. Zimmermann, Dries Van Noten, Manolo Blahnik, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera — that is a lineup curated for someone exactly like me, not a 26-year-old browsing Instagram. Forty percent off Ferragamo is not the same as forty percent off Steve Madden. These are the designers where the markdown actually means something, and the email gets credit for leading with them. No prices are shown next to any of the images, which is a squandered opportunity.
- But here's the deal-breaker for me: nowhere in this email — not in the hero, not in a callout strip, not in the footer — is there a single word about loyalty, InCircle status, or what I'm earning back on this purchase. I do not buy at Neiman Marcus without knowing what my points situation is. Sephora ran "2X points + 30% off" in a single subject line last week and I knew exactly what I was getting. This email talks about what I'll save but says nothing about what I'll earn. Mark as read. Won't click today.

## What's working

- The product grid is doing real work — Zimmermann, Manolo Blahnik, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, and Carolina Herrera read as curated for a 45–55 luxury shopper, not a mass-blast to everyone on file
- Visual hierarchy is still clean: offer lands in the first scroll position, SHOP SALE button is unambiguous
- No render bugs anywhere — images load, text is legible, layout holds
- The "Refresh your summer wardrobe with new sale styles" line has a quiet editorial voice that doesn't shout
- Category grid at the bottom (SHOP WOMEN'S DRESSES, SHOP HANDBAGS) gives me a fast path to what I actually want without starting at the sale homepage

## What's weak

- This is the same typographic "Sale" hero creative I've seen in at least three of the last four Neiman Marcus emails — the creative equity has been fully spent
- "Up to an extra 40% off" is stacking language that makes me suspicious: extra on top of what, exactly, and for which items?
- "Exclusions Apply" sits in small text under the hero with zero context — I've been burned before clicking through and finding half the email's implied inventory is excluded
- No loyalty or points-back signal anywhere visible — this is a hard blocker for how I actually shop
- No pricing shown next to the product images — if Zimmermann is in a sale email, tell me it was $595 and is now $357; the brand name alone doesn't close the deal

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this creative immediately** — even a full-bleed editorial shot of the Zimmermann piece against a summer backdrop would read as fresh compared to the same "S ale" splash for the fifth time in four days
- 2. **Put actual sale prices under each product image** — "Was $495 / Now $297" does more persuasive work than any headline copy at this point
- 3. **Add one InCircle or loyalty line** — "InCircle members earn Double Points through midnight" is the sentence that changes my behavior; its absence is the single biggest mechanical miss
- 4. **Clarify the "extra" stacking** — if this is additive to existing markdowns, say "an additional 40% taken at checkout on already-reduced prices"; otherwise it reads like pricing sleight-of-hand
- 5. **Surface two or three exclusion categories upfront** — "Excludes Chanel beauty, timepieces, and final-sale items" builds more trust than a blanket disclaimer

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your sale access expires tonight — the designers you've been watching are still here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Prices we haven't seen in months. Manolo, Carolina Herrera, Giorgio Armani. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `InCircle Double Points + up to 40% off — but only through midnight.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Last Chance 40% Off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Salesforce Marketing Cloud deployment using AMPscript for personalization. QA returned 4 warnings, none failures — the primary concerns are compliance header visibility through the AgentMail relay, internal infrastructure details exposed in HTML comments, and plain-text body URL density.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped (unverifiable)**
All click-redirect URLs were excluded from HTTP probing. Destination URLs and UTM parameter correctness cannot be confirmed from this audit run.

**Plain-text version is 87% URLs**
QA: `5225 / 6027 chars are URLs`. A plain-text alternative that is almost entirely raw redirect URLs will be flagged by Postmaster-level spam filters and renders unreadably on plain-text clients. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking wrappers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<html>` element missing `lang` attribute**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
No `lang="en"` — screen readers cannot infer language. Violates WCAG 3.1.1.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants left in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Testing artifacts in production source. No functional impact, but increases HTML payload and exposes iterative debugging history.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; some clients render the title in preview or tab context.

**iOS 9 hamburger CSS still present**
```css
/*Hamburguer fix IOS9*/
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
```
iOS 9 shipped in 2015. This is dead code adding ~1KB of CSS noise. The keyframe animation fires on every body render for no recipient.

**Interactive checkbox-hack nav**
The CSS-only hamburger menu uses `:checked~` sibling selectors. This pattern has known failures in Gmail (strips `<input>` elements) and Outlook Web. If the hamburger is functional content rather than decorative, this needs a fallback audit against those clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Internal Data Extension names exposed in HTML comments**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables:
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
-->
```
`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` are internal SFMC Data Extension names. These comments are delivered verbatim to every recipient's inbox. View-source exposes your CRM schema.

**Ambiguous `@user` variable assignment**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The template comments define `@user` via a `Lookup()` call, but a second commented-out block sets it from a SHA-256 hash. It is unclear which path is active; the inline comment `/* ... */` inside an HTML comment suggests this may be an AMPscript block that was accidentally double-commented and silently no-ops, leaving `@user` populated only by the Lookup path.

**No-op concatenation on `@refid`**
```html
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
`CONCAT(x, "")` returns `x` unchanged. This is either dead logic or a placeholder that was never completed.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" operator instruction in source**
```html
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW
```
This is a template-construction note aimed at the email developer, not resolved before send. It indicates a manual decision step exists in the template prep workflow — a process risk for accidental sends with the wrong scenario selected or no selection made.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
QA: `List-Unsubscribe header not found`. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policy (enforced Feb 2024) for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. NM is well above that threshold. If the header is being injected by the SFMC sending configuration and simply not captured by the AgentMail relay, confirm via raw header inspection on a delivered copy.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected**
QA: `List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. One-click unsubscribe is now a hard requirement under the same Gmail/Yahoo policy. Without this, Gmail may display a less prominent unsubscribe path or apply additional filtering weight.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
QA: `Authentication-Results header not found`. `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — DKIM alignment should be configured. The AgentMail relay is not surfacing the auth results header, so pass/fail cannot be confirmed here. Verify DMARC alignment on `neimanmarcus.com` against the `e.neimanmarcus.com` sending domain.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 tracked outbound links were skipped during probing — UTM parameter presence and correctness cannot be verified from this run. No issues confirmed, but also none ruled out.

The `@refid` / `@base_refid` pattern suggests campaign attribution parameters are appended via AMPscript at render time. If the no-op `CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is the actual refid construction logic and `@base_refid` is unset, links could be sending with a blank or null `refid`, silently breaking campaign attribution.
