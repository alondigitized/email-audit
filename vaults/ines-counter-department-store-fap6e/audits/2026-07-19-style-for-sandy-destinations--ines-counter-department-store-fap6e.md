# Style for sandy destinations
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. I'm going to assume that's where I am with Neiman Marcus this July, and after three consecutive days of LAST CALL banners and 70%-off subject lines, I'll admit "Style for sandy destinations" made me do a small double-take. It's their editorial cleanse — same move they pulled with "Summer Aisle Style" on the 17th, which I gave a 7. A beautiful woman in a floral print on a beach, a raffia tote that I'd genuinely shop, and a men's module that reminds me I'm sharing this email with someone who isn't me.
- Here's my problem: this is gorgeous for what it is, and I appreciate that NM finally gave the sale drum a rest. But there's no offer. No points event. No early-access hook. Nothing that makes me feel like a known customer rather than a pretty eyeball. Saks sends editorial like this, but they layer in a "Double Points This Weekend" line somewhere — it earns the editorial. NM just expects me to click on vibes alone.
- I'll mark it read. I won't forward it. I might tap "Shop Bags & Raffia" because I've been looking for exactly that bag, but I won't feel rewarded for doing so.

## What's working

- **The hero image earns it.** The floral dress, the muted beach palette, the pose — this is aspirational without being alienating. The model's styling reads 35-50, which is close enough to me that I don't feel like the email was built for my daughter.
- **The raffia tote module is perfectly timed.** "Woven from a favorite summer material and ready for the beach, pool, and beyond" — that's a product story, not just a sentence. I'm in-market for exactly this, and the direct "Shop Bags & Raffia" link is specific and useful.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Logo, nav, hero, headline, CTA, product, CTA — my eye travels down without confusion. No Macy's-style four-banner overload happening here.
- **Tonal reset is earned.** After the LAST CALL barrage, an email that doesn't scream at me actually lands. NM made a real creative choice here, and I respect it even if I'm not sure I'll act on it.

## What's weak

- **Men's module is dead air for me.** "Relaxed, But Ready" with a man in coral separates — that's half an email I just scrolled past. If NM knows I shop women's, this slot should be mine. Nordstrom would have segmented this out.
- **No offer, no loyalty hook.** I refuse to use a card without points back; this email gives me zero reason to put NM's card in my hand right now. Sephora's 4X points email earlier this week was the standard. This doesn't clear that bar.
- **"Post-Worthy Looks" headline is a little too millennial.** I know what it means, I use Instagram, but the phrasing puts me on the younger side of their target — slight miss on voice for a 47-year-old customer.
- **"Shop Vacation" as a CTA is vague.** It's a category, not a destination. "Shop the Look" or "Shop Raffia Totes" would earn the click better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a loyalty/points callout, even a small one.** One line — "Earn triple points on full-price vacation styles through Sunday" — and this email becomes an action item instead of a catalog page. That single change is worth 2-3 points of click lift.
- 2. **Pull the men's module for female subscribers.** Segment this. A woman who cross-shops beauty counters doesn't need vacation separates for a man she may or may not have. Give that real estate to a second women's product story.
- 3. **Make "Shop Vacation" specific.** "Shop Raffia Totes" or "Shop the Vacation Edit" lands harder. Specificity signals they curated for me, not just pointed at a category.
- 4. **Rephrase the hero headline.** "Post-Worthy Looks" is doing influencer-speak. Try "Your Packing List Starts Here" or "Dressed for Every Shore" — something that respects my age and still sounds current.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Raffia, florals, and everything you need at the shore`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your vacation wardrobe, curated`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The tote, the dress, the sandal — shop NM's summer edit before it sells out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beach-ready looks from the brands you trust — plus triple points this weekend`

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) table-based template with a hamburger nav and responsive breakpoints. No critical rendering bugs are present, but two compliance gaps and one authentication visibility gap warrant attention before the next send.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links skipped** — All click-redirect URLs were excluded from HTTP probing (QA warning). These cannot be confirmed live without a full link audit against the click-redirect domain. No dead links were confirmed, but also none were verified.

**Plain-text version is URL-saturated** — 3,138 of 3,647 characters (86%) in the plain-text part are URLs. This is well above the 70% threshold flagged by the QA check. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) penalize high URL density in plain-text parts. The plain-text version appears to be auto-generated from the HTML rather than hand-curated.

No issues found with UTM parameter structure from the truncated source — tracking parameters appear present on redirect URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport tags** — Three alternate `<meta name="viewport">` tags are commented out in `<head>`. These are dead code and create maintenance noise, but do not affect rendering.

**Hamburger nav via CSS checkbox hack** — The mobile nav uses `:checked~` sibling selector on a hidden `<input type="checkbox">`. This pattern breaks in Gmail App (Android/iOS) and Outlook mobile, which strip `<style>` blocks. Users on those clients will see no hamburger trigger, degrading to a flat nav row. Known limitation of this pattern; no fix is flagged unless the client list requires it.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` loop** — A `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` with a 1s infinite animation is applied to `body` on mobile as an iOS 9 scroll-flicker workaround. iOS 9 is end-of-life (2019). This can be removed.

**`alt` attribute coverage** — Cannot confirm from truncated source, but the QA pass rate is 43% with 0 confirmed issues, suggesting the checker did not flag missing alts. Should be verified on full source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Commented-out SHA-256 scenario is dead code in production HTML** — The comment block at the top documents two persona scenarios. The line `/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */` is inside an AMPscript comment but present in the rendered HTML sent to recipients. This exposes internal variable naming conventions (`@user`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, `@base_refid`) to anyone viewing source.

**`@uEm` / `@cmdid` lookups** — These pull from `NM_CRM_Feed` via `Lookup()`. If a subscriber record is absent from that data extension, these variables resolve to empty string silently. Downstream personalization or suppression logic depending on `@cmdid` would fail open. No evidence of a fallback guard in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA WARN) — The AgentMail relay did not capture this header. CAN-SPAM does not mandate `List-Unsubscribe`, but Gmail and Yahoo Mail use it to surface the unsubscribe button in the UI. Its absence increases spam complaints. SFMC should be injecting this automatically on bulk sends; the gap is likely a relay-capture artifact, but should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers from a seed-list send.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA WARN) — One-click unsubscribe (Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirement) requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Same caveat as above — confirm against raw headers, not relay capture.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. `e.neimanmarcus.com` is the sending domain; DKIM signing should be configured on that subdomain. Cannot confirm from available data.

An unsubscribe link is present in the footer (standard SFMC placement) — CAN-SPAM physical address and opt-out mechanism are expected to be present; cannot confirm from truncated source.
