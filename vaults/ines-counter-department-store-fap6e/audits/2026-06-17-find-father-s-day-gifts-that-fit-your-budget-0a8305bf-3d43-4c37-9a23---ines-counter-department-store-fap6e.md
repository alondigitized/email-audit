# Find Father's Day gifts that fit your budget 🎁
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. That's where Kohl's sits in my June inbox — I actually checked before writing this. And this one shows up dressed as a Father's Day gift guide with a "fits your budget" angle, which is fine as a premise except I've been watching this exact 20% offer rotate through every possible holiday and life moment since June 1. It's the same coupon wearing a different holiday hat.
- That said, I'll give it this: somewhere in the middle of a very male-coded email about dad gifts and electronics, there's a Sephora at Kohl's banner offering up to 25% off shampoos and conditioners ending June 21. That's the only thing in this entire scroll that speaks to me directly. One beauty module buried under khaki shirts and JBL speakers. I wouldn't forward this to anyone. I'd click the Sephora section, ignore everything else, and maybe re-scan for the Kohl's Cash earn terms because I don't put a dollar through any register without knowing the points math.
- The Kohl's Cash callout at the bottom is the right instinct — I am exactly the person who reads that fine print. But this email treats me like a generic gift-buyer, not a loyalty customer who's been opening Kohl's emails for months. Compared to the June 13 send, which at least had a credible Father's Day deadline that created urgency, this one feels like they hit send one day too late with nothing new to say.

## What's working

- **Kohl's Cash earn mechanic is front and center** — "$10 for every $50 spent through 6/27" is exactly the math I want visible before I decide whether to add to cart. This is why I have the card.
- **Sephora at Kohl's partnership section** — I don't expect beauty when I open a Father's Day email, so finding 25% off shampoos mid-scroll was a small pleasant surprise. Neiman Marcus would have led with that for someone like me.
- **Budget price tiers are useful** ($15, $25, $50 under) — smartly structured for someone shopping as a gift buyer on a range, even if I'm not the dad.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no lorem ipsum bleeding through, text reads at normal zoom. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm" send I reviewed recently was far more cluttered layout-wise.
- **Free store pickup callout** — I do cross-shop in-store; that signal matters.

## What's weak

- **Twenty-second email, same 20% offer** — Sephora's June 22 follow-up send felt repetitive after five days; Kohl's has been running this same discount for 22+ sends. The offer has stopped feeling like an offer and started feeling like a default price.
- **The entire hero section is male-coded** — Dad, khakis, JBL speaker. I am the buyer, not the recipient, and nothing in the visual language acknowledges that. Neiman Marcus at least surfaces the shopper's desire alongside the gift occasion.
- **"Electronics, toys, shoes do not apply" buried under a category tile** — I almost didn't catch it. If you're showing me a speaker at 30% off and then exclusion-ing it at the bottom of the same tile, that's a trust erosion.
- **CTA copy is interchangeable** — every button says "Shop Now." Even a single button labeled "Shop Beauty Gifts" or "Shop Dad's Finds Under $25" would help me route faster.
- **The Sephora deal ends June 21** — today is the 17th. That's four days of urgency they completely failed to surface in the subject or hero. That's a miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the Sephora deal in the subject line** — there's a real, time-bound beauty offer ending in 4 days inside this email. That's the hook for me, and it's invisible from the inbox.
- 2. **Add a "Kohl's Cash ends soon" urgency bar** near the top, not buried in the footer. That's what makes me act.
- 3. **Swap at least one CTA to "Shop Beauty Deals"** — not all Shop Now. Give me a lane.
- 4. **Pull the electronics exclusion language into the tile header**, not below it. "30% off select electronics (exclusions apply)" is cleaner than the current hide-it-below approach.
- 5. **Vary the 20% offer** — even framing it as "20% off + earn Kohl's Cash" in the same line, instead of two separate asks, would feel fresher.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Beauty deals inside — plus a gift he'll actually use`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off Sephora + Father's Day picks under $25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sephora at Kohl's sale ends June 21 — plus earn Kohl's Cash on everything`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Gifts under $25, $50 and up — and your 20% coupon is waiting`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Father's Day Gifts" Email

Functional email with no critical failures, but carries three compliance gaps (unsubscribe headers, SPF/DKIM verification) and pervasive missing alt text across 13+ images. Tracking infrastructure is heavy but standard for Kohl's.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

No broken links detected. 22 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe — expected for wrapped URLs through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text missing on 13 images**, including content-bearing images (product shots and likely promo banners from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`):

| Image | URL pattern |
|---|---|
| Open-pixel | `click.chp.kohls.com/o/19e72a09...` |

| AAM pixel | `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` |

| 3× footer/misc images | `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` |

Tracking pixels (1×1) with missing alt is a minor violation — correct fix is `alt=""` (already done on the MessageGears gif via `aria-hidden="true"`, but not consistently applied elsewhere).

Product images (`54594746_2_2x`, `54594746_10_2x`, `54594746_5s_2x`) missing alt text is a real accessibility gap — screen readers will fall back to filename or skip entirely.

**`<title>` tag is empty.** Some email clients surface the title; leaving it blank is a minor omission.

**Malformed HTML:** `</custom>` closing tag appears after the open-pixel block — not a valid element, likely a template artifact:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility concern (WCAG 1.4.4).

**Google Fonts loaded three ways** (two `<link>` tags + two `@import` rules for the same families). Redundant fetches; minor performance issue, no rendering breakage.
