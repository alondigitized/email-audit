# 2 days only 👀 Save 30% on the Kohl's brands you love 💕
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Forty-five. That's my rough count of Kohl's in my inbox since around Memorial Day, and today's send has the nerve to open with "FIRST TIME EVER" — which, honestly, made me pause. Thirty percent off the house brands is genuinely newsworthy, and the clean white hero with that bold circular offer badge communicates it without the visual chaos I've come to expect. The two-day window reads as real urgency for once, not the "HOURS LEFT" theatrics from the July 5th send that had me rolling my eyes while I sipped my coffee.
- But right there in the hero badge, beneath the Kohl's logo, I can see there's small exclusion text — and I'm conditioned enough to know it says something I won't like about Kohl's Cash. That little block of fine-print-in-the-badge has a way of sucking the air out of a sale for me. Thirty percent off sounds incredible until I realize I'm not stacking my way to something extraordinary — I'm just getting a straight discount on brands I could have waited out another week. It's the same sting I felt with that Macy's "extra 25% off" confirmation email that buried all the exclusions once you'd already clicked through.
- I'd mark this as "revisit tonight" rather than "click now." The "FIRST TIME EVER" hook is doing real work, the two-day window would make me come back before bed, but I'm not forwarding this to a friend. Neiman Marcus's "Witness the big day in something elegant" had me texting someone within minutes of opening. This one just does its job.

## What's working

- "FIRST TIME EVER" is carrying legitimate news weight — if this is genuinely the first 30% on Kohl's brands, that's a real hook, not manufactured urgency, and it differentiates from the identical "20% off + Kohl's Cash" cadence I've been drowning in
- The hero is unusually clean for Kohl's — white background, single circular badge, clear focal point; after the cluttered July 6th home-sale-plus-summer-picks subject-line collision, this reads as disciplined
- The APT 9 / TEK GEAR / SONOMA navigation at the top tells me exactly which brands qualify without making me click blind
- "Styles that say designer / Prices that say otherwise" in the product grid section is a genuinely clever line that speaks to how I actually shop — I do exactly that mental comparison between Nordstrom labels and Kohl's house brands at the counter
- The summer section tagline "Summer picks that get invited back next year" earns a second look — it's warm and confident, not desperate

## What's weak

- The exclusion text visible in the hero badge is a loyalty-member gut-punch; I track my Kohl's Cash the way I track points on my Nordstrom card, and any caveat surfaced right in the offer badge is going to dampen the conversion regardless of where it sits
- "Shop Now" is as generic as CTAs get — after a specific "FIRST TIME EVER 30% off" setup, that button deserved "Shop Kohl's Brands" or at least a brand name; Neiman Marcus would have landed a verb
- The product thumbnails in the grid are too small to identify anything specific on mobile — I can see there are clothes and a pair of shoes, but nothing identifiable that makes me want to touch the screen; the "designer prices" promise needs a hero item to make it real
- Cadence fatigue is real; even a genuinely strong send like this one is filtered through 45+ emails since May — Kohl's is burning credibility faster than any individual good email can rebuild it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe or relocate the loyalty exclusion** — test moving any Kohl's Cash caveat to the footer where every retailer puts exclusion text; putting it in the hero badge trains your best customers to look for the catch before they feel the deal
- 2. **Give "Shop Now" a job** — "Shop APT 9 + Sonoma" or "Shop the First-Ever 30% Sale" earns the tap; "Shop Now" after a specific "FIRST TIME EVER" offer is a missed handshake
- 3. **Anchor one hero product in the badge section** — a single lifestyle image of an APT 9 blazer or Sonoma linen set with a visible price-before/after gives me something to desire before I scroll; right now it's all badge and no product
- 4. **Earn the "FIRST TIME EVER" claim with copy below the fold** — one sentence explaining when Kohl's has offered these brands before (and why this is different) makes the claim land as news instead of marketing hyperbole
- **Subject Alt A:** `First time ever: 30% off APT 9, Sonoma + more — 2 days`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This never happens: 30% off Kohl's brands this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `APT 9, Sonoma, Tek Gear — the lowest we've ever gone, no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Kohl's brands at the biggest discount we've ever offered`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-tracking links skipped by QA probe** — all links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com` redirect domains; destination URLs and HTTP status could not be verified.

**Three separate tracking pixel systems are active simultaneously:**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images are missing `alt` text**, including:
- The open-tracking pixel at `click.chp.kohls.com/o/e4b22597...` — technically intentional for trackers, but should carry `alt=""`
- 6 content images served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` with campaign parameters (`email_name=260707_DG_Email_Tuesday`) — these appear to be rendered content images, not trackers, and should have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients

`maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta prevents zoom — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure that harms users with low vision.

`<title>` tag is empty (`<title></title>`), which degrades screen reader experience and preview text fallback in some clients.

The `.hide-lg { display: none !important; }` rule in the global stylesheet (not scoped to a media query) permanently hides desktop-targeted elements by default with no counterpart show rule at wider breakpoints — likely a class-naming bug that could suppress content on desktop clients.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source. Subscriber ID is passed via `mi_u=604230016` on tracking pixels, confirming per-recipient resolution is functioning.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in HTML source.** CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. Not present in the visible truncated source — may exist below the fold, but QA did not detect it.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent.** Gmail and Apple Mail use this header for native unsubscribe UI; its absence increases spam folder placement risk and removes one-click unsubscribe for ESP-level compliance (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` also missing).

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** by the AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverifiable from this data. If DKIM is failing, inbox placement will degrade regardless of content.

The `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` tag suppresses referrer on outbound clicks — this is intentional for privacy but means GA4/analytics on landing pages will receive direct/none traffic instead of email attribution unless UTM params are appended to every link (see §6).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 outbound links pass through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com` redirect chains — UTM parameter presence on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects (blocked by QA probe).

The `no-referrer` meta policy (noted above) makes UTM params on destination URLs **mandatory** for any attribution. If any CTA links resolve to bare `kohls.com` URLs without `?utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`, the traffic will be unattributable in analytics.
