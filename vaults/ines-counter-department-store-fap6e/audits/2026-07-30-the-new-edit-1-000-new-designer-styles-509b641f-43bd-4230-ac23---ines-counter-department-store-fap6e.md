# The New Edit: 1,000+ new designer styles
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- No offer. No percentage, no dollar sign, nothing. Just "Brand New Styles Just Landed" in a black-and-white slab, followed by a grid of product tiles with designer names underneath and no prices. This is a straight-up "look what's new" catalog email, and while I actually don't mind that Neiman's occasionally sends a clean editorial piece with no hard sell — I gave the July 28th splash-image send a fair 5 for restraint — this one doesn't even earn that credit because it's not beautiful enough to justify the lack of an offer. It's just a grid of stock-looking product shots on gray backgrounds.
- The designer names are legit and this is where Neiman's differentiates from Macy's and Kohl's — Stella McCartney, Thom Browne, Balenciaga, Jacquemus, Burberry all in one send is genuinely a "my kind of store" lineup. But there's zero styling narrative, zero context on why these pieces matter, and half the grid is menswear I'm scrolling past. My gut reaction: skim the designer names for two seconds, register nothing I need today, and archive it. I would not click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here worth telling a friend about, no single showstopper piece the way the July 29th "Heel Appeal" mule roundup had one.

## What's working

- The designer roster is genuinely aspirational — Stella McCartney, Thom Browne, Balenciaga, Burberry all read as "yes, this is my store."
- Clean, uncluttered layout with clear section breaks between Women's and Men's — no Kohl's-style banner pile-up.
- "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" and "Shop All Men's New Arrivals" buttons are easy to spot and unambiguous.
- No fine print, no exclusions language, no bait-and-switch — refreshing after the July 30th app-only 15% email left me annoyed at the asterisk.

## What's weak

- Zero prices anywhere. I can't tell if that Stella McCartney bag is $800 or $2,800, and I'm not clicking six times to find out.
- No hero product — just an evenly-weighted grid of six women's and six men's items, so my eye has nowhere to land. Compare that to the July 29th roundup email, which put one gorgeous polka-dot mule front and center and made me want to look closer.
- Half this email is menswear I have no use for, pushing the content I care about further down the scroll.
- The product photography is flat catalog-on-gray, not the editorial styling Neiman's is capable of — Sephora's emails feel more curated than this, and that's a beauty brand, not even fashion.
- No loyalty/InCircle points messaging at all, which is odd for a store that knows I only shop with cards that give me points back.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices under every item. This is the single biggest miss — a "new arrivals" email with no prices reads like window shopping I don't have time for.
- 2. Pick one hero piece (my money's on the Stella McCartney bag) and give it a bigger, styled placement above the grid instead of burying it as tile #2.
- 3. Split into two separate sends — Women's New Arrivals and Men's New Arrivals — so I'm not scrolling past shoes I'll never buy to get to the bag I want.
- 4. Surface InCircle points or tier status somewhere in this email — even a small line reminding me what I'd earn shopping today would move me.
- 5. Add a one-line styling note per hero item ("the Thom Browne loafer that's replacing your loyalty to boat shoes") — something with a point of view, the way the July 29th roundup felt curated rather than dumped.

## Technical Audit: "The New Edit: 1,000+ new designer styles" (Neiman Marcus)

### 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional MSC (Salesforce Marketing Cloud) template with AMPscript personalization and a CSS/checkbox-hack mobile nav; automated QA passed 50% of checks with 4 warnings and 0 hard failures, concentrated in unsubscribe-header compliance and authentication visibility.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
The provided HTML excerpt is truncated before the body/link markup, so no individual link URLs could be inspected directly. Per QA, 30 tracking/click-redirect links were detected and **skipped from HTTP probing** (expected behavior for redirect-domain links, not a failure signal) — link-destination health is therefore unverified in this pass.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has **no `lang` attribute** — screen readers cannot reliably determine content language.
- Mobile hamburger nav is implemented via the CSS checkbox-hack (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~...`). This pattern has inconsistent support in Outlook (desktop/mobile) and some Gmail app builds, which don't reliably honor `:checked` sibling selectors in email contexts — the nav menu may fail to open in those clients.
- Client-reset boilerplate is present and correct (`.ExternalClass` line-height fixes, `mso-table-lspace/rspace: 0pt`, `-webkit-text-size-adjust`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) — no issues found here.
- `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has trailing whitespace — cosmetic only, not flagged as a defect.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
An HTML comment block at the top of the document exposes raw AMPscript build notes in the shipped email, including:
```
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
@cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This leaks internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and unresolved template-author instructions into production output, visible to any recipient via "View Source." This should have been stripped before send. Actual merge-token resolution in the rendered body could not be assessed since the excerpt is truncated before token usage appears.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found by QA (WARN). Per QA notes, this may be a relay artifact (AgentMail may not be capturing/forwarding the header) rather than a true absence at the source — needs source-level verification, not just relay inspection.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)**: not found. Without this, one-click unsubscribe is not supported, which is required for bulk senders under current Gmail/Yahoo sender guidelines.
- **Authentication-Results header**: not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this data and should be confirmed via mail server logs rather than relay headers.
- Unsubscribe link/footer markup itself was not visible in the truncated HTML, so physical-address/CAN-SPAM footer compliance could not be assessed directly.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable — the truncated HTML excerpt ends before any CTA/link markup is reached, so no UTM parameters or landing-page targets could be extracted or compared.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Strip AMPscript build comments (DE names, lookup keys, "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" notes) from the production template before send.
2. Add `lang="en"` to the `<html>` tag.
3. Implement RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers) at the source; separately confirm whether AgentMail relay is stripping an existing header vs. it being absent entirely.
4. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly against ESP send logs, since `Authentication-Results` wasn't observable via this relay.
5. Reduce plain-text URL density (currently ~87%, 5,289/6,104 chars) — likely driven by long tracking-redirect URLs — to lower spam-filter risk.
6. Test the checkbox-hack mobile nav in Outlook desktop/mobile and Gmail app to confirm fallback behavior when `:checked` isn't honored.
