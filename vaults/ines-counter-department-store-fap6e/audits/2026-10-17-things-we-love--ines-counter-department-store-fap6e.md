- The email has no shouting. After a week of "First Call," it respects my inbox.
- The beauty trio is well chosen. Chantecaille, Maison Francis Kurkdjian and Dr. Barbara Sturm are the names I'd shop for, and they say designer-level access without any promotion.

- It's seasonal without being stale. Fall layers, animal print and monotones all fit the moment.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no event, no deadline and no points. I buy at brand events and I want my card to earn, so nothing here says why today.
- The hero models are both very young, and the grid is half menswear. Only the Night Out look and the denim skirt could be mine.
- Every link is a small, underlined, grey "Shop ___" text link. The "Shop Weekend," "Shop Work," "Shop Night Out" and "Shop Now" labels don't say what I'll find. On a phone those are hard to tap.
- Beauty is my category, and it sits in a small row below the fold. Sephora's "MOST WANTED" Valentino hero led with one product and made the point in a second. Here I'd have to scroll to find the Barbara Sturm serum, and it's unpriced.
- "View in a Web Browser" is stuck at the very bottom in tiny type, and the legal text is hard to read.

- 1. Put one real reason to click above the hero fold. That could be a gift with purchase on the beauty trio, a points multiplier, or a beauty event date. Macy's runs a deadline in its subject, and Kohl's puts the Kohl's Cash earning right in its header.
- 2. Lead with beauty for subscribers like me who have bought it. Move the Chantecaille, Kurkdjian and Sturm row up and give it a bigger tap target and a real button.

- 5. Add one model who reads over 40, so I can see myself in the grid.
- 6. Space the sends out. Kohl's has taught me what a week of near-identical promos feels like, and Neiman's is heading there.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Things We Love"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is truncated, so most body-level checks (links, alt text, footer) could not be confirmed. Two issues are visible in the `<head>` and preamble: internal Marketing Cloud template notes leaked into the delivered HTML, and a viewport setting that blocks zoom. The QA warnings are mostly relay or capture limits rather than confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable:** QA skipped HTTP probing of 20 tracking/click-redirect links, so destinations, redirect chains and expiry are unconfirmed.
- **Plain-text part:** 3760 of 4277 characters (about 88%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. That points to long tracking URLs and little readable text. It can hurt spam scoring and plain-text readability. This comes from QA data, not source inspection.
- No broken links were detected, but the sample is too limited to rule them out.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` stops pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text) concern.
- **Weak title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and says nothing about the message.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** The large comment block comes before `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Some older clients, including IE-based Outlook and webmail, can switch to quirks mode when content precedes the doctype. This is a risk, not a confirmed failure.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** The responsive rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]`. Any element with more than one class, like `class="mobile hide"`, won't match. I couldn't check this because the body was truncated.
- **Hamburger menu:** The mobile nav uses a `:checked` checkbox with sibling selectors (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.hamburger`). Gmail and several Outlook builds don't support this. I'd confirm there is a fallback.
- **Not verifiable:** alt text, image `width`/`height`, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and contrast.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template notes leaked:** The delivered HTML begins with a comment block of internal Marketing Cloud (AMPscript-style) notes:
  - `REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")`
  - `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`
  - `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)` and `"cmdid"`
  - `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`

  This is not a rendering failure, but it exposes internal data-extension names and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`). It also shows developer instructions, including a scenario that was meant to be chosen, in production HTML. The comment has a stray `)` in `@user Lookup(...))`.
- **Not verifiable:** whether `@uEm`, `@cmdid` or `@user` (hashed email) are appended to URLs, and whether any tokens are unresolved in the body.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe not found (WARN):** QA noted it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. This is unconfirmed, not a verified absence.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (WARN):** There is no confirmation of RFC 8058 one-click support. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders, so check it in the raw headers at the sender.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown from this capture. The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
- **Not verifiable:** the physical postal address and the visible unsubscribe link, because the footer is outside the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM and landing-page alignment can't be assessed.** The 20 tracking links were not resolved, so the final URLs, UTM parameters and landing-page matching are unknown.
- The `@refid` / `@base_refid` variables suggest campaign attribution runs through a ref ID rather than UTMs. That is inferred from the comment, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the AMPscript/documentation comment block from production output. It leaks DE names and is placed before the DOCTYPE.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Check the raw headers at the sending side for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. The relay may be stripping them.
4. Resolve the 20 tracking links manually or with a tracking-aware probe, and confirm landing pages and params.
5. Shorten or reduce redirect URLs in the plain-text part, or add readable text, to bring URL share under 70%.
6. Re-run this audit on the full HTML. Alt text, token resolution, footer compliance and the continuity checks depend on content that was cut off.
