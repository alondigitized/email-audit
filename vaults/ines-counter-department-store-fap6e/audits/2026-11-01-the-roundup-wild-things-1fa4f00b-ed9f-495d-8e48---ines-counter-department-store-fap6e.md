# The Roundup: Wild Things
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's I like. The zebra-print tote sits on a quiet grey ground, and below it are six animal-print bags with only the designer names. There's no shouting, no countdown and no code. It's a catalog page, and a handsome one. After last week's gift-card and Fall Style Sale pushes, and yesterday's black-box "25% OFF" send, this is a relief.
- It's also a send with nothing to act on. I see no price, no event and no hint of why today. I don't know if the Ferragamo is $2,000 or $4,000, and I can't tell whether any of this earns points. For a woman who won't put a card down without points back, that matters. I'd look at it, enjoy it and probably not click. I might forward the Givenchy leopard hobo to a friend who loves a statement bag. I wouldn't send the email itself.

## What's working

- The zebra tote is a confident hero, and "Wild Things" with "Shop Animalia" tells me the theme in two beats.
- The six bags are cleanly cut out, with small caps designer names (Ahikoza, Balenciaga, Givenchy, Tom Ford, Etro, Ferragamo). That reads as designer access without any hype.
- The layout is calm and has plenty of white space. It's easy to scan, and the type is readable on a phone.
- Compared with Sephora, which sent me its twenty-first email in a week, or Kohl's seventh near-identical "20% off + $10 off" send, this one has no pressure at all. It respects my time.
- The "personal style advisor" and app links at the bottom are a nice touch for a customer who wants service.

## What's weak

- There are no prices anywhere. I can't tell whether this is a $900 bag or a $4,500 one, so I can't decide if it's worth a click.
- There's no loyalty or tier signal. Nothing mentions InCircle points, an event or a gift with purchase, which is the whole reason I'd choose Neiman's over Nordstrom or Saks.
- The only button is the generic "Shop Must-Have Handbags." The individual bags may be linked, but nothing in the image shows me that.
- "The Roundup" has no date, season or reason attached. Macy's at least gave me a deadline, even if it was dressed up.
- The top nav has "SALE" and nothing else that's specific to me. It feels like an email to everyone, not to me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or a "from $" under each designer name. That's the single biggest lift. Even one line like "from $1,150" turns browsing into a decision.
- 2. Add one member line near the hero, such as "InCircle members earn bonus points on handbags through Sunday." Sephora leads with its Beauty Insider tier, and Neiman's should lead with its own.
- 3. Make the CTA specific: "Shop animal-print handbags." Keep the "Shop Animalia" link, but give it a button.
- 4. Put the designer name and bag type in the hero too, so the lead image earns a click on its own.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Animal-print handbags: Givenchy, Tom Ford, Ferragamo`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The zebra tote everyone will ask about`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six standout bags, plus bonus InCircle points this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Snake, leopard and zebra from the designers you shop.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a standard table-based XHTML email. Its main confirmed problem is internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) template scaffolding left in the delivered source. The QA run shows 0 hard failures and 4 warnings, and most of those cannot be confirmed from the data given. The HTML source was truncated, so body markup, links, images and the footer were not inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** 15 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are on click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unknown.
- **Plain-text part:** 2825 of 3310 characters (about 85%) are URLs, which exceeds the 70% threshold. This is a confirmed spam-filter risk. The URLs are probably raw tracking links in the text alternative.
- No broken links are confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it. That conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text).
- **No `lang` attribute:** the root tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">`, so screen readers have no language hint.
- **Weak `<title>`:** it is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space and no campaign context. This is minor.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** the rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching if any element has more than one class. `*[class].abs`, `.menu` and `.hamburger` rely on `:checked` sibling selectors (the checkbox hack).
- **Checkbox-hack limits:** the hamburger nav uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. Gmail and several Android clients don't support this pattern, so the nav may fail there. Keyboard accessibility can't be assessed without the markup.
- **Not assessable:** image alt text, the preheader and link text were not in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment in production HTML:** the source begins with a comment block containing SFMC template scaffolding. It lists `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`, `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)`, plus the instructions "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`.
- **Why it matters:**
  - It exposes internal data extension names and field names to any recipient who views the source.
  - It shows the template was not cleaned before send.
  - The variable block also has stray, unbalanced parentheses (`@user Lookup(...))`), which shows it was never executed as written.
- **No visible failures:** no unresolved `%%…%%` or `@…` tokens appear in the visible rendered portion. Merge values inside hrefs can't be checked because the source was truncated.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` missing [WARN]:** the QA note says it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. This is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) missing [WARN]:** this is also unconfirmed for the same reason. It matters because Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` missing:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com`, a subdomain, so alignment should be checked at the source.
- **Footer not inspected:** the physical address and unsubscribe link in the body footer could not be checked because the source was truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Not assessable:** no hrefs, UTM parameters or landing pages are visible. Because the 15 tracking links were not followed, there is no data on UTM preservation or on whether the landing pages match the campaign ("The Roundup: Wild Things").
- **Possible identifier leakage:** `@uEm` and `@cmdid` come from `NM_CRM_Feed`, so they are probably appended to links as per-recipient identifiers. Verify this in the full source.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC variable and instruction comment block from the published template, or move it into an SFMC-side comment that is not rendered.
2. Re-run the audit on a source capture that includes the raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`). The relay probably drops them, so this will confirm or clear the three header warnings.
3. Cut the URL share of the plain-text part, using shorter or fewer links and more real text, to get under 70%.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and give the `<title>` real content.
5. Switch the exact-match `*[class=…]` selectors to `[class~=…]` or `[class*=…]`, and add a non-checkbox fallback for the nav.
6. Re-run the link probe with redirects followed (or against the final URLs) to check UTMs and landing-page continuity.
