# LAST DAY to shop the LOWEST PRICES OF THE SEASON ⏳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. I just counted the Kohl's folder. Seventeen emails in roughly two weeks, and this one shows up with "LAST DAY" stamped on it like I'm supposed to feel something. I don't. Kohl's said "won't be here forever" on June 6th. They said "lowest prices coming in hot" on June 3rd. They said this sale started on June 1st. At this point "LAST DAY" is just Tuesday's subject line wearing a countdown badge.
- That said — the Kohl's Cash callout at the bottom is real. Earn $10 for every $50 spent through 6/8. That's a concrete loyalty offer that actually changes my checkout math, and it's the most differentiated thing in this email. The 65% off bags section also landed for me — I've been half-looking for a weekend bag since spring, and that discount depth is real. But the hero is a young man and woman in swimwear, the categories span everything from sheets to sandals, and there is not a single beauty, fragrance, or skincare module anywhere in this scroll. Kohl's has a beauty section. They just didn't think to put it in this send. Nordstrom would never leave that on the table.
- Would I click? Possibly on bags — once, quickly, to see what's actually 65% off. Forward to a friend? No. What I'll actually do is the worst thing for Kohl's: tab-switch it away and let it sit unread until tomorrow when email eighteen arrives.

## What's working

- **Kohl's Cash earn is specific and timed** — "$10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent through 6/8" is an actual loyalty mechanic I can act on, not vague "rewards" language
- **65% off bags is the deepest discount callout** in the email and the product image is decent — that section earns a stop-scroll
- **Email renders clean on mobile** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds through the full scroll
- **The LAST DAY hero is immediately unambiguous** — whatever else is wrong with this send, you know in two seconds what the email is asking you to do
- **Price anchors are visible without clicking** — 85% off clearance, 50% off, 65% off bags are all right there in the body

## What's weak

- **The models are not me** — swimwear hero skews 28-year-old fitness couple, not the 47-year-old who walks the beauty counter and cross-shops Saks; Nordstrom consistently shoots aspirational-but-age-matched lifestyle and this is why
- **No beauty, fragrance, or skincare module** — for a department store with beauty counters, this is an inexcusable omission; Sephora and Macy's both lead with category relevance; Kohl's left their beauty customer invisible
- **"LAST DAY" is a spent signal** — this is the fourth or fifth time in two weeks some version of "this is the day" has appeared in a Kohl's subject; the urgency is completely hollow by now
- **"Shop Now" appears seven or eight times** in this scroll with no differentiation — by the third tile, the hierarchy collapses and everything starts looking like noise
- **The Kohl's Cash callout is buried at the bottom** — the most loyalty-relevant, most differentiated piece of information in this email is the last thing you see

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move Kohl's Cash above the fold** — put it in the hero row immediately below the LAST DAY banner; it's your best retention signal and you're hiding it
- 2. **Swap the hero image** — drop the swimwear couple; use a lifestyle shot that reads 40s-friendly (home, bags, shoes styled on an adult woman) to stop the demographic mismatch from losing the open
- 3. **Add a beauty/fragrance module** — one tile, mid-scroll; Kohl's has the inventory, and a beauty-focused subscriber will notice every time they don't see it
- 4. **Differentiate the CTAs** — replace all "Shop Now" buttons with category-specific copy: "Shop Bags," "Shop Clearance," "Shop Sandals"; Nordstrom does this and the scroll immediately gets more navigable
- 5. **Rest the LAST DAY language** — one more cycle of this and it will train the segment to tune out urgency entirely; try a factual "Sale ends tonight" or just lead with Kohl's Cash as the headline
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kohl's Cash ends 6/8 — plus 65% off bags & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: 85% off clearance + earn $10 Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent — today is the last day`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bags, shoes, home — deepest discounts end tonight`

### 1. Technical Summary

A large-scale promotional send built on a standard MSO-compatible table layout with three separate tracking pixel systems active simultaneously. No critical rendering failures detected, but compliance gaps and missing alt attributes on tracking pixels are confirmed issues.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Three concurrent tracking systems firing on open:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com` — CoherentPath engagement pixel
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/` — Messageinfo open tracker (`mi_u=604230016`)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx` — Kohl's ESP open pixel (Salesforce Marketing Cloud pattern)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel

**22 click links unprobed** — all pass through tracking redirects (likely `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com`). Destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be validated.

**Malformed HTML tag detected:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..."> </custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag is a stray/invalid element immediately after the third open-pixel `<img>`. This is a code generation artifact and may cause parser warnings in strict validators.

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly, not omit the attribute — screen readers may attempt to announce the src URL in its absence.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility barrier:
```html

```

**`<title>` element is empty** — some email clients surface the title as a preview fallback; leaving it blank wastes that slot and may cause accessibility tool confusion.

**`<html lang="en">` is present** — no issue.

**MSO VML namespaces declared** — consistent with Outlook rendering requirements, no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA WARN): Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Major ESPs (Gmail, Yahoo) use this header to surface the unsubscribe button in the client chrome. Its absence increases spam-folder risk and violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (>5K/day).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA WARN): RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024.
