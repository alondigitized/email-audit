# Woo-hoo 🎉 Up to 25% off select shampoos and conditioners 🫧
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- "Woo-hoo" is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that subject line, and none of it is the right kind. I've been receiving Sephora sale emails on almost a biweekly cadence — the 50% off beauty sale on June 11, a repeat on June 16 — and now they're back eight days later leading with 25% off shampoos and conditioners. The math does not add up in my favor. This is a step *down* from the offer I've been trained to expect, dressed up in exclamation-point energy to try to disguise it.
- Open the email and you immediately hit the disconnect: the hero is the 25% off hair deal, but scroll a half-inch and there's a "Spot It. Shop It." module pitching the *same 50% off Select Beauty* sale that ran on June 11 and June 16. So Sephora led with the weaker offer and buried the stronger one? That's an unusual hierarchy choice. If I hadn't scrolled, I'd have assumed I was getting a hair-specific targeting email — which would have been fine — but the 50% module inside undercuts the whole premise of the send.
- I'd mark it as read. I wouldn't forward it. There's nothing here that Neiman Marcus's clean "UP TO 60% OFF" designer-sale hero or Kohl's's "Put an end to 'I have nothing to wear' days" doesn't do better in terms of clarity and respect for my time.

## What's working

- **Loyalty cues are present.** The BeautyINSIDER section is visible and the "Save and Earn 500 Extra Points" headline is exactly the kind of hook that works on me — I do not shop without points accruing.
- **Hero deadline is clear.** "Save through Tues. 6/24. Don't miss out!" gives me a real date in the above-the-fold section, which Sephora's June 16 send also did well.
- **Category specificity is a reasonable idea.** Hair care is a legitimate Sephora pillar, and a dedicated send for it — if executed well — is smarter targeting than another generic "select beauty" blast.
- **Fenty and other recognizable brands appear in the product grid.** Seeing names I trust earns a small amount of browse intent.

## What's weak

- **The 50% off module inside contradicts the 25% off hero outside.** The best deal in the email is buried below the fold. The subject line advertised the lesser offer. This is backwards.
- **"Woo-hoo" is discount-bin energy.** Sephora is a prestige beauty retailer where I spend real money. Nordstrom doesn't open with "Woo-hoo." Saks doesn't. This reads like a TJ Maxx clearance push.
- **Father's Day module has no business being in a hair-care send.** I am not shopping for my father here. The "Father's Day Gifts. Handled." section is a full bleed insertion that breaks any sense of focused intent.
- **"Beauty for All — Essentials for every routine" is pure filler.** No offers, no specificity. This is a brand-awareness module sandwiched between two sale modules and it just adds scroll.
- **Cadence is becoming wallpaper.** The June 11, June 16, and now June 19 sends are blurring together. The June 1 send called me "Rosa." I have not forgotten.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 50% off sale, position the hair deal as the featured category.** Swap the hierarchy: "50% off select beauty — including 25% off shampoos and conditioners" tells a coherent story. Right now the email is fighting itself.
- 2. **Kill the "Woo-hoo."** Replace with tone-appropriate urgency: "Ends Tuesday" does more work with less damage to the brand.
- 3. **Remove the Father's Day module from this send.** Save it for a dedicated Father's Day email or gift-guide send. Its presence here is noise.
- 4. **Collapse the "Beauty for All" filler block.** Use the real estate for one anchor product at a specific price point.
- 5. **Preheader should echo the hair-sale theme**, not route to same-day delivery logistics.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Tuesday: 25% off shampoos & conditioners`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your hair routine — now 25% off. Through June 24.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top-rated Fenty, OUAI, and more — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `500 extra points when you shop select hair today`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from pinching to zoom on mobile. Drop `maximum-scale=1`.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` — screen readers cannot infer language, breaking WCAG 3.1.1. Add `lang="en"`.

**Aggressive `text-size-adjust: none` on nearly every element**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to `body`, `span`, `a`, and `td` globally. This suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility settings enabled. Scope it only to elements where layout breakage is documented (typically just `td` for Outlook).

**Generic `<title>` tag**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Some clients (Samsung Mail, webmail) display this as the email's window/tab title. Use a descriptive value matching the subject line.

**`robots` meta misspelling (low-severity)**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a valid robots directive; correct value is `noindex`. Harmless in email context since crawlers ignore email source, but indicates a template copy-paste error.
