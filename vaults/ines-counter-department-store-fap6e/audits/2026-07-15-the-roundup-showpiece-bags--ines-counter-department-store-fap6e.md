# The Roundup: Showpiece Bags
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Email eighteen from Neiman Marcus — I've been counting since they sent me two gift card pitches on the same afternoon last week — and this one lands as a complete creative pivot. No Last Call. No "extra 25% off." No clock ticking. Just six bags arranged on a white field with two CTA buttons and a hero shot that honestly belongs in a magazine. I respect the editorial restraint, and the bags themselves are genuinely beautiful — that sage quilted hobo and the structured caramel shoulder bag would both stop me mid-scroll at the counter.
- But here's the thing: I opened it right after a NM Last Call email offering me up to 70% off, and now they're showing me bags with zero prices attached. Nothing. Not even a soft "starting at" line. For a Gen X woman who cross-shops Saks *specifically* to compare price points before committing, this feels like a showroom where someone quietly removed all the tags. It's aspirational, yes. It is also unactionable.
- I would not click through. I would not forward this to a friend. I would do what I do with the Net-a-Porter editorial drops I'm not in the mood for: close it, feel mildly guilty, and promise myself I'll go back later. I won't go back.

## What's working

- **Hero image is genuinely premium** — the warm-toned lifestyle shot of the cream structured bag on that ornate surface feels like Vogue editorial, not retail blast.
- **"Showpiece Bags" is a confident editorial headline** — it tells me exactly what the curation premise is and signals that NM curated this intentionally, not algorithmically.
- **Two differentiated CTAs** — "Shop Crossbody Bags" vs. "Shop New Arrivals" give me a real fork in the road, which is more than Macy's usually bothers to do with their single "Shop Now" slug.
- **No render bugs** — clean grid, images load, text sits where it's supposed to. After some of the mobile-mangled Kohl's emails I've been reviewing, this is genuinely appreciated.
- **Product selection skews correctly for my demo** — the heart bag is a novelty but the rest read as occasion-appropriate, not twenties-influencer.

## What's weak

- **Zero price information, zero offer** — this is an editorial with no reason to buy today. After yesterday's "Save up to 70%" send, the whiplash is disorienting.
- **No InCircle acknowledgment whatsoever** — I accumulate points for a reason. A line like "InCircle members earn on full-price purchases" costs nothing to insert and would have given me a reason to justify the click at full price.
- **Brand labels under products are unreadable** — I can see text under each bag but I cannot tell which is which. Saks' product emails at least make the designer name legible at a glance; this requires me to click just to know what I'm looking at.
- **"Shop New Arrivals" is a wasted CTA** — I'm already in an arrivals-style editorial. That button adds no specificity and competes with the stronger crossbody CTA without directing me anywhere meaningful.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is email eighteen in roughly two weeks. Even if this were the best bag editorial I'd ever received, the volume has conditioned me to skim before I even open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor to each product tile.** Even "From $650" under each bag tells me whether I'm window-shopping or buying. Without it, the editorial floats above my decision-making entirely.
- 2. **Invoke InCircle directly in the body copy.** Something like "Earn InCircle points on every full-price pick" between the CTA buttons. I refuse to use a card without points back — remind me I'll be earning.
- 3. **Make brand names legible.** If Coach and Bottega Veneta are in the grid, say so in readable type. Designer attribution is half the reason I'm here.
- 4. **Replace "Shop New Arrivals" CTA with a category-specific one** — "Shop All Bags" or "Shop Structured Bags" — something that doesn't repeat the premise of the entire email.
- 5. **Reduce send volume or segment arrivals vs. sale.** The editorial-vs.-discount whiplash across consecutive days erodes both.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Roundup: Showpiece Bags"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with a multi-scenario variable setup that contains an unresolved template branch decision. Compliance posture is degraded: one-click unsubscribe headers are absent, and authentication results are unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 click-redirect URLs unprobed** — All outbound links pass through a tracking/redirect domain; HTTP probe was skipped for all 36. No broken-link verification is possible without resolving final destinations.

**Plain-text part is 86% URLs** — QA reports 3,138 of 3,647 plain-text characters are URLs. This typically results from auto-generated plain-text that strips HTML but retains raw tracking links. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_PHISHING` rules, Proofpoint) penalize high URL-to-text ratios in plain-text parts. A human-readable plain-text alternative should be authored separately.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables user pinch-zoom. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is a violation in some jurisdictions. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Three conflicting viewport tags in comments** — Three additional `<meta name="viewport">` tags exist as HTML comments (commented out), including a width=320/scale=0.29 variant and a width=1024 variant. While inert, this indicates unresolved viewport strategy; a future template edit risks accidentally un-commenting the wrong variant.

**CSS checkbox-based interactive nav** — The hamburger menu and carousel rely on `:checked` pseudo-class (`.mobileCont:checked~table`). Support is limited: Gmail (web) strips `<input type="checkbox">` and associated `:checked` rules. Users in Gmail will see a non-functional interactive element or a fallback layout depending on how the `display: none` defaults are set.

**`<title>` tag has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but it appears verbatim in browser tabs if the email is opened in a web view.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved template branch for `@user`** — The source contains:

```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This is an AMPscript line wrapped inside both an HTML comment and a `/* */` block comment, making it inert. The surrounding instruction reads "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," implying the template requires a manual branch selection before deployment. If `@user` was intended to carry a hashed email value for downstream personalization or link decoration, it is currently unset.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` from CRM Feed lookup** — Both variables are populated via `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)`. If a subscriber is absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, these resolve to empty strings. Any link or logic that depends on them (e.g., personalized offer IDs) would silently drop the parameter rather than error visibly.

**`@emailid` has a dual-path definition** — The comment documents two resolution paths for `@emailid`: from the sendable DE directly, or via `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`. No conditional guard (`%%[IF ...]%%`) is visible in the truncated source to confirm which path is active. A misconfiguration here would send a null email ID to downstream analytics.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA confirms this header was not captured. Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe` (and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click) for bulk senders (>5K/day) since February 2024. Missing headers increase the probability of being routed to spam or having the account flagged for non-compliance.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — One-click unsubscribe (machine-readable `mailto:` or `https:` POST endpoint) is missing. This is distinct from a footer unsubscribe link; mailbox providers use this header to surface a native "Unsubscribe" button. Its absence degrades sender reputation scoring on Gmail and Yahoo.

**`Authentication-Results` header unverifiable** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not surface the header. Sender domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. If the sending domain is not DMARC-aligned with `neimanmarcus.com`, bulk sender requirements are unmet.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot verify UTM parameters or landing page alignment because all 36 outbound links passed through tracking redirects and were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM coverage, parameter values, and landing page match are unaudited.

**Risk area:** The `@base_refid` and `@refid` variables are used to construct campaign identifiers appended to links. If `@base_refid` is empty (not populated in the sendable DE), all campaign-level attribution in GA/Adobe Analytics would be lost silently.
