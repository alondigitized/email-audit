# 📣 Starts today: Take 20% off new faves 🤗
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Well, it's Kohl's again, and I opened it mostly out of habit at this point — I've been getting near-identical "EPIC DEALS" red-tile blasts from them every single day this week, so a home-and-cozy theme is honestly a relief. The "CozyFinds" section with the throw pillows and knit blankets is genuinely pretty — soft, autumnal, makes me think about my living room — but then it collapses into the same category-icon soup Kohl's always runs: eight little circle icons for Bedding, Bath, Decor, Kitchen, Storage, Rugs. By the time I hit the third "Take $X off" coupon block near the bottom I'd completely lost track of which discount applied to what.
- Compare this to Neiman Marcus's Bottega Veneta send I read the other day — one gorgeous hero, one product, one clear feeling. Kohl's can't help itself; it's Macy's-style overload but somehow worse because there's no single glamour shot anchoring it. This is not a forward-to-a-friend email. It's a scroll-past-and-maybe-scan-for-the-coupon-code email, and even that takes real effort because the codes are buried in tiny print at the bottom with exclusion language I'd need reading glasses for.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I actually needed a bath towel — the Big One towels at $2.99 caught my eye because that's a real, concrete price. But nothing here says "shop now for something specific," it says "here's fourteen things, pick one."

## What's working

- The "CozyFinds" pillow/blanket photography at the top is warm and seasonally appropriate — actually made me want to nest.
- The $2.99 towel and $10 off $50 home coupon are concrete, real numbers I can do math on instantly.
- Kohl's Cash and Rewards member language is present and I do have a card, so that loyalty signal lands for me specifically.
- The "Your 'just one thing' plan had a good run" line has a little personality — better than the shouty "EPIC DEALS" banners I've been drowning in all week.

## What's weak

- Way too many sections stacked on top of each other — cozy finds, category icons, "just one thing," toy/reward bundle, product grid, coupon wall — it's five emails smashed into one.
- Four separate coupon codes with different minimums and exclusions by the time I reach the bottom; I'd need to write them down to keep them straight.
- No single hero product or model shot that says "this is THE offer" — my eye has nowhere confident to land, unlike that Bottega bag shot from Neiman Marcus that I remembered days later.
- The toy/holiday-adjacent bundle ("baby, toddler & kids' clothing") feels randomly dropped into a cozy-home email — jarring category whiplash.
- Fine print with "details," exclusions, and PIN codes crammed at the bottom feels like Kohl's hoping I won't actually read what I'm agreeing to.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero offer and lead with it big — either the cozy home 20% or the $10 off $50, not both plus three more further down. Sephora's Hispanic Heritage email had its own clutter problems, but at least it committed to one theme; Kohl's needs to commit to one number.
- 2. Cut the section count in half — merge the category icons into the "just one thing" carousel instead of running them twice in different visual styles.
- 3. Move one coupon code to the top near the hero, in large legible type, not buried in the footer with six others.
- 4. Drop the toy/kids bundle from a "cozy home" themed send — save it for its own email so it doesn't undercut the seasonal mood.
- 5. Simplify the fine print — one line "exclusions apply, details" link instead of four separate paragraphs of exclusion text.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus $10 off $50 home + free Kohl's Cash on toys`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New throws, towels, and rugs — 20% off through Sunday`
