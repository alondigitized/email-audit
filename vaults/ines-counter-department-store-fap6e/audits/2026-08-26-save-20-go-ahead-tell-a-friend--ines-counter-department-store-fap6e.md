# Save 20% ... go ahead, tell a friend ☺️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the seventh straight day of "Friends & Family 20%," and at this point I feel like I'm getting cc'd on Kohl's internal inventory memo rather than a personalized offer. My eye did catch one thing though — the Sephora + Kohl's block with 25% off MAKE UP FOR EVER, amika, Anastasia Beverly Hills. That's actually in my lane, and it's the first genuinely beauty-relevant thing this brand has sent me all week.
- But it's buried under cookware, dinnerware, a slow cooker, bath towels, and a mattress topper before I even get there. Compare that to Neiman Marcus's pre-fall editorial send I looked at recently — no clutter, one clean idea, "BE FIRST." This is the opposite: everything, all at once, priced in that garish red-strikethrough way that makes we wonder what anything actually costs.
- Would I click? Only for the Sephora Beauty Insiders block, and only because I already know the codeword game (CODE25 or whatever it is buried in there). Forward to a friend? No — my friends already get six of these a week too.

## What's working

- Membership status is right at the top again — "KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn 5% REWARDS" — so at least they know I'm not a stranger.
- The Sephora + Kohl's 25% off Beauty Insiders block is the one section that speaks my language, with actual brand names I recognize (Anastasia Beverly Hills, MAKE UP FOR EVER).
- "Earn $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent" gives a concrete, calculable incentive, unlike vague "save big" language.

## What's weak

- Beauty is one small block near the bottom of a home-and-family catalog dump — cookware, dinnerware, bedding, a mattress topper come first.
- Six-plus days running the same "Friends & Family 20%" banner in nearly identical layout — I flagged this exact fatigue on 8/25, 8/24, and 8/23 too.
- Visual hierarchy is a mess: red price tags, purple Sephora block, teal category buttons, maroon CTAs — nothing tells my eye where to look first.
- The barcode/coupon images at the bottom feel like a paper circular got copy-pasted into an email, not designed for a screen.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with beauty when I'm the recipient — segment this so a beauty-leaning customer sees the Sephora Insiders block in position two, not buried past cookware and towels.
- 2. Cut the category count in half. One offer, one hero, done — Neiman Marcus proved less is more just last week.
- 3. Stop repeating "Friends & Family 20%" as the banner seven days running; rotate the creative even if the offer mechanics stay the same.
- 4. Make the Beauty Insiders 25% off its own standalone send on the days it's live, not a footnote in a home goods blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Beauty Insiders: 25% off Anastasia, MAKE UP FOR EVER & more — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 5% Rewards + 25% off Sephora brands, just for today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus extra 20% off everything with your Friends & Family coupon`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Kohl's referral email uses a standard ESP tracking pipeline (Kohl's/AgentMail-relay) with heavy pixel tracking and MSO/media-query responsive markup; automated QA flags authentication-header visibility, missing physical address, and widespread missing alt text as the primary technical defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 82 tracking/click-redirect links detected and skipped for HTTP probing (domains: `click.chp.kohls.com`, `click.s.kohls.com`) — link destinations could not be verified as resolving; recommend manual spot-check of a sample before send.
- Multiple 1x1 tracking pixels present: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...`, `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif`, `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196`, plus several `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` opens — consistent with standard open-tracking/Adobe Audience Manager (demdex) beaconing, no anomalies found.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 9 images flagged with missing `alt` text, all tracking/beacon pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, five `p/rp/*.png` opens, `14aad519-...` click pixel). These are non-content 1x1 pixels, so the absence of alt text carries low user-facing accessibility risk, but `alt=""` should be set explicitly for spec compliance rather than omitted.
- Malformed `</custom>` closing tag noted above (Section 2) is also a rendering/validation defect.
- Responsive CSS present (`@media (max-width:500px)` block with `.w_50pc`, `.w_60pc`, `.container`, etc.) and Outlook-specific MSO conditional blocks are correctly structured — no issues found in the provided source for mobile/Outlook handling.
