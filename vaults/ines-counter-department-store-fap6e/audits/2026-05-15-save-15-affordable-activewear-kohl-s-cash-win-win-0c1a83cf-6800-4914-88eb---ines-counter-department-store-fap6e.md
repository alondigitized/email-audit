# Save 15% | Affordable activewear + Kohl's Cash ... win-win 🏆
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Kohl's is back in my inbox, and the déjà vu is real. May 8th was jewelry for Mom at 15% off. May 11th was summer style upgrades, also 15% off. Today it's affordable activewear — and yes, 15% off again. Three sends in eight days with the same discount mechanism, and the offer has officially stopped registering as an event. It reads like the store is perpetually on sale, which means it's never actually on sale.
- That said, something made me pause mid-scroll: the Sol de Janeiro collaboration. A brand I associate with Sephora and the Ulta beauty wall landing inside a Kohl's activewear blast is genuinely unexpected. That's the moment the email could have hooked someone like me. But it's flanked on both sides by TEK GEAR at $12.99 and "Busy families require teamwork" — and I'm reminded this wasn't built for a 47-year-old who cross-shops Nordstrom and Saks.
- The 15% coupon is real and usable, but it lives at the very bottom of a five-section scroll. Sephora would have put that offer center-frame in the hero — not buried it under a family activewear grid. I'd mark this read. I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The Sol de Janeiro brand partnership is a genuine editorial surprise — pocket-sized scents from a beauty brand I actually track breaks through a very cluttered email
- The 15% barcode coupon at the bottom is clean and scannable — no code to remember or type
- Kohl's Cash earn opportunity ($10 on $50) stacks with the discount, which is the one thing Kohl's does structurally right on loyalty
- Shop Now buttons are consistent and legible throughout — no ambiguous copy, no confusion about where to tap

## What's weak

- "Affordable activewear" is not my aisle — I'm not comparison-shopping TEK GEAR at $12.99 alongside anything in my regular rotation
- Three 15% sends in eight days means the mechanism has no psychological weight left; compare that to Macy's, which at least dresses its discount emails in different urgency clothing each time
- The layout is full Macy's-style overload: activewear hero → TEK GEAR callout → family grid → Sol de Janeiro → "future favorites" → actual coupon — five stops before I reach the thing the subject line promised
- "Busy families require teamwork" is aimed squarely at someone in a different life stage; the model mix skews young and family, not the Gen X woman with a beauty counter habit
- The 15% offer is buried at the bottom while the subject leads with it — Sephora would have surfaced that in the first scroll

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Sol de Janeiro.** That's the one hook that could convert a beauty-first subscriber. It should be the hero — not the fourth section in.
- 2. **Surface the 15% in the hero or second block.** The subject makes a savings promise; the email makes me earn the offer by scrolling through brand content first. That's a trust erosion pattern.
- 3. **Cut two sections.** Five content themes (activewear hero, TEK GEAR, family grid, beauty collab, future favorites) is three too many. Nail two and stop.
- 4. **Vary the offer cadence.** Three identical 15% sends in one week trains me to ignore the number. Even rotating the framing ("Kohl's Cash doubles this weekend") would reset the signal.
- 5. **Segment the list.** Someone engaging with beauty content should not receive the same activewear hero as a running-gear buyer. This is the highest-leverage change available.

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard retail batch email built on legacy HTML4 table layout with VML/MSO conditionals. Primary technical concerns are relay-obscured compliance headers and pervasive missing alt text on tracking pixels; no broken links or merge token leakage detected.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped** — all routed through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. HTTP probe cannot verify destination integrity or detect redirect chains; dead links would go undetected by automated QA.

**Three distinct tracking pixel systems** are active simultaneously:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — Coherent Path (personalization platform)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` and `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/...` — MessageGears
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager

No broken pixel endpoints detected, but the parallel open-tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `open.aspx`, and `b0c766cf...`) create triple-counted open events across three platforms — expected for multi-platform stacks but worth noting for metrics reconciliation.

**Malformed HTML tag:** `</custom>` closing tag on the `open.aspx` pixel `<div>` — not a standard element; likely a legacy ESP artifact. No rendering impact in major clients but invalid markup.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on all tracking pixels and several content images** (11 warnings from QA):

| Missing alt | Image |
|---|---|
| Open pixel | `click.chp.kohls.com/o/b0c766cf...` |
| Open pixel | `mi.kohls.com/p/up/.../o.gif` |
| AAM pixel | `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` |
| Content images | `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, `69494b48a79b4240.png`, `78308d1d9adb0751.png`, `916d6fa08d185233.png` |

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` `aria-hidden="true"` (the `o.gif` pixel has both — inconsistently applied). Content images without alt text render as broken image icons in image-blocking clients and fail WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — screen readers announce no document title; some spam filters flag empty titles.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, a WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**`max-height: auto`** in the mobile media query (`max-height: auto !important`) — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; browsers silently ignore it, but it indicates a copy-paste error in the stylesheet.

**Font double-loading:** Roboto and Montserrat each load via both `<link>` and `@import` inside the same `<![endif]-->` block — redundant round-trips on non-MSO clients.
