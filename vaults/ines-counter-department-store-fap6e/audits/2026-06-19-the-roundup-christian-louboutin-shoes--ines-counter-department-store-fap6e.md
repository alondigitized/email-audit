# The Roundup: Christian Louboutin Shoes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- I'll be honest — I almost marked this as read without clicking. Not because Louboutins don't interest me, but because this is at minimum the fifth Neiman Marcus email I've opened since June 15, and after the 60%-off Designer Sale send from June 19 that actually earned a real offer, this "Roundup" format feels like a gear-shift with no payoff. No price. No sale. No InCircle note. Just six beautiful shoes floating in white space asking me to go shopping without giving me a single reason to do it *right now*.
- What I'll give them: the edit is tasteful. "The Roundup" is NM's version of editorial curation, and it's the right tone for Louboutin. The red sole brand doesn't need hard-sell copy. But for me personally — the woman who refuses to use a card that doesn't give points back — a clean product grid with no offer attached is basically a catalog I didn't ask for. Saks Fifth Avenue sends me editorial roundups too, and the difference is they usually embed a member access angle or a trunk-show reminder alongside the looks.
- I wouldn't click. I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably scroll past and feel vaguely guilty about wanting the ankle-tie white heel but not wanting to pay full price for it today.

## What's working

- **The hero image is sharp and aspirational.** White shoe on a clean surface — no clutter, no busy editorial collage. It respects the brand.
- **"The Roundup: Christian Louboutin Shoes" is a clean subject.** Zero ambiguity. I know exactly what's inside before I open.
- **Product grid does real work.** Six distinct styles — classic nude strap, ribbon-tie heel, colorful espadrille wedge — shows range for a woman who shops differently at 47 than she did at 32. The ankle-tie heel in white is genuinely lovely.
- **No shouting.** This email doesn't scream at me. It's the Saks-adjacent register NM should be using with Louboutin specifically.
- **Personal style advisor footer.** The "Match with a personal style advisor" callout is actually the most relevant thing in this email for me — I use that service. Buried, but it's there.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** No price, no discount, no InCircle member access angle, nothing. After the June 19 "up to 60% off Designer Sale" email, this lands like a downgrade in intent. Kohl's — *Kohl's* — managed to give me a verb and a benefit in the same breath last week. NM gives me six shoes and two "Shop" buttons.
- **CTA pair is redundant.** "Shop Christian Louboutin" and "Shop Shoes" is essentially the same destination twice. Pick one.
- **No InCircle touchpoint.** I buy Louboutins when points are involved. If NM knows I'm an InCircle member, this is the email where a "earn triple points through Sunday" line would close me. It's not there.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email number six from NM in about five days. The editorial Roundup format doesn't earn a sixth slot in a week without a reason to act.
- **Espadrille wedge feels off-brief.** The multicolor woven platform in the bottom-right skews younger and more resort than the rest of the grid. It dilutes the Louboutin polish of the other five.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an InCircle or offer hook in the hero copy.** Even "Earn double points on Louboutin through Sunday" would give me a reason to click today rather than someday. Saks does this routinely inside their designer spotlights.
- 2. **Collapse the CTA to one button.** "Shop Christian Louboutin" — done. The "Shop Shoes" button just creates indecision.
- 3. **Lead with the ankle-tie heel, not the lifestyle hero.** The white ribbon-tie pump is the most timeless piece in the grid. It earns a solo hero slot at top.
- 4. **Pull the style advisor CTA up into the body.** "Want to see these in your size? Chat with your NM Style Advisor" as a line above the grid would make me pause. Right now it's footer furniture.
- 5. **Drop the espadrille wedge or reframe it.** "Vacation edit" or "resort picks" context would make it feel curated, not mismatched.
