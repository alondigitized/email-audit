# NEW SUNSCREEN 💦 ☀️ 🏖️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- Six emails in five days, Sephora. Six. And the one that breaks the streak of at least trying to have a concept — the bag edit, the points hook, even the Summer Juice Bar — is an all-caps subject line with three emojis that reads like a text from a brand intern who just discovered the shift lock key. That said, I opened it, because July sunscreen news genuinely does move me. I'm always looking for a new SPF that doesn't pill under makeup, and the hero section — yellow, sunny, "Something New Is Here" — at least commits to the season.
- But then I keep scrolling. And scrolling. And scrolling. There's a dry hair section at the bottom. I just got the 4X points on hair care email two days ago. It feels like somebody copy-pasted every product brief from the week into one template and called it a sunscreen email. The Whip product in the hero is the only thing that registers as the thing the subject promised.
- I'd mark it read. I might click the top sunscreen if I recognized the brand name clearly enough. I would not forward it — there's nothing here my girlfriend at the Nordstrom Beauty counter would text me back about.

## What's working

- **The yellow hero is seasonally right.** Summer color, outdoor vibes — it earns the sunscreen premise without feeling forced.
- **"Something New Is Here" with a shop-now button is a clean, low-friction entry.** No riddles, no mystery-box tease — just a forward signal.
- **The "Get a Mini" strip at the top is a smart awareness hook.** A free gift or trial size gets attention fast, even on a tiny mobile banner.
- **Sunscreen in July is correct timing.** After Neiman Marcus running 70%-off luxury and Kohl's Cash mechanics all month, a category-specific seasonal push at least feels deliberate.

## What's weak

- **All-caps subject line is a miss.** "NEW SUNSCREEN" with three beach emojis looks like a flash sale at a discount pop-up, not a curated Insider send. The 7/20 bag edit email — my 9/10 — opened with Charlotte Tilbury editorial energy. This does not.
- **The email doesn't know what it is.** Sunscreen hero, then summer products, then Dr. Dennis Gross, then something called Fable, then auto-replenish, then dry hair. I lost the thread before I got to the second section.
- **Dry hair at the bottom is tone-deaf given Tuesday's email.** I already got the 4X points on hair care pitch. Seeing it recycled here, untethered, reads like list fatigue, not curation.
- **I cannot read individual product names or prices at mobile scale.** If I can't see what I'm clicking toward, I'm not clicking.
- **No loyalty signal above the fold.** "Insider" appears in the footer — but a Beauty Insider like me wants to know if this new launch has early access, bonus points, or VIB pricing before I scroll all the way down.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Send the sunscreen story. Own it. Save the Fable launch and the dry hair section for tomorrow — Sephora's already sending daily, so use each slot for one clear idea.
- 2. **Move the Insider/loyalty signal above the fold.** If there's exclusive early access or bonus points on this new launch, say it in the first screen. That's why I'm an Insider.
- 3. **Make the lead sunscreen product readable at mobile scale** — brand name, key claim (SPF level, finish, skin type), and price visible without pinching in.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject out of all-caps.** Match the editorial register of the 7/20 bag edit that got a 9.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your next SPF is here — new launches for summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sunscreen just got an upgrade ☀️`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The new SPF formulas worth replacing your current one for.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Whip, protect, glow — new sunscreen drops you haven't seen yet.`
