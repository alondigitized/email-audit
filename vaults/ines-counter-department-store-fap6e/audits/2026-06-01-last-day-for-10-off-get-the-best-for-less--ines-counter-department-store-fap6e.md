# LAST DAY for 10% off 🚨 Get the best—for less.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Sephora opened this email by calling me Rosa. That is not my name. What makes this worse is I flagged this exact same problem in the May 26 send — the one scored 6/10 that led with "You're new here" — and here we are, eight days later, with "Rosa, here's a reason to keep shopping" sitting at the top of my screen. This is not a one-time mail merge hiccup. This is a pattern.
- Strip out the wrong name and the email is still the wrong email. "Your First Purchase." I have been a Beauty Insider long enough to have lived through the VIB Rouge era. I have the Sephora credit card in my wallet right now. Sending me a new-customer welcome offer — complete with the "Wondering Why You Got This? You're new here" block that apparently survived eight days of feedback — is not a personalization miss. It's a data systems failure dressed up as a marketing send. And the fine print sending me to kohls.co/sephora? I shop standalone Sephora and Nordstrom Beauty. That Kohl's barcode at the bottom is not for me.
- The countdown timer is clean, the code is legible, the button is there. But no — I would not click, and I would not forward this. I'd mark it read and file a mental complaint.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer earns its space**: "ENDS TODAY" with hours and minutes ticking — urgency reads at a glance, no scrolling required.
- **Offer is immediately legible**: 10% off, code SE3ARPH, visible above the fold. I know exactly what I'm looking at.
- **"SHOP NOW" button is findable**: Clean, above the fold, tap-friendly on mobile — no friction in getting to the action.
- **Render is clean**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapse. Technically, this email works.

## What's weak

- **Wrong name, again**: "Rosa, here's a reason to keep shopping." I am not Rosa. This is the second name failure in eight days from the same sender.
- **"Your First Purchase" offer sent to an established member**: I have Beauty Insider history, a Sephora credit card, and a shelf full of repeat purchases. A new-customer 10% offer is either a segmentation error or an accidental insult.
- **"Wondering Why You Got This? You're new here."**: This exact line appeared in the May 26 send and caused me to stop cold. It is back today, unchanged. Eight days have passed. Someone chose not to fix it.
- **Kohl's in the discount block**: "code for kohls.co/sephora below" and a Kohl's barcode at the bottom signals this creative was built for a different channel. Neiman Marcus ran a Saint Laurent editorial this week with zero mass-retail bleed. This cannot say the same.
- **Cadence fatigue has fully set in**: "LAST DAY for 10% off" is the fourth urgency-flavored subject since May 22. After "LAST CHANCE 🥺" and "ENDS TODAY‼️" and "Starts today!", this one reads as noise even before I open it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the segmentation before anything else**: A Beauty Insider member with purchase history must never land in the new-customer welcome flow. This is the single highest-impact fix — everything else is polish on a broken window.
- 2. **Replace the offer for returning members**: Established members respond to Bonus Points events, free gifts with spend, or early-access sale windows — not 10% off a "first purchase." Kohl's, of all brands, managed to feel more targeted this week with their lowest-prices pivot. Sephora should clear that bar.
- 3. **Remove the "Wondering Why You Got This?" block from non-new subscribers**: Segment it out. It is actively alienating to a known customer and has been for at least two consecutive sends.
- 4. **Move Kohl's attribution to the footer only**: If the code works cross-channel, say so in small text below the barcode — don't let it contaminate the primary offer block.
- 5. **Rest the urgency language for one cycle**: One non-urgency send — a product-led or editorial send, the way Neiman Marcus executed the Saint Laurent Mombasa email — would reset my trust in the deadline copy when it actually matters.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 10% is almost gone — here's what to grab`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bonus points on orders $75+ · valid today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack it with your Insider rewards before midnight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — User zoom disabled (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom for low-vision users on mobile. Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Issue 2 — Aggressive text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied globally across nearly every element. This disables OS-level text scaling for users who rely on it. Should be scoped only to elements with intentionally fixed font sizes (e.g., a specific pixel-locked header `td`), not `body` and `div` broadly.

**Issue 3 — Malformed robots meta value**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
The value is `no index` (with a space) rather than the correct `noindex`. Most parsers will ignore this entirely. Low severity in email context, but indicates template hygiene issue.
