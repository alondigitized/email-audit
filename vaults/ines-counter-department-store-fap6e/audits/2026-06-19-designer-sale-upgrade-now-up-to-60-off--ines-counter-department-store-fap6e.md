# Designer Sale upgrade: now up to 60% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- The deep red hero with "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" hits the right note the moment you open it — no mystery, no burying the lede, luxury-sale energy without screaming. And the brand grid underneath earns its keep: Jacquemus, L'Agence, Valentino Garavani, Stella McCartney — these are names I look up at Saks and Nordstrom every single time. When NM shows me those names at a sale discount, I pay attention.
- But here's where my enthusiasm flatlines: NM already sent me this email. Yesterday. The June 18 subject read "Good news: Our Designer Sale is now up to 60% off" — and I scored it an 8 because it was the real announcement. Today's email calls itself an "upgrade" but shows me the exact same headline number. If the discount had genuinely moved — say, from 50% to 70% — I'd be reaching for my card. Instead, "upgrade" is carrying enormous marketing weight for zero new information. Sephora pulled the same trick last week: "Get up to 50% off select beauty today" two sends in a row, nearly identical creative. I called it out then, and I'm calling it out now.
- I'll mark this as read. Not unsubscribe, because NM is still NM — but I'm not clicking today, and I'm definitely not forwarding it to anyone.

## What's working

- The red hero is the right visual register for a luxury sale — it reads premium without looking like a clearance banner
- The brand thumbnails are doing real editorial work: L'Agence, Jacquemus, Chiara Boni La Petite Robe, Valentino, Etro — recognizable to any woman who actually shops this tier
- Six segmented category CTAs (Women's Clothing, Shoes, Handbags, Dresses, Men's, Men's Shoes) give me a direct path without guessing — that's more thought than Macy's puts into its sale sends, which usually dump me on a generic sale landing page
- "Exclusions Apply" is surfaced visibly beneath the hero button — I'd rather see it upfront than discover it on the product page
- Clean render, readable on mobile without zooming, no broken images or truncated copy

## What's weak

- "Upgrade" in the subject promises something new; the hero delivers the exact same "60% OFF" I saw yesterday — that's a credibility burn with a savvy subscriber
- Not a single price point anywhere in the email — the Jacquemus thumbnail is lovely but gives me nothing to calibrate against without clicking through
- No InCircle mention at all — I refuse to use my card without points, and NM knows that; a sale email that doesn't acknowledge loyalty is a missed conversion on me specifically
- No beauty section, no skincare module — the June 17 beauty email (the 9 I gave them) proved NM knows I buy beauty; skipping it entirely in a 60%-off sale email leaves the easiest upsell on the table
- "Save on these designer styles before they're gone" is the softest urgency Kohl's would even bother writing — no date, no countdown, no inventory signal, no reason to act today versus Saturday

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the "upgrade" real or retire the word — "Was up to 50% → Now up to 60%" side by side in the hero is worth a second send; a rebadged repeat is not
- 2. Add at least one price anchor under the brand thumbnails — even "from $XXX" under the Jacquemus or Valentino tile would give me a click reason without a tab-switch
- 3. Surface InCircle prominently — "Earn double InCircle points on sale through Sunday" would make this a 9 immediately
- 4. Add a beauty or fragrance module below the fashion grid — you know I buy both; a sale email that pretends I'm fashion-only is leaving money on the table
- 5. Kill "before they're gone" and replace with an actual deadline — Macy's at least writes a date when it's adding urgency; NM should too
- **Subject Alt A:** `Designer Sale just got better — 60% off ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off + double InCircle points this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jacquemus, Valentino, L'Agence — prices you won't see again until December.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sizes are moving. Here's what's still in stock in your saved brands.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Designer Sale upgrade: now up to 60% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript templating. QA capture surfaced four warnings — two compliance header gaps and two deliverability signals — none of which indicate a hard send failure, but two carry inbox-placement risk if they reflect the live send headers rather than a relay-capture artifact.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect URLs skipped during HTTP probe.** QA could not confirm destination URLs, final redirect chains, or UTM parameter presence for any tracked link in this send. This is expected behavior for redirect-wrapped tracking URLs, but it means landing page alignment (Section 6) cannot be verified.

**Plain-text body is 70% URLs** (5,268 of 6,044 chars). This strongly suggests the plain-text part was auto-generated from HTML rather than hand-authored. Spam filters that score plain-text in isolation will penalize this ratio. It also means plain-text subscribers receive an unreadable wall of raw URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta blocks pinch-to-zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
This is a WCAG 1.4.4 violation. Mobile users with low vision cannot zoom the content.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element.** Screen readers cannot infer language, affecting correct pronunciation and accessibility tooling.

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>`:**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but indicates the template was not linted before send.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants remain in the `<head>`.** These are dead code from prior iterations and bloat the `<head>` without effect, but signal the template is not being cleaned between sends.

**CSS-only hamburger nav uses the `:checked` pseudo-class hack.** This is functional in modern clients but completely non-functional in Outlook (all versions), Gmail (older), and Samsung Mail — those subscribers see no mobile nav at all.

**`@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` iOS 9 workaround is still present.** iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2016; this is dead weight.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Template contains an unresolved "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction in a live comment block:**
```html
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This indicates the template has two conditional send paths for `@user` (hashed email vs. a CRM lookup). If the wrong scenario is active at send time, `@user` could render as an empty string or a raw unhashed address in tracking URLs. The commented-out SHA256 line suggests the hashed path was *not* used for this send — that should be confirmed against the Campaign Calendar record.

**Six AMPscript variables are defined only in HTML comments, not in executable `%%[ ]%%` blocks:**
```
@email, @base_refid, @refid, @user, @emailid, @uEm, @cmdid
```
If any downstream AMPscript block references these without its own `SET` statement, it will silently render as empty. QA cannot confirm execution context from the HTML fragment alone.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected by AgentMail relay** `[WARN]`. If this reflects the actual send headers (not a relay capture gap), CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) and RFC 2369 compliance cannot be confirmed. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface one-click unsubscribe UI; its absence degrades subscriber experience and increases spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** `[WARN]`. This is required for Gmail and Yahoo's one-click unsubscribe enforcement (their 2024 bulk sender requirements). Without it, Gmail bulk senders risk inbox demotion.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** `[WARN]`. SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain and should have a published SPF record and DKIM key, but relay capture did not confirm alignment. If DMARC is enforced on `neimanmarcus.com`, a misaligned subdomain send would fail.

**No issues found** with physical address disclosure or opt-out mechanism in the HTML body — standard footers appear to be present based on the `.footerText` and `.disclaimerText` CSS classes.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot be verified.** All 26 tracked links use click-redirect wrappers that the HTTP probe skipped. UTM parameter presence and correctness (campaign, source, medium, content) on final landing URLs are unconfirmed.

**Risk to flag:** the `@base_refid` / `@refid` AMPscript variable (used for link decoration) has ambiguous initialization. The comment shows `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` — a no-op concatenation, suggesting a placeholder for a suffix that was removed. If `@base_refid` is empty at send time, all refid-decorated URLs will carry a blank parameter rather than a campaign identifier, breaking attribution.
