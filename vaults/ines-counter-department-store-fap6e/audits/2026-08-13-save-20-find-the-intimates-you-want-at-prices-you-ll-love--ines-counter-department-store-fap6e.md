# Save 20% | Find the intimates you want at prices you'll love ❤️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Kohl's again — that's eleven of these in as many days now if I'm keeping honest count, and my thumb was already hovering over the delete swipe before I even opened it. This time it's intimates and bras, which fine, that's a real category, but the moment I hit "Semi-Annual" bras sale and then scroll into a random detour through dorm bedding, storage bins, a sweepstakes, a drink maker, bath towels, and Gloria Vanderbilt jeans, I lose the plot entirely. This isn't a bra sale email, it's a Kohl's homepage wearing a bra-sale hat for the first three inches.
- The bra and shapewear pricing is honestly not bad — $19.99 bras, 25% off Jockey, 5 for $35 panties — and the model casting (a woman who could pass for my daughter's college friend, fine) isn't offensive. But I don't shop Kohl's for intimates the way I shop Nordstrom or Saks for a proper fitting, and nothing here — no sizing chart, no fit guidance, no fabric info — gives me a reason to trust the fit sight unseen. Compare that to Neiman Marcus's sandal roundup I looked at recently: one hero image, one editorial point of view, no clutter. This is the opposite of that.
- Would I click? Maybe on the 25% Jockey banner since I actually know that brand. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on — same as I've done with nine of the last ten Kohl's sends.

## What's working

- The "Semi-Annual" framing at least signals this is a real periodic event, not just another random Tuesday markdown.
- Jockey call-out (25% off) is a brand I recognize and trust, which pulled my eye more than the generic "intimates" copy did.
- Pricing is stated plainly — $19.99, 5/$35, 25% off — no hunting for the number.
- The rewards status bar up top ("KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER, Earn: 5% REWARDS") at least acknowledges I'm a known customer, which Sephora's "Rosie" mixup last week reminded me not to take for granted.

## What's weak

- The email completely abandons its own subject line after the third scroll — bras become dorm bedding becomes a sweepstakes becomes a blender becomes jeans. I came for intimates, not a stockroom clearance.
- No sizing or fit information anywhere near the bra/shapewear section — for a category this personal, that's a real gap, especially next to how Saks or Nordstrom would handle a lingerie push with actual fit guidance.
- "Back to college made easy" and the sweepstakes are demographically tone-deaf for a 47-year-old — I am not furnishing a dorm room.
- Barcode coupon at the bottom is a nice touch for in-store but it's buried under six unrelated product pushes by the time I'd scroll there.
- This is the same crimson-and-cream template, same starburst-adjacent layout I've seen from Kohl's five times this week — nothing about the creative itself is fresh.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to intimates only. If Kohl's wants to cross-sell dorm goods, that's a separate send — jamming eight categories into one email is exactly the "Macy's-style overload" I complain about, except here it's Kohl's doing it to itself.
- 2. Add one line of fit/sizing reassurance near the bra hero — "true to size, extended sizes available" — something that makes me trust the purchase without an in-store try-on.
- 3. Lead with Jockey since it's the only recognizable name-brand anchor in the whole email — that's my actual reason to click.
- 4. Drop the college sweepstakes and dorm decor block from an intimates-themed send entirely; it reads like two different marketing calendars got merged by accident.
- 5. Vary the template — five near-identical crimson/cream layouts this week is starting to make me tune out the whole brand, not just this email.
