# $10 off your $25 purchase ✅ $5 Kohl's Cash for every $25 spent ✅
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth near-identical Kohl's send in my inbox this week, and by now I could recite the format in my sleep — Labor Day banner, $10-off-$25 stacked with $5-Kohl's-Cash-per-$25, then a bottomless scroll of category tiles: tees, bedding, activewear, dresses, floor care, bath towels, kitchen, athletic shoes, fleece, then a whole second "Clearance up to 85%" section with its own tiles. It's the "Fall home picks" email's structure with a Labor Day coat of paint.
- Nothing here says "beauty regular" or "counter customer" to me at all — this is a department-store-wide catalog blast, not a beauty or accessories play, and there's zero loyalty-tier language beyond the generic "KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER" status line at the very top, which they don't do anything with. Compare that to Sephora's stacking-weekend email, which at least ties the sale math to Beauty Insider points — Kohl's just repeats "Kohl's Cash" mechanically for the fourth day running.
- I'd mark this read, not click. Nothing pulls me in specifically, and the sheer volume of tiles (I count at least fourteen separate offers) means I'd have scrolled past before finding anything I actually want. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The Labor Day banner up top with the $10-off and $5-Cash math is at least legible and unambiguous — I know exactly what the two stacking offers are within two seconds.
- The clean nav row (Women / Men / Kids / Home) under the hero gives me a fast way to skip to a category if I were shopping.
- The "Picked with you in mind" section is a nice personalization gesture even if the picks themselves (a slushie maker, bath towels, a mattress topper, jeans) feel completely random for me.
- Coupon codes and barcodes at the bottom are handy if I were actually going in-store — Kohl's is one of the few department stores that still makes their coupons this scannable.

## What's weak

- Beauty is nowhere in this email — no counter brands, no fragrance, no skincare tile — which is a miss for someone like me who lives at the beauty counter; Sephora and Nordstrom would never leave that category out of a "shop everything" send.
- The tile count is exhausting — Home floor care, bath towels, kitchen, shoes, fleece, then a whole separate Clearance block with its own Home/Men/Kids repeat. This is the Macy's-style overload problem, except Macy's at least had a confirm-now urgency hook; here it's just volume for volume's sake.
- Zero designer or elevated-brand signal — everything reads mass-market (Big One towels, Ninja slushie maker, Serta topper) with no sense of occasion or event, unlike the Neiman Marcus wedding-guest email that led with one gorgeous hero shot and no percentage at all.
- The "your great taste" personalization line rings hollow when the actual picks (a frozen drink maker, bath towels) show no sign anyone knows my taste.
- This is basically the same Labor Day scaffolding as yesterday's "$10 off + 15% off home" send and the "STARTS TODAY" send before that — three Labor Day emails in three days with the same $10/$5 stack is cadence fatigue, not urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the tile count roughly in half and lead with ONE category hero tied to the season — right now this reads like every department dumped their assets into one file.
- 2. Add a beauty tile — even a small one — since that's the category that would actually make me stop scrolling.
- 3. Do something with the "KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER" status at the top other than just naming it — show me a personalized point balance or tier perk, the way Sephora ties its stacking sale to Beauty Insider tiers.
- 4. Stop repeating the exact $10/$5 stack for a third straight day — rotate the hook or escalate the offer so this doesn't feel like the same email recycled.
- 5. Replace the "Picked with you in mind" random grab-bag with actual browsing-history-based picks, or drop the "great taste" line entirely — it's currently just false personalization.
