# Save up to 50% on select beauty 💄 Plus spend $75 to earn 500 bonus points.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Finally, no "Rosie." I've been waiting nine days and three misfires for Sephora to remember my name — and the solution they landed on was to drop personalization entirely. Fine. I'll take a clean inbox over another misfire. The "Stacking Weekend" mechanic stopped me, because stacking is my actual shopping behavior: I wait for the double event, I show up at the counter with my points loaded, and I spend accordingly. The 50% off plus 500 bonus points combo in the hero is the exact offer that makes me calendar-block a shopping trip.
- That said, by the time this email ends, it has thrown five or six value propositions at me — the sale, the points threshold, an auto-replenish pitch, a hair section with zero connection to why I'm here, and a store-finder map I didn't ask for. Somewhere between "50% off" and "Frizz-Smoothout Picks" I started to feel like Macy's was running this send, not Sephora. The email opens like a Saks member event and closes like a Sunday circular.
- I'd click the hero offer. I would not forward this. It's functional enough for me to use but not sharp enough for me to share.

## What's working

- The "Stacking Weekend" mechanic hits both my triggers at once — a discount AND a points accelerator. That's not a sale, that's a reason to show up at the counter.
- The Beauty Insider barcode at the bottom is genuinely useful. I always have to dig through my app to find it; having it pre-loaded in the email is a smart counter-prep move for someone like me.
- The hero reads clearly top-to-bottom: discount → points → spend threshold → CTA. The hierarchy at the fold is clean.
- 500 points for $75 is concrete and credible — I can calculate exactly what I need to spend. No vague "earn more points" language.
- No "Rosie." Low bar, but we're clearing it this week.

## What's weak

- "Save up to 50% on select" is two hedges packed into five words. "Up to" and "select" together tell me the one product I want is probably excluded. Neiman Marcus ran a summer sale email this month that led with a specific product and let it breathe — this lead copy does the opposite, promising big and immediately narrowing.
- The "Frizz-Smoothout Picks" section came out of nowhere. I'm at Sephora for foundation and a new serum, not humidity control. This reads like a segment leak or a lazy cross-sell dropped in because hair had unsold inventory.
- The auto-replenish/Salta pitch interrupts the sale flow. A Stacking Weekend email should stay on the event. Subscription upsells belong in their own send.
- Five emails in nine days. After back-to-back "10% off ends soon, Rosie" sends, this lands with skepticism already baked in. The stacking offer deserves more runway than it's getting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the stacking mechanic, not the raw percentage.** "50% off + 500 bonus points this weekend" frames the two offers as a package. The word "stacking" is the hook — it implies strategy, it implies a Beauty Insider knows something a casual shopper doesn't. Use it earlier.
- 2. **Cut the hair section and the replenishment pitch.** A Stacking Weekend is one clean ask: come shop, earn big. Send the Frizz-Smoothout segment separately to people with a hair purchase history.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "This Weekend" is not a deadline. "Ends Sunday at midnight" turns "I'll go maybe" into "I'm opening the app right now." Kohl's at least puts a dollar figure in the subject — Sephora has a time peg and isn't using it.
- 4. **Lead with one featured product, not an immediate product grid.** Give me one hero item to want. The grid is fine for browsing; it doesn't create desire.
- 5. **Reintroduce tier context.** "Rouge member" or "VIB" next to the points offer would confirm Sephora knows exactly who is spending this money. Right now it reads like a broadcast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stacking Weekend: 50% off + 500 bonus points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Beauty Insider weekend — 50% off + 500 pts`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75, earn 500 bonus points. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50% off select beauty — stack your Insider points before midnight Sunday.`

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is 0 characters.**
CAN-SPAM does not mandate plain text, but its complete absence means:
- Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Barracuda) score the message higher risk
- RFC 2822 multi-part MIME best practice (`multipart/alternative`) is violated
- Some corporate mail gateways strip HTML-only messages entirely

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured by AgentMail relay.**
If the header is absent at delivery, the email violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024+) for senders above 5,000/day. Sephora's volume virtually guarantees they exceed this threshold. Evidence: QA header scan returned no `List-Unsubscribe` value.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header missing (RFC 8058).**
Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button in the UI cannot function, increasing spam complaints. This compounds the above.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found.**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay capture gap. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a relay instrumentation gap, not necessarily a sending failure — but it should be verified against raw headers from a seed account.
