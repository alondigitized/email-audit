# Reminder: Earn up to a $1,500 gift card today + Triple Points
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, now we're back to the tiered-math game — and honestly, this one's better built than most. "Earn a $75-$1,500 gift card" with the actual spend table right there ($500→$75, $10,000+→$1,500) means I don't have to click through to do arithmetic, which is more than I can say for some of Neiman's cousins in this inbox. Layering "Plus, TRIPLE POINTS for InCircle Members" underneath in that black bar is exactly the kind of member-first signal that keeps me loyal — I have the card, I know what it means, and it's stated cleanly instead of buried in fine print.
- Where it loses me a little: this is the second gift-card-tier email I've gotten from Neiman's this week on top of Wednesday's Brunello Cucinelli triple-points send, and the product grid underneath — Mac Duggal, La Mer, Burberry, Versace — is a nice scroll but it's generic "shop everything" merchandising, not curated toward me specifically. Compare that to Wednesday's Brunello send, which was laser-focused on one designer I actually buy.
- I'd click. The code's visible, the table does the math for me, and the InCircle line closes the deal. I wouldn't forward it — this is routine account math, not a "look what I found" moment.

## What's working

- The spend/earn table up top does my math for me — no mental gymnastics required, unlike Macy's "confirm now" scramble from a few days back.
- "TRIPLE POINTS for InCircle Members" in its own black banner reads like they know I'm a member, not a stranger.
- Promo code NMTREND is front and center on a real button, not hidden in body copy.
- Product grid at least stays in my aesthetic lane — Jimmy Choo, Dolce&Gabbana, La Mer — nothing that reads off-brand or off-demographic.
- Clean deadline stated later in the fine print (8/25/26, 10:00 PM CT) — I can find it, even if it should be higher up.

## What's weak

- This is the second "earn points/gift card" mechanic email from Neiman's this week — after Wednesday's Brunello Cucinelli triple-points send — and it's starting to feel like the same lever pulled twice.
- The product grid (9 items) is undifferentiated house-wide merchandising — dress, sweater, jeans, jacket, bag, shoes, cream, sweater, loafers — it's not telling me a story, it's just filling space.
- The actual redemption deadline and exclusions are crammed into dense fine print at the very bottom — I have to scroll past two full product grids and six category buttons to find when this actually expires.
- "Shop New Fall Collections" headline is vague — doesn't tell me what's actually new versus what's just been re-tagged for the promo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the expiration date (8/25/26) up near the code and offer table — right now urgency is an afterthought buried in paragraph six of fine print, and Macy's "confirm now" email at least got urgency right up top.
- 2. Cut the product grid from nine items to three-to-four genuinely new arrivals, or swap it for a "shop by category you buy" personalized block — I don't need Mac Duggal formalwear and MOTHER denim in the same three rows.
- 3. Pull the InCircle triple-points detail out of dense terms-and-conditions copy at the bottom and give it one clarifying line near the top table — I want to know if triple points stack with the gift card tiers without hunting for it.
- 4. Space out the cadence — two point/gift-card mechanic emails in one week starts to feel like the same trick twice, even if the tiers are different.
