# 🌟 THIS JUST IN 🌟
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixteenth Sephora email I've had in about a week, and the subject line is the whole problem. "🌟 THIS JUST IN 🌟" in caps with matching stars is the shouting I notice. At Nordstrom or Saks I'd get a plain line like "New arrivals for you." Here it tells me nothing, and it's also the third "this just in" Sephora has sent me this week.
- The inside is better than the subject. At the very top there's a Savings Event strip with a 30% badge, "Coming soon," and a Learn More button. Right under it comes a Carolina Herrera Good Girl Jasmine fragrance mini, which is a gift-with-purchase offer. I know this brand and trust it. But the email is trying to be a new-arrivals feed, a gift guide, a Savings Event teaser and an app ad all at once. The "Something New Is Here" hero is pretty, with the red butterfly bottle, but it's a fragrance travel spray at $37. I'd skim it, maybe click the Herrera, and probably not forward it.

## What's working

- The Carolina Herrera hero is elegant. The butterfly bottle is a real product image, and "New + Only at Sephora" tells me why it's worth my attention.
- The "New Must-Haves" grid is easy to scan. Each product has a name, a price and a "New / Limited Edition" tag. Prices like $26, $85 and $34 are visible without any clicking.
- The Savings Event strip at the top tells me a discount is coming, so I can hold my purchases. I do appreciate being told.
- The Beauty Insider tier row (Insider, VIB, Rouge) and "Rewards Bazaar" near the bottom remind me of the points program I care about.
- The gift sets and body care blocks are tidy, and the fragrance and skincare mix suits my age group.

## What's weak

- The caps and stars subject is shouty and says nothing. Neiman Marcus led with "UP TO 30% OFF" and made me feel respected. This one made me feel like a number.
- The Savings Event strip is crammed up top. The 30% badge, the "Coming soon" text and the other offers all compete, and I'd have to squint to see what it means for me.
- The email has no single offer. The Herrera mini needs a $40 purchase. The Savings Event is "coming soon." The new-product grid has no discount at all. That's overload, in the Macy's style.
- The legal fine print at the bottom is long and tiny, and I can't read it without zooming. That's the opposite of honest-feeling.
- Nothing is personal. Sephora knows what I buy, and the grid isn't based on it. Yesterday's "chosen just for you" email felt fake, and this one doesn't even try.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one real offer. Move the Herrera gift-with-purchase to the top, with a clear "$40 gets you a free mini" line and one button. Hold the Savings Event teaser for its own send.
- 2. Cut the new-arrivals grid to three or four products, and say why each is worth my attention, like "$26 and already sold out in stores."
- 3. Show my tier and points. Something like "You're X points from your next reward" would get me to click, because I won't use a card without points back.
- 4. Shorten the fine print, or give it its own "Details" link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Carolina Herrera's butterfly bottle, plus a free mini`
- **Subject Alt B:** `6 new launches, only at Sephora`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Good Girl Jasmine mini with $40, and your Insider points count.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Savings Event starts soon. See what's new before it does.`
