# Take 10% off, Rosie
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I opened it because my name was in the subject line. Then I looked twice because the name isn't mine — it says "Rosie." That's the kind of thing that stops me cold. Sephora has my data, I've been a Beauty Insider for years, and they're greeting me as someone else. That alone makes the whole email feel like a forward from a stranger.
- And then the offer lands: 10% off my **first purchase**. First purchase. I'm standing here with a Beauty Insider card, a barcode tattooed in my phone's wallet, and a very specific relationship with the Hourglass counter at my local store — and Sephora is treating me like I just wandered in from a Google ad. That's not a discount, that's an insult dressed up in their signature black and white.
- I'm marking this read. I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding. The in-store barcode at the bottom is the one thing that would ever make me pause — but not for this offer.

## What's working

- **Sephora's brand is immediately legible** — clean header, their exact typography, no visual noise. I know exactly who sent this before I read a word.
- **The offer is large and unambiguous in the hero** — "10% OFF" is impossible to miss; I don't have to hunt for the number.
- **"STARTS TODAY" creates genuine urgency** — feels like a real event, not a manufactured countdown.
- **The in-store barcode at the bottom is legitimately useful** — I shop the counter; having a scannable code that works on sephora.com and Kohl's is one detail they got right for someone like me.
- **Beauty Insider tier is visible** — I can see my program status is acknowledged in the email, which tells me they know I'm a member.

## What's weak

- **Wrong name in the subject line** — "Rosie" is not me. This is either a merge failure or I received someone else's personalized send. Either way, it destroys the intimacy the personalization was supposed to create.
- **"Your First Purchase" offer sent to an existing member** — I'm a Beauty Insider. This email is a new-customer acquisition send that landed in a loyalty member's inbox. The disconnect is jarring and slightly condescending.
- **"Wondering Why You Got This?" section** — This reads as defensive. Sephora is pre-emptively apologizing for targeting me, which undermines any confidence I had that this was intentional. It's the email equivalent of a salesperson saying "I know this isn't really your thing, but…"
- **10% off is a weak offer for a loyalty customer** — At my tier, I expect Rouge-level moments. A first-purchase coupon feels like they raided the welcome series by accident.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic** — for a beauty-heavy customer, "Shop Skincare" or "Explore New Arrivals" would pull harder than a verb-plus-nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the merge token immediately** — Shipping "Rosie" to a non-Rosie inbox is a trust breach. Audit the list before the next send; include a fallback ("Beauty Insider" or no name) for any record where first-name confidence is low.
- 2. **Segment first-purchase offers away from existing members** — This email should never hit a Beauty Insider inbox. If it's a re-engagement trigger, the offer and copy need to reflect that ("We've missed you — here's 10% back on your next order") not "here's a welcome coupon."
- 3. **Lift the "Recommended For You" section above the offer explanation** — Personalized product pulls are the most valuable thing in this email; they're buried below the fold and the "Why You Got This" section.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop Skincare" or "See Your Picks" ties the tap to something I actually care about.
- 5. **Cut "Wondering Why You Got This?" entirely** — If the targeting is right, you don't need to explain it. If it's wrong, this section doesn't fix it; it just makes Sephora look uncertain.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Beauty Insider reward starts today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `10% off — just for you, this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code SE3ARPH in store or online through May 25`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your personalized picks are ready — plus a discount to start`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Invalid `robots` meta directive**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` with a space is not a recognized directive; the valid token is `noindex` (no space). This affects the web/mirror version if indexed.

**Issue 2 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Suppressing zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Users with low vision who rely on pinch-zoom cannot enlarge content. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue 3 — Overly broad `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applying this to `body` overrides OS-level font size preferences. Limit to specific table cells where layout-breaking auto-adjustment is the actual risk.

No issues found with VML namespace declarations or Outlook conditional comment structure (not present in visible source, but namespace inclusion is standard).
