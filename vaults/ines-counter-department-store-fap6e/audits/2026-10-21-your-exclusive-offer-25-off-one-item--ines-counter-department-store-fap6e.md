# Your exclusive offer: 25% off one item
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- A personal code. Finally, an email from Neiman's with an actual offer for me and not a mood board. Mauve hero, "25% OFF" in big type, one regular-price item, a code in a black bar. I understood it in two seconds, which is more than I can say for the last three gift-card ladder emails. It also doesn't shout, which I appreciate.
- Then I hit the fine print. It excludes beauty and fragrance, my whole reason for being a Neiman's customer. It also excludes Chanel, Dior, Hermès, Gucci, Prada, Saint Laurent, Moncler and Van Cleef, so most of the names I'd actually shop. The grid shows Burberry, Chloe, Givenchy and Manolo, and some of those may not be safe either. "Select brands" is carrying a lot of weight in that paragraph. Jimmy Choo, Amiri and Alémais aren't on the excluded list as far as I can read, but I'm not hunting through 80 brand names to check.
- Would I click? Maybe once, for a shoe or a coat. I'd forward it only to a friend with a Burberry trench on her wish list. Compared with Sephora, the offer is cleaner here but the points payoff is nothing. Neiman's gives me no InCircle mention at all. I refuse to spend without points back, and this email never tells me whether I'd get any.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "25% OFF, one regular-price item" has no jargon and I can read it fast.
- The code sits in its own black bar, so I can copy it without hunting. Macy's makes me confirm first, and this doesn't.
- The tone is calm. "Treat yourself" is gentler than Kohl's "Lowest Prices of the Season" shouting.
- The 3x3 designer grid (Burberry coat, Chloe bag, Manolo slingback) matches the aspirational, designer-access tone I expect from Neiman's.
- The deadline is real and dated: 10/22 at 10:00 PM CT.

## What's weak

- The exclusions are buried in tiny type at the bottom, and they cover most of the top houses. That feels like bait-and-switch.
- Beauty and fragrance are excluded and the email never says so up top. For a beauty-counter regular, that's the dealbreaker.
- "Exclusions Apply" in the hero is too faint to read.
- There's no InCircle or loyalty message anywhere. Nothing says I'd earn points or that this is a member perk.
- This is roughly the 11th Neiman's email in a week. After "Ends today: Earn a $100–$1,250 gift card" yesterday and "Our Holiday Fantasy," a "just for you" offer feels like a mass send.
- The grid products aren't labeled with prices, and I can't tell which brands qualify.
- The six mauve "Shop" buttons have low contrast, grey text on mauve. They're hard to read and look disabled.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a one-line exclusion note under the hero, such as "Excludes beauty, fragrance & select designers," and link a brand-eligibility page. Honesty here would lift trust more than anything else.
- 2. Show only eligible brands in the grid, with the sale price visible. The code is the point, so show me what it saves.
- 3. Add an InCircle line, such as "Earn points on this purchase" or "Member offer."
- 4. Make the CTA concrete, like "Shop eligible styles." Make the shop buttons dark with white text.
- 5. Cut the cadence. Don't send a gift-card ladder and a personal code within 48 hours.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your code: 25% off one regular-price piece`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Coats, shoes and bags from Burberry, Chloe, Jimmy Choo. Code inside, beauty excluded.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One-time code, valid through 10/22. See eligible designers before you shop.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based, XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a mobile media-query block. The four QA warnings are mostly capture limits of the AgentMail relay, not confirmed sender defects. The source is truncated, so the body, footer, and link markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **30 tracking links not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains, and status codes are unverified.
- **Plain text is 5675 of 8001 characters URLs (about 71%).** This is above the 70% threshold. It most likely comes from long encoded tracking URLs repeated in the text part, and it is a spam-filter risk. Shorter or fewer tracked URLs in the text part would help.
- No broken links were confirmed. None could be, given the skipped probes and truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is restricted.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom in clients that honor it, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Exact-match class selectors.** The mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. These only match elements whose entire `class` attribute equals that value. Any element with a second class, such as `class="mobile ImgSize"`, will not get the responsive rule. Use `.contenttable` or `*[class~=...]`.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules depend on `:checked` support, which Gmail and most Outlook versions lack. The fallback navigation cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Legacy CSS.** `.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` and `-webkit-animation: bugfix` (an iOS 9 hack) are obsolete. They are harmless but are dead weight.
- **Title.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space.
- Alt text, `lang`, and `role="presentation"` on layout tables are not visible in the truncated source, so they could not be assessed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template internals shipped in delivered HTML.** An HTML comment at the top of the source exposes internal SFMC variable names, data-extension names, and lookup logic. Examples are `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, and `@uEm` and `@cmdid`. It is not rendered, but anyone viewing source can read it. It also contains a stray unbalanced `)` and the line `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`, which suggests the template-authoring comment was never stripped before send.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE.** Some clients and webmail parsers may handle this differently. Move or strip the comment.
- No unresolved visible merge tokens, such as `%%first_name%%`, were found in the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found (WARN).** QA notes the relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed. Verify in the raw message at the sending ESP.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found (WARN).** The same caveat applies. One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders, so confirm it on the original send.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN).** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should be checked directly.
- A physical postal address and an in-body unsubscribe link are not visible in the truncated source, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTM parameters and landing destinations could not be verified. Links go through click-tracking redirects that QA skipped, and the visible source shows no final URLs.
- The `@refid` and `@base_refid` variables in the header comment suggest a campaign ref ID is appended to links. Whether it survives the redirect to the landing page is untested.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Pull the raw `.eml` from the ESP and confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass for `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
2. Strip the SFMC authoring comment, which exposes DE names and lookup logic, from the production template, and add a pre-send lint for it.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Change `*[class=x]` selectors to `.x` or `[class~=x]` so responsive rules apply to multi-class elements.
5. Shorten or reduce URLs in the plain-text part to bring URL density below 70%.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a redirect-following probe on the 30 tracked links. That would verify unsubscribe placement, the postal address, alt text, UTM and refid persistence, and landing-page parity.
