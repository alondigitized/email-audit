# Stop scrolling: up to 40% off select Living Proof 🤍
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the subject promised "up to 40% off select Living Proof" and I had to scroll past a complimentary Kérastase serum offer, a "Beauty's Most Wanted" hero, Kérastase again, YSL fragrance, NARS, Sincerely Yours, MAC, Gisou, Sol de Janeiro, and Refy before I even hit the Living Proof deal — that's nine product blocks before the thing the subject line actually sold me on. This is the exact "wall of new products" problem I flagged in Sephora's "New beauty to obsess over" send from 9/25 — same top-to-bottom tile scroll, just with a bait-and-switch subject this time instead of an honest one.
- By the time I got to the hair section, it wasn't even clear the 40% off applied to Living Proof specifically — "Save big on Sienna Naturals & Hair Faves" reads like a different, broader promo, and "TODAY ONLY" is slapped on with no visible end time. Would I click? No. Would I forward this to a friend? Only if she's the one who buys Sienna Naturals and I wanted to give her a heads up about the deal buried at the bottom.
- Compare this to Sephora's own Hair Daily Deals send from 9/23 (which I scored 8/10) — that one opened strong with the discount visible immediately. This one hides the discount from the subject line eight scrolls deep. Neiman Marcus's "Between-season style additions" at least led with a real hero shot that did work in two seconds; this just throws products at me until something sticks.

## What's working

- The "Complimentary Kérastase" GWP banner at the very top is a clean, concrete hook — free gift with $80+ is a real number I can act on.
- "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" badges on Sincerely Yours and Gisou are a trust cue I actually look for at my age — I care what's in the bottle.
- The black-and-white hero model for "Beauty's Most Wanted" has an editorial, grown-up feel that doesn't scream Gen Z the way some Sephora sends do.
- "First Access: Holiday Drops" with "24 Days" callout is a smart bit of forward-looking urgency for someone like me who shops early for the season.

## What's weak

- The subject says Living Proof, 40% off — that offer doesn't show up until nearly the bottom, and even then it's framed as "Sienna Naturals & Hair Faves," not Living Proof by name. That's a mismatch I'd call out same as the fragrance-points merry-go-round Sephora's been running since 9/19.
- Nine unrelated product blocks (Kérastase, YSL, NARS, Sincerely Yours, MAC, Gisou, Sol de Janeiro, Refy) between the subject line and the actual deal — this is Macy's-style overload, not a targeted send.
- "TODAY ONLY" on the hair offer has zero visible deadline time, so I can't tell if I have three hours or "until midnight Pacific" — feels rushed rather than credible.
- No Beauty Insider points or tier messaging visible anywhere in this send — I'm a member, and nothing here acknowledges it, unlike Macy's confirmation email which at least tied the urgency to my account status.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Living Proof/hair offer since that's what got me to open — move it above the fold, name Living Proof explicitly (not "Sienna Naturals & Hair Faves"), and put a real countdown clock or timestamp on "TODAY ONLY."
- 2. Cut the middle six product blocks (YSL through Sol de Janeiro) or move them to a separate send — a subscriber who opened for a hair discount doesn't need a fragrance-to-skincare-to-makeup tour first.
- 3. Add my Beauty Insider tier/points balance somewhere in this email — even a small line would make me feel like a known customer instead of a stranger.
- 4. Give the "up to 40%" a floor example ("30-40% off Living Proof shampoo & conditioner") so it's not vague marketing math.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Living Proof: up to 40% off, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your hair deal is here — 40% off Living Proof`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free Kérastase serum with $80+ — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shampoo, conditioner & styling — all discounted through midnight`
