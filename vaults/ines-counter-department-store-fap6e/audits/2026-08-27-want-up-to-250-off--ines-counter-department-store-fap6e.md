# Want up to $250 off?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is déjà vu — Neiman's already sent me this exact "spend $400/save $100, spend $1,000/save $250" tiered promo yesterday, and here it is again with the code swapped in as the hero move. I know I flagged it before as clean math, and honestly it still is — the tier table right under the button is genuinely useful, no gift-card gymnastics, no fake clock ticking down. But two days running on the same offer structure, and I'm not opening this out of anticipation anymore, I'm opening it out of habit.
- The nine-piece grid underneath is the real problem for me — it's a shotgun blast from Mac Duggal to Amiri to a Jimmy Choo bag to Jacquemus sneakers, with zero curation logic I can find. Nobody at Neiman's looked at my purchase history and thought "Ines buys at the trunk show, she returns at the trunk show" — this is just "here's inventory."
- Would I click? Maybe, for the tier math alone since I do have things sitting in a cart. Would I forward it? No — there's nothing here worth telling a friend about, it's the same spend-and-save math I already saw Monday.

## What's working

- The tier table (spend $400/save $100, spend $1,000/save $250) is still the clearest math I've seen from any of these retailers this week — no gift-card trickery like the earlier "Earn up to a $1,500 gift card" send.
- Clean gold hero block, legible SHOP NOW button, code right there — no scavenger hunt for SHOPFALL.
- "Exclusions Apply" is stated up front instead of buried six paragraphs down in the fine print — small honesty win.
- Personal style advisor and "find a store" links acknowledge I might actually walk into a Neiman's, which none of Kohl's or Sephora's emails ever bother with.

## What's weak

- This is the same offer mechanic as yesterday's "$250 off" send — Neiman's is now three-for-three this week on repeat structures (gift card, gift card, gift card, now tier-promo twice), and I'm starting to tune it out the way I tune out Kohl's "Friends & Family 20%" wallpaper.
- The nine-product grid has zero point of view — a strapless Mac Duggal gown next to Jacquemus sneakers next to a Jimmy Choo bag tells me nothing about who this email thinks I am.
- No beauty or fragrance callout at all, and the fine print actually excludes beauty/fragrance from the discount — as a counter-loyalist that's the one category I'd want flagged, not silently carved out.
- No loyalty tier or InCircle points mention anywhere — I refuse to use a card without points back, and this email doesn't even try to remind me what I'd earn.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop running the identical tier-promo structure two sends in a row — rotate in something that references what's actually in my cart or my InCircle status, the way Saks' personal-shopper notes at least attempt to.
- 2. Cut the grid from nine random items to three, tied to a category I've actually shopped (shoes/handbags for me), the way the "Pre-order new designer arrivals" send at least had an editorial point of view.
- 3. Surface InCircle points earned on this purchase directly in the hero — that's the single lever most likely to move me to click.
- 4. Add a beauty-counter callout or explicitly note the beauty exclusion up top, not just in six-point fine print — don't let me find out at checkout.
