# 1,000+ styles to add to your fall lineup
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-07**
## Executive summary

- Well, finally — no black "S" banner, no mango, no "ends today" panic. This is Neiman's doing what Neiman's is actually good at: a clean grid of covetable pieces with real designer names attached — Givenchy, Alaïa, Bottega, Fendi. That leopard-print Givenchy bag stopped my scroll cold, and the Alaïa mules right under it kept me there. This reads like an editor's pull, not a sale flyer.
- But here's my problem — there's no price on a single item, no percentage off, nothing. I love browsing new arrivals, but I'm not buying an Alaïa mule off a thumbnail with zero context, and there's not a whisper of Neiman's InCircle or my status tier anywhere in this email. Compare that to Saks, which at least tucked SaksFirst points messaging into their new-arrival sends I've seen — Neiman's just assumes I'll click through blind.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of pure curiosity on the Givenchy bag. Would I forward it? No — there's no single hero moment worth sending a friend, it's a catalog page. I'd open it, scroll once, and archive.

## What's working

- The "Brand New Styles Just Landed" black banner is confident and on-brand without being the recycled "S" wallpaper I've now seen three times this month.
- The Givenchy leopard bag and Alaïa kitten-mule sandals are genuinely aspirational picks — this is the merchandising eye I subscribe to Neiman's for.
- Clean two-column grid, generous white space, legible brand names under every item — easy to scan on my phone without zooming.
- Smart split into Women's/Men's sections with a "Shop All" button after each — I know exactly where to tap if I want more.

## What's weak

- Not one price is shown anywhere. Even a "from $X" would give me a reason to click rather than just admire.
- Zero InCircle mention — no tier status, no points-earn callout, nothing that says "we know you're a member." Saks and Macy's at least gesture at loyalty tiers in their emails; this feels like it was sent to a stranger.
- No offer at all — fine for a pure lookbook, but after three straight promo emails from Neiman's this week, pivoting to zero discount signal with zero warning is a little jarring.
- Footer is a wall of legal/unsubscribe text with no personal-shopper or beauty-counter touch — feels colder than the merchandising above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add starting prices under at least the hero pieces (Givenchy bag, Alaïa mules) — that's the single biggest lever to turn browsing into clicking.
- 2. Work an InCircle line into the header or footer — even "Earn points on every new arrival" would make me feel recognized as a member, not a random inbox.
- 3. Give the Givenchy bag its own mini-hero spot above the grid — it's the strongest image in the email and it's buried as one of six equal-weight thumbnails.
- 4. Add a one-line personal-shopper CTA near "Match with a personal style advisor" — that's a genuinely on-brand feature Nordstrom leans on harder and Neiman's undersells here.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` and Apple-link color-reset CSS (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`) are present — correct mitigation for iOS auto-linking/dark-mode issues.

- Cannot confirm `alt` attribute coverage on images or full color-contrast compliance from the truncated source; a full-source pass is recommended before sign-off.
