# The Roundup: Sky-High Stilettos
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a nice change of pace — after three sends running with that big black "S" banner, Neiman's finally gave me something that actually looks like a Neiman's email: a gorgeous hero shot of a green slingback perched on a lacquered table, styled like an actual editorial. No mango banner, no "ends soon," no percentage-off math. Just shoes, and good ones — Jimmy Choo, Louboutin, Saint Laurent, Gucci, Tom Ford, all in a tidy grid with a designer name under each pair.
- That said, this is a pure content play with zero commerce urgency, and I have to be honest with myself about whether that gets me to click. There's no price anywhere, no "your size," no member perk — just "come look at pretty shoes." It's the kind of email I'd actually enjoy scrolling through on my phone at the nail salon, but it's not one that gets me off the couch. I'd open it, admire the Tom Ford lace pair, maybe screenshot it for my "want" folder — but I wouldn't forward it to a friend, and I wouldn't click through today. This is a browse-later email, not a buy-now email.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely beautiful — that green shoe on the pedestal table looks like a Vogue accessories page, not an email blast.
- "The Roundup: Sky-High Stilettos" is a clear, curated concept — I know exactly what I'm getting before I scroll.
- The designer roster (Jimmy Choo, Louboutin, Saint Laurent, Gucci, Tom Ford) is squarely my closet — this isn't a Gen Z sneaker drop, it's grown-up shoe money.
- Clean grid layout, generous white space, legible labels — easy to scan on a phone without zooming.
- Two clear CTAs up top ("Shop Stilettos" / "Shop Must-Have Shoes") so I'm not hunting for a button.

## What's weak

- Not a single price, sale flag, or member benefit anywhere — as an InCircle-adjacent shopper, I'd at least like a hint of what I'm getting into before I click.
- No sizing or availability signal, which matters a lot for a 4-inch heel — I'm not clicking blind on a stiletto that might not come in my size.
- Zero social proof — no "editor's pick," no reviews, nothing that tells me these are trending versus just six shoes someone pulled that morning.
- It's a bit thin on personalization — this could've landed in literally any Neiman's subscriber's inbox regardless of purchase history.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a light price range or "from $X" under at least the hero shoe — I don't need a sale, but I want a sense of the number before I tap through.
- 2. Swap one of the two generic CTA buttons ("Shop Must-Have Shoes" is redundant with "Shop Stilettos") for something like "Find Your Size" — that's the actual friction point on stiletto purchases.
- 3. Add a one-line "as seen on" or stylist note under the hero for social proof — even a small credibility nod would lift this past pure lookbook territory.
- 4. Compare to the 9/4 wedding-guest send, which also skipped pricing — two content-only sends in a row is fine once, but if this becomes the new pattern I'll start scrolling past them the way I do Sephora's repeat "50%" starburst.
