# 💘JUST YOUR TYPE 💘
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "Just Your Type" — and yet nothing in the top half of this email tells me what my type actually is. The hero banner admits it right out loud: "This is for YOU," which is email-speak for "we don't actually know who you are." I'm a Sephora Insider. I've spent real money here. And the brand's answer to that relationship is a heart emoji and a generic SHOP NOW button.
- To Sephora's credit, the Jo Malone London feature stopped my scroll. Sea Salt & Bergamot at $175 — that's my lane, that's a Saks-tier brand, and the price and size are right there on the page. But then I keep scrolling and I land on ONE/SIZE by Patrick Starr, and we've lost each other. That's not demographic slippage, that's a completely different customer. And the leave-in conditioners section? I'm not at CVS. I'm a woman who buys Cologne de $175.
- I will not forward this. I might click on the Jo Malone unit because it's a brand I actively track, but I'd feel annoyed that I had to excavate it from a catalog that reads like Sephora.com's homepage. Mark as opened, click one link, move on.

## What's working

- **Jo Malone London featured front and center** — prestige, adult, recognizable fragrance brand with a price point that signals Sephora isn't afraid to go there. This is me.
- **Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off** — the banner offer is clear and I can read the mechanic instantly. No math required.
- **Loyalty touchpoint is visible** — the GET 25 POINTS section reminds me there's a reason to click over a competitor. I do not spend money without points back, so this matters.
- **Product detail on the hero SKU** — "Sea Salt & Bergamot Cologne 3.4 oz. $175" with a star rating is a real information unit. Macy's would have hidden the price until you land on the PDP.
- **No render bugs** — the email loads clean. After the Neiman Marcus double-send debacle earlier this month, I notice when things just work.

## What's weak

- **"This is for YOU" is the laziest hero copy in email marketing** — it says personalization without doing personalization. Neiman Marcus at least names a category: "trending designer styles." Sephora gives me a pronoun.
- **ONE/SIZE by Patrick Starr is not for me** — I know who Patrick Starr is. I am not his customer. This section signals that the "just your type" targeting is either off or nonexistent.
- **Leave-in conditioners** — a functional haircare category wedged into what is supposed to be a prestige beauty send. The product mix is fighting itself.
- **SHOP NOW appears four or five times** — every section has the same CTA. Neiman Marcus would have at least made it "Shop Fragrances" or "Explore Jo Malone." Generic repetition flattens the hierarchy.
- **Ten emails in fifteen days** — I've been counting since July 1. The cadence has burned through whatever urgency Sephora might manufacture. "Just Your Type" lands tired.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the send to one hero story** — pick Jo Malone or pick the minis offer, then build the entire email around it. The current email is the website, not a message. Less is the only way to earn a click from someone drowning in their Sephora inbox.
- 2. **Make the hero copy earn its personalization claim** — if Sephora knows I've purchased fragrance, say "You have a thing for fragrance. We just got this." If they don't have the data, at least be category-specific: "The fragrance edit. Curated."
- 3. **Age up the product mix for this segment** — if Patrick Starr is in the email, he shouldn't be above the fold for a customer whose spend history is Jo Malone and La Mer.
- 4. **Give SHOP NOW a noun** — "Shop Jo Malone," "Shop Fragrances," anything with a referent. "SHOP NOW" four times in one email is a design failure, not a CTA strategy.
- 5. **Rest the inbox for 72 hours after this send** — a beauty-counter regular will churn before she subscribes to a better list.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your fragrance edit is waiting — plus 50% off minis when you pair them.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`robots` meta value has a space:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Correct value is `"noindex"` (no space). Robots don't crawl email, so this has no practical effect, but it's a malformed token.

**`<title>` is generic:**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Some clients (notably desktop Outlook, older Thunderbird) display the `<title>` as the window/tab label. Using the subject line or a descriptive variant is preferred.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or appropriate locale). Screen readers default to OS language when absent, which can produce incorrect pronunciation for non-English subscribers.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user zoom — a known accessibility barrier. Low client-side enforcement risk in email, but flagged per WCAG 1.4.4.
