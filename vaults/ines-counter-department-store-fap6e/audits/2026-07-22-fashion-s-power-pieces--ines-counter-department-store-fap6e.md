# Fashion’s power pieces
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-seven emails from Neiman Marcus this July, and after three straight days of the same 25% off code cycling through my inbox — July 20's "Just for you" version was the one I actually paused for, I meant that 9 — they've gone cold-turkey editorial. No offer. No code. No event. Just a model on stone stairs in a plaid jacket and a leather bag with copy about "dividends." I respect the brand confidence. The investment language is genuinely smart. But this pivot feels whiplash-fast when I was still holding that 25% off code in my wallet metaphorically.
- The handbag section stopped me. "Heritage bags pay stylish dividends for seasons to come" — that's how I actually justify a structured bag to myself when I'm standing at the counter. That copy earned a second look. The fashion hero did not: mini skirt, editorial stairs, young model. That's not me, and NM knows it should know better by now.
- Would I click? Probably the handbag link, if the bag name and price are on the other end. Would I forward to a friend? No — there's nothing actionable, nothing to share. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- "Gain Interest" is genuinely clever — the investment double-meaning is the kind of wit that makes a Gen X woman feel seen, not sold to.
- "Heritage bags pay stylish dividends for seasons to come" is quiet, confident copy that respects my intelligence — Saks occasionally pulls this off; NM nailed it here.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — none of the forty-items-in-a-grid overload that makes a Macy's email feel like a yard sale flyer. My eye went hero → headline → bag without friction.
- The brand mark is immediately recognizable; I didn't need a sender line to know who this was.
- Two distinct sections (fashion hero, then bag) give me a reason to scroll — more structure than a lot of aspirational sends bother with.

## What's weak

- Zero offer after three consecutive discount emails — the whiplash from "your 25% off code expires tonight" to "here is a mood board" in 48 hours is real.
- "Shop Designer Collections" is a placeholder CTA that could have come from any luxury brand on any day. It has no personality and no pull.
- The fashion hero model skews young — mini skirt, editorial posing. The bag spoke to me; the styling did not. Sephora's recent hair care email at least cast women who looked like they age into their products.
- No loyalty signal anywhere. I'm not opening my wallet — physical or digital — without points back, and this email acts like that relationship doesn't exist.
- Twenty-seventh email in July. The cadence is eroding my willingness to open even the good ones.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one InCircle member signal.** Even a single line — "Member early access to fall's power pieces" — transforms this from a broadcast into a relationship. That one line would do more for click rate than any CTA rewrite.
- 2. **Name the bag and link directly to its PDP.** "Heritage Hobo in Saddle, $XXX" — one line beneath the headline — turns aspiration into commerce. Right now "Shop Handbags" sends me to a category, not a product.
- 3. **Replace the CTA.** "Shop the Power Edit" mirrors the subject, gives the button personality, and still functions as a category link.
- 4. **Commit to editorial OR add one offer; never pivot between them in the same 48-hour window.** The whiplash is a brand-voice problem, not a creative problem.
- 5. **Dial back the cadence.** Fifteen great emails in July beats twenty-seven forgettable ones. This email would have landed harder on day ten than day twenty-seven.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Fashion's power pieces"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with a multi-scenario variable block that requires manual operator selection before send; at least one key variable (`@user`) appears to have shipped with its assignment commented out. Compliance headers are absent or unverifiable through the relay, creating CAN-SPAM and RFC 8058 exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**30 tracking links not probed.** All click-redirect links went through a tracking domain and were skipped by the HTTP probe — destination URL integrity and 404 exposure are unverified.

**Plain-text body is 84% URLs (2619 / 3120 chars).** This far exceeds the 70% threshold that commonly triggers spam filters. The plain-text alternative is likely auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking parameters, producing lines of bare redirect URLs with no surrounding prose.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three viewport meta tags, two commented out.** The active tag is correct (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`), but the two stale alternatives (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29` and `width=1024`) are noise that increases maintenance risk and suggests template hygiene issues.

**Two separate `<style>` blocks with different breakpoints** (`max-width: 600px` in the first block, `max-device-width: 480px / 520px` in the second). Gmail clips emails over ~102KB; duplicate style blocks consume byte budget unnecessarily and the breakpoint split (600px vs 480px) risks mid-range viewport layouts being unaddressed.

**CSS-only hamburger nav (checkbox hack).** The `:checked` pseudo-selector navigation pattern does not work in Outlook (all versions) or Yahoo Mail. Mobile nav is silently absent on those clients.

**`<title>` has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but may appear in client tab/preview text with the trailing whitespace visible.

**XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE.** The declared doctype (`-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN`) is obsolete; modern clients ignore it, but it forces quirks-mode parsing in some legacy clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable assignment is commented out and unset.** The template header contains:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

In SFMC, AMPscript inside HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) is still executed by the personalization engine — but the inner `/* ... */` is an AMPscript block comment, so the `SET` statement is suppressed. The preamble says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," indicating this is a dual-path template where the operator must uncomment one assignment block. If sent as-is with no scenario activated, `@user` is undefined. If `@user` is used in link parameters (common for hashed-email tracking), all 30 tracking links would carry an empty `user=` parameter.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` depend on `NM_CRM_Feed` lookup.** Both are populated via `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)`. Subscribers absent from that data extension will receive empty values, silently dropping CRM identifiers from tracking and suppression logic with no fallback defined in the visible template.

**`@refid` is a no-op concat:** `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` appends an empty string — this is either a placeholder for a suffix that was never filled in, or dead code.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (QA: WARN).** Not captured through the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5K/day) since February 2024. Its absence risks inbox placement demotion or bulk-folder routing.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (QA: WARN).** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not present. Gmail enforces this for bulk senders; absence means Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button either falls back to the `mailto:` method or is suppressed entirely.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured (QA: WARN).** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unknown — the relay did not surface these headers. Cannot confirm the sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is aligned with DMARC policy. This is a relay visibility gap, not a confirmed auth failure, but it must be verified against raw headers at the MTA.

**Sender domain:** `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Standard for SFMC; alignment is expected but unverifiable without authentication headers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameter completeness unverifiable.** All 30 destination URLs are behind tracking redirects that were not probed. Cannot confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` are present on landing pages.

**`@refid` / `@base_refid` are campaign calendar IDs** intended to be appended to URLs for attribution. If the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" block was not activated (see §4), these values may be populated from the sendable DE but `@user` could still be empty, producing partially-formed tracking strings like `...&user=&refid=NM20260722`.
