# Did you know that points = rewards?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Well, right there under the header it tells me "You have 0 points" — on an email literally selling me the fantasy of "Rewards Bazaar" and a Dr. Dennis Gross LED mask for 15,000 points. That's the kind of tone-deaf moment Saks pulls when it sends me a designer-access invite for a line I've never bought. It's not malicious, it's just careless — nobody at Sephora ran this build through a zero-balance account before it went out.
- That said, once I get past that stumble, this is a genuinely well-organized rewards catalog. Ten products laid out cleanly, points cost and scarcity ("7 AVAILABLE NOW") next to each one — that's a real merchandising email, not a "come back and shop" nag. I don't have points to spend today, so I'm not clicking, but I can see myself bookmarking this mentally for next time I've banked points from an in-store Beauty Insider event. I wouldn't forward it to a friend — nothing here is urgent or personal enough — but I'd mark it read and move on, not delete it in irritation.

## What's working

- The "750+ Point Rewards" grid is genuinely useful — I can see exactly what I'd redeem and at what tier, no guessing.
- Scarcity counters ("16 AVAILABLE NOW," "3 AVAILABLE NOW") create real urgency without shouting — a step up from the ALL-CAPS chaos of Sephora's own September 12th "50% off select faves 🌶️" send.
- The Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider / VIB / Rouge) at the footer respects that I'm a member and shows me where I sit.
- Clean single-column product cards with points cost bolded — no clutter, unlike the eleven-offer pileup I called out in that same September 12th email.

## What's weak

- "You have 0 points" sitting right at the top completely undercuts the whole premise of the email — I'm being sold rewards I can't afford to redeem.
- The hero banner text is white-on-mauve and a little low-contrast against the product photo background — readable, but not effortless on a phone in sunlight.
- Nothing here is remotely beauty-counter-luxe for someone my age — a Dr. Dennis Gross LED mask and Milk Makeup sticks vault skew younger Gen Z/Millennial than 47-year-old me.
- No personalization beyond the points balance — no "here's what's close to your tier" nudge, which Nordstrom or Saks would usually layer in for a loyalty member.

## Recommendations

- 1. Suppress or reframe this send for zero-balance members — either don't show the points count at all, or swap the CTA to "Shop to start earning" so it's not self-defeating.
- 2. Add a "you're X points away from your first reward" progress bar — turns the zero-balance problem into a motivator instead of a dead end.
- 3. Curate at least 2-3 rewards toward the top that skew a bit more classic/prestige (skincare serums, fragrance minis) rather than leading with Fugazzi and Milk Makeup, which read young.
- 4. Bump contrast on the hero headline text against the mauve photo background for mobile readability.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You have 0 points — here's how fast that changes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `September rewards are live: see what your points can get`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `750+ point rewards, from $150-value bags to LED skincare — see what's available now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New this month: Rouge and VIB rewards, limited quantities`
