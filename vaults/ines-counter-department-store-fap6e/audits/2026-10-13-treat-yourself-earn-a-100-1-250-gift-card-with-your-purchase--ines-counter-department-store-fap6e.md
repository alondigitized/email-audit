# Treat yourself & earn a $100–$1,250 gift card with your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- Neiman's again, and this is the same spend-and-save ladder in a gift-card costume. After the "$200 off" run since 10/6 and this morning's "Save today: $50–$200 off your purchase," they've swapped the discount for a gift card. It's the same ask: spend more, get a little back. The plum hero is calm. The spend table sits right under the button, and I could read it in two seconds.
- Then the math gets less friendly. $500 earns $100, $1,500 earns $375, and $5,000+ earns $1,250. That's 20–25% back, but as a card I can only spend later. The fine print says sale merchandise doesn't count and returns can forfeit the award. I won't act on this until I've read all of that. I'd probably open it and mark it read.
- I'd forward this only to a friend who is already planning a big coat purchase. I wouldn't click today. It's decent and respectful, but it's the fifth version of one idea this week.

## What's working

- The hero reads "Earn a $100–$1,250 gift card" with the spend and earn grid directly below. There's no hunting for the offer. Sephora's "The Chat" gave me no offer or price at all.
- It says "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" in the hero. That's the line a counter shopper like me needs, and it's up front.
- The tone is quiet: no shouting, no exclamation points, just a serif logo and a plum block. That's the Neiman's I like, closer to the plain black hero on 10/12's "Fall's bold new arrivals" than to Macy's "LAST CHANCE" urgency.
- The deadline is stated clearly in the fine print: code OURGIFT by 10/20/26 at 10 PM CT.
- The designer grid is on point for me: Moncler, Burberry, Manolo, Louboutin, Valentino. It fits the designer-access tone.

## What's weak

- The grid has no prices, no "new" tags and no beauty. The hero says Beauty & Fragrance, then the nine products are all clothes, shoes and bags. Nothing shows me what a gift card would actually buy.
- The offer reads as "spend $500 to get $100," but I can't tell that from the subject. The only deadline in the body is buried in the legal block, and the hero says only "Limited Time."
- "Exclusions Apply" is tiny, and the long fine print runs well past the footer. I only saw that sale items don't count, returns can forfeit the card, and it arrives within 8 weeks after I read all of it. Kohl's gives me Kohl's Cash with its dates on the first screen.
- There's no loyalty or tier mention, and nothing says "I know you." I'm a points-back customer, so a gift card that arrives weeks later with strings attached is weak next to a points bonus.
- The six purple category buttons are generic and the same as every other send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero, not just "Limited Time." Say "Ends 10/20" in the hero.
- 2. Show at least one beauty product, since you claim beauty counts. Tell me which beauty brands qualify and whether a $500 beauty basket earns the $100.
- 3. Tell me the card arrives in 8 weeks and can be forfeited on returns near the grid or CTA, not 40 lines down. Shoppers who return at the event will notice.
- 4. Add prices or "Just in" tags to the grid, and match some picks to the spend tiers, like "Under $500."
- 5. Recognize members, for example "As a member, you earn points on top." Skip the sixth identical send this week.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Neiman Marcus gift card: up to $1,250, beauty included`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Beauty & Fragrance count. Use code OURGIFT by 10/20 at 10 PM CT.`

- **Link inspection not possible:** the truncated source doesn't show the link `href` values. I can't confirm whether links are consistently wrapped or whether any are unwrapped or malformed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]`, `*[class=mobile]`, and similar. They match only when `class` is exactly that string. Any element with a second class silently loses its responsive rule.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** the rules `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger` depend on CSS `:checked` support. Gmail (including GANGA accounts) and most Outlook builds don't support it. The `*[class].menu` rule sets `display:none; visibility:hidden`, so the nav may be unreachable in those clients. A fallback can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Dead `bugfix` animation:** the `body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s }` rule is a hack for old iOS 9.
- **Leftover commented-out viewport tags:** three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in the source, including `width=1024`. This adds clutter but has no runtime effect.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Unverified:** image alt text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and the preheader weren't visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment leaked into delivered HTML:** the first comment contains ESP build instructions. They include internal data extension names and field names: `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`, `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)`, and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", ...)`. It also includes `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This is a production template-hygiene issue and a minor information disclosure. It also shows the `@user` and `@uEm` variables are tied to an email identifier or hash.
- **Unrendered tokens:** none are visible in the supplied portion. The rest of the body wasn't checked.
