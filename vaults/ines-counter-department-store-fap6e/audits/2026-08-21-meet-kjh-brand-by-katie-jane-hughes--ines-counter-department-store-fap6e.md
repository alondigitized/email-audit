# Meet KJH.brand by Katie Jane Hughes 🤩
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- A new brand launch — KJH.brand from celebrity makeup artist Katie Jane Hughes — and my first reaction is "who?" I know Sephora likes to plant celebrity-adjacent brands, but unless you're deep in the beauty-influencer world, "KJHbrand" means nothing on sight, and the email never really explains why I should care beyond "from celebrity makeup artist." No age or lifestyle cue in the models tells me this is for me versus a 24-year-old TikTok shopper.
- What I do appreciate: it's a clean, single-focus launch email — no clutter, no five competing categories stacked on top of each other. It scrolls forever with product-after-product ("That Flush of Color," "The Precision MVP," "Lotta Lip," etc.) which feels like a lookbook, not a targeted pitch. Sephora's own 8/17 "App Fest" send was busier but at least had a real, quantifiable offer up top (10% off + free gift). This one buries "Play Makes Perfect / Shop Now" as the actual CTA, and everything below is just product cards with no discount attached — brand launches rarely get promo pricing, and I know that, but it still means nothing here pulls me to buy today.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only if a friend is a makeup artist herself who'd get a kick out of a "new brand alert." Otherwise this reads as a page I skim once and mark read — not a "Beauty Insider treats me like a VIP" moment like a well-built Nordstrom Beauty Trend Event email would deliver.

## What's working

- The hero shot is clean and the "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" line at least signals exclusivity, which flatters my "I like being first" instinct as a counter regular.
- One consistent visual language (color-blocked backgrounds per product) makes this feel like a cohesive lookbook, not a jumbled sale flyer — a step up from Sephora's own 8/16 "They're Always SO Good" clutter.
- The top banner offering a free mini with $35+ purchase is a real, concrete incentive, even if it's generic and not KJH-specific.
- Same-day delivery mention is a nice practical trust cue for someone who shops on impulse.

## What's weak

- Zero personalization or Beauty Insider point balance anywhere in this send — which, after three straight "0 points" call-outs this month (8/16, 8/19, 8/20), is honestly a relief, but it also means no loyalty hook to pull me in at all.
- No price points on the hero items until you scroll deep — "$24" doesn't show up until "That Flush of Color," several screens down. Neiman Marcus's gift-card-tier email at least puts the math right up front.
- This is essentially an unbroken wall of "Shop Now" buttons with no urgency, no bundle, no reason to act today versus next month.
- No demographic signal that tells me — a 47-year-old counter shopper — this brand suits my skin type, tone range, or age group; it reads like it's aimed at a much younger, Sephora-app-native shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with why KJH matters to ME — her signature technique, who she's worked with, a "as seen on" credibility line — not just "celebrity makeup artist," which is vague enough to be meaningless.
- 2. Cut the endless product scroll down to 3 hero items max above the fold, with prices visible immediately.
- 3. Add a Beauty Insider angle — even "earn 2x points on KJHbrand this week" would give a reason to buy now instead of "just browsing."
- 4. Show diverse-age model shots, or at least one, so shoppers my age don't self-select out immediately.
