# 20% off + Kohl's Cash , right this way ➡️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Fifty-three. That's my approximate count of Kohl's emails since Memorial Day, and this one lands as the second in my inbox today — same Sunday, same 20% off, same Kohl's Cash pitch they have been running at me since at least July 10th. I opened it anyway, because muscle memory and because the Kohl's Cash mechanic actually does move me when I'm close to a redemption threshold. But let me be clear: the moment I saw the hero, "Vacation feel. Staycation prices." I felt the slight wince of a brand talking to itself instead of to me. That's a great tagline for a Target audience. I am not that audience. I shop Nordstrom for a reason.
- What keeps this from being a delete is the sandals block mid-scroll. Forty percent off plus an additional coupon on what look like Birkenstock-adjacent slides is a legitimately compelling offer for a July afternoon. That's something I would click. The clearance block at 70% also earns a second look — that's real savings language, not manufactured urgency.
- I'll mark it read, not delete it. I might circle back for the sandals. I would not forward it — not even to my sister who's a Kohl's loyalist, because she's already getting this exact email herself.

## What's working

- **The sandals offer is specific and stacked.** Two separate sandal blocks — 40% off plus coupon — feel like a genuine deal, not a soft discount disguised as an event.
- **Kohl's Cash integration is front-and-center.** The yellow "Plus, earn Kohl's Cash" strip under the offer banner is exactly what I need to see when I'm calculating whether it's worth a purchase today versus waiting.
- **"Outfits you'll wear on repeat (and for good reason)"** — this headline has some actual personality. It's the only line in the email that doesn't sound like a promotional template.
- **Clearance at 70% with a distinct red block** is hard to miss, and the color contrast works. It signals "final" in a way that feels credible.
- **Rewards member status visible at top** — I can see my standing without digging. That's the right call.

## What's weak

- **"Vacation feel. Staycation prices." is generic lifestyle copy**, not a message to a known customer. Sephora's recent "Obsessed? Same." was lazy too, but at least it had a voice. This hero belongs on a billboard, not in my inbox.
- **The 20% off mechanic is fully exhausted.** This is the fourth near-identical send in four days. The offer has lost its urgency because Kohl's has trained me to expect it to still be there tomorrow.
- **No beauty or Sephora-at-Kohl's mention anywhere.** I have purchased at the Sephora inside my Kohl's. That is a known product category in their own store. Zero reference to it here while Sephora itself is running cleaner, more targeted sends.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are everywhere and mean nothing.** Neiman Marcus at least tells me I'm being pointed at Saint Laurent. These buttons could go anywhere on the Kohl's site.
- **The "0 REWARDS" in the header actively undermines the loyalty pitch.** Showing me I've earned nothing while asking me to care about Kohl's Cash is the wrong data at the wrong moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the 20% off as the hero for at least one send.** Lead with the Kohl's Cash earning moment instead — "Spend $50, earn $10 back. Starts now." makes the transaction feel rewarding, not discounted.
- 2. **Add a Sephora-at-Kohl's module.** Even one product tile — a summer SPF, a bestselling fragrance, a Drunk Elephant travel kit — would make this feel like it was sent to me, not to a household.
- 3. **Kill two of the four "Shop Now" buttons.** Pick one anchor CTA per section: "Shop Sandals" and "Browse Clearance" are specific enough to click. Generic ones are noise.
- 4. **Surface the clearance 70% block higher.** It's the most compelling number in the email and it's buried below the fold.
- 5. **Swap the hero lifestyle photo** for something that doesn't read as a swimwear catalog — a patio setup, a summer kitchen refresh, or a pair of feet in those sandals would feel more earned for a 47-year-old who knows what she looks like on a beach.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The sandals deal + Kohl's Cash. Both. Today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your savings: 40% off sandals + Cash back on every $50.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Kohl's Cash window is open. Here's what to spend it on.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "20% off + Kohl's Cash" (2026-07-13)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional table layout with multiple tracking pixels across three separate vendor domains (CoherentPath, MessageIntelligence, Adobe Audience Manager). Compliance headers were not captured by the AgentMail relay, leaving CAN-SPAM and RFC 8058 conformance unverifiable from the intercepted message.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **22 click-redirect links skipped** — all links route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com`. Live reachability of destination URLs was not verified; broken landing pages are possible but unconfirmed.
- **Three distinct pixel/tracking domains in use:**
  - `click.chp.kohls.com` — CoherentPath open pixel (1×1, no `alt`, no `aria-hidden`)
  - `mi.kohls.com` — MessageIntelligence open pixel (`o.gif`, no `alt`, has `aria-hidden="true"` on one instance but missing on four content impression pixels)
  - `kohls.demdex.net` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel (0×0, no `alt`)
- **Open pixel uses `<custom>` tag:** `</custom>` appears after the `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx` pixel — this is an unclosed/invalid tag that will generate parse errors in strict renderers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **`<title>` is empty** — screen readers and some preview pane implementations rely on `<title>`; leaving it blank degrades accessibility and may affect spam scoring.
- **`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile; WCAG 1.4.4 violation.
- **11 images missing `alt` text** — all tracking and content impression pixels lack `alt=""`. The five content impression PNGs (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, and four others from `mi.kohls.com`) have no `alt` attribute at all. Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"`; content images need descriptive alt text.
- **Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` + duplicate `@import`** — both Roboto and Montserrat are fetched twice (once via `<link>`, once via `@import` inside a `<style>` block). Doubles the font-load DNS/fetch cost for clients that render web content (Gmail web, Apple Mail with remote content enabled). Both blocks are also wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, so Outlook strips them correctly — but the duplication is wasteful.
- **HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype** — modern email clients tolerate this, but it prevents valid use of HTML5 semantic elements and generates validator warnings.
- **`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** — correct practice for email HTML; no issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated source. The `mi_u=604230016` parameter in MessageIntelligence pixel URLs indicates subscriber-level personalization is active. No issues found in the visible source.

---

### 5. Compliance

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA relay did not observe this header. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe button (required for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 enforcement). Kohl's sending volume makes this a deliverability risk, not just a compliance note.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured** — one-click unsubscribe (POST method) is required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above 5k/day. Absence here is a flag; confirm at MTA level before treating as confirmed missing.
- **No physical mailing address detected in truncated source** — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in the message body. The source is truncated, so this may appear further down, but the QA scan did not find it. Must be verified against full HTML.
- **`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`** — suppresses referrer on outbound clicks, which will strip source attribution on any destination pages not using UTM params. Intentional, but see Section 6.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **All destination URLs are behind click-redirect wrappers** (`click.s.kohls.com`) — UTM parameter presence on final landing URLs cannot be confirmed without following the redirects (skipped by QA). If UTMs are not appended by the redirect handler, GA4/Adobe Analytics will attribute sessions as direct traffic rather than email.
- **`mi_ecmp=1017411_2026713`** present on MessageIntelligence impression pixels — confirms campaign-level tagging is in use at the pixel layer, but this does not substitute for UTM params on click destinations.
- **`no-referrer` policy** (noted above) means any destination page relying on `document.referrer` for attribution will receive an empty string. Click-tracking redirects should be the sole attribution mechanism; verify the redirect handler appends UTMs before forwarding.
