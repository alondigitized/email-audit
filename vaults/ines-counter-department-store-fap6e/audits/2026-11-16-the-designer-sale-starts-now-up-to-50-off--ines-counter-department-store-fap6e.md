# The Designer Sale starts now: up to 50% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- Fourth Designer Sale email in three days, and this one finally says what the sale is. Last week it was "$650 off" with a code, then "40% off" with another code. Now it's "up to 50% off" and a bare "Shop Now." The red block is loud, but it's the cleanest version of the pitch so far. I get it in about two seconds.
- The product grid is the first thing that has actually made me want to look. Dolce&Gabbana's cream bag, the Stuart Weitzman leopard slingback and the Casablanca bomber are real pieces I'd consider. The sale is also open to everyone with no code, which I appreciate. Still, I've now seen this sale four ways in 72 hours. The cadence is tiring, and the gorgeous quiet sends, like Alice + Olivia yesterday, are what keep me subscribed.
- Would I click? Probably, on the leopard slingback. Would I forward it? No. Do I see any points or Neiman's InCircle benefit? Not one word, and that's where I decide whether I buy here or somewhere else.

## What's working

- "Up to 50% off" is one clear offer in huge type. Compared with the three earlier sends with different codes, this is far easier to act on.
- No code to copy and paste. Kohl's still makes me stack things, and this doesn't.
- The nine-brand grid shows real designers (Dolce&Gabbana, Stuart Weitzman, Balmain). It looks like Neiman's and not like a clearance rack.
- The sale is clearly labeled and the shop-by-category buttons are easy to tap.
- The "Prep your holiday wardrobe" line is calm and fits the season.

## What's weak

- Nothing about InCircle or points. As someone who won't use a card without points back, I want to know if the sale earns them. Macy's at least puts the member angle up front, even when it overdoes the urgency.
- "Exclusions Apply" is tiny and dark red on red, and I can barely read it. The footnote says "select categories and merchandise," which is the part I'd want to know first.
- "Up to 50%" is vague. The grid shows no prices, no "was/now," and no discount per item, so I can't tell whether the Dolce bag is 20% or 50% off.
- The six category buttons are red with dark red text, so the contrast is poor. I'd have trouble reading them on my phone without squinting.
- Fourth sale email in three days. Sephora's ninth email this week made me tired too, and Neiman's is heading the same way.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show actual sale prices or "% off" tags on each product. Even three of them would help.
- 2. Add an InCircle line under the hero, such as "Members earn points on sale items." That's the one thing that would make me buy here and not at Saks.
- 3. Fix the contrast on "Exclusions Apply" and the category buttons: white text, or black buttons like the hero's.
- 4. Stop sending the same sale in a new costume every day. Consolidate into one send plus one reminder, and keep the Balmain/Alice + Olivia-style editorial sends in between.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Long plain-text URLs:** `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 5488/6504 chars` (about 84%). This points to long tracking URLs in the plain-text alternative, which spam filters can penalize and which make that part hard to read.
- No broken or malformed `href` values are visible in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the subject.
- **Hamburger menu relies on `:checked`:** the rules `*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` depend on checkbox-hack support. Gmail and Outlook don't support it, and the fallback isn't visible in the truncated source, so this is unconfirmed.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` only match elements whose `class` is exactly that value. They fail if any element carries multiple classes. I can't confirm that from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment shipped in the HTML:** the pre-DOCTYPE block `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->` was delivered to recipients.
  - It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It shows that a SHA-256 hashed email and CRM IDs are used for identity.
  - It is a QA miss: an unstripped developer note.
  - Content before `<!DOCTYPE>` can also trigger quirks-mode handling in some clients.
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible rendered portion. The `@` variables occur only inside the comments.

## 5. Compliance
