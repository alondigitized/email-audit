## Executive summary

- But I buy at the brand event and I want points back. A gift card that arrives within 8 weeks, only on regular-price merchandise, isn't a reward. It's a coupon for a future purchase. Sephora Insider at least ties its offers to Beauty Insider points I can see, and Kohl's opened with "Earn 5% Rewards" and my member status. ## What's working

- The hero is clear: "Earn a $50–$1,250 gift card" in big white type on plum, with one white "Shop & Earn" button.
- The spend/earn table is easy to read at a glance. $200 earns $50 and $500 earns $125.
- "Includes beauty & fragrance" is a small signal that gets my attention. Macy's would have buried it.
- The 3x3 product grid is quiet and well photographed. Maison Francis Kurkdjian, Burberry and Alice + Olivia feel like designer access, and Alice + Olivia connects back to the 11/15 holiday send.
- The six category buttons at the bottom are tidy and tappable.

## What's weak

- The table stops at $2,000 then jumps to $5,000+. The fine print adds a $3,000 tier for $750 that the hero never shows. That mismatch bothers me.
- The deadline (11/22, 10 PM CT) appears only in the fine print. A credible urgency cue should sit next to the button.
- The offer isn't honest at a glance. "Exclusions apply" is tiny, and the long text below excludes jewelry, sale items, shipping and tax, and some designers. It also excludes returns, which the card value is reduced for.
- The product grid doesn't tell me which items qualify. Because "select designers excluded," I can't tell if the Burberry bag counts.
- The "Claim your 15% off" text-signup block pulls attention away from the main offer.
- This is the fifth Neiman's promo email in about three days, and nothing says I'm a known customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline and the real tier ladder, including $3,000, in the hero.
- 2. Tell me who qualifies. Add a line like "Includes Chanel, Tom Ford and La Mer beauty" or show an excluded-brands link.
- 3. Add member recognition. For example: "Your InCircle points still apply."
- 4. Make the product grid shoppable by tier ("Under $500 gifts," "Beauty to hit $200").
- 5. Move the text-signup offer below the fold or drop it. It competes with the hero.
- 6. Give the cadence some space after the Designer Sale blast.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Buy More, Earn More: Get a $50–$1,250 gift card with your purchase"

The HTML was truncated before the body, so only the `<head>`, the CSS and a leading comment could be inspected. Findings about body markup, footer and links are limited to what the QA data reports.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template built in Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC). The only confirmed code issues are internal SFMC build notes left in the shipped HTML and a viewport setting that blocks zoom. The QA warnings are mostly capture limitations rather than confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 30 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified.
- No broken links are confirmed. The QA run reports 0 issues.
- The truncated source shows no link markup, so `href` values, `alt` text and `target` attributes could not be checked.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-zoom on mobile clients that honor it, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the HTML comment block precedes `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>`. Content ahead of the DOCTYPE can trigger quirks mode in some legacy clients.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** the mobile CSS uses exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. These only match when the element's class attribute is exactly that single value. Any added second class silently breaks the responsive rule.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** the CSS (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`, `.mobileCont:checked~table`) relies on `:checked` selectors. These are unsupported in Gmail and several other clients. Whether a non-interactive fallback exists can't be verified from the truncated HTML.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and no descriptive content. This is minor.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal build documentation shipped in the HTML:** the source opens with a comment naming SFMC variables and data extensions: `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)` and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"/"cmdid", ...)`. It also contains `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->` and the instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW". The comment was not stripped before send. This exposes internal data-extension names and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`) and indicates the template was published without a build step.
- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) are confirmed in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Missing `List-Unsubscribe`:** QA reports it was not found. The warning itself says it "may not be captured by AgentMail relay", so this is unconfirmed.
- **Missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** not found, with the same relay caveat. One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules.
- **Missing `Authentication-Results`:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA note says this is expected via the relay.
- The footer, physical address and visible unsubscribe link fall outside the truncated source, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTM parameters and landing-page URLs could not be evaluated. The 30 links are tracking redirects that were skipped, and the destinations are not visible in the truncated source.
- The template references `@refid` and `@base_refid`, so campaign attribution is probably carried through the redirect rather than as visible UTMs. This is inference, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip HTML comments at publish time, and remove the leading SFMC variable and lookup block, which discloses internal DE names and fields.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Move the DOCTYPE to the first line of the document, ahead of any comments.
4. Change `*[class=x]` selectors to `[class~=x]`, or use single-purpose classes, so the responsive rules don't break when a second class is added.
5. Confirm that the hamburger/checkbox nav has a working fallback in Gmail, which doesn't support `:checked`.
6. Re-test compliance against the raw SFMC send, or the original message headers, rather than the AgentMail relay copy. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment on `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
7. Resolve a sample of the 30 tracking links with a redirect-following probe. This confirms the final URLs, UTM or refid carry-through, and landing-page status codes.
