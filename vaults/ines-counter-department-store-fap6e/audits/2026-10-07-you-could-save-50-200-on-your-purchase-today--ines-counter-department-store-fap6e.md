# You could save $50–$200 on your purchase today
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- Third Neiman's offer email in two days. Yesterday I got "Grab it now: $200 off your purchase," and this is the same $50–$200 spend ladder with the same lavender block and the same NMCIRCLE code. The ladder is clear, and the "Plus, InCircle Members earn Triple Points" banner gets my attention. But I've seen all of it before. After the quiet Prada and Ralph Lauren sends on 10/5 and 10/6, it feels like they went back to the promo drawer.
- The hero is clean. "Up to $200 off," the code, one black SHOP NOW button, and the tier table are all right there. The 3x points line is the part that matters to me, because I don't put a card down without points back. What I notice is that the points are buried in a thin black strip with "see terms" under it. Then there's a wall of fine print that ends 10/12, so I have five days of urgency and no new reason to act today.
- Would I click? Probably, if I already had a bag in mind. Would I forward it? No. It reads as a reminder, not a discovery.

## What's working

- The spend ladder ($200/$50 up to $1,000+/$200) is readable at a glance. Sephora and Macy's usually make me do math, and Neiman's doesn't.
- The code is printed right under the headline, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The triple-points strip speaks to my loyalty habits. It's the strongest benefit in the email.
- The designer grid (Frame, Rag & Bone, Strathberry, Tod's, Jimmy Choo) is on-brand and feels like real designer access, not a clearance dump.
- The tone is restrained. There's no shouting, no emoji, and no countdown clock like Kohl's Deal Days.

## What's weak

- It's the same offer I got yesterday ("Grab it now: $200 off your purchase"), and the 10/1 week had the same ladder too. This feels like a fourth or fifth send of one promo.
- The 3x points line sits in a thin dark bar with small "see terms" text. Points are the real hook for me and they're an afterthought.
- Nothing in the product grid has a price, a "new" tag or a link to a specific item. Eight brand names with no context isn't a reason to click.
- The six lavender category buttons are flat, low-contrast and generic. The grid doesn't tell me which categories qualify.
- The fine print is dense, and the exclusions (no beauty, no handbags for points, no sale items) are a lot to take in. For someone who shops beauty counters, "beauty" being excluded from the points is a letdown.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 3x InCircle points into the hero, equal to the dollar offer. For a loyalty buyer it's the bigger number.
- 2. Give me a reason this send is different from yesterday's, or hold it back. Vary the creative, or send once and use a "last 5 days" reminder only on the final day.
- 3. Add prices or "new this season" tags to the grid, and link each tile to its own product page.
- 4. State plainly in the body which categories are excluded (beauty, handbags for points), not only in the footer.
- 5. Use stronger CTAs, like "Shop women's boots" instead of "Shop women's shoes", and give the buttons more contrast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `3x InCircle points + up to $200 off, through 10/12`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $50–$200 savings ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code NMCIRCLE: spend $200, save $50. Triple points on top.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Frame, Tod's, Jimmy Choo and more. See what qualifies.`

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "You could save $50–$200 on your purchase today"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with 32 click-tracking links. The visible source has no unresolved merge tokens, but ESP template documentation was left in the delivered HTML. The HTML source is truncated, so the footer, links and body markup are unreviewed. The three QA warnings are all header/relay artifacts and don't confirm real defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** 32 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe (click-redirect domains), so none were checked for resolution, redirects or dead destinations.
- **Not assessable:** the truncated source doesn't show any `href` values, so I can't confirm broken, malformed or `http://` links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Viewport blocks zoom:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` sets `maximum-scale=1`, which prevents pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 resize text).
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`, so screen readers can't select the correct language.
- **Weak `<title>`:** it is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space and no descriptive content.
- **Content before the DOCTYPE:** the large comment block precedes `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Anything before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some clients.
- **Hamburger nav depends on the checkbox hack:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules sit inside the `@media (max-width: 600px)` block. Gmail and Outlook don't support the `:checked` hack, so the mobile menu may not work there. The truncated source doesn't show the fallback, so this is unconfirmed.
- **Minor CSS issues:**
  - `*[class].show` declares `width` twice, with `auto !important` followed by `278px`.
  - `*[class=...]` exact-match selectors fail on elements with more than one class.
  - `.ReadMsgBody` is a legacy rule.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal ESP template notes in the delivered HTML:** the opening comment lists SFMC/AMPscript-style variables (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`). It also names internal Data Extensions and fields (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), and mentions `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. Anyone viewing source can read these internal data-model names. The comment also has unbalanced parentheses in `@user Lookup(...))` and `@uEm = Lookup(...))`.
- **No unresolved tokens in the visible source:** the lookups appear only inside the comment, so they are not executing as personalization.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found (WARN):** the QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a verified defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found (WARN):** RFC 8058 one-click support can't be confirmed, and Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it. This has the same capture caveat.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because the relay doesn't expose it. The sender domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
- **Footer not reviewed:** the in-body unsubscribe link and postal address are not in the truncated source, so I can't confirm CAN-SPAM footer elements.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. No `href`, UTM or `refid` values appear in the truncated source, and the tracking links were not probed. The template does reference `@refid` and `@base_refid`, which suggests campaign IDs are appended, but that can't be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the template-variable comment block from the production send. Use ESP-side comments such as `%%[ /* */ ]%%` or `<!--/* */-->` so they aren't emitted. This removes the internal DE and field names from the delivered source.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and give `<title>` a descriptive value without the trailing space.
4. Move any comments below the DOCTYPE so it is the first node.
5. Re-check the three header warnings against raw `.eml` headers captured before the relay: `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Treat them as defects only if they are missing there.
6. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML so the footer, UTM parameters and `href` values can be checked. Also add a click-redirect resolution check for the 32 skipped tracking links.
7. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the mobile nav in clients that don't support `:checked`, such as Gmail and Outlook. The fallback could be static links or a hidden menu.
