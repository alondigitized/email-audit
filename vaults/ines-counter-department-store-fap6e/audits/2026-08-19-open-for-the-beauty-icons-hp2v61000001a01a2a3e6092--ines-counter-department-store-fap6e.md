# Open for the beauty icons
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — "0 points" is sitting right up top in that little insider bar, same as the 8/16 email ("They're always SO good"). Three sends now in two weeks with my balance parked at zero, and it's stopped reading like a glitch and started reading like Sephora doesn't actually know who's opening these. That said, once I get past the header, this one's actually kind of gorgeous — the "BEAUTY'S MOST WANTED" hero into that moody Chanel Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu shot is genuinely elegant, more editorial than the chaotic emoji-soup subject lines Sephora's been running lately ("VIBES, AURAS & ERAS," "beauty forecast 🌶️🌶️🌶️").
- The problem is everything after the fragrance hero collapses into the kitchen-sink catalog scroll — eight products in a grid with no throughline, then App Fest (again, this is the third time this week I'm seeing App Fest messaging), then in-store events, then a Touchland lineup. It's Macy's-level overload dressed in nicer photography. Neiman Marcus's Brunello Cucinelli triple-points email last week told me exactly what I'd earn and on what — this just shows me a product wall and hopes something sticks.
- Would I click? Maybe on the Chanel fragrance shot, because that visual actually stopped my thumb. Would I forward it? No — nothing here is personalized or urgent enough to share. This gets marked read, scrolled past the grid, and quietly filed under "Sephora emails all blur together now."

## What's working

- The Chanel Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu hero shot is the best visual Sephora's sent me in weeks — moody, aspirational, and it actually looks like a fragrance ad, not a coupon flyer.
- "Choose a Trial Size" with the NARS/Fenty lip options at the very top is a smart, low-friction hook — free samples with $30 purchase is an easy yes.
- Product grid callouts like "Only At Sephora" and star ratings on Touchland and Josie Maran give me something closer to real social proof than most of their sends.
- The Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider / VIB / Rouge) at the bottom at least reminds me where I stand in the program, even if the "0 points" up top undercuts it.

## What's weak

- "0 points" again — that's the third email in under two weeks flashing my empty balance at me, and it's starting to feel like Sephora just doesn't track it right for people like me.
- The product grid — eight unrelated items (hair serum, hand sanitizer, eye cream, shampoo) with zero narrative — is straight-up Macy's-style overload, not curated "icons."
- App Fest gets a banner here too, same beat as the 8/17 "App Fest starts today" send — I've now seen this pushed three separate times this week.
- Nothing here acknowledges me as a 47-year-old Gen X shopper the way the 8/15 "Gen X" trio email did — this reverts back to generic, ageless catalog mode.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the "0 points" display from the header entirely until that balance actually reflects reality — it's actively working against the Insider program's credibility at this point.
- 2. Cut the eight-item product grid down to 3-4 curated picks with a reason they're grouped together (e.g., "new arrivals in fragrance layering") instead of a random assortment.
- 3. Stop repeating App Fest messaging in every single send this week — one clear placement, not three.
- 4. Lean into the Chanel fragrance hero as the entire email's spine — build out ingredient/scent notes, pairing suggestions, maybe a Rouge-exclusive early access window, rather than burying it under six more sections.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The new Chanel fragrance everyone's asking about`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Discover the ambery scent, plus your trial-size pick at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New from Chanel — shop before it sells out in-store`
