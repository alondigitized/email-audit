# Witness the big day in something elegant
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails from Neiman Marcus since July 2nd, and this is the one that finally made me stop scrolling. After a week of that big typographic "S/ale" block — the same oversized decorative letter I flagged on Thursday's "Last chance for up to 40% off" send — they've completely reset the tone. No discount badge. No countdown. Just a coastal editorial hero, a woman in a white gown, and the line "Love is in the Air." I felt the gear shift immediately and it worked on me.
- But here's my honest read: this email is trying to do two different things and the split dilutes both. The women's occasion editorial is genuinely elegant — the nuptial noir styling with the black tiered dress, pearl necklace, and white accessories is exactly the kind of curated story I'd expect from Neiman Marcus rather than from Nordstrom's department-store grid. That copy — "Call it nuptial noir — an unexpected LBD accented by pristine white looks wedding dressing an edge" — is real editorial voice. Then, about two-thirds down, I hit a men's suit. Olive jacket, khaki trousers, pocket square. Fine product, wrong audience for this send.
- I would click on the women's "Shop the Look" block. I would not forward this — it's not punchy enough to share. But I'd save the nuptial noir look and browse. That's a better outcome than the last five Neiman Marcus sends got from me.

## What's working

- **"Love is in the Air" hero with a clean coastal image** — after seven days of typographic sale blocks, this photography-led open feels like the brand I actually came to. The visual register is right for someone who cross-shops Saks.
- **The nuptial noir editorial block** — "Shop the Look" with the black tiered gown, pearl drop earrings, structured white bag, and heeled mules is genuinely styled and specific. It's the kind of look-building that justifies being in Neiman Marcus rather than just going to Macy's.
- **Two clear, labeled CTAs in the hero** — "Shop Wedding Guest" and "Shop Gowns" split the intent appropriately. If I'm shopping for myself as a guest versus buying a gown, I know which button is mine.
- **"Connect with a personal style advisor" in the footer** — I notice this. I actually use this service. For occasion dressing, that's a meaningful signal that this brand treats me like a client, not just an email address. Sephora has nothing equivalent for this category.
- **No render bugs, clean layout** — after years of Macy's emails with stacked badges, misaligned text, and four competing CTAs, this reads like someone breathed.

## What's weak

- **The men's section breaks the editorial spell.** I'm in occasion mode for myself. The men's suit and sunglasses copy ("exceptionally dashing… pocket square and stylish sunnies") is a completely different register and it interrupts the story. Kohl's does this — cramming two audiences into one email — and it always reads as lazy segmentation.
- **No price signal of any kind.** When I'm shopping for wedding-guest dressing, the difference between a $450 dress and a $1,800 dress actually matters to me. I have no idea if this editorial is aspirational browse-only or whether there's an entry point I can afford right now.
- **"Something elegant" in the subject is doing too little work.** Nordstrom would have put a brand name or silhouette in the subject: "The gown you'll wear to every wedding this summer" lands harder than "something elegant."
- **The "Shop the Look" CTA on the women's editorial is generic.** After copy this specific — "nuptial noir," "pristine white accents" — the button should be "Shop Nuptial Noir" or at minimum "Shop This Look" to maintain the momentum.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the men's section entirely from this send** — or send it to a separate segment. This is a wedding-guest editorial targeted at women. The men's suit costs you editorial coherence for a population that could receive a dedicated send.
- 2. **Add a price anchor to the editorial block** — even "starting from $695" or "styles from $395–$2,800" tells me whether I'm browsing or buying today. Sephora does this even on its editorial sends; there's no reason Neiman Marcus can't hold this standard.
- 3. **Upgrade the editorial CTA copy** — from "Shop the Look" to "Shop Nuptial Noir" to carry the editorial naming through to the click.
- 4. **Consider one sentence of urgency** — "New arrivals land weekly" or "Limited sizes in the gown" would create a reason to act now without resorting to countdown clocks.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Witness the big day in something elegant"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript deployment with a hamburger-nav responsive layout. The primary technical risks are a commented-out SHA-256 variable assignment that may silently break audience matching, missing one-click unsubscribe headers flagged by the relay, and unverifiable authentication chain (SPF/DKIM) due to the AgentMail relay not surfacing `Authentication-Results`.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**33 tracking links unprobed** — All click-redirect URLs were skipped by the HTTP prober (expected for ESP redirect domains like `e.neimanmarcus.com`). Destination URLs and HTTP response codes cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Plain-text body is 87.5% URLs** — QA reports 3,632 of 4,150 plain-text characters are URLs. This ratio is a known spam-filter signal. The plain-text version appears to be auto-generated from link-heavy HTML without meaningful prose content. Threshold typically cited by major filters is >60–70%.

No broken or duplicate tracking parameters can be confirmed from the truncated source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport variants left in `<head>`** — Three alternate viewport tags remain in the HTML as comments. These are inert but indicate unresolved configuration decisions:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
The active tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) suppresses user zoom, which is an accessibility regression on mobile.

**iOS 9 webkit animation hack present** — The `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` hack for iOS 9 hamburger rendering is still in the stylesheet. iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2019; this adds dead code weight.

**`max-device-width` deprecated** — Media queries mix `max-device-width` (removed in CSS Media Queries Level 4) with `max-width`. Functionally equivalent today but `max-device-width` is not forward-safe.

**Alt text not verifiable** — HTML is truncated before image tags; alt attributes cannot be audited.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA-256 assignment is commented out** — The template header documents two scenarios for setting `@user`, then the SHA-256 path is commented out:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

If `@user` is referenced anywhere downstream in the template body (e.g., for audience pixel firing, personalization lookups, or UID pass-through in URLs), it will resolve to an empty string or throw an AMPscript runtime error silently. The "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment suggests this was a deliberate toggle, but the active selection is not visible in the truncated source — the SHA-256 branch is confirmed inactive.

**Variable declarations are in HTML comments, not AMPscript blocks** — The `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` declarations appear inside `<!-- -->` HTML comments at the top of the file. HTML comments are not executed by the SFMC AMPscript engine. If these are the only declarations (i.e., no `%%[ ]%%` block exists in the un-truncated body), none of these variables would be set and all personalization lookups would fail silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — QA warns the header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 requires this header for bulk commercial email. Its absence (or relay-level stripping) increases Gmail/Yahoo complaint rates and disables the native unsubscribe button in Gmail UI.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not present or not surfaced. As of February 2024, Google and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Missing this header is a deliverability compliance gap.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain) should have its own DKIM signature; whether alignment is achieved with the `From:` domain (`neimanmarcus.com`) is unverifiable from this capture.

No CAN-SPAM issues are inferrable from the truncated source (physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed but are standard for NM).
