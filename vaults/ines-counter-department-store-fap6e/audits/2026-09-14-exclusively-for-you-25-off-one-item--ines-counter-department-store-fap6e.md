# Exclusively for you: 25% off one item
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, a unique code, "one regular-price item," a mauve-y hero that isn't trying too hard — this actually looks like a Neiman's email doing its job. But then I hit the fine print and it's a wall: the exclusion list reads like a phone book of every designer I'd actually want to buy — Chanel, Saint Laurent, Loewe, Ralph Lauren Collection, Golden Goose, Moncler — gone. That's the same bait I've seen from this brand all week, just repackaged from gift-card math into percent-off math.
- The merchandise strip below is genuinely nice — Zimmermann sundress, Chloe boots, a Burberry bag — but none of it is "shoppable" against this offer since half those houses are excluded, so it's basically decorative. Compare this to Etro's Fall Collection send on 9/12, which I liked precisely because it didn't do any tiered-math gymnastics — it just showed me the coat. This one wants credit for simplicity up front and then buries the real terms below the fold.
- I'd open it, I might even click "Treat Yourself" out of habit, but I wouldn't forward it — there's no way to know if it applies to anything I'd actually want until I'm in checkout typing a promo code, and that's not a gift I hand a friend.

## What's working

- Clean single-offer hero — "25% OFF one regular-price item" is legible at a glance, no tiered boxes like Kohl's 20%/15%/30% ladder I keep seeing.
- The unique promo code sitting right in the hero is a nice personalization touch — feels like it's actually mine, not a blast code.
- Merchandise carousel has real product photography and recognizable names (Chloe, Burberry, Zimmermann) that match my taste, not stock-model overload like Sephora's daily deals email.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" is a nice loyalty-adjacent touch for someone who buys at brand events like I do.

## What's weak

- The exclusion list at the bottom torpedoes the offer — nearly every brand I'd spend real money on (Chanel, Saint Laurent, Ralph Lauren Collection, Loewe, Moncler, Golden Goose) is carved out, so "exclusively for you" feels exclusively for someone else's closet.
- "One item" restriction isn't mentioned until you're already emotionally invested in the pretty pink hero — that's a bait-and-switch beat I flagged in Macy's "confirm now" email too.
- No urgency date visible until the tiny fine print — "Ends 9/15/26 10:00 PM CT" should be up top if they want me to act today.
- Nine products shown with zero indication which are even eligible under this promo — I have to guess or go check exclusions myself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the exclusion scope in plain English right under the hero — "Excludes select designer collections" with one tap-through, not an 18-line legal paragraph at the bottom.
- 2. Move the expiration date into the hero band — "Ends tomorrow 10PM CT" creates real urgency instead of hiding it in fine print.
- 3. Tag which of the nine products actually qualify for the 25% off, or don't show excluded-brand product at all — Burberry, Chloe, Zimmermann all read like eligible "shop the offer" picks, but none of them are guaranteed to be.
- 4. Lean into the "one unique code" personalization more — mention it's tied to my account, since that's the strongest trust signal in the whole email and it's underplayed.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 25% off code is ready (one item, your pick)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ines, use this before it expires tomorrow`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One code, one splurge — ends tomorrow at 10PM CT`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A little something just for you, before it's gone`
