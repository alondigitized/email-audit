# “Yes”-worthy wedding style
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Seventeen. That's my running count of Neiman Marcus emails in thirteen days, and today drops two of them — the same July 14 date as yesterday's Last Call send, which already had me doing the math on whether to unsubscribe. This one breaks from the urgency parade entirely — no "extra 25% off," no countdown, no gift card, just pure editorial: a bride in blue standing in a garden and some honestly beautiful jewelry. I'll give them this much: after the July 13 back-to-back gift card push (the same "$50–$500 gift card" offer sent twice in 24 hours, with the second one showing up as "Don't miss out" — as if I missed it the first time because I was *busy*), I appreciate not being yelled at.
- But the relief fades fast. I'm not a bride. I'm not in a wedding. And the bottom third of this email is groom content — a man in a white suit doing his best peacock impression under the header "Gallant Glamour" — which is squarely not for me. Neiman Marcus has my purchase history, my InCircle tier, and apparently seventeen send-opportunities in two weeks to figure out I don't need menswear suggestions. The jewelry in the middle is the only section where I slow down.
- I would not click. I might tab back to the jewelry if I were in a gifting mindset, but even then — no offer, no InCircle hook, no reason to act today versus never. I would not forward this to a friend; it's too unfocused to share with purpose.

## What's working

- The hero is genuinely gorgeous — model in a deep blue gown against a lush garden backdrop, clean and aspirational without going cold or exclusionary.
- "Something Stunning, Something Blue" is tidy copywriting; it earns the wedding reference without over-explaining it, which respects me.
- The jewelry trio (bracelet, dark stone ring, diamond earrings) is the only section with real self-purchase energy — I don't need to be getting married to want those pieces.
- Clean render start to finish: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapse. After Macy's dressed up a confirmation email in urgency clothing last week, a technically clean NM send is genuinely a relief.
- "Shop Wedding" and "Shop Jewelry" are both honest verb + noun CTAs — no "Discover" or "Explore" fluff.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. The July 14 Last Call send gave me up to 70% off designer styles; this one gives me vibes. The tonal whiplash in a single day makes it hard to know what kind of shopper Neiman Marcus thinks I am.
- The groom section cuts the relevance of the email in half. If I'm already unconvinced by the bridal angle, adding men's formal wear doesn't create cross-gender appeal — it signals the creative was built for a catch-all list, not for me.
- No InCircle signal anywhere. I am a member. Sephora at least acknowledges my Insider status in the copy; NM treats me like a stranger who happened onto a very nice website.
- No segmentation rationale visible — nothing to suggest they know I've been a loyal beauty and accessories buyer, not a bridal customer. The July 12 Saint Laurent bags email felt more on-brand for how I actually shop NM; this one feels mailed to the full file.
- The "Gallant Glamour" copy — something about "Peacocky Prince Charming" and a tux — reads like a brief that got approved before the copywriter had coffee. I don't know what it means and I suspect the brand doesn't either.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the jewelry and reframe it as self-purchase.** Bridal jewelry doesn't require me to be a bride. "The pieces worth keeping long after the wedding" opens the category to anyone buying for herself or gifting — which is me.
- 2. **Cut the groom section entirely** — or split it into its own send for a male or gift-buyer segment. Right now it's fragmenting a single creative that could've been tight.
- 3. **Add an InCircle hook.** One line — "As an InCircle member, earn points on every bridal style through July" — turns a generic editorial into a member perk and gives me a reason to click rather than scroll past.
- 4. **Create urgency.** Even editorial can have a deadline: complimentary alterations through a certain date, early access to bridal jewelry for members, anything. The Last Call email two weeks ago proved NM knows how to use urgency; deploy it here.
- 5. **Reduce cadence pressure.** Seventeen emails in thirteen days is the context every future NM send opens into. That fatigue is the invisible 11th criterion working against every rubric metric.

Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, authentication) are unverifiable through the AgentMail relay, which is the highest-severity finding given Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links not probed**
All click-redirect domains were skipped by the QA checker. No dead-link verification was possible. Cannot confirm that destination URLs are live or that UTM parameters are correctly appended.

**Plain-text version is URL-heavy**
2,965 of 3,471 characters (85%) in the plain-text part are URLs. This is above spam-filter thresholds and may increase junk folder placement rates, particularly on older corporate mail filters.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport tags**
Three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` tags appear as comments in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert, but indicate prior viewport strategy churn. Only the active tag (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) applies.

**CSS-only interactive hamburger menu**
The mobile nav relies on a `<input type="checkbox">` hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu`) for show/hide behavior. This pattern works in Apple Mail and a subset of webmail clients, but is silently non-functional in Gmail app, Outlook mobile, and most Android clients — those subscribers see no nav at all unless a static fallback is provided.

**Truncated CSS property in source**
`.TxtSize2x2 { max-wid…` — the source truncation cuts off what is likely `max-width`. If this truncation reflects the actual rendered HTML (rather than a display artifact in this review), the rule is invalid and the 2×2 text cell will not constrain correctly on narrow viewports.

**Legacy iOS9 webkit animation bugfix included**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
iOS 9 is well past EOL. This adds parse overhead with no current benefit. Low severity.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` is commented out — likely null at render time**

The setup comment block reads:
```html
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The `SET @user` assignment is inside an HTML comment wrapping an AMPscript comment — it will not execute. If `@user` (SHA-256 hashed email) is referenced in tracking pixels or URL parameters downstream, it will resolve to an empty string. This silently breaks identity stitching in analytics.

**Syntax anomalies in variable declarations**

Several variable assignments in the comment block have missing `=` operators and/or double-closing parentheses:
```
@user Lookup(...)           ← missing =
@cmdid Lookup(...)          ← missing =
@uEm = Lookup(...))         ← double ))
@emailid = Lookup(...))     ← double ))
```
Because these are inside HTML comment blocks (`<!-- -->`), they don't execute — they're documentation only. The real AMPscript presumably lives in a hidden block or the sendable DE. No runtime error here, but the documentation is misleading and doesn't match the actual execution model.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid, "")` is a no-op**
Concatenating with an empty string produces `@base_refid` unchanged. This appears to be a template placeholder where a suffix (e.g., campaign variant code) was intended but left blank.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not confirmed** `[WARN]`
The AgentMail relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Google and Yahoo require this for bulk senders (>5k/day) as of February 2024. Absence risks deliverability downgrades to Gmail and Yahoo Mail. If the header is present in the actual SMTP envelope but stripped by the relay, that must be verified at the MTA level.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent** `[WARN]`
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is not detected. Gmail enforces this for bulk senders; without it, Gmail renders its own unsubscribe prompt which bypasses the sender's preference center, creating list-management inconsistencies.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication unverifiable** `[WARN]`
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm alignment. If DMARC policy on `neimanmarcus.com` is `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` and the ESP subdomain is misaligned, deliverability impact is severe.
