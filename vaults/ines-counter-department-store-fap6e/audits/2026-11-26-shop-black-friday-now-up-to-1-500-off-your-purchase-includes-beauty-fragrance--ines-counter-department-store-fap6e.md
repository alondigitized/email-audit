# Shop Black Friday now: up to $1,500 off your purchase (includes beauty & fragrance)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- Here's the black box with the red border again. Neiman's has sent me that panel since 11/19. This is the same $1,500 offer I got earlier today, relabeled "Black Friday" and with "includes beauty & fragrance" added. The only new thing is the spend/save table, and I'll give them credit for it. I can read the whole offer in five seconds: spend $250, save $50. That's 20%. Spend $5,000, save $1,500. That's 30%. Nobody has to decode anything.
- The email then stacks a second and third offer under it: the Designer Sale at 60% off, then another black box with an "Extra 25% off" for up to 70%. I opened this to find one offer and found three. Sephora's Cyber Week email at least committed to one idea, which was 30% off, brand after brand. Kohl's piles on too, but I expect that from Kohl's. I don't expect it from Neiman's.
- Would I click? Only the beauty button, because the headline says it's included and I buy at the counter. I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read and wait for the Saint Laurent-style send they do when they stop shouting.

## What's working

- The spend/save table is honest and instantly clear. I can do the math on what I'd actually get back.
- "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" is stated right in the hero. For a counter regular, that's the line that matters.
- The Guerlain and Creed tiles are in the product grid, so beauty isn't an afterthought.
- Brand names sit under every tile (Mac Duggal, Amiri, Jimmy Choo). That reads as designer access, not clearance rack.
- The code THANKFUL is right there in the hero. No hunting for it.

## What's weak

- Three stacked offers: up to $1,500 off, up to 60% off, and extra 25% off for up to 70%. They don't say how they combine, so I can't tell what I'd actually pay.
- "Exclusions Apply" appears three times in small type. The real fine print at the bottom says beauty isn't clearly included in every tier, and the "includes beauty" claim in the headline made me feel baited.
- Beauty is only one of the nine product tiles and just one button in a grid of six. A beauty regular gets one lonely Guerlain bottle.
- The black and red box has been in my inbox for a week. It reads as shouting, not luxury.
- Nothing recognizes me as a member. There's no Neiman Marcus Rewards or points mention, and I won't use a card without points back.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer per send and let the others rest. If it's the spend/save ladder, drop the Designer Sale and extra-25% panels or move them to a separate email.
- 2. Say what's excluded in the body, not only in the fine print. For example: "Includes most beauty. Excludes Chanel and La Mer."
- 3. Add a points angle for members, such as "Earn bonus points on top."
- 4. Give beauty its own row of products (Guerlain, Creed, a Tom Ford) instead of one tile.
- 5. Rest the black box with the red border for a few days. I've seen it too many times.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with a hamburger-menu checkbox hack. It has an internal AMPscript/data-extension comment leaking into the delivered markup and a zoom-blocking viewport tag. The source was truncated, so the footer, body links and plain-text part were not available for inspection.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Plain-text part:** 5862 of 7785 characters (~75%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. This is typical of long tracking URLs in a multipart alternative, and it can hurt spam scoring.
- **No broken or malformed links confirmed** in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick a pronunciation profile.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Fragile media queries:** exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` only match elements whose entire `class` attribute equals that value. Any element with multiple classes will silently skip the mobile rules.
- **Global paragraph rule:** `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` forces centered text for every `<p>`, unless each one is overridden inline.
- **Hamburger menu:** the nav depends on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. This is unsupported in Gmail and some other clients, and the visible source shows no fallback.
- **Legacy hacks:** `.ReadMsgBody` and `-webkit-animation: bugfix` are harmless but dead weight.
- **Not assessable:** image `alt` text, since it fell outside the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked internal data:** the delivered HTML begins with a comment containing the ESP's internal logic: `@email`, `@base_refid`, `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)`, `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"/"cmdid", ...)`, and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This exposes data-extension names, field names and the hashing approach to every recipient. It should be stripped at send time or moved into server-side AMPscript.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** this can trigger quirks mode in some legacy renderers.
- **Unresolved tokens:** none seen in the visible portion, but the `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and `@user` usage can't be confirmed resolved in link parameters because the body was truncated.

## 5. Compliance

- **[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe support is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **[WARN] Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com`, a subdomain, so alignment should be checked on the original message.
- **Not assessable:** the footer's visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address were not in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **Likely `@refid` mechanism:** the header comment suggests `@refid` (derived from `@base_refid`) is used for campaign attribution instead of standard UTMs. This is inference, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the AMPscript comment** (the leaked `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` lookups) from the rendered output, and keep the DOCTYPE as the first line.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
