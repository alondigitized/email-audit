# Things We Love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's email I'd actually keep. It has no percent-off box, no countdown and no red border. After a week of "Designer Sale" and "Private Event: 40% off" shouting, a quiet editorial page feels like someone respecting my time. The Mackage houndstooth coat up top is lovely. This is how a department store should talk to a woman who already knows what she likes.
- It's also a catalog with no reason to act today. There's no offer, no loyalty mention, and every link just says "Shop Now." I'd read it on the commute and feel good about the brand. I wouldn't click until I wanted something. Mulberry and Chantecaille are the only two that might get me there. I wouldn't forward it, because there's nothing in it for a friend. I'd mark it read and keep it as a mood board.
- Compare Sephora Insider's Dyson send. That one promised one thing and delivered a wall of sets. This one promises "style guide" and delivers exactly that. Macy's "LAST CHANCE" shouting makes this look like a different, better-mannered company.

## What's working

- The Mackage houndstooth hero is gorgeous and sets the tone before I read a word.
- It's calm. After the 11/19 "Private Event: 40% off" repeats, no shouting felt like a relief.
- Chantecaille is in there. A beauty brand tucked among the designers is how I shop Neiman's anyway.
- The brand names are clearly labeled (Mulberry, Roger Vivier, Marc Jacobs), so I know instantly what I'm looking at.
- The text is large enough to read without zooming, and the layout is clean with plenty of white space.

## What's weak

- There's no offer at all. Kohl's gives me "Take an extra 20% off." Neiman's gives me nothing for this send, not even a gift-card reminder.
- Every CTA is a small underlined "Shop Now." Those are tiny tap targets on a phone and they say nothing specific.
- There's no loyalty or InCircle mention anywhere, and I don't spend without points back.
- The Church's men's block is a full-width image that pushes everything down. It's irrelevant to me.
- "Brand New Styles Just Landed" shows three small products with no prices. I can't tell whether Seroya at that fur-trim price is a stretch or a laugh.

- 1. Add one line of member value, such as an InCircle points or gift-card reminder, under the header. It's the single biggest lift for someone like me.
- 2. Turn the underlined "Shop Now" links into specific buttons, such as "Shop Mackage outerwear" or "Shop Chantecaille skincare."
- 3. Show starting prices on the three-product row.
- 4. Move the beauty block (Chantecaille) higher, or give it a hero. I'd click that faster than a men's loafer.
- 5. Cut the men's block, or segment it away from women on my profile.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible part of the HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with a checkbox-hack mobile nav. It has two confirmed code-level issues: the ESP's internal template comment shipped in the delivered source, and the viewport blocks user zoom. The source is truncated and the compliance and authentication headers weren't captured, so the footer, links and UTM parameters could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **20 tracking/click-redirect links were not probed** (QA INFO warn). Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is >70% URLs** (3760 of 4277 chars). This points to long tracking URLs in the text alternative. It is a spam-filter heuristic and makes the text part hard to read.
- No broken links were confirmed. Nothing in the visible source shows a dead or malformed `href`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) on supporting clients.
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't select the right language.
- **Fragile media-query selectors.** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=showmobile]` use exact-match attribute selectors. They fail on any element carrying more than one class, so those mobile rules won't apply to such elements.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav** (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). This relies on `:checked` and sibling selectors, which Gmail and several Outlook builds don't support. The fallback is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Title.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't match the subject "Things We Love". This is minor.
- **Legacy `.ReadMsgBody` / `.ExternalClass` rules** are harmless but obsolete.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal ESP documentation comment shipped in the delivered HTML.** It is the first thing in the source: `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) ... @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
  - It exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names, and references a hashed-email identifier.
  - It also shows the template was sent with an unresolved "choose one scenario" placeholder left in.
  - Template authoring notes should be stripped before send.
- No unresolved visible merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the visible portion of the source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found** and **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is unconfirmed rather than a proven sender defect. If the headers are really absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown, and the relay is the expected cause. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, but alignment can't be confirmed here.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** they are not in the truncated source, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters and destination URLs:** they could not be verified. The visible source has no readable `href`s, and the 20 tracking links were not resolved.
- **Variable-driven tracking:** the template comment shows `@refid`, `@base_refid`, `@cmdid` and `@uEm` are used for tracking, so attribution probably runs through these rather than UTMs. This is an inference, not a confirmed finding.
- **Landing-page alignment:** no landing page was checked.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the SFMC/AMPscript documentation comments, including the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" placeholder, from the production template, or strip HTML comments at send time.
2. Change the viewport meta to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` and drop `maximum-scale=1`.
3. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
4. Switch the mobile selectors to `*[class~=…]` or `[class*=…]`, or keep one class per element, so combined classes still match.
5. Confirm the nav fallback for Gmail and Outlook, where the checkbox hack isn't supported.
6. Check the raw source (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. If they are missing, add one-click unsubscribe headers.
7. Shorten the plain-text part's URLs, or give it fuller copy, to bring the URL ratio well below 70%.
8. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML with the tracking links resolved. That would let it confirm the footer unsubscribe link, postal address, UTM parameters and landing-page continuity.
