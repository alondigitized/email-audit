# The style list: 1,000+ new arrivals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- I opened it. Of course I opened it — it's Neiman Marcus, and after the "$50–$500 gift card is here" email I got less than 24 hours ago, I wanted to see the follow-up. What I got instead was a catalog page. A nice one, but a catalog page. Givenchy, Loewe, Oscar de la Renta — brands I actually care about — all just sitting there in a grid, no price, no offer, no reason to click *today* over tomorrow.
- Here's my problem with this send: it arrives right on the heels of what felt like genuine VIP treatment. The gift card email made me feel like Neiman's knew who I was. This one treats me like an anonymous browser. The brands are right. The photography is editorial-clean. But half of this email is menswear, and there's not a single InCircle point mention anywhere. For someone who literally will not swipe her card without earning something back, that omission registers.
- I'll browse it. I won't click it, and I definitely won't forward it — there's nothing to share. It's essentially a digital window display, which is fine if that's all you're going for. But after the cadence they've built this month, this one lands flat.

## What's working

- **Brand lineup hits my exact tier** — Givenchy, Loewe, Oscar de la Renta, Bottega Veneta. This is not Macy's One Day Sale energy. Neiman Marcus earns the premium lane and this email stays in it.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean and editorial** — Hero copy reads first, women's grid second, CTA third. No Macy's-style banner-bomb overload. Sephora opens like this on their best days, and Neiman Marcus matches it here.
- **The red Givenchy dress lands immediately** — Top-left position in the women's grid, vivid against the neutrals around it. That's how you use color to direct the eye.
- **Layout renders cleanly** — Nothing broken, no overlapping text, images load. The basics, yes, but after some of Kohl's recent render disasters, it matters.
- **"Shop Women's" CTA is immediate** — Right under the hero, above the fold on my phone. I don't have to scroll to find out what to do.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no event, no hook** — 1,000+ new arrivals is not a reason to buy today. It's a reason to bookmark and forget. The June 24 gift card email gave me a deadline and a benefit; this one gives me a grid.
- **Half the email is menswear** — Zegna, Off-White, Jacquemus, Dior. Fine brands. I don't shop for men. That real estate should be women's accessories or beauty — the areas where I actually spend.
- **No InCircle acknowledgment anywhere** — I earned my status at this counter. One line — "Earn InCircle points on every new arrival" — and my click likelihood doubles. Macy's at least puts Star Rewards in the footer.
- **1,000+ is the opposite of curation** — The subject sets up scale, the body delivers scale, and nothing in between says "we picked these *for you.*" Contrast that with the June 23 shoulder bag email, which showed me exactly one category and made me want it.
- **No price signal at all** — I'm not asking for a sale. I'm asking to know if this Toteme jacket is $400 or $4,000 before I click. The complete absence of pricing makes this feel more like a lookbook than a shop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the men's section to one thumbnail row or remove entirely for the women's segment.** If Neiman Marcus has segmentation data — and they should — a 47-year-old woman cross-shopping luxury womenswear should never receive a 50/50 women's/men's split. Reclaim that space for a shoe or accessory callout.
- 2. **Add InCircle language in or near the CTA** — Something as simple as "Shop now and earn InCircle points" next to the button. It costs one line; it converts the loyalty member.
- 3. **Curate to 3 hero picks, not 6+** — Lead with the Givenchy dress, the Loewe bag, and one shoes pick. Call them "Our editors' picks from the 1,000+ just landed." That's a different email. That's the email that gets forwarded.
- 4. **Add "From $X" anchoring under the hero** — Even a range signals the tier and filters the right customer in.
- 5. **Subject should name a brand or a category** — "The style list" is editorial but vague. The June 23 shoulder bag subject worked because it was *specific*.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "1,000+ New Arrivals"

**From:** NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com | **ESP:** Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard SFMC template with AMPscript-driven personalization against two CRM lookup tables. Four QA warnings surfaced — two compliance gaps (unsubscribe headers), one deliverability risk (plain-text URL density), and one authentication gap — none of which are visible in the rendered email but each carries real inbox/compliance exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 90%+ URLs (critical deliverability risk)**
QA: `5214/5767 chars are URLs`. The plain-text alternate is composed almost entirely of raw click-redirect URLs with no surrounding prose. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Gmail) penalize this pattern heavily. A well-formed plain-text alternate should mirror the email's readable content with minimal bare URLs.

**25 tracking links not probed**
All click links route through a redirect/tracking domain. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity could not be verified from external probing. This is expected for SFMC deployments, but means no programmatic confirmation that redirects resolve, or that UTM params are present.

**`@refid` is a no-op concat**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
The empty string argument produces `@refid == @base_refid` with no differentiation. If this variable was intended to append a segment suffix or creative variant identifier before being appended to tracking URLs, it is silently broken.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport tags**
Three conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` variants are present as comments, including one with `user-scalable=no` (an accessibility violation under WCAG 1.4.4):
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inactive but indicate historical viewport instability in this template. The active viewport tag (`maximum-scale=1`) also suppresses user zoom — this is functionally equivalent to `user-scalable=no` and fails WCAG 1.4.4 for visually impaired users.

**CSS-only hamburger nav and interactive content**
The template implements a checkbox-driven hamburger nav and tabbed content switcher (`mobileCont:checked~table`) using only CSS. This pattern has uneven support across email clients (broken in Outlook, partial in older Gmail app). No fallback is visible in the truncated source.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but visible in some browser-based clients and tab previews.

**Duplicate breakpoints at 480px and 520px**
Two separate `@media` blocks target nearly identical viewport ranges. This is not a bug per se but adds maintenance surface and specificity risk if rules conflict.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA256 path is commented out — scenario selection is manual**
The template header includes:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
```
The active path sets `@user` via a lookup to `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated.EMAIL_ID`, not a hashed email. If `@user` is passed to onsite personalization or ad-match pixels expecting a SHA256 value, the lookup path will silently send a numeric subscriber ID instead. The `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` comment indicates this is a manual toggle with no runtime guard — wrong scenario activation is a silent failure mode.

**Five AMPscript lookups on send-time**
```
@user    → NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated (EMAIL_ID by EMAIL_ADDRESS)
@emailid → NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated (EMAIL_ID by EMAIL_ADDRESS — duplicate lookup)
@uEm    → NM_CRM_Feed (uEm by EMAIL_ADDRESS)
@cmdid  → NM_CRM_Feed (cmdid by EMAIL_ADDRESS)
```
`@user` and `@emailid` both look up `EMAIL_ID` from the same DE against the same key — this is a redundant lookup that doubles the DE read cost at send time for high-volume sends. If either DE row is missing, both silently return empty strings with no `IIF`/`EMPTY()` guard visible in source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
QA: `List-Unsubscribe header not found`. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (effective Feb 2024) for senders above 5,000/day. If the header is present on the SFMC envelope but stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a capture artifact — confirm against a raw `.eml` delivered directly to a seed address. If genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance violation.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
QA: one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to expose a native "Unsubscribe" button. Without it, users fall back to the in-body unsubscribe link — higher friction increases spam complaints.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
QA: `Authentication-Results header not found`. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have SPF and DKIM pass records visible in the `Authentication-Results` header of delivered mail. This could not be confirmed via relay. If unset, DMARC failures will follow.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully assess: all 25 outbound links route through a click-tracking redirect domain and were not probed. The following cannot be confirmed without direct link resolution:

- UTM parameters present and consistent (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`)
- `@refid` value appended to destination URLs
- Redirect chain resolution (no 404s, no redirect loops)

The `@cmdid` and `@uEm` variables pulled from `NM_CRM_Feed` suggest an onsite personalization or ad-match handshake — if `@uEm` (likely hashed email) is passed as a URL query param, its presence in final redirect URLs should be verified.
