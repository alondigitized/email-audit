# Labor Day savings + 15% off home ... let's go 🏃‍♀️‍➡️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay, six days running now — this is Kohl's Labor Day email number six, and it's the same scroll I've been living in since Wednesday: $10 off $25, $5 Kohl's Cash per $25, kids in matching outfits, luggage, a Sephora banner shoved in the middle, then a wall of category tiles. The "15% off home" in the subject line is technically true — it's the second banner down — but I had to actually go looking for it under the discount stack, and by the time I got there my eye had already bounced through activewear, luggage, jeans, dresses, bras, and floor care. This is not how you sell me on home décor.
- Would I click? Only if I already needed a specific thing — the Serta mattress topper at $114.99 caught my eye for half a second, and my mom-in-law is due a housewarming gift so the "Picked with you in mind" section actually landed. But forward to a friend? No. This is the sixth near-identical Kohl's send this week and I've stopped reading past the top two banners. Neiman Marcus knows how to let one gorgeous image do the work; this is the opposite of that — it's a department store flyer stapled to twelve other department store flyers.

## What's working

- The "Picked with you in mind" module near the bottom actually feels like personalization, not just another discount tile — the Ninja slushie maker and the towel set look like real picks.
- Barcode + promo codes at the bottom is genuinely useful for me since I always end up digging through Kohl's coupons at checkout anyway.
- Reward tier line at the very top ("Status: Kohl's Rewards Member — Earn: 5% Rewards") is a nice touch — it does acknowledge I'm a member, unlike a lot of these blasts.

## What's weak

- I'm now six emails deep this week and every single one leads with the identical $10-off-$25 / $5-Kohl's-Cash stack — Kohl's isn't sending a Labor Day campaign, it's sending the same email on a loop.
- The subject promises "15% off home" but home décor is buried as the second-tier banner competing with women's/men's/kids/home nav buttons, luggage, jeans, dresses, and bras — there's no hierarchy telling my eye where to land.
- The Sephora + Kohl's 50%-off-beauty banner is dropped in the dead center of a home/apparel email with zero connective tissue — it reads like a completely different email got pasted in.
- Kids in the activewear photo skew way younger than anything relevant to me — I'm not the activewear-for-little-boys shopper, and that photo does nothing for a 47-year-old browsing home goods.
- No sense of occasion or curation anywhere — it's twelve discount tiles stacked vertically with no editorial voice, which is the same complaint I had about the 9/5 and 9/4 sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails — a Home-focused Labor Day send and a separate apparel/beauty send. Trying to cram décor, luggage, jeans, dresses, bras, and Sephora into one scroll guarantees home gets lost, which is exactly what happened here.
- 2. Lead the hero with the actual 15% home offer and one lifestyle shot of a styled room, not a coupon-code graphic — right now the top banner is all numbers, no aspiration.
- 3. Cut the Sephora insert entirely from a home-and-apparel email, or give it its own dedicated send like they did on 9/4.
- 4. Vary the creative day-to-day — even swapping hero imagery would help; six straight days of this exact banner format is training me to skip straight to trash.
- 5. Age up the lifestyle photography for the home and apparel sections — a single shot of an adult styling a space for the actual holiday would sell "Labor Day" better than another kids-in-activewear shot.
