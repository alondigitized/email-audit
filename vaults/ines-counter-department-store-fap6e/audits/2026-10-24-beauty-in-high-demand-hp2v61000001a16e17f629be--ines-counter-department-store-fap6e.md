# Beauty in high demand 📈
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the twelfth Sephora email I've had in about a week. My first thought was "another catalog," and it is one. The free Paula's Choice trial sizes at the top got my attention, though. A free deluxe sample with code LOVEPAULA is something I'll actually use. The "Beauty for You" hero underneath is clean and pretty, and the Summer Fridays lip balm shades are lovely.
- The email tries to do too much, and that costs it. The hero says "for You," but nothing in it is specific to me. The $45 minimum on the freebie is in tiny type at the bottom. The footer has Insider, VIB and Rouge tiers, and my own points status is only a thin "You have 0 points" strip. That strip reads as a miss for someone who buys at the brand event and wants points back.
- I'd open it and scroll. I'd probably only click the Paula's Choice code, and only if I was already planning an order. I wouldn't forward it. It's a decent mid-pack send.

## What's working

- The Paula's Choice freebie sits at the very top with the code, "Online only" and the $45+ threshold all visible. It's a concrete offer.
- The Summer Fridays lip balm hero has big product shots, named shades and prices ($24). I can see exactly what I'd be buying.
- Prices are printed under almost every product, including Marc Jacobs at $168 and Clarins at $142. I don't have to click to find out whether something is out of my range.
- "Only at Sephora" tags on Rejuran and EADEM give me a reason to shop here instead of Nordstrom.
- The Clean at Sephora badge is a useful trust cue for skincare buyers.

## What's weak

- The "Beauty for You" hero is generic and has no personalization. It also pushes the actual offer below the first screen.
- The Paula's Choice terms are in tiny print. The "Free" in the badge makes me squint for the catch, and it's a $45 minimum, online only.
- The email has about six modules with no clear hierarchy: the freebie, Summer Fridays, Trending, Emi Jay, Makeup Minis and Florals. It feels like Macy's-style overload.
- My tier status is buried in a thin strip and a footer graphic. Neiman Marcus at least ties its lookbook to an event. Here there's no points multiplier and no tier benefit.
- The Emi Jay hair clip block isn't for me. The models and products skew younger.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer, the Paula's Choice freebie. Put the threshold in plain type next to the button instead of in fine print.
- 2. Show my real tier and points balance, with a tie to a points event. Ulta and Macy's both make member status more visible.
- 3. Cut the content to three modules: the freebie, one hero and one trending row. Kohl's runs one template for ten emails, but at least its message is clear.
- 4. Use more skincare, such as Rejuran, Clarins and Paula's Choice, for the 40+ shopper. Lead with that instead of hair clips.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 material links were probed and 47 skipped. The QA data reports no broken links among the probed ones. The skipped links, mostly footer and social, are unverified.
- Possible malformed query string on the `evtEmail` tracking pixel: `https://www.sephora.com/gway/v1/evtEmail?apikey=…&context=6rMyANpz7hjTknEn1dS2ML×tamp=202…`. The `×tamp` is `&timestamp` with `&times` decoded as an HTML entity. This may be an artifact of the QA extractor. If the source has a bare `&` rather than `&amp;`, some parsers will corrupt the `timestamp` parameter. Check the raw attribute.
- The `evtEmail` pixel URL carries `apikey=nQc7BFt78yJBvfYDKtle9APd5RrX984i` in the query string. It is client-visible by design, but confirm it is a public, scoped, write-only key.
- The open-tracking pixel at `app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF2g…` is present and normal.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` disables pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- The CSS sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` and `text-size-adjust: none` on `div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td`. This blocks user text scaling in WebKit clients.
- The `<html>` tag shows no `lang` attribute (`<html xmlns=… xmlns:v=… xmlns:o=…>`), so screen readers can't pick the correct language.
- `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` is an invalid value (should be `noindex`). It is harmless in email but sloppy.
- `<title>Sephora</title>` is generic and has no per-send context.
- Four images lack alt text:
  - Two `evtEmail` pixels (`gway/v1/evtEmail?…`).
  - The open pixel (`app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF…`).
  - `2021_Navs_Header_06.jpg` (`images.harmony.epsilon.com/ContentHandler/images/de0a3226-…`).
- The three pixels are decorative and should carry `alt=""`. The nav header is a content image, so it needs real alt text. If it is a nav image, its links are unlabeled when images are blocked.
