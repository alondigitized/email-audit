# Discover 1,000+ new additions for effortless elegance
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails since June 15 — I stopped counting and started categorizing. Two days ago, the "$200 off last chance" actually worked on me: I opened, browsed the designer sale, and nearly pulled the trigger. And then on the same morning — the same morning — Neiman's drops this one into my inbox. Pure editorial mode. No offer. No event invite. No member pricing. Just "1,000+ new styles," organized, and left for me to supply my own reason to buy.
- The email itself is clean in a way I respect. The black hero is confident, the product curation reads designer without being intimidating, and seeing Tom Ford, Ralph Lauren, and Brunello Cucinelli product shots flat on white reminds me of the actual store experience. But roughly half of this email is men's apparel. Half. I'm a 47-year-old woman shopping for herself on a Sunday morning. I scrolled past all of it and felt nothing.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. No urgency, no loyalty play, no offer — and Neiman's already had my attention with the last two sends. This one needed to either close the loop on my browsing momentum or at least signal that it knows me. It did neither. Wouldn't forward. Would move on.

## What's working

- The black hero is brand-right and confident — "Brand New Styles Just Landed" is minimal, premium, and lands exactly the way Neiman's should open a new-arrivals send.
- Product curation is genuinely on-target for my taste: Ralph Lauren blazer, Tom Ford blouse, Brunello Cucinelli shoes — these are my labels.
- Flat product photography on white is consistent with the site experience; clicking won't feel like a gear-shift, it'll feel like a continuation.
- The "Shop Women's" and "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" CTAs are unambiguous and appropriately prominent — no hunting for the button.
- Clean render, no broken images, legible without zooming on a phone.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. After a "$200 off" promotional email was sent the same day, pivoting immediately to editorial with no price hook feels like a dropped hand-off. I had momentum; this email doesn't catch it.
- Men's apparel eats roughly half the scroll — there's no framing (not a gift guide, not a "for him" module) that gives me a reason to be there. Saks wouldn't dedicate half a women's send to the men's floor without context.
- No loyalty acknowledgment at all. I carry an InCircle number. One line — "Preview new arrivals as an InCircle member" — changes my entire posture. This email treats me like a cold prospect.
- No prices or even a "from $XX" anchor. Sephora shows starting prices even on editorial sends. Looking at a Tom Ford blouse with no number attached is like shopping a window with the tags turned.
- "Effortless elegance" in the subject is filler. Compared to the June 21 "Don't miss out on our Private Designer Sale" — which at least implied exclusive access — this reads like a Macy's batch-and-blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the product grid.** "From $295" under the Tom Ford blouse changes my click calculus immediately. Give me something to calibrate against before I invest the tap.
- 2. **Cut or collapse the men's section.** Lead with the full women's edit, give it breathing room, and either segment men's into its own send or compress it to one row at the bottom with a clear "shopping for him?" frame.
- 3. **Add an InCircle / loyalty signal.** Even a single-line callout — "New arrivals, members first" or "Your InCircle preview" — signals that I'm a known customer, not a prospecting target.
- 4. **Coordinate the two June 22 sends.** Sending a "$200 off last chance" promotional and a new-arrivals editorial on the same morning is a cadence collision. Stagger by 48 hours, or let the promo email carry a "and new arrivals just dropped" tertiary module.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject** — drop "effortless elegance" and get specific about the designers or the access angle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: Tom Ford, Ralph Lauren & 1,000+ more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: designer new arrivals, InCircle early`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The new-season edit is live — shop before it sells through.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Curated by designer, starting from $295. New drops, just landed.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "1,000+ New Arrivals" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy SFMC deployment, but carries two material compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) that now violate Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements, plus a commented-out AMPscript personalization variable that likely breaks SHA-256 hashed-email tracking across all 26 redirect links. Authentication status is unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links — unprobed**
All click links route through a redirect/tracking domain. QA skipped HTTP probes on these, so broken destinations cannot be ruled out. Manual spot-checks against the redirect chain are needed, especially for any new-arrivals landing pages.

**Hashed-email token likely unset**
The AMPscript setup comment block contains:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This line is inside an HTML comment, meaning it never executes. Depending on which scenario branch is active, `@user` may fall through to the `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` value instead of the SHA-256 hash. If tracking links or personalization segments are constructed using `@user` expecting a hash, they will silently pass the wrong value (a raw EMAIL_ID or empty string) for every recipient.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` dependency**
Both fields are pulled from `NM_CRM_Feed` via lookup. If a subscriber record is missing from that DE (e.g., new opt-ins not yet synced), both will be empty strings. Whether downstream links degrade gracefully is not visible in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`max-device-width` deprecated**
Two `@media` blocks use `max-device-width: 480px` and `max-device-width: 520px`. This property was removed from the CSS specification and is no longer reliable on Android 5+ or iOS 13+. The paired `max-width` rule fires correctly, but the device-width branch is dead weight that could cause unexpected overrides on older parsers.

**CSS attribute selectors missing quotes**
`*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, etc. are technically invalid (must be `*[class="contenttable"]`). Most email clients tolerate this, but strict-mode parsers (some older Android mail, Lotus Notes) may silently drop the rule, breaking the responsive layout.

**Interactive CSS — near-zero client coverage**
The hamburger nav relies on a CSS checkbox hack:
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
```
and CSS transitions/`visibility` toggling. This pattern is supported only in Apple Mail and a handful of progressive web mail clients. Outlook (all versions), Gmail app, and Samsung Mail ignore it entirely — recipients there see whatever the default `display:none` state renders.

**`@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` for iOS 9**
The "Hamburguer fix IOS9" keyframe block targets an OS version from 2015. It is dead code and can be removed.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Missing `lang="en"` prevents screen readers from selecting the correct language profile and fails WCAG 2.1 criterion 3.1.1.

**Stale commented-out viewport tags**
Three `<meta name="viewport">` alternatives are commented out inline. Not a functional issue, but they expose internal A/B test history and inflate source size.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` scenario comment is ambiguous**
The block says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" but the SHA-256 override is commented out with no clear indication which scenario is active in production. Without seeing the live SFMC send definition, it is unknown whether `@user` resolves to the hashed email or the raw EMAIL_ID lookup.

**No visible fallback guards**
Variables `@uEm` and `@cmdid` are looked up without visible `IIF(EMPTY(...))` guards in the truncated source. Missing CRM records will produce empty substitutions silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not detected (WARN)**
Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5,000/day) since February 2024. Its absence risks:
- Deliverability demotion to the Promotions tab or spam
- Loss of Gmail's native "Unsubscribe" button, increasing spam complaints

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` / RFC 8058 one-click — not detected (WARN)**
One-click machine-readable unsubscribe is a co-requirement under the same Gmail/Yahoo policy. Both headers are likely present at the MTA level and simply not captured by the AgentMail relay, but this must be confirmed directly against raw SMTP headers from a delivered sample.

**Authentication-Results — not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status are unknown. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain (Salesforce Marketing Cloud); DKIM alignment with the header `From:` domain should be verified. A raw header sample from Gmail's "Show original" view is the fastest confirmation path.

**Plain-text version is 90% URLs (5,180 / 5,733 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is essentially a URL dump. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `HTML_MESSAGE`, Barracuda) penalize this pattern. CAN-SPAM technically requires only that physical address and unsubscribe mechanism be present in the plain-text body — those may be included in the remaining 553 chars — but the ratio is a deliverability risk.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 26 tracking links were not probed, so UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed. No evidence in the truncated source of hardcoded UTM strings on nav or CTA links — these are likely injected by the SFMC redirect wrapper. Spot-checking two or three decoded redirect URLs from a test send against expected `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` values is the minimum verification step before the next broadcast.
