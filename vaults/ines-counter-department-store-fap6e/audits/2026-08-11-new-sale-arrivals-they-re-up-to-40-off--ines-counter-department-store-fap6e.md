# New. Sale. Arrivals. (& they’re up to 40% off)
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- That giant calligraphic "S" swallowing "ale" is genuinely striking — it's the kind of hero image that makes me pause my scroll for a beat, which most of these emails don't manage. But then I hit "40% off" and immediately clank into déjà vu: Neiman's ran an almost identical crimson-block "up to 70% off" LAST CALL yesterday, and now it's this dusty-blue sale block with a lower number and no urgency stamp at all. No deadline, no "ends tonight," nothing — after yesterday's genuine countdown, this feels like a step backward into generic sale mode.
- The nine-item product grid is doing real work for me though — Tory Burch, Jimmy Choo, Ungaro, Alice + Olivia — that's a legitimate cross-section of what I actually buy, and it reads like a curated edit rather than a dumping ground. Compare that to Kohl's, which just piles clearance tiles under a banner with zero editorial hand, or Sephora's "something new" emails that are all product, no narrative — Neiman's at least frames this as "these coveted styles," which flatters the customer a little.
- Where it loses me: that black "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF — join our text list" block near the bottom. I'm InCircle-adjacent when I shop Neiman's, or at least I expect to be treated like someone with history there, and asking me to hand over my phone number for a discount I could probably already beat with existing loyalty pricing feels like it's targeting a stranger, not a regular. I'd scroll through this, maybe click into the sale grid out of habit, but I wouldn't forward it — nothing here is urgent or personal enough to share.

## What's working

- The oversized "S...ale" typographic hero is genuinely elegant and stopped my scroll — better art direction than most retailers bother with.
- The nine-product grid spans categories I actually shop (shoes, dresses, handbags, denim) with real designer names visible, not generic stock photography.
- "Exclusions Apply" is printed small but visible right under the CTA — I don't have to hunt for the catch, unlike Macy's confirm-to-save games.
- Clean nav bar and shop-by-category buttons (Dresses, Handbags, Women's Shoes) give me a fast lane if I don't want to scroll the whole grid.

## What's weak

- No deadline anywhere — after yesterday's "ends tonight at 10PM CT" LAST CALL, this just says "up to 40% off" with no clock, so it doesn't feel urgent enough to act today versus next week.
- The text-list opt-in offering 15% off treats me like an acquisition target, not an existing customer — where's my InCircle points balance or member pricing instead?
- "Up to 40% off" after yesterday's "up to 70% off" reads like a discount downgrade, and nothing in the copy explains why — it just feels like a weaker version of yesterday's email.
- Zero social proof or "selling fast" signal on any of the nine products, so nothing nudges me to act on a specific item versus browsing at leisure.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real deadline on the 40%-off banner — even "through Sunday" beats no date at all, especially right after a send that had one.
- 2. Replace the generic "Claim your 15% off / join our text list" block with InCircle points messaging — tell me what my tier gets me on this sale, not what a new subscriber gets.
- 3. Add a one-line "why now" under the hero — new markdowns, first cut of fall sale, whatever the actual news is — so it doesn't read as a rerun of yesterday's LAST CALL with a smaller number.
- 4. Tag 2-3 of the nine grid items with "selling fast" or a stock indicator to convert browsing into urgency.
