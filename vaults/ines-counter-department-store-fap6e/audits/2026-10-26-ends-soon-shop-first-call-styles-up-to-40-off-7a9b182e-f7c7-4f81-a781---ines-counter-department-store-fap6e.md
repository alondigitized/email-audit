# Ends soon: Shop First Call styles up to 40% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This one finally has a number in the hero. After Saturday's wedding-guest lookbook and the suede grid, I'll take it. The red-and-black "FIRST CALL, UP TO 40% OFF" is loud, but it's honest loud. It tells me the offer before I scroll. It also lands in an inbox where Neiman's has sent me five or six emails since the 23rd. The "Ends soon" in the subject is the only urgency, and the body never gives me a date.
- The fine print undercuts the hero. The small type at the bottom says savings are 25-40% on select categories. So the "up to 40%" is the ceiling, and the typical discount is lower. That's the same bait-and-switch feeling I got on 10/25 with the "up to 40%" subject and the 30% hero. A "Shop Now" button and "Exclusions Apply" under it are not what I'd call generous.
- Would I click? Probably, once, to see whether the Zimmermann dress or the Ferragamo sandal is really marked down. I wouldn't forward it. Nothing here treats me as a customer who has a relationship with the store.

## What's working

- The hero puts "UP TO 40% OFF" in huge type. I get the offer in one second, with no hunting.
- "Save with new markdowns on fall styles" tells me this is fresh, not leftover stock.
- The brand names under each product are the ones I shop: Zimmermann, FRAME, Ferragamo, Theory, rag & bone. That reads as designer access.
- The six category buttons at the bottom are big, red and easy to tap on a phone.
- The "Match with a personal style advisor" link is a nice touch. It feels like the counter experience.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere. I can't see a single original or sale price on those nine products, so "40% off" is only a promise.
- There's an empty gap in the top row of the grid, between the Zimmermann dress and the FRAME jeans. It looks like a missing image, and it makes the email feel sloppy for a luxury house.
- "Ends soon" has no date or time, and the body never says when it ends. I can't plan around it.
- The footnote says 25-40% on select categories, and "Exclusions Apply" is in tiny type under the button. That's the buried fine print I notice.
- Nothing about my status. There's no InCircle points angle, no tier, no "your points on this." I won't put a card down without points back, and Neiman's didn't even try.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show prices. Put "was/now" prices under at least the dress, the sandal and the bag, so I can see 40% on something real.
- 2. Fix the blank slot in the grid. Fill it or reflow the row.
- 3. State the deadline in the hero ("Ends Sunday 10/29") and match the real range to the headline ("25-40% off").
- 4. Add one line for InCircle members, such as bonus points or early access. That's what would make me feel known.
- 5. Link each product to its own page, and cut the cadence. Sephora sent me thirteen emails in a week and I stopped reading them. Neiman's is heading the same way.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a mobile media-query block. The source has one confirmed defect: an internal ESP variable and lookup comment block is shipped in the delivered HTML. The QA run reports 0 failures and 4 warnings, and all four are relay or verification limits rather than confirmed defects. The HTML was truncated and 29 links were not probed, so the link and UTM checks below are incomplete.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 29 tracking links were skipped because they are click-redirect domains (`[WARN] 29 tracking link(s) skipped`). Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified.
- **No broken links confirmed:** the QA run reports 0 issues, and the truncated source shows no malformed `href` values.
- **Plain-text part:** 5512 of 6455 characters (about 85%) are URLs (`Plain-text is >70% URLs`). This is typical of tracked-link templates, but it hurts plain-text readability and is a minor spam-filter signal.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 resize-text problem.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. These only match when `class` is exactly that value. Any element with a second class, such as `class="mobile hide"`, will not get the responsive rule.
- **CSS-only hamburger nav:** the nav relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~…` sibling selectors. These are unsupported in Gmail and several other clients, where the menu may stay hidden or unusable. I could not confirm whether a fallback exists in the truncated source.
- **Legacy and dead code:** the source carries `.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` and the iOS9 `bugfix` keyframes. It also has three commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants. These add weight but are not defects.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and says nothing specific about the email.
- **Not assessable:** alt text, image dimensions and the lang attribute are not visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked template comment:** the HTML opens with `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) … Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …) … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
  - It reaches every recipient and exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It also shows that the template embeds subscriber identifiers (`@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`).
  - The comment sits before the `<!DOCTYPE>`. Content ahead of the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some Outlook and legacy renderers.
  - The comment text itself contains a stray `))` and an unresolved "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction.
- **Merge tokens:** no unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[[…]]` tokens are visible in the truncated source. Link parameters could not be inspected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe missing:** the header was not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a sender defect.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post missing:** the RFC 8058 header was also not found. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders, so this needs checking on the original message.
- **Authentication-Results missing:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because the relay does not provide the header. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, which is normal.
- **Footer not in view:** the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address fall outside the truncated source, so CAN-SPAM body requirements are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Cannot assess:** the 29 tracked links were not resolved, and the truncated source shows no destination URLs or UTM parameters. I can't confirm UTM presence, landing-page match or redirect behavior.
- **Tracking approach:** the leaked comment points to ESP-side tracking built on `@refid`/`@base_refid` rather than visible UTMs. That is a design observation, not a defect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the `<!--REQUIRED variables … -->` and `<!--/* SET @user … */ -->` comments from the production template, or move them into server-side comment syntax that is not rendered. Put the `<!DOCTYPE>` first.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Check the original `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC in `Authentication-Results`. Capture them directly instead of through the relay.
4. Resolve a sample of the skipped tracking links and confirm landing-page destinations, redirect hops and UTM or refid parameters.
5. Replace `*[class=x]` selectors with `*[class~=x]` or `.x` selectors, and confirm the hamburger nav has a non-CSS-checkbox fallback for Gmail.
6. Give the `<title>` descriptive content and trim the trailing space.
7. Add a plain-text part with readable copy rather than URL-dominated content.
