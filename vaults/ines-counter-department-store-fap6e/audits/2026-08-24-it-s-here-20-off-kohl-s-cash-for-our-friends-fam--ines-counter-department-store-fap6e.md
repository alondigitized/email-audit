# IT'S HERE: 20% off + Kohl's Cash for our friends & fam 🎉
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- Well, this one at least changed the wallpaper. After six straight days of that same kid at the lockers, Kohl's finally swapped in a Martha Stewart slow cooker and a wall of home goods, and honestly it's a relief just to see something different in my inbox. But "Friends & Family 20% off" is generic mass-blast copy dressed up as an insider perk — I'm a Rewards member (it says so right at the top, "Status: KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn: 5% REWARDS"), and nothing here treats me any differently than a first-time shopper who wandered in off a Google ad.
- The Martha Stewart collection debut is the one genuinely interesting beat — I like Martha, I like a good slow cooker, and that's the kind of "new arrival" I'd actually stop scrolling for, the way Sephora's Tower 28 foundation callout worked in that App Fest email I read. But after that it's the Macy's problem all over again: Women, Men, Home, Kids, Home again, Beauty, Shoes, six more product tiles with prices — by the time I hit "Picked with you in mind (Your great taste makes it easy)" I've stopped believing anything is actually picked for me, it's just everything.
- I'd click through on the Martha Stewart slow cooker out of curiosity, maybe. I would not forward this to a friend — there's no single offer or product worth passing along, just a long scroll of "buy stuff." Skims, click maybe once, archive.

## What's working

- The Martha Stewart collection launch is a real, specific hook — new designer/brand tie-in is exactly the kind of "here's something you haven't seen" that gets me to stop scrolling.
- The 20% off banner up top is clean and readable — big blue block, clear percentage, end date (Aug. 30) right there, no squinting required.
- It shows my Rewards status and 5% earn rate at the very top, which at least acknowledges I'm a member, even if nothing downstream builds on it.
- Prices are specific and visible on the "Picked with you" items — $2.89-$9.99 towels, $137.99 mattress topper, actual numbers instead of vague "great deals" language.

## What's weak

- "Picked with you in mind (Your great taste makes it easy)" sits over a Ninja slushy maker, a bath towel, jeans, and a mattress topper — that's not personalization, that's four random categories, and calling it "picked for me" when it so clearly isn't feels a little insulting.
- The email is a marathon — Friends & Family banner, Kohl's Cash banner, four nav buttons, Martha Stewart block, "Every room deserves an upgrade" with four more tiles, "Fresh favorites for fall" with six more tiles, then the "picked for you" section. That's Macy's-level overload without Macy's occasional restraint.
- Zero designer-access or beauty-counter tone anywhere — Sephora gets one small icon buried in a shoe/home/beauty row, no ingredient or product detail, nothing that speaks to how I actually shop the counter.
- The coupon code block near the bottom ("Code: SHOPPERS PIN 9355") is where the real offer detail lives, and it's crammed at the very end after I've already scrolled past a dozen unrelated tiles — that should've been up near the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Martha Stewart launch, not the generic 20% off banner — that's the actual news this week, put the collection and a hero product shot first, then the discount as the "and it's 20% off" kicker.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Four separate "shop by category" grids (Home, fall favorites, "picked for you") is too many — pick one curated set of 6-8 items max, tied to something I've actually browsed, not a full store directory.
- 3. Move the coupon code and PIN up near the hero banner, not buried after ten product tiles — if you want me to use it, don't make me scroll for it.
- 4. Actually use my Rewards status for something — a member-only early-access window on the Martha Stewart line, or bonus Kohl's Cash multiplier for Rewards members, would make that top banner mean something instead of just decoration.
- 5. Drop or shrink the beauty/Sephora tile — it's one small square with no product callout, which reads as an afterthought next to Sephora's own emails that at least show a face and a product.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rewards members: your 20% off code + first look at Martha Stewart`
