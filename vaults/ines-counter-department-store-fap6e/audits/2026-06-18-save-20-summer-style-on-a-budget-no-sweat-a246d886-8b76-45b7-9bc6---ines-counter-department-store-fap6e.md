# Save 20% | Summer style on a budget? No sweat 😎
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-two. That's my Kohl's email count in June — I checked before I opened this one. "Summer style on a budget? No sweat" has a casual, breezy hook, which I'll give them. But the second I'm inside, I'm looking at a male athlete, Nike and Adidas logos, and a hero that reads "Move more. Spend less." I cross-shop Nordstrom, Macy's, and Saks. I buy at the beauty counter. That opening image was not built for me.
- The structure is classic Kohl's: athletic hero, family lifestyle section, an entire Haggar menswear block, a home cooling grid, a second athletic push, and then the generic 20% close. Nothing connects. Nothing narrows to a person. The Kohl's Cash badge at the bottom is the one signal that makes me feel like a known customer rather than a mass address — but it's buried in the footer. Compare that to the Neiman Marcus Designer Sale email from the same week, where the 60% off message lived in the subject AND the hero AND the CTA, all pointed at the same thing. This one is trying to be everyone's email and ends up being no one's.
- I'd mark it read and move on. I would not forward it to a friend. I might forward it to my husband so he can look at the Haggar pants.

## What's working

- **20% off is the first thing I see** — the banner at the top leads with the offer number, no hunting required.
- **Kohl's Cash badge at the bottom** — that $10 cash-back reward is the one loyalty signal in here; it's the actual reason I stay subscribed.
- **Summer home goods grid** ("Finds that keep their cool, even when summer won't") — bedding, fans, and storage in June is actually useful and seasonal; the one section that felt like it was thinking about real life.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no lorem ipsum. Everything loaded.

## What's weak

- **Male athletic hero** — the very first human I see is a man in activewear. I'm 47, I shop beauty counters and designer floors. That image is pointed at someone else's inbox.
- **A full Haggar section** — dedicated men's pants space in what the subject calls a summer style email. It eats real scroll depth and has nothing to do with me.
- **Zero beauty content** — Kohl's has Sephora at Kohl's in stores. This email doesn't know that. Not a single skincare product, fragrance, or cosmetic anywhere. Sephora's 50% off beauty email from this same stretch was laser-focused; this is the opposite.
- **Cadence is exhausted** — the June 17 send gave me "Take 20% off & save on new faves," the June 16 send gave me bedding and a cast-iron skillet, and now this one. Same discount, different wrapper, no sense of differentiation. Even Neiman Marcus — which sent about as many emails this month — gave each one a distinct reason to exist.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero for a women's or beauty image** — if my purchase history skews women's or home, the opening visual should reflect that. A male sprinter is dead weight in my inbox.
- 2. **Surface a Sephora @ Kohl's row** — one dedicated beauty callout ("Summer skincare, 20% off") would make me feel like this email knows who I am. That's the version I'd save to my wallet.
- 3. **Move Kohl's Cash above the fold** — the loyalty hook is the conversion driver for someone like me, not the seasonal pun. Pull it up to just below the hero.
- 4. **Put a hard deadline on the 20% off** — Kohl's Cash already has "through 6/22," so the infrastructure is there. Apply the same logic to the main offer. "Ends Sunday" changes my behavior; "save 20%" does not.
- 5. **Kill the Haggar section or relocate it** — if this is a general women's summer email, that block is wasted real estate. Save it for a family or Father's Day send.

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% / Summer Style" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with three independent tracking stacks (CoherentPath, MessageGears, Adobe AAM) and passes QA at 33% due to pervasive missing alt text and absent compliance headers. One invalid HTML tag in the body is the most acute rendering risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid closing tag in body**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?XXUMK42PYS4U3LXHHGSTH275YI.60264..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is a stray/invalid tag — almost certainly a template rendering artifact from the ESP. Depending on the mail client's HTML parser, this can break DOM structure for everything that follows it.

**22 click-tracking links unverified**
All destination URLs pass through `click.chp.kohls.com` (CoherentPath) and `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears). Final redirect targets and UTM parameters could not be probed. This is expected behavior for tracked emails but means destination URL correctness is unverifiable from source alone.

**Three overlapping tracking stacks**
- `click.chp.kohls.com` — CoherentPath CDP
- `mi.kohls.com` + `click.s.kohls.com` — MessageGears ESP
- `kohls.demdex.net` — Adobe Audience Manager

Redundant open/click tracking is common at this scale but increases pixel count and load time.

**Empty favicon `<link>` tag**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
An empty `href` on a `<link>` causes some clients to fire a request to the current URL. Harmless in most email renderers but is noise; this should be removed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Zoom disabled on mobile**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` together prevent pinch-to-zoom. This is an accessibility failure under WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked by some accessibility-focused email clients.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the title when entering a message. An empty title leaves a blank announcement. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.

**12 images missing alt text** (per QA)
Includes both tracking pixels and content images:
- Tracking pixels (`1f22b1a7…`, `o.gif`, `event`, `demdex`) — these should use `alt=""` (decorative); several are missing it entirely rather than having an empty string
- Content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d…`, `415c8a18…`, product images `54703482_root_2x`, `54703482_1_2x`) — these require descriptive alt text; without it, image-off rendering (Gmail app, Outlook) produces no fallback text

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` and `@import` — duplicated**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...");
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...);
```
Both Roboto and Montserrat are loaded twice (once via `<link>`, once via `@import`). The `@import` inside `<style>` is inside an `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional, but the `<link>` tags are unconditional. This causes duplicate font requests in non-MSO clients. More critically, Google Fonts are blocked by the majority of email clients (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, Gmail, Outlook) — the fallback stack (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) should always be tested as the primary render.

### 5. Compliance

The visible HTML body contains no physical address. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. Either it appears below the truncation point or it is absent — verify against the full source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`no-referrer` meta strips referrer on outbound clicks**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This directive instructs the browser/webview to send no `Referer` header when the subscriber clicks through. If the CoherentPath/MessageGears redirect does not inject UTM parameters into the final destination URL before handing off to the browser, analytics on kohls.com will classify these sessions as Direct rather than Email. Since all 22 links go through tracked redirects, verify that the redirect resolves to URLs with explicit `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters — do not rely on referrer passthrough.

**Campaign ID visible in pixel parameters**
`mi_ecmp=1015491_2026618` and `email_name=260618_DG_Email_Thursda` (truncated) are present in MessageGears render-tracking pixels, confirming campaign attribution metadata is set. Verify the same `mi_ecmp` value maps to the correct campaign in the MessageGears dashboard.

**Destination URL correctness unverifiable**
All 22 click links resolve through `click.chp.kohls.com` redirects — final landing page URLs and their UTM strings cannot be confirmed without probing the redirect chain.
