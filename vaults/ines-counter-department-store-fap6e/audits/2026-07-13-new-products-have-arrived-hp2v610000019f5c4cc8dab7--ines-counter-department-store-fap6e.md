# 🎉 New products have arrived
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Nine emails in thirteen days from Sephora, and today's lead is 🎉 New products have arrived. I'm a Sephora Insider with a three-digit yearly spend at this brand — and they send me the same subject line a college freshman would compose in thirty seconds. I opened it out of habit, not interest.
- Inside, there's actually a real email hiding under the clutter. The "Take Your PICK" hero is pretty — soft pastels, editorial, they know their aesthetic. The Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off banner at the top is exactly the kind of offer I'd stop for. The Rare Beauty free gift with $35 is the second smart mechanic. But then the email explodes into five product grids, an app promo, a Benefit launch section, and a loyalty footer, and I've lost the thread. This is Macy's beauty-floor energy on a Saturday — not my Nordstrom counter rep who remembers I'm on my third bottle of Glossier serum.
- "NEW COLOR + SPIRIT OF SPRING." It is July 13th. Spring has been over for three weeks. Neiman Marcus wrapped their gift card campaign clean before the deadline language went stale — Sephora left seasonal copy running into summer and expected me not to notice. I mark this as read. I might come back for the Rare Beauty gift, but I am not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The Buy 2 Minis banner up top is the strongest offer in the email — it should have been the subject line.
- "Take Your PICK" hero copy is clean, confident, and on-brand — editorial without being precious.
- Rare Beauty free gift with a $35 threshold is a realistic spend for how I already shop that brand; it's an offer that fits my behavior rather than demanding I change it.
- Beauty Insider section at the bottom acknowledges I'm a member — I notice when loyalty is invisible.
- No render bugs anywhere I can see; clean layout on what I assume is mobile.

## What's weak

- "🎉 New products have arrived" is nine emails into this month's cadence and tells me absolutely nothing about what I'm opening — no product, no category, no offer.
- "NEW COLOR + SPIRIT OF SPRING" copy running in mid-July is stale and lazy; a 47-year-old shopper clocks that immediately.
- Below the hero fold, it's a catalog dump — five product grids with no editorial voice or hierarchy, which reads like the Sephora website on shuffle, not a curated send.
- Zero urgency or deadline anywhere in the email; the Rare Beauty offer feels permanent, so I won't act on it today.
- App Early Access module is irrelevant to how I shop — I am a counter regular, not an app-first customer, and this wastes scroll real estate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the actual offer in the subject.** "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" is the clearest value in this email — hiding it inside the body while leading with a balloon emoji is burying the lede.
- 2. **Kill the Spring copy.** Update the seasonal module for July — "Summer Newness" or "Mid-July Arrivals" costs nothing and doesn't make the send feel like a re-run.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** The Rare Beauty gift offer has no end date, which is the same as having no urgency. Even "through Sunday" would move me to act today.
- 4. **Edit the product grid down by half.** Pick the four or five items that fit the "Take Your PICK" theme — Marc Jacobs, Rare Beauty, Benefit — and cut the rest. Curation is the brand promise; the catalog scroll undermines it.
- 5. **Replace the App Early Access module with a loyalty-tier moment.** Tell me what my Insider tier unlocks this month. That's what keeps me from sliding to Nordstrom Beauty.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals + Rare Beauty gift with $35`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free gift with any $35 Rare Beauty purchase, plus minis half off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Locks zoom at 1×, preventing low-vision users from magnifying content. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or set `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue 2 — Global `text-size-adjust: none` on interactive elements**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  ...
}
```
Suppressing text-size-adjust on `<a>` and body overrides user accessibility settings. Scope this reset to layout containers (`table`, `td`) only, not inline/interactive elements.

**Issue 3 — Robots meta value has a space**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`"no index"` (space) is not a recognized directive; the correct token is `"noindex"`. Low-severity for email (most clients don't index), but should be corrected for consistency.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot confirm from truncated source. Ensure all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes; decorative images should use `alt=""`.
