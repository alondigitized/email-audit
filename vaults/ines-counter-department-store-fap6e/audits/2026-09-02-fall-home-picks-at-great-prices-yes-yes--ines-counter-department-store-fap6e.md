# Fall home picks at great prices ... yes & yes 😍
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one I'll actually give Kohl's some credit for — home is a category I browse even if I don't buy at Kohl's, and the Martha Stewart collection banner is a real "oh, wait" moment. Martha Stewart mixer and slow cooker in that cream color, the pumpkin-orange quilted pillow, the little florals-in-a-basket vignette — someone on their creative team actually styled this like a home magazine spread instead of a circular. It's the first Kohl's send all week that didn't open with a jersey or a "TODAY ONLY" siren.
- But then it does the thing Kohl's always does to me: two clean, pretty screens of fall home styling, and then it face-plants into the same CLEARANCE 85% off pink starburst, the same four-button department nav (Home/Women/Men/Kids), and a "Picked with you in mind" grid that's slamming together a $349 slushie machine, bath towels, Gloria Vanderbilt jeans, and a mattress topper — none of which reads as "picked" for anyone, let alone a 47-year-old beauty-counter shopper. It's the exact same bait-and-switch structure as their 8/31 clearance email, just with a nicer cold open this time.
- Would I click? Maybe on "Shop Home" up top, out of mild curiosity about the Martha Stewart stuff — that's a real hook. Would I forward it? No. Sephora's Marc Jacobs "free gift" email at least stayed on-message start to finish; this one can't resist turning into the department-store dump truck by scroll three.

## What's working

- The Martha Stewart collection block is genuinely eye-catching — recognizable name, cohesive cream-colored styling, feels like a real launch not a markdown.
- "20% off Fall for the home" with the pumpkin pillow and floral arrangement photographed nicely — this is the kind of seasonal styling Kohl's usually skips.
- Rewards status bar up top ("Kohl's Rewards Member / Earn 5% Rewards") is a nice small touch of "we know who you are."
- The CTAs are all clearly labeled buttons ("Shop Home," "Shop Now") — no ambiguity about where to click.

## What's weak

- The clearance/department-nav block completely resets the tone the Martha Stewart section just built — feels like two different emails stapled together.
- "Picked with you in mind (Your great taste makes it easy)" is a lie when the four products are a frozen drink maker, bath towels, jeans, and a mattress topper — that's not curation, that's inventory.
- Nothing here is beauty, nothing is designer-access, nothing is loyalty-tier specific — this reads generic-shopper, not "known Rewards member."
- Price ranges everywhere ($2.99-$9.99, $13-$29.99, $229-$349.99) with no single clear hero offer once you're past the fold — visual hierarchy collapses.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email in half — send the Martha Stewart + fall home styling as its own send, and let the clearance/"picked for you" grab-bag be a separate, honestly-labeled clearance email. Combining them undercuts both.
- 2. Kill "Picked with you in mind" copy entirely unless it's actually personalized — swap it for "Trending in Home & More" so it's honest about being a mixed grid.
- 3. Lead with the Martha Stewart mixer as the hero image at the very top, above the "Home refresh essentials" banner — that's the strongest asset in the whole email and it's buried below a generic maroon CTA block.
- 4. Add a real anchor price or % off next to the Martha Stewart products — right now there's zero pricing on the thing I'm most likely to want.
