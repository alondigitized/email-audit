# New from Marc Jacobs Beauty 💌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift" up top actually caught my eye for a second — a free gift with purchase is a real hook for someone like me who buys at the counter and wants something tangible for it. But then it's a full-on brand launch page, not really a "deal" email — Marc Jacobs Beauty relaunching online and in-store, a new concealer, a wall of product tiles (eyeshadow, lipstick, bronzer, blush, eyeliner, mascara) with zero pricing shown except that one concealer at $36. That's a lot of scrolling for a brand I don't have strong loyalty to yet.
- The model is younger — dewy, bare-faced, early-20s energy — which isn't really speaking to me at 47. And by the time I get to the bottom, there's the now-standard "50% off Select Beauty, TODAY ONLY" banner slapped on, which after yesterday's send with the same exact mechanic (8/31, "$5 off 250 points, 4x points") just feels bolted on rather than intentional.
- I wouldn't click through the whole product wall, but the free-gift banner might get a tap if I'm curious what the "puffy makeup bag" looks like. I'm not forwarding this — it's a brand-marketing email dressed up as a deal, and Sephora's done sharper work than this even this same week.

## What's working

- The free gift banner ("Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift... Stock up... to snag this puffy makeup bag") is a clear, tangible incentive with a visible CTA button.
- New concealer callout is specific — shade-inclusive language ("multiuse formula for medium, skin-like coverage"), price shown ($36), and a "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" badge gives it some exclusivity.
- Product tiles are clean and legible — good spacing, readable product names, no overlapping text or broken images.
- Beauty Insider tier bar at the bottom (Insider / VIB / Rouge) at least nods to the loyalty structure, even buried down there.

## What's weak

- No real "why should I care" beyond gift-with-purchase — this reads like a catalog wall, not a curated recommendation, and there's a LOT of product before any relevance signal for me.
- The hero model skews noticeably younger than my demographic — no diversity in age represented at all, which is a miss compared to how Nordstrom's counter events actually mix up their casting.
- The 50% off banner at the bottom feels tacked on and disconnected from the Marc Jacobs content above it — two unrelated offers crammed into one email, no cohesive story.
- Zero points/Insider-tier personalization anywhere near the top — after five sends in under two weeks hammering "0 points" language, this one skips it entirely, which is inconsistent and confusing.
- "Exclusions/terms apply" sits right under both offers (free gift AND the 50% off) — that's the same fine-print-adjacent-to-offer pattern that's dinged Sephora before.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the gift-with-purchase mechanics up front — tell me the spend threshold to unlock the bag, not just "stock up," so I know if I'm already there.
- 2. Cut the product wall from six tiles to two or three "editor's picks" — I don't need every SKU in the line, I need the ones worth trying.
- 3. Split this into two emails or pick one story — the Marc Jacobs launch and the 50% off Select Beauty banner compete with each other and dilute both.
- 4. Show my actual points balance or Insider tier status near the top — Neiman's done this better lately by at least tying the offer to a spend tier I can picture myself hitting.
- 5. Cast a model who reads a little closer to a Gen X counter regular, even in a secondary shot — Saks does this better when they run multi-generational beauty content.
