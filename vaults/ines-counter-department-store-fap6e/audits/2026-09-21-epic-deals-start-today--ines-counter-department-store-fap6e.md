# 🎉 EPIC DEALS START TODAY 🎉
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- Well, here we go again — the fifth Kohl's email I've logged this week, and it's not even a new idea, it's just "Epic Deals" slapped on top of the same 40%-off DNA I've been staring at since Saturday. This is a wall. A literal, scroll-forever wall of 50% off Carter's, 60% off home, 30% off toys, 30% off IT clothing, 40% off dresses, 24.99 flannels, then a hot-pink "CLEARANCE Save up to 70%" banner, then MORE coupon barcodes at the bottom. I get that Kohl's business model is "confetti of percentages," but there is no single hero here — my eye has nowhere to land, it just bounces category to category like a flyer stapled to six other flyers.
- The one thing that did register: "Kohl's Rewards Member · Earn 3x Rewards through Sept 27" up top, which at least acknowledges I'm a member and not a cold lead. But compare that to Macy's "confirm now for extra 25% off" — annoying urgency theater, sure, but at least it was ONE offer. This is the opposite problem: too many offers, zero focus. Sephora's fall routine email at least gave me one seasonal narrative with a real face; this gives me a spreadsheet dressed as a hero banner.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking anything specific because there's no specific thing to click — it's a Home/Women/Men/Kids nav bar wearing a coupon costume. Marking as read, not forwarding. If my daughter needed Carter's kids' clothes I might screenshot just that one tile for her, but that's about it.

## What's working

- The "KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn 3x Rewards" banner up top at least treats me like a known customer, not a stranger.
- The Carter's 50% off tile and the flannel 24.99 tile have clean, readable pricing with real strike-through comparison prices — that's honest pricing, not buried fine print.
- Kohl's Cash and the $10-off coupon calls are visible with barcodes ready to scan in-store, which matches how I actually behave — I buy at the event, I use the physical coupon.

## What's weak

- There is no single hero offer — six-plus "EPIC DEAL" tiles stacked back to back is Macy's-catalog-overload energy, except Macy's at least segments by day; this is all of them at once.
- The subject and hero say "EPIC DEALS START TODAY" but there's no one epic thing shown — it's just the category nav repeated as a banner, which is stale creative I've basically seen from Kohl's all month.
- Zero demographic signal — no models styled toward a 47-year-old woman, just product flat-lays and a couple in loungewear that skews younger and more casual than my beauty-counter, structured-blazer sensibility.
- The clearance banner and the coupon barcodes at the bottom fight each other for "last important thing" — by the time I've scrolled that far I've forgotten what the original subject line promised.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE epic deal for the hero — the 60% off Home deal is the strongest number here — and build the top fold entirely around it, pushing the category grid below the fold.
- 2. Cut the tile count by half. Six "EPIC DEAL" boxes in a row isn't epic, it's exhausting — Sephora proved one clear seasonal moment beats ten stacked promos.
- 3. Add one lifestyle image that actually looks like it's for a Gen X shopper — not just the loungewear couple — so I feel personally invited, not just present in the algorithm.
- 4. Move the Rewards 3x/coupon math higher and make it one clean callout instead of three separate barcode blocks scattered through the email.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, towels, and more — plus Kohl's Cash on top`
