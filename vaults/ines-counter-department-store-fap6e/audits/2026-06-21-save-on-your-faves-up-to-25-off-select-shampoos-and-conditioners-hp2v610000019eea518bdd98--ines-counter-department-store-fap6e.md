# Save on your faves: up to 25% off select shampoos and conditioners 🌟
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Two days. That is how long it took Sephora to send me the same hair-care sale with a different subject line. The June 19 "Woo-hoo 🎉 Up to 25% off select shampoos and conditioners" is sitting three positions above this in my inbox right now, and the offer is identical — same percentage, same category, same "select" caveat. The swap from emojis-in-subject to a star and a "faves" hook doesn't disguise the rerun. I recognized it before I finished the subject line.
- Inside, the email opens with a solid black hero — "EVERYDAY ICONS," clean layout, model with gorgeous hair, a proper SHOP NOW button. That part works. But then it does what the June 20 send did, the one I counted seven modules in: brushes ranking, a product grid, a notification opt-in prompt, a Kérastase new-arrivals section, a Best of Skincare block, Beauty Insider points — and by that point I've stopped looking. The email is trying to be a department store and a flash-sale mailer at the same time.
- I would not click. I would not forward it. I'd mark it read in half a second and make a mental note that Sephora's sale fatigue problem is getting worse, not better.

## What's working

- **The hero is genuinely clean.** Black background, white type, one offer, one model, one button — that first scroll-stop moment earns its keep.
- **Rouge member callout at the top banner.** The fast-shipping / free gift perk is right where I see it without hunting. Saks rarely makes their loyalty tier feel this visible in the first three pixels.
- **The #71 brush ranking is a smart credibility anchor.** A numbered best-seller rank makes the product feel earned, not just promoted — that's more persuasive than Neiman Marcus shouting "Private Designer Sale" with no proof behind it.
- **Kérastase new-arrivals section.** A new SKU drop from a prestige hair brand is actual news, and it doesn't feel like a filler module the way the skincare grid does.

## What's weak

- **This is a repeat offer, two days later, no disguise.** The June 19 email had the same discount, same category, same "select" qualifier. There is no reframe, no escalation, no new product hook that justifies the resend. Cadence trust is real, and Sephora is burning through mine.
- **Seven-plus modules again.** I said it after the June 20 send and I'll say it again — the email can't decide if it's a hair-care flash sale or a general catalog. The "Hear about the Important Stuff First" notification opt-in block buried mid-scroll is the clearest sign that editorial discipline has left the building.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest button copy they make.** Kohl's managed "TODAY ONLY: 40% off" with a time pressure hook; this email gives me a verb and nothing else.
- **No deadline visible.** The June 11 "STARTS TODAY" email at least told me the clock. This one floats — is it one day? the weekend? Forever? "Select" + no end date = I'll look later, and later never comes.
- **The skincare grid competes with the hair-care hero.** If the send is about shampoos and conditioners, the Best of Skincare module dilutes the message and pulls attention sideways.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four modules.** Keep hero, brushes rank, Kérastase launch, Beauty Insider. Drop the notification opt-in, the skincare grid, and anything else that doesn't serve the shampoo/conditioner story. The June 11 email worked at 9/10 partly because it wasn't trying to sell me everything at once.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" turns a browseable email into an actionable one. Kohl's got an 8 from me on Father's Day largely because the clock was real and visible.
- 3. **Reframe the resend.** If Sephora is going to run this offer twice in 48 hours, make it obvious it's the last chance — "Final day: your hair-care 25% off ends tonight" is a different email than a quiet reshoot of Friday's send.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "Shop Kérastase Now" or "Shop Your Hair Faves" beats a generic SHOP NOW on a targeted send.
- 5. **Swap "everyday icons" framing for something tied to summer.** This is June. Hair humidity, frizz control, heat protection are all real seasonal hooks sitting unused.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 25% off shampoos + conditioners ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Kérastase moment: up to 25% off hair faves`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rouge members ship free — grab your shampoo picks before the offer closes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kérastase just dropped. Plus 25% off select conditioners through tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** (line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`) — blocks user-initiated zoom on iOS. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by Gmail's accessibility linter. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

- **No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element** — the opening tag is `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">` with no `lang="en"`. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) cannot determine language for pronunciation. Add `lang="en"`.

- **`text-size-adjust: none` applied to nearly every element** — the selector `div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td` globally disables font scaling. This suppresses the OS-level accessibility setting for users who rely on larger text. Scope this only to elements where auto-sizing actually breaks layout (typically just `td`).

- **`<meta name="robots" content="no index">` is malformed** — the correct value is `noindex` (no space). While search engines don't index raw email HTML, some ESPs cache a web-view version; the malformed value means the intended no-index directive is silently ignored.

- **`<title>Sephora</title>`** — generic, not email-specific. Web-view and some clients display this as the tab/window title. Minor, but `Save on Your Faves | Sephora` would be more precise.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
