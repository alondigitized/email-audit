# New at The Designer Sale: Up to 50% off Arkis, Cinq a Sept, and more!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because I always open Neiman Marcus, not because the subject made me feel anything. After yesterday's Beauty Event email — the one I would have printed out and taped to my bathroom mirror — this one lands like a Tuesday morning memo. A red hero, a percentage, a grid of small product images. I've seen this layout from NM at least twice this week. "The Designer Sale" is an evergreen Neiman Marcus fixture and the creative reflects that: solid, safe, and forgettable.
- What nags at me is the Men's section. Half the CTA buttons below the product grid send me somewhere I will never go. I am not buying men's shoes. I am not forwarding this to someone who buys men's shoes. That real estate feels like NM is speaking to their database, not to me personally.
- The 15% text-list sign-up at the bottom is where I stopped cold. I'm already on every NM list there is — I got the Beauty Event GWP email, I got the gift card tiers, I got the June Edit. Asking me to "claim" 15% off by joining their texts reads like they have no idea who I am. Mark as read. Would not forward.

## What's working

- The hero is decisive — red field, large type, "50% OFF" visible on first glance, SHOP SALE button centered. No ambiguity about what the email is selling.
- The brand selection in the product grid (Zimmermann, Valentino Garavani, Burberry, Rag & Bone, Akris) is well-targeted for a 47-year-old who shops Saks and Nordstrom. These are not random off-price brands.
- The overall layout is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing screaming for me to "view in a web browser" above the fold. NM's production quality is consistent.
- "Save on these designer styles before they're gone" is a better urgency line than I expected at this tier of send. Quiet FOMO, not shrill FOMO.

## What's weak

- Four emails in roughly 48 hours is one too many. Yesterday's Beauty Event was worth it. "The June Edit" the same day was already coasting. This one confirms the cadence has gone soft — I'm starting to gloss over the sender name.
- The men's CTA buttons (SHOP MEN'S, SHOP MEN'S SHOES) occupy the same visual weight as the women's options. For a subscriber who has never clicked men's in my life, this is pure noise.
- "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" via text sign-up is a cold acquisition ask dropped into what should be a retention send. Macy's ran a better-executed version of loyalty reinforcement in their confirmation email last week — at least that one felt like it was for existing cardholders.
- "Up to 50% off" with "Exclusions Apply" in small type under the button is the standard NM boilerplate, but combined with the vague "LIMITED TIME" (no end date), the offer feels slippery. Sephora at least gives me a specific end date on sale events.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Men's CTAs or relegate them to a single collapsed row.** If segmentation is working, women's subscribers shouldn't see equal billing for men's categories. Give that space to a curated "Top picks for you" module or a beauty entry point.
- 2. **Replace "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" with an InCircle loyalty acknowledgment.** I want to know my points balance is going somewhere. "InCircle members: your points are doubling this weekend" would have made me click before I finished reading. Kohl's gets this right almost every send — they anchor the offer in loyalty mechanics instead of cold acquisition.
- 3. **Add a deadline to "LIMITED TIME."** One line: "Ends Sunday, June 15" changes the calculus entirely. As it stands I'll plan to look later and then forget entirely.
- 4. **Lead the subject with the brand names, not the sale frame.** "Zimmermann, Valentino, Rag & Bone — up to 50% off" is more click-worthy than a sale category name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Valentino, Rag & Bone — up to 50% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off the designers you actually want. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals just dropped into The Designer Sale — Burberry, Akris, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your InCircle points go further this weekend. Shop before Sunday.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus `e.neimanmarcus.com`
**Subject:** New at The Designer Sale: Up to 50% off Arkis, Cinq a Sept, and more!
**Audited:** 2026-06-09

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The send infrastructure is sound (Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript, standard ESP redirect chain), but the template carries unresolved AMPscript variable risks, a silently commented-out identity hash, and missing compliance headers that will flag in Gmail/Apple Mail one-click unsubscribe enforcement. Plain-text body is URL-dominated, creating a deliverability signal risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links not probed**
QA skipped all 26 click-redirect URLs. No dead-link confirmation is possible for this send. Blind spot covers all product CTAs and nav links.

**`@refid` is a no-op CONCAT**
```ampscript
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string does nothing. If the original intent was to append a suffix (e.g., a content block identifier), it is silently dropped. Any downstream attribution relying on `@refid` variants will show undifferentiated campaign IDs.

**`@user` hash is commented out**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This AMPscript assignment is inside an HTML comment, so it never executes. If `@user` is referenced elsewhere in the template (e.g., for identity-stitching in tracking pixels or personalization lookups), it resolves to null/empty at render time. This silently breaks any SHA-256 hashed-email downstream integrations (e.g., clean room matching, onsite personalization).

**CRM Lookup failure is silent**
```ampscript
@uEm  = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm",   "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
AMPscript `Lookup()` returns empty string on miss with no error. If a subscriber is absent from `NM_CRM_Feed` (e.g., new opt-ins, suppressed records), `@uEm` and `@cmdid` populate as empty strings into query parameters — producing malformed tracking URLs of the form `...&uEm=&cmdid=`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code. No rendering impact currently, but signals the template has been patched across multiple campaigns without cleanup. Any future editor uncommenting the wrong line would break mobile scaling.

**Fragile `*[class=hide]` media-query selector pattern**
```css
*[class=hide] { display: none !important; }
```
This attribute-selector pattern fails in Outlook desktop (all versions), which ignores `display:none` on table elements regardless of media query. Content intended as desktop-only that uses `class="hide"` will show on Outlook mobile. This is a known pattern choice but worth flagging if Outlook is a significant client share for this list.

**Duplicate breakpoints for responsive grid**
Two separate `<style>` blocks define conflicting responsive rules: one at `max-width: 600px` and another at `max-width: 480px/520px`. No functional conflict observed in the truncated source, but the split-block pattern creates maintenance risk (a future editor modifying one block may not find the other).

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, no client impact. Clean up for hygiene.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@emailid` has two resolution paths with no fallback guard**
```ampscript
@emailid (from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New:
  Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
```
The comment documents two code paths but the HTML source doesn't show a conditional guard (`IF EMPTY(@emailid) THEN ... ENDIF`). If the variable comes from the sendable DE, a null value is never caught by the fallback Lookup. An empty `@emailid` in a tracking pixel or URL would corrupt session attribution.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment is a manual process gate**
The template comment instructs the sender to manually select a deployment scenario. This is a human error vector; there is no code enforcement preventing both or neither scenario from being active in a given send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** *(QA WARN)*
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to display the one-click unsubscribe button in the client UI. Its absence increases spam-complaint rate and, as of Gmail's 2024 bulk sender requirements (>5k/day), is a compliance violation for high-volume sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** *(QA WARN — RFC 8058)*
One-click machine unsubscribe (the `POST` method required by Gmail's enforcement) is not confirmed present. Without it, Gmail downgrades to the less reliable `mailto:` unsubscribe path.

**Note:** Both headers may be injected by the ESP relay and simply not captured by the AgentMail intercept. Verify directly against raw headers in a Gmail "Show original" view before treating as a confirmed send defect.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown** *(QA WARN)*
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from available data. Verify via Gmail "Show original" — expected to show `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass` for a major ESP send; if not, escalate to deliverability.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameter integrity cannot be confirmed**
All 26 outbound links were redirected through a click-tracking domain and not probed. UTM presence, correctness, and landing page alignment are unverified. The `@refid`/`@cmdid` no-op and potential null-value issues (see §2) compound this — even if UTMs are present in the redirect chain, their values may be empty strings for a subset of recipients.

**No issues found** with the sending domain itself (`e.neimanmarcus.com`) — it is a dedicated ESP subdomain, consistent with standard NM infrastructure.
