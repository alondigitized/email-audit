# Save today: $50–$200 off your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- Neiman's again, with the same $200 ladder for the fifth or sixth time in a week. The lavender hero is clean and the offer is readable, so I know what I'm getting in about two seconds. But I got "Grab it now" on 10/6, "You could save" on 10/7, and "Want $50–$200 off" just yesterday morning. Now it's "Save today." The offer hasn't changed, only the verb has. That repetition makes me trust the urgency less, even though it ends tonight at 10 PM CT.
- The product grid is the Neiman's I like. Mac Duggal, Akris punto, Roger Vivier, Eton and Stuart Weitzman are real designer names, and the tweed jacket and boot are things I'd actually look at. But nothing here is a beauty counter moment, and that's where I spend with Neiman's. Beauty and fragrance aren't even in the shop buttons.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I already needed something. I wouldn't forward it. It's the fifth near-identical send, so I'd skim it and mark it read.

## What's working

- The "UP TO $200 OFF" hero is big and calm. It doesn't shout, and the code NMCIRCLE sits right under it.
- The spend ladder ($200→$50, $500→$100, $750→$150, $1,000+→$200) makes the math clear. I can see exactly what I'd get without reading the fine print.
- The "Triple Points" black bar respects me as a loyalty member. Points matter to me, and I won't use a card that doesn't give them back.
- The designer grid has real names and sensible pieces, including a tweed jacket, a knee-high boot and a Vivier clutch. It reads as designer access, not clearance.
- Free shipping at $150+ with a code is a useful second offer, and the blue block is clearly separate from the hero.

## What's weak

- This is the fifth or sixth send of the same ladder since 10/6. The "limited time" label no longer feels limited, and I know another one is coming tomorrow.
- The beauty category is missing. The shop buttons list clothing, men's, shoes, jewelry, handbags and home, but no beauty. As a counter regular, I feel like a stranger here.
- The fine print is a huge wall of small gray text. It includes "Exclusions apply" twice and a note that sale items don't qualify, so the offer feels less honest than the hero suggests.
- There's no hero product. All nine tiles look equal, so my eye has nowhere to land after the offer.
- The three-column grid will be small on my phone. The labels are tiny and the shop buttons are pale lavender with light text, which is hard to read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop sending the same ladder every day. Alternate it with editorial sends like the CHANEL Fall Winter 2026 Collection (10/11) or Saint Laurent's Fall 2026 Collection (10/10). I scored those higher.
- 2. Add a beauty tile and a beauty button. Even one Triple Points beauty product would speak to me.
- 3. Lead with one hero product above the grid, not nine equal tiles.
- 4. Move a 2–3 line version of the terms above the fold, and darken the shop button text.
- 5. Say what's different today. Say "ends tonight at 10 PM CT" and name the Triple Points window (10/6–10/12). Right now the deadline is buried in the footer.
- For comparison, Sephora Insider's "Beauty for literally everybody" at least opened with a category headline. Kohl's buried its savings in a maroon block, but at least that offer was new to me. Macy's "LAST CHANCE" email was loud, but it had a real action to confirm. Neiman's is calmer than all three, but it's also the most repetitive.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Save today: $50–$200 off your purchase"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a mobile media-query layer. No hard QA failures were recorded (0 issues, 3 warnings). The source was truncated, so footer, link, and image checks are incomplete. The main findings are a leaked internal ESP comment block and a zoom-restricting viewport tag.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 33 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, redirect chains, and status codes are **unverified**.
- The truncated source shows no `href` values, so I can't confirm broken links or missing alt text on linked images.
- No other link issues are confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom restriction:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Missing language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers can't pick a pronunciation profile.
- **Brittle selectors:** the mobile CSS uses exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. These fail if an element carries more than one class. They also rely on the `*[class]` hack for the hamburger rules.
- **CSS-only hamburger menu:** the nav depends on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`, a checkbox hack. Support is inconsistent in Gmail and Outlook. Check that the nav stays usable without it, since the truncated source doesn't show the fallback.
- **Small text:** `.footerText` and `.disclaimerText` are 12px, with 16px line-height on the disclaimer. The mobile override `.f12` bumps only elements that carry that class.
- **Leftover code:** commented-out viewport tags remain in `<head>`, and the `<title>` has a trailing space ("Neiman Marcus ").

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked template comment:** the first node of the document is an HTML comment containing internal ESP variable definitions:
  `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) … @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …`
  - It exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It also contains the instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW", plus a stray `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. These look like developer scaffolding that wasn't stripped before send.
  - The Lookup expressions have unbalanced closing parentheses (`@email))`). That is harmless inside a comment, but it suggests the block was pasted unedited.
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible body text. I can't check the truncated remainder.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** not found. The QA tool notes it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is **unconfirmed**.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** not found, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so verify it against the raw message at the origin.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are **unknown**. The QA tool attributes this to the relay.
- The visible source doesn't show the footer, so the unsubscribe link and postal address can't be confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters or landing URLs are visible in the truncated source.
- The 33 links were skipped, so landing-page alignment with the "$50–$200 off" offer is **unverified**.
- The comment references `@refid` and `@base_refid`, so campaign attribution probably runs through a ref ID rather than UTMs. This is inferred, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the `<!--REQUIRED variables … -->` and `<!--/* SET @user … */-->` comments from the production template or at send time. They expose internal DE and field names.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta. Use `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the origin message. Compare against the raw message from the sender, not the relay copy, and check SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
5. Resolve the 33 tracking links against their final destinations. Check for 200 status, offer alignment, and any UTM or ref-ID carry-through.
6. Replace the exact-match `*[class=…]` selectors with `.class` selectors where client support allows. Verify the hamburger nav degrades gracefully.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML so the footer, image alt text, and `href` values can be checked.
