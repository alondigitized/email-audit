# Take 20% off during our Customer Appreciation Event 🥳
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again — Kohl's, and I'm honestly losing count of how many times this week (Wednesday's cozy fall refresh, Sunday's Customer Appreciation Event that apparently is still going, the Sunday and Monday "extra 20%" emails). This is the same three-tier discount wall — 30% cardholder, 20% Rewards, 15% nonmember — in the same little colored boxes at the top, followed by the same "shop by category" pink buttons, the same clearance banner, the same "picked with you in mind" product grid with a slushie maker and bath towels thrown at me like I'm furnishing a dorm room. Nothing here says "Ines" — it says "we email everyone the same thing and change the banner color."
- The outerwear moment with the moody model shots and "these layers are about to get a full-time job" is genuinely the best creative Kohl's has sent me all week — I'll give them that one line of credit. But it's buried under so much category-button clutter and randomly-priced housewares that I scroll right past it. Compare this to Neiman Marcus's "Style Lessons from a Tastemaker" I looked at recently — no urgency banner, no percentage wall, just a stylist and an outfit I could picture wearing. That's the difference between a brand that thinks about me and one that's just moving inventory.
- Verdict: I will not click. I might glance at the outerwear photos for two seconds, but the ladder-of-discounts format has trained me to skim past the top of every Kohl's email now, and there's nothing here that reads as beauty-counter or department-store special-occasion — it's mass-market noise. Not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The outerwear campaign photography (moody, layered, autumn palette) is the one visual moment that doesn't feel like clip-art stock.
- Kohl's Rewards status is called out right at the top ("Status: KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn: 5% REWARDS"), so at least they know I'm a member.
- The clearance banner ("up to 70%") is a clear, legible callout if I were in bargain-hunting mode.
- Kohl's Cash mention gives a loyalty-adjacent hook, even if it's generic.

## What's weak

- The 30%/20%/15% discount ladder is identical in structure and color-coding to the last four Kohl's sends I've reviewed this week — I can predict the layout before I even open it now.
- Way too many category button rows (Women/Men/Kids/Home, twice over, plus Clearance's own set) — it reads like a site directory, not a curated email.
- "Picked with you in mind" then shows me a slushie machine, bath towels, and a mattress topper — nothing about that says "with me in mind," it says "everything in the warehouse."
- No beauty, no special-occasion, no designer-access tone anywhere — as someone who cross-shops Nordstrom, Macy's and Saks for the counter events, this email doesn't even try to speak that language.
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is at minimum my fifth near-identical Kohl's email since Sunday.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the discount-tier boxes down to one clear offer for my segment (I'm a Rewards member — just show me the 20% and drop the cardholder/nonmember boxes that don't apply to me).
- 2. Lead the whole email with the outerwear photography and copy — that's the only asset with actual brand voice; the discount ladder can go below the fold.
- 3. Replace the random "picked with you" grid (slushie maker, towels) with actual purchase-history-based picks, or cut it if there's no real personalization behind it.
- 4. Throttle send frequency — five emails in a week from the same brand with the same skeleton is what makes me stop opening at all.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New outerwear just dropped — plus $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the layers everyone's asking about, 20% off through Sunday.`
