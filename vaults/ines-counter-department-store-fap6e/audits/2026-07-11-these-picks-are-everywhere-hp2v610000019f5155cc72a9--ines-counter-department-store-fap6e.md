# These picks are everywhere
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Seven Sephora emails in eleven days, and this is how the streak lands — a subject line that sounds like something my college roommate would text me about a rumor, followed by a Patrick Starr setting spray as the hero. I opened it because Sephora, fine, but I felt the cadence before I even got inside. Post-Stacking Weekend pivot to editorial mode is a smart strategic move; this execution of it is not.
- What I found inside is a lot. "Hot on Social" as a framing is doing the work here, and for me — a person who shops beauty counters, not TikTok — it is actively pushing me away. Patrick Starr and rhode by Hailey Bieber are both fine brands; they just skew fifteen years younger than I am. If I'm standing at a Saks counter asking about what's trending, I'm asking the beauty associate, not checking Instagram. The 4X Points line is the one thing that made me slow down, because I will always investigate a loyalty multiplier, but by the time I got there I'd already scrolled past four competing sections that all wanted to be the main character.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I would archive this and open the Neiman Marcus email underneath it that offered me up to 70% off designer styles — specific, curated, my world.

## What's working

- **4X Points is visible and in my language.** I never touch a purchase without points on the table, and the loyalty section at least proves Sephora knows I exist as a Insider member. That section stops my thumb.
- **"Get a Mini" leads the email above the fold.** Structurally smart — put something free or promotional first. I just wish I could tell more easily what the mini actually is and with what trigger.
- **Production quality is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, everything renders. After a week of high-volume sends, they haven't let anything go sloppy. That's discipline.
- **"New from rhode" has its own dedicated section.** Even if it's not for me, the containment is clean — I can ignore it cleanly and move on.

## What's weak

- **"Hot on Social" is not how I shop.** This framing signals explicitly that this email is for someone who buys based on viral reach. I buy based on a beauty associate I trust, a brand event, or a loyalty play. Those are three different shopping motivations and none of them are TikTok.
- **Five-plus competing sections with no clear hierarchy.** Get a Mini, Hot on Social, product grid, 4X Points, Bundled Beauty, New from rhode, Beauty Insider footer — each one fighting the others. Nordstrom does one story per send and it feels like a personal recommendation. This feels like a catalog airdrop.
- **After five urgency-based Stacking Weekend emails, the tone whiplash is jarring.** Going from 🚨 "ENDS TODAY" energy to a vague "these picks are everywhere" in three days is a relationship credibility hit. Even the July 8th send at least stayed in beauty editorial mode — this feels like Sephora just pivoted the content calendar and forgot I was watching.
- **No price detail on any hero product.** Even Macy's, in their "extra 25% off Insider" emails, shows me the before-and-after number. I have no idea what the Patrick Starr spray costs without clicking into it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 4X Points as the headline, not the influencer hero.** If there's a loyalty multiplier active, that IS the email. Everything else is supporting content. Macy's structures their Insider emails this way — the benefit is the headline, the products are the scroll.
- 2. **Collapse to one story.** Either a loyalty send (4X Points + here's what to buy with it) OR a curated edit of three elevated picks. Not both plus three more sections. Neiman Marcus sent one hero, one price, one CTA this week — it read like an invitation. This reads like a mall.
- 3. **Age up the hero.** Patrick Starr and Hailey Bieber as the featured brands for a Beauty Insider who cross-shops Nordstrom and Saks is a mismatch. What's the Charlotte Tilbury restock? The La Mer intro offer? Even an existing favorite brand's new SKU would speak to me.
- 4. **Replace "Hot on Social" with a frame I trust.** "What our beauty associates keep recommending" or "The quiet bestsellers" signals curation, not virality — completely different register for a 47-year-old.
- 5. **Show me a price.** One concrete number somewhere in the hero section. It anchors the scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Three restocks our beauty clients keep requesting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Multiply your points on the picks our counter teams actually reach for.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Loyalty rewards, a new launch, and the spray your associates won't stop recommending.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WCAG 1.4.4 violation — zoom disabled:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This blocks users with low vision from enlarging text and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1` or using `maximum-scale=5`.

**OS text-size-adjust suppressed globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to 9 element types including `body`. Disabling text-size-adjust at `body` level overrides user accessibility preferences on iOS/Android. Standard practice is `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` (preserve layout without blocking user zoom), not `none`.

**Duplicate CSS rule block:**
The `.appear` responsive rule is defined twice with identical declarations — lines immediately following `.break`:
