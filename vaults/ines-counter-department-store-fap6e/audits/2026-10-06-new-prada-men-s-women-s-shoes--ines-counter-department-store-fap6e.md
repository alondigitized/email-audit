# New Prada men’s & women’s shoes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a quiet, pretty email, and after Neiman's week it's a relief. Earlier today I got "Grab it now: $200 off your purchase," and on 10/5 the Ralph Lauren Fall 2026 send. Today there's no percentage, no code and no countdown. It's a mirrored tray, a Prada derby next to an espresso, and "Pep in Your Step." That is how a department store should talk to someone who knows what she wants.
- But it's a pretty email with no reason to click today. I see no price, no points, no tier benefit, no event. I'm a brand-event buyer, and I won't spend on Prada because the photography is nice. Sephora's "points = rewards" email at least told me what my loyalty was worth. This one tells me nothing about what I get for being a customer.
- I'd read it, enjoy the women's half, and probably not click. The men's derby hero is a miss for me, though the slingback and Mary Jane row is on target. I wouldn't forward it, and I'd leave it unread until I'm in the mood for boots.

## What's working

- The hero is restrained and expensive-looking. The Prada derby on a chrome tray with espresso feels like a counter display, not a flyer.
- "Shop Prada" is a clear outlined button directly under the headline. I know exactly where to tap.
- The slingback, ankle boot and Mary Jane row is the right mix for fall. It feels like someone curated it.
- Nothing shouts. Compare that to Kohl's "DEAL DAYS ALERT 🚨," which has arrived six or seven times since 10/4. This one respects my inbox.
- The "match with a personal style advisor" block reminds me the service side exists, which is the designer-access signal I like.

## What's weak

- No price, no offer and no loyalty hook anywhere. Macy's at least gave me a deadline and a percentage. I'm left to guess why today.
- The hero is a men's derby. The email opens with half its audience, and I'm not that half.
- I can't tell which shoe is which. There are six products in two rows, and none shows a price or a name. The "Shop Now" link goes to the whole Prada category, not the boot or the Mary Jane.
- The links are thin gray underlined text, and the footer links ("Find a store," "Contact us") are small. On a phone those are fiddly tap targets.
- No preview text shows in the render. That leaves no second reason to open.
- No tier or points signal. I won't use a card that doesn't give me points back, and this email never says whether Prada purchases earn any.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one line of loyalty or event value near the hero, such as "InCircle members earn bonus points on Prada through Sunday." That single line would move me from admiring to clicking.
- 2. Split the sends by gender, or lead with the women's row for me. A men's hero shouldn't be the first thing I see.
- 3. Give the six products prices or names, and link each one directly. The ankle boot should go to the ankle boot.
- 4. Make the secondary links real buttons or larger text so they're easy to tap on a phone.
- 5. Write a real preheader that adds information instead of leaving the inbox blank.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Prada fall boots and slingbacks are in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `InCircle: bonus points on new Prada shoes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mid-heel slingbacks, ankle boots and Mary Janes, plus points for members.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fall's best Prada shoes, styled by an advisor if you'd like help.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom restriction:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers have no language hint.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` doesn't describe the message and has trailing whitespace. This is minor.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** a large HTML comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Some clients (older Outlook/IE-based engines) can switch to quirks mode when anything precedes the doctype.
- **Checkbox-hack nav:** the CSS uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` for the mobile hamburger nav. Gmail and some other clients don't support this pattern, so the nav may not work there. I can't confirm it from the truncated source.
- Alt text, image dimensions and tap-target sizes can't be assessed because the body is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the leading ESP/AMPscript template comment before send. It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and variable logic, and it sits ahead of the DOCTYPE.

5. Resolve the 16 tracking redirects with a followed-redirect probe to validate final landing URLs and their UTM parameters.
