# Score Active Sale deals on Nike, adidas, Tek Gear & more 💪
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Well, at least this one's honest — it says "Active Sale," and then it actually shows me activewear: shoes, a woman mid-stretch in a tank, some kids' Adidas tees, a basketball guy in the Tek Gear banner. That's more coherent than the last four Kohl's sends I've opened this week. But "Nike, adidas, Tek Gear" in the subject line — Tek Gear is Kohl's own house brand, not a name-brand co-star, so pairing it with Nike and Adidas in the subject is a little bit of a bait. I clocked that immediately.
- The bigger problem is I've now seen this brand five times in five days and the visual grammar hasn't moved an inch — same navy-to-red diagonal color block, same white sans-serif "UP TO XX% OFF" stacked huge, same black CTA button. Yesterday it was "Ultimate Clearance Event," today it's back again as a second banner below the activewear one. It's the same offer wearing a different hat. I'm not opening this at a register, I'm scrolling past it on my phone between meetings, and it reads as more Kohl's noise, not a beauty-counter or even a "known customer" moment.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I actually needed running shoes — the shoe tile up top isn't bad. Would I forward it to a friend? No. This isn't shareable, it's just Tuesday at Kohl's.

## What's working

- The subject actually matches the content for once — activewear brands are genuinely what's pictured, unlike the vague "Score great deals" line from 2026-08-01.
- My Rewards status bar up top ("Kohl's Rewards Member... Earn 5% Rewards") is a small but real nod that they know I'm enrolled — Sephora's Insider banner does this far better, but at least Kohl's attempts it.
- The three-tile product grid (shoes, apparel, kids' Adidas) gives me actual merchandise to look at instead of just a banner, which is more than some of last week's sends bothered with.
- One clear hero offer — "Up to 40% off Activewear and shoes" — sits above the fold with a real button, "Shop Active Sale."

## What's weak

- Two competing full-width offer banners in one email — 40% off Active Sale, then Ultimate Clearance at 70% off — and I genuinely can't tell which one I'm supposed to act on. Neiman's at least keeps its "1,000+ new arrivals" email to one clean idea, even if it's a little sterile.
- "Coupons do not apply to select brands" sitting right under the 40% off headline is exactly the buried-fine-print energy that makes an offer feel less honest — Sephora's Insider Celebration doesn't undercut its own headline like that.
- Same navy-circle-and-diagonal-block creative I've now seen across four sends this week (07/31 through today) — my eye just glides past it now. Compare that to Sephora, which changes its visual energy send to send and still feels premium.
- Tek Gear billed alongside Nike and Adidas in the subject reads like padding — I'm not a Tek Gear shopper, I'm here for name brands or nothing.
- Nothing here signals I'm a beauty-counter customer at all, obviously, but even purely as an apparel/activewear play, there's zero "your size," "your fit," or loyalty-tier specificity — it's generic to everyone on the list.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero offer for this send — either the 40% Active Sale or the 70% Clearance — and cut the other to a small footer link. Two big red banners fighting for my attention just cancels both out.
- 2. Drop Tek Gear from the subject line, or lead with the name brands only — Tek Gear is house-label and it dilutes the "designer/name-brand" credibility the subject is trying to buy.
- 3. Move the "coupons do not apply to select brands" disclaimer smaller and further from the headline — right now it reads as a catch.
- 4. Vary the creative treatment — even just flipping the diagonal color or swapping the badge shape — because five nearly-identical sends in a week is training me to stop looking.
- 5. Add a Rewards-tier callout tied to the actual offer (e.g., "Rewards members earn extra on Active Sale") rather than just a static status line, so the loyalty mention feels connected to today's deal, not decorative.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, Rewards members earn extra on every activewear pick.`
