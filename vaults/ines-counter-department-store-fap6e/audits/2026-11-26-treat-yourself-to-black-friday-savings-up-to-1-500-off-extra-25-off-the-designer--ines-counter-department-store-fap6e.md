## Executive summary

- It's the black box with the red border again. That's the third Neiman's send I've gotten since yesterday morning. This one stacks all three offers, $1,500 off, an extra 25% off and The Designer Sale, into a single panel. Earlier today's "Shop Black Friday now" email had the $1,500 offer. Yesterday's "Want up to $1,500 off?" send had all three. I've now seen this panel so many times I could draw it from memory.
- The email itself is tidy. The black panel is legible, nothing is broken, and the product grid below it is classy. But the subject line is a run-on of three offers, and the body doesn't tell me which one is mine. Kohl's at least commits to one headline. Sephora's Cyber Week email gave each brand its own big "30% OFF" panel, so I knew where to look.
- Would I click? Probably one of the beauty buttons, since "Includes Beauty" is the only line that speaks to me. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and wait for the Saint Laurent-style roundup that doesn't shout.

## What's working

- The three offers are clearly separated in the hero. Red-bordered boxes for $1,500 and 25%, and a solid red block for the Designer Sale, make it easy to tell them apart.
- "Includes Beauty | Code | THANKFUL" is the line I care about most. It tells me beauty counts and shows me the promo code.
- The nine-product grid (Creed, Valentino Garavani, Jimmy Choo, Ferragamo) looks like designer access. It reads like a department store, not a clearance rack.
- The brand names under each product are legible without zooming, and the "Shop Beauty" button is easy to find and tap.
- Nothing is broken. Images load, and the text and layout are clean on my phone.

## What's weak

- The subject line is a pile-up of three offers joined by "+" signs, and it runs well past what my phone shows.
- The hero offers a $1,500 tier, an extra 25% and up to 60% off, and none of them is the clear lead. The "Shop All" button is generic, and the exclusions are hiding in tiny type at the bottom.
- The $250 minimum and the Dec 2 versus Nov 28 end dates are only in the fine print. That's what I'd call buried. The real "up to $1,500 off" depends on a huge purchase, and the email doesn't say so up front.
- Nothing recognizes me as a customer. There's no tier, no points, no beauty event, no member benefit. Macy's at least makes me "confirm" something that feels like mine.
- The deadline isn't in the hero, so the urgency I'm supposed to feel isn't there. The products have no prices, and the grid is mostly eveningwear and boots that don't match what I'd buy for beauty.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer, with the $1,500 tier and its $250 minimum stated right in the hero. Move "Ends 11/28" out of the fine print and into the hero.
- 2. Give the beauty offer its own panel and button, the way Sephora gave each brand its own panel. I'd click that.
- 3. Segment by category. Send me the beauty and fragrance version, not a dress and boot grid. Show a loyalty line like "InCircle members: extra points" if that applies.
- 4. Cut the repeat sends. After a week of the same panel, one fresh creative like the Saint Laurent roundup beats three reskins.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus Black Friday Email

The HTML I received is truncated, so body links, the footer and the unsubscribe markup could not be inspected. Findings below are limited to the visible `<head>`, the leading comment and the QA output.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email ships with an unstripped Salesforce Marketing Cloud authoring comment that exposes internal data-extension names and lookup logic. It also has a few head-level accessibility gaps. The QA warnings on headers and tracking links are inconclusive because of the AgentMail relay and the HTTP-probe skip.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 29 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe (click-redirect domains), so their destinations and status are **unverified**. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- The plain-text part is 5488/7424 characters (about 74%) URLs. This exceeds the 70% heuristic and probably comes from long tracking URLs. It is a minor spam-filter risk.
- I could not inspect body `<a href>` values because the source is truncated, so I can't confirm broken links, missing `alt` text or mismatched hrefs.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on supporting clients, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right language.
- The HTML comment sits **before** the `<!DOCTYPE>`. In IE ≤9-based and some legacy renderers, content before the doctype triggers quirks mode. This is low impact for email, but it is avoidable.
- `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- The mobile rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=hide]`. They silently fail on any element that carries more than one class.
- The hamburger nav uses the checkbox hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). It only works in clients that support `:checked`, such as Apple Mail and iOS Mail. It is not keyboard-accessible, and Gmail and Outlook will ignore it. I can't confirm a fallback because the body is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Confirmed issue:** The leading comment is raw template scaffolding and was not stripped at send time:
  `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) ... Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`
  - This discloses internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) and an unresolved scenario choice ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW").
  - The visible text also shows a mismatched paren in the `@user` lookup. This suggests the comment is stale documentation, but it still shouldn't ship.
- No unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens appear in the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. Verify against the raw message headers. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both.
- `Authentication-Results` is absent, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment is unknown. Check on the raw message.
- The visible body footer, postal address and unsubscribe link are outside the truncated source, so I can't assess CAN-SPAM body requirements.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Not assessable. No hrefs, UTM parameters or landing URLs are visible in the truncated source. The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the comment suggest campaign tracking happens through a reference ID, but I can't confirm that. The 29 skipped tracking links also block redirect-chain verification.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC authoring comment from the production template or build step. It leaks data-model details and sits before the doctype.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the raw message, bypassing the relay. Also confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass.
4. Re-run QA with the full HTML and a resolver for the tracking domain, so redirect destinations, UTM parameters and the footer can be audited.
5. Switch the mobile selectors to `[class~=…]` or plain class selectors, and provide a non-checkbox fallback for the nav.
6. Trim the plain-text alternative: shorten the tracking URLs or reduce the number of links.
