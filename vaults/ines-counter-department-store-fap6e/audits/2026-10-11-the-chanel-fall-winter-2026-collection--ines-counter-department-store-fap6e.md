# The CHANEL Fall Winter 2026 Collection
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's I like. After a week of the $200-off ladder (10/6 "Grab it now: $200 off your purchase," 10/7 "You could save $50–$200," then 10/10 and this morning's "Want $50–$200 off your purchase today?"), a CHANEL editorial with no coupon in sight is a relief. It's quiet and it respects my intelligence. The pink bouclé-style jacket, the croissant-shaped croc bag and the flowers behind the model look like a magazine page, not a flyer.
- It also gives me almost nothing to act on. There's no price, no piece name, no InCircle points and no "in boutiques" detail beyond those two words. I'm a beauty-counter regular who buys at the brand event, so "in boutiques" tells me to go somewhere else to buy it. I'd read it, enjoy it, maybe click "See more accessories" out of curiosity, and not forward it. Sixth Neiman's send in six days, but this one at least earns its place.

## What's working

- The hero image is gorgeous and uncluttered. The eye goes straight to the model and the bag.
- No shouting. The CHANEL wordmark and one short paragraph read like a boutique, which is how I want to be addressed.
- After the $200 ladder, this is the first Neiman's send this week that doesn't treat me like a coupon-clipper.
- The three bordered buttons are big and stacked. They're easy to tap with a thumb, and "See more accessories" is exactly where my eye went.
- The footer is tidy: store finder, personal style advisor, account link.

## What's weak

- Nothing says what's actually available. "In boutiques" with no Neiman Marcus store or online availability makes me wonder whether I can even buy this from Neiman's.
- No points or InCircle mention anywhere. I don't put a card down without points back, and Neiman's usually knows that, so the omission feels like a stranger's email.
- The three CTAs all go to broad categories. I'd have to click and hunt for the jacket I just saw.
- The model is lovely but young, and nothing in the send speaks to someone my age or what I'd wear it with.
- The paragraph ("function and fiction… paradoxes") is brand-speak. It's pretty, but it doesn't help me decide anything.

## Recommendations

- 1. Link the hero to the actual jacket or bag and name it with a price. "Shop the bag" beats three category buttons. Sephora's "Beauty for literally everybody" at least lets me jump to a product.
- 2. Add one line about InCircle points or an early-access window. Macy's leads with its offer in the first inch, and I'd take a points tease over silence.
- 3. Say where it can be bought: Neiman's boutique, online or by appointment. "In boutiques" alone is a dead end.
- 4. Cut the three buttons to one primary CTA and a text link. Kohl's buries me in options too, just with coupons.
- 5. Add a personal style advisor nudge right under the CTAs, since I'm the type who books one for a big purchase.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional build from Salesforce Marketing Cloud. The delivered source still contains internal developer comments, and the viewport meta blocks zoom. The QA run found 0 hard failures and 4 warnings, and most of those come from the capture path, not the email. The HTML was truncated, so anything past the `<style>` blocks is unverified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **17 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so their destinations, redirect chains and HTTP status are unverified.
- **The plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 3216 of 3709 characters (87%) are URLs, which means raw tracking URLs are printed in the text alternative. Spam filters can score this negatively, and the text part is hard to read.
- **No link problems are visible in the truncated source.**

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **The `<title>` is generic.** It reads `Neiman Marcus ` (with a trailing space) and doesn't match the subject line.
- **Fragile responsive selectors.** The rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=mobile]`. They break if a second class is ever added to those elements.
- **Hamburger menu depends on a checkbox hack.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules only work in clients that support `:checked` (Apple Mail, iOS Mail and similar). Gmail and Outlook ignore them, so the menu needs a fallback. I couldn't confirm one exists in the truncated HTML.
- **Dead code in the head.** Three commented-out viewport meta tags remain.
- **Alt text and image dimensions are unverifiable.** They fall after the truncation point.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Unrendered template instructions ship in the delivered HTML.** The first comment block lists the following (and ends with `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`):
  - the required variables `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid`
  - the `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …)` and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", …)` calls
  - a leftover `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`
- **This exposes internal data-extension names and field names** (`NM_CRM_Feed`, `uEm`, `cmdid`) to every recipient. It also shows the template wasn't cleaned before send.
- **Visible merge tokens or fallbacks can't be checked.** The truncated source doesn't show any. The comment shows `@user`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid` are used somewhere, probably in link parameters, but I couldn't verify they resolved.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are missing.** QA couldn't find either header. QA itself says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders, so check the original message.
- **`Authentication-Results` is missing.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. QA expects this from the relay, so it isn't evidence of a sender problem.
- **The body footer is unverified.** The unsubscribe link, physical postal address and sender identification all fall after the truncation point. The From address (`NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com`) is a clear, consistent sending subdomain.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Insufficient data. No hrefs, UTM parameters or landing pages are in the truncated source, and QA skipped probing all 17 tracking links. The `@refid` / `@base_refid` variables suggest campaign tagging goes through a refid parameter, but I couldn't confirm it.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Remove the template comments before send.** Strip the `REQUIRED variables … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` block and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` comment in the SFMC build or a post-render step. This stops the data-extension names leaking and keeps the HTML smaller.
2. **Drop `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1` is enough). Delete the commented-out viewport tags too.
3. **Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"`** to `<html>`. Make `<title>` match the subject and remove the trailing space.
4. **Check the plain-text part.** Cut the printed URLs to a few key links and put readable text around them.
5. **Re-run QA against the original message.** Use the raw MIME or a seed inbox that preserves headers, and confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment) for `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
6. **Run a full-source pass.** Resolve the 17 tracking redirects to their final URLs and check the status, UTM or refid parameters and landing-page match. Also check the footer unsubscribe link and postal address, image alt text, and the hamburger-menu fallback for Gmail and Outlook.
