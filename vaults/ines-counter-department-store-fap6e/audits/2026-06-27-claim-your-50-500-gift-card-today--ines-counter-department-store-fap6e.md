# Claim your $50–$500 gift card today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- I opened it — and then I sighed out loud. Because here we are again. Three days ago Neiman Marcus told me my gift card was "already here." Two days ago they asked me to "shop now & earn" the same card. Now the subject says "Claim your $50–$500 gift card today" — implying I'm sitting on something I just need to collect — but the email opens with "EARN A $50–$500 GIFT CARD." That's not a claim. That's a purchase requirement. The subject is doing a little bait-and-switch, and I noticed it immediately.
- That said, the email itself is clean. The spend tiers are right there above the fold, the NMJUNE code is visible, and the brand grid — Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, FRAME, Veronica Beard — reminds me why I still open these. It's luxury, it's my zone, and the layout doesn't fight me.
- Would I click? Maybe — but only because I'm already mid-consideration on a FRAME denim and I know June ends in three days, making the "NMJUNE" code actually credible this time. Would I forward it? No. This is the fourth variation of the same hook this month. I'd feel embarrassed sending it.

## What's working

- **Spend-tier table is front and center.** $500 spend = $25 back, $1,000+ = $500 — laid out in a grid I can scan in two seconds. That's the right way to present a tiered offer.
- **"NMJUNE" code is visible.** I don't have to hunt for it. It's right there under the headline, which means I can screenshot it and close the email.
- **Brand grid earns attention.** Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, Veronica Beard, Theory, SIMKHAI — those names do work on me. It reads like a curated floor, not a clearance rack.
- **"SHOP & EARN" button is unambiguous.** Clear verb-noun CTA, good contrast, no mystery about where it goes.
- **No visual breakage.** Layout is clean, images loaded, text doesn't overlap. Compared to the Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email I saw recently, which felt like it was wearing three outfits at once, this is composed.

## What's weak

- **"Claim" in the subject vs. "Earn" in the body is a lie of emphasis.** I've been burned by this pattern enough times to clock it immediately. Claim implies I already have something. Earn means I need to spend $500 minimum. That's a meaningful difference.
- **Fourth rodeo on the same offer.** The June 24 send told me the card was "already here." The June 25 send asked me to earn one. This one says claim it today. I know the game. The urgency is diluted.
- **No beauty anchor whatsoever.** I'm a beauty counter regular. Sephora's "Last chance to save up to 50% on select beauty" email at least knows what I buy. This email has zero beauty product — no skincare, no fragrance, no cosmetics. It treats me as a fashion-only customer.
- **Fine print wall at the bottom.** I can see the promotional gift card terms block — dense, small, unavoidable. It's not buried exactly, but it's a wall of text that reminds me to be skeptical of the offer's conditions.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject-body contract.** If the subject says "claim," the hero should say "claim." If the mechanics require earning, the subject should say "earn" or "unlock." The mismatch is a trust cost that compounds across sends.
- 2. **Add a beauty entry point.** One "Shop Skincare & Beauty" button in the category grid would make this relevant to me and the other women who buy at the beauty counter first, fashion second. Sephora knows what I buy and leads with it. NM is leaving that signal on the table.
- 3. **Show one hero product, not nine brand logos.** The brand thumbnails are fine, but a single aspirational item — a specific FRAME jacket, a Zimmermann dress — with a link would make me click immediately. Right now it's a directory, not a recommendation.
- 4. **Acknowledge the cadence.** Macy's did it clumsily in their confirmation email, but at least they tried to signal they know my history. Something like "Your last chance to use NMJUNE" would land differently than yet another cold re-send.
- 5. **Kill or rewrite the app/style advisor module.** It's buried at the bottom and the design is lightweight. It reads like a checkbox feature, not a real offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport meta tags**
Three conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` declarations are present in comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate historical viewport thrashing. The active tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) is fine; the dead code should be cleaned up to avoid future confusion.

**Dual breakpoint media queries**
The template defines responsive rules at both `max-width: 600px` and `max-width/max-device-width: 480px/520px` in separate `<style>` blocks. This is not a bug but increases specificity conflict risk if the template is modified.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; no rendering impact, but some spam filters flag unnecessary whitespace in headers.

**No issues found** with table-based layout, character encoding (`UTF-8` declared), or iOS auto-link suppression (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` is correctly implemented).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable comment left in production source**
The required-variable block is present as an HTML comment in the live send:
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE) @base_refid ...-->
```
This is documentation-style commentary, not executable AMPscript, so it does not affect rendering. However, it exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) to anyone who views source. This is a minor information-disclosure risk and should be stripped pre-send.

**Scenario selector comment present**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256_EMAIL */ -->
```
This is a dormant AMPscript block inside an HTML comment — it will not execute. If SHA-256 hashing of `@user` is required for any downstream personalization or tracking pixel, this block needs to be activated (remove the outer `<!-- -->`) or confirmed intentionally disabled.

**No unfired merge tokens detected** in the truncated source (no `%%variable%%` literals visible outside of AMPscript blocks).

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** `[WARN]`
The QA relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM does not mandate `List-Unsubscribe`, but major inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) use it for one-click unsubscribe placement. If the header is set by the ESP at send time and simply not forwarded by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay gap — confirm against a raw `.eml` capture. If it is genuinely absent, it should be added.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** `[WARN]`
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is absent or not captured. Gmail and Yahoo have required this for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. This is a compliance gap if Neiman Marcus qualifies as a bulk sender — which, for a national promotional campaign, it almost certainly does.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** `[WARN]`
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain — verify that DKIM is signed for this subdomain and that the `neimanmarcus.com` DMARC policy covers it (or that `e.neimanmarcus.com` has its own aligned record).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 links are wrapped in the ESP click-tracker and were not probed, so UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed. The following should be verified against a decoded sample URL:

- `utm_source` set to the ESP/channel (e.g., `email`)
