- Well, wait — this says Neiman Marcus but that layout, those exact black hero blocks with underlined "Shop" links, three-column product grid, "Match with a personal style advisor" — that's such a dead ringer for the Saks format it's almost funny. Anyway. It's clean, it's quiet, there's no discount language screaming at me, and honestly after a week of Neiman's own gift-card-threshold math and Macy's "LAST CHANCE" urgency theater, a simple "brand new styles just landed" grid is a relief for my eyes even if it does nothing for my wallet.
- But there's no offer here at all — no percentage, no member perk, nothing tying this back to InCircle or any tier I might be sitting in. It's a lookbook, not a marketing push, and eighteen products with zero context (no price, no "why these") makes me do all the work. I'd scroll through out of habit because it's Neiman's and I trust the taste level, but I'm not clicking anything specific — I'd maybe tap "Shop Women's New Arrivals" if I'm bored on the train, and I'm not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- The black "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED" hero is genuinely elegant — no clutter, no coupon code shouting over it, just confident typography.
- Product grid is well-curated for someone like me: Alaïa, Chloe, Alémais, Versace mules — this is the taste level I go to Neiman's for, not the Kohl's-style commodity grid.
- Two clear CTA buttons ("Shop All Women's New Arrivals," "Shop All Men's New Arrivals") break up the grid nicely so I'm not lost.
- No fine-print traps, no exclusion list buried at the bottom like Wednesday's "25% off one item" email — refreshingly honest because there's simply no offer to hide behind an asterisk.

## What's weak

- Zero mention of InCircle status, points, or any loyalty signal — if I'm a member, this email doesn't know it, and that's a miss compared to how hard Sephora and Kohl's lean into tier language even in their weaker sends.
- No prices anywhere. I'm 47, I don't have time to click through eighteen products to find out a Versace mule is $1,400 before I decide if it's worth my time.
- No offer or urgency at all — after the Macy's "confirm now for 25% off" push and Neiman's own gift-card deadline email two days ago, this just feels like inventory, not marketing.
- Men's and women's are mixed in one long scroll with no anchor nav, so on my phone this is a lot of thumb-scrolling to get anywhere useful.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one line of loyalty framing near the top — "InCircle members, shop new arrivals first" — turns this from a catalog into something that acknowledges I'm a repeat customer.
- 2. Put prices under each product name. I'm not clicking blind on a bag or a blazer without knowing the number first.
- 3. Give this a reason to exist beyond "new stuff exists" — early access window, a small perk for browsing, something with urgency, even soft urgency ("first pick before it sells out").
- 4. Trim the grid to 6 hero items with a "see all" link instead of 18 flat thumbnails — Etro's fall email last week did this better with fewer, bigger, more editorial product shots.
