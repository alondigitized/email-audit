# Labor Day savings + $10 off your $25 purchase ... win-win 🤑
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- Fifth Kohl's Labor Day email in my inbox this week, and I felt my thumb reaching for "archive" before I even finished scrolling. It's the exact same $10-off-$25 plus $5-Kohl's-Cash stack I've seen in every single send since Wednesday, now dressed up with a slushie machine and a mattress topper thrown in for good measure. There's genuinely nothing here for someone like me — I'm not shopping Kohl's for jeans and toaster ovens, I'm cross-shopping Nordstrom and Saks for the counter brands, and this reads like a circular ad, not a beauty-adjacent retail email.
- What mildly caught my eye was the "Status: Kohl's Rewards Member" line up top — that's the one gesture toward treating me like a known customer — but it's immediately undercut by a wall of category tiles that has nothing to do with my membership tier or my actual purchase history. Compare that to how Macy's at least dresses its urgency emails in something resembling personalization, or how Sephora leads with an actual sale mechanic up front instead of ten scrolls of merchandise.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Absolutely not — there's nothing forward-worthy here, it's just noise. Mark as read and move on, same as the last four.

## What's working

- The rewards status bar ("Kohl's Rewards Member, Earn 5% Rewards") is at least a nod that they know I'm a member.
- The $10-off-$25 and $5-Kohl's-Cash offers are stated clearly at the top in bold red/pink blocks — no hunting required for the headline mechanic.
- Barcodes at the bottom mean I don't need the app to redeem in-store, which I appreciate as someone who still likes brick-and-mortar returns.

## What's weak

- This is a total category dump — jeans, toaster ovens, towels, luggage, sneakers, a slushie maker — with zero curation toward anyone with actual taste or a defined style, let alone a 47-year-old beauty-counter shopper.
- "Picked with you in mind (your great taste taste makes it easy.)" is almost insulting when the "picked for you" items are a frozen drink maker and a bath towel — that's not personalization, that's a random assortment dressed up in flattering copy.
- No beauty presence at all this time — the 9/4 send at least buried a Sephora + Kohl's beauty callout at the bottom; this one drops it entirely, which is a step backward for anyone like me who'd actually engage with that line.
- Visual hierarchy collapses fast — after the first two offer blocks it's just an endless stack of near-identical red-tag callouts, so nothing after the fold reads as "hero."
- Same offer, same layout, same red/pink palette I've now seen four times this week — there's no reason to open this over Tuesday's or Wednesday's version.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the category-dump format entirely for repeat opens this week — if I've already seen this offer three times, show me something new (clearance highlights, a beauty callout) instead of the identical $10/$5 stack.
- 2. Bring back and elevate the Sephora/beauty tie-in from the 9/4 send — that's the one thing that could actually pull me, a counter shopper, into this email, and it's missing here.
- 3. Make "picked with you in mind" mean something — tie it to browsing or purchase history, not a slushie machine and jeans.
- 4. Trim the email length dramatically — nine+ product blocks before Clearance is fatigue, not persuasion.
- 5. Vary the subject line so it doesn't read as a rerun of Wednesday's near-identical "$10 off your $25 purchase STARTS TODAY" send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Kohl's Cash is stacking up — here's what's new today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Beauty + Labor Day: 50% off Sephora at Kohl's, plus $10 off $25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns just added to Clearance — up to 85% off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sephora at Kohl's: 50% off select beauty, this weekend only`
