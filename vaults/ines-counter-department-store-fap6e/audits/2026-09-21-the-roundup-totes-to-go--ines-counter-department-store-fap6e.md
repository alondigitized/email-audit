# The Roundup: Totes to Go
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a nice change of pace visually — that hero shot with the black Christian Louboutin bag perched on the orange pleated fabric is genuinely editorial, better composed than anything Neiman's sent me in the "Friendly reminder" jewelry email on the 20th. But then I scroll down and realize... there's no price anywhere. Not one number. Six bags, six designer names, zero indication of what any of this costs or whether there's a discount at all.
- That's where this loses me. I don't buy totes on impulse — I buy them at the Neiman's beauty/handbag event when there's a gift-with-purchase or a spend threshold, the same way I've told this brand I like it before (their gift-card mechanic on the 21st I actually respected). This email gives me gorgeous merchandising and nothing to act on. Compare this to Saks, who at least tells me "up to 40% off" even when the styling is tone-deaf — here NM assumes I'll click through on brand names alone.
- I'd flip past this one. Not annoyed, just unmoved — it's a lookbook, not an offer, and I already have six open tabs of actual sales to get through this week.

## What's working

- The hero image is legitimately beautiful — the orange pleated backdrop against the black Louboutin bag is a real style moment, more artful than Neiman's own jewelry send last week.
- "The Roundup: Totes to Go" is a clean, simple concept — a curated edit, not a screaming sale banner.
- Two clear CTA buttons ("Shop Totes" / "Shop Must-Have Handbags") sit right under the hero, so I'm not hunting for where to click.
- Designer names (Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga, Prada) are doing exactly what they should — signaling to someone like me that this is worth a look, the same trick Neiman's Brunello Cucinelli send leaned on.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere in the entire email — not on the hero, not under any of the six product tiles. I have no idea if this is full price or a promotion.
- No offer, no percentage off, no "shop the sale" framing at all — this reads as pure merchandising, not a campaign.
- No loyalty/InCircle callout, and I'm exactly the customer who cares about that.
- Product labels are just designer names with no style name or size/fit detail — I can't tell if the Bottega is a mini or full-size tote without clicking through blind.
- Generic footer CTAs ("Download the app," "Match with a personal style advisor") feel like filler after six unpriced bags — nice touches, but not what gets me to buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add pricing under every product tile.** This is the single biggest lift available — right now I can't self-select by budget, and that's the first thing I do before I click anything.
- 2. **Lead with an offer if one exists**, or reframe as an editorial/lookbook send explicitly (no fake urgency) if there isn't one — right now it's ambiguous which this is.
- 3. **Add an InCircle points or member-pricing callout** — even a small line would make this feel like it's talking to a known customer instead of a cold catalog scroll.
- 4. **Give each bag a style name and one detail line** (size, strap drop, leather type) so I can decide before clicking through.
