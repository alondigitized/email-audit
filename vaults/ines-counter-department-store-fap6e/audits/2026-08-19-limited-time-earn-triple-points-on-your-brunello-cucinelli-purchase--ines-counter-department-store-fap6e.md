"TRIPLE POINTS on Brunello Cucinelli" — I have an InCircle card, I know exactly what triple points means, and I know exactly what Brunello Cucinelli costs, so my brain is already doing the math on that cashmere sweater I've been eyeing. - The single-brand focus is smart — no overload, no "shop 47 categories" grid. ## What's working

- "TRIPLE POINTS" in that oversized serif is the whole pitch and I got it instantly — no scrolling required.
- Single-designer focus keeps it from turning into the Macy's-style everything-dump I complained about with their "confirm now" email.
- The nine-tile product grid is genuinely covetable — that chocolate slip dress and the suede lace-up boots are exactly my closet.
- Fine print is present but tucked at the bottom, not weaponized mid-email like Saks sometimes does with "exclusions apply" bait.
- Loyalty tiering ("President's Circle and Chairman's Circle members earn 15 points per dollar") is spelled out for those of us who track our tier.

## What's weak

- All nine product tiles just say "Brunello Cucinelli" with zero item names or prices — I have to click through blind to know if that blazer is $2,000 or $4,000.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA button in the hero, which is a faint gray flag before I've even scrolled — feels like the fine print wanted a little too much visibility right up front.
- No end-date urgency in the hero itself — I had to dig into the tiny legal block at the bottom to learn it ends 8/27/26.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real price or price range under each product tile — I don't shop blind, and Saks at least shows me the number before I click.
- 2. Move the "Ends 8/27/26" date up into the hero, near the CTA, so the urgency is visible without hunting through legalese.
- 3. Swap the generic "Brunello Cucinelli" caption under each photo for the actual item name (e.g., "Silk Slip Dress," "Suede Chukka Boot") — it's basic product info I need to decide if I'm clicking.
