# Save up to 85% on must-have clearance ➡️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a lot. Kohl's decided to cram a whole department store circular into one email — clearance, denim days, a "denim guide," Halloween pumpkins, and then a jarring "Picked with you in mind" block that puts a $349 frozen drink maker next to a $52 pair of jeans and a memory foam mattress topper. None of that is "picked with me in mind" — that's just four random SKUs with no logic behind them, and slapping that headline on it actually made me trust it less, not more.
- The top is fine — "CLEARANCE, save up to 85% after an extra 50% off" is a real, specific hook, and the Rewards status bar up top ("KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn 5% Rewards") is a nice touch that at least acknowledges I'm not a stranger. But it's the eleventh-ish send this month with this same pink starburst-adjacent color-blocking, and by the time I hit the third row of category buttons I'd already mentally filed it as noise. Compare this to Neiman Marcus's recent "Classic Crossbodies" roundup, which was one clean editorial hero and nothing else — no math, no six sub-sections. This Kohl's send is the opposite instinct: throw everything at the wall.
- Would I click? Maybe, straight to the clearance banner, because 85% off is a real number. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing personal or beauty/style-specific enough here to share, it reads like a store flyer, not a curated pick.

## What's working

- "Save up to 85% after an extra 50% off" is a concrete, specific number up top — that's a real reason to click, unlike Sephora's vague "Tens across the board" tagline from their last send.
- The Rewards member status bar at the very top is a good personalization signal — it tells me Kohl's knows I'm a member before I even scroll.
- The Levi's denim block with "Up to 30% off jeans" is at least a named, recognizable brand with a clear crop of a real product, not a stock lifestyle shot.
- Clean pink/white color system keeps it scannable even though there's a ton of content stacked here.

## What's weak

- "Picked with you in mind" over a $349 slushi machine, a bath towel, jeans, and a mattress topper is not personalization — it's four unrelated clearance dregs, and calling it out that way just highlights how random it is.
- This is basically the same pink-and-white color-blocked layout Kohl's has run for the "Friends & Family 20%" sends I've been getting since 8/28 — different headline, same bones. Cadence fatigue is real at this point.
- Six category button rows (Home/Women/Men/Kids, then Women/Men/Kids again, then Home/Women/Men/Kids/Jewelry/Pet) is redundant — I don't need the same three buttons twice in one scroll.
- The "Denim Guide" section with the model collage feels like it belongs in a different, denim-only email — bolting it onto a clearance blast dilutes both messages.
- No urgency or end date on the clearance offer itself — "Starts Today" tells me nothing about when it ends, unlike Macy's "LAST CHANCE" send which at least had a real deadline mechanic (even if oddly executed).

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends — one clearance email, one denim/guide email. Trying to do both plus a random product grid dilutes the actual 85%-off offer, which is strong enough to stand alone.
- 2. Cut the "Picked with you in mind" block entirely or actually personalize it — if it's not based on my purchase history, don't claim it is; that's the fastest way to lose trust with someone like me who's shopped this brand's tiers for years.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the clearance headline ("Ends Sunday" beats "Starts Today") — urgency needs a close, not just an open.
- 4. Trim the duplicate category button rows down to one clean set.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance: up to 85% off, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your extra 50% off clearance is live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rewards members: stack an extra 50% off clearance now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just dropped across the store`
