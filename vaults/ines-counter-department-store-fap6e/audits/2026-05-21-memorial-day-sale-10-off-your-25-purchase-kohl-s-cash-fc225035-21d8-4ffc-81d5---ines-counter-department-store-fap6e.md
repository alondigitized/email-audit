# Memorial Day Sale ✅ $10 off your $25 purchase ✅ Kohl’s Cash ✅
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Seven. That is my Kohl's email count in the past two weeks, and I have been keeping a mental tally. May 15th: activewear at 15% off. May 16th: summer entertaining, also 15%. May 17th: still 15% but now with Kohl's Cash appended like a bonus track. May 19th finally climbed to 20%. And now this — a flat $10 off $25. I'll give them credit for changing the mechanism: if you spend exactly $25, that's 40% off. That is genuinely better math than anything they've sent this month, and "Memorial Day Sale" is at least a name for the event rather than a perpetual rolling discount with no narrative.
- But the email itself is a full department store in vertical scroll form. Women, men, kids, beauty, clearance at 70% — everything Kohl's carries is stacked in here, each pod fighting for the same real estate. There is no read of me as a specific customer. No beauty angle in the hero. No sense that they know I'm 47 and here for the NYX display and the shoe wall. The hero couple is pleasant but generic; the intimates section further down is closer to my demographic but buried below four other sale pods.
- I will scan this, find the coupon barcode, and close. The Kohl's Cash expiration through Tuesday is the only thing that actually speaks to how I shop — I do not let points expire. I am not forwarding this to anyone.

## What's working

- **$10 off $25 is the strongest single offer they've sent since May 8th.** Switching from a percentage to a flat dollar amount makes the math concrete and the event feel real.
- **"Memorial Day Sale" hero is legible and properly scaled.** The eye lands there first; the hierarchy is logical even in a dense email.
- **Kohl's Cash is visible with a credible deadline — "through Tuesday."** That is the one loyalty signal in here that actually moves me toward the click.
- **Multiple "Shop Now" CTAs** throughout the scroll mean I never have to hunt for a button; they're present after every product pod.
- **Timing is correct.** A Wednesday delivery before Memorial Day weekend is the right cadence for a holiday event launch — even if the seven-email run-up has dulled the moment.

## What's weak

- **The triple-✅ subject line reads like a checklist, not a brand.** Macy's wouldn't send this. Saks absolutely would not. Those checkmark emojis used back-to-back trip the spam-pattern part of my brain before I've even opened the email.
- **No beauty callout, anywhere near the top.** I know Kohl's carries Bliss, e.l.f., and NYX. Sephora led with a personal offer this month — wrong name on the email, but at least a category signal. Kohl's gives me a couple in casual shorts and zero beauty intent.
- **Seven emails in two weeks is inbox abuse.** Even with a new offer, the cadence has flattened the urgency. Memorial Day should feel like an event, not the eighth beat of an ongoing drumline.
- **The subject line is 61 characters.** On my phone the $10 offer gets truncated before "Kohl's Cash" even renders — so the loyalty signal I actually care about disappears in the preview pane.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a beauty pod in the top third of the email.** One row — three products, a headline like "Your Memorial Day beauty picks" — would activate an entire segment they're leaving cold. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Cut the subject line and kill two of the three checkmarks.** One strong signal beats three cluttered ones every time.
- 3. **Add an explicit expiration date to the $10 off coupon bar code.** "Through Tuesday" is stated for Kohl's Cash but the primary offer shows no deadline I can see. That's where the urgency lives.
- 4. **Suppress the Wednesday email for subscribers who opened the May 19th send.** If I already saw 20% off and didn't bite, send me something different — not $10 off $25. Macy's uses confirmed-action triggers; Kohl's could too.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus earn $5 Kohl's Cash through Tuesday, May 27`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beauty, fashion, clearance — one coupon covers it all`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped** — all click URLs route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect domains; the QA probe correctly deferred these and could not confirm final destination health or UTM passthrough.

**Three distinct tracking pixel vendors** are loaded at document open:

- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — MessageGears open pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager (AAM) segment fire
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — secondary open tracker (ESP-level)

This is 4 parallel open-tracking calls on render — no functional bug, but worth noting as a pixel audit surface.

**Malformed tag detected:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The stray `</custom>` closing tag has no matching open tag. Most parsers silently ignore it, but it is invalid HTML and could cause rendering edge cases in strict parsers.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** — all are tracking pixels or dynamic content images served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`. While tracking pixels conventionally carry `alt=""`, the dynamic content images (e.g., `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`) are likely rendered content and should have descriptive alt text for screen-reader compatibility.

Confirmed instances with empty/missing alt on non-pixel images:
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/f441d45d2128ae53.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-to-zoom, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` renders as a blank tab/window title, which affects screen reader announcement when the email opens in a browser view.

**Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import`** for the same two families (Roboto, Montserrat). This is a duplicate load — double HTTP requests on render for clients that execute `<link>` tags.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN) — The relay did not capture this header. If it is absent at the SMTP layer, this is a deliverability risk: Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5K/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. Cannot confirm presence or absence without raw SMTP headers.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (QA WARN, RFC 8058) — One-click unsubscribe (`POST` method) is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Same caveat as above — relay may not have captured it.

**No physical mailing address detected in truncated source** (QA WARN) — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The source is truncated; the address may appear in the footer. This should be verified against the full source.

**`robots` meta tag present:** `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` — correct practice for email web-view pages.

**`referrer` meta present:** `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` — suppresses referrer on outbound clicks, which will cause landing page analytics to show direct traffic rather than email-attributed traffic unless UTM params are used (see §6).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 outbound links pass through `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears click tracking). The probe could not inspect final redirect destinations or confirm UTM parameter presence on landing URLs.

**Known risk:** The `no-referrer` meta (§5 above) means HTTP `Referer` headers will be stripped on click. Any landing page attribution that falls back to referrer parsing will misattribute these sessions. UTM params on destination URLs are the only reliable attribution signal — their presence on the actual `click.s.kohls.com` destination targets is unverifiable from the truncated source.
