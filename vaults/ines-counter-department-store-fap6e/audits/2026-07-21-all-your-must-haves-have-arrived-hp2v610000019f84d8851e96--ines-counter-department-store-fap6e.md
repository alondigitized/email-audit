# All your must-haves have arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Five emails in four days, Sephora. I'm keeping count. That said, when I opened this one I did stop scrolling for a second — "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" with a wall of brand names I actually recognized felt more like flipping through an editorial page than a mass promo blast. Jo Malone London exclusive to Sephora? That's genuinely news. Hung Vanngo and Mary Phillips are insider brands I follow, and the 25% off tucked into the middle section is real money.
- But here's the thing: this email is trying to be everything at once. Counter, catalogue, editorial, event teaser — all in one scroll. By the time I reached Innisfree at the bottom I'd lost the thread. There's a reason a Neiman Marcus SA hands you one item to consider and lets you breathe. Sephora keeps handing me the whole case.
- I'd click the Jo Malone section if I saw a direct link — that's the only thing here that actually feels like my counter. The rest I'd scan, maybe save for later, and never return to. Forward to a friend? No. She'd get the same email tomorrow anyway.

## What's working

- **Jo Malone London exclusive placement** — "NEW | ONLY AT SEPHORA" is the kind of prestige signal that actually earns my attention; NM and Saks don't carry this lineup this way right now.
- **The 25% off offer is real** — visible mid-email, not hidden in footer fine print; that's the price signal that makes me calculate in my head.
- **JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE hero is clean and confident** — the hierarchy lands; I saw the headline before anything else.
- **Celebrity MUA brands (Hung Vanngo, Mary Phillips) feel editorial, not drugstore** — these are counter-credible names that speak to someone who knows the industry.
- **No render bugs** — everything loaded, nothing overlapped, images crisp.

## What's weak

- **Subject gives me nothing to work with** — "All your must-haves have arrived" is the email equivalent of "something good is happening." Neiman Marcus said "25% off one item" and I stopped scrolling for that; this made me shrug and open out of habit.
- **Seven-plus brand sections is a catalogue, not a curated edit** — by Innisfree I'm out. Yesterday's Charlotte Tilbury bag edit had focus; this has quantity.
- **The 25% off has no visible context** — is it one brand? Sitewide? New arrivals only? I can't tell from where it lands in the layout and that friction kills urgency.
- **No Insider tier acknowledgment** — the 7/19 4X points email made points feel like mine; this one treats me like a stranger browsing a homepage. Where's my Rouge/VIB status?
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — fifth email in four days, and the 7/18 soccer-pun email already tested my patience. Sephora is starting to feel like Kohl's.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the prestige hook** — Jo Malone exclusive at Sephora is editorial-worthy on its own. Make that the hero, not buried item five of seven. One brand, one moment.
- 2. **Clarify the 25% offer prominently** — put the terms in the hero subhead or directly under the CTA. "25% off all new arrivals this week" is a sentence that drives clicks; mystery offers don't.
- 3. **Acknowledge Insider tier** — even a "Rouge members, your early access starts now" line at the top changes the entire tone from mass blast to known-customer.
- 4. **Cut to four brand sections max** — quality over quantity. The Sephora bag edit on 7/20 earned a 9 because it had editorial restraint. This email is spending that goodwill.
- 5. **Surface 4X points if that's live** — I see a 4X reference in the upper section and I genuinely cannot tell if that's still running or residual from the 7/19 email. Points are currency to me; make it explicit.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Charlotte Tilbury, Jo Malone, Hung Vanngo — see what's exclusive this week`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Pinch-to-zoom disabled — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming in on mobile. This is a Level AA failure (Resize Text). Fix: remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**[CONFIRMED] Broad `text-size-adjust: none` — WCAG 1.4.4 concern**

```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

Applied to virtually every element. This suppresses OS-level text scaling for users with low vision. The Outlook-specific `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` on `td` is a known workaround, but the wholesale application to `body`, `div`, `a`, and `span` is overly aggressive. Scope it to `td` and `table` only.

The `.appear` class rule is declared twice identically within `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)`, adding unnecessary CSS weight (no functional impact, but signals template hygiene issues).
