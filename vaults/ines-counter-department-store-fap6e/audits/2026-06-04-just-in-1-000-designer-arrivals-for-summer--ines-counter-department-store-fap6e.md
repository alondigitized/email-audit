# Just in: 1,000+ designer arrivals for summer
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Neiman Marcus sent me the gift card event email earlier this morning — up to $1,250 back, concrete tiers, felt like it was trying to earn my attention. Then this walks in right behind it: a pure "look what's new" drop with zero offer attached. No event, no points multiplier, no incentive of any kind to actually spend. The hero is clean — black background, white text, "Brand New Styles Just Landed" — honest at least, but after a gift card send, a full-price arrivals grid is a cold handshake.
- What saves it is the brand curation. Missoni and Lafayette 148 New York in the women's grid are exactly the kind of names that make me pause. These are brands I actually shop. Bottega Veneta jeans and Etro in the second row are also in my wheelhouse. But then I keep scrolling and realize I'm looking at Valentino Garavani printed shirts and Hudson denim — a full men's section that takes up roughly half the email. I am not shopping for a man right now, and that real estate should not be mine to wade through.
- Mark as read. I might circle back to the Missoni piece on my own schedule, but I'm not clicking through today without a reason to swipe.

## What's working

- Missoni and Lafayette 148 New York visible in the first women's product row — two names I recognize and buy, no scrolling required.
- Hero is restrained and clean — no "SALE" banners, no stacked exclamation points, none of the Kohl's "LOWEST PRICES 🔥" energy from the June 1 send I audited.
- "Shop Women's" CTA appears in the hero section itself, before any product grid — the click opportunity lands early.
- Layout is honest: no fake deadlines, no buried exclusions, no bait-and-switch hiding in the fine print.
- The Cartier sunglasses and Prada outerwear in the men's section at least signal this is genuinely designer level, not outlet noise.

## What's weak

- No offer whatsoever. After the gift card event email that landed earlier today, sending me a full-price arrivals drop with zero tie-in feels like two departments not talking to each other.
- Men's content is nearly half the email. As a 47-year-old woman who cross-shops women's contemporary and designer, I did not come here to scroll past Valentino Garavani menswear and Hudson denim.
- "1,000+ arrivals" reads like a stockroom inventory, not a curated edit. Compare this to how Saks handles new arrivals — they typically lead with three to five editorial hero picks. Neiman Marcus is leaning Macy's-volume when it should lean Saks-precision.
- This is the second Neiman Marcus email in my inbox today and at least the fifth in three days. The "A point of Pride" send on June 3 already felt like filler; this one isn't doing enough to justify the slot.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or collapse the men's section.** One row maximum, or a text link at the bottom — "Explore Men's New Arrivals →" — and give the rest of the email to women's.
- 2. **Lead with a curated hero product.** Pick one designer, one piece, tell me why I want it now. Missoni for summer is a story. "1,000+ arrivals" is a warehouse.
- 3. **Tie the arrivals to the gift card event running today.** "New arrivals + earn up to $1,250 back" is a send worth opening twice. Right now these two emails are working against each other on the same date.
- 4. **Add an InCircle hook.** Even one line — "InCircle members earn bonus points on new arrivals this week" — speaks directly to how I decide whether to open my wallet.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer edit just arrived at Neiman Marcus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `InCircle members earn extra points on new arrivals through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Missoni, Bottega Veneta, Cartier — curated new arrivals, just landed.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Just in: 1,000+ designer arrivals for summer"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template is a standard SFMC AMPscript/HTML email with functional CSS structure, but carries several compliance gaps flagged by the relay and unresolved template hygiene issues in the source. The plain-text companion is effectively non-functional due to extreme URL density.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links skipped — destinations unverifiable**
All outbound links pass through a tracking redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped them, meaning no destination URL, UTM parameter, or 404 check was performed on any link in this send. This is a coverage gap, not a bug — but it means broken destinations would not be caught pre-send.

**Plain-text version is 90.5% URLs (5,270 / 5,820 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML and consists nearly entirely of click-tracking URLs. Plain-text clients and spam filters that weight the text/plain part will see an incoherent blob of redirector URLs with no readable content. This also inflates spam scores on content-ratio heuristics.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three dead viewport `<meta>` tags left in source**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are commented-out alternatives from past iterations. Not rendered, but indicative of template drift. The `width=320, initial-scale=0.29` value in particular suggests very old lineage (pre-iPhone 5 era sizing).

**iOS 9 hamburger `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` hack still active**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
This no-op animation loop was a workaround for an iOS 9 rendering bug (2015). iOS 9 has ~0% market share. The animation fires on every render for no purpose.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer document language; some accessibility-aware clients flag this.

**`<title>` tag contains a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Cosmetic only, but visible in Gmail's browser-based preview tab.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable declarations are inside HTML comments in the visible source**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ...-->
```
In SFMC, AMPscript inside `<!-- -->` HTML comments is not executed. The actual executable `%%[ ]%%` blocks that set `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, and `@cmdid` must exist elsewhere in the full (non-truncated) template. If those blocks are missing or conditionally excluded, all downstream personalization tokens would render as empty strings silently.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" comment leaves ambiguity about which `@user` path is live**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This comment notation (`<!--/* */-->`) does not execute in SFMC — it is documentation only. It signals the SHA-256 hashed email path was intended, but without seeing the live AMPscript block, it cannot be confirmed from this source fragment that the correct lookup vs. hash scenario is active.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** *(QA: WARN)*
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Google and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day). Its absence risks inbox placement degradation and is a CAN-SPAM risk factor.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header absent** *(QA: WARN)*
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024. Without it, Gmail will surface a manual unsubscribe prompt instead of the one-click button, increasing spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** *(QA: WARN)*
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable through the relay. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — alignment between the `From:` header domain and DKIM `d=` tag cannot be confirmed from this source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**All 26 destination URLs are behind redirect domains — UTM coverage is unverifiable**
Because the QA probe skipped all tracking links, it is unknown whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended consistently, present at all, or whether any links resolve to 404s. This is the highest-risk gap in this audit: a broken hero CTA would not have been caught.

No further site-continuity findings can be confirmed from the available source.

---
