# New designer styles you’ll love—now up to 70% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is somewhere around email thirty from Neiman Marcus in July, and I say that because I'm still thinking about yesterday's "Tick. Tock. 70% off Last Call" send — which I actually gave an 8 — and the "1,000+ new luxury arrivals" that landed the same morning. Today's subject line stitches both of those together into one phrase and the seam shows. "New designer styles you'll love" is editorial, warm, discovery-forward. "Now up to 70% off" is clearance. Those two things are not the same sentence. If these are styles I love, why are they at 70% off the morning after they were positioned as fresh arrivals?
- Open it and the email is genuinely better than the subject deserves. Red hero, clean typographic hierarchy, extra 25% stacking on top of already-marked-down Last Call pieces — and a product grid anchored by Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, and Isabel Marant. That's a respectable designer lineup. There's some filler (AKEA? Bugabichi?) mixed in that dilutes the luxury signal, and there's zero beauty content, zero InCircle acknowledgment, zero personalization — classic NM bulk blast. But the underlying math is real, and the Balmain jacket alone earns a click from me.
- I'd click — specifically to see if the Manolo or Stuart Weitzman styles are in my size. Forward to a friend? No. Compared to Saks, which sends me two thoughtful emails a week and I open both, NM is training me to reflexively skim for a deal and move on.

## What's working

- **Red hero reads before anything else.** "EXTRA 25% OFF" in white on red registers instantly — no work required. Cleaner than Kohl's, which drowns its Cash mechanics in supporting copy before you get to the number.
- **Stacked discount math is genuinely good.** Extra 25% on top of up to 70% off Last Call is real money, and NM lays it out without the theatrical "PASS CODE ACTIVATED" energy Macy's deployed in its last triggered email.
- **Designer anchor names earn the scroll.** Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, Weekend Max Mara — all in my wheelhouse. Those names in a product grid make me slow down.
- **Layout is clean and mobile-logical.** Logo → nav → hero → grid → category CTAs. My eye follows naturally. No broken images, no layout blowouts.
- **"SHOP SALE" is a real CTA.** Verb plus noun, no "Discover" or "Explore."

## What's weak

- **"New designer styles you'll love" is a bait-and-switch.** These are clearance styles. The body admits it immediately — LAST CALL® — so the subject creates cognitive dissonance at the inbox that erodes trust before the hero even loads.
- **Not a molecule of beauty content.** I'm a beauty-counter regular. NM has one of the most edited fragrance-and-skincare floors in retail and not one product made it in. Sephora at least tried to connect with a product grid, even if last week's Summer Juice Bar send missed the mark.
- **No InCircle signal anywhere.** I hold a card. Whether this stacks with points — or earns triple points — is the difference between buying now and waiting. Silence on loyalty reads like they don't know I exist.
- **Product tier mixing is jarring.** Balmain and Manolo Blahnik next to AKEA and Bugabichi in the same grid is brand whiplash. Either curate to luxury or own the accessible tier — doing both in one row undermines the editorial credibility.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Four or five NM emails in three days. I noticed yesterday and I notice it again today.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the subject-body contradiction.** Don't call clearance styles "new." Lean into the value and the designer names directly.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last Call edit: extra 25% on Balmain, Manolo & more today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off. Extra 25% stacked. The good sizes go first.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your extra 25% before the best sizes disappear — real designer markdown math.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Balmain jacket. Manolo pumps. Stuart Weitzman wedge. All in the Last Call.`
- 2. **Add one beauty row.** Even a single La Mer, Charlotte Tilbury, or Tom Ford Beauty item in the product grid doubles my dwell time. NM carries the inventory — use it.
- 3. **Surface InCircle.** One line: "Earn InCircle points on every Last Call purchase." That's the entire ask. It tells me I'm a known customer, not an anonymous inbox.
- 4. **Tighten the product grid.** Six tight luxury picks instead of nine mixed-tier ones. Curation signals editorial respect. I'd rather see six great things than nine where three make me wonder what happened to the standards.
- 5. **Give the urgency a date.** "Last Call ends Sunday" beats the vague registered trademark pressure of LAST CALL® with no deadline visible in the body.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "New designer styles" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional structure. The primary technical risks are a potentially inert `@user` variable assignment due to double-comment nesting, missing one-click unsubscribe headers, and unverifiable tracking coverage across 26 redirect links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links not probed**
QA flagged all 26 click-redirect URLs as skipped. No broken-link confirmation is possible without following the redirect chain (e.g., `e.neimanmarcus.com` click-tracking domain). These must be manually spot-checked or probed through an authenticated send.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs**
QA finding: 5,225 of 6,058 chars in the plain-text alternative are URLs. This is a strong spam-filter signal. Plain-text versions should be human-readable summaries, not raw link dumps. Likely caused by auto-generated plain-text from the ESP that serializes tracking URLs verbatim.

**`@refid` depends on `@base_refid`**
The template comment shows `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid, "")` — a passthrough with no transformation. If `@base_refid` is empty or unpopulated from the Campaign Calendar DE, all links using `@refid` for tracking will carry a null/empty parameter silently.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
No `lang="en"` is set. Screen readers cannot determine language; this also fails WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1.

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor — but indicates template hygiene issues; preheader/subject tooling that reads `<title>` may pick up the trailing space.

**Dead commented-out viewport variants**
Three additional `<meta name="viewport">` tags are present as HTML comments, including a fixed-width `width=1024` variant. These represent unresolved template decisions and add noise to the source without functional impact.

**iOS hamburger menu CSS hack**
The `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` animation (infinite, zero-effect) is a workaround for an iOS 9 rendering bug that is now obsolete (~7 years). No rendering risk today, but adds dead CSS weight.

**No issues confirmed on `alt` text** — HTML is truncated; cannot audit image `alt` attributes from the provided source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` assignment appears inert**

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

This line is wrapped in both an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`) and an AMPscript block comment (`/* */`). In SFMC, AMPscript inside an HTML comment is **not parsed** — the engine only processes AMPscript in `%%[ ]%%` blocks or `<script runat="server">` tags, not inside `<!-- -->`. If `@user` (the hashed email used for identity/personalization) relies on this line, it is never being set. This is a high-confidence finding given the comment structure; confirm by checking the full template for a `%%[ SET @user = ... ]%%` block elsewhere.

**Multiple DE lookups with no fallback guards**

```
@uEm   = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```

If a subscriber is not present in `NM_CRM_Feed`, both `@uEm` and `@cmdid` resolve to empty string. No `IIF` or `empty()` fallback is visible in the source block. Downstream link parameters that interpolate these will silently drop their values.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment with no visible second scenario**

The setup comment instructs the template author to pick one of two `@user` assignment paths. From the available source, only the commented-out SHA256 path is visible. If the second scenario (presumably a plain `EMAIL_ID` path) is absent or also commented out, `@user` is unset for the entire send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA confirmed: no `List-Unsubscribe` header was captured through the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo Mail require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 enforcement policies. Its absence risks inbox placement degradation or bulk folder routing on those platforms.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` mechanism) is now required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders. Without it, mail clients that surface an "Unsubscribe" button will either fall back to a mailto flow or suppress the option entirely — both worsen the subscriber experience and compliance posture.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` was not captured by the relay. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm SPF pass, DKIM signing, or DMARC alignment from available data. Needs verification via a live header capture or MX Toolbox check against the sending domain.

**Unsubscribe link in footer**
A footer unsubscribe link is expected in a send of this type; cannot confirm its presence or correctness from the truncated source. Must verify the full HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Tracking parameter integrity depends on AMPscript execution**
If `@user`, `@uEm`, or `@cmdid` are unset (see §4), any links that embed these as URL parameters (e.g., `&uEm=%%=v(@uEm)=%%`) will produce malformed or parameter-stripped URLs. This breaks attribution and any personalized landing page logic keyed on those values.

**`@refid` / `@base_refid` chain**
If Campaign Calendar DE is not populated before deployment, the `@refid` appended to links will be empty, causing all click attribution in analytics to land under an untagged/null campaign bucket.

**26 redirect links unverified**
Without probing the `e.neimanmarcus.com` redirect chain, it is unknown whether destination URLs return 200, are seasonally expired, or carry correct UTM parameters. Given the email promotes "up to 70% off" (time-sensitive), stale links are a non-trivial risk.
