# Get last-minute gifts FAST with free store pickup + save 20% 🛍️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-three. I checked my Kohl's folder before I even clicked into this one, and that's where my count sits for June alone. So when "last-minute gifts FAST" shows up on the 20th — the day before Father's Day — my first instinct is less *oh good, they're timing this well* and more *they know exactly when my guard is down and they're going for it*. I'll give them credit for the setup: free store pickup for a last-minute gift is genuinely useful intel, and the 20% off headline is visible before I've scrolled a pixel.
- Inside, though, I'm essentially looking at a men's clothing lookbook with a Dad wrapper slapped on it. Sonoma polos at $9.99, Nike tees, a grooming kit, some kitchen stuff. This is solidly mass-market Kohl's — nothing wrong with that, but it's the same creative DNA as the June 17 Father's Day send that also underwhelmed me. The "Dad deserves a great gift, you deserve a great deal" line is the most personality this email shows, and it's buried below the fold.
- I wouldn't click for myself. I might have clicked once for a gift — but I already bought Father's Day this week, and if I hadn't, Nordstrom would have earned that purchase before Kohl's got here. Mark as read.

## What's working

- **The 20% banner is above the fold immediately** — no hunting, no scrolling, the offer lands before I form an opinion about anything else.
- **"Last-minute" + "free store pickup" is a genuinely useful combination** — it solves a real problem on June 20 in a way a pure online discount doesn't.
- **Kohl's Cash callout in the footer** — I notice this every time. I won't use a card without points back, and knowing I'll earn toward $10 Kohl's Cash is the kind of loyalty signal I actually track.
- **The seasonal relevance is tight** — unlike the vague summer-style emails from earlier in the month, there's a real occasion tethering this one, and they didn't overstay it.

## What's weak

- **The models and product mix read as for men, styled by men** — I'm the one doing the gifting, and nothing in the visual hierarchy acknowledges me. Compare this to Macy's, which at least surfaces "last chance" confirmation language directly at the account holder. Kohl's just shows me polos.
- **$9.99 Sonoma hero is doing this email no favors** — leading with a near-clearance-level item right under the 20% banner undercuts any sense that this is a curated gift pick. Even Sephora's sale emails manage to anchor on a brand that feels aspirational before they go broad.
- **"Shop Now" is as generic as it gets** — every section, same button copy. After the fourth "Shop Now" I've already stopped reading.
- **This is the second Father's Day email in three days** — they sent one on June 17 too, and that one scored a 6 from me. Sending the same occasion twice without escalating the offer or changing the creative angle is how you train subscribers to tune you out.
- **The kitchen and home section at the bottom feels completely off-brief** — I'm here for last-minute Father's Day gifts, not a stand mixer. It reads like they stitched two separate campaigns together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Address the buyer, not the recipient** — flip one hero image to show a woman handing over a gift, or add a line like "You made it. He'll love it." The gifter is the customer here.
- 2. **Kill "Shop Now" everywhere; write the destination into the button** — "Shop Nike Tees," "Shop Grooming Gifts," "Shop Under $25." That's the standard Neiman Marcus sets even for its roundup emails.
- 3. **Cut the kitchen section entirely** — or save it for a dedicated "Dad is also a homebody" segmented version. This undercuts the focused urgency.
- 4. **Move Kohl's Cash higher** — it's a loyalty signal that actually differentiates Kohl's from Nordstrom for a gift purchase. It belongs near the hero, not the footer.
- 5. **Consolidate the Father's Day cadence** — one send on the 17th, one on the 20th is fine; but if the creative is nearly identical, combine them into a single better email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last-minute Father's Day? Free pickup saves you. Plus 20% off.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `He'll think you planned it. (Free pickup + 20% off.)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by tonight, pick up tomorrow — gifts he'll actually use`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off + earn Kohl's Cash — still time to land the gift`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Last-Minute Gifts" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally standard but contains a malformed HTML tag, at least one unpopulated merge token in an image URL, and compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers, no physical address in rendered HTML) that warrant attention before treating this send as a template baseline.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable.** All destination links are wrapped through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. HTTP probing was skipped — destination URLs and their UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the source alone.

**Three distinct open-tracking mechanisms fire simultaneously:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/f7798928-...` (CoherentPath pixel)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbb.../o.gif` (Marigold/MI pixel)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?M2ASCMQE7UHEPLK2TRWCMMXT4M...` (ESP open pixel)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel)

Triple-firing open trackers is normal for enterprise stacks, but the CoherentPath pixel carries a large base64-encoded payload in `cp_tp=v3.eJyF...` — if that payload contains PII or cookie sync data, it warrants a privacy review.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed closing tag — confirmed HTML error.**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>   <!-- ← should be </div> -->
```
The `</custom>` tag is invalid HTML. Most email clients will auto-correct, but it can cause layout shifts in strict parsers (Outlook Web, some Android clients).

**Empty `<title>` tag.**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some preview panes surface the `<title>`. A blank title degrades accessibility and misses an opportunity for preheader reinforcement.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (AA) prohibits this restriction. iOS 10+ ignores it in Safari but it still affects some Android WebViews.

**11 images missing `alt` text** — 4 are decorative tracking pixels where `alt=""` is present on some but not all, and 7 are apparent content images (the `.png` files served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`). Images-off rendering will produce blank gaps with no fallback text for those content images.
