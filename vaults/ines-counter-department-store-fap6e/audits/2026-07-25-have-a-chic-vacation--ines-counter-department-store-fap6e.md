# Have a chic vacation
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a mood shift. After a solid month of "70% off Last Call," "1,000+ new luxury arrivals," and tiered-spend panic emails, Neiman Marcus sends me a woman leaning on a vintage Mercedes convertible outside a beach post office in Summerland with zero mention of a discount anywhere on the page. It's gorgeous, it's editorial, it looks like something out of a Cote d'Azur mood board — and it is, in its own quiet way, refreshing. But it's also July 25th and I am not booking a European vacation off an email that shows me one pleated maxi dress and one skirt with no prices attached.
- I like the restraint here more than I expected to. It's the same instinct behind "Prada's perfect sneakers for summer" from the 24th — Neiman's clearly capable of a clean, non-hysterical email when they want to be. But that one at least had a specific named product to anchor around. This one just has vibes and a "Shop Vacation" button in a font so quiet I had to squint.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about the skirt — that pleated pink number is genuinely pretty. Would I forward it to a friend? No. There's nothing here worth forwarding — no offer, no urgency, no reason to act today versus whenever. I'd open it, admire the photography for four seconds, and archive it.

## What's working

- The hero image is beautiful and shot like a real editorial spread, not stock catalog filler — the vintage convertible and the "United States Post Office" signage give it a specific, lived-in place instead of a generic beach.
- The model reads age-appropriate for aspirational dressing without feeling like it's targeting my 22-year-old niece — long pleated skirts, sandals, a structured bag. That's closer to my actual wardrobe than a bikini spread would be.
- "Rev Up Your Look" is a decent bit of copywriting that ties the car visual to the fashion concept without being cute about it.
- Clean nav bar, minimal footer clutter, no five stacked promo blocks — a relief after Kohl's "Instant Kohl's Cash" pileup with the Fire TV Stick bribe on top.

## What's weak

- There is no offer. None. Not a percentage, not free shipping, not even a vague "new arrivals" nudge — just "Shop Vacation" and "Shop Skirts." For a brand that's been running $500-off tiered promos and 70%-off Last Call sales all month, this feels like tonal whiplash in the other direction now.
- Two products, two CTAs, and neither tells me what I'm buying — no price, no designer name visible, nothing about the skirt or the dress beyond "keep your style in overdrive with versatile separates." I have no idea if that skirt is $95 or $895.
- Subject line "Have a chic vacation" is pleasant but forgettable — it's a mood, not a reason to open. Sephora's "Save the date" send at least had a concrete calendar hook behind its restraint; this has nothing behind it.
- As a Circle loyalty member, I see zero mention of points, InCircle status, or member perks anywhere in this email — after a month of tiered-spend $500-off promos, going completely silent on loyalty feels like a missed beat, not a design choice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or a designer name under at least one of the two hero looks — I don't need a sale, but I need to know what price bracket I'm even looking at before I click "Shop Skirts."
- 2. Add one InCircle line — even something as small as "InCircle members earn 2X points on vacation dressing" would connect this pretty editorial moment back to why I actually shop Neiman's over, say, Nordstrom.
- 3. Tighten "Shop Vacation" into something with more teeth — "Shop the Look" or "Shop Resort Wear" tells me what's on the other side of the click.
- 4. Give the second image a real headline instead of a single unbolded sentence floating in white space — right now "Keep your style in overdrive with versatile separates" reads like alt text that escaped.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your resort edit has arrived`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pack this: the pieces for your next getaway`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pleated skirts, breezy separates, and the sandals to match`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New resort styles, curated for wherever you're headed`
