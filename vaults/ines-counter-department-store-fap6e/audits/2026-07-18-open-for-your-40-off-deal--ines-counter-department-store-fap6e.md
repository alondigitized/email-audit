# Open for your 40% off deal 😍
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Sixty. That's where I am in my Kohl's count since Memorial Day, and I already opened one from them this morning — the "Enjoy 40% off" send with the shopping bags emoji. So when this one drops a few hours later with a nearly identical subject and the same heart-eyes emoji, I'm not reaching for my wallet. I'm reaching for the unsubscribe link, and the only reason I didn't click it is muscle memory from a decade of Kohl's Cash.
- What stops me cold when I actually open it: this is a home email. Floral duvet covers. A sofa. "For your home's soft side." I don't go to Kohl's for my sofa. I go for the Sephora counter tucked near the registers — the one that's expressly excluded from the Kohl's Cash earn in the fine print at the bottom of this very email. They sent me a home promotion, told me I'd earn Kohl's Cash, and then quietly noted that the reason I actually shop there doesn't qualify. That's not a segmentation miss. That's an active insult.
- The one saving grace: when I scrolled to the barcode, it said "extra 45% off" — which is five points better than the 40% the subject line promised. Under-promise, over-deliver is not nothing. But I nearly didn't scroll there. Mark as read, and hope tomorrow is quieter.

## What's working

- **Barcode coupon is in-store ready**: Scannable, wallet-forward, no printing required. This is how I actually redeem at Kohl's, and having it right in the email removes every step of friction.
- **Kohl's Cash dates are clearly visible**: I can see the earn window and know exactly how long I have. For a loyalty junkie like me, that's a functional detail that matters.
- **CLEARANCE header is honest and direct**: "NEW MARKDOWNS TAKEN, Save up to 70%" — that's Neiman Marcus LAST CALL energy without the Neiman price point. I respect directness on clearance.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, no overlapping text. After some Kohl's sends this spring that loaded half a hero image, this one actually displayed correctly.
- **Body out-delivers the subject**: 45% off in the barcode vs. 40% in the subject is a pleasant surprise rather than a bait-and-switch. That's the right direction.

## What's weak

- **Completely wrong category for me**: I'm a Sephora-at-Kohl's shopper. This email is 100% home goods — bedding, throw pillows, a lifestyle sofa photo. Nothing here maps to why I hand Kohl's my card.
- **Sephora at Kohl's is excluded from Kohl's Cash**: It's right there in the fine print. They sent me a "earn Kohl's Cash" pitch knowing full well that my reason for being in their store doesn't qualify. That's a trust problem.
- **Second email today, same offer angle**: I already opened a 40%-off Kohl's send this morning. Two emails in one day with overlapping creative doesn't read as "great deal" — it reads as "someone hit send twice by accident." Macy's sends a lot, but they at least vary the hook.
- **Four competing sections kill the hierarchy**: Summer essentials → $3.99 product tile → sofa at 50% off → clearance → barcode. The eye bounces. There's no single story. Even Sephora's awkward World Cup email from last month had a cleaner lead than this.
- **Heart-eyes emoji in the subject is juvenile**: I'm 47. 😍 doesn't read as excitement — it reads as a blast to the full subscriber list. Neiman Marcus, which I audited recently at 9/10, doesn't need emojis to make me open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by purchase category**: If my last five Kohl's purchases are Sephora items, send me a beauty or Women's email. This home send is a category mismatch that erodes trust faster than any discount can rebuild it.
- 2. **One email per day, hard cap**: Sixty emails in roughly seven weeks means I've become numb to 40% off. Pull back to three times a week and each send would feel like news.
- 3. **Lead with the barcode coupon**: The 45% off wallet code buried at the bottom is the most compelling thing in this email. Put it in the hero or at minimum above the sofa section.
- 4. **Align subject and body offer**: The subject says 40%; the barcode says 45%. Pick one number and use it everywhere — 45% is the better story, use that.
- 5. **Acknowledge the Sephora carve-out upfront or remove it**: If you know I'm a beauty shopper, don't promote Kohl's Cash and then exclude my category in the fine print. Either send me a non-Cash promotion or find an offer that includes Sephora.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your extra 45% off — plus new clearance drops`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kohl's Cash starts now. 45% off in your wallet.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Scan the barcode at checkout — earn period runs through July 20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New clearance markdowns just landed. Shop before they're gone.`

### 1. Technical Summary

One unrendered merge token will cause a broken experience for all recipients. Authentication and unsubscribe headers are unverifiable at the relay layer, which creates compliance exposure.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **22 tracking/click-redirect links skipped** during HTTP probing — destination URLs could not be validated. All links route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. No confirmed broken destinations, but live validation was blocked.
- Three separate open-tracking pixels are present (Coherent Path, MessageIntelligence `o.gif`, and `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx`). This is expected for enterprise ESPs but worth noting for data reconciliation.
- `<custom>` tag appears in the open-pixel block (`</custom>`), which is malformed HTML and may cause parse errors in strict renderers:
  ```html
  <img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
  ```

### 5. Compliance

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — may not have been captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; Gmail and Apple Mail also use this header for one-click unsubscribe UI. If the header is absent at the MTA level, inbox providers may suppress or penalize delivery.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since 2024) cannot be confirmed. If absent, Google may show a degraded unsubscribe experience or route to spam.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (subdomain); alignment with `kohls.com` DMARC policy should be verified directly with mail headers from the original MTA. This is a relay capture limitation, not a confirmed failure.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` suppresses referrer data on outbound clicks, which will cause direct/none attribution in site analytics regardless of UTM params — this is intentional for privacy but worth flagging for analytics teams.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- `mi_ecmp=1017882_2026718` and `email_name=260718_DG_Email_Mystery` appear consistently across render pixel URLs, confirming campaign tagging is in place at the ESP level.

- The `%%offer_evnt_nm%%` unrendered token may appear in a link href, which would produce a broken or malformed URL when clicked.
