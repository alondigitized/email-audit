# Dresses with dramatic flair
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- There's something genuinely calming about this one landing after the chaos Neiman Marcus has been running in my inbox since Tuesday. No "CLAIM YOUR GIFT CARD NOW," no sale siren — just a woman in a spectacular leopard-print dress owning a staircase. The editorial restraint is the Neiman Marcus I signed up for, not the promo machine that sent me three promotional emails in 24 hours. I noticed it.
- Then I looked closer and realized there's nothing to do here except browse. No price. No member language. No "your InCircle points apply." And on the exact same morning they sent me the "Claim your $50–$500 gift card" email — which I haven't even redeemed yet — this lands like a completely different brand from a completely different department. The left hand is screaming gift cards; the right hand is whispering about drama. It reads like a scheduling accident, not a campaign.
- I'd click through on a slow Friday morning when I'm in cocktail-dress mode, which honestly I might be. But I wouldn't forward this, and without a price point or a loyalty hook on the landing page, I'd close the tab before finding my card. Beautiful wallpaper.

## What's working

- **The hero stops the scroll.** Leopard print with that oversized dramatic hem is genuinely arresting — it earns "Spotlight-Loving Dresses" without having to explain itself.
- **"Shop Cocktail & Party" is a legitimately good CTA.** Names the occasion, not just the category. Nordstrom could learn from this one.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean and Saks-caliber.** Logo → hero → headline → split CTAs → secondary product row → footer. Nothing is fighting for my eye.
- **No render bugs anywhere.** After the noisy sends from earlier this week, a clean, professional render feels like its own form of respect.
- **The three secondary dresses — gold draped, black patterned, red fitted — give me something to compare.** Three distinct silhouettes, three occasions. That's smart even at thumbnail size.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** A purely editorial email that landed the same morning as a "gift card waiting for you" email is a missed layup — at minimum, one subheader line: "Your gift card applies to everything here."
- **No InCircle signal anywhere in the body.** I shop Neiman Marcus specifically because I collect InCircle points on my card. The email treats me like a stranger walking off the street.
- **The model skews very young and very editorial.** The hero dress is gorgeous but styled on someone who looks 28 on a runway. Macy's and Nordstrom increasingly show women who look like me wearing this kind of piece. NM doesn't, and it's starting to feel like a tell.
- **"Shop Dresses" as the second CTA is dead weight.** It goes to the same destination as the hero. Give me "Shop Under $500" or "New This Week" — something that actually filters to different results.
- **Two NM emails, same day, completely different brand registers.** One is shouting about gift cards; one is whispering about drama. That's not a campaign — that's two teams with different send schedules and no visibility into each other's calendars.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cross-reference the gift card from this morning.** "These dresses are waiting for your gift card" — one sentence, five seconds to write, completely changes the email for anyone who opened the earlier send.
- 2. **Swap "Shop Dresses" for "Shop Under $500" or "New Designer Arrivals."** Give me a destination that's different from the hero link and does the filtering work for me.
- 3. **Add one InCircle line in the body.** "InCircle members: double points on cocktail categories through July 4." Fifteen words. Completely changes this email for high-value subscribers.
- 4. **Show me a model closer to my age in the secondary product row.** The hero can be aspirational — that's fine — but the product row is where I imagine myself. Sephora does age-inclusive casting; NM can too.
- 5. **Consolidate or stagger the same-day sends.** Roll the gift card story and the editorial into one email, or put 24 hours between them. Two sends in one morning at different brand temperatures reads like an ops problem.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your gift card looks good in leopard print`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cocktail season, meet your new statement dress`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three occasions. Three dresses. Your gift card already applies.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spotlight-worthy looks from the Designer floor — InCircle points count.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Dresses with dramatic flair"

### 1. Technical Summary

SFMC/ExactTarget-templated email with a CSS hamburger nav and responsive grid. QA produced a 43% pass rate driven by four warnings — two compliance header gaps, one deliverability signal, and 36 unprobed tracking links.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links not probed** — all click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP probe. Link-rot and destination mismatch cannot be confirmed or ruled out without manual spot-checks or a tool that follows redirects.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs** (`3138 / 3647` chars). Most spam filters and ISPs penalize plain-text parts that are effectively just URL dumps. This is a deliverability risk, not just a cosmetic one.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are dead template cruft. Not a rendering bug, but they signal unclean template maintenance and should be removed.

**Active viewport sets `maximum-scale=1`**, which prevents user zoom on iOS — an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```

**Redundant ExternalClass rules** — `.ExternalClass` is reset twice (once as standalone, once as a compound selector list). No rendering impact but adds byte weight.

Alt-text coverage cannot be assessed from the truncated source; confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**SHA256 user variable is commented out.** The template header declares two scenarios and instructs the sender to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW." The active scenario uses a Lookup:
```
@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
But the SHA256 assignment is double-commented (HTML comment wrapping an AMPscript block comment), making it unreachable:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
If downstream links or pixels depend on `@user` being a hashed email (common for clean-room/pixel matching), they will receive a numeric `EMAIL_ID` instead. Verify which value downstream ad/analytics systems expect.

**`@refid` is a no-op CONCAT:**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string does nothing. If a suffix or campaign qualifier was supposed to be appended here, it is missing.

**`@cmdid` and `@uEm` sourced from `NM_CRM_Feed` via Lookup** — if a subscriber record is absent from that DE, both variables silently resolve to empty string. No null-guard or default is visible in the header comments.

The `List-Unsubscribe` gap is the highest-priority item here. Even if SFMC injects it at send time and AgentMail simply didn't capture it, that should be verified against a raw `.eml` export from a test send — not assumed.
