# ⭐️ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Sephora had me exhaling on June 29 with that "treating you to something new" break from the summer sale drumroll — it felt like the brand was finally talking to me instead of at me. Two days. That's all I got. Because here comes "STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE" in all caps and double star emojis, and any goodwill built by that palate cleanser just evaporated.
- The email underneath the subject is actually more interesting than the screaming suggests. The layered mechanic — credit card rewards, base Beauty Insider points, and what looks like a free sample bag offer, all running concurrently from Thursday through Monday — is exactly the kind of stacked-value play that makes me set a phone reminder and show up. I refuse to swipe any card without points back, and a weekend that promises to multiply both is worth a calendar block. But "stacking" is loyalty-program jargon. A lapsed member or a casual opener doesn't decode that in half a second. They skim the 30% badge, get confused about who qualifies, and close the tab.
- I'm not forwarding this. The Dolce&Gabbana drop buried near the bottom might earn a tap, and the loyalty mechanics might earn a store visit — but this email made me work for both. Mark as read, grudgingly.

## What's working

- The barcode and account number at the bottom signal that Sephora actually knows I exist — after Macy's "confirm now" email last week that felt like a cold form letter, this reminder of membership lands as genuine personalization.
- The Thu–Mon timeframe is visible near the CTA, so I don't have to hunt for the window in fine print. That's a small thing that earns credit.
- The stacking mechanic itself — card rewards layered over base points layered over a free bag — is legitimately compelling for a loyalty-first shopper. The idea is right even if the execution is rushed.
- The "Just Dropped: Dolce&Gabbana" block at the bottom is an actual editorial moment. New prestige arrivals are what push me from browse to cart.

## What's weak

- ALL CAPS subject with double star emojis is a real step backward. Neiman Marcus ran "Surprise: Up to an extra 40% off" this week with a clean typographic hero and felt premium. Sephora's subject reads like a Kohl's push notification — and I've been weathering Kohl's emoji-blast cadence all of June, so I know exactly what that register sounds like.
- The hero doesn't explain what stacking means. Someone who missed the email two days ago has no context. "Stacking Weekend" needs a five-second headline translation: *"Layer your card rewards, Insider points, and free bag — this weekend only."*
- The app-download section mid-email is a dead end. I'm already in the email. Redirecting me to a different platform mid-scroll is a conversion killer, not a cross-channel win.
- Four competing offers with no declared winner. The 30% cardmember benefit, the 2X points, the sample bag, and the Dolce&Gabbana drop all crowd each other out. No single offer gets to breathe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Declare a lead offer in the hero.** If "stacking" is the concept, make the math legible: "Earn 2X points + 30% back on your card + a free bag — Thursday through Monday." One sentence. Everything else is secondary.
- 2. **Cut the app-download block** or push it to the footer. I'm already engaged. Don't interrupt the funnel with a channel-switch ask.
- 3. **Surface the Dolce&Gabbana drop above the fold**, or give it a dedicated section near the top. New prestige arrivals are impulse-click bait for me — don't bury the lede.
- 4. **Drop the all-caps subject.** Even just removing the caps and keeping the emojis would lift the perceived register by a full tier.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your rewards stack this weekend — here's the math`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stacking Weekend: points × card × free bag, Thu–Mon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 2X points, bank 30% back on your card, and grab a free Smooth Bag — this weekend only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Dolce&Gabbana just landed. Your Beauty Insider points work harder this weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Disables the OS-level text inflation that mobile browsers apply to improve readability. Combined with `maximum-scale=1` above, this creates a compounded accessibility barrier. Scope this only to containers where size inflation would genuinely break layout, not every element globally.

**Minor — `robots` meta value uses non-canonical spacing:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
The canonical value is `noindex` (no space). Most crawlers tolerate the space, but `no index` is not the spec-compliant token. Change to `content="noindex, nofollow"`.

**Note — VML namespaces present:**
```html
xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml"
xmlns:o="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:office:office"
```

Correct for Outlook background image support. No issue.
