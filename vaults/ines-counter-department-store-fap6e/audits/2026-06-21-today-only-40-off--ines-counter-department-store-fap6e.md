# TODAY ONLY: 40% off⏳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That is where my Kohl's count sits for June, and I mentally tallied it before I'd even scrolled past the hero. So "TODAY ONLY: 40% off" with an hourglass shows up on Father's Day — the one day in June where a sale email might actually earn its keep in my inbox — and I am cautiously open to it. The 40% is a real step up from the run of 20%-off sends this month, and the Kohl's Cash line below the hero immediately gets my attention because I genuinely track those.
- Then I scroll. And I keep scrolling. FLX athletic line. A twelve-dollar tee. A Shark robot vacuum. Summer storage bins. I'm not saying any of those are bad products — I'm saying none of them are *me.* This email is trying to be a circular, not a campaign. Where the June 19th "I have nothing to wear" send felt like someone actually thought about women shopping for themselves, this one is a Father's Day clearinghouse dressed up as a focused flash sale. The robot vacuum section sits directly below a women's athletic module and I genuinely can't figure out what journey they think I'm on.
- I would not click through. I would not forward it. I'd scan the 40% banner, check whether it stacks with my Kohl's Cash, and close the tab.

## What's working

- **The offer is real.** Forty percent is not 20%, not an "up to" hedge — it's the strongest number I've seen in this June run from Kohl's, and it shows up clearly in the hero without buried asterisks.
- **Kohl's Cash is front and center.** I noticed "Plus, earn Kohl's Cash" immediately beneath the hero. For someone who refuses to run a card without points, that's a legitimate hook — and Macy's buried the equivalent benefit in their late-May "confirm now" send, which felt like an afterthought by comparison.
- **Father's Day timing is earned.** Unlike that June 17th "fits your budget" send that felt opportunistic, this one lands *on* the holiday and owns the moment. The "Happy Father's Day" header makes sense.
- **The hero visual hierarchy is clean.** The 40% badge reads first, the day-urgency reads second, and the CTA is visible without hunting.

## What's weak

- **This email has no idea who I am.** Father's Day men's apparel into robot vacuums into summer storage into women's athletic wear — this is a warehouse sweep, not a communication. Sephora packed seven modules into their last send and I called them out for it; this email matches that count easily.
- **The "TODAY ONLY" subject line shouts.** In all caps in the inbox. I'm 47. I notice when I'm being yelled at, and it triggers the same reflex as a spam folder subject. The urgency is real — the execution is a billboard.
- **Athletic wear is not my lane from Kohl's.** FLX and TEK GEAR eat two full modules. When I cross-shop Nordstrom I'm not comparing their athletic basics to these. This email doesn't know that.
- **Demographic mismatch below the fold.** The Shark vacuum section reads like someone decided to "add one more category" and no one pushed back. It's not that I don't own a vacuum — it's that this doesn't belong in an apparel flash-sale email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to three modules, max.** Hero offer → Father's Day gifting for him → one section that acknowledges she might want something too (a brief "treat yourself" module for women's). The vacuum and storage bins go to a separate send or the weekly digest.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to stop shouting.** Drop the all-caps. The hourglass emoji does the urgency work already without needing caps lock.
- 3. **Lean harder on Kohl's Cash in the body.** One clear callout mid-email that says "stack your 40% off with Kohl's Cash" would convert me faster than a fourth apparel module. Neiman Marcus's "Living for the weekend?" scored a 4 partly because there was no loyalty hook at all — this is a real differentiator Kohl's is underusing.
- 4. **Add one personal hook for female shoppers on Father's Day.** A line like "Treat the dad in your life — then treat yourself" with a women's offer would acknowledge I might be here for someone else AND for me.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off today only — plus Kohl's Cash on every order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day final hours: 40% off + Cash back`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop gifts for him. Something for you too — offer ends midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 40% stacks with Kohl's Cash — here's what to grab before it's gone.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "TODAY ONLY: 40% off" (2026-06-26)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard HTML 4.01 Transitional table-based layout with VML/MSO conditional comments and a responsive media query block. No blocking rendering issues, but several compliance and accessibility gaps were flagged by QA — most are missing headers likely lost at the AgentMail relay layer, not provable ESP-side defects from the HTML alone.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking/click-redirect URLs skipped (not probed)** — all links route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. This is expected for ESP click-tracking, but means dead-link detection has zero coverage for this email.

Three distinct tracking pixel systems are active simultaneously:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` (CoherentPath/CHP — 1×1 open pixel)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` (MessageGears open pixel)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud open pixel)

Four separate open-tracking pixels is unusual and increases page weight. No functional bug, but worth confirming all four are intentional and de-duplicated at the attribution layer.

**Malformed HTML tag:** The open-tracking `<img>` for `click.s.kohls.com` is followed by `</custom>` — a non-standard closing tag with no matching open tag. Evidence:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""><custom>
```
This will be silently ignored by most parsers but is invalid HTML and may trigger strict spam filters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text missing on 9 images**, all tracking/rendering pixels and several content images (`mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png`). The content-image alt failures are the meaningful ones — images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate mail) will produce blank content blocks.

Affected content images (no `alt` attribute):
- `2a5adc5afb647aad.png` — `email_name=260621_DG_Email_Mystery`
- `f441d45d2128ae53.png`
- `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`
- `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, open pixel) have no alt text but are `aria-hidden="true"` on one instance; the others lack that attribute. Correct fix is `alt=""` (empty string) on all decorative/tracking images.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** disables pinch-to-zoom, which is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure. Not a legal blocker for most retail email, but worth noting for ADA exposure.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — accessibility issue for screen readers that announce the email document title.

**Google Fonts loaded three times** (two `<link>` tags + two `@import` rules for Roboto and Montserrat). Redundant fetches add latency with no rendering benefit.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated HTML (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`, etc. patterns not present). The `mi_u=604230016` parameter in MessageGears pixel URLs appears to be a resolved subscriber ID, not an unfired token. No issues found in the visible source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — QA confirms absence. RFC 2369 compliance and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024+) both require this header. If the ESP sends it and AgentMail relay strips it, that is a relay configuration issue that needs to be fixed — Gmail will not show the one-click unsubscribe UI without it.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is absent. Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policy requires this for senders above 5,000/day. Same caveat applies re: relay stripping.

**No physical mailing address detected** in the visible HTML. CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML is truncated, so the address may exist in the footer — but QA did not find it in the scanned content. Needs manual footer verification.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` are present** — correct for email HTML; prevents web-archived versions from being indexed and suppresses referrer leakage to linked domains.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All destination links route through `click.s.kohls.com` (Salesforce MC click-tracking) and `click.chp.kohls.com` (CoherentPath). Because all 22 links were skipped during HTTP probing, UTM parameter presence on final landing URLs cannot be confirmed.

The `mi_ecmp` parameter in pixel URLs (`mi_ecmp=1015695_2026621`) appears to be the MessageGears campaign ID — consistent with the email date (2026-06-21). No UTM parameters are visible on the tracking pixel URLs themselves, which is expected (pixels aren't destinations).

**Cannot confirm:** whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` parameters survive the click-redirect chain to kohls.com. This requires live link resolution testing against the actual click URLs.
