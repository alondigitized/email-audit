# The Roundup: Festive Heels
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- Neiman's did something restrained here, and I noticed. It's one hero image and six shoes with only the designer names underneath. There's no shouting and no countdown clock. That's a relief after Kohl's, where I've opened three or four in two days, and after Macy's "LAST CHANCE" confirmation email, which was all costume and no substance. This one treats me like an adult.
- The problem is that it gives me nothing to act on. I see no prices, no offer and no points, and I don't get any "why now." The lace Louboutin on a dessert plate is a pretty picture. But I refuse to put a card down without points back, and I'd want to know if Neiman's has an event or a gift-card tie-in before I chased a $900 heel. Sephora's Daily Deals put the number in the subject, and I know exactly what I'm getting. Here I'd have to click just to find out if it's worth my time.
- Would I click? Maybe, on a slow evening, for the Valentino Garavani rockstuds or the Louboutin python pump. Would I forward it? No. It's "read and admire," not "text my friend." It's also the fourth Neiman's send I've seen in about three days, right after the Rag & Bone and Bottega Veneta emails.

## What's working

- The Louboutin lace pump on the plate is a genuinely lovely editorial hero, and it made me stop scrolling.
- Designer names under each shoe (Versace, Chloé, Valentino Garavani, Louboutin, Loewe, McQueen) signal the designer access I shop Neiman's for.
- The layout is clean, with no overlap, no broken images and plenty of white space. It feels like a proper luxury retailer, not a Macy's-style pile-up.
- "Festive Heels" is an honest, seasonal frame for party-shoe shopping, with no bait-and-switch.
- The personal style advisor link at the bottom is a nice touch for someone who likes being helped.

## What's weak

- There is no price, offer, points or event anywhere, so I have no reason to act today.
- The only CTA is a small underlined "Shop Party Shoes" text link under the headline. It isn't a button, and it's a tiny tap target on a phone.
- There are no prices under the shoes and no shoe names, only brands, so I can't tell a $400 pump from a $1,400 one.
- There are no models and no styling. That's fine for shoes, but I can't picture these for my life or my wardrobe.
- There's no loyalty or InCircle recognition. A known customer gets treated like a stranger.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to click now. That could be InCircle points, a gift-card-with-purchase event or free alterations on shoes. Say it in the subject, and put it in a real button under the hero.
- 2. Replace the small text link with a full-width button. "Shop party heels" works.
- 3. Put prices or "from $X" under each brand, and add a product name.
- 4. Add a member line, such as "Earn bonus points on shoes this week," so I feel recognized.
- 5. Space out the cadence. Four Neiman's emails in three days makes even a quiet one feel like noise.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "The Roundup: Festive Heels"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a conventional XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with a hamburger-nav media query. It ships an unstripped Salesforce Marketing Cloud developer comment, and the viewport meta blocks pinch-zoom. The 4 QA warnings are relay-capture gaps or unverifiable items rather than confirmed defects, except for the URL-heavy plain-text part.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** 15 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified.
- **URL-heavy plain-text part:** The plain-text alternative is 2619/3120 characters URLs (about 84%, over the 70% threshold). It is essentially a list of raw tracked URLs, which is a spam-scoring and readability risk for text-only clients.
- **Truncated source:** The HTML was truncated, so I could not check individual `href` values for empty, `#` or malformed links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents zooming on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** The developer comment sits before `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. In some clients and older engines, content before the DOCTYPE can trigger quirks mode. The practical risk is low, but it is avoidable.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** The media queries use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and similar. These match only when the class attribute is exactly that value. Any element with multiple classes will silently miss the responsive rules.
- **Hamburger nav:** The nav relies on the checkbox `:checked` hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). Gmail and Outlook do not support it, so those clients depend on whatever fallback exists. The truncated source doesn't show one.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. This is minor.
- **Alt text:** I could not check alt text on images because the source was truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Exposed internal logic:** The top of the delivered HTML contains a developer comment. It lists required variables (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`). It also names internal Data Extensions (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and their lookup fields (`EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`). It ends with the unresolved instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" and a commented `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`.
- **Why it matters:** Leaving this in the sent message discloses internal data schema and the fact that the send uses a SHA-256 hashed email. The template comment should have been stripped at send time.
- **Rendered tokens:** No unrendered merge tokens were visible in the visible body portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both are missing in the captured message. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. If they are absent at the source, one-click unsubscribe fails Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not present. This is expected via the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment is unknown. The `From` domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated sending subdomain, but that is not proof of authentication.
- **Body compliance elements:** The visible portion doesn't show the unsubscribe link or postal address, because the footer was truncated. These are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. The tracking links were all skipped by the probe, so no UTM parameters, final URLs or landing-page content could be checked. The comment references `@refid` and `@base_refid`, which suggests reference-ID-based tracking rather than UTM parameters. That is an inference from the comment, not confirmed link data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC variable/scenario comment from the sent HTML. It exposes the DE names, lookup keys and hashing approach.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta (or add `user-scalable=yes`).
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the original message. Check raw headers outside the relay, since the QA warning may be a relay artifact.
4. Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC from the raw headers of a direct-to-inbox send.
5. Move the comment below the DOCTYPE, or remove it.
6. Reduce URL density in the plain-text part, for example with shorter link text and fewer duplicated links.
7. Change `*[class=x]` selectors to `[class~=x]` or `[class*=x]` so multi-class elements match.
8. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a redirect-following probe on the 15 tracking links, to check UTMs, landing pages, alt text and the footer.
