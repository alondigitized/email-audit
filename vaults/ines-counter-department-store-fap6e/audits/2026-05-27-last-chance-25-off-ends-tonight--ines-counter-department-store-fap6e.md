# Last chance: 25% off ends tonight
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen-plus emails from Neiman Marcus in roughly a week, and here we are again with another "Last Chance." The subject is legitimately good — 34 characters, a clean discount, and a real deadline — and for a split second I felt the pull. Then I opened it and remembered I've been down this road twice in the last 72 hours. The offer is different from the tiered spend-and-save grid they ran on the 24th, and the 25% off regular price plus extra 40% off sale styles is a dual-tier structure that's actually useful if you're cross-shopping. But there isn't a single skincare launch, a fragrance offer, or a beauty event in sight — this email is entirely fashion and accessories, and my InCircle status might as well not exist.
- For women who live in the designer clothing bays, this is a perfectly constructed Memorial Day push. For me — someone who picks up a La Mer serum at the beauty counter and then swings by Stuart Weitzman — it hits only half my world. Jacquemus and Givenchy in the product grid do register, and Stuart Weitzman showing up helps, but the Private Event designer sale block buried at the bottom would have been a better anchor for the whole send.
- I'd mark it read and maybe come back. I wouldn't forward it — Macy's did a better job of making me feel like a known customer with their triggered confirmation email last week, even if that one had its own awkwardness.

## What's working

- **Dual-tier offer is crystal clear** — "25% OFF Select Regular-Price Styles + EXTRA 40% OFF Select Sale Styles" reads at a glance and I don't need the fine print to understand what I'm getting.
- **Designer brand recognition in the product grid** — Jacquemus, Givenchy, Stuart Weitzman, Polo Ralph Lauren all showing at once is the right flex for a Neiman Marcus sale email; Saks does the same name-dropping but their grid is usually cleaner.
- **Visual hierarchy lands on the offer first** — the Memorial Day Sale headline reads before the product tiles, and the SHOP NOW button is unambiguous.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images all loaded, no overlapping text. On a busy morning that alone puts it ahead of some of the Kohl's sends I've been wading through this week.
- **"Private Event" framing for the designer sale** — it's a small thing, but naming that section a private event does feel like a small reward for being a subscriber.

## What's weak

- **Zero beauty content** — I'm a regular at the beauty counter and there isn't a single product from that category in this email. Sephora would never send me an apparel grid without a skincare anchor; NM seems to have forgotten that half my NM basket isn't in a fitting room.
- **No InCircle or points mention anywhere** — I refuse to use a card without points back, and NM knows this about me. The Macy's Star Rewards confirmation email last week at least acknowledged I had a tier, even awkwardly. This email treats me like a cold prospect.
- **"End summer" preheader for a Memorial Day send** — Memorial Day is the *beginning* of summer in the US. That phrasing is a copy error that undermines the seasonal relevance.
- **Fifteenth email in a week means the credibility of "last chance" is wearing thin** — the previous NM send I audited also led with "LAST CHANCE." At this cadence, every night is the last chance, and I'm starting to tune it out the same way I tune out Kohl's.
- **Category shop buttons feel like a fallback** — "SHOP WOMEN'S," "SHOP HANDBAGS," "SHOP ACCESSORIES" are placeholders, not editorial. At least one shoppable look tied to a specific occasion (Memorial Day weekend outfit, garden party, etc.) would give me a reason to click rather than browse.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add at least one beauty or fragrance offer** — even a "beauty starts at 25% off too" footer row would make this email relevant to a significant portion of the NM customer base I represent. Right now this reads like it was built exclusively by the RTW team.
- 2. **Surface InCircle benefits explicitly** — one sentence: "InCircle members, your points apply." That's it. Macy's Star Rewards does this even in triggered emails; NM has the tier structure and isn't using it here.
- 3. **Replace the generic category buttons with at least one editorial hook** — "Dresses for the long weekend" linked to a filtered landing page would outperform "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING" for a customer who's already seen that button 14 times this week.
- 4. **Fix the preheader copy** — "End summer" for a late-May send is a factual miss. Change to something that earns the click rather than just restating the subject.
- 5. **Pull the Private Event designer section up** — for customers who cross-shop Saks and Nordstrom, that's the most differentiated thing in this email. Don't bury it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day: 25% off regular-price + 40% off sale, ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day offer expires at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer styles, shoes, and handbags — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Regular-price and sale styles included — shop before midnight`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "25% off ends tonight"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends successfully but carries two significant infrastructure risks: AMPscript variable logic is fully exposed in HTML comments (including internal data extension names), and compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, authentication) are unconfirmed at the relay layer. Plain-text body is URL-dominated, which is a deliverability liability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links — status unverified**
QA skipped HTTP probes on all 26 click-redirect URLs (flagged as tracking domains). No broken-link confirmation is possible; probe coverage is zero for this send.

**Plain-text body: 79.8% URLs**
```
5,490 / 6,882 chars are URLs (QA threshold: 70%)
```
A plain-text part composed almost entirely of raw redirect URLs is a known spam-filter signal (SpamAssassin `RAZOR2_CF_RANGE_51_100`, Barracuda heuristics). The plain-text body should contain readable prose with links inline, not a URL dump.

**`@refid` is a no-op concat**
```ampscript
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string adds no value and suggests a placeholder that was never completed. If `@refid` is appended to tracking links, verify it resolves to the correct campaign ID and isn't silently empty.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting/dead viewport declarations**
Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These don't render but indicate unstable viewport history. The surviving tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) suppresses user zoom — an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). `maximum-scale=1` should be removed.

**Stale device targeting in CSS comments**
```css
/*Media for Android 4.4.4 414*/
```
```css
/*Hamburguer fix IOS9*/
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
```
The iOS 9 `webkit-animation` hack and Android 4.4.4 targeting suggest the CSS has not been audited against current client support matrices. These are harmless but increase payload size and indicate template age.

**Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
The template uses three separate breakpoint values: `max-width: 600px`, `max-device-width: 480px`, and `max-device-width: 520px` — all in separate `@media` blocks. This creates overlapping and potentially conflicting layout rules for mid-range viewports (481–599px).

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but tab/window title will render with a trailing space in clients that surface it.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block fully exposed in sent HTML**
The entire pre-send variable setup comment is present in the delivered source, including internal data extension names and field mappings:
```html
<!-- REQUIRED variables: @email ... 
  Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
  Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
  Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
  CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This exposes: internal DE names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`), and the fact that two unresolved "scenarios" exist for `@user`. These comments must be stripped before send via a pre-flight content scrub or suppression in the template compiler.

**`@user` scenario is ambiguous and unresolved**
`CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` appears above a commented-out `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. It is not clear which scenario is live. If `@user` drives personalization or identity-resolution pixels, a null or wrong value will silently break attribution.

**`@emailid` has dual-path logic**
```
@emailid (from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New)
```
No fallback guard is visible in the source. If the DE field is empty and the lookup also misses, `@emailid` will be empty — confirm null-handling exists downstream wherever this variable is used in links or pixels.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)
```
QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay potentially not forwarding headers. However, if either header is genuinely absent from the SMTP envelope, this is a CAN-SPAM violation and will cause Gmail/Yahoo to suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI (required for bulk senders under Yahoo/Google's 2024 enforcement). Confirm headers are present at the MTA level, not just in the template.

**Authentication-Results — unconfirmed**
```
[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found — SPF/DKIM status unknown
```
`NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com` sends via `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). SPF and DKIM must pass for that domain. The relay is masking the header — verify SPF/DKIM pass status at the ESP (likely Salesforce Marketing Cloud) before assuming alignment.

**Unsubscribe link — not auditable**
The unsubscribe mechanism itself cannot be verified from the truncated source, but given the List-Unsubscribe header warning, this should be confirmed end-to-end: link present in footer → POST handler responds within 10 days (CAN-SPAM) → suppression list updated.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters — not verifiable**
All 26 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM parameter presence, consistency (`utm_campaign` matching `@base_refid`), and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed. Manual spot-check of at least 3–4 representative links (hero CTA, product links, nav links) is required.

**`@base_refid` and `@refid` campaign ID chain**
If `@base_refid` flows into UTM parameters for GA4/Adobe Analytics attribution, and `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is the only transformation, confirm `@base_refid` is populated in the sendable DE for every record. An empty campaign ID will attribute all traffic to `(not set)` in analytics.
