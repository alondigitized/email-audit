# Our LOWEST prices are coming in hot 🔥
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. That's where the Kohl's counter sits in my inbox now — fifteen emails in roughly two weeks — and this one walks in wearing almost the exact same outfit as the June 1 send. Same "LOWEST PRICES OF THE SEASON" hero, same clearance badge, same cheerful family stock photo. I pulled up that June 1 email to double-check and the creative delta is basically a subject-line swap and a fire emoji. If you're going to hit me this many times in a fortnight, at least change the clothes.
- To be fair, the execution is clean. The hero loads, the discounts are real (85% off clearance is not nothing), and the Kohl's Cash callout in the footer is the kind of loyalty signal I actually look for. If I'm being charitable: this is a brand that knows what it is and executes it consistently. No broken images, no lorem ipsum, no Sephora "hey Rosa" nightmare — and yes, I'm still thinking about that May wrong-name fiasco. Kohl's at least addresses me as a category, not as someone else entirely.
- But would I click? Probably not today. I shopped the June 1 version of this email in my head already and moved on. Neiman Marcus pivoted to Father's Day this week, which felt like a genuine editorial shift. This one feels like a printer ran out of ink halfway through "new idea." Mark as read.

## What's working

- **Hero loads immediately and earns its real estate** — "LOWEST PRICES OF THE SEASON" plus the 85%-off clearance badge is the kind of specific, scannable offer that registers in under two seconds.
- **Multiple category tiles organized cleanly** — Women's, Men's, Kids, Home each have their own panel; visual separation is legible even on a small screen.
- **Kohl's Cash footer** — Earn $10 Kohl's Cash is visible and it's the loyalty hook I actually care about. Loyalty math front-and-center is the right call.
- **No render bugs** — After watching Sephora mislabel customers for two sends running, a technically clean email earns a small amount of goodwill.
- **Dollar-amount pricing visible** — $9.90 and $7.00 anchors give me something concrete to react to beyond a percentage.

## What's weak

- **Creative fatigue is real** — June 1 was already "lowest prices + clearance 85%." Sending essentially the same hero two days later signals either bad editorial calendar discipline or a bet that I won't notice. I noticed.
- **Fifteen emails in two weeks** — The cadence has become background noise. Even a genuinely good offer gets discounted when it's the fifteenth version of the same shout.
- **Nothing here for me specifically** — No beauty, no fragrance, no accessories that speak to a beauty counter regular. Every tile is apparel or home. Macy's finds ways to surface its beauty floor even in a promotional blast; Kohl's doesn't have that floor to offer, but it also doesn't try to compensate.
- **"Coming in hot" reads younger than I am** — That phrase is comfortable for a 28-year-old scrolling TikTok. At 47, it lands as trying too hard. The fire emoji amplifies this.
- **No urgency deadline anywhere** — "Coming in hot" implies now, but there's no "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" countdown. Without a deadline, lowest prices today is the same as lowest prices tomorrow, and I'll get to it tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the creative pattern** — Two days after the June 1 "lowest prices" email, lead with a specific category story instead of the same hero. Summer sandals, Father's Day gifts, workwear transition — anything that doesn't feel photocopied from Monday's send.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Lowest prices through Sunday" or "clearance pricing ends June 8" converts browsing intent into same-session action. Without it, I'm filing this under "whenever."
- 3. **Surface a hero product, not a hero percentage** — One aspirational item with its markdown price (was $79, now $29) does more work than a repeating banner. Neiman Marcus does this well; Kohl's can do a mass-market version of it.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence** — If I'm receiving 15 in 14 days, individual email effectiveness drops below the noise floor no matter how clean the execution. Batch the low-price messaging into two or three sends per week max.
- 5. **CTA copy beyond "Shop Now"** — "Shop Women's Clearance" or "See All Under $10" gives me a reason to click a specific panel, not just the front door.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Women's, men's and kids all marked down. Kohl's Cash on every order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the biggest markdowns of summer before they're gone.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Lowest Prices of the Season" (2026-06-03)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-rendered promotional send (MessageGears / `s.kohls.com`) with three distinct tracking pixel systems firing in the `<body>`. Core HTML is structurally sound but contains one malformed tag and several accessibility gaps; compliance signals are degraded by the AgentMail relay stripping headers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed open-pixel wrapper** — The `click.s.kohls.com` open-tracking pixel block closes with an orphaned `</custom>` tag:

```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?BO37QIYYRDPEHIRNVLCM2J4VCI.60257..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
```

`<custom>` is not a valid HTML element. While most email clients will silently ignore it, it invalidates the document and could cause rendering edge cases in strict parsers.

**22 click-redirect links unverified** — All destination URLs route through `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears click-tracking). The QA probe correctly skipped these; no broken final destinations could be confirmed or ruled out.

**Three concurrent pixel systems firing:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com` — Coherent Path (personalization/recommendations)
- `mi.kohls.com` — MessageGears open/render tracking
- `kohls.demdex.net` — Adobe Audience Manager (AAM) sync pixel

No technical defect here, but the AAM pixel (`d_sid=13245196`) fires unconditionally with no consent-gate visible in the source, which may be relevant for state-level privacy laws (see §5).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed). Affected images include both tracking pixels and apparent content images:

| Image | Type | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c8e336d6...` (chp.kohls.com) | Tracking pixel | Missing `alt=""`; no `aria-hidden="true"` |
| `o.gif` (mi.kohls.com) | Open pixel | Missing `alt=""` |
| `event` (demdex.net) | AAM sync pixel | Missing `alt=""` |
| `2a5adc5a...`, `f441d45d...`, `c2f81e9a...`, `415c8a18...`, `69494b48...`, `78308d1d...`, `916d6fa0...` (mi.kohls.com rp/) | Render-proof pixels | Missing `alt=""` |
| `54462797_2s_2x` (images.am.kohls.com) | Content image | Missing `alt` — screen reader will announce filename |

The `o.gif` pixel already has `aria-hidden="true"` on its wrapping `<td>` but still lacks `alt=""` on the `<img>` itself. All 1×1 tracking pixels should carry both `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` on the `<img>` element.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several clients (notably Gmail web, Apple Mail) surface the email title in tab/window labels. An empty title is a minor a11y gap for screen-reader users navigating multiple windows.

**Empty favicon href:**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
Will trigger a redundant HTTP request to the email's base URL in some clients. Should be removed or pointed at a real asset.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — Non-standard value; the spec requires `user-scalable=no`. Functionally equivalent in most clients today but technically incorrect.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA confirmed) — The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If it was set at the ESP layer and stripped in transit, Gmail and Apple Mail will fall back to their own unsubscribe prompts, which is acceptable. If it was never set, this is a deliverability and compliance gap — major ISPs (Google, Yahoo) require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** (QA confirmed) — One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above 5K/day threshold since February 2024. Its absence will suppress the one-click UI in Gmail.

**No physical mailing address detected in truncated source** (QA confirmed) — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. This may be present in the portion of the HTML not included in the truncated source; it should be verified against the full render.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers. This is likely a relay artifact, not a sending failure, but should be verified in the raw message source from a delivered copy.

**AAM pixel fires unconditionally** — `kohls.demdex.net` is an Adobe cross-site tracking domain. California (CCPA) and other state laws may require opt-out or consent before firing behavioral tracking pixels. No consent check is visible in the HTML.
