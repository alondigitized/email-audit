# 1,000+ new arrivals for your summer getaway
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is email five from Neiman Marcus in under 48 hours. I want to say that first, because it colors everything else. Yesterday's Designer Sale — the 50% off, The Row, Louboutin — that email earned its send. Even the services email earlier today was a stretch I could rationalize. This one is where the trust account runs dry. I'm being handed a lookbook when I already cleared my Saturday for a sale.
- The creative itself is not the problem. Neiman Marcus logo, black hero, luxury brand names I actually know — The Row, Balmain, Valentino, Jacquemus, Loewe. That's a real edit. But there's no beauty, no fragrance, no InCircle hook, and exactly half the email is trying to sell me men's clothing I will never buy for myself. It reads like a category page that got an email wrapper. Compare that to Sephora's expert-call send last week — the one that opened with actual editorial authority — and this feels like a step backward from a brand that priced itself above editorial laziness.
- I'd glance at the women's grid, note that Valentino and Giuseppe Zanotti are both in there, and tap away. Not clicking. Not forwarding. Marking read and opening preferences to dial NM back to weekly.

## What's working

- **Brand names are doing heavy lifting.** The Row, Valentino, Thom Browne, Giuseppe Zanotti, Jacquemus, Loewe, Ralph Lauren Purple Label — that's a real Neiman Marcus edit. Not a Macy's event dump.
- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Logo, nav, hero, women's, men's — the eye flows naturally even on a fast scroll. No clutter, no visual noise.
- **Immediate text CTAs under the hero.** "Shop Women's" and "Shop Men's" get me to the right section before I've processed the full email. Smart triage for a mixed-gender send.
- **Seasonal peg holds.** "Summer getaway" gives the arrivals a reason to exist — which is more than the "Welcome to our world" filler from yesterday could claim.
- **No render bugs.** Flat-lay product photography is clean and appropriately elevated. No broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Zero beauty.** Not one fragrance, serum, or counter brand. I am a beauty regular — NM has my purchase history. Sephora's expert-call email at least knew why I was there. This email could have landed in any stranger's inbox.
- **Men's takes half the real estate.** If there's any segmentation at all happening here, a women-primary buyer shouldn't be scrolling past Jacquemus shirts and McQueen loafers for half the email.
- **No InCircle signal.** Not a line. Not a point. After the Designer Sale built urgency around exclusive access, this one treats me like someone who just wandered in off the street. I don't spend at this price point without points back.
- **Cadence is the real damage.** Five emails in 48 hours. Kohl's made this exact mistake over the last two weeks — a daily drum of near-identical promos — and I started deleting on sight. NM is one more send from the same fate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with beauty new arrivals for known beauty buyers.** One hero swap — La Mer, Sisley, a summer fragrance edit — would make this relevant to me before I even see the grid. NM has the data. Use it.
- 2. **Suppress or bury the men's section for women-primary subscribers.** Segmentation 101. Move it below a "shop for him" fold or kill it entirely for this send.
- 3. **Add an InCircle hook.** Even one line: "Earn InCircle points on every new arrival." The loyalty-minded customer who won't shop without points back needs to see that signal. Macy's buries their Star Rewards three screens down and it's maddening — NM should do better.
- 4. **Pull back the cadence.** Skip tomorrow's send. One well-targeted email in 72 hours lands with more weight than a daily carpet-bomb. The Designer Sale email was worth opening because it wasn't the third one that week.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to signal curation, not volume.** "1,000+" reads warehouse, not edit. I don't need to know how many items there are. I need confidence you've already sorted them for me.

### 1. Technical Summary

The send infrastructure is functional but carries four open compliance gaps surfaced by the relay: missing `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and unverifiable SPF/DKIM status. AMPscript has two vestigial patterns (a no-op `CONCAT` and a commented-out SHA-256 branch) that signal stale template hygiene and merit cleanup before the next send.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 90.4% URLs by character count (5,214 / 5,771 chars)**
The plain-text alternative appears auto-generated by stripping HTML tags, leaving raw redirect URLs as the dominant content. This pattern is a known Bayesian spam trigger and can depress inbox placement on Gmail and Outlook.

**26 click-redirect links skipped by HTTP probe**
All outbound links route through a click-redirect domain (confirmed by QA). None could be probed for destination health, broken targets, or UTM parameter presence. Manual spot-check of at least a sample set is required before treating link integrity as confirmed.

No issues found with link count or obvious dead-anchor `href="#"` patterns in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Deprecated `max-device-width` media query**
```css
@media only screen and (max-device-width: 480px), screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
`max-device-width` was removed from the CSS Media Queries Level 4 spec. While major email clients still honor it for now, the correct equivalent is `max-width` only. The duplicate rule partially compensates but adds unnecessary specificity complexity.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but represent debug artifacts. `user-scalable=no` in particular is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) — its presence, even commented out, is a risk if accidentally re-enabled.

**CSS checkbox-hack interactive elements have limited client support**
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
```
CSS-only interactivity works in Apple Mail and some webmail clients, but fails silently in Outlook (all versions), Yahoo Mail, and Gmail Android. The fallback state (`.contentInteractive { display: none !important; }`) means interactive content is invisible to the majority of the Outlook audience. No progressive enhancement fallback is visible in the truncated source.

**Layout tables lack `role="presentation"`**
The truncated source shows bare `<table>` elements used for layout. Without `role="presentation"`, screen readers announce these as data tables, degrading accessibility for visually impaired recipients.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor — some preview pane renderers surface this as the preview title. Should be trimmed.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid` is a no-op `CONCAT`**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces no transformation — `@refid` equals `@base_refid`. If `@refid` was intended to carry a suffix (e.g., segment tag), this is a silent bug. If it's intentional, the variable is redundant.

**SHA-256 scenario is commented out without documentation**
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The active path sets `@user` via a Lookup to `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated.EMAIL_ID` (an internal integer ID, not a hash). The comment says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," indicating a conditional branch, but only one branch is active and the selection rationale is undocumented. If downstream systems (e.g., ad matching, CDP sync) expect `@user` to be a SHA-256 hash, this is a data contract violation.

**Two separate DE lookups per recipient on send**
```
Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)  ← for @user / @emailid
Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)                     ← for @uEm / @cmdid
```
Double lookup per record increases AMPscript execution time and adds a second point of failure if either DE is unavailable or slow during send. Consider consolidating into a joined view or a single sendable DE that includes all required fields.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
`[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found` — The QA relay did not capture this header. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, this violates Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5,000 msgs/day to Gmail). Evidence: QA automated finding.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo for bulk senders) depends on this header. Its absence means Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button routes to a landing page rather than triggering a silent POST, increasing friction and complaint risk. Evidence: QA automated finding.

**SPF/DKIM status unverifiable**
`[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found` — The AgentMail relay did not forward authentication results headers. Cannot confirm SPF pass, DKIM signature validity, or DMARC alignment from this sample. A raw header capture directly from the receiving MTA is needed to confirm deliverability posture.

No issues found with physical mailing address presence or unsubscribe link availability (both are standard for Neiman Marcus sends, though the footer was truncated and could not be fully verified from this sample).
