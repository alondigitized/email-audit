# All over your feed 🤩
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- Sephora again. This is the fifth send I've had from them in about a week, and the subject, "All over your feed 🤩," tells me nothing. It sounds like a social-media roundup, not a beauty offer. When I open it, the top banner shows three small tiles: a Savings Event, "30% off," and "Coming Soon." The first thing that actually grabs me is a free Emi Jay trial size, which is a hair clip. That's not what I come to Sephora for.
- After that it's a very long scroll. There's a giant "TRENDING ON SOCIAL" headline, an Emi Jay x Dedcool collab, a "We Think You'll Love These" row, a grid of limited-edition gift sets, an auto-replenish deal, a Venmo promo, and Florals for Fall. It reads like a catalog with the price tag hidden. On 10/11, "Beauty for literally everybody" was also a catalog, and on 10/8 the free Dr. Groot trial size was buried the same way. I won't click. I'd skim the gift sets, maybe note the Tom Ford Fleur Fantastique mention, and mark it read. I'm not forwarding it.

## What's working

- The Sephora logo and Insider bar at the top make it clear who this is from, and I trust the brand.
- The product grid shows named items with prices, like the Merit cheek set at $68, Clarins at $154, and Tom Ford at $405. That's more honest than the "Beauty Your Way" email from 10/11.
- "New + Only at Sephora" and "Limited Edition" tags give me a quick reason to look.
- The Insider tier banner (VIB / Rouge) at the bottom reminds me where I stand, which I like.
- The auto-replenish "Hot Deal" shows a real number: 15% off.

## What's weak

- The subject and the hero don't line up. "All over your feed" promises a social trend, and I get a free hair-product trial with a "$30+ purchase" asterisk I can barely read.
- The top banner crams three offers into tiny tiles: the Savings Event, 10% off, and "Coming Soon." I can't tell which is the real deal, and the 30% tile reads like a teaser with no dates.
- The Emi Jay and Dedcool hair clips and the Venmo block feel aimed at a 22-year-old, not at me. I'm shopping Tom Ford and Clarins.
- The email is far too long. Neiman Marcus gets across its $50–$200 ladder in two seconds, while here I scroll past six sections and still can't find a single clear offer.
- The fine print at the bottom is a wall of tiny text. If I have to hunt for the terms, the offer doesn't feel honest.
- Cadence is wearing on me. Sephora has hit me with SAYPOINTS, trial sizes, Insider catch-alls and now this, and none of them have felt different.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one offer. If the Savings Event is the point, put the real percentage, the dates, and my tier in the hero. Macy's at least tells me "extra 25% off" and a deadline, even when it's dressed up in urgency.
- 2. Cut the length by half. Keep the gift sets and one product row, and drop the Venmo and Florals blocks.
- 3. Use my Insider tier to personalize. Show me what Rouge gets, not a generic "Insider" banner.
- 4. Make the trial-size terms readable at the top: the spend minimum, in plain type.
- 5. Match the subject to the content. If the email is about gift sets, say so.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Limited-edition gift sets, from $25`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Insider savings event: dates inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Merit, Clarins, Tom Ford and more. See what's new and only at Sephora.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Rouge members: your event savings and a free trial size with $30+.`
