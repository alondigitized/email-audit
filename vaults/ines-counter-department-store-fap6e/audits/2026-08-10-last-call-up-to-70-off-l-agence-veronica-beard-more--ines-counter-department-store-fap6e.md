# Last. Call. Up to 70% off L’Agence, Veronica Beard & more
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- There it is — that same crimson block again. Neiman's has run "EXTRA 25% OFF" in this exact red rectangle with the same white block letters at least twice this week, and now it's stacked on top of "LAST CALL" for up to 70% off L'Agence, Veronica Beard, and friends. I clicked their extra-40%-off email yesterday because it had a real deadline — this one gives me "Ends 8/31/26 at 10:00 PM CT" buried down in the fine print, which is three weeks out, so the "LAST CALL" branding feels more like a permanent department than an actual event.
- That said, the merchandise grid does its job — Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, real names I recognize and actually shop, laid out cleanly with prices... except there are no prices. I have to click through to find out what "up to 70% off" means for the pink Balmain jacket I'm actually looking at.
- I'd open it because Neiman Marcus is a name I trust, and I might scroll through the product grid out of habit, but I'm not clicking today. This is the third red-block sale email this week and nothing here tells me why today's different from Thursday's.

## What's working

- The designer name-drop under each product (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, Theia, AREA, Weekend Max Mara) is exactly the kind of specificity that gets my attention — I know these labels.
- Clean nine-product grid, no clutter, easy to scan on my phone without pinching to zoom.
- "SHOP" category buttons at the bottom (Handbags, Shoes, Women's Dresses) give me a fast lane if I don't want to scroll the whole thing.
- The exclusions line under the hero ("Exclusions Apply") is upfront rather than hidden — I respect that Neiman's doesn't pretend everything's included.

## What's weak

- No prices or percentages on any individual product — I'm staring at a Balmain jacket and a Manolo mule with zero idea what "up to 70%" actually means for those pieces.
- The "Ends 8/31/26" deadline is stuffed in six-point legal text at the very bottom — compare that to their "Ends tonight" email from yesterday, which put the countdown right up top and got my click. This one buries the one thing that would make me act now.
- This is the same red hero block, same all-caps stacking format I've now seen from Neiman's three times this week — the escalating "EXTRA 25%," "EXTRA 40%," "LAST CALL" cadence is starting to blur together instead of feeling special.
- "LAST CALL" as a headline with a three-week-out deadline is a bit of a stretch — Saks and Nordstrom both know better than to slap urgency language on something that isn't actually urgent.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the real deadline in the hero, not the fine print — "Ends 8/31" belongs next to "LAST CALL," not six paragraphs down. That's the single biggest lever after seeing how much better their "ends tonight" email performed for me yesterday.
- 2. Add price or percentage callouts on at least the top row of products — even a small "40% off" badge on the Balmain jacket would get me to click that specific item instead of the generic "SHOP SALE" button.
- 3. Vary the hero treatment — three near-identical red blocks in one week trains me to skim past it. Try the moody editorial styling from their Fendi eyewear send for one of these instead.
- 4. If it's genuinely three weeks out, drop "LAST CALL" from the subject and hero — call it what it is, an extended sale, so the next real last-call push still has teeth.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns just added — shop before your size sells out`
