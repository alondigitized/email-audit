# Exclusively for you: 20% off your regular-price purchase
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's email I'd actually take seriously, and it's the first one this week that stopped shouting at me. The black panel is still there, but the red border is gone. It's a clean "20% OFF" in a serif face with one white button, and it feels like the 11/26 "40% off top designer styles" send, which I liked. Then I reached the fine print and my enthusiasm dropped. Beauty and fragrance are excluded, and so are Chanel, Dior, Gucci, Hermès, Loewe, Louis Vuitton and a long list of other names.
- The product grid is where it falls apart for me. The only brands in it that aren't excluded are the ones I'd rarely buy at Neiman's. Chloé, Jimmy Choo, Ferragamo and Rickie Freeman for Teri Jon are all fine, but I'm a beauty-counter person. Yesterday's "$1,500 off (includes beauty & fragrance)" at least acknowledged what I come here for. Today there's nothing for me in the lineup.
- Would I click? Maybe, for a pair of boots. Would I forward it? No. A one-time, one-use code that "cannot be combined" and can't be used on beauty gets a mark-as-read from me. Sephora's "50% OFF starts now" put the offer up front and didn't make me read a paragraph of brand exclusions to find out what it covered.

## What's working

- The hero is calm and legible. One big "20% OFF," one white "TREAT YOURSELF" button, and nothing competing with it.
- The promo code sits in its own cream band right under the hero. I don't have to hunt for it, which is better than Macy's making me "confirm" before I get my 25%.
- "Just for you" and "exclusive code" make me feel like a known customer, not a mass-blast recipient.
- The nine-product grid has clean white backgrounds and brand names under each item. It reads as a designer edit, not a Kohl's-style wall of tiles.
- The "Ends 11/28/26 at 10:00 PM CT" deadline is stated precisely. It's credible, and it doesn't use shouty countdown language.

## What's weak

- I counted no beauty or fragrance anywhere. The fine print excludes them outright, and that's the one category I'd use a code on.
- The exclusions list is enormous. Chanel, Dior, Gucci, Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Moncler and Prada are all out. "Exclusions Apply" is in tiny type under the button, and the real list is at the bottom of the email, where most people won't read it. That feels a little bait-and-switch.
- The code is one-time use and tied to my email address. That's restrictive. No loyalty or InCircle points mention appears either, and I won't use a card without points back.
- I was told "20% off your regular-price purchase for a limited time," but the grid doesn't show a price or an "after 20%" figure. Nothing in it tells me what I'd save.
- The tile grid's six-button category block is generic ("Shop Women's," "Shop Women's Clothing," "Shop Women's Shoes"). The first two overlap, and it looks like filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the exclusions up top in plain language, for example "Excludes beauty, fragrance and select luxury brands." That's more honest and would cut the disappointment when I get to checkout.
- 2. Show products that are actually eligible, with the 20% price math visible. Add at least one beauty-adjacent or accessory pick, such as scarves or jewelry.
- 3. Add a tier or points line. If this is "exclusively for me," acknowledge my account status.
- 4. Collapse the six shop buttons to something more specific, like "Shop boots" or "Shop handbags."
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off regular-price designer styles, ends tomorrow 10 PM CT`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your one-time 20% code: boots, bags & outerwear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code inside. Excludes beauty & select brands. Ends 11/28.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Chloé, Jimmy Choo, Ferragamo & more, 20% off through Saturday.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source shows internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) template comments left in the delivered markup. It also has a viewport setting that blocks pinch-zoom, and no `lang` attribute. The source was truncated and the QA run had a 62% pass rate with 0 failures and 3 warnings, so link, footer and header checks could not be confirmed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** QA skipped the HTTP probe on all 30 tracking links (click-redirect domains), so redirect resolution, 4xx/5xx and destination checks were not performed.
- No broken links are confirmed. Nothing in the truncated source lets me flag any either.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom on iOS and Android and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **CSS-only hamburger menu:** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu` pattern depends on `:checked` support. Gmail and the Outlook desktop clients don't support it, so the nav is `display:none` with no fallback there. It is also not keyboard- or screen-reader-operable.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar are exact-match attribute selectors. They fail if an element carries more than one class, and Gmail's support for attribute selectors is inconsistent.
- **Infinite body animation:** `body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }` runs continuously on mobile as an iOS 9 workaround. It is obsolete and wastes CPU and battery.
- **Global paragraph override:** `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` forces centered text on every `<p>`, which risks unintended alignment in nested content.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and no descriptive content. This is minor.
- **Not assessable:** Image `alt` text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables and the Outlook VML/MSO fallbacks are all past the truncation point.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template variables exposed in the HTML:** The delivered source opens with an unstripped developer comment: `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid ...`. It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and key fields (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
- **Malformed comment text:** The comment has unbalanced parentheses and an unresolved instruction ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"), followed by a second comment `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. This suggests the template scaffolding was shipped without cleanup.
- **Content before the DOCTYPE:** The comments sit ahead of `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some clients and parsers can treat this as quirks mode.
- **Unresolved tokens in the body:** None confirmed. The `@` variables seen are inside comments, and the body is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found (WARN):** The QA note says it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (WARN):** The RFC 8058 one-click check also failed. This matters for Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements, but it is likewise possibly a relay artifact.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA tool states this is expected via the relay.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source, so CAN-SPAM body requirements can't be verified.
- The sender domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain of the brand domain, and `From` is well-formed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTM parameters, `@refid` use and landing-page parity can't be assessed. Links are redirect-wrapped and unprobed, and the body is truncated.
- The `@base_refid` / `@refid` variables in the header comment indicate a `refid`-style tracking scheme. That scheme could not be validated against actual hrefs.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip developer and AMPscript scaffolding comments, including the DE names and `Lookup()` fields, from the production send. Move them to a pre-send build step.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. Replace the `:checked` hamburger nav with a visible fallback for Gmail and Outlook, such as a short link row. Drop the infinite `bugfix` animation.
4. Switch exact-match `*[class=x]` selectors to `.x` class selectors or `[class~=x]` for resilience.
5. Re-run QA against raw `.eml` headers from the original SMTP delivery to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF, DKIM, DMARC). The relay may have stripped them.
6. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML. That would let you confirm the unsubscribe link, physical address, `alt` text, UTM and `refid` parameters, and the destination URLs behind the 30 skipped tracking links.
