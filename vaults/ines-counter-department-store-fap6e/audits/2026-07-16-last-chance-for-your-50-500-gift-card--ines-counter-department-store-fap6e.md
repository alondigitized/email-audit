# Last chance for your $50–$500 gift card
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- I've lost count somewhere around nineteen. Nineteen Neiman Marcus emails since the first of July, two of which arrived on the same afternoon last week, and now this one lands on the same day I'm still processing yesterday's showpiece bags roundup. That said — the gift card earn structure in this hero is the first thing they've sent me this month that made me actually stop and do math. Spend $500, earn $50. Spend a thousand, earn $125. I can work with that. That is the language of someone who refuses to leave value on the table, and NM knows their customer well enough to know we count.
- What they don't know — or don't care about — is that I go to Neiman Marcus at least partly for what's behind the beauty counter. Not a single skincare product, fragrance, or palette anywhere in this scroll. Six products featured: Jimmy Choo shoes, Ulla Johnson top, Zimmermann skirt, FRAME denim, Veronica Beard bag, Theory separates. All perfectly decent. None of them what I came here for. Then at the very bottom, bolted on like it was added in a separate meeting, a pitch to join their text list for 15% off. Two offers, one email, neither one landing cleanly.
- I'd screenshot the code AVPKC and close the tab. I wouldn't click. I wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- The spend/earn table is immediately legible — I can do the value math in five seconds flat without hunting for footnotes
- "EARN A $50–$500 GIFT CARD" as the hero headline is unusually honest for NM this month; no percentage games, just a dollar figure I can plan around
- Brand selection — Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, Veronica Beard — is calibrated for someone who knows these names without being told they're "luxury," which is me
- The promo code AVPKC sits directly above the table, not buried three screens down
- Layout is clean, images all loaded, no broken hierarchy — this is one of the better-rendered NM sends of the month

## What's weak

- Not one beauty product in the entire email — as a counter regular, NM's beauty hall is half my reason for spending there, and this completely ignores that
- The fine print block at the bottom is so dense it makes me assume there are carve-outs that would invalidate my purchase after the fact
- The 15% text-list signup in the black banner at the bottom splits attention and contradicts the urgency of the gift card offer above it — two competing calls to action, neither one winning
- "Last chance" in the subject with no visible end date anywhere in the email body is a claim, not a deadline
- After nineteen emails this month, they still haven't used my name or surfaced my category — Sephora's "Just Your Type" send from this week at least made the attempt, even if it missed

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two versions** — one for apparel shoppers (this one, roughly), one for beauty regulars with La Mer, Charlotte Tilbury, or fragrance in the hero earning toward the same gift card; I would have clicked the beauty version
- 2. **Kill the text-list 15% offer** — it undercuts the gift card urgency and feels like it was appended by a team that didn't read the brief; one offer per email
- 3. **Add an explicit expiry** — "Ends July 18" printed next to the earn table turns "last chance" from marketing language into an actual reason to act today; Macy's does this in their confirmation trigger emails and it reads as honest
- 4. **Consolidate to one CTA button** directly under the table: "Shop now to earn" with the code pre-applied — the six category-level shop buttons are navigation, not calls to action, and they dilute the moment
- 5. **Surface one hero product per tier** — "Spend $500 on these Jimmy Choo sandals, earn $50" is a story; a grid of six brands with no anchor is a department store floorplan
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn $50–$500 back. Ends soon — shop now.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your gift card is waiting: earn up to $500 back`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Jimmy Choo, FRAME — shop the names, earn the gift card. Limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend on what you love. Earn $50 to $500 back. Code: AVPKC.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Commented-out viewport meta tags** — three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` tags are left in HTML comments. These are inert but suggest copy-paste debt from template iteration. Not a render risk, but adds noise.
- **`maximum-scale=1` on active viewport** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks user zoom on mobile. Accessibility concern for low-vision recipients; WCAG 1.4.4 discourages this.
- **`<title>` trailing whitespace** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space. No functional impact, but appears in some preheader/preview contexts.
- **No `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — the `<html xmlns="...">` element lacks `lang="en"`. Screen readers may default to incorrect language.
- **Image `alt` text not visible in truncated source** — cannot confirm all product images have descriptive `alt` attributes. Should be verified in full source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Several AMPscript variables are declared but their resolution path has a documented fallback risk:

```
@user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256   ← commented out, replaced by Lookup block
@uEm  = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)
@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", ...)
```

- The comment `/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */` indicates a scenario toggle was left in source. If the wrong scenario block is active at render time, `@user` could be empty or a raw SHA256 hash passed into a URL parameter — potentially exposing a PII signal in click logs.
- No `ISNULL` or `DEFAULT` guards visible for `@uEm` or `@cmdid`. If a subscriber is absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, these render as empty strings. Depending on how they're used in link parameters, this could break personalization tracking silently.
