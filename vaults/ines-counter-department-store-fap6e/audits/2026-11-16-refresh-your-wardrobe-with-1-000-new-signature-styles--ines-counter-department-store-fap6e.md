# Refresh your wardrobe with 1,000+ new signature styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's email I'd actually want after four days of Designer Sale shouting. Last week's "up to 50% off" send and the "$650 off" and "40% off" code emails were all noise. This one is quiet, black and white, and has no code, no countdown and no red. It looks like a store that respects me.
- It's also a catalog with no point of view. There's no offer, no event, and nothing about InCircle points. The brand names do the talking (Saint Laurent, Theory, MOTHER, Coach), and a 47-year-old who knows what she wants can scan that. But the hero's two little underlined text links are easy to miss. I'd browse it on my commute and probably tap one product. I wouldn't forward it. I'd click, but not urgently.
- Compared with the others in my inbox: Sephora's ninth email in a week was a catalog too, but it at least had gift framing. Kohl's finally dropped the panic with its "Check off your holiday list" send, and Neiman's has done the same here. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" shouted at me. This one doesn't.

## What's working

- The black hero with "Brand New Styles Just Landed" tells me in two seconds what this is. After the Designer Sale blitz, the calm is a relief.
- The brand names under each tile (Valentino Garavani, Brunello Cucinelli, Theory, MOTHER) give me the designer access I shop Neiman's for.
- The women's block has a good mix. The suede boot, the plaid coat, the wide-leg jeans and the burgundy Coach bag all look wearable for me, not just runway pieces.
- The split between women's and men's, each with its own "Shop All New Arrivals" button, is tidy. The outlined buttons are readable and large enough to tap.
- The footer's "Match with a personal style advisor" is a nice service cue, the kind of thing I'd expect at a counter.

## What's weak

- The hero's "Shop Women's" and "Shop Men's" are small underlined text links on a black block. They don't look like buttons, and they're the weakest tap targets in the email.
- There are no prices anywhere. I can't tell if the suede boot is $400 or $1,400, and I won't click through to find out.
- There's no InCircle or points mention. I won't use a card that doesn't give points back, and nothing here tells me whether I'd earn anything.
- Nothing says why now. "1,000+ styles" is in the subject but not in the email body, and nothing in the body says what's new beyond the word "new."
- The men's half is about as long as the women's half, and it's irrelevant to me. That's a lot of scrolling for a women's shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put prices or "from $" under each tile, and add one line about InCircle points or early access on new arrivals. That would lift this more than anything else.
- 2. Turn the hero's text links into real buttons, and put "Shop Women's New Arrivals" first and larger.
- 3. Let me pick women's only (or put the men's section in a single strip) so I don't scroll through half an email that isn't for me.
- 4. Repeat "1,000+ new styles" in the hero so the subject's promise is kept.
- 5. Space the sends out. After four Designer Sale emails in three days, even a good one feels like part of a pile.

- **Subject Alt B:** `1,000+ new arrivals, just landed at Neiman Marcus`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Suede boots, plaid coats, and the Coach bag everyone's asking about. Earn InCircle points.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The truncated source is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style template with a responsive, hamburger-nav layout. It has no hard QA failures, but it has real code-level problems: an internal ESP comment ahead of the DOCTYPE, a zoom-blocking viewport, and no `lang` attribute. Header-based compliance and authentication checks could not be verified because of the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **30 tracking links not probed.** The QA run skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is 87% URLs** (5693 of 6527 chars). The text alternative is mostly raw tracking URLs, which raises spam-filter risk and makes it unreadable as plain text.
- **No hrefs appear in the truncated portion**, so I can't assess individual link formatting.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 on supporting clients.
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick a pronunciation profile.
- **Comment before the DOCTYPE.** The large `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block precedes `<!DOCTYPE>`. Content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some legacy clients.
- **Brittle selectors.** The exact-match selectors `*[class=contentInteractive]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` fail on elements with more than one class.
- **Checkbox hamburger nav.** It relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` (`:checked` sibling combinators). This is unsupported in Gmail and most Outlook clients. The truncated source doesn't show whether a non-interactive fallback exists, so I can't confirm this one.
- **Duplicate `width` declarations** inside `*[class].show` (`width: auto !important` and `width: 278px`) are harmless but sloppy.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template metadata is shipped in the HTML.** The comment lists `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid`. It also names the data extensions `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`, and the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm` and `cmdid`. Anyone viewing source can read these, and it shows unresolved authoring notes ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW").
- **Unbalanced parentheses** appear in the `Lookup(...)` examples in that comment, which suggests it was copied in unedited.
- **No visible unrendered tokens.** I saw no `%%…%%` or `@var` leakage in the visible markup, but the source is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found (WARN).** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may be stripping it, so this is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found (WARN).** One-click unsubscribe under RFC 8058 can't be confirmed, and it is expected by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found (WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` are unknown.
- **Footer not visible.** The physical address and unsubscribe link fall outside the truncated source, so I can't verify them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The 30 tracking links were skipped, and no destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the truncated source. The comment suggests identifiers (`@refid`, `@cmdid`, `@uEm`) are carried in the links, but that's inferred, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the authoring comment (variable and DE documentation) from the sendable template, or move it into the ESP's template metadata.
2. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Re-run the QA against a raw `.eml` source to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the `Authentication-Results` values.
5. Shorten or condense the plain-text part, or use a cleaner text alternative, to reduce the URL ratio.
6. Resolve a sample of the tracking links in a full run to verify landing pages and UTM or `refid` parameters.
7. Confirm a fallback for the checkbox hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook.
