# Cleo Wade's signature style for fall
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- Now this is more like it. After that whole exhausting $250-off math-homework saga Neiman's just put me through — the mango banner, the tiered spend table, four acts of the same rerun since 8/26 — they finally remember what Neiman Marcus is actually for: telling me a story, not doing my mental arithmetic. Cleo Wade as the face of "Signature Style" is a real editorial idea, and the peachy palette with that loose hand-lettered "Fall 2026" feels like the September issue, not a clearance flyer.
- That said, I'm 47, and I notice who's modeling this. The woman throughout has that willowy 20-something editorial look, and while I don't need someone my exact age in the shot, there's zero acknowledgment I'm a repeat counter customer — no tier status, no beauty-event nod, nothing that says "we know you." It's gorgeous and it's generic luxury wallpaper.
- I'd read this one through instead of speed-scrolling to the unsubscribe link like I did with the tiered promo. Would I click? Maybe "Shop the Look" for that Proenza skirt-and-top combo — that's a genuinely covetable outfit with real designer names attached (Carolina Herrera, Christian Louboutin, Emily P. Wheeler). Would I forward it to a friend? Only if she's the one friend who still buys Proenza at full price — this isn't a "hey look at this deal" forward, it's a "look how pretty" forward, and I don't have many of those in my group chat.

## What's working

- The Cleo Wade quote — "I don't put on anything if it doesn't make me smile" — actually sounds like something a real person said, not ad copy.
- "Shop the Look" flatlay at the bottom names every designer (Proenza Schouler, Carolina Herrera, Christian Louboutin) — that's the kind of specificity I want when I'm deciding if a piece is worth it.
- No math table, no tiered code, no urgency banner shouting at me — a genuine palm cleanser after the 8/26–8/30 tiered-box run.
- Clean editorial hierarchy: hero image, personal styling quote, then the shoppable pieces — my eye knows where to go.

## What's weak

- No loyalty signal anywhere — I'm a repeat NM shopper and there's no InCircle mention, no points, no "as a member" anything.
- Zero offer or price visible at all — fine for brand storytelling, but if I'm deciding whether to open my wallet today, there's nothing here nudging me to actually buy versus just admire.
- The models skew a solid 20 years younger than me with zero styling nod to how a Gen X woman would actually wear this — Sephora at least name-drops a specific product I could picture using; here I have to imagine myself into someone else's life.
- "Shop the Story" and "Shop Fall Collections" links sit in tiny text against the peach background — easy to miss on a phone, and neither tells me what's actually in the collection.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an InCircle callout somewhere — even a small "InCircle members earn double points on Fall arrivals" line would turn admiration into action for someone like me who won't touch a card without points back.
- 2. Add one concrete hook — a percentage off, an early-access window, or a gift-with-purchase — Sephora's "Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift" line at least gave me a reason to click today, not just to look.
- 3. Make the flatlay pieces individually shoppable with visible prices, not just names under each item — I want to know before I click whether that Proenza skirt is $400 or $1,400.
- 4. Show one more mature-adjacent styling moment — even a small "as worn on you" mixed-age lookbook link would make this feel like it includes me instead of just aspirational at me.
