# Gone in a flash: Extra 40% off Last Call sale styles ends soon
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Now THIS is the Neiman's I opened yesterday and liked — same "EXTRA 40% OFF" hero, same crimson block, and I'm not mad about it. My eye landed on that number in under a second, no scrolling, no squinting. Second time this week they've led with the hard number instead of a mood board, and I'll take that consistency over the "Sand & Style" beach scroll or the styling-ideas catalog they were sending me two days ago.
- Where it loses me a little is the merchandise grid underneath — Zimmermann, Akris, Cinq à Sept, all designer names I recognize, but zero prices on any of them. I have to click through to find out if "up to 75% off" applies to the sequin Akris dress or the Fendi sunglasses. Compare that to Kohl's, whose "20% off fall home" email at least tells me the discount is live the second I see the product tile — Neiman's makes me do the math myself, or trust the fine print.
- Bottom line: I'd click. The red banner earns the click on its own, this is Last Call, I know exactly what that means (final sale, deep cuts), and I've bought from these racks before. Wouldn't forward it to a friend though — this is routine designer-clearance shopping, not a "you need to see this" moment.

## What's working

- The "EXTRA 40% OFF" headline is unmissable — big, bold, no scrolling required, exactly what got me to open yesterday's near-identical send.
- "LAST CALL" branding front and center tells me instantly this is final-sale clearance, not a random markdown — I know the rules going in.
- Designer names are visible and legit: Zimmermann, Akris, Fendi, Stella McCartney — this isn't bargain-bin merchandise, it's actual counter-adjacent brands I shop.
- Clear deadline in the fine print — "Ends 8/9/26 at 10:00 PM CT" — gives me a real reason to act this weekend instead of letting it sit.
- Category shortcuts (Women's Clothing, Shoes, Handbags, Accessories) at the bottom let me skip straight to what I care about instead of browsing everything.

## What's weak

- Not one price or percentage shown on any of the nine product tiles — I'm staring at a Stella McCartney bag and a Fendi sunglasses pair with zero idea if they're the 40% or the 75% tier.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits in tiny type under the button with no link or detail — after the "no adjustments for prior purchases" line in the footer, I've learned to read that as "read the fine print before you get excited."
- This is the second "EXTRA 40%" red-banner Last Call email from Neiman's inside a week — the format is identical to the 8/6 send, just with "ends soon" tacked onto the subject. I noticed the repeat immediately.
- No loyalty or InCircle points messaging anywhere — I'm a member, and there's nothing here acknowledging that, unlike a well-run beauty-counter email that at least nods to tier status.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or a percentage under each product tile — even just "40% off" or "$XXX" — so I'm not guessing before I click. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Vary the creative from the 8/6 send — same red block, same "SHOP SALE" button, same layout. Rotate the color or headline treatment so loyal openers like me don't feel like I'm rereading Tuesday's email.
- 3. Add one line acknowledging InCircle status or points earned on this purchase — Saks does tier-tone reasonably well when they bother; Neiman's skips it here entirely.
- 4. Make "Exclusions Apply" a tappable link instead of dead gray text — if there are real exclusions, let me check before I add nine things to a cart I can't return for adjustment.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Last Call favorites just dropped another 40%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Fendi, Akris & more — up to 75% off before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final sale on designer sale styles. Sunday 10PM CT cutoff.`
