# Cultivate a summer aesthetic all your own
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Ten emails in ten days from Neiman Marcus — I've been counting — and this one lands at the end of that run as the pure editorial send, no offer attached. Honestly, after the "$50–$500 gift card is here" drop two days ago and the 60% off designer sale before that, the gear-shift to "here's some inspiration" feels a little whiplash. I'm not mad at the concept. "Well Appointed" is the kind of headline Neiman Marcus earns the right to use. The Tom Ford Tara bag stopped me mid-scroll — that saddle silhouette with the gold hardware is genuinely beautiful, and naming the specific style gives it heft that Macy's "shop handbags" can never match. But there is zero beauty content in this email, and I am a woman who spent $340 at the La Mer counter in this store six weeks ago. Not a mention of fragrance, a serum, a summer lip. That's a miss.
- The Marc Jacobs section at the bottom — the pink dress with the "Pretty Things" headline — reads sweet but small. It feels like a second draft that didn't get promoted to hero. The sunglasses strip is even thinner: three frames shown at thumbnail size with copy that just says "chic array of shades." I want to know the designers. I want to see the frames better.
- I would not click. I would not forward. I would mark it read, note the Tom Ford bag for reference, and wait for the email with an actual offer on it.

## What's working

- The Tom Ford Tara callout is the standout — naming the bag, naming the hardware detail, treating me like someone who shops by designer and silhouette, not just "handbags."
- "Well Appointed" as a headline has real editorial voice — this is NM talking to me the way Saks talks to me, not Macy's talking at me.
- The hero model is appropriately styled for my age bracket — wide-leg trousers, layered tailoring, not a crop top in sight.
- Clean layout, no render issues, no broken images — the email loads beautifully and hierarchy is easy to read without zooming.
- Summer-relevant content (shades, light fabrics, color) — at least the season is right.

## What's weak

- No beauty content whatsoever. I am a beauty counter regular at this store. The omission feels like they pulled the wrong segment list.
- No offer, no price, no loyalty touchpoint — after a string of gift card emails and 60% off designer sale pushes, this editorial send lands soft with nothing to act on.
- The sunglasses section is three thumbnail-sized frames under two lines of vague copy. Show me Tom Ford eyewear or Celine. Don't make me squint.
- "Pretty Things" for Marc Jacobs feels like it undersells the brand. That collection deserves more than a small split-panel at the bottom.
- Ten emails this month and still no InCircle mention, no member-pricing signal. I have a card. Treat me accordingly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Tom Ford bag as a full-bleed hero** — give it the same treatment the June 23 shoulder bag email got. That email worked precisely because the bag *owned* the frame.
- 2. **Add one beauty module** — a summer fragrance, a bronzing edit, anything that acknowledges who actually reads this email.
- 3. **Surface InCircle or a subtle member offer** — "Members: earn triple points through Sunday" costs nothing and converts the editorial into a reason to click.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Cultivate a summer aesthetic all your own"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) deployment with no hard render failures detected. Four QA warnings point to missing compliance headers, unverifiable tracking links, and a bloated plain-text alternative — all addressable at the send-configuration or template level.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links skipped — destinations unverifiable**
All 34 click-tracked links route through a redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped them, meaning broken destinations or misconfigured UTM params cannot be ruled out without a manual click-through pass or a pre-send link checker (e.g., Litmus, Email on Acid).

**`@refid` is a no-op CONCAT**
In the AMPscript variable block at the top of the template:
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces the same value as `@base_refid` with no modification. The second argument appears to have been a suffix placeholder that was never populated. If `@refid` is used to build tracking URLs or affiliate parameters, any intended suffix is silently dropped.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out viewport meta tags left in production HTML**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but represent template clutter that increases HTML payload and signals stale template hygiene.

**Interactive hamburger nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-class**
The mobile nav uses a CSS checkbox hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu`). This pattern has no support in Outlook (any version) and is unreliable in Gmail's webmail clients. Users on those clients will see no functional nav without a JS fallback, and the truncated HTML doesn't show one.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor, but some clients surface the `<title>` value as a preview/tooltip string.

**Alt text not verifiable from truncated source** — image `alt` attributes should be confirmed in the full HTML; the responsive image classes (`bgimage`, `mobile`, `ImgSize`) suggest significant image usage.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Ambiguous `@user` variable — two conflicting definitions**

The comment block declares two scenarios but does not clearly indicate which is active in this send:

```
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
vs. the commented-out:
```
/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */
```

The SHA256 scenario is commented out, so the lookup path appears active — but the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" instruction means this is a manual toggle with no guard against accidentally shipping the wrong scenario. If `@user` is passed to any tracking pixel or personalization endpoint expecting a hashed email, the raw EMAIL_ID will be sent instead.

**`@emailid` has redundant double-lookup**
The comment shows `@emailid` is populated by a `Lookup()` with a fallback `Lookup()` — standard pattern — but `@emailid` and `@user` appear to resolve to the same `EMAIL_ID` field from the same data extension. If both are used downstream, one is redundant.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024 for >5k/day senders). Its absence from the relay-captured headers may be a relay capture gap, but it must be confirmed against the raw SMTP headers — Neiman Marcus at this volume cannot afford to omit it.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected (RFC 8058)**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button in the UI falls back to directing users to the unsubscribe URL rather than firing a POST silently. This increases friction and raises spam-complaint risk.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
QA finding: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. The AgentMail relay did not surface authentication results. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have DKIM signing and a valid SPF record — this needs to be verified against raw headers or a DMARC reporting tool, not assumed.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable on all 34 links**
Because all links route through click-redirect domains, UTM parameter integrity cannot be confirmed programmatically. Any misconfigured `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, or missing `utm_medium` values won't surface until post-send analytics show unattributed traffic.

**`@refid` no-op risks broken campaign attribution**
As noted in §2: if `@refid` is appended to landing page URLs as a campaign reference ID (common in NM templates), the CONCAT error means the refid value carries no suffix. Downstream attribution in NM's analytics layer may bucket this send under a default or empty refid, making it unidentifiable in reporting.
