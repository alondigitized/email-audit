# Summer-break style
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Six emails in three days, and then this. After Neiman Marcus spent the better part of this week hammering me with "up to 40% off" and "up to 60% off" in that bold typographic-hero splash I genuinely gave them credit for on Wednesday, they've done a hard 180 into pure editorial mode — no offer, no percentage, no urgency. Just a woman in a blush midi skirt standing somewhere sunny under the headline "Fashion Your Escape." The creative is genuinely beautiful. The layout breathes in a way their sale emails don't. The secondary "Easy Going" block about lightweight layers and mix-and-match packing reads like something a real editor actually wrote. That part I appreciate.
- But here's my problem: Neiman trained me, Pavlovian-style, with their own emails this week to expect a discount. After getting "up to 60% off" sent to my inbox just yesterday, seeing zero offer today feels like showing up to the open bar only to find it closed. There is no bridge between their week of sale messaging and this quiet editorial pivot — nothing that says the sale is still on, or that these new arrivals come with member pricing, or that InCircle points are stacking. Just silence where the offer used to be.
- I won't forward this. I might click "Shop New Arrivals" out of mild curiosity, but I won't add to cart without a loyalty price or event code in sight. Mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The "Fashion Your Escape" hero is clean and elegant — a deliberate step up from the discount-screaming creative they've been running all week.
- Layout breathes. Two clear sections, strong visual hierarchy. This is closer to Saks First Avenue editorial quality than Macy's stack-of-banners chaos.
- Both CTAs — "Shop Vacation" and "Shop New Arrivals" — are visually distinct buttons with unambiguous copy, easily tappable.
- The blush skirt / stripes combination in the hero is sophisticated styling that reads 40s-appropriate, not junior-department.
- The "Easy Going" secondary block earns its space — "await the time to pack dilemma" is specific, useful pre-trip framing.

## What's weak

- Zero offer. After back-to-back sale emails, a no-discount send from this brand reads as an editorial palate cleanser, not a reason to click through to cart.
- No loyalty mention anywhere. I do not buy without points. Kohl's dedicates a callout line to Kohl's Cash in every single email; Neiman has my InCircle status on file and chose not to use it here.
- Cadence fatigue is real. This is email six from Neiman Marcus since July 2nd. The editorial pivot doesn't erase the wear-down.
- "Shop Vacation" links to a category, not a product. Sephora routes me to a named collection with specific SKUs; this routes me to browse. Not the same.
- The app download + "Watch with a personal style advisor" footer feels like product features I never asked for, dropped into an already low-offer send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface one offer, even a quiet one.** "Complimentary shipping on summer arrivals" or "InCircle double points this week" gives me a reason to click beyond pure curiosity. Right now there's nothing closing the loop.
- 2. **Name a specific product or hero piece.** "This season's lightest linen pant" beats "Shop Vacation" every time. Sephora does this well — they name the thing, then link me to it.
- 3. **Bridge the sale narrative.** Even one line — "New arrivals, plus our summer sale is still on" — would have connected this editorial send to the discounts they spent three days training me to expect.
- 4. **Lead with InCircle.** Members should see a status-tier callout or upcoming event preview above the fold. They have the data. Use it.
- 5. **Pull back on send cadence.** Six emails in three days is Macy's behavior. Neiman Marcus should be treating inbox placement as scarce.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport tags** — three conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` variants are left in comments. While inert, they indicate stale template hygiene and could cause confusion during future edits:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but some clients surface the title tag in tab/preview text.

**Dual breakpoint media queries** — `@media (max-width: 600px)` and a separate `@media (max-device-width: 480px), (max-width: 480px)` for product grids. This is a known pattern but creates a gap between 481px–600px where product image classes (`ImgSize`, `ImgSize2x2`) receive no override. Devices in that range may render oversized product images.

No issues found with `alt` attributes or table structure in the visible source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Dead/unreachable variable scenario comment left in production HTML:**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This AMPscript block is inside an HTML comment and will not execute — it is inert dead code. If `@user` is expected to resolve to a hashed email for downstream personalization or tracking, this path is silently broken.

**Multiple lookup chains for `@emailid`** — the comment block documents a fallback lookup (`Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`) but if the sendable DE field is missing and the fallback lookup fails silently in SFMC, `@emailid` will be empty. No null-guard is visible in the truncated source.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are looked up from `NM_CRM_Feed` — if a subscriber is not in that feed, these variables resolve to empty string with no fallback, potentially generating malformed tracking parameters.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (WARN)
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If absent at delivery, the email does not support one-click unsubscribe in Gmail/Apple Mail header UI — a material UX and compliance gap under RFC 2369.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** (WARN — RFC 8058)
One-click unsubscribe (as required by Google and Yahoo bulk sender rules since February 2024) depends on this header. Its absence means Gmail will not show the native "Unsubscribe" button and may route to spam for users who click "Report spam" instead.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed through the relay. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — authentication should be verified directly against MX/DNS records outside the relay layer.
