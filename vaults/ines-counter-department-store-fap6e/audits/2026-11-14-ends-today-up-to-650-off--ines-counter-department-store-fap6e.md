# Ends today: up to $650 off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Neiman's pitch for the same $650-off code in two days, and it's the cleanest of the three. I know what it is in three seconds: the teal block, "UP TO $650 OFF," the code NOVSAVE, a button and the spend ladder. "Ends today" is the only thing that's new, and for once the deadline is real. The fine print says 11/14 at 10:00 PM CT.
- The rest is a grid of nine designer products and six teal buttons. Then the black 40% Designer Sale banner shows up and competes with the hero. I came for one offer and got two codes, NOVSAVE and NMSALE, and I can't tell if they stack. The fine print says one discount per customer, which means they don't, and the banner doesn't say so.
- Would I click? Yes, because a last-day deadline is the one kind of urgency I believe. I wouldn't forward it. I'd skim it, check whether the $300 tier is worth a coat, and move on.

## What's working

- The spend ladder ($250/$50 up to $2,000+/$650) is honest. It shows what I have to spend to get the headline number, which Macy's usually hides behind "up to."
- The code is printed right under the headline, so I don't have to hunt for it.
- The brand names under each product (Frame, Jimmy Choo, Sartorio, rag & bone) tell me this is real designer inventory. That's the access tone I expect from Neiman's.
- The subject line has a real deadline, and the fine print confirms it, with the date and the time.
- The product shots are clean and uncluttered. I can read the whole email without zooming.

## What's weak

- "Exclusions Apply" under the ladder is tiny and low-contrast, and the real exclusions are a dense paragraph at the bottom. Beauty is excluded, and I buy beauty. That's what this customer cares about, and the hero doesn't say it.
- The 40% Designer Sale banner competes with the $650 offer, with a second code and no word on stacking.
- The six teal buttons (Shop Women's, Shop Women's Clothing, Shop Shoes...) are redundant. "Women's" and "Women's Clothing" sit side by side.
- There's nothing for loyalty members, no points or InCircle mention, and no sign that I'm a known customer.
- Nothing in the products looks like it's for me. There are no beauty or fragrance items and no models, just a leather jacket, a slingback and a clutch.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fold the 40% banner into the main message, or drop it from the last-day send. One offer, one code. If the two stack, say so, and if they don't, say that.
- 2. Put "Excludes beauty and fragrance" in plain text next to the ladder. Don't make me read the fine print.
- 3. Cut the six buttons to three, or link each product to its own page.
- 4. Add a countdown or "Tonight at 10 PM CT" to the hero so the deadline is visible in the email body.
- 5. Add an InCircle line, such as points or an extra perk for members.
- Sephora's latest send is cleaner on loyalty, since the Insider tier is front and center. Kohl's Super Saturday is louder, but it puts Kohl's Cash right in the hero. Macy's "LAST CHANCE" email at least tells me what happens if I don't confirm. Neiman's is the quietest of the four, and I like that, but it leaves the loyalty benefit out.

- **Exact-match class selectors:** The mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. These match only when `class` is exactly that value, so any element with a second class silently skips the responsive rule.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't match the subject line. This is minor.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** A comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE>`, which can trigger quirks mode in some older engines. This is minor.
