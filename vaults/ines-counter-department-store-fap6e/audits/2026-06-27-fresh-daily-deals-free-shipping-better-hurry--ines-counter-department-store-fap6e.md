# Fresh Daily Deals + FREE SHIPPING ... better hurry 🏃‍♀️‍➡️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Thirty-three. That's roughly where my Kohl's count lands for June, and I knew it the moment "better hurry 🏃‍♀️‍➡️" showed up in my subject line. I'll give Kohl's a half-point: the running woman emoji actually made me pause in a way that "WOW 🎉" and "Say less 🤗" didn't earlier this week. There's some personality in that tiny choice. But once I opened, the Deal Days chassis was waiting — the same stacked 50%/40% grid, the same Instant Kohl's Cash callout, different product photography.
- The one genuinely new element is "Perfect for the 4th!" buried near the bottom — red, white, and blue merchandise curated around a real calendar event with a "9 Days of Deals" countdown to anchor it. That's a narrative. That's a reason to exist in my inbox today specifically. Neiman Marcus dropped a single leopard-print editorial this week and I thought about it for two days. If Kohl's had led with the 4th of July story instead of parking it below four interchangeable deal blocks, this email would be a completely different conversation.
- I'm marking it read. I won't click for myself. But if my sister asks where to get patriotic swimwear before the long weekend, this email is the one I'd think of — and that's the highest compliment I can honestly give it.

## What's working

- **"9 Days of Deals" countdown** is the first series spine I've seen in this June run — it gives the campaign a shape, a finish line, and a reason to keep the streak going
- **"Perfect for the 4th!" section** is timely, seasonally coherent, and curated around a real date nine days out; the red/white/blue product grouping is the clearest editing decision in the entire email
- **Kohl's Cash near the header** — visible enough to register before I scroll; I don't engage with a brand if rewards aren't in the frame early
- **Free shipping called out in the subject** — honest, up front, no minimum buried in footnotes
- **Clean render throughout** — images loaded, layout held, no broken blocks; the mechanics are sound even when the strategy is not

## What's weak

- **Zero beauty content in the featured deals** — the nav bar shows a Beauty tab, the offer grid delivers bedding, sneakers, and casual apparel; I came to the beauty counter and got handed a bath sheet
- **"FREE SHIPPING" in all-caps in the subject** reads like a store PA announcement; Sephora ran a "Last chance to save up to 50%" email this same morning with genuine urgency and never once went uppercase
- **Four deal blocks at equal visual weight** — no hero, no champion offer, just a wall of percentages; by block three the 50% figure has lost all meaning
- **Demographic mismatch in the 4th of July section** — the styling and models read young/casual/active, not the 47-year-old who buys at the brand event and has opinions about fit
- **Cadence is doing real damage** — this is the fifth structurally identical Kohl's email in three days; even a fresh seasonal section can't surface through that level of inbox noise; Macy's ran a clunky triggered confirmation email this week and it still felt more intentional than this because it linked to one specific next action

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 4th of July story — move it to the hero.** The countdown and the patriotic curation are the only differentiated news in this send. Put that section first, use the "9 Days of Deals" as the narrative hook, and let the generic deal grid support it rather than lead.
- 2. **Add one beauty offer to the featured deals grid.** Kohl's carries beauty brands. If there's a 40% off skincare or fragrance SKU in this promotion, it should be in the featured grid — not behind a nav click. One slot. Show me I was segmented.
- 3. **Lowercase "free shipping" in the subject and cut the ellipsis.** The ALL CAPS competes with the percentage blocks in the body, and the "..." reads like hesitation. "Fresh Daily Deals + free shipping — better hurry" is cleaner and still urgent.
- 4. **Cut two deal blocks; go deeper on the two that remain.** Pick two products — give each a name, one line of context, a button. Neiman Marcus built an editorial email around one dress and it outperformed six nameless deal blocks on recall alone.

### 1. Technical Summary

Structurally sound HTML4 email with adequate client compatibility shims, but the relay pipeline is dropping compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) and a significant number of tracking/content images lack alt text. No merge token failures detected in the truncated source.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped** by HTTP probe — all routed through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com` redirect domains. This is expected for click tracking but means link health (404s, redirect chains) cannot be verified automatically.

Three distinct tracking pixel vendors are active simultaneously:
- `click.chp.kohls.com` (CoherentPath/Marigold)
- `mi.kohls.com` (Marigold/Selligent)
- `kohls.demdex.net` (Adobe Audience Manager)

No issues with the links themselves, but the overlap creates redundant open-tracking calls and increases page weight.

Malformed HTML tag detected in open-pixel block:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?TIMBRMZ5Q5FU5M4FUYDI73HFPA.60264..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag is invalid HTML and has no matching opening tag. Most clients will silently ignore it, but it is a markup error.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA scan. All are functional/tracking images or dynamic content images served from `mi.kohls.com`:

| Image | URL fragment |
|---|---|
| Open pixel | `click.chp.kohls.com/o/5de14407...` |
| Open pixel | `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbb.../o.gif` |
| AAM pixel | `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` |
| Content images (×7) | `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` |

Tracking pixels (1×1) should have `alt=""` explicitly set — several are missing it entirely vs. relying on the attribute being absent. The `aria-hidden="true"` pattern is correctly applied to the `o.gif` pixel but not consistently to the others.

`<title>` tag is empty (`<title></title>`). Screen readers and some clients use the title element; it should match the subject line or be a descriptive fallback.

`user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag blocks pinch-to-zoom — an accessibility regression for low-vision users on mobile.

`max-height: auto !important` in the mobile stylesheet (`img { max-height: auto !important; }`) is not a valid CSS value. `auto` is the default; this is a no-op but indicates a copy-paste error.

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FNAME]`) visible in the truncated source. Recipient ID is embedded directly in pixel URLs (`mi_u=604230016`), indicating personalization resolved correctly at send time.

Both `mi_kc_redm_end_dte` and `mi_kc_event_pe` appear to be unpopulated dynamic fields passed as empty query params. This suggests optional personalization fields that were not populated — likely harmless for image loading but indicates template variables that did not resolve.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM — WARNING:** No physical mailing address detected in the truncated source. This is a required CAN-SPAM element. The source may be truncated before the footer, but the QA scanner did not detect it — requires manual footer verification.

**List-Unsubscribe — WARNING:** Header absent from the captured relay copy. RFC 2369 `List-Unsubscribe` and RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (one-click) headers are missing. These are likely present on the original SMTP envelope but stripped by the AgentMail relay.
