# Obsessed? Same.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Eight emails in eleven days, and Sephora serves me "Obsessed? Same." — a subject line that sounds like my niece captioning a TikTok. I know this brand, I shop this brand, so I opened it, but nothing about that subject pulled me; I clicked on muscle memory, not anticipation. Once inside, I see a Going Viral editorial with RHODE front and center, a skincare GWP banner up top for AESTURA (which is doing real work here), a You May Also Like carousel, Hair, Lips, Harlem Perfume Co., and a Beauty Insider footer — all in one scroll. That is six conversations happening at once, and none of them are speaking to me specifically.
- The AESTURA GWP at the top is actually the most interesting offer in the email — free trial sizes for a hydrating serum and calming moisturizer is exactly the kind of thing I'd pick up at the counter to try before committing. But Sephora buries it in a small gray banner while they give the hero real estate to viral TikTok content. I'm 47, not 22. RHODE is Hailey Bieber. I'm not walking into Nordstrom to buy Hailey Bieber.
- Yesterday's "These picks are everywhere" used the same social-media-speak format and got me — the Patrick Starr reference at least felt like an insider tip. Today's "Obsessed? Same." is the same creative beat, back-to-back, and it's already losing the novelty. I would not forward this. I might click on the AESTURA banner. I would not save it.

## What's working

- **AESTURA GWP is specific and credible** — "hydrating serum and calming moisturizer" with a clear "SHOP TO REDEEM" button is the kind of skincare language I respond to, and it's right at the top where I can't miss it.
- **Beauty Insider badge at the footer** — a quiet but reassuring reminder that my points relationship is intact; Sephora almost always remembers to close with this.
- **"HOT ON SOCIAL +" is a real editorial signal** — even if I'm not the core TikTok audience, I appreciate knowing which products are generating conversation; it gives me something to tell my daughter.
- **Harlem Perfume Co. section is a discovery moment** — an independent perfumery with copy like "to-be-gifted, to-be-savored" is the kind of find I'd actually bring up with my counter rep; small brand, intentional language.
- **No render bugs visible** — everything is loading clean, images are crisp, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Eight emails, two days in a row with the same "viral picks" editorial format** — after "These picks are everywhere" yesterday, "Obsessed? Same." reads as a rerun of the creative strategy, not a new reason to engage.
- **RHODE as the hero is a demographic mismatch** — Hailey Bieber's brand is aggressively Gen Z. Putting it as the Going Viral anchor in an email to someone like me tells me Sephora's segments aren't doing enough work.
- **No price and no event** — I buy at the brand event. I redeem at the counter. There's no event language, no 2X points window, no flash offer in the hero. Compare this to Neiman Marcus's "$50–$500 gift card with purchase" send, which gave me a concrete reward for spending money I was going to spend anyway. This email gives me vibes.
- **The subject does zero selling** — "Obsessed? Same." tells me nothing about what's inside. Macy's "confirm now to get your extra 25% off" at least told me what was waiting. This requires me to open on faith.
- **Hierarchy is fractured** — six distinct sections (GWP, Going Viral, You May Also Like, Spot It Shop It, Hair/Lip, Harlem Perfume) with no visual throughline. The eye lands everywhere and therefore nowhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the AESTURA GWP the hero, not the banner** — that's the transactional offer with a clear mechanic. Give it a full-width hero image, the product names, and a deadline. The GWP is doing the heavy lifting; don't hide it in a strip.
- 2. **Pick one editorial angle and commit** — Going Viral OR You May Also Like OR Spot It Shop It. Not all three in the same send. Each section dilutes the one before it.
- 3. **Swap RHODE for a hero product that skews toward my demo** — La Mer, Tatcha, Charlotte Tilbury, Dior Beauty — brands I reach for at the counter. "Going Viral" can still be the framing, but the product should be something I'd actually obsess over.
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook to the hero** — "Earn 2X points through Sunday" or "Insider exclusives this week" would pull me from browse to intent. I will not use a purchase without points back.
- 5. **Break the same-format streak** — two consecutive social-editorial sends dulls the signal. Mix in a tier email, a personalized restock nudge, or an event invitation.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Going viral this week (and why you should care)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free hydrating serum + calming moisturizer with your next purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The social finds worth adding to your basket — plus a free trial on us.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). iOS Safari since v10 ignores this, but Android WebView still respects it.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` — confirmed issue**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers cannot infer document language; pronunciation and braille output degrade. WCAG 3.1.1 failure.

**Overly broad `text-size-adjust: none` — confirmed issue**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to `body` and all block/inline elements. Suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility settings. Standard practice is to scope this to `100%` on `body` only, not `none` on every element.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 480px` only**
Modern phones (iPhone 15 Pro: 393px, Pixel 8: 412px) fall under this, but the single breakpoint leaves no room for tablet-intermediate layouts. Low severity for email; noted for completeness.

**No `alt` attributes visible**
No `<img>` tags appear in the truncated source; cannot confirm alt text coverage. Must be verified in full source — images without alt text are a WCAG 1.1.1 failure and render as broken blocks if images are blocked (common in Outlook/Gmail default settings).
