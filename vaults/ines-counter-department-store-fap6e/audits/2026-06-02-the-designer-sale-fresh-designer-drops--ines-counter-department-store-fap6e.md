# The Designer Sale: Fresh designer drops
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-something emails deep into the Designer Sale cycle and here we are again — same red hero, same 50% off headline, different day. But I'll say this: "fresh drops" did enough work to get me to pause. I've been watching this sale since mid-May and I know there are Zimmermann and Loewe pieces in there that actually move on me, so the product grid earns a second look. The layout is clean, the hierarchy works, and nothing is broken.
- That said, this is a loyalty customer email from Neiman Marcus and my InCircle status is nowhere to be found. No member early access, no InCircle points reminder, no exclusive offer for someone who has been opening their emails since the sale launched. Sephora at least puts my name at the top — wrong as it may be. This email treats me the same as someone who's never bought a thing at NM in their life.
- I'd click. I wouldn't forward it. A friend who already knows about the Designer Sale doesn't need this sent to them — and a friend who doesn't know about it would need a warmer entry point than a generic sale grid.

## What's working

- Zimmermann and Loewe brand names are visible in the grid immediately — those names mean something to me and they're doing real work here.
- Red hero is clean and uncluttered; the "Up to 50% off" message lands before I even process the rest.
- Sub-category CTAs (SHOP HANDBAGS, SHOP DRESSES) give me a directed path rather than dumping me on a generic sale page.
- No broken images, no layout weirdness — this rendered properly on my screen.
- "Fresh designer drops" in the subject line is the right frame for a sale that's been running weeks; it signals new inventory without requiring me to re-read old emails for context.

## What's weak

- No InCircle mention anywhere — for a brand built on loyalty, this is a miss that Macy's Star Rewards wouldn't have made.
- "Up to 50% off" paired with "Exclusions Apply" is the classic department store hedge; Kohl's did the same thing in their June 2nd send and it felt just as hollow.
- "LIMITED TIME" in the hero but no actual end date — I've been watching this sale for three weeks; the urgency is crying wolf.
- Men's CTAs (SHOP MEN'S, SHOP MEN'S SHOES) occupy significant space in the CTA strip — a waste of real estate for a solo female shopper.
- The product grid is random. Rhude next to Zimmermann next to Robert Graham is not curation — it's a database dump.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with InCircle access or member-exclusive framing** — even one line: "InCircle members: your access runs 48 hours ahead of the public" transforms this from a blast into a privilege.
- 2. **Put a close date on the offer** — "Ends Sunday" does ten times the work of "LIMITED TIME." That single word swap would noticeably move my urgency calculus.
- 3. **Collapse the product grid into a curated women's edit** — ditch the men's entirely or wall it off below; show me three hero looks with price, not nine random SKUs.
- 4. **CTA copy on the hero button needs a noun** — "SHOP SALE" is just barely a verb+noun. "SHOP DESIGNER DRESSES" or "SHOP HANDBAGS" as the hero CTA would improve click-through over the catchall.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Loewe & more: just dropped in the Designer Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in the Designer Sale — these go fast`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh styles added today — InCircle access ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Zimmermann dresses, Loewe bags, and more. Limited time.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email template ships with unexpunged AMPscript development comments that expose internal Salesforce Marketing Cloud data extension names and field mappings. Compliance header capture is unverified, and the plain-text part is URL-saturated at a level that will flag spam filters on several major MTAs.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links — destinations unverifiable**
All CTAs route through a redirect domain (flagged by QA as tracking/click-redirect). Without following the chain, destination URL correctness, UTM parameters, and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 26.

**Plain-text part is 87% URLs**
QA measured 5,140 of 5,910 plain-text characters as URLs. The warning threshold is 70%; this email is at 87%. Gmail, Outlook.com, and several enterprise SEGs weight the plain-text ratio in spam scoring. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from HTML without editorial trimming.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
Active viewport tag:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be zoomable to 200% without loss of functionality. This is a minor but real accessibility failure.

**Three dead `<meta viewport>` tags left in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code; signals iterative editing without cleanup. No functional impact but adds payload weight.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack with no ARIA**
The mobile nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-class state on a hidden `<input>` with no `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, or `role="button"` on the toggle. Screen readers cannot announce or operate the nav.

**Two separate `<style>` blocks**
A second `<style>` block handles the 3-up and 2×2 product grid breakpoints at `max-width: 480px` and `520px`. No functional breakage, but the split means rule precedence depends on document order — merging into one block would reduce risk of future ordering conflicts.

**`<title>` has trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Cosmetic, but preheader/tab display may show the space in some clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: Template documentation comment shipped in sent HTML**
The top of the document contains a raw HTML comment block with full AMPscript scaffolding notes:
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE)
  @base_refid (from Campaign Calendar)
  @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
  @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
  @emailid ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)
  @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
  @cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
  CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
```
This exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and internal field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) to every recipient who views source. These comments must be stripped in the build/publish step.

**SHA-256 user hash assignment is dead code**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The AMPscript `SET` statement is wrapped in both an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`) and a JS-style block comment (`/* */`). It will not execute. If `@user` is used downstream for identity resolution or personalization, this variable is unset — any dependent logic silently falls back or breaks. Verify whether `@user` is consumed anywhere in the template body.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured (WARN)**
QA did not find a `List-Unsubscribe` header. The QA note acknowledges this may be an AgentMail relay capture gap, but the header should be independently verified against the raw SMTP envelope. Gmail and Yahoo (per their 2024 bulk sender requirements) require `List-Unsubscribe` for senders at volume. If missing from the actual delivered message, this is a policy violation.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is absent per QA. Both Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies have required one-click unsubscribe since February 2024. If the header is genuinely missing from the delivered message, this is a compliance failure.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Verify DKIM alignment on the `d=` tag matches the `From:` domain or an authorized subdomain.

**CAN-SPAM**: The truncated HTML does not allow full footer verification, but the presence of a physical address and unsubscribe mechanism in the footer should be confirmed against the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage unverifiable — 26 redirected links**
Because all 26 CTAs route through click-redirect tracking URLs, it is not possible to confirm whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) are appended to final landing page URLs. This should be spot-checked by manually following 2–3 redirect chains, including at least one hero CTA and one product CTA.

**`@refid` / `@base_refid` — campaign tracking token**
The `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` pattern (empty concat) suggests a placeholder that was never extended with a variant suffix. If `@refid` is appended to destination URLs for campaign attribution, the empty concat means all clicks merge into a single campaign bucket with no content-level attribution.
