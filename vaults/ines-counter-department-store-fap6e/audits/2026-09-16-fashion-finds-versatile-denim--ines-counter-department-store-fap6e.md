# Fashion Finds: Versatile Denim
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Nine pieces of denim, zero percent signs, and not one word about price. That's my first reaction — this is a clean, editorial "here's what's in the assortment" email, not a sale pitch, and honestly that's a nice change of pace from the last two Neiman's sends I got, which were either buried in unique-code fine print or a dead ringer for Saks' layout. This one at least looks like Neiman's, not Saks in a trench coat.
- But "clean" and "worth clicking" aren't the same thing. It's a lookbook grid — jacket, jeans, dress, jeans, embellished shirt, jeans, jacket, crystal jeans, jacket — with no styling narrative, no prices, and no reason given for why I should care about denim today versus any other Tuesday. Compare that to the "Style Lessons from a Tastemaker" email I got yesterday from this same brand — that one had a stylist, a mood, an actual outfit I could see myself in. This is just a merchandising wall.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I'm already thinking about jeans — that wide-leg pair and the cropped jacket are genuinely nice. Would I forward it to a friend? No. There's nothing here worth sharing — no story, no price, no "this is the one."

## What's working

- The grid is actually readable — nine products, clean white space between them, no clutter.
- Product photography is consistent and high quality across every tile — no mismatched crops or lighting.
- Designer names at the bottom (Veronica Beard, Zimmermann, Rag & Bone, Frame) give it real credibility — this reads like a curated edit, not a dump.
- No mauve urgency banner, no "one item, one code, expires tonight" theater — a relief after back-to-back promo emails this week.
- "Shop Denim" and "Shop Contemporary" buttons right under the hero give me an immediate way in without scrolling the whole grid.

## What's weak

- Not a single price anywhere. I'm 47, I cross-shop Saks and Macy's too, and I need to know if I'm looking at $128 or $895 before I click — this email makes me do that work myself.
- No loyalty or InCircle mention at all. I'm a member-tier shopper — Sephora's Beauty Insider tier language shows up in nearly every one of their sends, and Kohl's never lets me forget I'm a Rewards member. Neiman's just... doesn't say anything here.
- The copy is one throwaway line ("ranges from polished pairs to distressed street styles") — there's no point of view, no occasion, no "wear this to."
- Nine near-identical denim tiles in a row starts to blur together by the third row — I stopped differentiating jacket #2 from jacket #3.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices under at least the hero pieces — this is the single biggest gap. I shouldn't have to click through nine times to find out what's in my range.
- 2. Give it a point of view like yesterday's stylist email did — group by occasion ("Weekend" vs "Office Denim") instead of one undifferentiated grid.
- 3. Work in an InCircle callout — even a small "earn points on every piece" line would make me feel like a known customer instead of a browser.
- 4. Trim the grid to 6 hero pieces with more breathing room, save the rest for "Shop Denim."
