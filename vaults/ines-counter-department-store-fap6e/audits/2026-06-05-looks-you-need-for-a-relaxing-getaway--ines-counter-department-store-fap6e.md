# Looks you need for a relaxing getaway
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Five emails in three days. I'm Ines Counter, not a forgiving person when it comes to inbox clutter, and Neiman Marcus just sent me a vacation mood board on a Thursday morning with zero offer attached. The hero is beautiful — that floral dress on the landing page is exactly the kind of piece I'd pick up at a Neiman's event — but beautiful does nothing when there's no price, no promotion, no reason to move faster than my weekend browse. The June 4th gift card email earned my attention because it had tiers and dollars. This one opens with "Soak up the Sun in Style" and floats three dress silhouettes, three sandals, and three perfume bottles in the air like a Pinterest board that forgot to include the price tag.
- The fragrance section near the bottom is the one place this email speaks to me. I'm at that beauty counter twice a month. Bond No. 9 in the lineup? A travel fragrance story? That's actually interesting. But it's buried below the fold, below the sandals, competing with the quiet footer. Sephora would have led with the scent story and built the email around it. Neiman Marcus treated it as an afterthought.
- I won't click, I won't forward. I'll mark it read and wait to see if email six brings me a reason to open my wallet.

## What's working

- **Hero image is genuinely aspirational.** The floral dress, the pose, the resort vibe — it feels like Neiman's premium positioning, not a Macy's sale banner.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Logo, hero, headline, CTA, then three category sections in clear sequence — the eye moves without confusion.
- **Fragrance section is the right idea.** Travel scent is a real category and a real reason to shop. Bond No. 9 as a visual anchor gives it credibility.
- **No render bugs.** Everything rendered cleanly. After seeing Sephora call me "Rosa" twice in their last two sends, a clean, bug-free email feels like table stakes worth acknowledging.

## What's weak

- **No offer, anywhere.** Not a percent off, not a member price, not free shipping. For my fifth Neiman Marcus email in three days, this is the one with nothing to justify the interruption. The June 4th gift card send had up to $1,250 back. This has... a sandal slideshow.
- **Hero CTA is a category dump.** "Shop Vacation" goes to a landing page, not a product. That is not how I shop. Macy's at least puts a specific product in the CTA link. I want to land somewhere curated.
- **No loyalty signal.** I don't use a card without points. If I'm spending at Neiman's, I want to know what my InCircle account gets me. This email acts like loyalty programs don't exist.
- **Cadence is exhausting.** Email five in seventy-two hours with no offer attached. Kohl's is at fifteen sends in two weeks and this is beginning to feel adjacent to that.
- **Model skews younger.** The hero model is editorial-gorgeous but looks early thirties. I'm 47. I buy the floral dress too — but when I see a woman thirty years younger wearing it, I mentally translate rather than immediately relating.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach an offer.** Any offer. Free shipping on orders over $200. Double InCircle points on vacation shop purchases. Something. An aspirational lookbook with no offer is a catalog, not an email.
- 2. **Lead with fragrance, build the story around it.** The travel scent angle is the most differentiated thing in this email. Sephora leads with beauty stories. Neiman Marcus buried theirs below the fold.
- 3. **Swap the hero CTA to a specific product or edit.** "Shop Our Editors' Vacation Picks" with a curated 8-item page does more work than "Shop Vacation."
- 4. **Surface InCircle benefits.** Even one line — "Earn points on every piece" — gives me a reason to prefer Neiman's over clicking over to Saks or Nordstrom for the same dress.
- 5. **Dial back the cadence or consolidate.** Email five in three days should earn its slot. This one didn't.

### 1. Technical Summary

The template carries several AMPscript configuration defects that may silently misfire in production, and is missing both RFC-required unsubscribe headers flagged as mandatory by Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender policy. Authentication state is unverifiable at relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**32 tracking/redirect links not probed**
QA skipped all click-redirect URLs — destination validity, HTTP status, and UTM parameter presence are unconfirmed. This is a blind spot; broken redirects or missing UTM params will not surface until post-send analytics.

**Plain-text body is 88% URLs**
QA measured 3,870 of 4,396 plain-text characters as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Gmail, Proofpoint) treat URL-saturated plain-text as a strong spam signal. A properly constructed plain-text alternative should mirror readable copy with selective links, not a link dump.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack — no fallback**
The interactive mobile nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-class state (`.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive]`). This is not supported in Gmail (any client), Outlook (any version), or Samsung Mail. There is no `display:block` fallback, so mobile nav will silently vanish in the majority of mobile opens.

**Unsupported CSS properties in nav styles**
`.menu` block uses `transition`, `opacity`, `visibility: hidden`, and `box-shadow`. None are rendered in Outlook or Gmail. These are inert in most clients and contribute to bloat.

**Four `<meta name="viewport">` declarations, three commented out**
```html
<!-- active -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
<!-- dead cruft -->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Commented-out meta tags are inert but indicate unresolved template archaeology. `maximum-scale=1` also suppresses user zoom on iOS, which has accessibility implications.

**Image alt text unverifiable**
HTML is truncated before `<body>` content. Alt attribute coverage on product images cannot be confirmed from this source snapshot.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA256 assignment is inside an HTML comment — it will not execute**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
AMPscript wrapped in `<!-- -->` is treated as HTML comment by Salesforce Marketing Cloud's rendering engine. If this line was intended to set `@user` to a hashed email identifier (e.g., for onsite personalization or analytics passthrough), it is silently skipped. `@user` falls back to the Lookup-based `EMAIL_ID` instead. If the hashed email value is used in any link parameter downstream, it will be wrong or empty.

**`@refid` concatenation is a no-op placeholder**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces `@base_refid` unchanged. This reads as an unfinished template where a suffix (e.g., a variant code or segment tag) was intended but never populated.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` Lookup calls lack visible error handling**
Both perform `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)` against a secondary data extension. If a subscriber row is absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, the Lookup returns empty string silently. If either value is used in a tracking URL or personalization token, those parameters will be blank for non-CRM subscribers. No `IIF(EMPTY(...))` guard is visible in the available source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** *(QA: WARN)*
Required under RFC 2369 and explicitly mandated by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5,000/day) since February 2024. Absence can trigger suppression or spam-folder routing.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** *(QA: WARN — RFC 8058)*
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail's bulk sender policy. Without it, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button in the UI may fall back to a less reliable mailto: flow or be absent entirely.

**Authentication-Results unverifiable** *(QA: WARN)*
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is not captured at the AgentMail relay layer. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm DKIM signature presence or DMARC alignment from available headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Source is truncated before footer content — physical mailing address presence cannot be confirmed. Should be verified in the full render.
