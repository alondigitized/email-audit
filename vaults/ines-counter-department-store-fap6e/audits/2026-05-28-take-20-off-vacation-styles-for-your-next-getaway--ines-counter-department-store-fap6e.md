# Take 20% off vacation styles for your next getaway 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Twelve. That's the number I've now assigned to Kohl's in my inbox — twelve emails in roughly two weeks, and this morning they finally changed the subject. Vacation styles instead of Memorial Day countdowns. I noticed, the way you notice a coworker in a different shirt after wearing the same one two weeks straight — it registers, but it doesn't impress.
- The fundamentals here are correct. Twenty percent off with a coupon expiring May 31, Kohl's Cash stacking on top, 2X Rewards called out right under the logo. I won't spend money without earning points back — full stop — so Kohl's at least knows that much about its customer base, and the offer is legitimate. The coupon code is right there in the email body, no link-click to reveal, which is a small courtesy I notice.
- But this email doesn't know me. It knows women between 25 and 60 who might need something for summer. The moment "Starting at $4.99" appears for sandals, I feel like I accidentally clicked into a Ross circular. I shop Nordstrom Anniversary Sale. I wait for Macy's Star Rewards events. Sephora at least knows exactly what I'm there for — beauty, not clearance racks. I'd click the Women's Dresses block once, out of habit, and close. I would not forward this to anyone.

## What's working

- The 20% off + Kohl's Cash stack is genuinely compelling — both are visible before I scroll, both have the same May 31 deadline, and they layer in a way that feels like real value
- The vacation pivot is the right creative move after eleven consecutive Memorial Day sends — it doesn't erase the fatigue, but it does give the eye something new to land on
- The coupon code "SHOPPERS" is printed in the email body with a scannable barcode — no treasure hunt, no app required, actual respect for my time
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping elements, no lorem ipsum ghost text anywhere
- "Summer styles for getting everyone out the door… eventually." shows some actual wit — that's a Gen X nod and the only line in this email that didn't feel written by algorithm

## What's weak

- "Starting at $4.99" for sandals in the middle of an email promising vacation style is a brand-register collision — Nordstrom doesn't put its clearance floor price in the hero zone, and neither should an email that opened with vacation aspiration
- The CLEARANCE block is enormous — a full-width banner competing directly with the vacation-style creative, which tells me Kohl's doesn't trust its own concept enough to commit
- "Easy to wear. Easier to love." is the copywriting equivalent of leaving the placeholder — it says nothing and signals that no one in the room had a stronger idea
- Four navigation tabs plus a Boys and Girls section means this was built for everyone, which is another way of saying it was built for no one who'll feel spoken to
- Twelve emails in two weeks: the cadence has crossed from aggressive into numbing — a fresh subject line doesn't reset that debt

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with the Kohl's Cash mechanic, not the coupon percentage** — "Earn $10 Kohl's Cash on every $50, through May 31" is what converts a loyalty customer; the 20% coupon feels like the same note they've played all month
- 2. **Bury or cut the $4.99 sandals feature** — it signals clearance rack and undercuts every aspirational signal in the first scroll
- 3. **Shrink the CLEARANCE block to a single text link** — one line at the bottom, not a full-width banner competing with the vacation edit above it
- 4. **Edit to Women-only if the data says I don't shop Kids or Home** — Macy's segments its triggered sends by department; Kohl's is still broadcasting to the household
- 5. **Find a voice with a point of view for the body copy** — that "getting everyone out the door" line proves the copywriter has it in them; use it earlier and more often
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pack light, save 20%: vacation styles for your next trip`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer getaway starts here — 20% off, ends May 31`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack it: Kohl's Cash earns on every $50 through May 31.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Coupon code inside. Plus, Kohl's Cash through May 31.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard HTML4 table layout with multiple ESP and third-party tracking stacks (Coherent Path, Message Intelligence, Adobe Audience Manager/Demdex). The main technical concerns are a stray invalid closing tag in the rendered body, redundant font loading, missing CAN-SPAM physical address, and unverifiable compliance headers at the relay layer.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped** by QA probe — all wrapped in `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect domains. Destination URLs and any UTM parameters on landing pages cannot be verified from source alone.

Three distinct tracking pixel stacks are present:

- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?…` — ESP open pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel

No broken or 4xx-returning links detected (redirect domains intentionally skipped).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stray invalid closing tag.** The ESP open-pixel `<div>` block contains a bare `</custom>` tag with no matching opener:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""><custom>
```
This is invalid HTML and may cause parser errors in strict renderers or accessibility tools.

**Empty `<title>` element.** `<title></title>` — no document title set. Screen readers and preview panes that fall back to `<title>` will show nothing.

**Empty favicon href.** `<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">` will generate a spurious GET request to the email's base URL in some clients.

**Double font loading.** Both Roboto and Montserrat are loaded via `<link>` *and* `@import` in the same `<head>`, resulting in two network requests per font family in clients that execute CSS:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto…">
<style>@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto…");</style>
```

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta.** Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Evidence: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0">`.

**11 images missing alt text** (QA-confirmed), including all Message Intelligence render-pixel images (`mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png`) and the Coherent Path open pixel. Tracking pixels with `aria-hidden="true"` or `alt=""` are acceptable; the `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` images appear to be content-adjacent render pixels and should have `alt=""` at minimum.

### 5. Compliance

**Physical mailing address absent** from visible HTML. CAN-SPAM §7(d)(6) requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. QA did not detect one; if it appears below the truncation point this warning can be cleared, but it should be confirmed.

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** at the relay layer. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since 2024) mandate this header for senders at Kohl's volume. Absence may be an AgentMail relay capture gap, but cannot be confirmed clean.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not found.** One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for high-volume senders. Same caveat — may be stripped by relay.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown. Given Kohl's sending infrastructure (dedicated `s.kohls.com` subdomain), alignment is expected but unverified.

`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are both present — correct for email HTML.
