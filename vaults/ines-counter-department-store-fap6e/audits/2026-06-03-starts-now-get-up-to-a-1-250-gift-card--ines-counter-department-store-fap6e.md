# STARTS NOW: Get up to a $1,250 gift card
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the first gift card promotion I've seen from Neiman Marcus in the past three weeks, and I'll give them credit: it lands differently than the Designer Sale drum I've been hearing since May. The subject pulled me in — $1,250 is a number with weight to it, and "STARTS NOW" told me this wasn't recycled copy from last Tuesday's batch. The dark teal hero is a genuine departure from that red Designer Sale banner that was plastered on every email through the end of May.
- Inside, the tiered earn table is right there, no hunting. Spend $250, earn $50. Spend $500, earn $100. I can do that math in five seconds, which is what I need. I've been trained by Macy's confirmation emails and Sephora's wrong-name personalization to expect friction, so seeing the whole structure laid out cleanly — no asterisk hiding the real threshold — actually builds trust.
- That said, I'm a beauty-counter regular. I walk into Neiman Marcus for La Mer, for the Chanel beauty events, for whatever Tom Ford counter thing is happening on a Saturday. This email shows me Oscar de la Renta, Veronica Beard, Chloé separates — gorgeous, yes — but not a single skincare product, not one fragrance. And that "Exclusions Apply" link in small type below the earn table is doing exactly no work to reassure me that beauty counts. I'd click through, but I'd be clicking straight to beauty to confirm it qualifies before I spend a dime. I would not forward this to a friend. The spend floor to earn anything meaningful is $250, and that's not a casual Tuesday for most people I know.

## What's working

- The tiered earn table is upfront and legible — no bait-and-switch, no click-through required to see the structure. Saks hides their tier details behind a "learn more" link; NM put it in the hero.
- Code JUNEGC is visible immediately without scrolling. No friction hunting for the promo mechanic.
- The pivot away from Designer Sale is noticeable and earned — after the May 31 Saint Laurent Mombasa editorial and a cascade of "50% off" banners through early June, a gift card earn structure feels like a different brand showing up.
- Designer brands in the product grid — Jacquemus, Chloé, Veronica Beard — are age-appropriate and aligned with what I'd actually consider buying on a big-spend trip.
- The email renders cleanly; no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- Zero beauty representation. I am a beauty-counter regular and this entire email is clothing, shoes, and handbags. If the gift card earn applies to beauty — which I assume it does — that's the lead they buried.
- "Exclusions Apply" does a lot of damage when it sits right under the earn table with no specifics. It turns what felt like a clean offer into a maybe.
- The 15% off text-list signup section at the bottom is a second ask that muddies the message. One CTA wins. Two competing offers make me commit to neither.
- No name, no acknowledgment that I'm a known customer. Kohl's sends me fifteen emails a week and at least one of them usually has my name in the header — NM is treating me like a cold acquisition, not a loyalty customer.
- No end date on the promotion. "STARTS NOW" tells me when it begins; it tells me nothing about how long I have to plan a $500+ purchase. That kills urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with beauty** — even one hero product slot dedicated to fragrance, skincare, or makeup with explicit language that beauty qualifies for the earn. That's the segment of my wallet NM should be protecting from Sephora.
- 2. **Add a promotion end date** — "STARTS NOW through June 15" creates actual scheduling urgency. Right now "STARTS NOW" just means "this week, maybe."
- 3. **Confirm exclusion-free (or name exclusions)** — replace the "Exclusions Apply" link with one line of copy: "Applies to clothing, shoes, handbags, and beauty. Excludes gift cards and sale items." That's it. Honesty is faster than hedging.
- 4. **Kill the 15% text-list secondary ask** — move it to a post-purchase confirmation email or a footer note. It undercuts the gift card as the hero offer.
- 5. **Add one customer acknowledgment signal** — even "As a valued Neiman Marcus customer" is warmer than no recognition at all. Macy's at least attempts the confirmation-email trick to make me feel like I have a deal in progress.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend on new arrivals through June 15. Beauty, shoes, and more qualify.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tiers start at $250 — see how much you can earn back today.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "STARTS NOW: Get up to a $1,250 gift card"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript template with a table-based layout. Three actionable issues exist: a missing one-click unsubscribe header now required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules, an indeterminate `@user` personalization variable that may silently produce empty values in tracking URLs, and a CSS-only interactive nav that fails in the majority of inbox clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** All click-redirect links were excluded from HTTP resolution checks. Cannot confirm destination URLs resolve, that redirect chains terminate correctly, or that UTM parameters are present and consistent.

**Action required:** Run a targeted link-validation pass against the actual click-wrapper domains (e.g. `click.e.neimanmarcus.com`) to confirm 200-series terminal responses and inspect final destination URLs for UTM coverage.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS checkbox-hack interactive nav will silently fail in most clients.**
The template includes this rule:
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
```
The `:checked` pseudo-class is stripped by Gmail (all surfaces), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Subscribers on those clients will either see the wrong content state or a broken nav — with no visible error. Apple Mail is the only major client where this works.

**Erroneous quotes on generic font keyword in `.disclaimerText`:**
```css
font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif';
```
`'sans-serif'` in quotes is treated as a named font, not a generic fallback. When Arial and Helvetica are unavailable, no valid fallback is applied. Should be `sans-serif` (unquoted).

**Three commented-out viewport `<meta>` tags remain in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code — no rendering impact, but indicates the template has accumulated conflicting viewport experiments without cleanup.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — negligible but confirms the template was not linted before send.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable state is ambiguous.**
The template header comment says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" with two options for `@user`: a CRM lookup or a SHA-256 hash. The hash option is commented out:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This is inside an HTML comment — it does not execute. If the active AMPscript block (not visible in the truncated source) does not define `@user`, any tracking URL or personalization token referencing `@user` will render empty. SFMC will not throw a visible error; it will silently drop the value.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.**
Concatenating with an empty string produces the same value as `@base_refid` alone. If this was intended to append a campaign suffix, the suffix was never populated.

**Six required variables are documented in comments only.** `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` — their actual `%%[ SET ... ]%%` blocks must exist elsewhere in the full template. This could not be confirmed from the truncated source provided.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA: WARN).
This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo Mail for bulk senders (>5,000/day) under rules that took effect February 2024. Its absence risks inbox placement degradation or bulk/spam foldering on both platforms.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** (QA: WARN).
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not declared. Gmail enforces this for qualifying bulk senders — missing it is an active compliance gap, not just a best-practice miss.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown** (QA: WARN).
`Authentication-Results` was not captured through the AgentMail relay. `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com` uses a subdomain envelope, which should have its own SPF record and DKIM key. Cannot confirm alignment without direct header inspection from a delivered copy.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism were not visible in the truncated HTML — assumed present in the footer but should be confirmed in the full source.
