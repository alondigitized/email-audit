# Shop now & earn a $50–$500 gift card
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- Neiman Marcus was in my inbox less than 24 hours ago telling me my gift card was "already here" — and now this one is asking me to spend to *earn* one. That whiplash is real. The June 24 send made me feel like a VIP with unclaimed money sitting on a table with my name on it. This one resets me to aspirant-with-a-promo-code status, which is a meaningful step back in tone. I noticed, and it cooled me off before I even got to the offer.
- Taken on its own, the email is clean. The earn tier table is right up front, the "SHOP & EARN" CTA is clear, and the brand grid — Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, Veronica Beard, FRAME — is exactly the right company for me. No emoji clutter, no shouted all-caps discount, none of the Kohl's-style inbox noise I've been dodging all June. NM's design restraint shows. But that fine print block at the bottom is enormous — practically a second body of copy — and as someone who has been stung by gift card exclusions before, I read that as "this is more complicated than the headline suggests."
- I'm marking this read and moving on. The spend threshold to unlock even the minimum ($500 for $50 back) requires real commitment, and I already have unresolved confusion about whether I'm in the earn phase or the redeem phase of this promotion. I would not forward this to a friend.

## What's working

- Earn tier table is directly below the hero — I understand the mechanic in five seconds without scrolling.
- "SHOP & EARN" beats the vague "Explore" language Sephora leans on when it's being coy about what you're clicking into.
- The brand grid is tuned right for me: Zimmermann, Veronica Beard, FRAME, Jimmy Choo — these are names I actually open browser tabs for.
- "ONLINE ONLY" is called out clearly above the headline, so I won't show up at a counter expecting this to work in-store.
- Clean layout, no render issues, the NM logo and nav hold exactly where they should.

## What's weak

- Two days after "Your $50–$500 gift card is here," the messaging flips to "earn one by spending" — that's a confusing relationship between two sends and it makes me feel like I misunderstood what I already have.
- Not one beauty product in the grid. Nine brands, all fashion and apparel. I spend heavily at the NM beauty counter and nothing here speaks to that.
- No deadline visible anywhere — "shop now" is soft urgency I can file under "maybe next week" indefinitely.
- The fine print block at the bottom is large enough to read as a warning sign, not standard boilerplate. It signals exclusions before I've decided I want the offer.
- The product thumbnails are too small to do any real editorial work — that SIMKHAI piece could be a sweater or a cover-up and I genuinely cannot tell. Compare this to the June 23 "chicest shoulder bags" send, where one hero image made the whole email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reconcile the two sends with one line:** If June 24 was "your card is here" and June 25 is "earn one," add a bridge — "Used yours already? Earn another." Without it, I'm confused about my own status in this promotion, and confused customers don't click.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline above the CTA:** "Offer ends July 4" or equivalent. Kohl's Cash always shows its expiration date prominently — that's the one thing Kohl's does better than this. A credible deadline converts the vague "shop now" into actual urgency.
- 3. **Surface one beauty product in the grid:** La Mer, Chanel, or even a Charlotte Tilbury thumbnail would tell me this offer applies to my primary spend category at NM. Fashion brands only reads like a different customer's email.
- 4. **Pull one editorial hero image:** The nine-brand product grid is a discovery mode layout, not a decision mode layout. The June 23 bag image made me pause mid-scroll. This email never produces that moment.
- 5. **Reduce the visible weight of the fine print:** A collapsed "offer details +" link instead of a full paragraph block keeps the offer feeling clean rather than lawyered.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn up to $500 back — no loyalty card required`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next $50–$500 NM gift card is one shop away`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals from Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann & more — use code NM2ME at checkout`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $500, earn $50. Spend more, earn more. Here's exactly how it works.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Earn a $50–$500 Gift Card"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard AMPscript-driven Salesforce Marketing Cloud send with no hard errors in the reviewed source. Three warning-level issues require attention before future sends: missing RFC-compliant unsubscribe headers, unverifiable authentication chain, and a commented-out SHA-256 hashing path that introduces ambiguity in subscriber identity resolution.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by QA probe** — all routed through an ESP click-redirect domain. Destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be verified from headers alone. No broken anchors confirmed, but no clean confirmation either.

No issues found with raw `href` structure in the visible source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<html>` tag missing `lang` attribute** — screen readers cannot infer content language. Should be `<html lang="en" xmlns="...">`.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor but will appear literally in some preview panes and tab titles.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code that signals unresolved historical breakpoint decisions. Should be cleaned up.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints** — media queries fire at `max-width: 600px`, `480px`, and `520px` in separate `<style>` blocks. Overlap between the 480px and 520px blocks (`.ImgSize` vs `.ImgSize2x2`) can cause layout collisions on mid-range narrow viewports.

**Interactive hamburger nav uses CSS `:checked` toggle** — Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo all strip `<style>` blocks or ignore `:checked` pseudo-class in email context. The interactive menu is non-functional for the majority of recipients. The fallback (`display: none !important` on `.contentInteractive`) means the content simply disappears in non-supporting clients rather than degrading gracefully.

**iOS 9 webkit animation bugfix still present** — `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` targeting a 2015 OS. Dead code.

No `alt` text violations confirmed from the truncated source, but images are not fully visible in the provided excerpt.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Ambiguous `@user` resolution path** — the template comment block declares two mutually exclusive scenarios but neither is clearly active:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

The SHA-256 path is commented out with a `/* */` block inside an HTML comment — this is valid AMPscript suppression, but the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" instruction above it implies a manual selection step. If the active scenario was not confirmed before deployment, `@user` resolves via the `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` lookup instead, which may produce a different identifier format than downstream personalization systems expect.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op** — concatenating an empty string literal does nothing. Likely a template stub left from a prior campaign where a suffix was appended. Should either add the suffix or simplify to `SET @refid = @base_refid`.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` sourced from `NM_CRM_Feed`** — if a subscriber is present in `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` but absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, both lookups return empty string silently. No null-guard or `IIF` fallback is visible in the provided source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN) — CAN-SPAM requires a functioning opt-out mechanism. Gmail and Yahoo have enforced `List-Unsubscribe` header presence for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Its absence — even if caused by AgentMail relay stripping — is a deliverability risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (QA WARN) — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is now a Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirement. Missing header means recipients must navigate a multi-step unsubscribe flow, which increases spam complaints and risks sender reputation degradation.

**`Authentication-Results` header not visible** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers. The relay (AgentMail) may be suppressing these. Should be verified at the raw SMTP envelope level for the actual delivered message, not the archived copy.

No physical mailing address or opt-out link was visible in the truncated source, but these are standard NM footer elements and likely present in the unshown portion.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully verify UTM parameter integrity — all 26 links route through ESP click-redirect and were skipped by the QA probe. The following should be confirmed manually:

- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` present on all tracked links
- `@base_refid` value populating correctly into `@refid` for link tagging
- Landing pages for the gift card offer match the stated `$50–$500` range (no bait-and-switch risk)
