# Something special awaits...This is what your hair wants.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, subject line said "this is what your hair wants" and then I open it and the actual hero is 50% off Makeup Forever for "National Lip Day." Nothing about hair until three scrolls down. That's the third Sephora email this week where the subject and the hero don't match — Sephora, please pick a lane.
- Once I got past the bait-and-switch, the Dae Dreamriser hair mousse section is actually fine — clean product shot, clear "NEW · ONLY AT SEPHORA" badge, benefit copy I understand (48-hour blowout). But then it just... keeps going. Bestsellers, Trending Picks, eight more products, a Clinique points push, Dior bestsellers, a new 100-Point Rewards banner — this is basically the "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" wall-of-brands email from 7/21 wearing a hair-care costume. I scrolled through five separate promotional concepts before hitting the footer.
- Would I click? Maybe on the mousse, since I do buy Olaplex and care about blowout longevity — but I'd have to hunt for it past a lip liner offer I didn't ask for. Forward to a friend? No. Mark as read and move on — this is not a "stop everything" email, it's a Tuesday-afternoon scroll.

## What's working

- The Dae Dreamriser hero has a real, specific benefit claim (48-hour blowout) — that's the kind of concrete copy that got Sephora an 8 out of me on the 7/22 hair care 4X points email.
- "CLEAN" badges are consistent and easy to spot across every product tile — I trust that shorthand at this point.
- The 100-Point Rewards banner is new information, not another discount — I'm a loyalty member, so a fresh redemption mechanic actually catches my eye more than one more percent-off.
- Price transparency is solid — every product has a visible price, several show the bundle "value" (Josie Maran $27/$31 value, Glow Recipe $30/$44 value), which is the kind of honest math Neiman Marcus does well in their tiered-spend emails.

## What's weak

- Subject promises hair, hero delivers 50% off makeup — that's a bait-and-switch on the very first fold, and it's a bigger miss than anything Macy's "LAST CHANCE" confirmation email pulled off with its urgency costume.
- This is not one email, it's five stacked inside one: makeup sale → hair launch → bestsellers → trending grab bag → points/rewards → Dior. Sephora already did this exact overstuffed format on 7/21 ("All your must-haves have arrived") and I called it out then too.
- Six sends in the last six days and this is the second one built around a single points mechanic (4X on Clinique here, 4X on hair care three days ago) — the novelty's gone, it reads like the same lever pulled twice.
- Nothing here is segmented to me specifically — no "based on your last purchase," no VIB/Rouge tier callout beyond the generic footer logo strip. Kohl's at least tries (badly) to make things feel urgent; this just feels like a catalog dump.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Match the subject to the actual hero.** If the lead offer is 50% off Makeup Forever, say that in the subject. Don't promise hair care and open with lip liner — that's the single biggest trust hit in this send.
- 2. **Cut this to one concept per email.** Either this is the Dae hair launch email or it's the National Lip Day sale email. Stapling five promotions together is exactly what tanked the 7/21 send for me.
- 3. **Lead with loyalty status, not a generic points banner.** Tell me my tier (VIB/Rouge) and how close I am to Rouge before pitching a new 100-point mechanic — that's what would make this feel like it knows me.
- 4. **If hair is really the hook, put the mousse above the fold**, not after a full unrelated sale banner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your blowout, minus the heat damage — new Dae Dreamriser mousse`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off Makeup Forever, today only (National Lip Day)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `48-hour hold, zero heat protectant guesswork.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: new 100-point rewards just landed.`
