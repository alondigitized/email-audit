# Make a splash in summer looks
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a change of pace — after weeks of Neiman Marcus alternating between "70% OFF LAST CALL" sirens and that spend-and-save grid I've now seen three times, they sent me a single beautiful editorial image with no offer, no urgency, and one vague button. I actually like the photo — that flowy printed dress on the beach is gorgeous, très resort-wear — but as someone who cross-shops Nordstrom and Saks and buys at the events, I need to know *what* I'm shopping and *why now*, and this tells me neither.
- "Sunshine State of Style" and "Shop Vacation" is pure mood board, zero information. I don't know if this is a new collection, a sale, or just seasonal inspiration. Compare this to Sephora's Lip Day send, which opened with an actual 50%-off banner I could act on in five seconds — this makes me do the work of clicking through to even find out if there's a price attached.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity about that dress, since I do love a statement vacation piece. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth sharing, it's just a pretty picture. This gets marked read and probably ignored unless I'm bored on the beach myself.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely striking — model, dress, and setting all read "luxury resort," which is exactly Neiman's lane and exactly my taste.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no banner overload, no maroon urgency box, a nice break from the Kohl's-style "hurry, 40% off, Rewards bar" clutter I get elsewhere.
- Simple nav (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) keeps it scannable — I know where to go if I want to browse myself.
- No fake urgency or bait-and-switch language — refreshing after Neiman's own spend-and-save table showed up three times last week.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no price, no percentage — nothing for a event-and-return shopper like me to actually act on.
- "Shop Vacation" is generic; it doesn't tell me if I'm clicking into swimwear, dresses, or a full vacation shop — and the button styling (thin gray outline) barely reads as clickable.
- Zero mention of Neiman's loyalty program (InCircle) — I'm a member, and if you want me to feel like a known customer rather than a stranger, that's the easiest lever to pull and it's just missing.
- The subject line "Make a splash in summer looks" promises "summer" but the image and headline pivot to "vacation" — a small mismatch that makes me feel like this was assembled quickly rather than intentionally.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook — even a soft one like "New arrivals" or "InCircle members: early access" would give me a reason to click beyond the pretty picture. Sephora and even Macy's beat this send on offer clarity.
- 2. Make the CTA specific — "Shop the Dress" or "Shop Resort Dresses" instead of "Shop Vacation," so I know exactly what's behind the click.
- 3. Surface InCircle status or points somewhere in this email — I won't use a card without points back, and a loyalty nod costs nothing to include.
- 4. Align subject and hero copy — pick "summer" or "vacation" and carry it through consistently.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals: resort dresses just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your beach bag is missing this dress`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flowing prints, vacation-ready silhouettes — shop new resort styles`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `InCircle members: first look at summer's newest arrivals`
