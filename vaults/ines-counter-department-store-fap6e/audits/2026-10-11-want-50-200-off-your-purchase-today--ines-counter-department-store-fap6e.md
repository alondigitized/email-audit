# Want $50–$200 off your purchase today?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-11**
## Executive summary

- Neiman's again, with the same $200 ladder. I've now had this offer on 10/6 ("Grab it now: $200 off your purchase"), 10/7 ("You could save $50–$200"), 10/9, and 10/10 ("Don't miss out on up to $200 off..."). This is the seventh send in six days. The purple hero is clean and the spend/save table is easy to read, but I recognized the whole email before I finished scrolling. It also ends tomorrow, 10/12, at 10 PM CT, so the urgency is real. It just doesn't feel fresh.
- The email doesn't shout, which I appreciate. It's one calm hero, a code and a clear table, and no exclamation points. That's better than Macy's, which would have stacked three banners on top of this. But the grid under the hero is a random mix: a pink Mac Duggal sheath, an Akris punto jacket, Amiri sneakers, an Eton men's shirt. Nothing says "for you." Sephora's "Hey, Insider" at least pretends to know who I am. This treats me like a stranger who shops the whole store.
- Would I click? Maybe, because I have a dress purchase in mind and the 3x points on my InCircle card is the sort of thing I'd use. I'd forward it to no one. Mark as read, and come back tomorrow only if I still need something.

## What's working

- The spend/save table ($200→$50, $500→$100, $750→$150, $1,000+→$200) is clear. I can work out what I'll pay without doing math.
- The code NMCIRCLE sits right under the headline, so there's no hunting for it.
- The "Triple Points" band sits right under the hero. As someone who won't use a card without points back, that's the line that matters to me.
- Designer names under every product (Akris punto, Roger Vivier, Stuart Weitzman) give it the designer-access tone I expect from Neiman's. It doesn't read like a discount-bin email.
- It's calm. The one-hero layout is quieter than Kohl's "20% off... $20 off your $100+" send with the emoji.

## What's weak

- It's the same ladder for the seventh time in six days. By now it reads as a permanent sale, not a limited-time one.
- The "LIMITED TIME" header and the "Ends 10/12/26" fine print are far apart. The deadline is buried in the legal text at the bottom, and the hero never says it ends tomorrow.
- The product grid isn't tied to the offer. Nothing is priced, and I can't tell which of these are "regular-price" and eligible. With "Exclusions Apply" under the table, I'd have to dig through the fine print to find out.
- The nine products are a catch-all. There's a dress, a sweater, boots, a men's shirt and sneakers, and none of it comes from my beauty-counter world. There's no beauty at all. In fact, the Triple Points fine print says beauty doesn't earn bonus points, which is the category I actually buy.
- The fine print is a wall of dense text with exclusions on top of exclusions, and it's tiny on a phone. The "Offer feels honest" signal takes a hit here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the deadline and drop the repeat.** Say "Ends tomorrow" in the hero. If it's the same offer I got Tuesday, tell me so and tell me what's new, instead of re-sending identical creative every day.
- 2. **Cut the cadence.** One offer email a week with a real deadline works better than seven in six days. Fold the Saint Laurent editorial and the offer into the same cycle.
- 3. **Segment the grid.** Show me InCircle tier status or my past categories. At minimum, add beauty or fragrance, because that's where I spend.
- 4. **Put the exclusions near the hero** as a short line ("Excludes beauty, sale items") so I know before I click, not after.
- 5. **Shorten the fine print** and put a "full terms" link where the wall of text is now.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tomorrow: up to $200 off + 3x Points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day for your InCircle $200 offer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code NMCIRCLE ends 10/12 at 10 PM CT. Triple Points on eligible purchases.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $200, save $50. Spend $1,000, save $200. Final hours.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pre-DOCTYPE content:** The delivered HTML starts with a long `<!--REQUIRED variables: ... -->` comment before `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>`. Content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some renderers and adds weight toward Gmail's ~102KB clipping threshold.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks user zoom on iOS, an accessibility concern under WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Brittle class selectors:** The mobile rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=showmobile]`. They stop matching if an element carries more than one class. I can't confirm from the truncated source whether any element does.
- **Checkbox hamburger nav:** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` pattern depends on `:checked` support, which Gmail and several webmail clients lack. The nav has no confirmed fallback in the visible source.
- **Global `p` override:** `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` forces centered text on every paragraph unless it is overridden inline.
- Alt text, `lang` and `role="presentation"` can't be assessed in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
