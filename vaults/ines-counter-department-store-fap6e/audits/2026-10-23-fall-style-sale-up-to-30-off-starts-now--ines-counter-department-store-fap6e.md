# Fall Style Sale: up to 30% off starts now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a Neiman's email with a number on it. After a week of catalogs and "Fantasy" mood boards, a blush hero with a big red "30% OFF" is the first thing I see. It reads as a sale and not a lookbook. The hero is also calm: no shouting, no countdown clock, and one black button. That's the Neiman's restraint I like.
- Then the email loses me. "Select regular-price items" and "Exclusions Apply" under the hero tell me this is a narrow 30%, and the fine print confirms it. Beauty and fragrance are excluded. As a beauty-counter regular, I'm shut out of the sale on the first read. The nine-product grid is all clothing, shoes and bags, and there's no price anywhere on it. Below it, the black Saint Laurent triple-points block is the part that speaks my language. It's a repeat of the 10/21 "Triple InCircle Points" send, and it's buried at the bottom.
- Would I click? Maybe, on the points block, if I wanted a $500 Saint Laurent piece. I wouldn't forward it. It's a decent sale email that doesn't know who I am.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF" in big red serif on blush is the clearest hero Neiman's has sent me this week. I got the offer in one second.
- A single black "SHOP NOW" button under the hero tells me exactly where to go.
- The "Limited Time" Saint Laurent block puts points back in front of me, which is how I decide where to spend.
- The tone stays on brand. It's serif and quiet, and it doesn't shout like Kohl's "Lowest Prices of the Season."
- The six category buttons (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Home and so on) are easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- "Select regular-price items" plus "Exclusions Apply" under the hero is hedging on top of the offer. "Up to" 30% means I don't know what I'm actually getting.
- Beauty and fragrance are excluded, and you only learn that in the footnote. There's nothing for the beauty-counter shopper.
- The nine products show no prices, no sale prices, and no hint of which items are actually in the sale. A Dolce&Gabbana bag could be 30% off or 0%.
- The points offer needs a $500 Saint Laurent purchase, and the double footnote is a wall of tiny legal text. The "Bergdorf Goodman" and "Exemplar Luxury Group" caveats are a lot.
- This is the third send from them in two days with a similar promo shape. After the 10/22 "25% off one item" pair, a new 30% offer feels like discount whiplash.

## Recommendations

- 1. Say what's in the sale. Put a sale price or "30% off" tag on each product, and drop one "select" qualifier from the hero.
- 2. Move the triple-points block up to second position. It's the differentiated offer, and it shouldn't sit under a grid of unpriced products.
- 3. Add one line near the top saying whether this stacks with the 10/22 25%-off code, because I'll wonder.
- 4. Cut the footnote to a few plain sentences with a "details" link. Sephora Insider keeps its terms much shorter, and Macy's "LAST CHANCE" email at least states its deadline up front.
- 5. Make the end date visible in the hero ("Ends 10/29"), not only in the fine print.

- The truncated source shows no `href` values, so I can't confirm link-level problems.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 resize-text concern.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. They match only when the `class` attribute is exactly that value. Any element with a second class won't get the mobile override.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` depend on `:checked` support, which is unreliable in Gmail and Outlook. I can't see the fallback markup in the truncated source, so I can't confirm the nav degrades gracefully.
- **Dead code:** three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants, including `width=1024` and `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, ship in the production head. They are harmless but are cruft.
- **Alt text and image sizing:** can't be assessed from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template documentation comment shipped in the delivered HTML:**
  `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …) @cmdid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`
- This exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option to every recipient. It is also evidence of an unfinished template ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO") that wasn't stripped at send.
