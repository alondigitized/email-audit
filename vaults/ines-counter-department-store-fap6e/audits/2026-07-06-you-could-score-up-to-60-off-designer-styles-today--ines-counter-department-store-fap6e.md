# You could score up to 60% off designer styles today
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Seven emails from Neiman Marcus since July 2nd, and this one just offered me ten percentage points more than the last one. The creative has actually shifted — gone is that overworked typographic "S/ale" block I flagged on July 5th; in its place is a proper red hero panel with "THE DESIGNER SALE" in clean white type. It reads more like the Neiman Marcus I actually trust and less like a last-minute clearance email from a brand identity crisis.
- What saves this send is the product grid. Jacquemus. Chiara Boni La Petite Robe. Veronica Beard. Chloe. Valentino Garavani. These are names I'm genuinely tracking, and someone at NM knew that putting real labels in front of me would work better than a typographic red splash alone. It respects my editing eye.
- That said — the cadence is wrecking trust in a way one good email can't fully repair. And there's no InCircle mention, no member pricing, not a word about points. For someone like me, who literally will not swipe a card unless there's something coming back, that omission stings. I'd click the shoe grid. I would not forward this.

## What's working

- **Red hero panel is a genuine creative reset** — after three-plus sends of that same typographic "S ale" treatment, this one reads fresh and on-brand for the NM I know from Saks-level editorial quality
- **Designer name-drops in the grid land hard** — Jacquemus, Veronica Beard, Valentino Garavani are exactly the labels I'm cross-checking against Nordstrom and Saks right now; this grid is doing real editorial work
- **Visual hierarchy is clean and mobile-readable** — offer first, product second, category CTAs third; I never have to hunt for the point
- **60% off is the highest number NM has shown me this cycle** — it escalates with enough credibility to feel like an actual pricing event, not just repeated noise
- **Category CTAs at the bottom are specific and useful** — SHOP HANDBAGS and SHOP DRESSES are how I actually browse, not a generic "shop now"

## What's weak

- **No InCircle loyalty signal anywhere** — I have points sitting in that program and this email acts like they don't exist; Sephora's July 3rd send name-dropped my Insider tier and a specific percentage; NM can't be bothered with four lines of footer
- **"SHOP MEN'S" CTA rows feel out of place** in what is clearly a women's-first grid; it fragments my focus and adds visual clutter that doesn't serve me
- **No pricing on the product tiles** — I can see the Valentino heels but I have no idea if they're $180 or $980; Macy's at least shows the crossed-out original price, which I find motivating even when I find Macy's exhausting
- **"Exclusions Apply" beneath the hero button is tiny** — that qualifier can mean anything from "a few styles" to "basically everything I want," and the fine-print placement doesn't build trust
- **Seventh send in five days** — even a genuinely good email like this one is fighting accumulated subscriber fatigue; I almost filtered it on habit

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface InCircle in the hero** — one line of copy: "InCircle members, your points multiply on sale." Costs nothing to add, and it's the difference between a browse and a purchase for someone like me.
- 2. **Show struck-through prices on the product tiles** — even two or three anchor prices in the grid change the editorial feel from "trust us" to "here's proof"; Macy's does this badly and clutteringly, but NM could do it cleanly.
- 3. **Suppress or segment the MEN'S CTAs** — I've never opened a men's category from an NM email; behavioral suppression is easy; using it would make the email feel targeted rather than blasted.
- 4. **Add a deadline that's a date, not a vibe** — "before they're gone" is soft urgency. "Ends Tuesday midnight" is something I act on.
- 5. **Rethink cadence above all else** — one excellent email after a 36-hour pause would outperform three mediocre ones per day on every metric; NM is burning subscriber goodwill faster than the sale is building it.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Up to 60% Off Designer Styles"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with standard table-based layout and a CSS hamburger nav. No hard rendering bugs were found in the truncated source, but four automated QA warnings point to observable infrastructure gaps: missing compliance headers, unverified authentication, and an abnormally URL-dense plain-text part.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe** — all outbound links appear to route through a click-redirect domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). This is expected for ESP-tracked email, but it means destination URLs and redirect chains could not be validated. Any broken redirect or UTM misconfiguration on those 26 links is undetected by the current QA pass.

No issues found with link syntax in the truncated HTML.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Legacy reset CSS present but non-harmful:**
- `.ReadMsgBody` / `.ExternalClass` blocks target Outlook 2003 and very old Android — inert in modern clients but add noise.
- Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants (`width=320`, `width=1024`) left in `<head>` — dead code, zero render impact, but signals template hygiene debt.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, causes a literal space in browser tab / screen reader title announcement.

**No `alt` text visible** in the truncated source on image tags — unable to confirm full coverage without complete HTML. If product images lack `alt`, screen readers skip product names entirely.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS `:checked` trick** — functional in Gmail App and Apple Mail but broken in Outlook (all versions) and some Android clients. No Outlook-specific fallback (`<!--[if mso]>`) is visible in the truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The AMPscript variable block at the top contains a **commented-out alternative scenario** left in production HTML:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This comment exposes the variable name and hashing strategy in the raw source. It is not a data leak (it's a comment, not a rendered value), but it reveals internal variable naming conventions to anyone who views source.

**Variable dependency chain is fragile:** `@user`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` all depend on `Lookup()` calls against `NM_CRM_Feed` and `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`. If a subscriber record is absent from either DE, these variables resolve to empty string — no null-guard or `IIF()` fallback is visible in the truncated block. Downstream personalization failures would be silent.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay — one-click unsubscribe surfacing to inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) may be degraded |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | Absent — Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders (≥5K/day) to maintain high deliverability under 2024 sender requirements |
| CAN-SPAM physical address | Cannot confirm — footer truncated | Verify footer contains valid postal address |
| Unsubscribe mechanism in body | Cannot confirm — footer truncated | Confirm opt-out link is present and functional |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header is the most actionable finding here. Gmail and Yahoo Mail enforce this for bulk senders; absence can trigger spam folder placement.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM validation blocked** — all 26 outbound links route through a click-redirect domain and were not probed. UTM parameter presence and correctness on final landing URLs is unverified.

**`@refid` construction is a no-op:** `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` appends an empty string — this is either a template placeholder that was never completed or a vestigial pattern. If `@refid` is used in UTM params or URL query strings, its value equals `@base_refid` exactly, which is fine, but the CONCAT is meaningless overhead worth cleaning up.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Compliance risk:**
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are injected by the ESP at send time. If SFMC is not auto-injecting them, add them to the email header configuration. This is a deliverability requirement for Yahoo/Gmail bulk sender policy.

**P1 — QA coverage gap:**
- Probe the 26 click-redirect links through to final destination URLs and validate UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` at minimum). Current QA skips these entirely.

**P1 — Authentication:**
- `Authentication-Results` header not found in relay capture — confirm SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass for `e.neimanmarcus.com` via a direct inbox test (e.g., mail-tester.com or Google Postmaster Tools). This is unverified, not confirmed broken.

**P2 — Plain-text part:**
- 87% of the plain-text body is URLs (5,268 / 6,036 chars). Spam filters weight URL density in plain-text. The plain-text alternative should contain human-readable copy mirroring the HTML content, not a URL dump.

**P3 — Template hygiene:**
- Remove commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` comment from production sends.
- Add null guards (`IIF(EMPTY(@uEm),"",@uEm)`) to CRM Lookup variables to prevent silent empty-value failures.
- Clean up the no-op `CONCAT(@base_refid,"")`.
