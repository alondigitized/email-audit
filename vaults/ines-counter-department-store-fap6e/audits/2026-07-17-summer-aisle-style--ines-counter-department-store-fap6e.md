# Summer Aisle Style
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-one — and this one arrives after Neiman Marcus already hit me this morning with the gift card extension that I clocked as email number twenty. "Summer Aisle Style" lands as the brand's editorial cleanse: no countdown clock, no gift card carrot, no "last chance" in red. Just a model in a pink column dress standing in what looks like a garden, and the word "couplings" doing quiet double duty. I respect the creative pivot after days of discount fatigue — but respect and a click are two different things.
- Here's the problem: I'm not shopping for an evening gown this week, and nothing in this email tells me why I should be. There's no offer, no InCircle nod, no acknowledgment that I exist as a cardholder who earns points on every dollar. It's gorgeous the way a magazine spread is gorgeous — aspirational, impersonal, and easy to close. Sephora sent me "Today only: 10% off your $100+ purchase" earlier this month and I clicked through in under four seconds. This email wants me to linger in the mood, and I don't have time for that between school pickups and a 4 PM call.
- I'd mark it read without clicking. I would not forward it. If a friend texted me "I need a wedding guest dress" I might pull up the Neiman site directly — but not because this email moved me.

## What's working

- **The creative rest is earned.** After the gift card barrage, a quiet editorial email is exactly the tonal break this sender needed. The silence where a countdown clock would be is a feature, not a bug.
- **"Perfect Couplings" is sharp.** The headline does the wedding-season wordplay without spelling it out — that's exactly the intelligence level Neiman's should be operating at. I noticed it.
- **Tom Ford callout in the second block.** Naming a specific house gives the email a reason to exist beyond category navigation. That's the kind of anchor a real customer — not an algorithm — would respond to.
- **Clean layout, no render issues.** Nothing broken, nothing stacked awkwardly, text is readable. After the Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" triggered email that dressed urgency in borrowed clothes, a clean editorial feels premium.
- **The "Watch with a personal style advisor" footer touchpoint.** Small, but it's the closest thing to a service cue in the email, and I actually use that kind of concierge service at counters.

## What's weak

- **No InCircle signal anywhere.** I have the card. I earn points. This email treats me like a stranger off the street rather than a loyal cardholder. After yesterday's gift card earn push, the total absence of any loyalty acknowledgment here is jarring.
- **Zero offer.** Nothing reduces my price, nothing makes me feel like today is different from tomorrow. Kohl's running-woman emoji email at least came with an extra 20% off. At this tier, a loyalty pricing reminder or a cardholder preview would do the same work.
- **The models skew editorial, not relatable.** I'm 47 and I shop for gowns for real events — galas, charity dinners, weddings. The hero model is striking but I'm not her, and nothing in the casting nods to who's actually buying these $2,000 gowns on an NM card.
- **"Shop Black Tie" and "Shop Evening Gowns" both go to categories.** No hero product, no specific look I can fall in love with and share. Sephora earns clicks by giving you one thing to focus on; this email gives me two broad doorways and no urgency to walk through either.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an InCircle or cardholder moment** — one line, early, above the hero or inside it: "Cardholders earn 2x points on evening occasions through July 31." That single line converts this from browse content into action content for the exact customer who already trusts this brand with a card.
- 2. **Anchor on one specific product or Tom Ford look.** Pull one item from the editorial with a price and a "Shop This Look" link. Give me something to screenshot and send to my friend.
- 3. **Move the style advisor CTA up.** That footer touchpoint is actually a differentiator — Nordstrom pushes their stylist service hard and it works. Bury it in the footer and it disappears.
- 4. **The subject does zero selling.** It's poetry when it should at least be prose.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your summer wedding guest look — Tom Ford & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `What to wear to every summer event (Cardmember picks inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Evening gowns, black-tie separates, and the Tom Ford pieces selling fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn double points on occasion dressing through July 31 — shop now.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer Aisle Style"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript/table-based template with a CSS hamburger nav. Four QA warnings surfaced: missing compliance headers (likely relay-capture gaps), unverifiable tracking links, and a high URL-to-text ratio in the plain-text part. One AMPscript variable scenario appears intentionally commented out but introduces ambiguity.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 36 click-redirect links skipped — destination URLs and UTM params unverifiable | QA: `36 tracking link(s) skipped` |
| Plain-text body is 86% URLs by character count | QA: `3116 / 3625 chars are URLs` — well above 70% threshold; spamfilter risk |
| `@refid` is a no-op assignment | Source: `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` — concatenating with empty string adds no value; if `@base_refid` is empty, all tracked links carry a null refid silently |

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Three commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants left in `<head>` | `<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, ... user-scalable=no" />-->` × 3 — template debt, harmless but signals copy-paste fragility |
| iOS9 `-webkit-animation` bugfix keyframe is still present | `.hamburger fix IOS9` block — iOS9 market share is negligible; dead weight in every send |
| `alt` attributes not visible in truncated source | Cannot confirm; should be verified against full source — missing `alt` text on promo images is a WCAG 2.1 failure and affects screen readers + image-blocked clients |
| `<title>` has trailing whitespace | `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — cosmetic, but indicates template wasn't linted |

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Ambiguous `@user` variable scenario | Comment: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` — one branch sets `@user` via `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`, the other (commented out) sets `@user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. If the active scenario returns null (subscriber not in DE), `@user` is empty and any downstream use (e.g., personalized URLs) silently breaks |
| Multiple chained Lookups with no fallback | `@uEm` and `@cmdid` both use `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)` — no `IIF(EMPTY(...))` guard visible; null results would produce empty merge tokens in URLs |
| `@emailid` defined twice | Comment block assigns it both from sendable DE and from a conditional Lookup — precedence depends on AMPscript block ordering, which can't be confirmed from truncated source |

### 5. Compliance

| Finding | Severity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header absent | High | QA: `List-Unsubscribe header not found` — required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance (>5k/day threshold) |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent | High | QA: one-click unsubscribe not supported — Gmail has enforced this since Feb 2024 |
| `Authentication-Results` absent | Medium | QA: `SPF/DKIM status unknown` — may be an AgentMail relay capture gap, but should be confirmed against raw headers; unverified auth increases spam folder risk |

Note: The QA tool flags these as possible relay-capture gaps. Confirm by pulling raw headers from a seed-account inbox before treating as send-side failures.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| UTM params on all 36 links unverifiable | All links are behind click-redirect domains; destination URLs, UTM coverage, and campaign tagging cannot be audited without expanding the redirects |
| `@base_refid` sourced from Campaign Calendar DE | If that field is blank or misconfigured at send time, `@refid` is empty and all click-tracking attribution is lost — no fallback default in source |

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` at the ESP layer.** Pull raw headers from a Gmail seed account. If headers are genuinely missing (not a relay gap), add them in the SFMC send classification or Sender Profile — both Gmail and Yahoo now enforce one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.

2. **Resolve the `@user` variable ambiguity.** Remove the dead commented-out scenario or gate it with `IIF`. Add a null-check fallback: `IIF(EMPTY(@user), "anonymous", @user)`.

3. **Add empty-value guards to all Lookup calls.** Wrap `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, and `@emailid` lookups in `IIF(EMPTY(...), "", ...)` to prevent silent null-token injection into URLs.

4. **Fix or remove the no-op `@refid` assignment.** Either concatenate a meaningful suffix or assign directly: `SET @refid = @base_refid`.

5. **Expand and audit the 36 redirect URLs out-of-band.** Use a headless browser or redirect-following script against seed links to confirm UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present and consistent on all destination pages.

6. **Audit plain-text part.** Reduce URL density below 70% by replacing raw redirect URLs with short descriptive anchors where the email client renders them, or add more copy to the plain-text version.

7. **Strip dead CSS** — the iOS9 `-webkit-animation` bugfix and the three commented-out viewport tags add byte weight to every send with no benefit.

8. **Confirm `alt` text on all images** in full source — required for WCAG 2.1 AA and for clients that block images by default.
