# Things We Love
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's I like. After the week they've had, it's a relief. On 10/1 I got the tier table, then 15% off beauty, then JUST4U with a code, then "Last chance: 15% off beauty." Now there's no percentage, no code and no countdown. It's just a Loewe bag and some pretty things. It reads like a magazine page, and nobody is shouting at me.
- It also asks nothing of me, and I notice that. The subject gives me no reason to open it today. Sephora at least said "LAST CALL: up to 40% off hair faves," and Macy's put "extra 25%" right in the subject. Neiman's doesn't tell me anything about the contents until I'm already inside. I'd open it because I trust the sender. I'd browse, maybe tap the Creed, and I wouldn't buy off the email.
- I'd skim it and leave it unread in a folder. I wouldn't forward it. Nothing in it says "you need this before Friday." Still, after four days of math from them, I'd rather get this than a fifth discount email.

## What's working

- The Loewe Whisker bag fills the top of the email. The bag, not a banner, is the offer, and that's the designer-access tone I expect from Neiman's.
- There's no fine print and no exclusion language. After Kohl's "mystery savings" run from 9/29 through 10/4, a send with nothing to decode is welcome.
- Creed Aventus Iris and Mind Games sit in the beauty tier, with real scent notes (lavender, iris pallida, gardenia). That's the ingredient detail I use to decide.
- The layout is clean and the text is readable without zooming. The serif title is calm, and the Marc Jacobs and Gianvito Rossi tiles give the eye room.
- The Helmut Lang and Veronica Beard jackets read as fall, so the timing is right. I also saw Veronica Beard on 10/4, so I know the brand is in rotation.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, no points and no event. I won't use a card without points back, and nothing here ties to Neiman's InCircle or any tier benefit. That's the opposite of the tier table they sent on 10/1.
- Every "Shop Now" is a small underlined text link. On a phone those are thin tap targets, and there's no button to find. "Shop Contemporary" is just as understated.
- The middle row is labeled "Brand Name" under the black jacket. That reads like a placeholder, so I can see the label but can't tell which brand it is.
- The models are very young and styled for a different customer. The beauty tiles are the only part that fits a 47-year-old who buys at the counter.
- This is the fifth Neiman's send in five days, and it has no urgency. The cadence feels automatic.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a member line near the top, such as "InCircle: earn points on beauty through Sunday." Without it, this is a brand mood board. With it, I have a reason to act.
- 2. Turn "Shop Now" into full-width buttons with specific copy, such as "Shop the Whisker bag" and "Shop Creed Aventus Iris." I'd also fix the "Brand Name" label on the black jacket.
- 3. Give the beauty tiles more weight. Fragrance is my category and it's at the bottom. Move Creed and Mind Games above the footwear, or lead with a beauty-counter hero.
- 4. Space out the sends. If something goes out every day, vary what it asks of me.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- The truncated source contains no `href` values, so I can't comment on UTM or link structure.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-zoom on some mobile clients, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language declared:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't select a pronunciation profile.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and no descriptive content. Some clients and screen readers use it.
- **Dead code in `<head>`:** three commented-out viewport variants remain (`user-scalable=no`, `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`). They are harmless but clutter the source.
- **CSS-only hamburger nav:** The CSS uses the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` pattern. Gmail and several Outlook builds don't support `:checked`. If there is no fallback markup, the nav could be unreachable in those clients. This is inferred from CSS only and is not confirmed, because the body is truncated.
- **Images/alt text:** not assessable from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found** (QA WARN). The tool notes the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so absence is **unconfirmed**.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found** (RFC 8058) (QA WARN). Same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements expect one-click unsubscribe.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the template comment** (the `REQUIRED variables…CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` block and the `SET @user` line) in the send pipeline, or move it out of the deliverable. It leaks DE and field names.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta (use `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`).
3. **Add `lang="en"`** to `<html>` and give the `<title>` descriptive text (for example the subject line).
4. **Check the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook.** Confirm a non-`:checked` fallback exists, or that the nav links are in the footer.
5. **Shorten the plain-text part** by using short or branded link wrappers, or by trimming repeated product URLs, to get below the 70% URL ratio.
