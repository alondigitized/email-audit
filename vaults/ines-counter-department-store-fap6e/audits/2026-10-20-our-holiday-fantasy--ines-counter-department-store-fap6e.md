# Our Holiday Fantasy
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is pretty and I have no reason to act on it. It's a gown with a giant red bow, a tailor's fitting scene, and a girl on the floor in a purple skirt. It's a mood board, not a sale, and I'm not sure which product I'm meant to want.
- This is also well past a dozen Neiman's emails in about a week. Earlier today I got "Ends today: Earn a $100–$1,250 gift card with your purchase." Before that came the tomato-red "First Call" up to 40% off blocks and the Tom Ford beauty-shot send on 10/18. After all that shouting, a quiet editorial email is nice. But there's no offer, no price and no InCircle mention. I'd still be looking for a reason to open my wallet.
- Would I click? Maybe "Shop Holiday Dressing" if I had an event coming up. Would I forward it? No. I'd read it as a lookbook and mark it read.

## What's working

- The red-bow gown is a real stop-the-scroll image. It's holiday without being tacky, and it feels like Neiman's rather than a clearance rack.
- After the earlier gift-card ladder and the red "First Call" blocks, this one lets me breathe. It doesn't shout.
- The fitting-room photo makes the tailoring and personal-service angle feel high-end.
- The model looks like a grown woman, not a teenager. That's more respectful of a 47-year-old shopper than a lot of retail email.
- The footer's "Match with a personal style advisor" is the one thing that sounds like it's for me. The app download is a fine utility add.

## What's weak

- There's no offer anywhere. No percent, no gift card, no points event, no free alterations. Sephora Insider at least opens with "4X points" and a code, and Macy's tells me my extra 25% is waiting.
- The "Hol / iday / Fa / nt / asy" headline is chopped into fragments. It looks arty, but I had to stop and sound it out.
- The only CTAs are two small underlined text links, "Shop the Story" and "Shop Holiday Dressing". They aren't buttons, they're a thin tap target on a phone, and they don't say what I'd see.
- The third photo, the woman on the floor, has no copy, no link and no product name. I can't tell what I'm supposed to buy. Is it the skirt, the blouse or the shoes?
- There's no mention of InCircle or my tier. I'm a regular and this email treats me like a stranger. Neiman's has a loyalty program and it's absent here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete reason to act in the hero. That could be an InCircle points multiplier, free alterations on holiday dressing, or the gift-card tier with its deadline. That's the single biggest lift.
- 2. Make the CTA a visible button with specific copy, like "Shop party dresses" or "Book a style advisor".
- 3. Tag each of the three images with a product name and a link. Dead imagery is wasted real estate.
- 4. Set the headline on a single line, or break it at word boundaries.
- 5. Surface my tier or points balance near the top.
- 6. Space the sends out. Give me one strong email a day instead of three similar ones.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Our Holiday Fantasy"

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Plain-text part:** `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 3216/3722 chars are URLs` (about 86%). The text alternative is dominated by raw tracking URLs. This is a real deliverability and readability concern.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on supporting clients (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the correct pronunciation.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the subject. This is minor.
- **Checkbox hamburger nav:** The mobile menu uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. Gmail and many Outlook clients don't support `:checked`. The CSS hides interactive content with `display:none` by default, but I can't confirm a fallback because the body is truncated.
- **Attribute selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]` and similar exact-match selectors break if an element has multiple classes. Whether this affects any element can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Alt text, image dimensions, and contrast:** Not assessable.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal documentation leaked:** The first HTML comment (`<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) ... Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) -->`) is present in the delivered source. It exposes Data Extension names, field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option, plus a stray `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` template instruction. This is an authoring artifact that should be stripped at send time.
- No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `@var`) appear in the visible rendered portion, other than inside that comment.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found` and `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`. The QA notes say the relay may strip these headers. They are **unconfirmed**, not a verified defect.
- `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status are **unknown**. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice, but it can't be verified here.
- The footer unsubscribe link and physical postal address were not in the truncated source, so they are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Not assessable. The links, UTM parameters, and landing pages are all in the truncated body or behind skipped tracking redirects. The comment references `@refid`/`@base_refid` campaign tracking, but I can't see how it is applied to URLs.

1. **Strip the leading variable-documentation comment** from the production send, ideally in a pre-send build step.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta.
3. **Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"`** to `<html>`, and give `<title>` a meaningful value without the trailing space.
4. **Shorten the plain-text part** by using fewer or shorter URLs, and add descriptive text around them.
