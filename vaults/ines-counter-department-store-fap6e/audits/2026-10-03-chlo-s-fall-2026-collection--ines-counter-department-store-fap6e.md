# Chloé’s Fall 2026 Collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the Neiman's I like, and it's the right send after four days of math. On 10/1 I got the tier table, then 15% off beauty, then the shoulder-bag roundup, and this morning JUST4U. Now there's no code, no percentage and no countdown. There's one big image of a woman in a pom-pom knit with a black Chloé bag on her shoulder, and a serif headline. I'll admit the bag works on me. It looks like a real bag you could carry to work and not just a runway piece.
- It's also a little too quiet. "Up Your Cool Quotient" is a cute line, but it doesn't say anything. I don't see a bag price, a collection name, or any hint of what's new for Fall 2026. The model is maybe 25, and the sweater is styled young. I'm 47 and I'd be buying the bag, not the look. I also see nothing about InCircle points. Sephora put SAYPOINTS and "Ends 10/7" at the top of its last email, and I knew exactly why I was there. Here I have to guess.
- Would I click? Probably, just to look at the bag, and only because the brand has earned some trust this week. I wouldn't forward it. It's a mood board, not a reason to act. I'd mark it read and maybe come back on the weekend.

## What's working

- One hero image and one idea. After the tier table and the JUST4U mustard block, the white space is a relief.
- The black Chloé bag is easy to see, in focus, and carried on the shoulder like a person would carry it.
- The serif headline and the thin-outlined buttons feel like a department store counter, not a clearance rack. Macy's would have put three banners above this.
- The two CTAs, "Shop Chloé" and "Shop Designer Collections," are verb-plus-noun and sit right under the headline.
- The footer is tidy, with the app, the style advisor, find a store, and my account. The personal style advisor is a quiet, useful nod to how I actually shop.

## What's weak

- "Up Your Cool Quotient" is a pun with no product behind it. I can't tell if this is a collection, a trend or a sale.
- There's no price, no offer, no InCircle points and no event. Without a deadline there's no reason to click today. Sephora and Kohl's both tell me what I earn.
- The model and the styling skew very young. A cream pom-pom knit with leather pants isn't my wardrobe. I'd want to see the bag on someone my age.
- The footer reads "Find a store" and "Contact us" in the same weight as the style advisor. The advisor is the real benefit and it's buried.
- Both buttons are light grey text in thin outlines. On a phone they're readable, but they don't pop, and "Shop Designer Collections" is wide-open with no hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the product and the benefit in the headline: the bag, its price range, or "new this week." Tell me what I'm clicking into.
- 2. Add one InCircle line under the buttons, like "Earn double points on Chloé through 10/10." That's what makes me pick Neiman's over Nordstrom or Saks.
- 3. Link the hero image straight to the bag. Don't send me to a general Chloé page.
- 4. Show a second look or a second woman in a different age range, so I see the bag on someone like me.
- 5. Move "Match with a personal style advisor" up and make it a button. It's the one thing in the footer that's worth a click.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a standard table-based SFMC email with responsive CSS. It has two code-level defects: unresolved ESP template comments and a viewport meta that blocks zoom. The four QA warnings are mostly relay-capture limitations, not confirmed defects. The source is truncated, so body links, alt text and the footer could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** 16 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so redirect health and destination status are unknown.
- **Plain-text part:** 2,820 of 3,308 characters (about 85%) are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. This is consistent with long redirect URLs in the text part. It is a modest spam-filter risk.
- No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing `lang`:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, which screen readers use to pick pronunciation.
- **Doctype and comment order:** a large comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some older Outlook/IE-based renderers can drop into quirks mode when content precedes the doctype. This is low risk.
- **Hamburger nav:** the mobile menu relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` (checkbox hack), with a `-webkit-animation: bugfix` iOS 9 workaround. It will not work in Gmail or Outlook, and no fallback is visible in the truncated source. Treat this as unverified.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. It is minor.
- Alt text, image dimensions and contrast could not be checked because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment leaked into delivered HTML:** the opening comment exposes internal ESP scaffolding:
  - `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`
  - Data Extension names `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`
  - Lookup logic
  - An unresolved authoring instruction: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`
  - `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`
  
  This is an information disclosure about internal data architecture and indicates incomplete template cleanup. It should be stripped at send time with an AMPscript or SSJS comment (`%%[ /* */ ]%%`) rather than an HTML comment.
- No unrendered `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens were visible in the portion provided.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** the header was not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip it, so this is **unconfirmed**. Verify against the raw message at the sending MTA.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** not found, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click support, so confirm it.
- **Authentication-Results:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Test it at the source, not through the relay.
- The footer unsubscribe link and postal address were not in the truncated source, so they could not be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No issues could be confirmed. The 16 links were not probed, and no UTM parameters or landing URLs appear in the visible source.
- The `@refid` and `@base_refid` variables suggest campaign tracking is handled by reference IDs, possibly instead of UTMs. This is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the leaked SFMC variable and DE-name comment from the rendered output, including the `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` text.
2. Change the viewport to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`, dropping `maximum-scale=1`.
3. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the original send, and check SPF, DKIM and DMARC from the raw headers.
5. Re-run the link probe against the 16 redirect links by following the redirects to the final landing pages, and check for UTM or refid parameters.
6. Shorten the text-part URLs, or reduce the number of links in the plain-text version, to lower the URL ratio.
7. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the mobile nav, or confirm it degrades gracefully in Gmail and Outlook.
