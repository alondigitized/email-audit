# 1,000+ new arrivals for wardrobe building
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a whiplash moment. After living through that entire $250-off tiered saga with its mango banners and math homework, Neiman's suddenly remembers they're a luxury department store and sends me... a clean grid of new arrivals. No code, no threshold, no urgency copy — just STAUD, Versace, Chloe, Miu Miu, Dior. That's the Neiman's I actually shop.
- The problem is there's zero offer and zero personalization here. I'm a beauty-counter and designer regular who cross-shops Saks and Nordstrom, and this reads like a catalog blast to literally anyone with a Neiman's account — no nod to my purchase history, no "since you loved Chloe last season" thread. It's pretty, but pretty isn't the same as relevant.
- I'd skim it on my phone during coffee, maybe linger on the Chloe striped blouse and the Prada loafers, but I wouldn't click through past the thumbnail grid, and I definitely wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here worth telling a friend about. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED" hero is confident and on-brand — no discount screaming, just editorial black-and-white type, which is exactly the Neiman's tone I expect and Macy's never quite pulls off.
- Clean women's/men's split with real designer names front and center — Versace, Chloe, Dior, Kiton — that's the aspirational signal that gets me to trust the send.
- "Match with a personal style advisor" at the footer is a nice touch for a customer like me who actually uses that service in-store.
- No render bugs — grid is tidy, images loaded, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- Zero price visibility anywhere — not even a "starting at" — so I have no idea if I'm looking at $200 or $2,000 pieces before I click.
- No loyalty or InCircle mention at all, which stings given I'm the exact tier-conscious shopper who checks for that first.
- Totally generic — this could've landed in anyone's inbox regardless of what I've bought before; compare that to Sephora's "Your A-list-beauty edit," which at least gestures at personalization even if it's thin.
- After the $250-off saga and the Roundup crossbody send on 8/30, this is now the third distinct creative approach from Neiman's in a week — cadence is getting noisy even when each individual send is fine.
- CTA buttons ("Shop All Women's New Arrivals") are generic verb-noun with no reason to act now — no urgency, no scarcity, nothing time-bound.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price points or a "from $X" under at least the hero pieces — I shouldn't have to click through nine products blind to gauge whether this is in my range.
- 2. Surface InCircle status or points-earning language somewhere — even one line like "Earn double InCircle points on new arrivals this week" would make me feel like a known customer, not a cold lead.
- 3. Personalize the grid to categories I've actually bought (I'm not going to open the men's Ralph Lauren blazer section) — segment women's-only sends for shoppers with that purchase history.
- 4. Space this out from the $250-off/Roundup cadence — three structurally different emails in a week from one brand starts to feel like inbox spam even when the creative is good.
- 5. Give the hero a soft deadline or exclusivity hook ("First look before it sells out") — new arrivals emails do better with a scarcity nudge than a flat announcement.

# Technical Audit — "Neiman's New Arrivals: 1,000+ new arrivals for wardrobe building"

## 1. Technical Summary
This is an SFMC (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)-templated email whose raw AMPscript build documentation was left un-stripped in the delivered HTML `<head>` comment, and automated QA flagged missing unsubscribe/authentication headers plus a near all-URL plain-text alternative.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 30 tracking/click-redirect links were present but **skipped by the automated HTTP prober** (expected behavior for redirect-domain links; destinations not independently verified — flag as unverified, not broken).
- Plain-text part is **86.7% URLs** (5,289 of 6,098 chars) — QA WARN. This ratio is a known spam-filter heuristic trigger (Barracuda/Proofpoint/Cisco score plain-text URL density); the plain-text alternative should carry more prose relative to link count.
- No `href` values were visible in the truncated HTML sample, so individual link destinations/UTM tagging could not be directly inspected (see Section 6).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- No `<img>` tags were present in the truncated source, so `alt` text and image-fallback coverage could not be assessed — flag as **unverified**, not confirmed absent.
- Responsive CSS is present and reasonably defensive: `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `.ExternalClass` resets, iOS blue-link override (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`), and multiple `max-width` breakpoints (480px/520px/600px) for mobile column collapse.
- No issues found in the visible CSS/meta portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Finding:** The delivered HTML `<head>` contains a literal, un-rendered HTML comment documenting the AMPscript personalization contract — variable names and internal data-model references are exposed verbatim in the sent source:
  - `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`
  - `@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`
  - `@emailid` (same lookup table)
  - `@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`
  - `@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`
  - A commented-out scenario switch: `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` with `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`.
- This indicates the actual AMPscript block (`%%[ ... ]%%`) that should populate/resolve these values isn't in the excerpt — either it was correctly stripped downstream and this comment is inert leftover documentation, or (if this comment ships as-is to inboxes) it discloses internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`) to end recipients — a data-model disclosure risk, not a functional bug per se.
- **Recommendation:** verify this comment is removed by the ESP's pre-send compile step and does not appear in an actual delivered/rendered message (View Source on a live test send).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** (QA WARN) — may simply reflect the relay (AgentMail) not capturing/forwarding the header rather than the sender omitting it; cannot confirm from headers alone whether NM's actual outbound MTA sets it.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (QA WARN) — one-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via this relay; cannot confirm authentication is correctly configured from this data alone.
- No visible unsubscribe link/footer markup in the truncated HTML sample — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer requirements (physical address, unsubscribe link) are met; truncation limits this assessment.
- **Recommendation:** re-run header capture against the raw SMTP transaction (not via relay) to get authoritative List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results values before concluding non-compliance.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No link `href` values were present in the truncated HTML to inspect for UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) or landing-page alignment.
- The 30 tracking links skipped by the prober are the most likely carriers of UTM/click-tracking parameters (typical SFMC pattern: click-redirect domain → resolves to `neimanmarcus.com` landing page with UTM tags appended after redirect) — this could not be verified end-to-end here.
