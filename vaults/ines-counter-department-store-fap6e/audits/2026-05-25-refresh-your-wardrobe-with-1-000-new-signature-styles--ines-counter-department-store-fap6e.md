# Refresh your wardrobe with 1,000+ new signature styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Fourteen emails in roughly six days. That's where Neiman Marcus and I are right now, and this one doesn't earn any special grace from me. I open it, I see "Brand New Styles Just Landed," and I am looking at a layout I've seen in some variation from every luxury department store for the past decade. Oscar de la Renta floral, a Versace bag, Brunello Cucinelli wedges — yes, those are all things I care about. But half this email is Giorgio Armani sport coats and Ferragamo loafers. I am not shopping for my husband today, and I wasn't asked.
- What really bothers me is the absence of anything resembling a reason to click now rather than later or never. There's no offer. No loyalty hook. No "Circle member exclusive" or "InCircle preview." Just a grid of beautiful things that I could find by visiting neimanmarcus.com on my own. After the spend-and-save grids and the China Chow hosting editorial and the "LAST CHANCE for up to $500 off" that wasn't actually last chance, a new arrivals send with zero value-add feels like an afterthought dropped into the pipeline to fill a slot.
- I will scroll this. I will not click. I will not forward it to anyone. There is nothing here that my friend couldn't find herself with a basic browse.

## What's working

- The women's brand lineup is legit — Polo Ralph Lauren, Givenchy, Brunello Cucinelli, Oscar de la Renta, Versace, Burberry. That row of six reads like a Saks Fifth Avenue edit I'd actually want to see in a curated mailer.
- The black hero block is stark and elegant. Neiman Marcus has never had a brand voice problem; the layout respects my intelligence and doesn't shout at me the way Kohl's has been doing all week with their "$10 off $25" drumbeat.
- Clean render, no broken images, no overlapping text. Technical execution is flawless, which is the bare minimum for a brand at this price point, but still worth noting after some of the Sephora preheader misfires I've seen recently.
- "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" and "Shop All Men's New Arrivals" CTAs are clearly separated and correctly labeled — at least they tried to segment the email internally even if they didn't segment the send list.

## What's weak

- **Half the email is men's.** I am a 47-year-old woman who buys beauty and fashion for herself. Giorgio Armani jackets and Thom Browne trousers occupy the bottom third of an email that didn't ask me a single thing about my preferences. This is what list segmentation is for.
- **No offer of any kind.** The email before this one was "LAST CHANCE for up to $500 off!" If that was the deal, this feels like a step backward. Macy's at least dangles a confirmation incentive when they go transactional. Here there's nothing — not a member preview, not free shipping, not an InCircle point multiplier.
- **Zero loyalty acknowledgment.** This is a brand where I use a card specifically for the points. Sephora's recent send — messy as it was — at least told me I could earn 500 bonus points at $75. Neiman Marcus apparently forgot I have a relationship with them beyond a random email address.
- **Cadence is broken.** This is the fourteenth email in roughly six days. Even the prettiest grid starts to look like inbox clutter at that pace. There's no reason this couldn't have been folded into yesterday's send or saved for a "Just In: Your Weekend Edit" on Friday morning.
- **"1,000+ new signature styles" in the subject is a volume brag, not a curation signal.** One thousand. That number is supposed to excite me? At Neiman Marcus? I shop here because you curate. Showing me the size of the warehouse is not the flex they think it is.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the send — women's inbox gets a women's email.** Pull the men's block entirely for female subscribers. The same 12-product grid restructured as 6 women's items in two clean rows would feel like a considered edit rather than a catalogue dump.
- 2. **Add a loyalty hook to the new arrivals frame.** Something as simple as "Shop new arrivals and earn double points this weekend, InCircle members" transforms a browse email into an action email for people like me.
- 3. **Pair new arrivals with a specific reason to buy now.** A "New this week, shipping free on orders over $150" or "Members get early access through Sunday" gives the click a purpose.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency or consolidate.** Two sends per day for six days is not a strategy — it's a pipeline on autopilot. A single "This week at Neiman Marcus" digest on Wednesday and Saturday would hit harder and leave fewer people reaching for unsubscribe.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to lead with curation, not volume.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: Oscar de la Renta, Versace, Brunello Cucinelli`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just arrived — brands worth a look this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh styles across ready-to-wear, shoes, and bags — InCircle members, log in to shop first.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals from the designers you already love. Shop before sizes go.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "1,000+ New Arrivals" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with multiple unresolved or conflicting variable scenarios left in the source, and the AgentMail relay dropped both `List-Unsubscribe` headers, eliminating one-click unsubscribe compliance. The plain-text alternative is spam-signal territory at 90.5% URL content.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirect URLs were bypassed by the QA probe. Destination integrity (broken links, wrong landing pages) cannot be confirmed for any tracked CTA.

**Plain-text body: 5,270 of 5,820 chars are URLs (90.5%)** — far above the 70% warn threshold. A plain-text part that is nearly entirely URL strings is a known spam filter trigger (SpamAssassin `RAZOR2_CF_RANGE_51_100`, Postmaster scoring). The plain-text part should be a human-readable summary with selective links, not an auto-dump of every href.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are dead experiments left in production source. Not breaking, but indicate template hygiene issues.

**CSS-only interactive (hamburger nav) will not render in major clients.** The checkbox hack (`navbar-checkbox:checked ~`) is used for the mobile hamburger menu. Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore `:checked` pseudo-class logic. Subscribers on those clients see a broken or non-functional nav element.

**iOS 9 webkit animation bugfix still present:**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2016. This is dead code running on every render cycle — remove it.

**Two separate responsive breakpoints in separate `<style>` blocks** — `max-width: 600px` in the first block and `max-device-width: 480px / max-width: 480px` in the second. This is intentional (different grid behaviors) but splitting media queries across multiple `<style>` tags causes specificity ordering issues in clients that concatenate styles (some Outlooks, older Samsung Mail).

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, but some clients render this in preheader or tab text.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation:**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string accomplishes nothing. If `@refid` is intended to differ from `@base_refid` (e.g., a suffix appended per campaign segment), the logic is incomplete or was stripped out without updating the variable definition.

**`@user` has a conflicting, commented-out definition:**
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The variable is first assigned via `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)`, then a commented-out override to `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` is left in source inside both an HTML comment and a CSS comment block (double-commented, definitely inert). The comment block "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" immediately above suggests two deployment scenarios exist in the template but the scenario selector was not finalized before send. If links or pixel fires depend on `@user` being a hashed email, they are receiving the raw `EMAIL_ID` integer instead.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are defined via Lookup but not visible in the truncated source — cannot confirm whether they render correctly or fall back to empty string on no-match rows.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND**
QA finding: `Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay`
This is a CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirement (enforced February 2024 for senders >5k/day). Without this header, Gmail will not show the native unsubscribe button, and Yahoo may route to spam. The header must be present at the SMTP envelope level — confirm whether the AgentMail relay is stripping it or whether SFMC is not injecting it.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): NOT FOUND**
One-click unsubscribe (POST method) is required by Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender policy. Its absence, combined with the missing `List-Unsubscribe` header, means this email does not meet current major-provider compliance requirements.

**`Authentication-Results` header: NOT FOUND**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay interception. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` must have valid SPF and DKIM alignment with the envelope-from domain. Cannot verify from available data — requires raw header inspection at delivery.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

With 26 tracking links skipped, UTM parameter correctness cannot be verified for any CTA. The following cannot be confirmed:
- Whether `utm_campaign` matches the `@base_refid` / `@refid` value for this send
- Whether `utm_source=email` and `utm_medium=email` are consistently applied across all 26 links
- Whether any links point to a staging or pre-production NeimanMarcus.com URL (a common issue when templates are promoted from QA without link replacement)

These must be manually spot-checked or the tracking link probe re-run against unredirected URLs.
