# Save up to 40% on new sale arrivals from Tory Burch, Ramy Brook & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- That big calligraphic "S" for Sale is the second time in three days Neiman's has pulled this exact hero-typography trick — they did it with "New. Sale. Arrivals." on 8/11 too, just in that gorgeous serif "S" swallowing the rest of the word. It's a good device, but running it twice in one week starts to feel like the design team has one move. Still, it's a far cry from the crimson "LAST CALL / EXTRA 25% OFF" block they hit me with twice earlier this week, and I'll take restrained dusty-blue over screaming red any day.
- The nine-item grid is where this actually earns my attention — Tory Burch denim bag, a Jimmy Choo bucket bag, Schutz mules, that Ungaro emerald gown. This is a well-curated wall of "coveted styles," and it reads like Neiman's talking to someone who already shops there, not blasting a mass list. Compare that to Kohl's, which is still stacking red starbursts and green Kohl's Cash banners at me for the tenth time this week — Neiman's restraint is the whole difference in brand tier.
- Would I click? Probably, on the Tory Burch bag or the Ungaro gown specifically — not the "SHOP SALE" button, which dumps me into an undifferentiated sale page. I wouldn't forward this to a friend; it's nice but not gasp-worthy the way yesterday's countryside editorial was. This is a solid mid-tier open-and-scroll, not a save-and-share.

## What's working

- The "S / ale" hero typography is genuinely elegant and stops my thumb mid-scroll, same as the "New. Sale. Arrivals." send did on 8/11.
- The nine-product grid names real designers I recognize and shop — Tory Burch, Jimmy Choo, Alice + Olivia, Ramy Brook — which flatters me as a customer who knows these labels, not a stranger.
- "Up to 40% off" is stated plainly once, with "Exclusions Apply" in small type rather than buried fine print — that's honest, unlike Macy's confirm-to-get-25%-off gymnastics.
- Category CTA buttons at the bottom (Dresses, Shoes, Handbags) let me jump straight to what I actually want instead of one generic "Shop Now."

## What's weak

- This is the same hero-typography trick as yesterday's "New. Sale. Arrivals." email — twice in three days is starting to feel repetitive, not fresh.
- No loyalty or InCircle mention anywhere — I'm a rewards-card holder who refuses to use cards without points back, and Neiman's InCircle program is invisible here. Sephora at least surfaces my tier (badly, but it tries); this doesn't even attempt it.
- "SHOP SALE" as the primary CTA is vague — a verb-plus-noun like "Shop Sale Handbags" would tell me what I'm walking into.
- Nothing here signals urgency or an end date, so there's zero reason to act today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put my InCircle status or points balance somewhere near the hero — even a small line would beat total silence and would finally give this brand an edge over Sephora and Kohl's on loyalty visibility.
- 2. Swap "SHOP SALE" for something specific tied to the top row, like "Shop New Sale Arrivals," so the CTA matches the products I just looked at.
- 3. Add a soft deadline ("Through Sunday") to the hero block — even a credible, non-urgent one — since right now there's no reason to act this week versus next.
- 4. Retire the giant-letter hero device for a few sends; it's memorable but I've now seen it twice in 72 hours and the novelty is wearing off.
