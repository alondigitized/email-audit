# Pretty in pink for weddings
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Five emails in three days. I've been keeping count, and this one lands right after Neiman Marcus spent the better part of this week hammering me with 40% off and then 60% off headlines. So when I saw "Pretty in pink for weddings" in my inbox this morning, my first reaction was genuine relief — finally, a breath of editorial air. And the hero delivers on that promise: a full-length blush pink gown, architectural and confident, the kind of image that makes you stop the scroll.
- But then I got into the body and something shifts. The pivot from fire-sale mode to bridal aspirational with zero price signal anywhere is jarring when I've been conditioned all week to look for a discount. And the copy on the second panel — "Give flower-girl energy" — pulled me right out of it. I'm 47. I'm not trying to channel flower-girl anything. I'm a wedding guest, maybe a mother of the groom someday, and I want someone to dress me like I know what I'm doing.
- I'd mark this read and move on. I wouldn't click through, and I wouldn't forward it. If I'm seriously shopping for a wedding event look I'm going straight to the Oscar de la Renta brand page anyway, not through this funnel.

## What's working

- **The hero is genuinely editorial.** That blush pink gown shot is the best single image NM has sent me all week — clean, high-fashion, nothing the Macy's email on Tuesday could touch.
- **"Shop Oscar de la Renta" is exactly the right CTA.** Naming a specific designer I recognize and respect earns trust instantly. This is how Saks would do it.
- **"Blushing Bridal Looks" as the headline is elegant.** Not shouting at me, not pasting a price in 72-point type — it lets the image carry the weight, which is correct at this price tier.
- **Clean layout, no render issues.** Three product shots, two clear CTAs, nothing broken. After a week of email overload, a clean email earns points.
- **The floral gloves look is interesting.** The second section — the garden-party floral with ladylike gloves — is unexpected and specific enough to read as curated, not filler.

## What's weak

- **"Flower-girl energy."** That phrase is doing real damage. The rest of the email is positioning me as a luxury buyer who knows Oscar de la Renta. Then the copy calls me a flower girl. One of those two things is true, and it's not the second one.
- **Five emails in three days — this one pays the price.** The cadence problem is at a fever pitch. After Monday's editorial "S a l e" send (the one I actually liked), Wednesday's 60% off, and Tuesday's 40% off refresh, this email arrives already fighting for attention it hasn't earned.
- **No price anchor anywhere.** I understand the luxury editorial choice. But a single "Starting at $895" or "From the sale: select gowns now included" would have bridged the pivot from heavy-sale week to aspirational look-book. Without it, I feel like I've been taken off the sale floor and walked into a different store.
- **No loyalty mention.** I have a Neiman Marcus InCircle card specifically because of the points program. This email doesn't acknowledge I exist as a member. Sephora's July 4th send at least reminded me I'd earn 2X points. NM sent me an Oscar de la Renta campaign and forgot I have a card.
- **The bridal and cocktail sections feel like two different emails.** Gowns for wedding guests, then "Shop Cocktail & Party" — the through-line isn't clear. Am I dressing for a wedding or a party? Pick one and commit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "flower-girl energy" and rewrite the second section.** Something like "Garden-party polish: a beaded print and ladylike gloves" reads aspirational rather than juvenile. The look is beautiful — the copy just needs to match who's buying it.
- 2. **Add one loyalty bridge.** Even a single line — "InCircle members earn points on every gown" — would have made me feel like a known customer instead of a stranger who stumbled in from the sale.
- 3. **Add a soft price anchor in the product grid.** Not a discount, just a starting price. Nordstrom always does this on their editorial sends — "from $495" — and it lets me self-qualify without breaking the luxury register.
- 4. **Earn the editorial pivot.** After a week of sale emails, one transition line matters: "Beyond the sale: wedding season's most beautiful looks." It acknowledges the shift and makes the no-discount editorial feel intentional rather than forgetful.
- 5. **Consolidate the two looks into one story.** Either "The Wedding Guest Edit: from gowns to garden-party" with a clear narrative, or split them into two separate sends spaced further apart.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Your wedding-guest look — Oscar de la Renta and beyond"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"The gown. The garden party. The invite's already here."`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Blushing bridal gowns and cocktail looks — shop curated styles for every wedding this summer."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"From pink gowns to ladylike florals — your summer wedding wardrobe, curated by NM."`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email template contains unresolved AMPscript authoring artifacts — including a "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" decision left uncommitted in production — and is missing both `List-Unsubscribe` headers required for Gmail bulk sender compliance since February 2024. ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**35 tracking links skipped** — All click-redirect URLs were excluded from HTTP probing. No dead-link confirmation is possible for this send.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation:**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces the same value as `@base_refid` with no modification. If a suffix was intended (campaign variant, segment code), it was never added. Links built on `@refid` may be misattributed in reporting.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs** — QA flags 3,311 of 3,823 plain-text characters as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Postfix milters) treat URL-dense plain-text as a phishing signal. The plain-text alternative should include human-readable copy, not just expanded tracking links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate the active viewport rule (`maximum-scale=1`) was chosen by elimination rather than intent. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure for users who rely on zoom. The attribute should be removed.

**`<title>` has a trailing space:**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor but will appear verbatim in browser tab and screen reader announcements if the email is viewed in a webmail client.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack** — relies on `:checked` pseudo-class, which is unsupported in Outlook (all versions) and several Android clients. This is a known trade-off for this pattern but should be confirmed against target client mix.

No issues found with character encoding (`UTF-8` declared) or table structure (from visible source).

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction left in production HTML:**
```
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The SHA256 hashing scenario is commented out. The active path sets `@user` via a Subscriber lookup returning `EMAIL_ID` (an internal integer), not a hashed email. If `@user` feeds any downstream personalization, analytics pixel, or CDP event, it is currently passing a raw internal ID rather than a privacy-safe hashed identifier. This is both a data hygiene and potentially a privacy compliance issue depending on what the value is appended to.

**Double-closing-parenthesis in lookup comment block:**
```
Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email))
```
Appears in `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, and `@cmdid` declarations. These are in HTML comments so they do not execute, but if copy-pasted into an AMPscript block they would cause parse errors. Indicates a copy-paste pattern error in the template comment documentation.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header missing** (QA: `[WARN]`)
Gmail has required this header for bulk senders (≥5,000/day) since February 1, 2024. Its absence means Gmail may suppress delivery or move the message to spam for non-engaged recipients.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header missing** (QA: `[WARN]`)
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by both Gmail and Yahoo Mail for bulk senders. Without it, list hygiene degrades — users who can't easily unsubscribe mark as spam instead.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA: `[WARN]`)
