# Win-win: 15% off & you’re in the know! ​
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a welcome email, and as welcome emails go, Neiman Marcus earns it. "You're on the List" — the hero line — is the right register for this brand: understated, editorial, a little exclusive. The product grid below it is genuinely curated — Veronica Beard, Chloe, FRAME, Rag & Bone, Marc Jacobs. Those aren't random SKUs; that's someone at NM who actually knows their customer. The 15% offer is front and center, the code is readable, and the layout is calm. After the Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now!" drama I sat through recently, the restraint here is appreciated.
- But then I saw the subject line — "Win-win: 15% off & you're in the know!" — and stopped. "Win-win" is Kohl's language. That's the same energy as the three back-to-back Kohl's sends I've gotten in the last eight days, and it nearly cost this email the open before I even got to the elegant body underneath. There's a real tone mismatch between what lives in the subject line and what renders on screen.
- The bigger miss for me personally: not a word about InCircle. I check my loyalty status before I add anything to a cart, full stop. This email treats me like a stranger who just wandered in, not someone whose purchase history NM should already know. I'd click through — the brand mix is too good not to — but I'd be annoyed doing it.

## What's working

- **"You're on the List" hero** — quiet, confident, on-brand. NM whispering is more effective than anyone else shouting.
- **Product grid curation** — Veronica Beard, Chloe, FRAME, Golden Goose, Marc Jacobs. Every single one is on my actual consideration list. Someone made real choices here.
- **Promo code is legible and copy-ready** — no hunting, no squinting. NMWELC prefix tells me it's a welcome code, which feels legitimate rather than spammy.
- **"Match with a personal style advisor"** — NM's service culture in one line. That's a differentiator Macy's and Kohl's simply can't touch.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — headline → offer → code → CTA → products. Nothing fighting anything else for my eyes.

## What's weak

- **"Win-win" is not Neiman Marcus language** — that subject line tone belongs on a Kohl's Cash mailer, not a luxury welcome email. The body recovers beautifully, but the subject almost loses me first.
- **No InCircle anywhere** — I refuse to shop without points. One line — "Points apply at checkout for InCircle members" — would have removed my only hesitation.
- **"Start Shopping" CTA is too vague** — a generic verb-dump. Sephora wouldn't say "Start Shopping" on a welcome email; neither should NM. "Shop Veronica Beard" or "Claim Your 15% Off" earns the click better.
- **No beauty in the product grid** — I am a beauty-counter shopper first. The entire grid is fashion and footwear. La Mer, Westman Atelier, even a Tom Ford lip — one card would have told me NM knows me.
- **No expiration on the code** — welcome codes that live forever feel like a junk-drawer coupon. A 14-day window would create real urgency without being pushy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to match the editorial body.** "Win-win" is the single biggest drag on this send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You're on the list — 15% off your next Neiman Marcus order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Welcome. Here's 15% off — no code hunting required.`
- 2. **Write a real preheader that names the brands or the benefit.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Veronica Beard, Chloe, Marc Jacobs — and your welcome code inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 15% off code expires in 14 days. Don't leave it here.`
- 3. **Surface InCircle** in the body — even one sentence below the CTA: "InCircle member? Your points apply automatically." That's all.
- 4. **Add one beauty card to the product grid** — La Mer, NARS, anything. Beauty is why half of NM's most loyal customers walk through the door.
- 5. **Put an expiration on the code.** "Valid through [date]" converts welcome curiosity into a real reason to act this week.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Win-win: 15% off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) deployment using AMPscript for personalization. Two infrastructure-level concerns stand out: unresolved merge token logic in the HTML comments and missing authentication headers at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**27 tracking links skipped (click-redirect domains)** — QA could not probe destination URLs; broken destination links cannot be ruled out. These are presumably routed through NM's click-tracking domain (e.g., `click.e.neimanmarcus.com`).

**Plain-text part is 70% URLs** (4,745 of 6,137 chars) — The plain-text alternative is almost entirely raw redirect URLs with no human-readable copy. This triggers spam filters that use text-to-link ratio heuristics and degrades the experience for plain-text clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**9 product images missing `alt` text:**
```
product1.png – product9.png
src: https://neimanmarcus.com/promo-email/PROMOTIONAL/cat86620752/product*.png
```
All 9 are flagged with no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked (common in Outlook, corporate mail), these cells render as blank space with no fallback label. This also fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**Commented-out viewport meta variants** — Three `<meta name="viewport">` tags are commented out directly above the active one. Dead code; negligible risk but signals template hygiene issues.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS `:checked` trick** — Works in Apple Mail / Gmail app but is non-functional in Outlook (all versions) and many webmail clients that strip `<input type="checkbox">` interactions. No Outlook fallback is visible in the truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block is in an HTML comment, not an AMPscript block:**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` line is inside an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`), not wrapped in `%%[ ]%%` or `<script runat="server">`. If this is meant to execute at send time, it will **not** run — it will be treated as a static comment and stripped. The `@user` variable will be uninitialized, silently producing empty output or a runtime error wherever `@user` is referenced downstream (e.g., identity resolution, suppression logic).

The `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` pattern (concatenating with an empty string) is a known SFMC workaround to force string type-casting, but it implies `@base_refid` may arrive as a non-string type from the sendable DE — worth validating at the data extension level.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **MISSING** | Not captured by AgentMail relay |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **MISSING** | One-click unsubscribe not confirmed |
| `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM) | **UNKNOWN** | Header absent at relay; raw delivery status unverifiable |

`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` are required by Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender mandates for lists >5K/day. Their absence (even if only at the capture layer) warrants verification against the original SMTP headers before treating as compliant. The missing `Authentication-Results` header means SPF/DKIM pass/fail cannot be confirmed from this audit — must be checked at the MTA.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot verify UTM parameters** — All 27 destination links are behind a click-redirect domain and were not probed. UTM tagging, promo code pre-population, and landing page alignment are unverifiable from this audit. A separate link-destination spot-check is needed.

**Promo code delivery mechanism** — The subject references "15% off" but the HTML source is truncated before any coupon/promo-code block. If the discount code is rendered via an AMPscript variable (e.g., `%%=PromoCode=%%`), and the personalization block above is broken (see §4), the code may render empty for some recipients.

---
