# Your top styles could now be up to 40% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "not again." This is the fourth Neiman's send I've seen in about 24 hours. The Beauty Event email with Triple Points and the train case was the one I wanted, and this is not that. It's a clean, quiet sale email. The big black "S" on sage green is handsome, and nothing in it shouts. But there's no beauty in it, no InCircle points and no reason for me specifically, even though the subject says "Your top styles."
- It does respect me more than Macy's does. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email dressed a confirmation step up as a fire drill. Neiman's just says what's on sale and lets me look. Sephora's 10% email at least opened with a gift-with-purchase and my Insider tier. This one gives me "up to 40%" and a grid of nine products with no prices. I'd glance, maybe tap Shop Sale if I were already browsing, and not forward it. I'd mark it read and keep the Beauty Event one.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. "Up to 40% off" and a black SHOP SALE button sit right under the giant "S," so my eye lands on the offer first.
- It's calm. There's no countdown, no caps lock and no exclamation points. That's how a department store should talk to a woman my age.
- The brand names do the work. Vince, Theory, Polo Ralph Lauren and Stuart Weitzman are labeled under each product, which tells me this is real designer inventory and not clearance filler.
- The six category buttons at the bottom let me skip straight to Dresses, Shoes or Handbags.
- The fine print is readable and honest about the range. It says savings of 25–40% and that interim markdowns may have been taken.

## What's weak

- There's no beauty and no InCircle anywhere. After yesterday's Triple Points email, this reads like a stranger's email, not a known customer's.
- "Your top styles" promises personalization, but I see a generic grid with Amiri and Tory Burch side by side. Nothing feels like it came from my browsing.
- There are no prices, no sale prices and no "was/now." Nine products and I can't tell what any of them costs. Nordstrom's sale emails at least show the markdown.
- "Up to 40%" is soft. The footer admits it's mostly 25–40%, so the headline oversells.
- It's the fourth send in a day. Yesterday's "New sale arrivals: up to 40% off Amiri, Vince & more" had almost the same pitch. That's the same hero idea twice, and it feels like noise.
- There are no models and no sizing or fit information. Products float on grey squares.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show prices. Put the sale price and original price under each of the nine products. This one change would lift clicks the most.
- 2. Make it mine. If the subject says "your top styles," the grid should be items I viewed or brands I bought. Otherwise drop the claim.
- 3. Add a member line. Something like "InCircle members: earn points on sale styles" would pull me in.
- 4. Space out the sends. Two near-identical sale emails within a day trains me to ignore the next one.
- 5. Give the hero a real deadline, such as "ends Sunday." Right now nothing says when this stops.

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Your top styles could now be up to 40% off"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML (a truncated head and CSS) is a table-based SFMC template. It has leftover template scaffolding before the DOCTYPE, and it restricts mobile zoom with `maximum-scale=1`. ## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links unprobed:** 29 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped (`[WARN] 29 tracking link(s) skipped`). Destination validity, redirect chains and final status codes are unverified.
- **Link markup not visible:** The truncated source ends inside the CSS, so I can't check `href` values, `target` attributes or malformed anchors.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs:** 5517 of 6489 characters (about 85%) are URLs (`[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`). That is consistent with long tracking URLs in the text alternative, and it can raise spam-filter scoring.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Content before DOCTYPE:** A long HTML comment with SFMC variable documentation comes before `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>`. Content before the DOCTYPE can put some legacy clients (IE-engine/older Outlook) into quirks mode.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic. This is minor.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** The mobile rules use exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They silently fail on any element that has more than one class, so the mobile layout is fragile.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** The nav relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`. It isn't supported in Gmail or most Outlook clients, and it has no visible keyboard or ARIA handling. Whether a non-interactive fallback exists can't be seen in the truncated source.
- **Conflicting declarations:** `*[class].show` declares `width:auto !important` and later `width:278px`. The second is dead code.
- **Not verifiable:** Image `alt` text, image dimensions and total HTML size (Gmail clips near 102 KB) aren't visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template notes shipped to recipients:** The pre-DOCTYPE comment lists `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and the data extension names `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`. It also has an unresolved scenario toggle: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`, `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This is scaffolding that should have been stripped before send. It exposes internal data-model details and adds payload weight.
- **No unresolved tokens in the visible markup:** I see no `%%...%%`, `{{...}}` or `@var` placeholders outside the comment, but the body wasn't available to check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found:** The QA note says the relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found (RFC 8058):** One-click unsubscribe can't be verified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements make it mandatory for high-volume senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain of the brand domain, but alignment isn't verified.
- **Body-level requirements not visible:** The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated footer, so I can't assess them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. No campaign URLs are visible, and all 29 tracking links went unprobed. The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the comment suggest campaign attribution runs through a `refid`-style parameter rather than UTMs, but that is inferred from the template notes, not observed in any link.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the pre-DOCTYPE SFMC comment block before send. This removes the quirks-mode risk and the exposure of internal DE names.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`, and give the `<title>` a descriptive value without the trailing space.
4. Switch the mobile selectors to `[class~=...]` or plain class selectors, and remove the duplicate `width` declaration.
5. Confirm that the hamburger nav has a non-interactive fallback in Gmail and Outlook.
6. Check `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` against the original message, since the relay may have stripped them. Likewise check SPF/DKIM/DMARC in the raw headers.
7. Shorten the URLs in the plain-text part, or add more descriptive text around them.
8. Re-run the QA with the tracking links resolved, and with the full HTML including the footer. That would verify the final destinations, parameter hygiene, alt text, the unsubscribe link and the postal address.
