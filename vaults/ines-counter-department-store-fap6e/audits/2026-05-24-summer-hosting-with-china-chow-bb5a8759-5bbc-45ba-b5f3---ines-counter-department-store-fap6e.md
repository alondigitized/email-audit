# Summer hosting with China Chow
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- Thirteen. That's the count now of Neiman Marcus emails I've absorbed in roughly five days, and this one is the palate cleanser — a "How I Host" editorial with China Chow on a picnic blanket in what looks like her grandmother's garden. After the Memorial Day Sale lockups and the Private Designer Sale and the welcome-series 15% nag, Neiman has remembered they're supposed to be aspirational, not just transactional. I respect that.
- But here's my problem: I don't actually know what they want me to do. There's no offer, no clear CTA above the navy "Download the Neiman Marcus app" tile at the bottom. The shoppable strip — hat, ice bucket, dress, flatware — sits in the middle with no prices, no copy, just product shots floating on white. It's a lookbook pretending to be an email. Macy's would have shoved an extra-25%-off banner over the top of this; Saks would have at least labeled the items "Shop Cult Gaia" or "Shop the Hat."
- Would I click? Maybe the hat. Probably not. Would I forward it? No, but I'd screenshot the table setting and text it to my sister. Mark as read.

## What's working

- The "How I Host" hero feels like *Town & Country*, not a department-store circular — and after twelve sale emails in a row, that's a relief.
- China Chow is a smart casting choice for my demographic — she reads as 40s, polished, the kind of woman I'd actually trust on a tablescape.
- The cream-and-gold tabletop styling is genuinely beautiful and on-trend for late spring entertaining.
- "Read the news" / "Shop summer hosting" gives me two clear lanes: editorial OR commerce. I appreciate the choice.
- No render bugs, no overlap, no broken images — clean Neiman polish, as always.

## What's weak

- No offer, no price, no urgency — which is fine for an editorial, but then *give me a stronger editorial hook*. "Summer hosting with China Chow" is a yawn of a subject.
- The shoppable product strip is anonymous — I can see a hat and a white dress and an ice bucket, but no brand names, no prices, nothing telling me whether this is $80 or $800.
- The "For me, setting a good table comes down to quality, ease, and intention" pull quote is the kind of luxury filler I've read 500 times. It says nothing.
- The China Chow portrait next to the pull quote — she's holding a bowl, but I can't tell what the bowl is or what I'm supposed to buy. Saks would have at least credited the kaftan.
- After the "Last chance: 15% offer expires soon" panic email two days ago, this whiplash from "BUY NOW" to "let's daydream about garden parties" feels uncoordinated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add prices to the product strip.** This is the single biggest miss. I will click "Hat — Cult Gaia, $385" — I won't click an anonymous hat.
- 2. **Sharpen the subject.** "Summer hosting with China Chow" is a snooze; lead with the *takeaway*, not the byline.
- 3. **Credit the products in-line.** Caption each item with brand + price so the eye can shop while it browses.
- 4. **Add one soft CTA in the hosting language** — "Shop China's Table" or "Get the Look." Right now the only visible buttons are "Download app" and "Find a store," which is backwards.
- 5. **Pair this with a tier-program nudge** — InCircle members get early access to her curated edit. Make me feel like a known customer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `How to host like China Chow (the edit, shoppable)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From the Cult Gaia hat to the $48 napkins — every piece, priced.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Her summer table, shoppable. Plus a kaftan I'm adding to cart.`

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
**Critical:** The AMPscript scaffolding block at the top of the document is exposed in the raw HTML as an HTML comment:
```
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) ...-->
```
This is internal developer documentation that leaks:
- Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`)
- Lookup column names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`)
- The SHA256 hashed-email scheme (`SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`)

It also contains an unbalanced parenthesis (`Lookup(..., @email))`) suggesting copy-paste from template scaffolding. Recommend stripping all `<!--REQUIRED variables...-->` comments at send time.
