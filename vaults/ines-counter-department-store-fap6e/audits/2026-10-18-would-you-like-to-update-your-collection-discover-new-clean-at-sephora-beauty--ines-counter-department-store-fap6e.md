# Would you like to update your collection? Discover new Clean at Sephora beauty.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh Sephora send I've had in about a week, and this one finally has something I'd act on. The 4X points banner on all moisturizers is the first thing I read, and 4X points is the only kind of offer I take seriously. Points are why I use a card there at all. But the subject asks if I want to "update my collection," and then the body opens with a free Kosas gloss. That's a different pitch from the subject.
- The "New and Clean at Sephora" hero is calm and readable. The Kosas product shot is nice. Then it turns into a long, repetitive grid of a dozen green "Clean" badges. The points deal I care about is a thin banner above the fold, and the Kosas freebie has a $30 minimum buried in an asterisk. Compared with the 10/17 "Beauty this good moves fast" send and the 10/13 makeup sets one, this is quieter and more respectful. It still makes me work to find the deal.
- Would I click? Probably, but for the moisturizer points, not the Clean story. I'd save the code, not forward it, and I'd look for the same deal at Nordstrom beauty first.

## What's working

- The 4X points banner on all moisturizers, in store and online, is a real loyalty offer, and it has a code (4XHYDRATE) and a hard end date of 10/21.
- The "New and Clean at Sephora" hero is clean and calm. It doesn't shout the way Kohl's "ENDS TONIGHT" stacking does.
- The product grid has prices on every item (Briogeo $39, Obart $44, Yepoda $39), so I can judge value without clicking.
- "NEW" and "ONLY AT SEPHORA" tags show me what's exclusive. I buy at brand events, so exclusives matter to me.
- The Beauty Insider tier strip (Insider / VIB / Rouge) near the bottom reminds me where I stand.

## What's weak

- The subject promises a collection update and Clean beauty. The best offer, 4X points on moisturizers, never shows up in it.
- The 4X points banner is a small strip. The Kosas freebie below it is bigger and has the "FREE*" in large type, so the hierarchy fights itself.
- The Kosas gift needs a $30+ purchase, shown in tiny type beside the code. It's the kind of fine print that makes me distrust the "FREE."
- The identical green Clean badges on every product blur together. Nothing in the grid tells me which item is the hero.
- The ending is long: the "Spot it. Shop it." block, the rhode feature, then legal text and a barcode. It feels like a Macy's-style pile-on. The Beauty Insider tier strip is buried under all of it.
- Nothing says "designer access" or treats me as a known Rouge-level customer. It reads like a broadcast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move 4X points into the hero and the subject. That's the offer that earns the click. Make the Clean story the supporting act.
- 2. Put the $30 minimum next to the gift in plain type, not in the asterisk.
- 3. Cut the grid from 10 products to 4–6 and mark one hero item with a price and a reason to buy.
- 4. Move the tier strip up and say what my tier earns, for example "Rouge: 4X points plus free shipping."
- 5. Trim the footer. One offer, one barcode.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4X points on moisturizers through 10/21`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Clean beauty + 4X points this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code 4XHYDRATE in store or online. New Kosas shades and a free trial size with $30+.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clean beauty you can earn points on: moisturizers, makeup and hair, ending 10/21.`
