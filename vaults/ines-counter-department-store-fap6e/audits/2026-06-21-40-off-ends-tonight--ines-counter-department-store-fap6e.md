# 40% off ENDS TONIGHT 🕛
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-four. That's where my Kohl's count sits for June — and this is the *second* email from them today alone. I already opened "TODAY ONLY: 40% off" with the hourglass this morning. Now at some point tonight I'm getting the urgency sequel: "ENDS TONIGHT." Same offer, same clock metaphor, just a different emoji. The double-tap on Father's Day feels less like customer care and more like a panic button someone pressed in the afternoon when click rates looked soft.
- That said, the email itself is structurally functional. The hero reads fast — 40% off, today, done. Kohl's Cash is in the header and repeated at the bottom, which matters to me because I am absolutely keeping score on what I earn back. The Father's Day framing makes sense for June 21. But this email wasn't built for me — it was built for whoever's buying patio sets and LEGO. I'm looking at outdoor furniture photography and kids' toys while there's a "Beauty" category link buried three scrolls down that did nothing with the occasion.
- Would I click? Possibly on the Women's or Beauty tab out of habit — the 40% off is real and Kohl's Cash is legitimate points. Would I forward? No. Would I feel respected as a customer? Not particularly. This is the brand equivalent of a store PA announcement at closing time.

## What's working

- **Kohl's Cash is prominent.** Showing "$10 Kohl's Cash on every $50 spent" in both the header area and the bottom footer speaks directly to my loyalty math. I don't shop anywhere without points back, and this makes the case twice.
- **The hero reads in two seconds.** "40% off" in the banner is large, immediate, and the "TODAY ONLY" qualifier is visible without hunting. Clean execution on the discount communication.
- **No render bugs.** Images loaded, text didn't overlap, the grid held together on my screen. After Sephora's clapping-emoji TikTok subject line disaster last week, I appreciate basic competence.
- **Father's Day timing is correct.** They picked the right calendar moment. That's not nothing — Macy's has been running near-identical discount confirmation emails this week that didn't bother to dress for the occasion at all.

## What's weak

- **Two emails in one day with the same 40% off offer is disrespectful of my inbox.** The morning "TODAY ONLY" email and this "ENDS TONIGHT" email are the same message in different packaging. That's not urgency; that's spam behavior.
- **This email is about patio furniture and kids. I am not buying patio furniture today.** The visual real estate is dominated by outdoor living sets, summer tumblers, and children's toys. The "Women" and "Beauty" categories exist only as small nav icons near the bottom — completely buried.
- **The grid is exhausting.** Four product tiles with overlapping badge callouts, then a six-category icon strip, then another offer footer. There's no breathing room and no editorial point of view. Neiman Marcus sent "Summer's chicest tops" this week with a focused, curated hero — this feels like a circular in email clothing.
- **No demographic warmth.** The models are either backyard lifestyle shots or children. Nothing here signals that Kohl's knows I exist as a 47-year-old woman who might actually want something for herself around a gifting holiday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Send one email, not two.** Pick morning *or* evening. Sending "TODAY ONLY" at 9am and "ENDS TONIGHT" at 7pm for the exact same offer is the single change that would recover the most subscriber trust. Every second send on the same day erodes the credibility of every future urgency claim.
- 2. **Give women a reason to care on Father's Day.** A secondary hero — even a strip — with "Treating yourself while you're here?" or "40% off Women's too" would double the relevance for female subscribers who are the ones actually buying the Father's Day gift.
- 3. **Promote the Beauty category explicitly.** Kohl's carries Makeup Revolution, Neutrogena, OGX, and fragrance. A single Beauty callout with "40% off beauty picks" would make me scroll. Right now Beauty is a ghost.
- 4. **Tighten the grid to three tiles max.** Four content tiles plus a six-icon category bar is visual clutter. Edit to the three strongest product stories and let them breathe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 40% off — closing in 3 hours`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight: 40% off sitewide + Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Women's, Beauty, Home — everything's in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn $10 Kohl's Cash on every $50. Ends at midnight.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "40% off ENDS TONIGHT" (2026-06-21)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard retail ESP build (Coherent Path + Marigold/MessageMedia stack) with no critical rendering blockers. Primary technical concerns are missing compliance elements and pervasive missing alt text on tracking and content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe** — all route through `click.chp.kohls.com` (Coherent Path) and `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageMedia/Marigold). This is expected behavior for a dual-ESP setup; redirect chain validity could not be confirmed by automated checks.

**Three distinct tracking pixel systems active simultaneously:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — Coherent Path open pixel
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — Marigold open/unique pixel
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — MessageMedia open pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager segment sync pixel

Four independent open-tracking systems firing on render is unusually high and may inflate open-rate metrics if any system double-counts. The AAM pixel (`demdex.net`) also performs cross-site identity resolution on open, which carries GDPR/CCPA exposure if consent logic is upstream-only.

**Malformed tag detected:**
```html
<img src="...open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The stray `</custom>` closing tag after the tracking pixel is invalid HTML. Most parsers will ignore it, but it will surface as a markup validation error.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on 10 images** confirmed by QA, including all six content image tiles served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`:
- `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, `69494b48a79b4240.png`, `78308d1d9adb0751.png`

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, open pixel) appropriately have `aria-hidden="true"` or empty `alt=""` — those are not issues. The content images missing alt text will render as broken image icons in image-off environments (e.g., Outlook default) with no fallback text.

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the title on email load; an empty title degrades accessibility in some clients.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — an accessibility regression for low-vision users and a violation of WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**Google Fonts loaded three ways** (two `<link>` tags + `@import` inside `<style>`). The triple-load is redundant and adds unnecessary HTTP round-trips on supporting clients. Non-supporting clients (Outlook, Gmail app) will fall back to `Arial`/`Helvetica` correctly.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

One image URL contains an unfilled or conditionally empty merge token:

```
mi_kc_redm_end_dte=&mi_kc_event_pe
```
(`916d6fa08d185233.png` — trailing `mi_kc_redm_end_dte=` has no value)

This suggests a dynamic expiry date or event parameter was not populated for this recipient segment. If this image is a countdown timer or conditional banner, it may render incorrectly or pull the wrong asset variant.

No plaintext merge tokens (`{{name}}`, `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, etc.) visible in the truncated HTML source — personalization appears image-based.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** by QA relay. RFC 2369 compliance requires this header for bulk mail; its absence increases spam-filter scoring on Gmail, Yahoo, and Outlook.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.** Gmail and Yahoo have enforced one-click unsubscribe for senders above 5,000/day since February 2024. Missing this header is a deliverability risk for high-volume sends.

**No physical mailing address found** in the truncated HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. This may be present below the truncation point — needs full-source verification before flagging as a violation.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is set in the head. This suppresses referrer data on outbound clicks, which will cause GA4/Adobe Analytics to misattribute email-driven sessions as Direct traffic unless UTM params are fully self-contained on every link (see §6).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameter coverage could not be verified** because all 22 links are behind click-redirect domains (`click.chp.kohls.com`, `click.s.kohls.com`) and the QA probe skipped them. If UTM params are injected by the ESP at redirect time, the `no-referrer` meta (§5) will not interfere. If UTMs are baked into destination URLs, coverage needs spot-check via manual link expansion.

**`email_name=260621_DG_Email_Mystery`** appears in six image URLs — confirms the internal campaign identifier is `DG_Email_Mystery`. Verify this matches the UTM campaign value on destination links to ensure attribution consistency across platforms.
