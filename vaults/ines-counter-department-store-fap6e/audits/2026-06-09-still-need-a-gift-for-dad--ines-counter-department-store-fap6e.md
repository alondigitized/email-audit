# Still need a gift for Dad?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I've gotten five emails from Neiman Marcus in three days. Five. The Beauty Event send on June 8th — the one with the cosmetics case, the La Mer table, the $125 GWP threshold I practically memorized — set a high watermark that nothing since has come close to. This Father's Day email is not terrible. The "THANKS, DAD" hero is graceful and quiet, the product grid covers the obvious luxury categories, and the "Still need a gift for Dad?" subject is actually the first NM subject line in this sprint that made me stop and think "yes, actually, I do." That question hook knows where I am on the calendar.
- But the email answers a question I need answered — *what should I buy, and why buy it now from Neiman Marcus specifically* — without giving me anything actionable. No InCircle hook. No offer. No urgency with a real deadline attached. The secondary module about artisan men's jewelry is the most interesting copy in the entire email and it's buried below the fold in a thumbnail.
- I'd open this. I'd scroll once. I would mark it read and go directly to the website if I actually decided to buy something, which means NM loses the attribution. Wouldn't forward it — there's nothing here that would make my sister need to see it.

## What's working

- **"THANKS, DAD" lands first and lands clean.** After the Designer Sale grid-dump yesterday, the editorial restraint here is noticeable. No countdown clock. No screaming copy. Just a quiet nod to the occasion over a well-styled collage.
- **The product grid earns its place.** Cologne, watch, bag, sunglasses — the Tom Ford fragrance is the one thing I can name with confidence, and that's the right anchor. These are things I'd actually consider buying for a gift.
- **Both CTAs are labeled, not vague.** "Shop Father's Day Gifts" and "Shop Dad's Favorites" both tell me where I'm clicking before I do it. This is already ahead of Sephora, whose "LAST DAY" send this week put me on a "Discover More" button that took me to a homepage.
- **"Make His Day" module has the best writing in the email.** Positioning men's jewelry as "sculptured heirlooms" that transform ordinary pieces into something worth passing down — that's a real gift story, not just merchandise. I genuinely paused on it.
- **Email rendered clean.** No broken images, no layout gaps, no overlapping text. After the Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" email I dealt with earlier this cycle, I appreciate the basics done right.

## What's weak

- **No InCircle mention anywhere.** I have the card. I spend on it. I am exactly the customer who runs a purchase through the loyalty math before clicking buy. Kohl's — budget Kohl's — had a clear benefit signal in their Father's Day email last week and scored a 9 from me. Neiman Marcus, with one of the better luxury loyalty programs in retail, is asking me to shop with no signal about what I get back.
- **No offer of any kind.** Not even a soft one. No double points on menswear. No gift-with-purchase over $250. Macy's would have buried a "25% off with confirmation" somewhere in here — I'd roll my eyes at how it was executed, but at least I'd know the stakes. NM is asking for a full-price gift purchase with no incentive, in a week when I can walk into Nordstrom and get points and a gift bag.
- **The product grid blurs on mobile.** I can name Tom Ford and I can see it's a watch, but I couldn't tell you if that's an Omega or a Rolex or a fashion brand from the grid view. If I'm buying a watch as a gift, the brand matters enormously. Small-grid merchandising hides the thing that would actually justify the price.
- **Cadence fatigue is real at email five.** The Beauty Event send was worth it. The Designer Sale was a step down but still earned its open. This one arrives in my inbox feeling like an afterthought deploy. Sephora at least creates one event per cycle — they get my name wrong sometimes, but they don't spray five sends in a row at me until I go numb.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the InCircle hook in the email.** Even a single quiet line — "InCircle members earn points on every gift" — changes my mental math from browsing to buying. This is the single change that converts me.
- 2. **Commit to one hero product.** Pull the Tom Ford cologne out of the grid, give it a proper product shot with the name, the size, the price, and a "Shop Now" below it. Let me make a decision, not a scroll.
- 3. **Give the "Make His Day" jewelry copy room to breathe.** That module has the best gift positioning in this email and it's sitting below the fold at thumbnail scale. Move it up, expand the image, let the heirloom story do some work.
- 4. **Add a real deadline.** "Father's Day is Sunday. Free shipping on orders placed by Friday at midnight" fits the brand register — no countdown clock required. Just put it in clean NM type below the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance for Dad — free shipping ends Friday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The gift he'll keep: Father's Day, curated by NM`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tom Ford, watches, leather — InCircle members earn points on every gift`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day is Sunday. Shop gifts now, ship free through Friday.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Still need a gift for Dad?"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) send with table-based layout and a CSS hamburger nav. The primary technical concerns are a commented-out SHA-256 identity variable, unverifiable tracking link destinations, and missing compliance headers that violate 2024 Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender mandates.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking/redirect links — destinations unverified**
All 34 click-tracked links were skipped by the HTTP probe (redirect domains). No destination URL, UTM parameter set, or landing page alignment could be confirmed. This is the largest blind spot in this audit.

**Plain-text body is 85.4% URLs**
QA measured 2,995 of 3,506 plain-text chars as URLs (flagged at the >70% threshold). The plain-text alternative appears to be auto-generated from tracked redirect URLs rather than human-readable copy. This is a spam-filter risk: filters that score the plain-text part independently will see a near-empty message with wall-to-wall redirect URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS hamburger nav has limited client support**
The mobile nav relies on the `:checked` pseudo-class pattern (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked ~ *[class].menu`). This works in Apple Mail and a narrow set of WebKit clients. It is silently ignored in Outlook (all versions), Gmail (web/Android), and Yahoo Mail. Subscribers on those clients receive a broken or non-functional nav with no fallback.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code left in production. No rendering impact, but signals that the template has had undocumented viewport experiments. The active tag is correct (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`).

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
`<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, but some clients surface the title as a preview or tab label.

**No `alt` text visible in truncated source**
HTML was truncated before image tags could be inspected. Alt text coverage cannot be confirmed; flag for full-source review.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA-256 assignment is commented out**

```ampscript
/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */
```

This line is inside a block comment and will not execute. The variable `@user` is instead being populated by a Lookup against `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` returning `EMAIL_ID`. Any downstream use of `@user` that expects a SHA-256 hashed email value (e.g., identity resolution pixels, personalization APIs, ad-matching parameters) will receive an internal subscriber integer ID instead. This is a silent data-type mismatch — no render error, wrong value passed.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op**
Concatenating with an empty string produces the same value as `@base_refid` unchanged. If this line was intended to append a campaign suffix, the suffix is missing. If it's intentional, the CONCAT is unnecessary.

**Duplicate Lookup calls against the same table**
Both `@user` and `@emailid` perform independent `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` calls keyed on `EMAIL_ADDRESS`. If the subscriber is not in that DE, both return empty strings and any conditional logic depending on either value degrades silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
QA reports the header was not present at the relay layer. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5,000/day) since February 2024. Missing it risks inbox placement demotion or sender-reputation penalties on both platforms.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
One-click unsubscribe is a separate 2024 Gmail/Yahoo mandate for high-volume senders. Without this header, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" button falls back to mailto, which Gmail now penalizes in spam classification. If SFMC is sending this header and the AgentMail relay is stripping it, that relay behavior needs investigation.

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the relay. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` has a published DMARC policy; whether this send passes alignment cannot be confirmed from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot be fully assessed — all 34 tracked links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Specific issues that should be manually verified:

- Whether redirect destinations include UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`)
- Whether `@refid` and `@cmdid` values are correctly appended to destination URLs for attribution
- Whether the `@uEm` value (from `NM_CRM_Feed`) is passed to the site for logged-in personalization continuity

The no-op `CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` assignment raises the question of whether `@refid` is correctly formed for link tagging.
