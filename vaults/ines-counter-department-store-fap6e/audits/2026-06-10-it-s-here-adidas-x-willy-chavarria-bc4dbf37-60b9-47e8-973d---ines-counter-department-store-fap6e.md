# It’s here: Adidas x Willy Chavarria
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is my fifth Neiman Marcus email in three days. I'm keeping count now. Monday had the CHANEL Métiers d'Art drop — that stopped my scroll because I know exactly what that collection means. June 8th gave me the Beauty Event with the $125 GWP threshold and the La Mer table I practically memorized. And now I'm looking at a man in wide-leg pants and a track jacket.
- I know who Willy Chavarria is — I read enough fashion coverage to recognize the name. This is a real cultural moment in menswear. But it was sent to me: a woman who spent forty-five minutes at the NM Chanel beauty counter last month and has never browsed the men's floor without a gift in mind. There is nothing in this email that looks like me, sounds like me, or would make me reach for my card.
- I would not click. I might forward it to my nephew who actually wears streetwear, but that is emphatically not the outcome Neiman Marcus was hoping for.

## What's working

- The editorial layout is immaculate — clean white, structured hierarchy, nothing competing for attention, very much on-brand NM tone.
- "Power Play" lands as a headline — punchy, short, makes the email feel intentional rather than templated.
- No render bugs anywhere: logo, hero image, footer links — all crisp and readable without zooming.
- The Neiman Marcus wordmark at the top means I knew the sender before I read a single word of copy.

## What's weak

- The model is male, the product is menswear streetwear, and I am a 47-year-old woman who shops the beauty and women's designer floors. This email missed me by the full width of a department store.
- No offer, no price, no GWP, no threshold — compare this to the Beauty Event send from June 8th, which gave me a concrete number to plan my cart around. This is pure editorial with no commercial hook.
- Two equal-weight CTAs ("Shop New Arrivals" / "Shop Designer Collections") dilute each other. When everything is primary, nothing is. Kohl's runs a single CTA on nearly every send and consistently drives higher response — it's not a coincidence.
- No InCircle signal anywhere. I don't see my tier, I don't see points language, I don't see early access copy. I'm a member; treat me like one, or I start feeling like I'm on the same blast as someone who bought once on sale in 2019.
- Fifth email in three days is starting to feel like batch-and-blast. Sephora got called out two audits ago for exactly this — using my name wrong and sending regardless. NM doesn't use my name wrong, but the cadence is eroding the trust the Beauty Event earned.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** This should never have hit a female beauty-regular segment. Macy's would have bucketed me differently. If NM's system knows I bought at the beauty counter and has never shown me a men's purchase, this send should not have my address on it.
- 2. **For women, frame it as a gifting angle.** The one way to salvage reach into my inbox: "Shopping for someone who lives in streetwear?" Kohl's does exactly this for Father's Day — leads women into a men's category by making it about gifting, not personal shopping.
- 3. **Collapse to one CTA.** "Shop the Capsule" — done. Give me one door to walk through.
- 4. **Add an InCircle access line.** Even one sentence: "InCircle members, early access is live." Zero incremental design effort, immediate relevance signal for members.
- 5. **Slow the cadence or add a preference center link above the fold.** Five emails in three days with no preference control in sight is how you lose subscribers.
