# Tick. Tock. Shop new markdowns up to 70% off during our Last Call Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Twenty-eight. That's where we are with Neiman Marcus in July, and walking into this one right after last night's "your private offer ends tonight: 25% off one item" is a specific kind of déjà vu. That said — this isn't the same email with a new subject line. The Last Call construct is genuinely different: an extra 25% stacked on top of already-marked-down designer inventory at up to 70% off. That's a real moment, and the email does communicate it cleanly.
- The product grid is doing the heavy lifting here. Isabel Marant, Balmain, Stuart Weitzman, Manolo Blahnik, Weekend Max Mara — I stopped on that zebra print coat almost immediately. These are brands I actually spend money on at NM, not aspirational filler for someone else's persona. The email looks polished, the hierarchy is clear, the "LAST CALL" sub-brand framing is earned. NM built that brand inside the store, and it translates.
- What bothers me isn't the email, it's the month. If this had landed once, maybe twice, with some context that this was the final clearance window, I'd have my credit card out. Instead it's number twenty-eight, and every time NM said "ends tonight" this week, something else mysteriously went on sale the next morning. I'd click — but mostly because I've already been stalking the Last Call section on my own, not because this email earned the click.

## What's working

- **The designer product grid is the actual headline.** Weekend Max Mara, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Isabel Marant — six brands in one scroll that fit who I actually am and what I actually buy at NM.
- **The stacking offer is clear and different.** Extra 25% off on top of sale prices already up to 70% off is a different mathematical story than the personal-offer emails I've been getting; the email communicates that correctly without overcomplicating it.
- **"Save on these designer styles before they're gone."** One clean, non-gimmicky line that respects my intelligence. I trust this more than a fake countdown.
- **Visual hierarchy flows.** Logo → hero offer → designer grid → category CTAs — my eye never got lost.
- **"LAST CALL" sub-brand works.** It signals real inventory liquidation, not a manufactured flash sale. Macy's "LAST CHANCE: confirm now" from earlier this week tried to manufacture the same urgency with a confirmation mechanic; NM earns it by leaning on a legitimate sale event.

## What's weak

- **No InCircle mention, anywhere.** I'm a member. A Last Call event with stacked discounts is exactly when loyalty tier matters — am I getting this because of my status or because it blasted to everyone on the list? Sephora's 4X points framing (which I've now seen twice this week) irritated me on repetition, but at least it translated the offer into *my* currency. NM didn't even try.
- **"Exclusions Apply" with no link.** Fine print with no click-through is a friction flag I've learned to take seriously at NM. The footer confirms the qualifications: "up to 25% off the prices marked, certain items may have no additional discount." That's a meaningful asterisk for a brand that trained me to check.
- **Twenty-five percent again.** After "Just for you: 25% off," then the Reminder, then "ends tonight" — my brain is now auto-pattern-matching "25% off" from NM to "another promo I'll get again tomorrow." The construct here is genuinely different but the visual echo is getting expensive.
- **No beauty or fragrance in the product grid.** I know this is a fashion send, but Last Call has serious fragrance and skincare markdowns. One La Mer or Sisley row would have spoken to me directly.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the stack math on a specific piece, not the generic percentage.** A hero image of the Weekend Max Mara coat with a visible "was $895, now $178 with your extra 25%" lands harder than "up to 70% off."
- 2. **Add an InCircle callout**, even a single line — "InCircle members: your extra discount stacks automatically." If Sephora can tie every email to points, NM can mention a tier program that actually matters to me.
- 3. **Surface a real end date.** "Tick. Tock." plays at urgency without delivering it. "Through Sunday, July 27" is more credible and more useful.
- 4. **Add one beauty unit to the product grid.** Even a single shelf — La Mer, Sisley, Chantecaille — extends the email to my strongest purchase category at NM.
- 5. **Pull back the July cadence.** Twenty-eight emails in one month means I've already emotionally priced in "there will be another one tomorrow." That's a trust problem.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Last Call Sale" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

SFMC-built email using AMPscript for personalization, sent from a dedicated subdomain (`e.neimanmarcus.com`). QA pass rate is 43% with four warnings; the critical gaps are unverified authentication headers and missing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe support.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped** — QA could not probe click-redirect destinations (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). No broken-link confirmation is possible for those URLs.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs** (5,268 of 6,101 chars). This indicates the plain-text alternative is auto-generated from the HTML link list rather than authored prose. Spam filters treat a high URL-to-text ratio in the plain-text part as a signal; this also degrades readability for plain-text recipients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport meta tags** — three `<meta name="viewport">` variants are left in the `<head>` as HTML comments, indicating unresolved template indecision. These don't render but add noise and suggest the template was not cleaned up before send.

```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**`<title>` has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but shows in some preview panes and tab labels.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack** — the mobile nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-selectors and `visibility`/`opacity` transitions. This is non-functional in Gmail (which strips `<style>` blocks), meaning mobile Gmail users see a broken or collapsed nav with no interaction available.

No issues found with legacy table-based layout, XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE, or standard email client resets — these are expected for broad-client compatibility.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid` CONCAT is a no-op** — the AMPscript comment block documents:

```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```

The second argument is an empty string literal, making this a passthrough with no appended suffix. If the intent was to add a campaign variant or version tag (e.g., `CONCAT(@base_refid,"_A")`), it was not filled in. This would cause all tracking `refid` values to equal `@base_refid` with no differentiation.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment unresolved** — the header comment instructs the sender to select between two `@user` assignment scenarios. One option (`SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`) is visible but commented out. The selected scenario is not visible in the truncated source. If the wrong scenario was left active (or both were commented out), `@user` would be undefined and any personalization or tracking that depends on it would silently fail or send a null value.

**Variable declarations are inside HTML comments** — the entire AMPscript variable block is wrapped in `<!-- ... -->`. In SFMC, AMPscript inside HTML comments does not execute. If these declarations are only in comments (and not duplicated in a live `%%[ ]%%` block elsewhere), the variables `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, `@emailid`, and `@user` would be undefined at send time.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — QA notes this may be a relay-capture gap, but the header must be present. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe` for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` not detected (RFC 8058)** — also required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Without this, Gmail shows a two-step unsubscribe flow rather than the one-click button, increasing friction and friction-driven spam reports.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Sender subdomain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a standard ESP subdomain; authentication is expected but unverified for this send.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot verify landing page alignment or UTM parameters for the 26 skipped tracking links — destination URLs are behind click-redirect domains and were not probed. No issues confirmed, but no clearance either.
