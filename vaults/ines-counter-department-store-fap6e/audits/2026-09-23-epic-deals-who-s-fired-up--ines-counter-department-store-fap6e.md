## Executive summary

- Kohl's, again. There is nothing here that reads as a beauty-counter customer being spoken to — this is a Costco circular dressed up as an email, and I say that as someone who does actually own a Kohl's Cash coupon or two.
- The one thing that made me pause was the jewelry and handbags block — 60% off fine and silver jewelry, up to 50% off handbags — because that's at least adjacent to my world. But it's buried seven scrolls deep under kids' tees, bedding, towels, a mattress topper, and toys, so by the time I'd get there on my phone I'd have already bailed. Compare this to Sephora's "Hair Daily Deals" send I scored an 8 on — that one opened with one clear offer and got out of my way. This is the opposite: no hierarchy, no restraint, just tile after tile until my thumb gives up.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Absolutely not — this isn't "look what I found," it's "delete." Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The jewelry tile (60% off fine and silver) and handbag tile (up to 50% off) are the only two blocks that even brush against my category.
- Top banner is at least legible — "EPIC DEALS," 3x Kohl's Cash rewards, clean red/white contrast.
- The Kohl's Rewards member callouts (3x rewards, $10 off $50 coupon) are visible if you're already in the loyalty program, so there's some member-recognition attempt.

## What's weak

- This is a mass-market grid, not a targeted send — kids' clothes, bedding, a mattress topper, LEGO, a slushi machine, and vacuum cleaner all crammed into one email with zero segmentation for who I am.
- No hero, no single offer — my eye has nowhere to land, it's just tile-after-tile the way Neiman Marcus's outerwear email at least avoided this month.
- Same "Epic Deals" red-banner creative I've now seen five-plus times this week — Kohl's isn't rotating a single asset.
- The jewelry/handbag section that's actually relevant to me is scrolled past everything else — no attempt to lead with it for someone with a beauty/accessories profile.
- "Coupons do not apply" fine print sprinkled next to the floor care and toy deals feels like the bait-and-switch Saks would never let through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this list by shopper profile — send me the jewelry/handbags/beauty-adjacent version as its own email instead of forcing me through kids' bedding and mattress toppers to find it. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Lead with ONE hero offer above the fold — pick the jewelry deal, put it first, and cut the grid down from fifteen-plus tiles to five.
- 3. Stop reusing the identical red "EPIC DEALS" banner — I've now seen this exact creative language three sends running; give it a new visual treatment or I stop registering it at all.
- 4. Move the loyalty math ($10 off $50, 3x Kohl's Cash) up near the hero, not buried mid-scroll — that's the actual differentiator for a rewards member like me.
- 5. Cut the "coupons do not apply" exclusions out of the visible tile or make them less prominent — it reads like a trick.
