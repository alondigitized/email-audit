# Surprise: Up to an extra 40% off starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- The typographic "S ale" hero is genuinely editorial — clean, confident, not screaming. After that meandering swimwear send on 6/30 and the back-to-back designer-sale blasts the week before, I expected to mark this read on sight. But "up to an extra 40% off" on a grid that includes Zimmermann, Manolo Blahnik, and Giorgio Armani actually stopped me. "Extra" is doing real work here — it implies stacking on already-reduced prices, not just a baseline sale. That's a meaningfully different offer than the 6/29 "up to 60% off" send, and savvy cross-shoppers like me will notice.
- What kills it is the word "Surprise." Five emails in four days and Neiman Marcus is calling this a surprise. Gen X women don't miss that kind of hollow framing. We bought concert tickets over dial-up; we can read a pattern. I also hit a small render glitch in the hero fine print — the text below the CTA looks like "Extremely Apply" in the screenshot, which I assume is "Exclusions Apply" garbled. Small thing, but it's exactly the kind of detail that makes me wonder what else is sloppy.
- I'd click SHOP SHOES or SHOP HANDBAGS — the secondary CTAs are specific enough to earn that. I would not forward this to anyone. It's a competent sale email, not a conversation-starter.

## What's working

- **Brand lineup is exactly right for me.** Zimmermann, Manolo Blahnik, Marni, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera — this is the luxury edit a Neiman Marcus woman in her late 40s actually wants to be reminded is on sale. It's a stronger brand stack than the Kohl's "20% off w/code GOSHOP20" send that's been running this week, even if Kohl's was better on structural clarity.
- **"Extra" in the offer is doing clever work.** Stacking language implies a different event than the base sale. That phrasing pulled me in more than the straightforward 60%-off hero from 6/29.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean and editorial.** Logo → nav → typographic hero → product grid → category CTAs. My eyes moved exactly in that order. Macy's "confirm now for extra 25%" was more cluttered at the same scroll depth.
- **Secondary CTAs have real specificity.** SHOP WOMEN'S DRESSES, SHOP SHOES, SHOP HANDBAGS — those are destinations, not vague "Explore" buttons. Sephora's "▶ We're treating you to something new" gave me a direction without a destination. NM tells me where I'm landing.
- **No spam-register noise.** Subject stays calm and lowercase. No 🚨, no !!!. After the cadence I've been dealing with from multiple brands this week, that registers as respect.

## What's weak

- **"Surprise" is doing the opposite of surprising me.** Sending daily and labeling the fifth email a surprise is the kind of thing that erodes a brand. I'm conditioned to expect a Neiman Marcus email with my morning coffee. The word should go.
- **Hero fine print appears garbled.** The text below the CTA button in the screenshot reads like "Extremely Apply" — I assume it's "Exclusions Apply" but it doesn't render cleanly. Fine print I can't parse is fine print I assume protects the brand, not me.
- **No InCircle acknowledgment anywhere.** I cross-shop Neiman Marcus specifically because of the loyalty program. This email treats me like a cold prospect. Macy's triggered email at least knew I had a Star account.
- **Men's CTAs are noise.** SHOP MEN'S CLOTHING and SHOP MEN'S SHOES take up a full CTA row in a send that led with Zimmermann and Carolina Herrera. Segmentation gap.
- **No end date on the offer.** "Starts now" tells me when the clock started. It doesn't tell me when it stops — which is the only date that converts me from "I'll look later" to "I should act today."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "Surprise" entirely.** Lead with the offer or the brand name. Emotional manipulation labels erode faster than they ever build trust.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** "Through Sunday" or "Ends July 6" next to the 40% off — one line is enough to shift urgency from theoretical to real.
- 3. **Acknowledge InCircle tier in the preheader or hero subhead.** "InCircle members: stack your points on extra 40% off" turns a broadcast into a personal message for your highest-value segment.
- 4. **Kill the men's CTA row on female-segmented sends.** Use that space for a beauty or fragrance callout instead — NM's beauty floor is a genuine differentiation versus Nordstrom, and it's invisible in this email.
- 5. **Show one hero product with a before/after price.** The Manolo at $420 → $252 base sale → $151 with extra 40% is a specific, visceral proof point. That converts where "up to extra 40%" is abstract.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on styles you've been watching — this one stacks.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Surprise: Up to an extra 40% off starts now"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard SFMC AMPscript template with a CSS-based hamburger nav and multi-breakpoint responsive layout. No hard errors are present, but two compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) are notable for a sender at this volume, and the plain-text part is URL-saturated in a way that may trigger spam filters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links — destinations unverified**
All click-redirect links were skipped during HTTP probing. No broken-link confirmation is possible from this scan. Manual spot-checks of 3–5 representative links (primary CTA, nav, unsubscribe) are recommended post-send.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs by character count**
`5268 / 6070 chars = 87%` (QA reports ">70% URLs"). A plain-text alternative consisting almost entirely of redirect URLs — with no human-readable copy — can trigger Spamhaus and Gmail's content heuristics. The plain-text part should include a condensed, readable version of the email body.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags in `<head>`**
Three alternate viewport declarations are left as HTML comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code; no functional impact, but indicates template hygiene issues and inflates send size slightly.

**CSS hamburger nav (`:checked` pattern) won't render in most clients**
The interactive mobile nav is implemented via `input[type=checkbox]:checked ~` CSS sibling selectors. Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore this. The fallback `.hamburger` / `.close` / `.menu` states are declared but the checkbox-driven toggle will silently fail for the majority of the recipient base. The `.contentInteractive` / `.nocontentInteractive` toggle has the same problem.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; no functional impact, but shows in some preview panes.

**Alt text not verifiable from truncated source**
Image `alt` attributes could not be confirmed from the available HTML. Images-off rendering in Outlook/corporate clients should be verified separately.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` assignment is commented out**
The template comment block includes:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
This is inside an HTML comment — it will not execute. If `@user` is expected to carry the SHA-256 hashed email (e.g., for audience matching or personalization URLs), this assignment must appear in an active AMPscript block (`%%[ ]%%`), not a comment.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating an empty string is a placeholder pattern. Confirm whether a suffix is intentionally omitted or whether this should be `CONCAT(@base_refid, @some_suffix)`.

**Multiple `Lookup()` calls with silent-fail risk**
`@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, and `@cmdid` all depend on `Lookup()` against `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`. If a subscriber's email is absent from either data extension, these return empty strings with no error. If any of these values appear in tracking URLs or personalized content, missing values will produce broken or unattributed links. Confirm fallback behavior or default values for each variable.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment is unresolved in the HTML comment block**
The documentation comment instructs the template author to choose a scenario for `@user` — it's unclear whether the correct scenario was selected before send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this header for senders above 5,000 messages/day. Its absence risks Gmail demoting delivery to the Promotions tab or triggering a spam classification. Neiman Marcus at this send volume almost certainly triggers the threshold.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header absent**
QA finding: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` under Google/Yahoo policy. Without it, the mail client cannot surface the native one-click unsubscribe UI.

Both headers should be injected by the ESP (SFMC) or the sending MTA. Their absence here may indicate the AgentMail relay is stripping them before delivery confirmation, or they were never set in the SFMC send classification. Verify at the MTA layer, not just in the HTML.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent**
QA finding: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. Since the envelope sender is `e.neimanmarcus.com`, a dedicated ESP subdomain, SPF and DKIM alignment should be verifiable via `nslookup` against that domain's DNS. Confirm independently.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism**
Not verifiable from the truncated HTML. These are expected to appear in the footer — confirm they are present in the full render.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Tracking link destinations unverified**
All 26 click-redirect URLs were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM parameter presence and correctness (campaign, source, medium, content) cannot be confirmed from this scan. Manually inspect 2–3 representative CTAs for:
- `utm_source=email`
- `utm_medium=email` (not `cpc` or blank)
- `utm_campaign` matching the campaign calendar ID tied to `@base_refid`

**`@refid` / `@cmdid` / `@uEm` in tracking URLs**
If these AMPscript variables are interpolated into click-tracking URLs (common in SFMC to attribute opens/clicks per subscriber), the no-op `@refid` concatenation and potential empty `@cmdid` / `@uEm` values noted above will produce attribution gaps in downstream reporting.
