# Fresh takes on the animal print
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is a mood, not a marketing email. One gorgeous shot — a model in a pink striped dress with a zebra bag slung over her shoulder — "Wild & Wonderful," a ghost button that says "Shop Animalia," and that's it. No price, no percentage sign, no "SALE" anywhere in the body copy. It's the kind of image I'd screenshot and text to a girlfriend saying "isn't this bag cute" — but as an actual purchase trigger, it's asking a lot of me to click through with zero idea what a zebra-print bag like that costs.
- Compare this to what I've been getting from this same brand all week — the 25% off Friends & Family send from yesterday and the 40% off A.L.C./Frame sale two days ago. Those told me exactly what I'd save. This one tells me nothing except "here's a vibe." It reminds me of the Style Lessons from a Tastemaker send on the 15th — same editorial restraint, no urgency banner, no exclusion wall — and honestly I liked that one too, but two moody no-price sends in three days starts to feel like Neiman's alternating between "buy now, 25% off, unique code" and "just look at this, no price, figure it out yourself."
- I wouldn't click immediately — I'd screenshot the bag for myself, maybe forward it to a friend as a "cute right?" text, but I'm not opening my wallet off this alone. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The zebra bag against the pink dress is a genuinely eye-catching styling choice — animal print done chic, not costume-y, which is exactly the "fresh takes" angle the subject promised.
- Clean, minimal layout — no mauve wall of fine print, no "unique code just for you" theater like some of their other sends this week.
- "Shop Animalia" is a cute, on-brand button name that ties the whole email together thematically.
- Nav bar (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) is simple and I know exactly where to go if I want to browse instead.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing information anywhere — not even a "starting at" or "new arrivals" framing. I can't tell if this bag is $95 or $895, and at Neiman's it could genuinely be either.
- No indication this is even a sale email — if I'm scanning my inbox for a deal, this gives me nothing to latch onto.
- Single hero image with one product means if the zebra bag isn't my thing, there's nothing else to catch my eye — Ulta and Sephora at least give you a grid of options in a themed send like this.
- No loyalty/InCircle mention at all — I'm a member, and a "fresh takes" seasonal moment would've been a nice spot to remind me what my points could get me toward this bag.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or "from $X" under the hero — even luxury brands like Saks will drop a starting price on an editorial send; going in totally blind costs them the impulse click.
- 2. Turn this into a mini shoppable grid — 3-4 animal-print pieces (bag, dress, a shoe) instead of one image, so there's more than one reason to click.
- 3. Work in an InCircle nod — "earn double points on new arrivals" or similar — since I'm already primed to notice when a brand treats me like a member vs. a stranger.
- 4. Give the CTA button more visual weight — that thin ghost outline on "Shop Animalia" is easy to miss scrolling on a phone.
