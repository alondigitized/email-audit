# First Call: Up to 40% off starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- Neiman's finally stopped with the "$200 off" ladder, and I'm glad. The tomato-red "First Call" block with a black frame is loud for them, but it's one offer and I got it in two seconds. It's still the sixth or seventh sale-flavored send since 10/6. Yesterday's "sweater weather" cape was the Neiman's I like. This is the Neiman's that wants to clear racks.
- The grid helps. Alice + Olivia, A.L.C., AG, Acler, Veronica Beard, Theory and Polo Ralph Lauren are all labels I'd actually try on. Nothing shows a price, though, so "up to 40%" has to carry the whole email. The footnote also says "25-40% off," so the real floor is 25%, and I only find that out at the very bottom. Nordstrom would put a was/now price under at least one of these.
- I'd click, mostly for the Veronica Beard blazer and the Theory cardigan. I wouldn't forward it. It's a clearance sweep, not something I'd text a friend about.

## What's working

- One clear offer in the hero. "Up to 40% off" with a black SHOP NOW button is readable at a glance. Sephora's recent magazine-style sends buried the point far longer.
- The designer names are labeled under every tile. I can scan for my labels without guessing.
- The grid is mostly my age and taste: a blazer, a good coat, a cardigan, denim. It isn't all teen-sized sequins.
- The text-list incentive (15% off) and the personal style advisor link are quiet, useful touches. The style advisor is the kind of service I use at the beauty and designer counters.
- No emoji and no all-caps screaming in the subject. It's calmer than Kohl's "Save 20% 🍂" or Sephora's "MAKEUP SETS" heart-and-caps subject.

## What's weak

- There are no prices. I can't see a single was/now figure, so "40% off" is abstract. I want to see $ saved on the Theory cardigan.
- The "Exclusions Apply" line sits right under the button, and the footnote's "25-40%" and "select categories" are buried. That reads as honest-ish but hedged.
- The hero is a huge block of red, and the button is small next to it. On my phone I'd scroll past a lot of red before I see a single product.
- Nothing about my status. There's no InCircle points angle, no member early access, no "you" at all. Macy's at least leads with a confirm-for-extra-25% hook that feels personal. This feels like a stranger's blast.
- The text-list "15% off" box asks me to hand over my number while I'm already an email subscriber. It also pulls attention off the products.
- It's the same sale cadence as this week's "$200 off" ladder and the gift-card send, just in new clothes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real price on each tile (was/now) and name the discount depth per item. That single change would lift this the most.
- 2. Tie it to loyalty. Add an InCircle line like "Earn bonus points on First Call this week," or I'll just wait for a points event.
- 3. Move the exclusions and the true range ("25-40%") up near the hero instead of the footer.
- 4. Shorten the hero so the first product row shows above the fold on mobile.
- 5. Space out the sale sends. After a week of the $200 ladder and a gift-card send, vary the voice, as yesterday's "Comfort, Dressed Up" did.

- **Bloated plain-text part:** 5893 of 6869 chars (about 86%) are URLs (`[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`). This points to long, unshortened tracking URLs in the text alternative, which is a spam-filter and readability risk.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't select the right pronunciation.
- **CSS-only mobile nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger`/`.close` rules need `:checked` and sibling selectors. Gmail (web and app) and Outlook desktop don't support these, so the nav will likely degrade or be unusable there. Whether a fallback exists is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` match only when the `class` attribute is exactly that string. Any element with an additional class won't get the mobile rule.
- **Minor:** `.show` declares `width: auto !important` and later `width: 278px` in the same rule, so the second declaration is dead. The `<title>` is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space.
- **Not assessable:** alt text and image dimensions are outside the truncated portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens:** none are visible in the supplied portion of the rendered body.
- **Leaked template comment:** the top of the document contains the authoring note `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) … Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"/"cmdid", …) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the authoring/variable comment, including the DE names and the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" note, from the production template at send time. If one comment must stay, put it after the `<!DOCTYPE>`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta to restore pinch-zoom.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Confirm that raw SMTP headers at the source MTA include `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and that SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass. Don't rely on the relay-captured copy.
5. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the nav in Gmail and Outlook. Use `[class~=…]` or plain class selectors instead of exact-match `[class=…]`.
6. Shorten or simplify the tracking URLs in the plain-text alternative, or reduce the number of links in it.
7. Re-run QA with the full, untruncated HTML and a probe that follows redirects on the 31 tracking links. That would let you verify final destinations, UTM/`refid` parameters, alt text and footer compliance.
