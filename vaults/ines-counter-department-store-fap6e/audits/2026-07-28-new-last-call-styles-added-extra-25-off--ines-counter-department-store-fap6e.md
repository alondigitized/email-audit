# New Last Call styles added + Extra 25% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a relief after the week Neiman Marcus has put me through. No fourth rerun of that $200/$50-to-$2,000/$500 spend-and-save grid I've now seen three times since Friday — this is a straight Last Call clearance push with an extra 25% off and, more importantly, actual merchandise: Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Theia. That's the kind of designer-name-drop that makes me stop scrolling, because I know what those brands cost at full price at Saks.
- That said, I want to be clear-eyed about who this is for. I'm a beauty-counter regular first — I open Neiman Marcus emails hoping for a Chanel GWP or a beauty event invite, and this is pure clearance apparel and accessories. It's well-executed clearance apparel, but it's not speaking to the part of me that actually keeps this brand in my inbox.
- Would I click? Probably, out of habit — I'd at least glance at the Manolo Blahnik mules and that Isabel Marant hobo bag. Would I forward it to a friend? No. This is a "browse later, maybe" email, not a "you need to see this" email. Compare that to Sephora's National Lip Day send, which I gave a 9 — real 50% off, real urgency, immediate payoff. This is fine. It's not that.

## What's working

- The hero does its job in one glance: EXTRA 25% OFF, up to 70% off, LAST CALL, SHOP SALE — I don't have to hunt for the offer.
- Real designer names under every product tile (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Weekend Max Mara) — that's the "you're shopping with a luxury retailer" signal that Kohl's and Macy's can't fake.
- Clean product grid, nine distinct items, not a generic "Shop Now" banner pointing at the homepage.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text is crisp, nothing overlapping. After some of the messier sends I've seen lately, that's not nothing.
- It doesn't repeat the tiered spend-and-save table I've now seen from NM three times this week — a genuinely different creative, which matters for my patience with this brand.

## What's weak

- Zero beauty content. I'm a beauty-counter regular and this is bags, blouses, and cocktail dresses — not one lipstick or skincare SKU in sight.
- No InCircle loyalty mention anywhere. If I'm a member, treat me like one — Neiman Marcus knows this and still leaves it out.
- The actual deadline (8/31 at 10pm CT) is buried in six-point fine print at the very bottom, not stated anywhere near the CTA. "Last Call" implies urgency; nothing in the layout backs that up.
- No sizing or fit info on any product — for a Balmain jacket or Stuart Weitzman sandals, that's exactly the detail I need before I'd click through and buy, not just browse.
- Fourth-ish touch from this brand in as many days (7/25 spend-and-save, 7/25 reminder, 7/26 designer editorial, 7/26 reminder again) — I'm starting to feel the cadence, even if this creative is fresher than the last two.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a beauty or InCircle-member line somewhere in this email — even a small footer module — so it doesn't read as "generic department store blast" to someone who shops here specifically for the counter.
- 2. Move the actual end date out of the fine print and into the hero or right under the CTA — "Ends 8/31" next to SHOP SALE would do more for urgency than the word "LAST CALL" alone.
- 3. Add a size range or "available in XS–XL" type cue under at least the apparel tiles — I don't click through on clothes without knowing my size is even in stock.
- 4. Ease off the cadence — this is a strong send, but it's landing in the same week as three near-identical sale reminders. Space it out and it'll perform better.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer bags, dresses & shoes up to 70% off before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your size is still here — for now. Extra 25% off Last Call.`
