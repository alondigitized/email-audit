# Summer in full swing: 1,000+ new arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Ten. That's roughly how many Neiman Marcus emails have hit my inbox in the past 48 hours — I know because I audited the "New designers just added: The Designer Sale" send earlier today, which I'd rate as one of their better efforts this cycle. So I opened this one with approximately zero goodwill in reserve, and what I found didn't replenish it.
- "Brand New Styles Just Landed" on a black hero. Clean. Fine. But this is a no-discount, no-offer, no-InCircle touchpoint wedged between a flash sale and a designer-event push. It's a new arrivals catalog email, which has its place — but not as the tenth send in two days, and not when half the real estate is given over to Men's. I am a 47-year-old woman who buys Louboutins and Veronica Beard. I am not cross-shopping Canali suiting.
- The layout is technically tidy. The brand names in the product grid (Louboutin, Givenchy, Veronica Beard) are exactly what I'd expect from NM, and they'd pull me on any other week. But this email feels like it was queued and launched without anyone asking whether the inbox was already on fire. Mark as read. No click. Definitely not forwarding.

## What's working

- **Recognizable luxury brand names in the grid** — Christian Louboutin and Veronica Beard in the first two rows are precisely the names that make me stop scrolling; NM knows its customer here.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — black hero to product grid to CTA, no clutter, the eye moves naturally down the page without fighting noise.
- **"Shop Women's" appears first** — the Women's section leads, so if I'm on mobile and I only see the top, I at least land in the right hemisphere.
- **Seasonal framing is accurate** — "Summer in full swing" is where we are, and the product assortment (the red silk top, the nude Louboutin pump) reads as summer, not a stale archive pull.
- **No render bugs visible** — unlike the Sephora sends that have been addressing me as "Rosie," this one at least renders cleanly.

## What's weak

- **Half the email is Men's** — I had to scroll through two full product rows of men's suiting, Santoni shoes, and Ferragamo before I hit the footer. I am not your men's customer. Macy's does a better job filtering their sends by gender even when they're running blanket promotions.
- **No offer, no hook, no reason to act today** — "New arrivals" is ambient. The previous Designer Sale email gave me 50%-off The Row as a reason to click immediately. This gives me nothing except the concept of freshness.
- **1,000+ new arrivals is not a curation — it's a shrug** — Saying "1,000+" tells me you didn't edit. Saks would've led with three hero pieces and made me feel like they pulled the racks for me. This feels like a database query.
- **No InCircle or loyalty signal anywhere** — I accumulate points obsessively. The previous NM sends at least gestured at the InCircle program. This one treats me like a stranger off the street.
- **Cadence is genuinely punishing** — This is email ten in two days. Kohl's has been doing seven in two weeks and I'm already noting the fatigue there. NM is lapping that pace. I'm one more send away from hitting unsubscribe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut Men's entirely or move it below the fold** — Run a Women's-only version for female subscribers. The Men's half is real estate I have to scroll past to feel like this email is for me.
- 2. **Add one editorial hero product with a specific reason to want it now** — "The Louboutin Iriza just landed in three summer colors" beats "1,000+ new arrivals" every time. Give me one reason, not a warehouse count.
- 3. **Surface InCircle status or points language** — Even a single line — "Earn 5x points through Sunday" — turns a catalog email into a loyalty email.
- 4. **Drop the cadence before the send** — This is a product problem, not a copy problem. No subject line fixes ten emails in 48 hours.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer edit is here — new arrivals from the names you know`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Curated just for you — fresh drops across women's ready-to-wear, shoes, and bags`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New this week: the pieces landing in carts right now`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer in Full Swing" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) deployment with legacy HTML table structure. No critical rendering blockers detected, but four QA warnings merit attention — primarily around authentication header visibility and one-click unsubscribe compliance.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by probe** — all click-redirect domains (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) were excluded from HTTP probing. This is expected behavior for redirect-wrapped links, not a defect, but means broken-destination URLs cannot be confirmed programmatically.

**Plain-text body is 91% URLs** (5,270 / 5,827 chars). This is a strong spam-filter signal. The plain-text alternative appears to be auto-generated from the HTML by stripping tags, leaving behind raw tracking URL strings. A human-readable plain-text version should be authored separately.

No issues found with link structure in the HTML itself based on available source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** (lines 8–10 of `<head>`) indicate historical viewport experiments that were never cleaned up. These are inert but add noise and suggest the responsive strategy has been revised multiple times without cleanup.

**`<title>` is generic**: `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — trailing space, no subject-line echo. Minor; affects tab/preview labeling in some webmail clients.

**CSS media query breakpoints are split across two `<style>` blocks** (600px in block 1, 480px/520px in block 2). This is functional but increases maintenance surface and ordering sensitivity in clients that process styles sequentially.

**Hamburger nav implemented via CSS `:checked` hack** — relies on `<input type="checkbox">` which has partial support in Outlook and some Android clients. Fallback appears present (`*[class=hide]`), but this pattern is fragile.

No `role`, `aria-label`, or `alt` text issues are confirmed from the truncated source, but the audit cannot verify image `alt` attributes without the full HTML.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable comment block is exposed in the HTML source** (the `<!--REQUIRED variables: ...-->` comment at line 1). This is present in the *sent* message, not just the template — meaning recipients can view-source and see internal data dictionary annotations including field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`). While not exploitable directly, it leaks internal data model naming.

**`@user` variable assignment conflict**: the comment block defines `@user` as a `Lookup()` result, then immediately below sets `@user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` in a second commented block labeled "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW." If the wrong scenario was uncommented at send time, `@user` could resolve to the raw `EMAIL_ID` integer instead of the SHA-256 hash. The audit cannot confirm which scenario was active — this requires template-side verification.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are pulled from `NM_CRM_Feed` by email address lookup. If a subscriber's record is absent from that DE, these resolve to empty string silently — confirm null-handling downstream if these values feed tracking parameters.

The three header warnings are likely relay-capture artifacts (AgentMail stripping headers before logging) rather than true absences — Neiman Marcus at this send volume would be expected to have SPF/DKIM configured. **Verify against raw SMTP headers** from a delivered copy (e.g., Gmail "Show original") before treating these as real failures.

RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024. If genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk with both providers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully assess UTM parameter coverage without the complete link set (26 links were skipped). The AMPscript variable `@refid` (`CONCAT(@base_refid,"")`) appears to be the primary tracking token appended to destination URLs. Confirm:

- All 26 tracked links include `@refid` (or equivalent `nm_refid` / `cm_mmc` parameter)
