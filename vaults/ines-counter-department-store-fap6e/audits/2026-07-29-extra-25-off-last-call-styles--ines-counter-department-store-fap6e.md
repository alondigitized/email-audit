# Extra 25% off Last Call styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, credit where it's due — this is Neiman Marcus doing the thing I actually asked for after the week I've had with them: one clean red banner, "EXTRA 25% OFF" in a font size I don't have to squint at, "SHOP SALE" button right where my thumb lands, done. No fourth spend-and-save grid, no "70% OFF" siren screaming at me before I've had coffee. This is basically the same beat as their 7/28 "New Last Call styles added + Extra 25% off" send I gave an 8 — same red block, same clean hierarchy — just a slightly different subject on the same promo, which tells me they're still working through the same Last Call clearance rather than dropping something new.
- Where it loses me is the product grid underneath. A gold suede hobo bag, a Balmain tweed jacket, a Bugatchi men's shirt, wedge sandals, a navy knit dress, kitten-heel mules, a boning-detail mini dress, a pink wrap top, and a zebra-print blouse — that's nine items with zero connective tissue. It reads like someone dumped "top sellers across all departments" into a template rather than curating for a beauty-counter, Chevron-blazer-and-good-jewelry Gen X woman like me. No beauty, no fragrance, no accessories tier callout, nothing that says "we know what you actually buy at the Neiman's counter."
- Would I click? Probably, because the SHOP SALE button is right there and I trust the brand enough to browse. Would I forward it to a friend? No — there's nothing here worth texting anyone about, it's just another markdown wave.

## What's working

- The red hero block is legible top to bottom — EXTRA 25% OFF, the "up to 70%" context line, and LAST CALL branding all land in one glance without me scrolling.
- SHOP SALE button has real contrast (white on red) and sits exactly where I'd tap it on my phone.
- "Exclusions Apply" is printed right under the CTA instead of buried in six-point footer text — that's the kind of honesty Nordstrom doesn't always bother with.
- Nine-item product grid at least has designer names I recognize (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman) — that still whispers "our sale isn't clearance-bin sale," which matters to me.
- Category buttons below the grid (Shop Women's Clothing, Shop Handbags, Shop Shoes, etc.) give me an easy out if none of the nine hero pieces fit what I'm shopping for today.

## What's weak

- The product grid is a grab-bag — bag, jacket, men's shirt, sandals, dress, mules, dress, top, blouse — with no thread tying it together. Sephora's Daily Deals email I scored a 9 on stayed locked to one category (lip) the whole way down; this jumps departments like it's throwing darts.
- Nothing here says "beauty" or "fragrance" at all, which for someone who lives at the beauty counter is a miss — I'd have clicked instantly on a Charlotte Tilbury or La Mer callout, and there's zero signal that's even in this campaign.
- A men's dress shirt (Bugatchi) sitting in slot three of a nine-item grid with no gender label is just wasted real estate for me — I'm not shopping menswear off a general sale blast.
- The black "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF — join our text list" block at the bottom undercuts the whole email a little — I'm already getting 25% off up top, now you want my phone number for 15% more? Feels like a second ask stapled on, not integrated.
- No InCircle loyalty callout anywhere. I'm the exact customer who cares about points-back and status tier, and this email treats me like an anonymous list subscriber, not someone with an account and history.

## Recommendations

- 1. Curate the nine-item grid by category, not department roulette — pick either "dresses for your next event" or "accessories update" and stay there, the way Sephora stayed locked to lip. Pull the men's shirt out entirely; it doesn't belong in a female-persona blast.
- 2. Add a beauty/fragrance module — even one row of three products — since that's the actual reason I open Neiman Marcus emails over generic department-store blasts.
- 3. Surface InCircle points or tier status somewhere near the hero — "InCircle members earn X points on this sale" would move me from browsing to clicking.
- 4. Move or cut the 15% text-list ask — it competes with the 25% offer instead of supporting it. If it stays, frame it as "stack with your 25% off" so it reads additive, not competing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off Last Call — plus your InCircle points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Call: 25% off designer dresses & accessories`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns just added — up to 70% off before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Isabel Marant, Manolo Blahnik, Balmain — all in the sale`
