## Executive summary

- Now this is a Neiman's email I'd actually stop and look at. No crimson "LAST CALL" block, no percentage sign shouting at me — just that gorgeous zebra-print calf-hair mule as the hero, and a curated grid of designer sandals from Givenchy, Jacquemus, Louboutin, Stuart Weitzman, Bottega Veneta, and Versace. This is editorial curation, not a clearance dump, and it reads like Neiman's actually trusts me to recognize good design without a discount code attached.
- But there's zero price anywhere on this email — not on the hero, not under a single one of those six sandals. I love a lookbook, but I still need to know if I'm looking at a $650 Bottega mule or a $1,200 one before I click through six product pages to find out. And there's not a word about my InCircle tier — no points multiplier, no "double points on shoes" nudge, nothing that says they know I'm a member. Compare that to the "S for Sale" hero they ran twice this week (8/11 and 8/12) — those at least told me what I'd save.
- Would I click? Yes, actually — that hero shot alone earns a tap, and I'm a sucker for a good animal print. Would I forward it? Maybe to my friend who's shoe-obsessed, purely for the Louboutin and Bottega picks. But it won't move me to buy today because there's no price, no urgency, no reason it has to be now versus next week.

## What's working

- The zebra calf-hair mule as the hero image is genuinely striking — it stopped my scroll, unlike the flat "S" typography trick they've now run twice this week.
- Six-brand curated grid (Givenchy, Jacquemus, Louboutin, Stuart Weitzman, Bottega Veneta, Versace) feels like an actual editor picked these, not an algorithm.
- Clean, quiet layout — no crimson banners, no "LAST CALL," a relief after Neiman's own sale emails this week and a world away from Kohl's ten-emails-in-ten-days clapping-emoji fatigue.
- "Shop Sandals" CTA is simple and unambiguous — I know exactly what tapping it does.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere, on the hero or any of the six sandals — I have to click into every one just to know if it's in my budget.
- No InCircle status or points signal at all, even though I'm a loyalty member who checks for that every time — Kohl's manages to slap "Kohl's Rewards Member" front and center even when I'm annoyed at the cadence.
- No sizing or availability hint under any shoe — for a $700+ sandal I want to know before I click whether my size still exists.
- Zero urgency or context for why this roundup landed in my inbox today versus any other day.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price (or "from $X") under each of the six sandals — this is the single biggest lift; I shouldn't need six clicks to window-shop.
- 2. Add an InCircle line — even a small "Earn 2X points on shoes this week" would make me feel recognized as a member, not just a subscriber.
- 3. Swap "Shop Sandals" for something with a beat of specificity, like "Shop the Roundup" or "See All 6 Styles."
