# Save 20% | There's still time to treat Dad to a great gift 🎁
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Nineteen. That's where the Kohl's counter sits in my inbox now, and this one shows up with the same 20% off offer I saw on June 11, dressed in a Father's Day costume. I want to give it credit where it's due: the deadline is real (June 21 is right there), the Kohl's Cash callout at the bottom tells me they know I won't spend without points back, and the hero image — a dad with his family, warm and seasonal — beats the endless "LOWEST PRICES OF THE SEASON" grid from the June 7 send. The layout doesn't scream. That's progress.
- But here's the problem: I am not the audience for polo shirts starting at $12.99 and JBL Bluetooth speakers at 30% off. I shop the Chanel counter on a Saturday. I might absolutely buy my dad a Father's Day gift at Kohl's — but this email doesn't know that, doesn't try to know that, and is clearly going to everyone on the list regardless. Neiman Marcus sent me "Extra 20% Off Select Designer Styles" last week and at least that had a product I might touch. This is a flyer, not a send.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't forward it. If a friend texted me asking where to buy a Father's Day gift, I'd say Macy's — they at least triggered a specific "confirm now for your extra 25% off" action that felt like it knew who I was.

## What's working

- 20% off is front and center, no hunting required — Sephora buried their offer below a full-bleed banner in the May send and I almost missed it entirely
- Kohl's Cash ($10 for every $50 through June 21) is the one loyalty hook that moves me; it's in the email, and that matters
- Father's Day June 21 gives the urgency a real anchor — not "won't last forever" hand-waving like the June 6 clearance send
- The hero photo is warm and human instead of a product grid, which respects the occasion

## What's weak

- Nineteen emails in two-plus weeks; the 20% off has appeared at least three times now and has no punch left
- Nothing in here for me as a beauty shopper — Kohl's sells fragrance and skincare and I see zero of it; this is 100% a men's gifting and clearance send
- Below the fold becomes a department store flyer: 30% off electronics, 70% off clearance, $10 & Under, $25 & Under — the hierarchy collapses and I'm exhausted before I reach the footer
- "Explore the Gift Shop" is vague; compare "Shop Gifts for Dad" — same words, actual destination

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the men's version.** If I've never opened an electronics or menswear email, route me to a women's gifting version — fragrance, robes, jewelry — which Kohl's actually carries. The click rate on a targeted list will beat a blast every time.
- 2. **Cut the clearance section or move it to a secondary email.** 70% off clearance undercuts the 20% off story. If the real deal is in clearance, say so and commit — don't hedge with both.
- 3. **Pull the Kohl's Cash deadline above the nav bar.** Right now it's in the footer, which is where things go to die. For someone like me, that deadline is the entire reason to click today instead of Sunday.
- 4. **Give the CTA copy a real job.** "Explore the Gift Shop" → "Shop Father's Day Gifts." Verb + noun, one extra word, tells me what I'm getting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's day is June 21 — take an extra 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last weekend: 20% off gifts + earn Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 — through Sunday only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kohl's Cash + 20% off. Father's Day ends June 21.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% / Treat Dad" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard HTML 4.01 Transitional email rendered via CoherentPath/Responsys stack with three concurrent tracking pixel systems. One malformed HTML tag and ten images missing `alt` attributes are the primary code-level defects; compliance header gaps are relay artifacts but still require verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redundant tracking pixel stacks (3 systems firing simultaneously):**
- CoherentPath open tracker: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/ef5e913d-...`
- Kohl's MI open tracker: `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?L2IGND2DFLWEZOYY...`
- Adobe Audience Manager pixel: `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196`
- Kohl's MI render pixels: `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/...` (multiple)

This is standard for this stack but inflates payload and adds latency on slow connections. No broken links confirmed in the truncated source.

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable:** All CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity cannot be confirmed without resolving redirects (skipped per QA config). This is a coverage gap in the audit, not a confirmed defect.

**Malformed HTML tag on open-tracker div:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is an invalid closing tag with no opening counterpart. Most parsers will silently discard it, but it is invalid HTML and could cause rendering anomalies in strict parsers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>` as the document label. An empty value degrades assistive technology experience.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is a real accessibility defect for low-vision users.

**10 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/ef5e913d-...` (CoherentPath pixel — should be `alt=""`)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (AAM pixel — should be `alt=""`)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png` through `916d6fa08d185233.png` (content or render-tracking images)

Tracking/spacer pixels should have `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to signal intentional decoration to screen readers. Content images require descriptive alt text.

Note: `mi.kohls.com/p/up/.../o.gif` correctly includes `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` — that pattern should be applied consistently to the other tracking images.

**Duplicate Google Fonts loading:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">      <!-- link tag -->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...");    <!-- also @import -->
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...);
```
Both `<link>` and `@import` load the same two font families. This doubles the external font requests for no gain.
