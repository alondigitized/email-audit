# Move more & spend less during the Active Sale 👏
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- Same navy-and-red Kohl's playbook I've been getting all week, just retargeted at activewear instead of the general clearance grab-bag. At least this send has a real organizing principle — Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, Tek Gear, all logo'd right there on the hero, which is more than I can say for yesterday's "everything under the sun" clearance email. But I'm a beauty-counter regular, not someone shopping mid-tier athleisure for a preteen — the kid in the Adidas cap and the boy in Tek Gear tell me this is a family/back-to-school play, not a "Ines" play.
- What worked for me, mildly, is that "Up to 40% Off Activewear and shoes" is at least a real number attached to a real category, and I appreciate that they didn't bury it. But then they tack the "Ultimate Clearance Event — Save up to 70%" banner right underneath, and now I've got two competing sales in one email and no idea which one Kohl's actually wants me to click. Sephora's 4X points email from earlier this week was one offer, one audience, one CTA — this is Kohl's shouting two sales at once because they can't commit to a single message.
- I'm not clicking this one. It's not for me, and even if it were, the layout is doing too much work to convince me which deal is real.

## What's working

- The "Up to 40% Off" callout is large, legible, and sits right on the hero image — no hunting for the number.
- Brand logos (Nike, Adidas, Champion, Under Armour, FLX, Tek Gear) stacked down the left side of the hero at least tell me the scope of the sale at a glance.
- "Kohl's Rewards Member / Earn 5% Rewards" status banner up top is a nice touch — it acknowledges I'm a known customer, which Sephora also does well and Kohl's usually skips.
- Category nav buttons (Home, Women, Men, Kids) at the bottom are clean and give me an easy out if I actually wanted to browse.

## What's weak

- Two sales, two offers, two colorways in one email — 40% off activewear up top, then a full-bleed "Ultimate Clearance Event, up to 70%" banner right after. I already flagged this exact "up to 70%" clearance creative on 2026-08-03 and 2026-08-05 — it's just spliced into a different email now instead of standing alone.
- Every product model shown — the girl in the Adidas cap, the boy in Tek Gear — reads tween/teen, not a 47-year-old browsing for herself. Nothing here says "this is for you."
- "Coupons do not apply to select brands" sits right under the 40% headline — that's the fine-print-undercuts-the-offer move I don't love, and it's a smaller, weaker version of what tanked yesterday's Kohl's clearance email for me.
- Preview text is just "Plus, take up to 70% off ULTIMATE clearance finds" — that's the second sale's pitch leaking into the preheader before I've even opened the email, which just confirms this is two campaigns duct-taped together.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two separate sends — one Active Sale email with one clear CTA, one Clearance Event email — instead of merging them and diluting both. Sephora's 4X points send this week worked precisely because it stayed one offer, one audience.
- 2. If it has to stay one email, cut the second banner entirely or shrink it to a small footer teaser, not a full-width competing hero.
- 3. Show me an adult woman actually wearing the activewear, not just product-on-shoe shots and teen models — right now nothing in this signals "for someone my age."
- 4. Move "Coupons do not apply to select brands" into the fine print at the bottom, not directly under the headline offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off activewear — Nike, adidas, Under Armour`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Rewards are ready: 40% off Active Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, adidas, Under Armour & Tek Gear — all on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the Active Sale before select styles sell out`
