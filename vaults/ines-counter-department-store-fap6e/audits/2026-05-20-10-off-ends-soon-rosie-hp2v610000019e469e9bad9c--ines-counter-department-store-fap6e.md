# 10% off ends soon, Rosie
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Here we go again. "Rosie." That's three emails in a week addressed to someone named Rosie, and my name is Ines. I've been a Beauty Insider long enough to remember when Sephora's personalization felt like a differentiator — now it feels like a liability. The name thing was forgivable once (May 14th). Twice starts looking like a systemic data problem. Three times means nobody is watching.
- And then I open it, and the body tells me I'm "new here." I'm not new. I have years of Insider history, purchase receipts going back to when the points program still felt generous, and I know which counter at Nordstrom will let me try something before I buy it. Telling me to "start shopping" because you've curated a first-purchase discount for a new subscriber is genuinely disorienting. The countdown clock hits ENDS TODAY, I feel a flicker of urgency — and then I read the fine print that says this is a first-purchase offer and I can't use it at all. The urgency evaporates completely.
- I would not click. I would not forward this. I would scroll to the bottom to confirm the barcode situation (there are two, one for Sephora, one for Kohl's, which is its own kind of chaos) and then mark this read out of obligation.

## What's working

- **The countdown clock is clean.** "ENDS TODAY" with the live timer is exactly the kind of urgency execution that makes me glance at my cart — the visual is unambiguous and the hierarchy lands.
- **The promo code is visible and prominent.** SE3ARPH is right there, date-stamped through 5/25/2026. No hunting. Macy's buried theirs behind a confirmation modal last week; Sephora at least puts it in plain sight.
- **"Recommended For You" is doing real work.** That product grid midway through looks like actual personalization — specific items, prices shown, not just generic category cards. When Neiman Marcus dropped eight emails in 48 hours last month, none of them had a product grid that felt this targeted.
- **The Beauty Insider branding is present.** At least the tier badge shows up, even if the offer contradicts my status.

## What's weak

- **"Rosie." Again.** Third send. This isn't a one-time glitch — it's a broken data pipeline, and every send with the wrong name makes me trust the brand less. Kohl's ran a better-targeted email last week with my actual name.
- **"You're new here" copy is an insult to a loyalty member.** The "Wondering Why You Got This?" section explicitly says this offer is curated because I'm new to Sephora. I'm not. The dissonance between my Insider badge and the new-customer framing is jarring.
- **The 10% off is a first-purchase offer — I can't use it.** This is the core failure. The urgency clock, the bold 10% hero, the SHOP NOW button — all of it is theater for an existing customer because the fine print kills the offer before I can act on it.
- **Two barcodes for two different retailers at the bottom is confusing.** One Sephora code, one Kohl's code — and there's a separate exclusion note about Kohl's.Sephora.com right in the body. The co-brand execution feels sloppy compared to how cleanly Saks handles their multi-channel promos.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the name merge tag before any other optimization.** This is the highest-leverage change in the entire send. One broken personalization token is undoing the rest of the creative work on every re-send.
- 2. **Suppress first-purchase offers from existing Insider members.** Segment on purchase history before triggering this flow. An existing customer seeing a new-subscriber welcome email is a relationship-damaging moment, not a conversion opportunity.
- 3. **If the offer can't be changed, change the framing.** At minimum, reframe "Your First Purchase" as "A thank-you offer" or tie it to something the existing member can actually redeem.
- 4. **Consolidate the Kohl's barcode footnote into a single clear section** — or remove it entirely from the main email if Kohl's redemption is handled separately.
- 5. **Surface a product or category from my actual purchase history** in the hero — not just the recommendation grid. Neiman Marcus gets credit for at least naming the collection in their hero. Sephora's hero is generic.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Beauty Win expires at midnight — here's the code.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Valid through 5/25. Use SE3ARPH at checkout.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) violation. Recommendation: remove `maximum-scale=1` or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — Blanket `text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed):**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to essentially all elements. Overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling for users with low vision. The `.sup` and `td` selector already carries this rule; the broad selector is redundant and harmful.

**Issue — `robots` meta uses invalid syntax (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with a space) is not a valid robots directive. The correct value is `noindex` (no space). Most crawlers will ignore the malformed directive. Low severity in email context but indicates a template-level copy-paste error.

**Note — `alt` attributes:** Not assessable from truncated source. Verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` text (or `alt=""` for decorative images).
