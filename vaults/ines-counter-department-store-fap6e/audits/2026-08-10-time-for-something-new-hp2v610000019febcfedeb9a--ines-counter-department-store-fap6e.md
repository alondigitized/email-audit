# Time for something new 🛍️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- "Something new is here" — okay, but this is the fourth "new stuff dropped" email Sephora's sent me this week, and my eye is officially trained to skim past it. That said, I'll admit the $10 bonus card banner up top ("earn $10 when you spend $75+ digital gift card") did make me pause for half a second, and the trial-size mascara offer from Charlotte Tilbury/Dior/Lancôme is a real, concrete reason to click — free samples from prestige lines is exactly the kind of thing that gets a beauty-counter regular like me in.
- But then it's just... a scroll. New MAC lip tin, new Too Faced palette, then a full grid of eight more "NEW" products — mango jelly cream, a serum, a setting spray, body butter, a hair mousse, a lip duo, a $98 perfume, a moisturizer. That's not a campaign, that's a catalog dump with "NEW" stamped on everything until the word stops meaning anything. Compare this to Sephora's own August 5th "4X points on foundations & concealers" send, which scored an 8 from me because it was one offer, one CTA, done. This one buries the one offer I actually care about (the $10 bonus card, the mascara trial) under nine unrelated product tiles.
- I'd skim, maybe redeem the mascara trial if I'm already in-store mode, and not forward this to anyone — there's nothing here worth telling a friend about.

## What's working

- The $10 bonus card banner at the very top is a real, specific offer with a clear threshold ($75+ digital gift card) — that's the strongest hook in the whole email.
- The trial-size mascara offer (Charlotte Tilbury, Dior, or Lancôme, pick one) is genuinely appealing to me — free deluxe samples from prestige brands is a proven Sephora move, same energy as the K18 deluxe-sample play from August 9th.
- Beauty Insider tier bar at the footer (Insider / VIB / Rouge) is present, which at least nods to loyalty status even if it's just decorative down there.
- Clean product photography, no render bugs, nothing broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- Nine "NEW" product tiles in a row with no organizing logic — skincare, hair, fragrance, makeup all mixed together. My eye has nowhere to land after the hero.
- "Something new is here" as hero copy is the vaguest possible framing — Neiman Marcus at least tells me a specific % off ("up to 70%"), this tells me nothing about what's in it for me.
- This is the fourth "new arrivals" style Sephora email in a week (August 4th "so hot right now," 6th "art of body care," 8th "ready set shop," now this) — the cadence is wearing me down and none of them have escalated the offer.
- A $98 fragrance and a $22 serum sitting in the same undifferentiated grid tells me nothing about who this email is actually for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the $10 bonus card or the mascara trial as the single hero offer — cut the nine-tile product dump entirely, or move it way below the fold as a secondary "browse new arrivals" link.
- 2. Give the mascara trial its own full-width CTA block instead of burying it as a small header banner — that's the offer with real intent behind it.
- 3. Segment the product grid by category (skincare / makeup / fragrance) if it has to stay, so I can skip straight to what I shop for.
- 4. Vary the subject line pattern — four "new drops" emails in a row is Sephora talking to itself, not to me.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Free mascara trial, your pick: Tilbury, Dior, or Lancôme`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $75 on a gift card, get $10 back — plus a deluxe mascara sample`
