# The hottest drops are here
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so it's a launch-day grid. "50% off Select Beauty" up top with "TODAY ONLY" — that got a flicker of interest, same play Sephora ran on 8/29 and 8/31, and I already know how that ends: exclusions apply and it's never the counter brands I actually buy. Then it's "JUST DROPPED" and a straight scroll through nine — nine — new product tiles: Byredo, Dior Backstage, Rhode, OUAI, Jung Saem Mool, Kayali, Touchland, amika. That's not a curated drop, that's a warehouse inventory dump with a headline on it.
- What actually stops me is Dior Backstage and Byredo — those are real counter names I'd cross-shop at Nordstrom or Saks, and "only at Sephora" on the Byredo body mist is a genuine differentiator worth noting. But by the time I've scrolled past Rhode's skincare patches and a hand sanitizer gift set, I've lost the plot. There's no signal for who I am as a shopper — no Beauty Insider tier callout until the very bottom footer logo, no points multiplier, nothing that says "we know you're a Rouge member." Compare that to Sephora's own 8/31 send, which opened with $5 off at 250 points and 4x points on top brands — that one respected my loyalty status from the first fold. This one doesn't, until it's too late.
- I'd skim, maybe tap Dior or Byredo out of curiosity, but I would not forward this. It reads like a catalog page, not a curated pick for someone who already has her brands.

## What's working

- The 50% off banner up top is a legible, high-contrast hook, same playbook that's worked for Sephora before.
- Byredo and Dior Backstage are recognizable, aspirational counter names that fit exactly how I shop — that's real targeting, not filler.
- "Only at Sephora" and "NEW · LIMITED EDITION" tags are used consistently, which does signal exclusivity even if it's repeated to the point of noise.
- The Clean at Sephora and Planet Aware badges (Rhode, amika) are a nice trust cue for anyone tracking ingredient/sustainability claims.

## What's weak

- Nine product tiles in a row with no editorial framing — this is a warehouse dump dressed as a "drop," not a curated edit like Nordstrom would send.
- No loyalty/tier signal anywhere near the top — Sephora's own 8/31 email led with points math, this one buries Beauty Insider in the footer logo.
- The 50% off hero and the "JUST DROPPED" new-arrivals section fight each other for attention — I don't know if this is a sale email or a launch email.
- Small print ("Exclusions/terms apply," "While supplies last") sitting right under the hero offer again — same complaint I had about the 8/29 "50% off" send.
- Nothing here reads demographically like it's for a 47-year-old — Rhode, Kayali, and the hand sanitizer set skew younger/TikTok, while Dior and Byredo skew where I actually shop. It's two different customers in one email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two emails — one sale-led, one drop-led. Cramming a sitewide discount and nine new launches into one send buries both.
- 2. Move a Beauty Insider points or tier line into the hero, the way the 8/31 send did — that's the version of this brand I actually respond to.
- 3. Cut the grid from nine tiles to three or four hero launches (Dior, Byredo, maybe one more) and drop the rest to a "see more new arrivals" link.
- 4. Put the fine print in the footer, not stapled to the hero offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Byredo just landed at Sephora`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off ends today + new from Dior, Byredo`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: 4x points on today's new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dior Backstage, Byredo, and 7 more just dropped`
