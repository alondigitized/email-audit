- The email itself is clean. The giant black "S" on sage green is pretty. "Up to 40% off" sits right there with a plain "Shop Sale" button. It's calmer than the Kohl's "20% off won't stick around forever (hint, hint)" send, which shouted at me for five days running. ## What's working

- The hero is clean. The oversized serif "S" and one clear "SHOP SALE" button make the offer obvious in two seconds.
- The 3x3 grid names the designers (Amiri, Vince, Theory, Schutz, Tory Burch). That feels like designer access, and it's what Saks does well.
- The sage and black palette looks like Neiman's and not like a clearance flyer.
- The six category buttons (dresses, shoes, handbags and so on) make it easy to jump to what I want.
- The 15% text-list offer in the black box is easy to spot.

## What's weak

- There's no beauty and no InCircle or points language. Sephora's "10% off our most-searched beauty" speaks to me directly, and Neiman's earlier Beauty Event send did too. This one doesn't.
- "Up to 40% off" with "Exclusions Apply" in the hero, and "Savings of 25-40%" in the fine print, makes the real discount feel smaller. I don't trust "up to" claims.
- There are no prices and no before/after figures. I can't tell whether the Vince jacket is a deal.
- No models are shown, only flat product shots. About half the items are menswear or younger-looking, and nothing signals "this is for a 47-year-old woman."
- The black "Claim your 15% off" text sign-up is a second offer competing with the first. It also asks for my phone number after I've had a week of emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with member value. Add a line like "InCircle members: earn Triple Points on sale" or show the actual sale price on a few items. That's the single biggest lift for someone like me.
- 2. Show real prices on the products, with the original price struck through. This is the Macy's habit I do like.
- 3. Space out the cadence. Two sends in one day, after four days of near-daily mail, trains me to ignore this brand.
- 4. Drop the text sign-up block, or move it below the footer links, so there's one offer in the email.
- 5. Put a woman's-wear item at the top of the grid and use a model photo.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with no confirmed rendering-breaking errors. Three real issues show up: internal ESP/data-extension comments left in the delivered source, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, and a missing `lang` attribute. The QA warnings on unsubscribe headers and authentication can't be confirmed as sender faults because of the relay.

Only the first part of the source was available (it was truncated), so the body, footer and links were not reviewed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 30 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the QA probe. Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified, not confirmed broken.
- **Plain-text bloat:** the plain-text part is 5705 of 6685 characters URLs (about 85%). That is above the 70% threshold and suggests long tracking URLs, which can hurt spam scoring. It is a real finding, though the cause is inferred.
- **Internal parameters:** the source comment names `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm` and `@cmdid`, so links probably carry per-recipient identifiers. I couldn't see the actual hrefs.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on supporting clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Exact-match selectors:** the mobile CSS uses `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. These match only when `class` is exactly that value, so any element with a second class silently skips the responsive rule.
- **Checkbox navigation:** the hamburger menu relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. Many clients, including Gmail, strip or ignore this, so I'd check that the nav degrades to visible links. I couldn't see the markup to confirm.
- **Small footer text:** `.footerText` and `.disclaimerText` are set to 12px. This is minor and not a failure.
- **Not assessable:** image `alt` text, image-to-text ratio and link text were outside the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked template comments:** the delivered HTML opens with an internal developer comment. It lists the data extensions `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`, the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm` and `cmdid`, and the instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW". It also contains `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. This exposes internal schema and shows template scaffolding wasn't stripped before send.
- **Merge tokens:** no unresolved tokens (such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}`) appear in the visible portion. The remainder is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** both were not found, but the QA note says the relay may not capture them. This is unconfirmed. If they are genuinely absent, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058, required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders) is missing.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. It's expected missing via the relay, so check it on a direct send.
- **Footer:** the in-body unsubscribe link and physical address were in the truncated part, so they're not confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
This is not assessable. The tracking links were skipped, so landing pages and UTM parameters couldn't be checked. The comment mentions `@base_refid` and `@refid`, so campaign attribution may use a `refid` parameter rather than UTMs. That's inferred, not verified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip developer comments from the rendered output (the leading `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block and the `SET @user` comment) so DE and field names aren't exposed.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Switch the responsive rules to `*[class~=…]` or plain class selectors so multi-class elements still match.
5. Test headers on a direct send, not the relay, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
6. Shorten tracking URLs in the plain-text part, or reduce the number of links, to bring the URL share below 70%.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and resolve the 30 redirect links to verify landing pages, UTM/refid parameters, the unsubscribe link and the postal address.
