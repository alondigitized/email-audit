# Don't miss out on a $100–$1,250 gift card with your purchase today
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the eighth Neiman's email in about six days, and the gift-card ladder is back. I got the offer in about three seconds: spend $500, get $100 back, up to $1,250 at $5,000. That's a clear reward table, and I respect it. But I've now seen this math in the gift-card sends before the "First Call" run, and the "First Call" block three times since. I'm tired.
- The table is the best part. A spend row and an earn row tell me exactly what I get. Then the email buries the rule that matters most to me, in 8-point type at the bottom. Sale and reduced-price merchandise doesn't count. I buy at the brand event, so I'd be shopping full price, which I rarely do at Neiman's. It also doesn't stack with other gift card offers, and the award takes up to 8 weeks to arrive. That's a long wait on a card I'd have to spend again.
- Would I click? Maybe, if I already had a Chanel or Tom Ford purchase planned. Would I forward it? No. I'd mark it read and wait for the next one.

## What's working

- The spend/earn table is the cleanest loyalty-style math I've seen from Neiman's this week. I can work out my return in two seconds.
- The "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" line caught my eye. I'm a beauty-counter buyer, and it's the only thing in the email aimed at me.
- The deadline and code, OURGIFT, are in the fine print: 10/20/26 at 10:00 PM CT. The deadline is credible.
- The designer grid has real names: Moncler, Burberry, Manolo Blahnik, Christian Louboutin. It feels like the Neiman's I know, not a clearance rack.
- The maroon hero looks calm. Kohl's stacked its "ENDS TONIGHT" banner five times, and this doesn't shout like that.

## What's weak

- Nothing in the grid is beauty. The hero says "Includes Beauty & Fragrance," then the nine tiles show denim, shoes and a dress, so the promise doesn't carry through. Sephora would have led with a fragrance or a skincare tile.
- The exclusion for sale and reduced-price items is in tiny type at the bottom, and "Exclusions Apply" in the hero is smaller still. It reads as buried, and the offer feels less honest because of it.
- The tiles show no prices and no "shop this" link. The nine items are all designer pieces at full price. A $5,000 spend is the only way to reach the top tier. That feels like a different customer than me.
- It's a repeat. The 10/17 "First Call" and "Things We Love" sends were just hours ago, and a gift-card send on top of those makes the cadence feel pushy.
- The six identical maroon category buttons feel like a menu, and none of them is beauty. I'd be hunting.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a beauty or fragrance hero tile in the grid and link "Shop Beauty & Fragrance" directly. That's the one change that would lift this for someone like me.
- 2. Move "Sale and reduced-price merchandise do not qualify" up, right under the table, in readable type. Honest up front beats a letdown at checkout.
- 3. State the award timeline near the table: "Gift card arrives within 8 weeks." Setting expectations like that builds trust.
- 4. Space the sends out. A gift-card send after two "First Call" sends in two days is too much.
- 5. Add one line on how Neiman's InCircle points interact. If my card earns points, I want to know whether this stacks. Macy's at least tells me when an offer is confirm-to-activate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $500, get a $100 gift card — ends Tuesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your beauty purchase can earn a gift card`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to $1,250 back, beauty and fragrance included. Code OURGIFT, ends 10/20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Full-price styles only. See the full spend-and-earn tiers.`

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing `lang`:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`. Screen readers can't pick a pronunciation language.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4.
- **Invalid font stack:** `.disclaimerText { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'; }`. Quoting `'sans-serif'` makes it a literal font name, not the generic fallback. If Arial and Helvetica are missing, the fallback fails.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block comes before `<!DOCTYPE>`. Older IE and some webmail engines can drop into quirks mode when a comment precedes the doctype.
- **Doctype URL:** the DTD URL uses `https://www.w3.org/TR/xhtml1/DTD/…`, not the canonical `http://` system identifier. This is harmless in email clients.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]`, `*[class=mobile]` and similar only match when `class` is exactly that value. Any element with a second class silently loses the mobile rule. The truncated source doesn't show whether any element does.
- **Checkbox/`:checked` hamburger menu:** the mobile nav depends on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~…`. Gmail and Outlook clients don't support this, so those users likely get no working menu. I can't see the fallback markup in the truncated source.
- **Body animation:** `body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }` runs forever and forces repaints on every client that honors it. A one-shot reflow hack would do the same job.
