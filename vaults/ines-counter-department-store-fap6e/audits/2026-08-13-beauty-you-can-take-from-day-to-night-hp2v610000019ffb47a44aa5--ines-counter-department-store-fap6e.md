# Beauty you can take from day to night 💕
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- "Beauty you can take from day to night" — fine, but this is barely a subject, it's a mood board caption. Compared to yesterday's "Rosie" mix-up disaster, at least nobody's misnaming me today, so I'll give Sephora credit for not repeating that exact mistake. But this email is trying to be six emails at once: a trial-size push, an "All Day. All Night." mood piece, a Makeup by Mario launch, a nine-product grid with zero context for who any of it is for, then fragrance minis, moisturizers, a lip lineup, and finally the Beauty Insider footer crammed in almost as an afterthought.
- What actually caught my eye was the Farmacy green clean claim and the Kérastase hair serum — those read like real product info I could use. But nothing here says "we know you're a member," nothing says "here's your tier," and the free trial-size gift buried up top requires a $30 purchase with "exclusions/terms apply" in tiny print, which is the same buried-fine-print move Sephora pulled with the K18 sample on the 9th. I'm not clicking this one — it's a browse-and-move-on email, not a forward-to-a-friend email. Neiman's "1,000+ new arrivals" send this week was cleaner and more confident than this scattershot grid.

## What's working

- The Farmacy "clean" badge and Kérastase "dry hair" callout give me real, specific reasons to consider a product — that's more useful than most of what Sephora sends me.
- "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" on the Mario eyeshadow is a legitimate exclusivity hook that plays to my brand-event instincts.
- Prices are visible right under every product (Prada $180, Sulwhasoo $89) — no hide-the-price games, which I appreciate after Macy's confirm-to-save nonsense.

## What's weak

- Nine unrelated products in a flat grid with no story — skincare, fragrance, haircare, sanitizer all mashed together like a clearance table, not a curated edit.
- The free trial-size offer up top has "*Exclusions/terms apply" in a font I can barely read — same trick as the K18 email's buried conditions.
- Zero personalization or tier acknowledgment anywhere until the generic Beauty Insider banner at the very bottom, and even that's just tier logos with no "you're a VIB" or points balance.
- "All Day. All Night." with a full-bleed product-swatch photo eats a huge chunk of scroll for basically no information — Neiman's this week said more with less.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero — either the Mario eyeshadow launch or the trial-size gift — and build the whole top of the email around it instead of stacking five different offers before I've scrolled past the fold.
- 2. Move my Beauty Insider status (points, tier) up near the header, not buried in the footer — Sephora's own INSIDER banner on the 11th at least led with the program, even if it embarrassingly showed "0 points."
- 3. Cut the nine-product grid down to 3-4 with a shared theme ("your next skincare refresh" or similar) instead of a random assortment.
- 4. Surface the trial-size terms in normal-size type, not fine print — I've been burned by buried conditions from this brand before.
