# The Roundup: Saint Laurent’s spot-on bags
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Fifteen. That's my count for Neiman Marcus since July 2nd. After a week and a half of Last Call urgency, 70%-off designer flash sales, a gift card earning moment that almost got me to click, and — inexplicably — a wedding dress email — today they walk in holding a leopard Saint Laurent tote and say *nothing*. No offer. No price. Just: here are some beautiful bags, go figure out the rest yourself.
- And honestly? The bags are gorgeous. That hero leopard calf-hair structured tote is exactly the kind of thing I'd stop in front of at the Saks Fifth Avenue handbag floor. The burgundy quilted YSL, the rust crescent hobo, the suede-looking mini — this is a clean, confident roundup and it doesn't shout at me. After all those "LAST CALL" subject lines and triple exclamation points from competitors, I appreciate that Neiman Marcus is capable of whispering.
- But I'm not clicking. There's no price, no offer, no InCircle point signal, nothing to justify moving from "I'm dreaming about this bag" to "I'm buying this bag today." It's a mood board delivered to my inbox. I'd look at it the way I'd flip through a magazine on a Sunday morning — appreciate it, close it, move on.

## What's working

- **The hero bag earns its space.** That leopard tote is editorial and aspirational — it makes me want to touch something. It pulls the eye immediately and the layout doesn't fight it.
- **Editorial tone is a relief after the sale gauntlet.** This email doesn't scream. After the 70%-off send on the 11th and the gift-card urgency yesterday, this one feels like NM remembers it's a luxury retailer.
- **The "THE ROUNDUP" label signals intent.** I know what this is — a curated product story, not a flash sale — and I can calibrate my expectations accordingly. That's a small trust cue.
- **Two CTAs that are actually sensible.** "Shop Saint Laurent" and "Shop Handbags" — both are verb + noun, both are unambiguous, and they don't try to be clever about it. Better than the Sephora "Obsessed? Same." category that made me work too hard.
- **Clean render, zero visual friction.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no mystery blank space. The layout holds. I'm looking at you, every Macy's sale email that's ever had a misaligned coupon box.

## What's weak

- **No price signal anywhere.** Not even a "from $XXX" line. I know Saint Laurent bags are expensive, but without any anchor, I have no reason to click now versus never. Saks would have at least given me the starting price so I could decide if I'm in the ballpark.
- **No loyalty or InCircle signal.** I am a points person. Full stop. I refuse to use a card without getting something back, and yet this email offers me zero information about what spending here earns me. Yesterday's gift-card email got that right; today it completely disappeared.
- **Five bags, no hierarchy below the hero.** The 2×2 grid at the bottom shows four bags at roughly equal size with no styling copy, no name labels, no price — just bags floating in white space. I don't know if I'm looking at a $1,200 bag or a $4,500 bag.
- **The cadence weight is real.** Fifteen emails in ten days is a lot of relationship. Even when the email itself is beautiful, arriving as #15 in a run means I'm already a little fatigued before I even open it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor under the hero** — even "starting at $XXX" signals whether this is a dream or a decision. Without it, I'm scrolling past.
- 2. **Name the bags in the grid.** Four bags with no copy underneath is a lookbook PDF, not an email that converts. One line — name, maybe one adjective — helps me attach to the specific product.
- 3. **Drop a single loyalty line near the CTAs.** Something like "InCircle members earn points on every purchase" costs nothing to include and would be the single line that takes me from "admiring" to "considering."
- 4. **Give me the style advisor CTA higher up** — that footer icon is exactly the kind of concierge signal that differentiates NM from everyone else. A line like "Not sure which bag is yours? Chat with your stylist" near the grid would be brand-appropriate and convert-friendly.
- 5. **Cut to four bags, not five.** The hero + four works in a clean 2×2. Five feels unresolved and the bottom row reads slightly off-balance.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript template with table-based layout and responsive CSS. No hard render-breaking bugs were found, but four compliance warnings from the QA relay and an exposed variable comment block warrant attention.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 tracking links skipped (unprobed)**
All click links route through a redirect domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com`). The QA probe correctly skipped these, but destination URL integrity — broken product links, 404s on landing pages — is unverified. Spot-checking redirect chains manually before send is warranted.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs** (3,138 of 3,631 chars)
The plain-text alternative is effectively just a URL dump. This is a deliverability signal that spam filters key on; Gmail and SpamAssassin penalize plain-text parts with URL density this high.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags (lines 7–9 in `<head>`)**
Three viewport meta variants are present as HTML comments. While inert, they indicate unresolved responsive strategy decisions left in production source — a code hygiene issue that can cause confusion during future template edits.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS `:checked` trick with `position: absolute`**
The mobile nav implementation (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`) relies on a checkbox hack. This is broadly supported but has known rendering gaps in older Samsung Mail and some Outlook Mobile builds. No alt fallback is present.

**`max-width` typo in CSS** — `.TxtSize2x2 { max-wid…` (truncated in provided source)
The source was truncated mid-rule. If this truncation reflects a genuine CSS parse error in the deployed template, `.TxtSize2x2` cells will not constrain correctly on screens ≤520px wide. Needs verification in the full source.

**No `role="presentation"` on layout tables**
Layout tables lack `role="presentation"`, which causes screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) to announce them as data tables. This is a known WCAG 1.3.1 violation pattern common in legacy email templates.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Variable comment block exposed in production source**
The template opens with an unrendered AMPscript comment block visible in the raw HTML:

```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user ... @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */-->
```

These comments are sent to recipients. They expose internal variable names (`@cmdid`, `@uEm`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`) and the hashed-email strategy. While HTML comments are not rendered, they are visible in "View Source" and to any inbox security scanner. Strip these before send.

**`@user` assignment is ambiguous**
The comment block shows two conflicting assignment strategies (`Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` vs. `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`) with a "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" note. If the wrong branch is active, `@user` (used for tracking/personalization) may be set incorrectly or left empty, silently degrading personalization without a visible send-time error.

| Check | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay — may be stripped upstream or absent |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | One-click unsubscribe not confirmed; required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5k/day) |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header is the highest-severity compliance issue. Gmail enforces RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders; absence can trigger inbox suppression or bulk folder routing.

The missing `Authentication-Results` is likely an artifact of the AgentMail relay not forwarding received headers, but SPF/DKIM should be confirmed at the MTA level independently.
