# Summer’s wedding-guest style
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- After what I count as six emails from Neiman Marcus in roughly four days, this one arrives as an editorial exhale. I'll give them that — the pivot from "LAST CHANCE" screaming to a quiet wedding-guest mood board is at least a change of temperature. The creative is genuinely beautiful. That hero model, the natural light, the posture — that is the quiet-luxury register NM does better than almost anyone. The LBD flatlay with the white kitten heel and that pearl-tone jewelry is an actually considered styling opinion, not clip-art commerce.
- But there is nothing to click on. No price. No offer. No InCircle hook. No "members earn points on every piece." Just two looks and a soft "shop the look," which, after three emails in a row about a ticking gift-card deadline, feels like the band stopped playing mid-song. I understand the palate cleanser instinct. I don't have time to act on a mood board.
- I'd mark it read and keep moving. Wouldn't click. Wouldn't forward.

## What's working

- The hero image earns it — the model, setting, and styling register as sophisticated and appropriately adult. Not a 24-year-old in fuchsia; a woman I recognize as someone who actually attends weddings.
- "Shop Wedding Guest" and "Shop Gowns" are specific, clean CTAs. Saks slides into "Explore the Edit" and you have no idea what you're clicking into. NM is clearer here.
- The LBD flatlay is an editorial point of view — the black tiered dress against white accessories is a real styling call, not a product dump.
- Visual hierarchy is clean throughout: hero, headline, dual CTAs, then the two looks in sequence.
- Renders without a flaw. I've had Macy's emails arrive with images broken in the left gutter; this is not that.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere. After three consecutive emails about a gift card event with deadlines, this is a full stop with no payoff for someone who won't use her card without earning something back.
- The men's suit block eats nearly half the email. I do not shop for a man. If Sephora knows to only show me the women's skincare aisle, NM can personalize a content template by purchase history.
- "Love is in the Air" is a headline I've seen in Nordstrom, Macy's, and every department store email from April through June since roughly 2009. NM is capable of something more specific.
- No price visible on either look. Even one anchor — a number next to the dress — tells me whether this is my register or not.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one offer signal** — "Complimentary shipping on all wedding guest orders through Sunday" or a pointed InCircle earn note changes the math completely. I don't browse NM categories without a reason.
- 2. **Cut or collapse the men's section** — personalize by gender and purchase history. Move it to a secondary row for customers who've bought menswear. I shouldn't have to scroll through a suit I'll never buy to get to the end of this email.
- 3. **Replace "Love is in the Air"** — NM has a distinct editorial voice and this is not it. Try: *"Black to a summer wedding. We made it work."* or *"The unexpected LBD. The white shoe moment. The right bag."* Specific and ownable.
- 4. **Price at least one look** — an anchoring number signals this is a real shopping email. The absence of any price makes this feel like a Pinterest board.
- 5. **Acknowledge InCircle** — one line: "InCircle members earn points on every piece." I earned on my last two orders and this email gives no indication they remember that.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Dead AMPscript inside HTML comment.** The SHA256 hashing assignment is wrapped in an HTML comment, meaning it will never execute in SFMC:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

AMPscript inside `<!-- -->` is not processed by the SFMC rendering engine. The comment header says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," implying a working conditional block exists further down the template — but if that block is missing or misconfigured, `@user` will be unset and any downstream lookup or personalization token using it will silently fail or render blank.

**Internal data extension names exposed in source comments:**
- `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`
- `NM_CRM_Feed`

These are visible to anyone viewing source. Minor information disclosure; not exploitable from email alone, but worth scrubbing from production sends.

**Dual email identifiers in use:** `@uEm` (from `NM_CRM_Feed`) appears to be a separate, possibly unhashed email value alongside the SHA256 `@user` field. If `@uEm` is appended to tracking URLs as a query param, that constitutes PII in URL logs.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — fails RFC 2369. Without this header, Gmail and Apple Mail cannot surface the native "Unsubscribe" UI button. QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay stripping headers; the actual send infrastructure should be verified directly.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** — fails RFC 8058. One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method variant) has been required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence is a deliverability risk: Gmail may route to spam or auto-suppress.

**Authentication-Results header absent.** SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed via the relay capture. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have DKIM alignment with the `From:` header domain — this needs to be verified at the MTA level, not through the relay.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are presumably present in the email footer (not visible in truncated source) — this must be confirmed in full source review.
