# Homepage snapshot · www.cvs.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- CVS is coming in with a Mother's Day push — I can see the tab right at the top and some pink flowers peeking through — which actually does speak to me as someone who's always picking up something for mom while I'm waiting on prescriptions. But before I can even *see* what they're offering, there's this big cookie-consent wall sitting right in the middle of my screen. That's the first thing I interact with, not an offer or my ExtraBucks balance. CVS clearly knows pharmacy is central to their brand — "Manage prescriptions" is right there in the nav — but the page feels like it's still loading its own pitch.

## What's working

- **"Manage prescriptions" nav tab** — that is literally why I drive to CVS every week. Seeing it prominently in the second navigation position tells me this site understands what I'm there for.
- **Mother's Day tab, first position in the quick-links bar** — timely and relevant. I am absolutely the person buying a card and a candle while I wait for dad's blood pressure meds.
- **Store locator strip** — "101 E. ALAMEDA AVE., Open, Closes 12:00 AM" tells me exactly which store I'm near and that it's open. That kind of practical information earns a little trust from me right away.
- **Search bar with "ask a question" copy** — that's a nice nudge. As someone who sometimes needs to check drug interactions or look up generic options, that framing makes the search feel useful, not just a lookup box.

## What's weak

- **The consent modal landing dead-center** — this is the first thing I'm interacting with, a wall of legal text about data sharing and third-party research partners. I didn't come here to read a privacy policy. I came to check if my ExtraBucks are about to expire.
- **"Here for what" — the hero headline is cut off** — I can't finish reading CVS's own tagline. The modal ate the bottom half of the hero. Whatever emotional hook they wrote is invisible.
- **Zero ExtraCare visibility** — I have the app. I print the long receipt. I track my ExtraBucks. None of that loyalty relationship shows up above the fold. CVS knows me; this page doesn't act like it.
- **"Use the CVS app" button dominates the utility strip** — that button is asking me to download something I already have. Wasted real estate that could show my current ExtraBucks balance or a personalized offer.
- **No concrete offer above the fold** — not a percentage, not a dollar amount, not a bonus bucks event. Nothing to trigger my "oh, I should grab that" instinct.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "Use the CVS app" with a personalized ExtraBucks CTA** — if I'm signed in, show my balance ("You have $4 ExtraBucks — use by May 14"). If I'm not, show "Sign in to see your ExtraBucks." That's the hook that keeps weekly shoppers engaged.
- **Move the consent modal to a slim bottom banner** — a small "We use cookies — Privacy Policy" footer strip is standard and doesn't cannibalize the hero. The current full-screen block is the first impression; make it the last.
- **Surface one concrete Mother's Day offer in the hero strip** — even a simple "$5 off $25 in beauty" tied to Mother's Day would convert someone like me who's already in that mindset, gift-buying alongside a prescription pickup.
- **Show the full hero headline** — "Here for what ___" is doing work, but I can't read it. Even just letting that copy finish before the modal fires would help the brand land its emotional message.
