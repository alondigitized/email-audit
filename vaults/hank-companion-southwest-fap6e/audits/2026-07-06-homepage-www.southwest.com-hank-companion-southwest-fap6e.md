# Homepage snapshot · www.southwest.com · 2026-07-06
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Southwest is doing Southwest things — no pretense, no lifestyle fluff, just "here's a low fare, want it?" The "Celebrating 250 Years of Freedom" banner is a July 4th tie-in I respect, and leading with a $71 one-way from Burbank is exactly the kind of hook that makes me stop scrolling Instagram mid-doom-spiral. This is a brand that knows its people don't need mood boards, they need prices.

## What's working

- **The $71 price is huge and centered** — biggest visual element above the fold, exactly how I scan email subject lines, now applied to the homepage. I clocked it in under a second.
- **"Wanna land a low fare?" hero copy** — this is the Southwest voice I signed up for. Not corporate, not aspirational nonsense. Direct.
- **"First checked bag is free" in the banner strip** — that's my love language, literally. Seeing that unprompted before I even have to dig for it is a win.
- **"Book now" in unmissable yellow** — no ambiguity, no "Discover" or "Explore" nonsense. Yellow = action.
- **July 4th "250 Years of Freedom" tie-in** — seasonal, timely, and the patriotic angle plays well with the Southwest brand without being cringe. It's relevant today.
- **Tabbed booking interface (Flights / Vacations / Hotels / Cars / Cruises)** — visible at the bottom, exactly where my thumb lands. Everything I'd book in one row.

## What's weak

- **No Companion Pass callout anywhere** — I have a Companion Pass and Southwest knows it. This is the single most valuable thing I own as a Southwest flyer and the homepage acts like it doesn't exist. Where's my "Fly with your +1 free" hook?
- **Rapid Rewards balance: invisible** — I'm logged in or at least prompted to log in, but I have zero idea what my points balance is. Delta shows me my miles on the homepage. Southwest gives me a "Log in" button that doesn't hint at what's waiting for me.
- **Asterisk wall on the $71 price** — "\*Seats/days/mkts lmtd; restr./excl. apply. First travel date available with travel days in schedule." That's four lines of fine print crammed into the hero below the price. I see it, I clock it, I feel slightly manipulated. Just say "select routes" and move on.
- **Airplane-window night sky image** — it's moody but tells me nothing. No family, no destination, no beach. It's the screensaver of airline stock photography.
- **No specific route context** — "From Burbank, CA" is the departure, but where am I going for $71? No destination call-out makes the price feel less real.

## Recommendations

- **Surface Companion Pass status for logged-in/recognized users** — even a "Your +1 flies free" strip above the hero would be massive for retention and make me feel seen as a high-value customer.
- **Swap the night-sky airplane window for a destination lifestyle shot** — show a family at a beach, a couple at a gate, something that connects the price to a real trip. The current image is generic to every airline.
- **Collapse the asterisk block to "Select routes & dates"** — one line, tappable to expand. The four-line fine print right in the hero erodes trust on first impression; move it to a tooltip or footnote anchor.
- **Add a Rapid Rewards points-balance teaser to the "Log in" CTA** — something like "Log in to see your 42,000 points" pulls loyal members into the funnel instantly and creates urgency around redemption.
