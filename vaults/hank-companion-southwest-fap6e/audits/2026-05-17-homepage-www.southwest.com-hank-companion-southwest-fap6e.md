# Homepage snapshot · www.southwest.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Southwest is doing exactly what Southwest does — no runway models, no pretense, just price. The $69 one-way from Burbank dominates the screen before I've even scrolled, and "Wanna land a low fare?" feels like the airline read my group chat. That said, the page is talking to a *prospect*, not a Companion Pass holder who flew 40+ segments last year — and that gap is real.

## What's working

- **"Wanna land a low fare?" headline** — Southwest brand voice at its best. Casual, direct, not airline-speak. Lands on mobile before I've processed anything else.
- **"$69" in giant navy type** — the price IS the hero image. For me, that's correct. I clock the number before the brand. Good prioritization.
- **Yellow "Book now" button** — the only yellow CTA on a blue/white page. Perfect color separation. My thumb goes there automatically.
- **Rapid Rewards in the nav + 50k points banner** — I can see the loyalty ecosystem without hunting for it. Even if the banner is acquisition-flavored, the presence of Rapid Rewards above fold tells me my points matter here.
- **"NEW!" badge on the booking module** — small signal that the product is alive and maintained, not stale.
- **"Now accepting reservations through January 05, 2027"** — as a family planner who books holiday flights in January, knowing the planning horizon upfront is actually useful.

## What's weak

- **Zero Companion Pass mention** — this is Southwest's most powerful loyalty product and it's completely invisible. I have a CP. I want to know at a glance that my companion flies free. This is a massive missed hook for the most loyal tier of Southwest flyers.
- **No points-and-cash pricing** — "$69 or X,XXX points + taxes" is how I actually book, and it's nowhere. Southwest's biggest member differentiator is buried behind a tap.
- **50k points banner reads acquisition, not retention** — "Earn 50,000 points + first checked bag is free" is a credit card pitch. I'm already in the program. This line doesn't add value for me.
- **No change-fee callout** — Southwest's "no change fees, ever" is their biggest competitive weapon against United and Delta, and it's not on the screen. A family flyer like me would convert faster if I saw that.
- **Fine print right under the price** — "*Seats/days/mkts lmtd; restr./excl. apply" sitting immediately below "$69" deflates the excitement on the same breath. I get it, but the placement is rough.

## Recommendations

- **Add a one-line Companion Pass hook** — below or alongside the fare, something like "Have a Companion Pass? Your companion flies for taxes only on this fare." One sentence, no extra screen space, massive relevance for high-LTV members.
- **Show points pricing alongside the dollar price** — "One-way as low as $69 or 4,000 Rapid Rewards points" in the hero subline. This costs nothing to add and immediately signals the page was built for members, not just prospects.
- **Personalize the top banner for logged-in vs. logged-out** — logged-out gets the 50k acquisition pitch; logged-in gets "You have X,XXX points — here's what you can book." The banner real estate is wasted on me right now.
- **Add no-change-fee language to the CTA area** — a single line below "Book now" like "Plans change? No change fees, ever." would close the deal for a family flyer who's burned by every other airline.
