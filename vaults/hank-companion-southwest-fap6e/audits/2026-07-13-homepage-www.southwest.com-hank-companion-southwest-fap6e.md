# Homepage snapshot · www.southwest.com · 2026-07-13
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Southwest is doing what it does best — leading with price and skipping the nonsense. The "$71 one-way" headline lands immediately and the Rapid Rewards banner up top is exactly the kind of loyalty hook I actually care about. This feels like a brand that respects my time and knows I'm here to book a flight, not browse a lifestyle magazine.

## What's working

- **The $71 hero price** — I clock the number before I clock anything else. Southwest knows I'm skimming for a reason to tap.
- **"Earn 50,000 points + first checked bag is free" banner** — this is my language. Two free bags is literally a deciding factor when I'm comparing carriers, and seeing it before I've even hit the booking widget is smart.
- **"Wanna land a low fare?" headline** — casual, direct, no airline jargon. Sounds like someone texted me a deal, not a press release.
- **"Recent searches" widget** — the fact that it pre-populates my last search is exactly the kind of low-friction re-engagement that gets me to actually finish booking instead of closing the tab.
- **Vacations tab marked "NEW!"** — I notice this. Companion Pass + package deal is a combo I've literally googled before.

## What's weak

- **Zero lifestyle imagery** — just a blue-to-white gradient behind a price. I'm a family guy with a Companion Pass. Show me a couple at a gate, a kid with a carry-on, something. The visual flatness makes it feel like a utility app, not a travel brand.
- **Fine print is aggressive** — "*Seats/days/mkts lmtd; restr./excl. apply." right below the hero price is a lot of asterisk for a number that big. It immediately makes me wonder if $71 actually means anything.
- **No change/cancel policy call-out** — Southwest's no-change-fee policy is their biggest competitive differentiator over United and Delta, and it's not visible anywhere here. That's a huge missed conversion signal for someone like me who's on the fence about booking now vs. waiting.
- **No Companion Pass mention** — I have a Companion Pass. If the page knew that (or even hinted at member-specific pricing), I'd be way more likely to book right here instead of price-checking elsewhere first.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "flying with someone?" or Companion Pass callout** near the booking widget — even a generic "Rapid Rewards member? Your companion flies free." line would turn this from a generic booking page into a personal one.
- **Swap the blue gradient hero for a lifestyle photo** — a couple or a dad with a kid at a Southwest gate, overlaid with the price, would hold me 10x longer than a flat color field.
- **Surface the no-change-fee policy above the fold** — one line: "Change or cancel anytime. No fees. No drama." That sentence alone closes bookings for people like me who are waiting for certainty before committing.
- **Make the asterisk fine print a toggle/expandable link** rather than raw inline text — it undercuts the $71 hook every time someone reads it, and it's the kind of anti-pattern I expect from legacy carriers, not Southwest.
