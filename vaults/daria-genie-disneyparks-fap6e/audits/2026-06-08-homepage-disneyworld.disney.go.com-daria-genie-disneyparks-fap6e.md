# Homepage snapshot · disneyworld.disney.go.com · 2026-06-08
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- I open this and immediately recognize the brand I've been planning around for seven months — the Walt Disney World logo, the signature blue, the hero shot of a family absolutely losing it on what looks like a water ride. It's the classic "Most Magical Place On Earth" pitch. Right now the site is pushing hotel exploration and ticket sales as its two lead actions, which tracks for summer trip season. Feels like a broad welcome mat rather than anything built for someone like me who already has the vacation half-planned and just needs to layer in Genie+.

## What's working

- **Family hero photo** — the image of kids and adults screaming with joy on a water ride does real work. That's the reason I'm here. It confirms I'm in the right place within 0.3 seconds.
- **"Get Tickets" CTA** — clear, prominent, above the fold. Button copy is unambiguous. I know exactly what happens if I tap it.
- **"Explore Resort Hotels" CTA** — placed before "Get Tickets," which is actually smart for someone mid-planning who still needs to bundle a hotel stay. The icon (little building) reinforces the action.
- **"Build Your Vacation" quick link** — this is the fast lane for someone like me who doesn't want to navigate a full menu. One row, one tap, I'm in the planning flow.
- **"Today's Park Hours" quick link** — relevant signal that the site is live and current. If I'm already inside the park ecosystem, I want to see this is updated daily.
- **Clean mobile rendering** — no layout shift, no text collision, the CTA buttons breathe properly. High-priority for me since I'm always on my phone at 7 AM checking Lightning Lane availability.

## What's weak

- **Zero Genie+ or Lightning Lane surface** — this is the feature I check before I even have coffee. There is no trace of it on the visible screen. I have to already know where to look.
- **No returning-visitor recognition** — Disney has my purchase history, my MagicBand data, my park reservations. Showing me the exact same homepage as a first-time visitor is a missed opportunity. A "Plan Your Next Trip" or loyalty-tier callout would change my entire engagement posture.
- **No offer or bundle promo above the fold** — Disney frequently runs "buy X days get Y free" or Genie+ bundle pricing. None of that is surfaced here. Someone like me reads every ticket-bundle email they send, and I'd expect to see at least a hint of current pricing or savings.
- **"Welcome to The Most Magical Place On Earth" is evergreen to the point of being inert** — It's technically summer, peak season, the height of when families like mine are in decision mode. The hero copy doesn't lean into that at all. "Plan Your Summer Now" or even a summer-specific tagline would do more work.
- **No social proof on this screen** — no "X million guests" stat, no star ratings, no partner badges. For someone cross-shopping Universal, a trust signal here would reinforce the Disney choice.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a Genie+/Lightning Lane upsell in the quick-links row** — add a third row below "Today's Park Hours" that says "Lightning Lane Availability" or "Add Genie+" with the clock icon. That's my 7 AM ritual and Disney is leaving me to find it myself every single time.
- **Add a returning-member banner or personalization hook** — even a generic "Disney Rewards Member? Sign in to see exclusive rates" banner below the hero would signal that this site knows return visitors exist. Full personalization is a bigger lift; this version ships next week.
- **Swap the hero copy to a summer-specific headline** — something like "Make This Summer Unforgettable" with a summer-specific background (nighttime fireworks, a July crowd shot) acknowledges the moment. The current tagline doesn't expire and that's exactly the problem.
- **Add a ticket bundle callout strip** — a thin promo strip between the hero CTAs and the quick-links saying "Multi-Day Tickets from $X — Build Your Bundle" would give me a reason to tap before I even decide on hotels. Concrete price anchor, no asterisk theater.
