# Homepage snapshot · www.glossier.com · 2026-07-02
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- Okay they literally put Boy Brow front and center and I felt that. This is Glossier being Glossier — no noise, no clutter, just the product that built the brand on a dark background with those iconic brush wands lined up like a lineup photo. Right now they're pushing their OG hero product and they're doing it with the same clean, confident energy the brand was built on.

## What's working

- **The Boy Brow hero itself** — they zoomed all the way in on the brush wands in every shade and it's genuinely beautiful. I can identify my exact shade (Brown) in that lineup. That's product porn done right.
- **"The original boyish, brushed-up brow."** — this copy does the job in six words. No adjective abuse, no "elevate your beauty routine" nonsense. It's confident because it's true.
- **Free shipping banner** — sticky at the top, not buried in a footer. $40 is easy to hit with one or two products, so it's actually useful, not a fake carrot.
- **"Shop Boy Brow" CTA** — unambiguous. Not "Discover" or "Explore." It says what you're going to do when you tap it.

## What's weak

- **Zero social proof above the fold** — Boy Brow has a cult following and I can't see a single star rating or review count. This is the easiest trust signal in e-comm and it's missing.
- **No "new colors" or "new formula" hook** — if there's anything fresh about Boy Brow I need to know, this hero doesn't hint at it. For an OG like me who already owns multiple shades, there's no reason to add to cart if I think I know what I have.
- **No people** — the brushes are gorgeous but there's no face, no brow moment, no real-person "after" to make me feel the product on skin. Glossier was built on casting real people and this is all product macro.
- **No loyalty hook anywhere** — I've spent a non-embarrassing amount of money here. A rewards balance or member callout would make me feel seen instead of just like a new-visitor transaction.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "New in Boy Brow" or "Limited Shades" badge to the hero** — even if the formula is the same, a scarcity or newness signal gives an existing customer a reason to click that an OG doesn't already have.
- **Drop a real face into the hero** — one lifestyle shot of a model with an actually good brow moment, styled to look effortless and Gen Z. The brush macro is striking but it doesn't close the "will it look like that on me" gap.
- **Surface star ratings on the hero card** — "400,000+ reviews" or a star line under the product name costs nothing to add and adds a ton of credibility for anyone who landed here cold.
- **Add a member/loyalty callout to the sticky banner** — rotate it alongside the free shipping message: "Glossier members earn points on every order." One line, five words, makes returning customers feel something.
