# adidas Anthony Edwards 3: Believe That
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, cinematic single-product launch email — the Anthony Edwards 3 signature basketball shoe — and it's a strong step up in production value from most of the recent adidas sends. The hero shoe shot on the glowing pink backdrop and the moody Ant Edwards portrait both look like real campaign photography, not stock lifestyle shots, and the "7th season, 3rd shoe, same goal" preheader gives real signature-athlete lineage that a sneakerhead persona would recognize instantly.
- Where it falls short is the same place nearly every recent adidas email falls short: no price, no release date, and a generic top banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") that undercuts the single-hero focus by offering an escape hatch before the reader even reaches the product. For a signature athlete drop, price and drop-date are the two pieces of information a serious buyer needs, and neither is visible anywhere in the render.
- Verdict: strong creative, weak commerce information — this will get opens and admiring scrolls from Ant Edwards fans but will lose people who want to know if it's in stock and what it costs before clicking.

## What's working

- Two full-bleed, high-production images (studio shoe shot, athlete portrait) carry real campaign weight, not template filler.
- "BUILT FOR ANT'S GAME AND TO MAKE YOURS EVEN BETTER. BELIEVE THAT." is a distinctive, on-brand headline that ties directly to the subject line.
- Three separate SHOP NOW CTAs (top banner, mid-page, bottom) give multiple clear click points as you scroll.
- Copy calls out specific tech (Hyperboost cushioning, low-cut design) rather than vague marketing fluff.
- No render bugs — text is legible, images loaded fully, layout is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere in the email — a signature shoe drop should lead with or near the price point.
- No release date or "available now" / "coming soon" signal, so urgency is completely absent.
- Top banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") competes with and dilutes the single-product story instead of reinforcing it.
- No social proof, sizing/fit info, or reviews — nothing to reduce purchase friction for a $100+ performance shoe.
- Model/product image alone can't confirm colorway options are available; no swatches or "shop the colorway" secondary CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price and availability status directly under the "SHOP NOW" button in the first product block — this is the single biggest missing piece of commerce information.
- 2. Cut or replace the top "best sellers" banner with something reinforcing the Ant Edwards 3 drop (e.g., "New: Anthony Edwards 3 — Shop the Drop") so the email stays single-hero from the first pixel.
- 3. Add a release-date or "Available Now" urgency line near the hero shot.
- 4. Add a small colorway strip (2-3 thumbnail swatches) beneath the hero image if multiple colorways exist.
- 5. Add one line of athlete credibility/social proof (e.g., "Worn by Anthony Edwards this season") near the portrait image to reinforce authenticity.
