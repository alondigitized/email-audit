# adidas 5.10's Sticky Secret
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure brand heritage email — 40 years of STEALTH rubber, told through the origin story of Five Ten. There is no product shot, no price, no offer, no urgency. The writing is genuinely good — "the like you weren't sure you had in you" earns its place — and the climbing hero image sets the right mood. But as a marketing email, it asks you to care about a backstory before it shows you anything to buy.
- Compared to the rest of this adidas sequence, this is a jarring pivot. Four earlier sends were either adiClub onboarding or a high-energy F1 collab. Those had clear briefs. This one feels like it got dropped into the same cadence by accident — it belongs in a content hub, not send #5 in 7 days. A subscriber who just got two emails on May 20 (app push + F1 launch) is already primed to mentally unsubscribe.
- Five Ten's STEALTH story is genuinely interesting to climbers and mountain bikers, but it's a niche sub-audience within adidas's general list. Without segmentation proof visible in the email, this reads like a broadcast to everyone — and for anyone who isn't a climber or MTB rider, it's dead weight.

## What's working

- **Subject line is a standout hook.** "Sticky Secret" is a rare case of wordplay that actually earns the click — tactile, intriguing, and on-brand.
- **Copy is confident and product-informed.** The origin story (5.10 grade → brand name → STEALTH rubber → pedals) flows cleanly without overselling.
- **Clean render, strong visual hierarchy.** Hero image → headline → body → CTA — the eye follows a single path down.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is unambiguous** even if generic — visible, well-contrasted black button.

## What's weak

- **No product visible anywhere.** This is a shoe brand. Not a single shoe is shown. A climber or MTB rider reading this has no idea what they'd even be shopping for.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible CTA for this brief.** After a 40-year heritage story, "SHOP NOW" is a total tonal collapse. It deserved "Shop Five Ten" at minimum.
- **No offer, no urgency.** Heritage storytelling can work, but it needs a release hook — 40th anniversary drop, limited colorway, anything. There's nothing here.
- **Fifth email in seven days.** adidas sent two emails on May 20 alone. This cadence will show up in unsubscribes. The onboarding sequence needed a cooldown before going editorial.
- **No loyalty/adiClub tie-in.** The last three emails trained this subscriber to expect points and rewards. Dropping that completely with no transition feels like a different brand is talking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product and a hook.** Show the actual Five Ten shoe. If this is a 40th anniversary moment for STEALTH, say so and link to a collection or limited drop — not a generic shop page.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA** to close the story loop: "Shop Five Ten" or "Find Your Grip" — something that connects back to the narrative.
- 3. **Give the heritage story a commercial reason to exist.** "40 years of STEALTH. Now in a new colorway." One sentence. That's all it takes.
- 4. **Hold this email one day** — sending the day after two May 20 emails guarantees low engagement and accelerates list fatigue.
- 5. **Tie back to adiClub** if this audience is in the onboarding window: "STEALTH shoes earn double points this month" would have connected the narrative to the member program that preceded it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Five Ten's STEALTH rubber: 40 years of grip`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The rubber adidas climbers swear by — 40 years on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Born in Yosemite in 1985. Still the standard for climbers and riders.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `How one rubber compound changed rock climbing — and what you can grab now.`

## Technical Audit — adidas "5.10's Sticky Secret"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken click-tracking redirect and a missing plain-text body. Several warnings around HTTP mixed content, missing image alt text, and unverifiable compliance headers due to relay capture gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — malformed relative URL in click target**
The QA probe resolved this click URL:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/90081c8a-bc12-4b1b-969b-0a20464be11f?cp_tp=v3...
```
…to a relative path `/g/90081c8a-bc12-4b1b-969b-0a20464be11f?txn=ba14ed00-550f-11f1-9519-4e61e507eb6e`, which is an `unknown url type` — the redirect chain resolves to a non-absolute URL and will produce a broken destination for recipients. This is a sending-platform (dv.adidas.com) issue, not an HTML authoring issue, but the send should not have gone out with this unresolved.

**[WARN] 17 tracking links unverifiable**
All click links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` (redirect domain). Link integrity and UTM payloads on those 17 links cannot be confirmed from the outside.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
Mixed-content images will be blocked silently in most modern email clients with HTTPS-only rendering (Gmail, Apple Mail):
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e4a6a7559-d9e3d0f6-3ee7-4aa5-8c67-42a1e5c83387-000000/FfxKmWd_4w-...
```
Both appear to be the same arrow asset served twice — the second is a tracking pixel wrapped in an `<img>`. Both domains support HTTPS; the `http://` scheme is incorrect.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**
- `90081c8a-bc12-4b1b-969b-0a20464be11f` (dv.adidas.com dynamic image)
- `FfxKmWd_4w-...` (click.comms.adidas.com tracking pixel)

The dynamic image missing alt is the higher-priority fix — if it's a product shot or hero, image-blocking clients render a blank.

**[INFO] Custom fonts never load**
Both `@font-face` blocks for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` reference only `local()` sources pointing to Arial/Helvetica/Calibri. No remote font URL is present. The declarations do not load brand fonts — they alias the custom family name to system fonts. This is a deliberate email-safe pattern, but confirms brand fonts are never rendered. `font-display: swap` has no effect in email clients and is dead code here.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` and `.hideMobile` declarations**
The main `<style>` block and a second block labeled "For Yahoo Android App" declare identical `@font-face` rules. `.hideMobile` is also declared twice inside the `max-width: 639px` media query. No functional breakage, but adds ~1KB of dead CSS.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` not captured**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If it is absent from the actual SMTP envelope, Gmail and Apple Mail will not display native unsubscribe UI, and Google's bulk sender requirements (>5k/day) treat its absence as a compliance gap.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` missing (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is required for Gmail bulk senders. Without this header, Gmail's "Unsubscribe" link routes to the landing page rather than firing a POST — increasing unsubscribe friction and risking spam classification.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via this relay. The sending domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com` — authentication records should be confirmed directly for that subdomain.

**[INFO] `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` present — correct.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` strips attribution**
This head tag instructs the browser to send no `Referer` header on click-throughs. Any analytics relying on HTTP referrer (rather than UTM params embedded in the link) will attribute sessions as direct traffic. This is standard email practice only if UTM params are consistently applied to all links — which cannot be confirmed since the 17 tracked links are behind a redirect and were not probed.

**[FAIL] Broken redirect (see §2) breaks landing page delivery for at least one link.**
