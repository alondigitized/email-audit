# Unlock more rewards with the adidas app
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the third adidas email in five days — the onboarding sequence has moved from Welcome (May 15) to Points education (May 17) to App download today. The brief is clear: get the new adiClub member to install the app. To sweeten that ask, adidas has stapled a 15% voucher to the send. That's a smart bribe, but it creates a structural problem: the email is now doing two jobs at once, and neither brief fully commands the send.
- The app download section is the stated hero. The 15% voucher section below it is arguably more compelling — a concrete, time-bounded, personal offer — and it threatens to swallow the app narrative. A subscriber who scrolls straight to the coupon code, redeems it online, and never downloads the app is the likely outcome here.
- The execution is clean. No render bugs, adidas branding is tight, and the Spotify partner offer adds a tangible third-party proof point for why the app is worth something. But the dual-brief structure prevents this from rising to the level of the May 15 welcome email. It's competent, not excellent.

## What's working

- **15% voucher is genuinely strong:** Specific percentage off, a real expiry (June 03, 2026), and a visible promo code — the offer passes the smell test immediately.
- **Spotify partner offer adds credibility:** "3 months of Spotify" in the phone mockup gives the app a concrete benefit beyond the adidas ecosystem, reducing friction for skeptical subscribers.
- **App download CTAs are clear:** App Store and Google Play buttons are immediately legible; "GET THE APP" button follows. No ambiguity about what adidas wants.
- **50-point incentive is specific:** The bullet calling out 50 points for downloading is better than generic "earn more" language — it ties directly to the adiClub mental model built in the May 17 send.
- **Voucher section is visually distinct:** The "YOUR ADICLUB VOUCHER" treatment with the coral "REDEEM ONLINE" button separates it cleanly from the app section.

## What's weak

- **Two briefs, one email:** Download the app OR redeem 15% off — a subscriber can do the second without doing the first. The voucher undercuts the conversion goal.
- **No urgency on the app download:** The 15% voucher has a hard June 03 deadline, but the app download CTA has zero time pressure. The incentive and the urgency are attached to the wrong offer.
- **Third email in five days is starting to crowd:** This cadence (May 15, 17, 20) is acceptable for an onboarding sequence but is bumping against heavy territory. One more send this week would feel pushy.
- **Subject carries no offer signal:** "Unlock more rewards with the adidas app" describes the email but omits the 15% voucher — the single most clickable thing inside. That's a missed open opportunity.
- **Product recommendations are generic:** "SHOP SHOES / SHOP ACCESSORIES / SHOP CLOTHING / SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" are category links, not personalized picks. This section adds length without adding pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject line with the voucher, not the app.** The 15% off is the hook; the app is the channel. Swap order of logic — get them to open with the offer, then explain the app delivers it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off is waiting — get the adidas app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off inside + 3 months Spotify free`
- 2. **Gate the voucher on the app download.** If the business goal is app installs, require the code to be redeemed inside the app, not "online checkout or in-store." This turns the voucher into fuel for the conversion rather than a parallel exit ramp.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Download the app, unlock 15% off — valid through June 3`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50 bonus points + 15% off when you get the app today`
- 3. **Add time urgency to the app CTA.** Move the June 03 expiry date up into the app download section — "Download by June 3 to unlock your voucher" — so the deadline creates pressure on the primary brief, not just the secondary one.
- 4. **Cut or defer the product recommendations.** The four category links at the bottom dilute focus and add scroll length without adding conversion value. Save the "RECOMMENDED FOR YOU" module for a dedicated product email once the app is installed.
- 5. **Trim the bullet list from three items to one.** "Get 50 points for downloading" is the most concrete benefit; the other two bullets (notifications, early access) are vague. One strong specific beats three soft generics.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not detected**
The relay did not surface this header. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders; absence risks automatic placement in spam or promotions with no one-click unsubscribe UI.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not detected**
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy (>5k/day). Without this header, one-click unsubscribe is non-functional even if the base header is present.

**`Authentication-Results` — not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm the sending domain `us-news.adidas.com` passes alignment checks. Should be verified at the MTA level.

**Plain-text body — 84% URLs**
9,919 of 11,813 plain-text characters are URLs. Spam filters (particularly Barracuda, SpamAssassin) penalize high URL density in the plain-text part. The plain-text alternative appears to be an auto-generated URL dump rather than a human-readable version.
