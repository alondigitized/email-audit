# First chance to dive into 30% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's summer clearance opener — a 30% off sitewide promotion anchored by the seasonal "SUMMER SAVINGS" hero with code SAVE. The offer is meaningfully better than the $30 off $100+ deal that ran twice in the past two weeks (June 18 and again on June 27), and a percentage format is easier for subscribers to internalize. The structural problem is the same one haunting the last several adidas sends: this is the sixth promotional email in fourteen days, and the audience has been conditioned to expect a discount regardless. "First chance" implies scarcity that the send history immediately deflates.
- The product grid anchors on four entry-level running shoes ($54–$63) — a mismatch with the lifestyle/street-style hero, which shows a casual male model who reads athleisure, not performance runner. Is this a runner's sale or a lifestyle sale? The CTAs don't answer it: SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS is the bluntest possible segmentation.
- Clear offer, clean layout, correct seasonal timing — but the cadence fatigue and abrupt creative gear-shift away from the US94 heritage arc (June 20, June 27) make this read as generic promo mode just as the brand was building thematic momentum.

## What's working

- **Offer is front-loaded and unmissable.** "30% OFF IS LIVE" lands immediately below the hero — no scrolling required to understand the deal.
- **Code SAVE is explicit in the preheader** — a subscriber scanning the inbox gets the action item without opening.
- **adiClub member pricing appears on all four product tiles** — the loyalty layer is reinforced at the product level.
- **Clean seasonal hero** — the SUMMER SAVINGS graphic is polished and seasonally correct for a June 30 send.
- **Gender-segmented CTAs** — SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS prevents landing subscribers in the wrong product feed.

## What's weak

- **Creative whiplash after the US94 arc.** The June 20 and June 27 sends were building a cultural narrative around FIFA/US94. This email drops it entirely for a generic summer sale — a reset, not a progression.
- **"First chance" urgency has no deadline in the body.** The subject implies scarcity; the email doesn't back it up with an end date or countdown. The offer just floats.
- **Running shoes don't match the lifestyle hero.** The hero sets up casual athleisure; four entry-level running shoe tiles break that expectation.
- **Three equal-weight CTAs dilute the primary action.** A decision fork between MEN / WOMEN / KIDS delays conversion rather than directing it.
- **Fine print is long and visually prominent** — the disclaimer block below the fold undermines the simplicity of the "30% off" pitch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an end date.** "Ends July 4" or "48 hours only" — anything that makes "first chance" land. Without it, the urgency claim is empty noise.
- 2. **Pick a lane: lifestyle or performance.** Street hero → lifestyle product grid (Samba, Gazelle, Superstar). Runner hero → running shoe grid. The current pairing confuses both.
- 3. **Collapse three CTAs to one hero CTA** ("SHOP THE SALE") and let gender segmentation happen inside the product tiles — that's what they're there for.
- 4. **Extend the US94 thread.** Three weeks of FIFA/US94 equity were built; a "US94 styles — now 30% off" frame would land as a payoff rather than an interruption.
- 5. **Rewrite the preheader.** "Exclusions apply" as the closer on the preview text is a conversion-killer at the inbox level.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is adidas's summer clearance opener — a 30% off sitewide promotion anchored by the seasonal "SUMMER SAVINGS" hero with code SAVE. The offer is meaningfully better than the $30 off $100+ deal that ran twice in the past two weeks (June 18 and again on June 27), and a percentage format is easier for subscribers to internalize. The structural problem is the same one haunting the last several adidas sends: this is the sixth promotional email in fourteen days, and the audience has been conditioned to expect a discount regardless. "First chance" implies scarcity that the send history immediately deflates.

The product grid anchors on four entry-level running shoes ($54–$63) — a mismatch with the lifestyle/street-style hero, which shows a casual male model who reads athleisure, not performance runner. Is this a runner's sale or a lifestyle sale? The CTAs don't answer it: SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS is the bluntest possible segmentation.

Clear offer, clean layout, correct seasonal timing — but the cadence fatigue and abrupt creative gear-shift away from the US94 heritage arc (June 20, June 27) make this read as generic promo mode just as the brand was building thematic momentum.

- **Clean seasonal hero** — the SUMMER SAVINGS graphic is polished and seasonally correct for a June 30 send.
- **Gender-segmented CTAs** — SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS prevents landing subscribers in the wrong product feed.

## 3. What didn't

- **Creative whiplash after the US94 arc.** The June 20 and June 27 sends were building a cultural narrative around FIFA/US94. This email drops it entirely for a generic summer sale — a reset, not a progression.
- **"First chance" urgency has no deadline in the body.** The subject implies scarcity; the email doesn't back it up with an end date or countdown. The offer just floats.
- **Running shoes don't match the lifestyle hero.** The hero sets up casual athleisure; four entry-level running shoe tiles break that expectation.

4. **Extend the US94 thread.** Three weeks of FIFA/US94 equity were built; a "US94 styles — now 30% off" frame would land as a payoff rather than an interruption.
5. **Rewrite the preheader.** "Exclusions apply" as the closer on the preview text is a conversion-killer at the inbox level.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing**
The text/plain MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly require a plain-text part, but its absence triggers aggressive spam filtering in many MTAs (SpamAssassin, Postfix-based ISP filters). It also makes the email inaccessible to text-only clients and some assistive technologies.
