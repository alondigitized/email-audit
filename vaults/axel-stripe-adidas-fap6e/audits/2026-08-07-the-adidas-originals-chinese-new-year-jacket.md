# The adidas Originals Chinese New Year Jacket
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This send tries to do two jobs at once and neither lands cleanly. The top two-thirds is a legitimate product story — the adidas Originals "Chinese New Year Jacket," a maroon quilted piece with the trefoil and 3-Stripes detailing, shot on a single model with clean "ORIGINALS" branding — and it's genuinely the best-looking creative in this brand's recent batch. Then, with zero transition, the email pivots into "THE BACK TO SCHOOL SALE" with a totally different photo set (three teens with a bike) and a 40%-off offer that has nothing to do with the jacket above it.
- The bigger problem is timing, not layout: today is August 6, and Chinese New Year fell in February. Leading a subject line and a full hero section with "Chinese New Year Jacket" content in the middle of back-to-school season reads like a stale or misfiled campaign — nothing in the copy explains why this product is relevant right now. That single issue undercuts an otherwise well-shot hero.
- This is the same "two campaigns bolted together" problem flagged in the 2026-08-03 Care Bears send (6.00/10) — adidas keeps stapling an unrelated sale panel onto product-launch creative instead of trusting one message per email. Net: nice photography, confused campaign logic, no price or size info on the actual product. Middling send.

## What's working

- The hero jacket shot is the strongest creative asset in recent adidas sends — clean composition, product clearly visible, trefoil logo reinforces "Originals."
- Two well-differentiated CTAs on the jacket section (SHOP NOW / FIND A STORE) give both online and in-store shoppers a clear next step.
- The Back to School section has a real, quantified offer ("up to 40% off") plus a specific adiClub loyalty perk (spend $130+, get a free month of Apple TV).
- No visual bugs — text, images, and buttons all render cleanly with no overlap or broken assets.

## What's weak

- Leading with "Chinese New Year Jacket" in early August is a seasonal mismatch — Lunar New Year was six months ago, and nothing in the copy justifies the timing.
- No price is shown anywhere for the actual hero product (the jacket) — a shopper who clicks in has to leave the email to find out what it costs.
- The email is really two unrelated campaigns stitched together (jacket launch + BTS sale) with no copy bridge between them, diluting the single message a subject line promised.
- Three separate "SHOP NOW" buttons pointing at two different offers weaken the primary CTA — there's no single obvious next action.
- No social proof, reviews, or fit/sizing detail for the jacket beyond "loose, relaxed fit."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split into two sends.** The jacket launch and the Back to School 40%-off sale are both strong enough to stand alone — combining them just cannibalizes the jacket story attention and confuses the subject-line promise.
- 2. **Fix the seasonal framing or drop it.** Either recontextualize the jacket ("year-round Originals heritage piece" rather than "Chinese New Year Jacket") or hold this creative until the actual CNY window in January/February.
- 3. **Add a price under the hero CTA.** A shopper interested enough to open a product-launch email shouldn't have to click through just to see cost.
- 4. **Collapse to one CTA per section** so "SHOP NOW" isn't repeated three times pointing at different things.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: the Originals heritage jacket, back in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3-Stripes, maroon, made in Shanghai — meet the new Originals jacket`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited colorway, available online and in select stores.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The jacket that started as a Shanghai design study.`
