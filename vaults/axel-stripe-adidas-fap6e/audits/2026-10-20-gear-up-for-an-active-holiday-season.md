# Gear up for an active holiday season
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a generic, offer-free holiday gifting send from adidas. It has two blocks: a "Mix and Match" lifestyle hero with three category buttons, and a gift card push. The photography is clean and on-brand. The lifestyle shot of two people at a bus stop in fleece and sneakers is believable and quiet, and the red gift card shot is a strong holiday signal.
- The email has no reason to click now. It shows no discount, no named product and no deadline. "Gear up early" is soft. The black bar at the top ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers / SHOP NOW") is a third pitch competing with the two below it. The Men/Women/Kids buttons all go to broad category pages. After the Messi (Oct 19) and low-profile sneaker (Oct 18) sends, which each had a single idea, this feels like filler.
- Verdict: tidy and safe, but low-energy. It reads as a seasonal placeholder rather than a campaign. It works as a brand touchpoint and not as a conversion driver.

## What's working

- The lifestyle hero is cohesive and on-brand. The olive and black styling suits the fleece-and-sneakers message.
- The red gift card image is bold and seasonal. It is the most arresting visual in the email.
- Buttons are consistent, high-contrast black with arrows, and clearly tappable.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, with clear headlines and short copy, so it scans quickly.
- The headers "MIX AND MATCH" and "ONE GIFT, ENDLESS OPTIONS" are readable and give a clear two-beat structure.

## What's weak

- No offer, price, percentage or member benefit appears anywhere. Nothing creates urgency.
- The top-of-email preheader text appears as a small underlined line, "Explore endless gifting possibilities.", at the very top of the render. It looks like a stray link.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner has a "SHOP NOW" button that duplicates the other CTAs. It adds a competing path before the hero.
- Three stacked Shop Men/Women/Kids buttons are generic and take up vertical space. No specific product is shown.
- The hero image does not show any product detail such as a named fleece or sneaker model.
- No reviews, loyalty or adiClub mention, and no gift card denominations or delivery detail.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook. Even a simple "Free shipping on gifts" or an adiClub member perk would give the send a reason to click. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. Cut the top black best-sellers banner, or turn it into the one primary CTA. Three CTA tiers is too many.
- 3. Replace the three category buttons with two or three named hero products (a specific fleece, a specific sneaker) with a price, or a "Shop gifts under $100" link.
- 4. For the gift card block, add detail: amounts, instant email delivery, "no expiry". That makes the pitch to a last-minute shopper concrete.
- 5. Fix the preheader so it is hidden from the visible body and complements the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fleece, sneakers, gift cards: your holiday list`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gift ideas they'll actually wear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fleece and sneakers for everyone, plus instant e-gift cards.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop men's, women's and kids' picks before the rush.`

Structurally sound table-based HTML with responsive media queries. The QA run found one hard failure (no plain-text part) and several mixed-content image sources. The HTML was truncated and the relay stripped the headers, so compliance and landing-page checks are inconclusive.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

Inconclusive. All links are wrapped in `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, and the probe skipped them. No UTM parameters were verifiable and no landing pages were resolved. The `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` tag also suppresses referrer data on outbound clicks, so attribution depends on the redirector's parameters.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Add a plain-text MIME alternative, which resolves the one hard FAIL.
2. Serve `image.link.adidas.com` assets and the tracking pixel over HTTPS.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to the `dv.adidas.com` hero image. Use `alt=""` on the tracking pixel.
4. Change the viewport meta to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.
5. Re-run the audit against the raw `.eml` to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
6. Follow the redirect chains with a resolver that supports tracking domains, to confirm landing pages and UTM parameters.
7. Re-run on the full, untruncated HTML to check footer compliance elements and remaining merge tokens.
