# The icon just got a makeover
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a two-product bolt-on dressed up as a single-story email, and the join is visible. The top half — "Samba Goes Metallic" — is a genuine product story with a hero shot that actually sells the shoe: light-blue metallic Samba, clean flat-lay, trefoil watermark, decent copy. Then it hard-cuts into a second, unrelated pitch: the Back to School Sale at up to 40% off, with its own hero image and its own CTA. Two campaigns stapled together under a subject line ("The icon just got a makeover") that only describes the first half.
- Compare this to 2026-07-30's "Up to 40% off for back to school" — that email led with the sale as the whole story and scored 6/10 for having a clean single-minded hero. Here, the sale is buried as act two behind a shoe launch, so neither story gets full attention, and a shopper drawn in by the subject line lands on Samba imagery, not the discount.
- The metallic Samba concept itself is a legitimate, well-photographed product idea, and the top nav bar ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") plus the back-to-school hero are both clean and on-brand. But this reads as two half-emails glued together, and the subject/preview only sells one of them. **5.5/10 territory — decent individual assets, poor single-email cohesion.**

## What's working

- The metallic Samba hero shot is well-lit and product-forward — no ambiguity about what's being sold.
- Clear, high-contrast black "SHOP NOW" buttons appear three times, so there's no shortage of CTAs.
- The Back to School hero (three friends with a bike, basketball, backpack) feels current and lifestyle-relevant, distinct from July's locker-themed sale image.
- "Save up to 40% off" section includes a concrete secondary incentive (free month of Apple TV with adiClub + $130 spend) that adds real value for loyalty members.
- Layout is clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem-ipsum.

## What's weak

- Subject line ("The icon just got a makeover") promises one story (Samba) but the email delivers two unrelated campaigns — bait-and-switch on cadence.
- No visible price, no percentage off, no color/material name for the Samba itself in the hero section — "sleek metallic look" is vague where a shopper wants a spec.
- The preheader ("Explore the Samba that gets more unique with every wear") is oddly poetic and doesn't mention the sale at all, wasting the one line that could've teased 40% off.
- Visual hierarchy is split down the middle — a shopper has to scroll past an entire product story before reaching the actual discount, which is the stronger hook.
- This is the second Samba-adjacent send this window (following the "Chinese New Year Jacket" and Care Bears collab emails) without a strong throughline — feels like grab-bag inventory, not a coherent campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split into two emails or lead with the sale.** If 40% off is live through August 13, that's the stronger open/click driver — put it first, with Samba metallic as a secondary "also new" module, or send it as a standalone drop separate from the sale blast.
- 2. **Add a spec line to the Samba section** — colorway name, "$XX" or "new colorway," something concrete instead of "sleek metallic look."
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to reference the sale**, since the subject already covers Samba — don't waste both lines on the same idea.
- 4. **Tighten the subject to match content**, or make it a true dual-subject that signals both stories exist.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Metallic Samba is here + up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Metallic Samba drops today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, save up to 40% for back to school.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the new colorway before it sells out.`
- 5. **Surface the Apple TV incentive higher** — a free month of Apple TV for adiClub members is a differentiated hook buried in body copy under a generic sale header.
