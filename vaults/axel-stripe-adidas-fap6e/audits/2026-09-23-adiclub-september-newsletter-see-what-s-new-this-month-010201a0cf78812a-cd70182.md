# adiClub September Newsletter: See what’s new this month
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is adiClub's monthly newsletter roundup, and it's an entirely different animal from the single-product launches adidas has been sending all cycle (the 2026-09-23 Adios Pro 5 launch, the 2026-09-18 Anthony Edwards 3 drop) — no discount, no sale, just a stack of five giveaways/entries: Shoe of the Month, Tennessee Athletics Legends, signed footballs, a hybrid training kit, and a Strava account-connect push. It reads as a loyalty-engagement email, not a sales email, and it's honest about that from the top.
- The execution is competent but visually flat — every module is the same text-block-plus-photo pattern repeated five times with no hierarchy telling the eye where to look first. There's no discernible reason for a general subscriber to open this over any other adidas send this week: no offer, no urgency, no product to buy, just contest entries that are mostly niche (Tennessee college sports, signed footballs) or non-purchase actions (link your Strava). Worth sending to loyalty members for engagement, but a low performer if judged as a driver of purchase intent.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent typographic treatment (all-caps black headlines) keeps the newsletter legible despite five distinct modules.
- The adiClub/Strava crossover banner is the most visually striking asset in the email — a genuine action shot with clear dual-branding.
- Each module has its own unambiguous "ENTER NOW" or "CONNECT ACCOUNTS" CTA, so there's no confusion about what to do next.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.
- The top utility bar ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers → Shop Now") gives at least one clear commerce path for anyone not interested in the giveaways.

## What's weak

- No hero hierarchy — the Shoe of the Month giveaway, Tennessee giveaway, football giveaway, and hybrid kit giveaway are all visually equal weight, so nothing tells the eye where to look first.
- Zero purchase incentive anywhere in the body — this is five contest entries and one account-linking ask, with no discount, sale, or shoppable product.
- Heavy reliance on entrants' geographic/team affinity (Tennessee Vols) and niche sport (signed rookie footballs) narrows relevance for a broad subscriber base.
- The email is long (five stacked modules) with no way to skip to the one giveaway a reader might actually care about — no jump-links or table of contents.
- "The adiClub September Newsletter" framing in the subject undersells what's actually inside; nothing in the subject signals "giveaways" or "win," the actual content driver.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the single most broadly appealing giveaway (Shoe of the Month) as a true hero with a bigger, more distinct visual treatment, and demote the niche ones (Tennessee, footballs) below a fold or into a smaller "more ways to win" strip.
- 2. Add a subject/preheader that names the incentive ("win," "giveaway," specific prize) instead of the generic "newsletter" framing — subscribers don't open loyalty digests without knowing what's in it for them.
- 3. Insert at least one shoppable/discount module — even a "20% off for adiClub members" band — since a newsletter with zero purchase path this month breaks cadence with a brand that's been sale-heavy (per the 2026-09-19 "Up to 50% off" send).
- 4. Add a jump-nav or numbered list at the top ("5 ways to win this month") so mobile readers can see the full scope without endless scrolling.
- 5. Tighten the Strava CTA copy — "Connect Accounts" is generic; "Link Strava, Earn Points" is more specific and mirrors the specificity used elsewhere.
