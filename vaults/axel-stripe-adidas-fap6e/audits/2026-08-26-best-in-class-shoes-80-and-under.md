# Best-in-class shoes $80 and under
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, price-anchored back-to-school email — three sneaker styles (VL Court, Country, Samba/Gazelle-style suede) shot on concrete steps, with a hard $80-and-under price ceiling and gendered CTAs. It's more product-forward than 2026-08-21's "up to 40% off" texture-only sale email, but it's a step down from 2026-08-23's Samba back-to-school send and well below 2026-08-25's Billie Jean King hero — no athlete, no named silhouette, just generic "school shoes" framing over three different models.
- The $80-and-under price ceiling is the most concrete, honest offer signal in this whole run — it beats vague percentage-off language because the shopper knows exactly what they're getting before they click. But the email undercuts its own good idea by never naming the shoes or showing individual prices, so the "shop for value" promise isn't backed up until you click through.
- Verdict: functional, honestly-priced, but generic — a mid-tier send that will convert on impulse trust in the adidas brand rather than on anything specific in this email.

## What's working

- Clear, unambiguous price framing ("$80 AND UNDER") gives a concrete reason to click, unlike the vague "up to 40%" language from 2026-08-21.
- Hero image is well-lit, in-focus, and shows three distinct silhouettes (low-top leather, striped runner, red suede) so there's something for different tastes.
- Gendered dual-CTA ("SHOP MEN" / "SHOP WOMEN") reduces friction for a shopper who doesn't want to browse a unisex catalog.
- Clean visual hierarchy: banner → hero image → headline → CTAs, no clutter or competing offers.

## What's weak

- No individual product names or prices shown — three different shoes are pictured but not identified, so the “$80 and under” claim isn't backed by visible proof.
- No back-to-school specific styling cues (backpacks, kids, classroom setting) despite the "school shoes" headline — the steps/concrete setting reads generic, same visual register as any other lifestyle shot.
- Top-of-email "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner competes with and dilutes the main $80-and-under message instead of reinforcing it.
- No urgency or deadline — nothing tells the shopper why to act now versus next week.
- No kids'/youth CTA despite back-to-school framing, which is a miss if kids' sizing is part of this promo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or "from $XX" tag directly on or under each shoe in the hero image — this single change would make the $80-and-under claim tangible instead of asserted.
- 2. Drop or reword the top banner so it doesn't compete with the main offer — either remove it or make it echo "$80 and under" instead of generic "best sellers."
- 3. Add a "SHOP KIDS" CTA alongside Men/Women if back-to-school stock includes youth sizing — as written, the headline promises school shoes but only offers adult categories.
- 4. Name at least one hero silhouette (e.g., "Samba, VL Court, Gazelle — all $80 and under") to give the shopper a reason to search for it directly.
