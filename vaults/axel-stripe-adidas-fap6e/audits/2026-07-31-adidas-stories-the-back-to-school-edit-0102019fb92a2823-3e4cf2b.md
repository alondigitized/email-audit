# adidas Stories: The Back-to-School Edit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a content/editorial send, not a sale — "adidas Stories" is a magazine-style roundup of styling tips, a Samba size guide, and an Adistar size guide, with zero discount and zero urgency anywhere on the page. Compared to the July 23 "Back to school in Samba" send (8/10, which fused a real hero product with a BTS hook) and the July 30 sale email (6/10, real % off), this one reads like a content-marketing filler piece dressed up in back-to-school language — it's the fifth BTS-adjacent adidas email in under two weeks, and it's the one with the least reason to click.
- The top hero shot (three teens on a bench, one holding a bike) is well-composed and on-brand, but everything below it is generic: "Get ready for the new school year with outfit ideas, comfy sneakers, backpacks and everyday essentials" is vague enough to describe any BTS email adidas has sent this month. The two size-guide sections are genuinely useful content, but they're utility, not motivation — nobody opens an email for a sizing chart unless they're already mid-purchase-consideration.
- Judgment: a clean, well-produced, cadence-fatiguing email with no offer and no single clear reason to click today. It'll perform on brand trust alone, not on this creative's own merits.

## What's working

- Hero image is sharp, diverse, and lifestyle-authentic — three models in adidas gear (Samba jacket, quarter-zip, Firebird tee) in a believable schoolyard setting, no render bugs.
- Clear visual hierarchy: bold "ADIDAS STORIES" masthead, then one all-caps headline + one CTA button per section, repeated consistently.
- The Samba trio product shot (leopard, white/black, forest green) is a strong, scannable product callout that ties directly to the size-guide copy beneath it.
- Three consecutive black "LEARN MORE →" buttons are unambiguous, high-contrast, and easy to tap on mobile.
- No layout breakage, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- Zero offer anywhere — no %, no $, no BOGO, no free shipping. This is pure content in a season where every other adidas email this batch has at least gestured at urgency.
- No urgency or timing signal at all — nothing time-bound to justify opening now vs. next week.
- Preview text ("Get ready to return to school with the latest sizing-and-styling tips from adidas") just restates the subject's "Back-to-School Edit" framing instead of adding new information.
- Three separate CTAs (Style, Samba fit, Adistar fit) split attention with no prioritization — there's no single hero action the eye is pushed toward.
- This is the fifth BTS-flavored email in under two weeks (July 20, 21, 23, 25, 30, now 31) — cadence fatigue is real and this is the weakest entry of the run on concrete value.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this from a 3-CTA content roundup to a single-focus send — pick either the Samba size guide (product-specific, purchase-adjacent) or the styling tips, not both plus Adistar. Splitting three ways dilutes all three.
- 2. Add a concrete incentive tied to the size guide — e.g., "free returns if the fit's wrong" or a first-purchase discount — to convert size-guide *research* into a size-guide *purchase*.
- 3. Replace the generic BTS body copy with something specific: name an actual product, price, or drop instead of "outfit ideas, comfy sneakers, backpacks and everyday essentials."
- 4. Space this out from the July 30 sale email — sending content-only the day after a %-off blast trains subscribers to skip the non-sale sends.
- 5. Tighten the subject/preview pairing so the preview earns its own line instead of echoing "Back-to-School Edit."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your back-to-school fit, sized right`
