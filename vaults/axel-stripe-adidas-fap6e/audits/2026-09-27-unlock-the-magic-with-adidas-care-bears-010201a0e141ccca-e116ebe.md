# Unlock the magic with adidas | Care Bears
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest visual concept adidas has run this cycle, and it holds up on second viewing — the pastel gradient cloud-and-rain hero with the Care Bear perched on the Superstar is genuinely charming and instantly reads as a collab, not a generic sale email. But the execution beneath the hero is thinner than the concept deserves: two identical Adistar Control 5 colorways at full price, then two kids' accessories, with no lifestyle shot of anyone actually wearing this collection.
- Compared to the 2026-09-22 send with the same subject line and hero, this is essentially the same email again — same pastel gradient, same Care Bear, same layout skeleton. Sending a near-identical creative twice in five days without a new offer or new hero variant reads as a resend more than a fresh push.
- Verdict: cute concept, thin follow-through, and a cadence problem — this is a repeat, not a new send.

## What's working

- The gradient hero with the Care Bear on the Superstar sneaker is distinctive and immediately signals "collab," not "another sale email."
- Both adidas and Care Bears logos are placed cleanly in the hero without cluttering it.
- Star ratings (4.6–4.9) are visible on all four products, giving quick social proof.
- The kids' backpack and bucket hat show real percentage-off pricing (-20%, -10%), which is more concrete than the shoes' flat price.

## What's weak

- Two of the four featured products are the exact same shoe (Adistar Control 5) in two colorways — this reads as padding, not a curated collection.
- No lifestyle or on-body shot anywhere — for a playful, nostalgic collab like Care Bears, showing it worn would sell the vibe far better than product-on-white shots.
- No urgency or scarcity signal (no "limited edition," no countdown) despite this being a licensed collab that will plausibly sell out.
- This is a near-repeat of the 2026-09-22 "adidas | Care Bears" send (6.00/10) — same hero art, same layout, same lack of urgency — with nothing new added to justify a second send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap one of the duplicate Adistar Control 5 colorways for a different product (apparel, a third accessory, or the kids' shoe line) so the grid doesn't feel repetitive — this is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Add a lifestyle/on-body shot near the top, even a single kid or adult wearing the backpack/hat, to make the "feel-good comfort" preheader promise visible rather than just stated.
- 3. Add a real urgency hook ("limited-edition drop," "while supplies last") since collabs sell on scarcity and none is currently signaled.
- 4. Don't re-send the same hero/layout as five days ago without new creative — if inventory is still fresh, at least reshuffle the product grid and swap the subject.
