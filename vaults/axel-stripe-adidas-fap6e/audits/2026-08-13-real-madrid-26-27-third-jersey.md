# Real Madrid 26/27 third jersey
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is another two-jobs-one-email send from adidas, same pattern as "Samba Goes Metallic" (2026-08-07) and the Care Bears launch (2026-08-03): a genuine product story bolted to a generic sale banner. The top half is actually strong — a clean, editorial hero shot of the Real Madrid 26/27 third jersey in vibrant pink with Emirates/Real Madrid crest detail, paired with copy that sells the story ("connects cultures," "unites communities") rather than just specs. Two clear CTAs (Shop Now, Find Store Now) sit right under the copy.
- Then it hard-cuts into "THE BACK TO SCHOOL SALE" — up to 40% off — which has nothing to do with the jersey, uses a totally different visual language (lifestyle photo of teens with a bike vs. the moody product shot above), and reads like it got stapled on from a different campaign. Given this brand's last several sends followed the exact same "product drop + unrelated sale bolt-on" formula, this is now a pattern, not a one-off — and it's diluting an otherwise good product story every time.
- Verdict: solid product marketing undercut by a lazy compound send. If I care about Real Madrid gear, the top half would get my click; the bottom half is noise I'd scroll past.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely well-art-directed — three-panel crop mixing a model shot, a crest close-up, and a group shot, all in the same pink/gold palette.
- Headline and body copy are specific and story-driven, not generic ("the iconic bold pink is back with a modern touch").
- Two distinct, clearly-labeled CTAs (Shop Now / Find Store Now) give both online and in-store shoppers a path.
- No render bugs — text is crisp, images loaded, buttons are legible black-on-white/white-on-black.

## What's weak

- The Back to School Sale section is visually and thematically disconnected from the jersey story — different photo style, different message, no transition.
- Top preview text ("Vibrant pink. Off-white details. A jersey that connects.") is on-brand but the subject line doesn't hint at any of it — pure product name only.
- adiClub/Apple TV offer and the 40%-off sale are buried in dense legal copy at the bottom rather than surfaced anywhere in the visual hierarchy.
- This is the same "two unrelated stories in one email" structural issue flagged in the 08-07 and 08-03 sends — the brand hasn't fixed this pattern across three consecutive audited emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split into two emails: one focused Real Madrid jersey launch, one dedicated Back to School sale. Compound sends consistently score lower across this brand's history (5-6/10 average) versus focused ones (Penn State launch hit 7/10 with zero bolt-on).
- 2. If it must stay one email, add a visual divider or "Also: Back to School Sale" eyebrow so the jump doesn't feel like two different emails glued together.
- 3. Surface the "up to 40% off" number or the adiClub Apple TV perk in the subject/preheader — it's a real, specific incentive currently buried in fine print.
- 4. Add a price or "new arrival" badge on the jersey hero — right now there's a rich story but zero purchase-decision info (price, sizes) visible above the fold.
