# Earn adiClub points to unlock more rewards
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is step two of adidas's adiClub onboarding sequence — a loyalty education email sent two days after the welcome send ("🎁 Welcome to adiClub! Explore your rewards," May 15). The brief is clear: get the new member to understand how points work and complete their profile for an instant 50-point bump. Adidas executes it cleanly. The three-section structure — hero with profile CTA, points explainer, and earning mechanics — is logical and easy to scan.
- The weakness is motivation. Nothing here creates urgency or even much excitement. Fifty bonus points is a real incentive, but the email never tells you what 50 points gets you, so the number lands hollow. A new member has no frame of reference for whether 50 points is meaningful or trivial.
- Overall: competent onboarding scaffolding with a soft conversion goal. It's not broken, but it leaves points on the table by not anchoring the reward to something the member actually wants.

## What's working

- **Structure is clean and scannable** — hero → mechanics → more ways to earn flows naturally, no cognitive overload.
- **Three earning levers are concise and credible** — purchase (10 pts/$1), review (50 pts), stay active — gives the member an immediate mental model.
- **Primary CTA is specific and action-oriented** — "COMPLETE MY PROFILE →" is unambiguous; verb + object, nothing vague.
- **Brand consistency is maintained** — adiClub wordmark, adidas tone, and clean minimal layout match the welcome email exactly.
- **No render bugs visible** — text is legible, layout holds, no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **"Hello," with no name** — the greeting is unmerged or unpersonalized; this is a glaring miss for a loyalty program email that theoretically knows who you are.
- **50 points has no context** — the email offers 50 bonus points but never explains what that redeems for, making the incentive feel arbitrary rather than motivating.
- **"SHOP NOW →" CTA is orphaned** — it appears below the hero with no attached offer or product; it feels like a default module that wasn't removed.
- **Product images are dark and unspecific** — the mid-email images are too small and dark to be aspirational or product-relevant; they add visual weight without adding desire.
- **Zero urgency** — no expiration on the profile-completion bonus, no seasonal hook, nothing that makes acting now feel different from acting next month.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the 50-point offer to a real reward** — add one line like "50 points = $5 toward your next pair" so the incentive is tangible. This is the single highest-leverage change in the email.
- 2. **Fix the personalization token** — "Hello, [FIRST NAME]" should be non-negotiable in a loyalty sequence where the platform has the data.
- 3. **Add a soft deadline to the profile bonus** — "Complete by [date] to earn your 50 points" creates urgency without pressure and gives a reason to act now.
- 4. **Remove or anchor "SHOP NOW"** — either tie it to a specific category (running, training) or cut it; a floating shop CTA dilutes the profile-completion conversion goal.
- 5. **Replace the dark product imagery** — use a hero product image tied to a reward tier the member can realistically unlock (e.g., a specific shoe at the next level).

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Takes 2 minutes. Points post instantly.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `50 points = [reward value]. Here's how to get yours.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — source truncates before any `<a>` tags or tracking pixel `<img>` tags appear.

**Confirmed issue — font asset CDN mismatch:**
- Both `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` font files are served from `s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com` (EU West 1, Ireland)
- Sending domain is `us-news.adidas.com` (US audience)

- Mitigation: front the S3 bucket with CloudFront using a US-region edge, or duplicate assets to an `s3-us-east-1` or `s3-us-west-2` origin

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**No issues — `lang` attribute:** `<html lang="en">` present; screen readers will announce language correctly.

**No issues — Outlook font fallback:** All `@font-face` declarations include `mso-font-alt:Arial`, which is the correct pattern for Outlook's MSHTML renderer.

**Minor — `font-display:swap` in email CSS:**
- Present on all four `@font-face` rules
- `font-display` is a CSS Fonts Level 4 property; no major email client rendering engine (MSHTML, WebKit/iOS, Gmail) honors it
- Harmless but dead code — remove to reduce stylesheet size

**Minor — media query breakpoints are correct but overlap potential:**
- `max-width:480px` and `max-width:639px` blocks are both present
- The 480px block redefines `.col-xs-1_3_2d` width; the 639px block does not reset it — confirm the cascade is intentional and tested on 375px/390px viewport widths (iPhone 15 viewport)
