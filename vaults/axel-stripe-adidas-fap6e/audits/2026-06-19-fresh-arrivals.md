# Fresh arrivals
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's Pixar Toy Story Samba drop announcement, dressed in the most anonymous possible subject line adidas could have written. "Fresh arrivals" is the central failure: a limited cultural collaboration — Toy Story Samba OGs in a visually arresting collab colorway — gets buried under four words that communicate nothing. The email earns opens it doesn't deserve.
- Inside, however, the structure is clean. The Samba OG hero leads with clear pricing and star ratings, the 2×2 product grid covers the full collab range (Jane, Junior, and OG variants) plus a Dropset Pro outlier, and a secondary module at the bottom runs a Road to Glory signed-cleat sweepstakes. That secondary unit is doing a different job than the product drop, and the two goals compete without resolution.
- After a run of heavy discount emails (June 12 Summer Kickoff 40% off, June 18 code DEAL $30 off $100+), this send smartly breaks to a full-price cultural drop. But pivoting to full-price only works if the inbox open rate is there — and "Fresh arrivals" is not doing that work.

## What's working

- **Pixar hero is unmistakable**: The Toy Story Samba OG colorway reads cultural collab immediately — a scroll-stopper for anyone who's a fan or a collector.
- **Product grid is tight and legible**: Four products, all with price and star ratings, no clutter — easy to compare and shop.
- **Star ratings on every product add purchase confidence**: Social proof at the grid level is a meaningful credibility signal on a full-price send.
- **Cadence break is the right call**: Three consecutive promotional emails (June 12, 13, 18) set the expectation of deals; a drop email with genuine product story is the smart counter-move.

## What's weak

- **Subject line completely wastes the Pixar collaboration**: "Fresh arrivals" could come from any retailer for any product. The collab IS the hook and it's invisible from the inbox.
- **Preheader appears to be leaked template text**: The top-of-email line is unreadable at render resolution and reads like placeholder or technical copy — not intentional preview text.
- **Zero scarcity signal for a limited drop**: No "while supplies last," no member early access badge, no quantity indication. The limited nature of a Pixar collab is the most natural urgency lever here and it's completely absent.
- **Dual-CTA structure dilutes conversion intent**: The email opens as a product drop and ends as a sweepstakes entry — two different conversion goals that pull the reader in different directions.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the Pixar collab in the subject** — this is the highest-leverage single change. "Toy Story Samba drops now" versus "Fresh arrivals" is not a close call for open rate.
- 2. **Set an intentional preheader** that teases the collab; the current leaked/placeholder text is dead inbox real estate.
- 3. **Add one scarcity line to the hero section** — "Limited run — while supplies last" or an adiClub early-access badge requires zero design effort and moves fence-sitters.
- 4. **Separate or move the sweepstakes module** — Road to Glory is a worthy engagement play but it belongs in its own send or demoted to a footer strip, not a full-width second-act module that competes with the primary CTA.

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect and a missing plain-text body are the highest-severity issues; they create a broken click experience and risk inbox filtering. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset URLs and absent RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe round out the findings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — relative URL leaked as click destination**

The QA checker resolved a `dv.adidas.com` click-tracking URL to a relative path instead of an absolute `https://` URL:

```
Resolved destination: /g/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?txn=4a4edf00-7119-11f1-9775-ae32f7ad8416
```

A relative path is not a valid standalone URL; the redirect chain is returning a location header without the scheme+host, which means the click lands nowhere (or 404s, depending on the client). The originating tracking URL:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

This is likely a misconfigured redirect rule in the adidas click-tracking service for this specific content block.

**[INFO] 27 tracking links not probed** — all route through `dv.adidas.com` or `click.comms.adidas.com`; none were verified for valid final destinations. Only the one above was resolvable and it failed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Four HTTP image loads (same asset, repeated)**

The arrow icon is loaded four times via HTTP, not HTTPS:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook all block mixed-content images by default. All four instances will render as broken images on affected clients.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel loaded over HTTP**

```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ee2220e0c-174ba939-a456-476c-91c8-252ef175b3ca-000000/...
```

Open-tracking pixel via HTTP will be blocked by the same mixed-content rules, making open-rate data for this send unreliable.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `dv.adidas.com/o/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?...` — no `alt`
- `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` (tracking pixel) — no `alt`

The first is a content image; a missing `alt` breaks the plain-text-images-off experience and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**[WARN] `viewport` meta missing `width=device-width`**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">
```

Without `width=device-width`, iOS Mail may not respect the scale. Standard value: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.

**[INFO] `@font-face` declarations are no-ops**

Both `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared using only `local()` sources — no remote font URLs. These fonts are not installed on any typical end-user device, so the declarations silently fall through to Arial/Helvetica on every client. The blocks are inert but harmless. `font-display: swap` inside `@font-face` in email CSS has no effect (it's a browser-only hint).

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`**

This strips the referrer on all outbound clicks. If the landing page uses `document.referrer` for attribution logic, it will see an empty value. This may conflict with email-to-site analytics if the landing page is not purely relying on UTM params.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is absent**

QA confirmed the `text/plain` MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly require a plain-text part, but:
- Gmail and several ISPs use its absence as a spam signal.
- RFC 2045/2046 multipart/alternative etiquette requires a plain-text part.
- Certain accessibility tools and corporate email gateways only process plain text.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**

The relay may have stripped it, but if it is genuinely absent, this violates Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day sends). Expected format:

```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>, <https://...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` absent (RFC 8058)**

One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024. Without it, Gmail surfaces its own unsubscribe UI, which may route to a spam report instead.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to the AgentMail relay. The sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` should have SPF and DKIM aligned to `adidas.com` under DMARC. Cannot confirm without raw headers.

**[INFO]** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` is present — correct practice to prevent email archive indexing.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All click URLs route through `dv.adidas.com` with proprietary campaign params (`cp_tp`, `cp_cid`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`). No standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are visible in the tracking URL structure.

If the adidas analytics stack translates `cp_*`/`mi_*` params into UTM equivalents server-side at the redirect layer, this is a non-issue. If not, Google Analytics / GA4 on the landing pages will attribute these sessions as direct traffic, breaking campaign-level reporting.

The broken redirect identified in §2 means at least one content block's click goes nowhere — that destination cannot be evaluated for landing page alignment.
