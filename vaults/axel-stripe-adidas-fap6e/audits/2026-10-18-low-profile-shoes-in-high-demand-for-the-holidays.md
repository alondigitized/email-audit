# Low-profile shoes in high demand for the holidays
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-idea adidas send. It pushes the low-profile terrace sneakers (Japan, Tokyo, Taekwondo) as holiday gifts. The hero photo does the work. Three shoes sit on weathered wood: lilac suede, black-and-white cow print, and washed denim blue. It's tactile and distinctive, and it looks like a gift-guide image.
- The copy and structure let it down. "STYLE MUST-HAVES" is generic and says nothing about the shoes. The body paragraph names three models but gives no price, no offer and no reason to act now. A black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner sits above the hero with its own SHOP NOW button. It points at a different thing than the hero does, and it splits the first screen between two CTAs.
- My judgement is a pretty but low-commitment send. It will get browsers and miss anyone who needs a nudge. Compared with the Oct 13 Bad Bunny Gazelle hype drop, which at least had a named collab to carry it, this has no hook beyond "gifting." I'd put it at the low end of the brand's recent 5–6 range.

## What's working

- The hero photo is distinctive. Three colorways on a wood slat background, with the Japan heel tag legible, make the silhouette easy to read.
- The gifting angle fits the season and answers "why now."
- The layout is simple: one hero, one headline, one paragraph and one button. Nothing is broken.
- The black SHOP NOW button has strong contrast and is easy to find below the copy.
- Naming Japan, Tokyo and Taekwondo gives sneaker fans real model names to recognize.

## What's weak

- No offer is visible: no price, no discount, no free shipping, no member perk. The only reason to click is browsing.
- The headline "STYLE MUST-HAVES" is generic and doesn't name the product or the occasion.
- The top banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") competes with the hero for attention. It is also left-aligned against the hero and doesn't line up with it.
- The hero is not clickable-looking and has no product labels, prices or per-shoe links. The three models can't be told apart without reading the paragraph.
- The preheader link ("Shop the style must-haves before the holiday rush.") shows as underlined plain text in the top-left. It looks unstyled and nearly repeats the headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete reason to buy now: a price anchor ("From $90") or a gift deadline ("Order by Dec 15 for delivery"). This is the biggest lift.
- 2. Remove the best-sellers banner or move it below the main CTA. Let one hero and one button own the first screen.
- 3. Label each shoe in the hero (Japan, Tokyo, Taekwondo) with a price and a link, so the click goes to the product rather than a category.
- 4. Rewrite the headline to say what the product is: "Low-profile classics, gift-ready."
- 5. Add social proof: a rating, "back in stock," or a "selling fast" line that is actually true. The subject claims high demand, but nothing in the body backs it up.
