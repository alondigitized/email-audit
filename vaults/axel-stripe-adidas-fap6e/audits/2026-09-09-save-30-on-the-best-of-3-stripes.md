# Save 30% on the best of 3-Stripes
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- This is essentially last week's "End of Summer Sale" email re-sent with a new subject line — and the fine print gives it away: the offer is valid "September 1, 2026 12:01am PST through September 8, 2026 11:59pm PST," but this email is dated 2026-09-09. That means adidas sent a 30%-off promo the day *after* it expired. That's a real deliverability/trust problem, not a nitpick — anyone who clicks SHOP MEN today and hits a full-price cart is going to be annoyed.
- Setting the expiration issue aside, the creative itself is clean and on-brand: bold "THE END OF SUMMER SALE" hero over a green fabric macro shot, a clear "30% OFF 3-STRIPES STYLES" headline, code EXTRA called out plainly, and three well-organized gender CTAs (Men/Women/Kids). The four-product grid with star ratings and "Prime options available" badges is a genuinely differentiated touch versus the brand's usual single-hero-shot sends (per history: JENNIE 9/2, Samba 9/3, Tyshawn 9/4 were all single-image).
- Judgment: solid execution wasted on a stale offer. This is the adidas history's fourth sitewide-discount-style beat since 9/1, and now it's arriving expired — cadence fatigue plus a broken promise. Score accordingly.

## What's working

- Clear, singular offer front and center: "30% OFF 3-STRIPES STYLES" with code EXTRA spelled out in body copy.
- Three distinct, well-labeled CTAs (Shop Men / Shop Women / Shop Kids) give every visitor an obvious next click.
- Product grid shows price, star rating with review count, and product name for four real SKUs — more substantive than a generic "shop now" banner.
- "Prime options available" badges are a smart trust/logistics signal for shoppers used to fast, easy returns.
- No overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem-ipsum — the render is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The stated offer window (through Sept 8, 11:59pm PST) had already lapsed by the time this email went out (Sept 9) — this is a bait-and-switch by omission, and it's the single biggest issue in this send.
- Subject "Save 30% on the best of 3-Stripes" reads as generic sale copy identical in spirit to the 9/1 "30% off for the End of Summer Sale" email — no new hook, no urgency that's actually true.
- The pre-header ("End the season on a high note") repeats the same tired end-of-summer framing already used in the hero — no incremental information.
- Fourth near-identical discount/promo framing in just over a week (9/1 sitewide, plus JENNIE/Samba/Tyshawn all leading with CTA banners) — cadence fatigue risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the date bug immediately** — either extend the promo and update the fine print, or pull this send. Sending an expired code destroys trust and generates support tickets; this should have been caught before send.
- 2. Replace the recycled "End of Summer" framing with something that matches the actual date — early fall / new-season language, since by 9/9 "end of summer" already reads stale.
- 3. Lead the subject with the code or a firmer deadline once the dates are corrected, so it doesn't read as a copy-paste of the 9/1 email.
- 4. Keep the four-product-grid-with-ratings format — it's a stronger pattern than the single-hero-shot sends this brand has been leaning on; consider standardizing it across future promos.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extended: 30% off 3-Stripes ends tonight`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code EXTRA gets you 30% off before it's gone`
