# Manchester United Bringback Collection
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the third consecutive heritage storytelling email from adidas in four days — Five Ten rubber (May 21), US94 World Cup (May 22), now the Man Utd 90/92 Away collection (May 24). The formula is consistent: archival photography, punchy headline, evocative prose, zero price. "The archive is open. Own the legacy." is a genuinely good close line, and the 90/92 United kit is legitimately iconic — one of the most recognized shirts in football history. The creative brief is coherent.
- The problem is the pattern. There is no offer, no urgency, no member benefit, and this is now the seventh adidas email in ten days. Each individual send is defensible; the cumulative cadence is starting to feel like wallpaper. The heritage angle works once or twice; by the third straight send with no transactional hook, readers start skimming without clicking.
- Verdict: well-produced brand content that a Man Utd fan will genuinely appreciate, but too niche to pull clicks from a broad subscriber base, and fatigue from cadence is becoming a real tax on open rates.

## What's working

- **Hero and headline land cleanly.** "MAN UTD BRINGBACK COLLECTION" over a moody archival kit photo — hierarchy is immediate, no confusion about what this email is.
- **Copy quality is above average.** "The archive is open. Own the legacy." earns its keep as a closing line. The body correctly names the specific season (cup run) and specific product (90/92 Away Jersey) rather than vague brand platitudes.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text — works as shown.
- **Subject is precise.** 38 characters, unambiguous — Man Utd fans know exactly what they're being shown.
- **"ORIGINALS" badge in the hero** correctly anchors this in the lifestyle/heritage sub-brand, setting the right product expectation.

## What's weak

- **No price, no offer.** Pure storytelling with a cold "SHOP NOW" CTA asks the reader to do all the conversion work themselves. The Five Ten send (May 21) had the same problem.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "SHOP NOW" after heritage copy is a gear-shift with no bridge. "Shop the 90/92 Collection" or "Wear the Archive" costs nothing and converts better.
- **Cadence tax is accumulating.** Seven emails in ten days — three of them pure heritage sends with no offer. The subscriber who opened all of them is now conditioned to expect no deal and may skim past.
- **No social proof or scarcity signal.** Bringback/archive collections are a natural home for "limited quantities" or "selling fast" framing — none of that appears here.
- **Preheader not confirmable from webview render**, but the opening line "This shirt 90s belonged to Manchester United" reads like body copy doing double duty — slightly awkward as a preview.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or limited-quantity signal.** Even "from $45" or "Archive quantities only" gives the reader a reason to click now rather than later (or never).
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA.** Replace "SHOP NOW" with something collection-specific: "Wear the Archive" or "Shop the 90/92 Kit."
- 3. **Break the no-offer streak.** After two consecutive heritage-only sends, this third one needed either a loyalty perk (adiClub member early access) or a real offer to justify the send. Consider interleaving storytelling with transactional beats.
- 4. **Add a secondary CTA block** — "Also trending: [related product]" — to catch readers who don't care about Man Utd specifically but might still convert on something adjacent.
- 5. **Subject line:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `The 90/92 Kit Is Back — Shop the Archive`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Man Utd's Iconic Away Kit, Reissued`
- 6. **Preheader:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The '90 Away Jersey defined a generation. Now it's yours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every stitch from the cup run. Archive quantities only.`
