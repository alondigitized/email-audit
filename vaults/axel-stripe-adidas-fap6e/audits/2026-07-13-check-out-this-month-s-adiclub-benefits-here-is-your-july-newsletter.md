# Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your July Newsletter
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's July adiClub loyalty newsletter — not a promotional send but a member engagement email built around two FIFA World Cup 26™ giveaways (a game-used match ball and a signed Adizero item) plus an app download push. The FIFA thread is smart: adidas has been leaning hard on World Cup 26™ since at least the June 20 US94 send and the July 3 Coca-Cola collab, and the match ball giveaway is a genuinely compelling exclusive only a loyalty program can offer.
- The problem is everything fell into the subject line. "Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your July Newsletter" is 70 characters of inbox filler. The giveaway — win an actual used FIFA match ball — is a headline capable of standing on its own. Instead it's buried below a newsletter masthead label in the body, while the subject reads like an internal calendar reminder.
- The email itself is clean and well-structured. The match ball hero lands clearly, the Adizero section adds a second reason to scroll, and the adiClub framing is consistent with the brand's loyalty positioning. But this is an email that had a strong card to play (FIFA giveaway, members-only) and refused to play it in the subject.

## What's working

- **Match ball giveaway is a legitimately strong hook** — game-used balls from every FIFA match is a rare, exclusive, member-only offer that stands out from the routine discount cadence.
- **FIFA World Cup 26™ timing is right** — adidas has been building this story since June, and the newsletter arrival mid-July keeps it warm without repeating a product launch.
- **Two distinct giveaway sections** — the Adizero signed-item draw adds a second click opportunity for sneaker-first subscribers who are less soccer-focused.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, buttons are visible and legible throughout.
- **adiClub framing is consistent** — "Exclusively for adiClub members" correctly signals this is a member benefit, not a spray-and-pray campaign.

## What's weak

- **Subject line wastes a strong hook** — "Win a game-used FIFA match ball" is right there in the hero; it never appears in the subject.
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere** — neither giveaway shows a close date, removing the one mechanic that would drive an immediate click.
- **"STAY IN THE KNOW" section is text-heavy and undifferentiated** — three loose benefit bullets with a generic "Download the App" CTA do not compel action.
- **"JOIN NOW" CTA copy is vague** — "join" what? Enter the draw, join a waitlist, or join the program? An adiClub member already joined.
- **Preview text appears to leak inbox-management junk** ("Move our newsletter") rather than surfacing any copy from the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the giveaway** — the match ball is the most compelling thing in this email and it's completely invisible in the inbox view. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win a FIFA match ball — July adiClub`
- **Subject Alt B:** `adiClub: enter to win a game-used World Cup ball`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Every match. Every ball. Members only — enter now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Semifinal and final match balls up for grabs this month.`
- 2. **Add a deadline to both giveaways** — even "Enter by July 31" changes the calculus on whether to click now vs. later (later = never). Copy the approach from the June 30 summer savings send, which used a credible time frame.
- 3. **Replace "JOIN NOW" with "ENTER THE DRAW"** — precise verb-noun CTA, no ambiguity.
- 4. **Collapse "STAY IN THE KNOW" into one punchy bullet list with direct links** — the app download pitch is weak as a standalone section; integrate it as a benefit ("Get notified the moment your draw result posts — download the app") to make it contextually motivated.
- 5. **Surface a secondary purchase hook** — the June 30 send showed 30% off and the July 3 collab had a clear product to buy. A loyalty newsletter can carry both a reward (giveaway) and a commercial (members-only discount). Right now there's no reason to buy anything.

## Technical Audit — adidas adiClub July Newsletter

### 1. Technical Summary

The email scores a 38% QA pass rate, driven by two hard failures: a broken click-tracking redirect and a missing plain-text fallback. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and missing RFC 8058 unsubscribe headers compound the risk profile.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken click redirect**
The QA resolver returned `unknown url type: '/g/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?txn=2383fd00-7ebc-11f1-a9f3-4ec26c495bcf'` for the following tracked URL:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The ESP's redirect chain is resolving to a relative path (`/g/...`) rather than an absolute destination URL. Any subscriber clicking this link lands on a dead end.

**WARN — 26 tracking links unverifiable**
All outbound links are wrapped in click-redirect domains (`click.comms.adidas.com`, `dv.adidas.com`). The checker skipped HTTP probing on these, so destination URL correctness cannot be confirmed programmatically.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 4× HTTP image source (same asset)**
The arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is embedded four times via `http://`:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail and Apple Mail block mixed-content images by default. All four instances will likely render as broken images.

**WARN — Tracking pixel also on HTTP**
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f5b92433a-32eeae6d-0bfd-4dab-bcfd-1f7c56e8abfc-000000/clyGbmgOndelfeWYGCUARWvnZ...
```
Open-tracking pixel served over HTTP — blocked by the same mixed-content rules. Open rate data will be undercounted.

**WARN — 2 images missing `alt` text**

| Image | src fragment |
|---|---|
| `f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf` (linked image) | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/f0732c31-...` |
| Tracking pixel | `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` |

Both fail WCAG 2.1 AA (SC 1.1.1). The linked image is particularly significant — a subscriber with images disabled sees no text label and no functional link affordance.

**INFO — Custom fonts not loaded remotely**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` blocks use only `local()` sources — no remote CDN URL. The custom typefaces will never render in any client that doesn't have them installed locally. This is likely intentional for email, but the fallback stack (`Arial, Helvetica, Calibri, Sans Serif`) is what all subscribers actually see.

### 5. Compliance

**FAIL — Plain-text alternative is 0 characters**
The `text/plain` MIME part is empty. CAN-SPAM and general ESP best-practice require a readable text fallback. Some spam filters penalize HTML-only messages.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header unconfirmed**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If it is present at the ESP but stripped by the relay, this is a relay configuration issue. If it is genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo (as of February 2024 bulk sender requirements) will suppress the native unsubscribe UI.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe — required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders — depends on both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` being present. Neither is confirmed.

**WARN — SPF/DKIM status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Cannot confirm DKIM signing by `us-news.comms.adidas.com` or SPF alignment for the `adidas.com` domain. Needs verification at the raw SMTP level.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**WARN — `no-referrer` meta suppresses HTTP Referer on all clicks**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">
```
This strips the `Referer` header from every outbound link click. Any server-side referral attribution relying on the HTTP Referer (rather than UTM parameters) will be blind to email as the source.

**UTM coverage unverifiable**
With 26 tracking links behind redirect domains that were skipped, UTM parameter presence on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed. The one broken redirect (`/g/f0732c31-...`) guarantees at least one destination receives zero UTM-attributed traffic.
