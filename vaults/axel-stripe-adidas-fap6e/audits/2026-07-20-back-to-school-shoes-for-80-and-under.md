# Back to school shoes for $80 and under
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's seasonal pivot to back-to-school mode, dropped on July 20 — the morning after the World Cup victory send that scored a 10. The tonal whiplash is real: yesterday was gold confetti and Yamal; today is flat-lay sneaker grids and a $80 price cap. That's not necessarily wrong — BTS is a genuine category moment — but the email itself doesn't earn the context switch. The hero section is clean and the price anchor works. The 25% off section is where it falls apart: same "DOWNLOAD THE APP" mechanical error that undercut the July 15 send, five days ago, in the same inbox.
- The email does the minimum viable job. Price cap + split gender CTAs + seasonal timing = a passable send. But it commits zero creative energy to the pivot. After the World Cup arc, subscribers were primed for something. This resets to catalog mode with no acknowledgment of what just happened. A line — even one — connecting championship energy to the next chapter would have earned it.
- The dual-offer structure (BTS $80 and under + App Exclusive 25%) is also a tension point. Two separate value props, two separate CTAs, one email. Neither one gets the full attention it deserves.

## What's working

- **Price anchor is immediate and specific** — "$80 AND UNDER SNEAKERS" is the first copy the eye hits below the hero; no hunting required.
- **Gender-split CTAs are the right call** — "SHOP MEN" / "SHOP WOMEN" reduces friction for a footwear send where the catalog diverges by gender.
- **Seasonal timing is correct** — July 20 BTS is early enough to be a planning purchase, not a panic purchase. Right window.
- **adiClub 2x points is present** — the loyalty benefit is surfaced in the app offer copy, rewarding members who read past the fold.

## What's weak

- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" again** — same broken CTA as the July 15 send. Subscribers with the app installed hit a dead end; the 25% off goes unredeemed. This is the second consecutive send with the identical flaw.
- **No product hero** — 90+ styles, zero specifics. The flat-lay is visually inert. One breakout shoe with a name and a price would have anchored the scroll.
- **No models, no aspiration** — purely product photography in a back-to-school send where competitor emails lean into first-day-of-school narrative energy. The category invites story; this email skips it entirely.
- **Zero carry-forward from the World Cup arc** — two days of 10/10 championship content, then a hard cut to sale grids. One transition sentence would have preserved brand momentum.
- **Two competing offers dilute both** — $80 and under (browse now) vs. 25% off in-app (download / open app) pull in different directions. The email tries to serve both and fully serves neither.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the app CTA immediately** — "DOWNLOAD THE APP" must become "SHOP THE SALE" or "OPEN IN APP" with a deep link to the 25% off collection. This is the same bug from July 15. If it shipped twice, it's a template problem — fix the template.
- 2. **Pick one primary offer** — Either this is the BTS $80 email or it's the app 25% email. They can co-exist as a secondary module, but they cannot share equal visual weight. The BTS send is the stronger seasonal hook; make that the hero.
- 3. **Add a product hero** — Pull one specific shoe (the most-clicked BTS style by gender) and give it a name, a price, and a direct PDP link. "90+ styles" is a liability, not a draw.
- 4. **Acknowledge the World Cup moment** — One line: "Champions wear cleats. You need something for the hallway." Corny example, but the principle holds — use the brand equity that was just built.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Your back-to-school lineup, $80 and under`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `New school year. Fresh kicks. Under $80.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `90+ styles to start the year right — find your fit`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop men's and women's picks before the rush hits`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — malformed internal URL**
The adidas click-tracking proxy (`dv.adidas.com`) resolved a link containing an invalid relative-style path:
```
unknown url type: '/g/dfecbfb9-d968-49b6-a045-5f7089279a60?txn=4d996a00-...'
```
The `cp_tp` payload decoded to a path beginning with `/g/` rather than an absolute URL. This is a redirect misconfiguration in the ESP/click-wrapper layer — the destination URL was never fully resolved. Any click on this link produces a broken experience.

**[WARN] 29 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` and `dv.adidas.com` redirect chains. The QA probe could not follow them to verify final destinations. The broken link above is the one confirmed failure among this pool.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 6× repeated HTTP image — arrow asset blocked by mixed-content policy**
The same arrow image is referenced six times via HTTP:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Modern email clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail) enforce mixed-content blocking. All six instances will fail to load in any client that proxies images over HTTPS. The image should be served from `https://`.

**[WARN] Open tracking pixel served over HTTP**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f7f9ece4c-.../oMCFGHKBRQ724IeiLPFC0t7TV...
```
This pixel is also HTTP, meaning open-tracking data will be lost in HTTPS-enforcing clients and the image will be blocked.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on 2 images**
- `dfecbfb9-d968-49b6-a045-5f7089279a60` (content image via `dv.adidas.com`)
- `oMCFGHKBRQ724IeiLPFC0t7TV...` (tracking pixel via `click.comms.adidas.com`)

The pixel missing alt text is lower severity; the content image missing alt is an accessibility gap for screen readers and image-off rendering.
