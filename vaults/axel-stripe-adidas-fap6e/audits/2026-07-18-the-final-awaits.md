# THE FINAL AWAITS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's Final eve send — dropped July 18, one day before the FIFA World Cup 26™ Final in New Jersey. It works as moment marketing: the LA FINAL hero image, the eight player names (Olmo, Cuelbar, Pedri, Yamal, Messi, De Paul, Mac Allister, Alvarez), and the "ONE STEP LEFT / One game. One moment. History." copy land with weight. The dual-CTA architecture (SHOP SPAIN / SHOP ARGENTINA) is the most clever structural choice adidas has made in this entire World Cup run — it forces a pick, which is inherently engaging.
- The miss is commercial. There's no offer, no kit image, no product hero, no price hook. The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top is actively confusing — it reads like a different email was stitched on. You're arriving at the biggest sporting moment adidas has spent months building to (the Coca-Cola collab on July 3rd, the adiClub giveaways on July 13th) and the conversion mechanism is a plain text button. That's a squandered moment.
- The send earns its emotion. It doesn't earn its click. For the Final itself, adidas needed either a kit on a player's back or a deadline offer — "Order your jersey before kickoff" — to turn cultural relevance into revenue.

## What's working

- **LA FINAL hero** is visually dominant and immediately communicates the stakes — the red block type and player silhouettes are clean and high-impact.
- **"ONE STEP LEFT / Spain or Argentina?"** is the best body copy in this whole World Cup run — activates tribal loyalty instantly.
- **Dual-CTA split (SHOP SPAIN / SHOP ARGENTINA)** is a smart engagement mechanic; it makes the reader pick a side before they click.
- **"Made for this moment."** preheader complements the subject without repeating it — one of the few times this cadence has gotten preheader right.
- **Timing is precise** — sent the day before the Final, which is exactly when fan intent to buy kit peaks.

## What's weak

- **No product shown** — not a single kit, shoe, or item is visible in the body; the email asks you to shop without showing you what you're shopping for.
- **"Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner** is a tonal mismatch — it belongs in the July 5th catalog email, not the Final eve send.
- **No offer or urgency hook** — no discount, no free shipping, no "get it before kickoff" deadline, which is the obvious lever on an event with a hard time boundary.
- **No adiClub callout** — the July 13th newsletter built anticipation with member giveaways; this send ignores that audience segment entirely.
- **ALL CAPS subject line** — "THE FINAL AWAITS" reads as a spam signal even if it's intentional brand voice.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline offer** — "Free shipping on all orders through kickoff, July 19" or a kit bundle deal. The Final has a hard clock; use it commercially, not just emotionally.
- 2. **Kill the best sellers banner** — the top-of-email "Explore the latest adidas best sellers / SHOP NOW →" strip undercuts the moment. One email, one story.
- 3. **Show a product** — put a Spain kit and Argentina kit side-by-side below the hero, linked to their respective shop pages. The SHOP SPAIN / SHOP ARGENTINA split demands a visual.
- 4. **Title-case the subject** — "The Final Awaits" reduces spam-filter risk without losing the weight.
- 5. **Surface adiClub for members** — even a one-liner ("adiClub members: early access to match-day drops") ties back to the July 13 newsletter thread and rewards the loyalty segment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spain vs Argentina — Shop Your Side`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Final is Tomorrow. Whose Kit Are You Wearing?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Last chance to get your jersey before kickoff July 19.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pedri or Messi. Pick your side before the whistle blows.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is adidas's Final eve send — dropped July 18, one day before the FIFA World Cup 26™ Final in New Jersey. It works as moment marketing: the LA FINAL hero image, the eight player names (Olmo, Cuelbar, Pedri, Yamal, Messi, De Paul, Mac Allister, Alvarez), and the "ONE STEP LEFT / One game. One moment. History." copy land with weight. The dual-CTA architecture (SHOP SPAIN / SHOP ARGENTINA) is the most clever structural choice adidas has made in this entire World Cup run — it forces a pick, which is inherently engaging.

The miss is commercial. There's no offer, no kit image, no product hero, no price hook. The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top is actively confusing — it reads like a different email was stitched on. You're arriving at the biggest sporting moment adidas has spent months building to (the Coca-Cola collab on July 3rd, the adiClub giveaways on July 13th) and the conversion mechanism is a plain text button. That's a squandered moment.

The send earns its emotion. It doesn't earn its click. For the Final itself, adidas needed either a kit on a player's back or a deadline offer — "Order your jersey before kickoff" — to turn cultural relevance into revenue.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent from captured message**

The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not present in the relay-captured copy. If genuinely absent from the envelope:
