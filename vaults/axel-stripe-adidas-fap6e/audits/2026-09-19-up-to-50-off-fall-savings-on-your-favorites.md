# Up to 50% off fall savings on your favorites
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest sitewide-sale execution adidas has sent this cycle — it takes the "Fall Kickoff Sale" concept from the 2026-09-10 send and tightens it considerably. The pink ribbon hero returns nearly identical to the earlier "Fall is here" email, but this time the product grid is deeper and more useful: four real SKUs with prices, discounts, star ratings, and Prime availability badges, spanning lifestyle sneakers, a collab colorway, a performance running shoe, and apparel. Three clear gendered CTAs (Men/Women/Kids) sit right under the discount headline, which is good structure.
- Where it falls apart is the back half. The email pivots hard into a "Back to School Backpacks" section with a clear vinyl backpack that reads as a completely different campaign bolted onto the sale — no discount, no price, no connection to the "up to 50% off" promise above it. That's two campaigns stapled together, and it dilutes the single clear offer the top of the email built so well.
- Net: a genuinely well-merchandised sale block dragged down by a mismatched, promo-less back-to-school tack-on and near-total reuse of a ribbon hero the brand already sent two weeks ago.

## What's working

- Four products shown with price, original price, percent off, star rating, and Prime badge — the most complete product data of any adidas send reviewed.
- Discounts are specific and vary by item (-20%, -50%, -40%, -60%), which reads as authentic rather than a flat blanket claim.
- Three gendered shopping paths (Men/Women/Kids) are immediately below the hero, reducing friction to the right category.
- "No code needed" is called out directly under the headline, removing a common purchase-flow objection.
- Clean black-and-white utility bar up top ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") gives a secondary path for browsers not sold on the sale.

## What's weak

- The backpack section has no price, no discount, and no rating — it breaks the "up to 50% off" promise the whole top half sets up.
- The pink ribbon "FALL KICKOFF SALE" hero is nearly identical to the 2026-09-10 "Fall is here and so are up to 40% off savings" send — same ribbon art style, same pink palette, just bumped from 40% to 50%. Subscribers who opened that one will feel the reuse.
- Preheader ("Get ready for fall with new styles.") is generic and undersells the actual 50%-off/specific-SKU offer sitting below it.
- The Coca-Cola Samba collab shoe has no visible size/availability signal beyond "Prime options available," which matters for a hyped collab that sells out fast.
- Two unrelated CTAs by the end (sale shop-now buttons + backpack shop-now) split attention instead of driving one action.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the backpack/back-to-school block entirely, or if it must stay, add a discount and price to keep the whole email inside one honest "up to 50% off" narrative.
- 2. Retire the pink ribbon hero treatment for at least a few sends — it's the second time in two weeks, and repetition this close together reads as template laziness rather than a fresh campaign.
- 3. Swap the preheader to reference the specific discount and product mix, not generic seasonal copy.
- 4. Add a stock/size urgency cue on the Coca-Cola Samba collab (e.g., "selling fast") given collabs are the strongest click driver in this email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off Sambas, Adizero & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall Kickoff: prices just dropped up to 50%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Handball Spezials, Adizero Evo, and the Coca-Cola Samba — all discounted.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No code needed. Shop men's, women's and kids' markdowns now.`
