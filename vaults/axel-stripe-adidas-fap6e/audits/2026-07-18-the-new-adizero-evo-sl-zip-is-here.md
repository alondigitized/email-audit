# The new ADIZERO Evo SL ZIP is here
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- The ADIZERO EVO SL ZIP launch is a clean, well-art-directed product introduction split across two beats: a new track-heritage sneaker with a tongue-zip closure and a Strava x adiClub member benefit unlock. The action photography is strong and the two-section structure keeps the email from overstaying its welcome. The Strava partnership is the most commercially interesting element here — a Strava subscription is a real, tangible reward for adiClub members, not just a logo collab slapped on a banner.
- The critical miss is commercial emptiness at the product level. No price, no discount, no urgency — the ADIZERO EVO SL ZIP section reads like a product catalog page that wandered into a marketing email. For a subscriber who just received the FIFA Final send earlier today (also July 18), this is the second adidas email on the same calendar day, which makes the cadence tax real. The prior July 15 App-exclusive sale send already had a CTA mismatch problem; this one compounds it with a different failure mode — a launch send with nothing to act on besides curiosity.
- Judgement: Decent product email with a strong partnership add-on, but it punts on the commercial fundamentals and lands on a day adidas already spent its audience's attention.

## What's working

- **Origin story is concrete and differentiating.** "Inspired by a 2000s adidas track & field shoe, featuring a tongue-length zip for expressive design and enhanced lockdown" is a real design detail — it actually explains *why* the ZIP exists.
- **Strava x adiClub section delivers subscriber value.** "Unlock a Strava subscription with adiClub" is a tangible member benefit, not a badge partnership.
- **Dual CTAs serve two different buyer modes** — "SHOP NOW" for e-commerce, "FIND STORE NOW" for in-person — without cluttering the layout.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight.** Hero → product name → short body copy → CTAs → partnership block; the eye moves naturally.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum bleeding through.

## What's weak

- **No price, no offer, no urgency.** The entire product section asks for a click with zero financial hook — not even an MSRP anchor or an "available now" with any scarcity signal.
- **Second adidas email on July 18.** The FIFA Final send already went out today; this email arrives right behind it and will feel like inbox crowding for anyone who opened both.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic to the point of forgettable.** The tongue-zip is the differentiating design detail; the CTA doesn't reference it at all.
- **Hero skews hardcore athletic.** The action/track photography signals a performance product, which may not resonate with a lifestyle or casual footwear buyer who shops adidas for the Sambas and Gazelles.
- **Preheader appears to leak a view-in-browser link** — the top of the render shows small gray text that reads as navigation junk rather than copy, wasting prime inbox preview real estate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price or an access hook.** Even "Available now from $[X]" or "Be first to shop" early-access framing gives the reader a reason to click today instead of mentally filing it for later.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to earn the zip.** "SHOP THE ZIP →" or "SEE THE ZIP DETAIL →" costs nothing and connects the button to the product's actual story.
- 3. **Don't double-send on the same day.** The FIFA Final send was a well-timed, high-stakes email — let it breathe before following with a product launch. Tomorrow would've been fine.
- 4. **Lead with the origin story in the subject.** The "2000s track & field" angle is more emotionally interesting than "is here" launch language.
- 5. **Surface the Strava unlock earlier in the subject or preheader.** A free Strava sub for adiClub members is the most concrete value in this email — it shouldn't be buried in the second section.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `adiClub members: unlock a free Strava subscription inside.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Adidas ADIZERO Evo SL ZIP Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: one broken click-redirect producing a non-routable destination URL, and a completely absent plain-text MIME part. Additionally, multiple images are served over HTTP and unsubscribe headers are missing from the relay capture, creating deliverability and compliance risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect**
The QA link checker reports `unknown url type: '/g/4bfd09dd-a06b-4fc5-9789-2b81320f5354'` for the outer URL:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/4bfd09dd-a06b-4fc5-9789-2b81320f5354?cp_tp=v3...
```
The redirect resolver is returning a relative path (`/g/...`) instead of an absolute destination URL. Any subscriber clicking this CTA lands nowhere. Investigate the `dv.adidas.com` redirect service for this specific `txn=ff149000-82c2-11f1-8333-22bb2a244d04`.

**[INFO] 29 tracking links unprobed**
Click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP prober — destination URL correctness for the remaining CTAs is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 6× repeated HTTP image source (will be blocked)**
The arrow decoration image is referenced six times over HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and most modern clients block mixed-content HTTP images. The arrow will render as a broken image in most environments.

**[WARN] Open/tracking pixel over HTTP**
```
http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f75f7a44b-3d507c21-ff74-41f3-b073-1f350e178491-000000/hDG_U...
```
Same mixed-content issue; open tracking may also be suppressed.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on two images**
- Tracking pixel: `src https://dv.adidas.com/o/4bfd09dd...` — no `alt` attribute
- Open pixel: `src http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` — no `alt` attribute

Even tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` to avoid accessibility scanner failures.

**[INFO] HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype**
`<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` — not a rendering failure, but some clients apply quirks-mode behavior. Not flagged as a bug given common ESP practice.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. No unfired merge token literals (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[NAME]`) visible in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to display the one-click unsubscribe button; its absence increases spam complaint rate and risks inbox demotion under Gmail's February 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
Without this header, one-click machine unsubscribes (as opposed to redirect-based) are not supported. Gmail requires both headers for bulk senders.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm the sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is properly authenticated.

**[INFO] `ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` present** — correct; prevents web-view indexing.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` is set in `<head>`**
This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound clicks. UTM query parameters in link `href` values will still pass to landing pages, so GA4/campaign attribution should be unaffected — but any server-side referrer logging at adidas.com will see no origin. Confirm this is intentional; if not, remove or change to `origin-when-cross-origin`.

**Broken CTA has zero site continuity**
The `dv.adidas.com/o/4bfd09dd...` link failure (Section 2) means at least one CTA delivers no landing page at all. If this is a primary product CTA, it is a direct conversion loss.

**Destination alignment for 29 remaining links unverified**
No HTTP probes were run against click-redirect domains, so landing page / UTM alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit.
