# Welcome Penn State
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a launch email for an adidas x Penn State collegiate collection, not a promo — no discount, no urgency, just "here's the new gear." The execution is clean: a tight hero shot of three people in Nittany Lions blue-and-white gear sitting in the stands, a bold "BLUE, WHITE, 3-STRIPES" headline, and two unambiguous "SHOP NOW" buttons. It reads like a well-produced but generic template that's been re-skinned for a specific school.
- The problem is targeting, not craft. Compared to the July 19-23 run (World Cup victory, Samba Metallic, BTS-Samba fusion — all scored 8-10), this send swaps a broad, high-interest hook for a single-school licensing play. If the recipient has no Penn State affiliation, this email has almost nothing to say to them — no general BTS angle, no sitewide offer, no seasonal urgency, just "Nittany Lions, welcome to adidas."
- Craft-wise it's solid; relevance-wise it's a coin flip that depends entirely on whether the subscriber is a PSU alum, student, or fan. As a generic subscriber with no demonstrated Penn State tie, this lands as a well-made email for someone else's inbox.

## What's working

- Hero image is a strong, on-brand casual/lifestyle shot — stadium seats, jersey numbers, natural poses, no stock-photo stiffness.
- Two clear CTAs ("SHOP NOW" banner up top, "SHOP NOW" button at the bottom) — no ambiguity about the next step.
- Clean visual hierarchy: banner → hero image → headline → body copy → CTA, no clutter, no overlapping elements.
- Preview text ("The newest chapter of Penn State excellence is here at adidas.") adds context instead of restating the subject verbatim.
- Simple three-color palette (navy/white/black) keeps the Penn State branding legible against adidas's own black-and-white system.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing that gives a reason to act now versus later.
- No urgency of any kind; this reads as evergreen collection copy that could run any week.
- CTA copy is generic ("SHOP NOW") rather than specific to the collection ("Shop Penn State Gear").
- No product detail — no price, sizing, or availability shown, so there's nothing to evaluate before clicking.
- Extremely narrow targeting: this only works for subscribers with a Penn State affinity, and there's no visible reason to believe adidas confirmed that before sending.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Confirm segmentation before send** — if this went to non-PSU-affiliated subscribers, that's the single biggest lever here; a mistargeted "Welcome Penn State" email to someone with no tie to the school reads as noise, not as personalization.
- 2. **Add a launch incentive** — even a modest "15% off your first Penn State piece" would convert this from an announcement into an action-driving email.
- 3. **Make the CTA product-specific** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop Penn State Gear" so the button itself confirms the destination.
- 4. **Show product, not just lifestyle** — a small product grid (jersey, hoodie, cap) under the hero would let people self-select instead of clicking blind into a full collection page.
