# F50 Sparkfusion is designed for her
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine women's product-launch email — Trinity Rodman fronting the F50 Sparkfusion boot, a specific women's-fit callout ("designed for the game's fastest women"), and a real product shot with a distinctive zebra-inspired iridescent colorway. It's more substantive than the barebones sitewide-sale sends this brand keeps reheating (2026-08-19 "Collaborations Archive Sale," 2026-08-21 "40% off") and closer in quality to the Messi jersey drop from 2026-08-20 — athlete, product, and a clear persona angle all present in one send.
- Where it falls down is structure: it bolts a completely unrelated $50-for-$40 gift card offer onto the bottom of an athlete/product story, diluting the "designed for her" narrative the top two-thirds worked hard to build. There's also a stray, unstyled "Shop the Trinity Rodman player pack" link sitting above the header like a preheader leak, and three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons in a row give zero visual differentiation between browsing the boot, browsing the women's line, and buying a gift card.
- Net: a real product story undercut by a bolted-on unrelated offer and a top-of-email rendering glitch. Solidly mid-pack — better than this brand's typical filler, but not a clean execution.

## What's working

- Trinity Rodman is a recognizable, credible athlete tie-in and the hero shot (her holding the boot, arms crossed, direct gaze) is strong and on-brand.
- Product photography on the F50 Sparkfusion itself is genuinely distinctive — the iridescent zebra-pattern midsole stands out from prior generic sends in this brand's history.
- Explicit persona targeting: "ALL HERS" / "designed for the game's fastest women" directly signals this is a women's-specific drop, not a repurposed unisex email.
- Clean black "SHOP NOW" CTA buttons are consistent and easy to spot throughout.

## What's weak

- A raw, unstyled text link ("Shop the Trinity Rodman player pack.") sits above the adidas logo — looks like a rendering bug or leaked preheader, not intentional design.
- The gift-card offer at the bottom is a jarring non-sequitur — it has nothing to do with the F50 Sparkfusion or Trinity Rodman and breaks the narrative thread of the email.
- Three near-identical black "SHOP NOW" buttons in a row give no visual hierarchy — a reader can't tell at a glance which one leads to the featured boot versus the general women's line versus the gift card.
- No price is shown anywhere for the actual hero product — a reader interested in the boot has to click blind to find out what it costs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Remove or properly style the orphaned "Shop the Trinity Rodman player pack" link at the top — it currently reads as a broken element, not a CTA.
- 2. Cut the gift-card module entirely, or move it to its own separate send — it dilutes a strong single-product story and this brand already runs gift-card promos elsewhere.
- 3. Add a visible price (or "from $X") near the hero product shot so the CTA has a concrete reason to click.
- 4. Differentiate the three CTA buttons with distinct copy ("Shop the Boot," "Shop Women's F50," "Get the Gift Card") instead of three identical "SHOP NOW" labels.

# Technical Audit: adidas "F50 Sparkfusion" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)-generated responsive HTML email with heavy media-query scaffolding; primary defects are a missing plain-text MIME part and several non-HTTPS/missing-alt image references, not structural markup errors.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- The QA tool's "doubled-domain" FAIL findings on `https://click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.adidas.com%2Fus%2Fhelp%2F...` are a **false positive**: this is the standard SFMC `CL0` click-tracking wrapper format (`click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/<url-encoded destination>/<job-id>/<subscriber-hash>/<token>`), not a literal repeated-domain merge error. The destination domain (`www.adidas.com`) is single-encoded inside the tracking domain (`click.comms.adidas.com`), which is expected behavior for SFMC Email Studio. No action needed.
- 24 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober by design (expected for `click.comms.adidas.com` / `image.link.adidas.com` CDN and redirect domains) — no conclusion can be drawn on link validity from this run; recommend a manual click-through pass if link integrity needs confirming.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Two images are missing `alt` text: `aa002b32-b176-4334-81d3-adaf9d6008ba` (`dv.adidas.com` hero/product image) and the tracking pixel/image served via `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`. Screen readers and image-blocked clients will have no fallback description for the hero image specifically.
- The "arrow" image (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is served over **plain HTTP** (`http://image.link.adidas.com/...`), flagged 5x in the QA pass (likely referenced in multiple breakpoint/`<img>` variants). Mixed-content blocking in Outlook/webmail clients over HTTPS may suppress this image.
- The `CI0` tracking image is also served over HTTP (`http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`) — same mixed-content risk, and doubles as the missing-alt-text tracking pixel noted above.
