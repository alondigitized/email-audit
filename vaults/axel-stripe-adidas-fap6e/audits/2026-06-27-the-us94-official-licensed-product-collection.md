# The US94 Official Licensed Product Collection
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the second US94 Official Licensed Product send in seven days — adidas ran this same concept on June 20, and this iteration swaps the body imagery for the "FROM '94 TO '25" hero graphic and staples a $30 off $100+ discount section beneath the heritage story. The result is a split-identity email: a brand-values piece in the first half, a promotional trigger in the second. Neither half benefits from the other. A subscriber who opened the June 20 version is now looking at what feels like a near-repeat with a coupon appended.
- The production quality is clean. The US94 hero graphic lands first, the CTAs are well-organized (SHOP NOW → SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026 → FIND STORE NOW), and the discount offer is legible. There's no visual damage to the brand. But "clean execution of a confused brief" is the honest read.
- The cadence is the real problem. The brand history here shows that adidas runs the same concept multiple times in quick succession — Summer Kickoff ran June 3 and June 12, and now US94 runs June 20 and June 27. A subscriber who is paying attention will feel the pattern. The email is serviceable, but the strategic decision to re-send this concept without a meaningfully differentiated angle is what keeps this from landing above a 7.

## What's working

- The "FROM '94 TO '25" hero typography is bold, legible, and emotionally clear — the graphic does more work than the subject line.
- Three CTAs at different depths (collection, FIFA hub, store locator) cover different purchase intents in one send.
- The $30 off $100+ offer with code DEAL is concrete and the body copy confirms the terms inline — no burying the mechanics.
- The adidas wordmark and FIFA World Cup 2026 co-branding give the collection institutional gravity that the June 20 send leaned on more lightly.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue**: this is the same US94 concept seven days after the June 20 send with no meaningfully differentiated entry point. A subscriber who opened that one will recognize the repeat.
- **Split email personality**: the SPEND LESS GET MORE discount section is a different campaign grafted on below the fold; the two halves don't reinforce each other.
- **Subject line wastes the discount**: the body has a real offer ($30 off $100+) but the subject reads like a product announcement, not a promotional trigger. The offer is invisible at inbox level.
- **Preview text ("For the memories and beyond") is a tagline**, not a hook — it doesn't add information that would move someone from scanning to opening.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the offer.** The $30 off $100+ is the clearest reason to click. A product-name subject is fine for a launch send; the second send of the same collection needs a reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `US94 Collection: Save $30 on $100+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$30 off the US94 drop — use code DEAL`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heritage style, summer pricing. Shop the US94 Official Licensed Product Collection.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $100, save $30 on the collection that brought American soccer culture back.`
- 2. **Integrate the discount into the US94 hero, don't append it.** The "SPEND LESS GET MORE" section breaks the storytelling. One unified section — US94 hero image, copy, and the offer inline — is stronger than two disconnected blocks.
- 3. **Differentiate this send from June 20.** If adidas is running two US94 sends in a week, the second one needs a sharper angle: specific product highlights, a sell-through urgency message, or the FIFA 2026 co-branding elevated to the hero rather than buried in a secondary CTA.
- 4. **Shorten and sharpen the hero copy.** "Footwear comes and goes, but the attitude lasts forever" is fine brand voice but takes up real estate without telling you what to buy or why now.

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken redirect link and a missing plain-text fallback are confirmed hard failures. Nine HTTP image sources and two missing alt attributes are confirmed rendering/accessibility issues. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, requiring sender-side verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — malformed internal path**
The QA resolver surfaced `unknown url type: '/g/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?txn=5816b280-74d3-11f1-a016-9651305e4573'` for the click-tracking URL that resolves through `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...`. The `/g/` path is being parsed as a relative URL, indicating the adidas dynamic content redirector (dv.adidas.com) is returning a relative location header rather than an absolute URL. This will produce a dead link in clients that do not resolve relative redirects. Confirmed URL:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

**[WARN] 35 tracking links not probed** — all route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Not an error, but means link health for the bulk of CTAs is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] 9× HTTP image sources — mixed content**
The arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is referenced **8 times** via plain HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
A ninth HTTP image is the open/track pixel routed via `click.comms.adidas.com`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default; the arrow will silently fail to render in these clients.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text**
- Dynamic content image: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?...`
- Tracking pixel: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f07429253-...`

The first is a visible content image; missing `alt` breaks accessibility for screen readers and degrades images-off rendering.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice — once in the main `<style>` block and again in the Yahoo Android App comment block, with identical `src: local(...)` fallbacks. No web font URLs are present in either declaration; both custom font families silently fall back to Arial/Helvetica in all clients. Not a breakage, but the declarations are dead weight.

**[INFO] Legacy DOCTYPE**
`HTML 4.01 Transitional` is used. Gmail strips the `<html>` and `<head>` anyway, but this DOCTYPE triggers quirks-mode parsing in some webmail clients. Modern email best practice is to omit DOCTYPE or use the HTML5 doctype.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent at delivery, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5K/day senders) and CAN-SPAM best practice. The relay may have stripped it — sender must confirm via raw message headers at the MTA.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024) cannot be confirmed. Same caveat about relay stripping applies.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unknown due to relay interception. Sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` must be verified to have valid DKIM signature and SPF alignment independently.

**[INFO] `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` present** — correct practice for email HTML.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` present** — suppresses referrer on link clicks, which is intentional but means UTM attribution relies entirely on link parameters.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `dv.adidas.com/o/` redirect (§2) is the primary continuity risk — if the `/g/` relative redirect is not resolved server-side, users clicking that CTA reach a dead end rather than the product landing page.
