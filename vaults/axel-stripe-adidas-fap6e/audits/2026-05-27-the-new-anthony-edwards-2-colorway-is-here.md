# The new Anthony Edwards 2 colorway is here 🏀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's second Anthony Edwards 2 email in three days — the "Believe That" Red & Black launch hit on May 25 and scored 5/10 for the same structural weaknesses. This time the brief is a kids-exclusive colorway, and the creative is nearly identical: large shoe hero, performance tech module, black "SHOP NOW" button, same copy cadence. The segmentation failure is the whole story. A kids-exclusive product sent to a general adult subscriber list is a mismatch that clean layout cannot rescue. Adults who follow AE2 will open out of brand habit, land on a kids shoe page, and bounce.
- The execution itself is competent: no render bugs, solid visual hierarchy, specific tech claims ("Propulsion Plate," "Articulated Fango," "Lightboost + LightStrike") that actually do product work. But without a price reduction, a deadline, or any adult angle, there is nothing to convert on. The store-limited rollout (four doors: NY Broadway, NY Fifth Avenue, Houston Galleria, Glendale Galleria) is buried in fine print — limiting the addressable audience even further without being upfront about it.
- Against this week's cadence — the 30% off blast (May 25), the voucher expiry (May 26), now a kids-only launch (May 27) — this is the weakest of the three. Segment it to a parent/family list, surface the store-limited scarcity, and it becomes a 7+. As sent to a general list, it's a mismatch that erodes subscriber trust faster than it moves product.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns the fold**: The AE2 shoe shot is well-lit, large, and the color/angle combination is visually clean.
- **Two-module layout works logically**: Buy-first (hero + CTA), then learn (Innovation Meets Performance) — a sensible flow for both impulse and considered buyers.
- **Tech copy is specific**: Naming actual technologies ("Articulated Fango for control") beats generic hype and signals genuine product confidence.
- **Subject line is the strongest asset**: Concrete, emoji-accented, 43 characters — pulls opens from AE2 followers before the kids reveal hits.
- **No render bugs**: Clean layout, button visible, no overlapping text or broken image gaps.

## What's weak

- **Kids exclusive sent to a general adult list**: "KIDS EXCLUSIVE" as the H1 tells adult subscribers this isn't for them within two seconds of opening.
- **Preheader front-loads the segment mismatch**: "The latest kids exclusives." appears before any shoe copy — adults see "kids" before they see "AE2."
- **Second AE2 email in three days, same template**: Same product, same layout, different colorway. The May 25 send already established the AE2 frame; this reads as a duplicate.
- **No offer of any kind**: No discount, no free shipping, no deadline. Full-price shop is the only action available.
- **Store availability buried**: Four specific retail doors mentioned only in the fine print at the bottom — sets up wasted clicks from subscribers in other markets.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment to parent/family list only.** This email has no business going to adult sneaker subscribers without kids context. Filter by household with children or prior youth-size purchase behavior, and both open rate and conversion improve meaningfully.
- 2. **Surface store scarcity in the body, not the footnote.** "Available now at 4 select adidas stores" as a visible callout — not fine print — reframes a limitation as exclusivity and creates genuine urgency.
- 3. **Break the template sameness.** Two back-to-back emails with identical visual structure trains subscribers to skip. Lead with athlete-and-kid content, invert the color palette, do something that doesn't look like Friday's email.
- 4. **Add a size-range anchor or age callout.** "Kids sizes 3.5Y–7Y" gives parents an immediate relevance check and a concrete reason to click — even without a discount.
- **Subject Alt A:** `AE2 just dropped in kids sizes 🏀`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The AE2 your kid will actually want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New colorway built for young ballers — 4 select stores now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids sizes 3.5Y–7Y. Four doors. Shop the drop.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken URLs — one a doubled-domain redirect bug, one a malformed merge token — will send a subset of clicks to error pages. Plain-text fallback is absent (0 chars), which harms deliverability and is a common spam-filter trigger.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Doubled-domain redirect** (2 flagged instances, same URL):
```
https://click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.adidas.com%2Fus%2Fstores/1/...
```
The click-tracker URL wraps an already-absolute `https://www.adidas.com/us/stores` destination. When the tracker resolves the redirect, it will double-expand to a broken path. Affects the link labelled *"Shop online or at select adidas stores.*"*

**[FAIL] Malformed merge token / broken internal URL:**
```
/g/36d4ab07-4312-4bba-8847-fa349fa15d46?txn=31da9000-59c6-11f1-b774-8e0ec7ba23ea
```
This relative path was detected as an unknown URL type inside the `dv.adidas.com` redirect chain. The merge token produced a relative URL instead of an absolute one, making the link unresolvable from an email client context.

**[WARN] 25 tracking links** via `click.comms.adidas.com` were skipped from HTTP probe — not confirmed broken, but unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× HTTP (non-HTTPS) image source** — same arrow asset repeated:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy/block mixed-content images. This asset will render as a broken image in clients with strict HTTPS enforcement.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text:**
- `36d4ab07-4312-4bba-8847-fa349fa15d46` (dv.adidas.com tracking pixel / dynamic image)
- Tracking pixel via `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`

Both are likely tracking pixels, but missing `alt=""` (empty string) means screen readers may announce the raw URL. Convention for non-content images is `alt=""`.

**CSS note:** `@font-face` declarations for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` map `src` entirely to `local()` system fonts — no remote webfont is loaded. This is intentional for email compatibility, but means the custom typeface never renders unless the user has it installed locally (unlikely). Not a bug, but worth confirming this is deliberate.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

This suggests the merge/personalization layer ran after URL encoding, or a conditional block emitted a partial path.

No other unresolved `{{token}}` or `%7B%7B` patterns detected in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

If the header is genuinely absent from the original SMTP envelope, Gmail and Yahoo Mail (which enforce one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders ≥5k/day since Feb 2024) will suppress the native unsubscribe button.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058) not found** — one-click POST unsubscribe is required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance. If this is a high-volume send, this is a deliverability and policy-compliance risk, not just a warning.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unknown via relay. Cannot confirm alignment for `us-news.comms.adidas.com` sending domain.

**HTML compliance:** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — correct for email to suppress indexing and referrer leakage.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link presence in the body were not verifiable from the truncated source; assumed present given the footer CSS classes (`mobile-footer-padding-block`, `mobile-privacy-transactional`).
