# Two icons come together ahead of FIFA World Cup 26™
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's launch email for the adidas Originals x Coca-Cola collab tied to FIFA World Cup 26™. The hero earns its place — "IT'S THE REAL THING" is a clever co-opt of Coca-Cola's most famous tagline, and the red/white diamond pattern immediately communicates both brands without needing to spell it out. The cultural logic is sound: two global icons, one global tournament.
- The structural problem is the email doesn't trust that collab story enough to stand on its own. Below the fold, the exact same "SUMMER SAVINGS — 30% OFF FOR THE SEASON" block from the June 30 send ("First chance to dive into 30% off") is stapled on verbatim. That email already introduced SAVE code at 30%. This one doesn't escalate the offer, doesn't add urgency, and doesn't acknowledge three days have passed. The subscriber who opened June 30 already knows the deal — re-serving it beneath a premium collab announcement undercuts both messages.
- Four CTAs before the footer (SHOP NOW → SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026™ → FIND STORE NOW → SHOP NOW again) is too many decision points. Pick a lane: this is either a collab launch email or a Summer Savings reminder. Right now it's trying to be both and fully optimizing for neither.

## What's working

- **"IT'S THE REAL THING" headline** — earned co-branding that lands without any logo explanation; the red/white argyle pattern reads Coca-Cola instantly.
- **FIFA World Cup 26 hook** — gives the collab a live cultural moment rather than a generic lifestyle drop.
- **30% off with a visible code (SAVE)** — concrete offer clearly rendered, no ambiguity for a subscriber who reaches it.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds at this viewport.

## What's weak

- **Subject buries the brands** — "Two icons" is intriguing but name-dropping Coca-Cola in the subject would have driven significantly higher open rates; the curiosity hook sacrifices specificity.
- **Preview text is completely disconnected** — "Explore the latest adidas web setters" says nothing about the FIFA collab or the discount; dead preheader real estate.
- **Summer Savings block is verbatim copy from June 30** — no escalation, no new urgency signal; a subscriber who opened three days ago gets no reason to act differently today.
- **Three CTAs in the collab section alone** — SHOP NOW + SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026™ + FIND STORE NOW creates decision paralysis before the main offer is even reached.
- **FIND STORE NOW deflects online conversion** — no reason to push foot traffic in the middle of a promo email unless inventory is store-exclusive, which isn't signaled.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name both brands in the subject.** The collab is the news. "adidas x Coca-Cola is real — ahead of FIFA World Cup 26" gives subscribers a concrete reason to open. Mystery works only when the brand is seeding intrigue; here the drop is real and deserves the spotlight.
- **Subject Alt A:** `adidas × Coca-Cola: IT'S THE REAL THING`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The collab FIFA World Cup needed`
- 2. **Write a preheader that earns the open.** "Explore the latest adidas web setters" is a wasted impression. Tie it to the collab or the offer.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Coca-Cola drop lands just in time for the World Cup`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two icons. One collection. 30% off sitewide too.`
- 3. **Cut to two CTAs maximum in the collab section.** SHOP THE COLLAB + SHOP ALL FIFA — drop FIND STORE NOW entirely unless brick-and-mortar exclusivity is a real hook.
- 4. **Escalate or reframe the Summer Savings offer.** If SAVE is still 30%, give it a deadline ("48 hours left") or a new angle ("Stock is moving — sizes going fast"). Same-offer, day-3 with no change is a trust cost.
- 5. **Lead with the collab's most distinctive product.** If the Samba or a specific hero shoe is the centerpiece, show it featured with its name visible. Right now the image shows the apparel/lifestyle context but the most clickable product isn't highlighted.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is adidas's launch email for the adidas Originals x Coca-Cola collab tied to FIFA World Cup 26™. The hero earns its place — "IT'S THE REAL THING" is a clever co-opt of Coca-Cola's most famous tagline, and the red/white diamond pattern immediately communicates both brands without needing to spell it out. The cultural logic is sound: two global icons, one global tournament.

The structural problem is the email doesn't trust that collab story enough to stand on its own. Below the fold, the exact same "SUMMER SAVINGS — 30% OFF FOR THE SEASON" block from the June 30 send ("First chance to dive into 30% off") is stapled on verbatim. That email already introduced SAVE code at 30%. This one doesn't escalate the offer, doesn't add urgency, and doesn't acknowledge three days have passed. The subscriber who opened June 30 already knows the deal — re-serving it beneath a premium collab announcement undercuts both messages.

Four CTAs before the footer (SHOP NOW → SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026™ → FIND STORE NOW → SHOP NOW again) is too many decision points. Pick a lane: this is either a collab launch email or a Summer Savings reminder. Right now it's trying to be both and fully optimizing for neither.

1. **Name both brands in the subject.** The collab is the news. "adidas x Coca-Cola is real — ahead of FIFA World Cup 26" gives subscribers a concrete reason to open. Mystery works only when the brand is seeding intrigue; here the drop is real and deserves the spotlight.

   - **Preheader Alt A:** `The Coca-Cola drop lands just in time for the World Cup`

3. **Cut to two CTAs maximum in the collab section.** SHOP THE COLLAB + SHOP ALL FIFA — drop FIND STORE NOW entirely unless brick-and-mortar exclusivity is a real hook.

4. **Escalate or reframe the Summer Savings offer.** If SAVE is still 30%, give it a deadline ("48 hours left") or a new angle ("Stock is moving — sizes going fast"). Same-offer, day-3 with no change is a trust cost.

5. **Lead with the collab's most distinctive product.** If the Samba or a specific hero shoe is the centerpiece, show it featured with its name visible. Right now the image shows the apparel/lifestyle context but the most clickable product isn't highlighted.
