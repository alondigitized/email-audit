# The race-day supershoe is here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident single-product launch email for the Adizero Adios Pro 5 — adidas' flagship marathon racing shoe — and it's a strong, focused execution. One hero image (a dynamic mid-stride shot of the shoe in motion against a moody teal background), one clear headline, tight benefit copy with real numbers (9% more energy return, 23g lighter), and two "Shop Now" CTAs. It reads like a premium performance-running send, not a generic sale blast.
- The tradeoff is audience width: this is a niche product (a race-day carbon-plate marathon shoe) presented with zero context for anyone who isn't already a serious runner — no price, no "who this is for," no sizing/fit note, no social proof. Compared to the 2026-09-18 Anthony Edwards 3 send, which had similar single-hero polish, this one leans even more heavily into technical spec-dropping and less into aspirational lifestyle framing.
- Verdict: well-crafted for its narrow target (performance runners), but that narrowness caps both broad appeal and clickability for anyone outside that niche.

## What's working

- Single powerful hero image with authentic motion-blur running action, on-brand red colorway that pops against the teal background.
- Headline and subhead are concrete and benefit-driven ("record-breaking innovation," "9% more energy return," "23g lighter") rather than vague marketing fluff.
- Two clearly placed "Shop Now" CTAs (top banner + bottom black button) with unambiguous copy and arrow affordance.
- No render bugs — image, type, and buttons all display cleanly with good spacing.
- Preheader ("The evolution of adidas marathon racing is here") sets context without repeating the subject verbatim.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere — for a premium supershoe this is a real friction point; performance shoppers often decide based on price point (these typically run $250+).
- No sizing, fit, or availability info despite this being a performance category where fit obsesses the buyer.
- No social proof (athlete endorsement, race results, reviews) despite this being positioned as a record-breaking product — a stat like "used by X elite marathoners" would add major credibility.
- Generic "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" top banner undercuts the single-product focus — it's a mismatched, recycled module that doesn't reinforce urgency or the Adios Pro 5 story.
- No sense of urgency or newness cue (e.g., "just dropped," launch date, limited release) beyond "has just landed" in body copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price and a direct product link/CTA copy like "Shop Adios Pro 5 — $250" so runners can qualify interest immediately without clicking through.
- 2. Replace the generic "best sellers" top banner with a launch-specific module (e.g., "NEW: Race Day Ready" or a countdown to a marathon season tie-in) to keep the whole email in the same narrative.
- 3. Add one social-proof line — an elite athlete quote, marathon PR stat, or "as worn at Boston/Chicago Marathon" — to build credibility for the "record-breaking" claim.
- 4. Add a secondary line about fit/sizing or a "true to size" note, since supershoe buyers care deeply about this.
