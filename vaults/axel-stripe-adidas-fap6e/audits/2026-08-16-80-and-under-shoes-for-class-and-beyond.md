# $80 and under shoes for class and beyond
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- This one actually has a clean, honest structure — a single price-point offer ($80-and-under school shoes) up top, with a genuinely separate adiClub giveaway module below, clearly demarcated by its own image and heading rather than crammed into the same story. Compared to the "Samba Goes Metallic" or Real Madrid jersey sends, this doesn't pretend to be one narrative — it's two distinct blocks and reads that way, which is a relief after a run of bolted-together emails.
- The hero shot is solid: three classic silhouettes (Sambas in black and red, a Gazelle-adjacent white/black pair) staged on concrete steps, backpack strap visible in frame, which ties nicely to the "class and beyond" back-to-school premise. But the offer itself is generic — "$80 and under" isn't a discount, it's just a price ceiling on unspecified products, so there's no actual percentage off or urgency to anchor a decision.
- Overall this sits in the middle of the pack — better organized than the recent two-story sends, but the core value prop is soft and the giveaway competes for the one CTA slot a shopper actually cares about.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean and on-brand — three shoe styles staged on stairs, no render bugs, no overlapping text.
- The "$80 and under" price-point angle is clear and immediately followed by two well-labeled CTAs (Shop Men / Shop Women).
- The adiClub giveaway is visually separated from the shoe sale rather than mashed into one story, unlike recent sends.
- Top black banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") gives a secondary, low-friction entry point.
- Legal/footer block and unsubscribe link are present and legible.

## What's weak

- "$80 and under" is a price ceiling, not a discount — no percent off, no dollar savings, no sense of what's actually on sale versus just cheap.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email — nothing time-bound to justify opening now versus next week.
- Gender-split CTAs (Shop Men / Shop Women) without any product preview under each means the shopper has to leave the email to find out if anything in their size/style is actually included.
- The giveaway section dilutes the primary offer — two competing CTAs (Shop Men/Women vs. Enter Now) split attention in one email.
- "School shoes" framing is vague — no kids/youth sizing signal, no specific silhouette called out as "the one for back to school."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach a real number to the offer.** Even "styles from $55–$80" or "up to 30% off" gives shoppers a reason to click now instead of "maybe later." This is the single biggest lever — right now there's no discount signal at all.
- 2. **Cut the giveaway from this send or move it to its own email.** It's well-executed on its own, but pairing it with a sale email splits the CTA and this brand already has a pattern (per history) of two-job emails underperforming.
- 3. **Add a deadline** ("Sale ends Sunday," "Back to school pricing through 8/22") to create urgency — nothing here is time-bound.
- 4. **Show 2-3 product thumbnails with prices under $80** rather than just Shop Men/Shop Women buttons, so the value is provable before the click.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Save on Sambas, Gazelles & more — $80 and under`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on classic styles, while sizes last.`
