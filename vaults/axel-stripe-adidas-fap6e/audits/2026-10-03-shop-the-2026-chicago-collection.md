# Shop the 2026 Chicago collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-message race-week send for the Chicago Marathon. The hero is a man and a woman running along dark blue water, wearing ADIZERO gear. It's atmospheric and on-brand. The headline "2026 CHICAGO COLLECTION" is big and obvious, and one black "SHOP NOW" button sits below it.
- The email has no offer, no price, no member benefit and no deadline. The only reason to click is the race and the shoe, and the only product named is the ADIZERO Adios Pro 5. It also repeats a pattern from this week: since 9/28, adidas has sent a launch email nearly every day, including "Spots 🤝 Samba" on 10/2, the Adizero Legacy Pack on 9/30 and the Adizero Horizon cleat on 9/29. This one is better timed than most because the race is close, but it's the same format with no reason to act now.
- Judgement: well made and forgettable. It's a brand-awareness send that would be stronger with a product image, a date and a specific shoe to click.

## What's working

- The hero photo is moody and cinematic, and it sets the running tone immediately.
- The headline is large and the hierarchy is clean: image, title, one sentence, one button.
- The CTA is a high-contrast black button that is easy to find.
- The Chicago tie-in is timely. The Marathon is days away, so this reads as current rather than evergreen.
- The preheader names the shoe, "ADIZERO Adios Pro 5. Built for Chicago.", which adds information the subject lacks.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, member perk or deadline, so nothing creates urgency.
- The hero shows runners, but the shoe is tiny and hard to see. The Adios Pro 5 is named only in a single line of body copy.
- The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top competes with the Chicago message and sends people to a generic destination. Its black bar is also offset from the image beneath it.
- "SHOP NOW" is generic. It doesn't say what the click leads to.
- The email stops after one button. There is no product grid, no specs (carbon plate, weight, drop), no reviews and no race date.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the shoe and the date.** Replace or supplement the lifestyle photo with a clear product shot of the Adios Pro 5, and add the race date, for example "Chicago Marathon: Oct 11".
- 2. **Add a reason to act.** Use an adiClub early-access perk, free shipping or a stated delivery-by-race-day cutoff. Even a soft deadline such as "Order by Oct 6 to have them for race day" gives the click a reason.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific.** Change "SHOP NOW" to "Shop the Adios Pro 5" and link it straight to the shoe's product page.
- 4. **Cut or move the best-sellers banner.** It dilutes the single message, so put it below the fold.
- 5. **Add proof.** One line of spec detail or a review pull-quote would help a runner decide.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by Oct 6 and they arrive before the start line.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `https://dv.adidas.com/o/c99f49af-1b10-4ae3-bfe3-4a37923b9ad1?cp_tp=…` has no `alt`. The tracking pixel above has no alt text either, though that one is expected for a pixel.
