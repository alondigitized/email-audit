# We’ve got something for you
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is an adiClub rewards email dressed up as a personal note. It has a points-balance bar (Level 1, 50 points) and a headline saying something is "waiting for you." Underneath is a four-tile grid of free giveaways and a Strava partnership. The layout is clean and the tiles line up, but the "something" is a raffle entry, not a reward or a discount.
- The strongest thing here is the membership framing. The personalized points strip and the "free to enter" line tell a low-balance member that they can still take part. The weakest thing is the subject, "We've got something for you." It reads like a generic tease and says nothing about raffles. Coming six days after the 2026-09-23 "adiClub September Newsletter," it covers similar ground and may feel like a second pass at the same content.
- Verdict: a decent engagement play with no purchase pull. It will do fine with adiClub loyalists and do little for anyone shopping for product.

## What's working

- The points bar (Level 1, 50 points to spend) makes the email feel personal and tied to the member's account.
- The headline is bold, all caps and easy to read, and it leads straight into the explanation that the raffles are free.
- The 2x2 tile grid is tidy. Each tile has a GIVEAWAY or PARTNERSHIP tag, a one-line description and an underlined CTA.
- The Hyperboost Euphoria tile is the only tile with a product shot, so it works as the de facto hero.
- Tile CTAs use specific verbs, "Enter now" and "Connect accounts," rather than a vague "Discover."

## What's weak

- The subject line never mentions raffles, giveaways or adiClub, so it gives an inbox reader no reason to open.
- Three of the four tiles carry the identical "ENTER NOW" CTA. The Strava tile is a different ask and competes with the giveaways.
- The two lifestyle tiles (the jersey and the signed football) show people, not the prize. The football tile doesn't show which athlete or which product.
- There is no deadline on any of the raffles, so there is no urgency.
- The terms and conditions appear only as a raw URL in the footer, with no asterisk or note in the body. That is a lot of fine print for giveaways.
- The top-left "Your rewards are waiting" link is styled as plain serif link text, and it looks unfinished next to the branded design below.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the prize in the subject. Naming the specific prize (Hyperboost Euphoria, a signed F1 jersey) beats a vague tease.
- 2. Add a deadline to each giveaway, such as "Ends Oct 6." Urgency is the missing ingredient.
- 3. Pick one hero. Make the Hyperboost Euphoria giveaway a full-width block and demote the others, so the eye has one place to land.
- 4. Give the tiles distinct CTAs, such as "Enter to win shoes" and "Win a signed jersey." Move the Strava connect to a separate, lower-priority slot.
- 5. Put a short "Terms apply" line with an asterisk next to the giveaways, and keep the full URL in the footer.
- 6. Restyle or hide the top-left preheader link so it looks like part of the design.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win Hyperboost Euphoria, free to enter`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50 points? You can still win big`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free raffles for adiClub members: shoes, signed jerseys and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Enter this month's giveaways before they close.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is table-based with `role="presentation"`, `lang="en"` and responsive media queries, but it has a staging image host in production and about 18 images with no `alt` attribute. It also embeds third-party tracking pixels that carry subscriber identifiers. The compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the headers weren't captured, and the footer is outside the truncated source.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Staging host in production:** two images load from `https://image.staging.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/110/` (`cc57a950-573b-40d7-91f6-f59c435298a5.png` and `3fe183ac-1f44-41a9-86a4-4929a2610275.png`). Staging assets can be taken down or access-restricted without notice, which would leave broken images.
- **Unverified links:** the QA probe skipped 24 tracking links on click-redirect domains (for example `click.link.adidas.com`). Destination status is unverified.
- **Third-party pixels:** several `beacon.krxd.net` 1x1 GIFs sit in the body (`1x1_ad_impression.gif?confid=rks80khh5&advertiserid=10973357&campaignid=4404327` and multiple `1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LEzkELqx_sfmc_10973357_subid&partner_uid=1562263179`).
  - They carry no `alt` attribute.
  - Many clients block them.
  - They add external requests at open.
- **Open pixel:** `click.link.adidas.com/open.aspx?...` uses `alt=""` correctly.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** QA flagged about 18 images, including:
  - `mi.adidas.com/p/rp/*.png` (`6b9d62d608a69724.png`, `bc2af5c7133250b4.png`, `5ac14dd12c6a6d81.png`, `8167bc83715661b0.png`, `1439f5e8f638fac5.png`, `6eb99cf10b353ccc.png`, `e9ed28f35732a305.png`, `c128fed4045d16d6.png`, `403520c99c839bc0.png`)
  - the two staging `.png` files
  - repeated `mi.adidas.com/p/up/b7ca433f5d84cc1a/o.gif` images
  - If the `rp/` images are content (they look like rewards-point content), they need descriptive alt text. The `o.gif` files are tracking pixels and should have `alt=""`. Missing alt means screen readers may announce filenames, and clients that block images show nothing.
- **Page title:** `<title>adidas </title>` is non-descriptive and has a trailing space.
- **Doctype:** the HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype is legacy and not a defect, but it is worth noting.
- **Preheader:** `div.preheader` is empty, and the actual text ("Your rewards are waiting") sits in a separate hidden div. The empty preheader block is redundant.
- **Web fonts:** AdihausDIN and AdineuePRO are declared with `mso-font-alt:Arial`, which is a sound fallback.
- **Plain-text version:** QA reports 4839 of 5777 characters (>70%) are URLs, so the text alternative is mostly raw tracking links.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens are visible in the truncated source.
- Image URLs embed per-recipient identifiers: `mi_u=94IW5HF2JM6L4FZA` and `mi_redeemablepoints=50.00000000000`. The float formatting (`50.00000000000`) is a precision artifact of the personalization engine.
- Not every rewards image carries `mi_redeemablepoints` (for example `1439f5e8f638fac5.png` and `e9ed28f35732a305.png` don't). That may be intentional, but it could mean inconsistent personalization.
- The recipient ID `partner_uid=1562263179` is also exposed in third-party beacon URLs.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are missing from the captured headers. QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay artifact, so it is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is expected via the relay, so it is inconclusive.
- **Footer:** the visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated portion and were not verified.
- **Sender:** `adidas@us-news.adidas.com` is a dedicated marketing subdomain, which is good practice.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM and landing pages:** they can't be verified. All 24 tracked links are wrapped in redirects and were not resolved, so final URLs, UTM parameters and destination alignment are unknown.
- **Campaign ID:** `mi_ecmp=4404327` on the image URLs matches `campaignid=4404327` on the Krux beacon, so the campaign ID is consistent across systems.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Replace the two `image.staging.link.adidas.com` assets with production-hosted URLs.
2. Add `alt` to all flagged images: descriptive text for the rewards content images, and `alt=""` for `o.gif` and the beacon pixels.
3. Resolve the 24 redirect links in a follow-up check to confirm destinations return 200 and carry the intended UTMs.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw sending path, since the relay may be stripping them. Also confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment against the original message headers.
5. Verify the footer for a working unsubscribe link and a postal address.
6. Set a descriptive `<title>` and trim the trailing space.
7. Review whether the recipient IDs (`mi_u`, `partner_uid`) should be exposed in third-party beacon URLs, and whether the `mi_redeemablepoints` formatting and the missing parameter on some images are intended.
8. Add a proper plain-text alternative rather than one dominated by tracking URLs.
