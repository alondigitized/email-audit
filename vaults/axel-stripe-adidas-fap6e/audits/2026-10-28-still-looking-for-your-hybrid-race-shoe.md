# Still looking for your hybrid race shoe? 🏃
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-product send for the Adizero Dropset Pro, and the photography is the best part. The split hero shows a runner mid-stride in coral, next to a close-up of the hot-pink shoe. It reads as performance footwear immediately. The copy is generic, though. It has no price, no promo, and no deadline, and nothing explains why a hybrid race shoe is worth buying.
- The subject, "Still looking for your hybrid race shoe?", promises something the body never delivers. It implies a segmented, behavior-based nudge. The body is a broadcast product announcement with "full speed and strength to every session" boilerplate. The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner above the hero pulls attention away from the one product the email is about.
- The footnote undercuts the CTA. The footnote says the shoe is available only at 7 named stores, first-come first-served, and "purchase is not guaranteed". "Shop Men" and "Shop Women" imply online availability. This is a pretty hero with a hedged offer behind it. Compared with the 10/26 gifting send ("footwear for the whole family at $80 and under"), this one has less to click for.

## What's working

- The split hero shows both action (runner) and product detail (shoe close-up), so the product is clear without reading.
- The coral and pink palette stands out against the grey-blue background.
- The "ADIZERO DROPSET PRO" headline is large and unambiguous.
- The subject's question and emoji are relevant to a runner and stand out in the inbox.
- Three stacked buttons are large, high-contrast, and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- No price, discount, or member benefit appears anywhere.
- The best-sellers banner competes with the hero and points to a different product.
- The footnote says the shoe is in-store only, first-come first-served, with purchase "not guaranteed". That contradicts the online "Shop Men/Women" buttons.
- The body copy is generic. It never says what "hybrid" means or gives any specs such as weight, cushioning, or use case.
- The preheader "Speed meets strength in Adizero Dropset Pro." sits in the top-left as underlined text in the render. It reads as a stray link.
- Neither the buttons nor the copy point to a specific product page, and there is no review or social proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Resolve the availability contradiction. If the shoe is in-store only, lead with "Find a store near you" as the single primary CTA. If it's online, drop the footnote's scarcity language.
- 2. Remove the best-sellers banner or move it below the product block, so the hero is the first thing the eye lands on.
- 3. Replace the generic copy with two or three concrete specs (weight, plate or no plate, what it's for). "Hybrid" needs a definition.
- 4. Add a price and, if it applies, a member benefit.
- 5. Collapse the three buttons into one primary CTA plus one secondary.
- 6. If the subject implies segmentation, send it only to the segment it implies.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Train in it, race in it. Find it at a store near you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hybrid speed and strength. Available now at select stores.`
