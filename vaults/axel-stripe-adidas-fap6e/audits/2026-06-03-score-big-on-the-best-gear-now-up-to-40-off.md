# Score big on the best gear, now up to 40% off.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's Summer Kickoff Event launch — "UP TO 40% OFF STARTS NOW" on a black background with a red graphic element and three gender-segment CTAs. Structurally and visually, it is the May 25 "Score 30% off" email (8/10) with a larger discount number, a seasonal event label, and a swap of "END OF MAY SAVINGS" for "SUMMER KICKOFF EVENT." The formula works, but adidas is running it for the second time in under two weeks, and the creative hasn't evolved to match the bigger offer.
- The email's core problem is that "up to 40% off" without a single product image, a category spotlight, or an end date asks the subscriber to do all the work. The copy explains "newly marked down favorites" but shows nothing. At 40% off — the deepest discount in this brand history window — the email should feel like an event. Instead it reads like a reskinned blast.
- The three gender-segment CTAs remain adidas's smartest structural move, and the visual hierarchy is tight. But the 40% headline deserves an anchor product or category to make it feel tangible, not just another sale banner.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy: "SUMMER KICKOFF EVENT" hero leads the eye directly to the offer copy, then the CTAs — no noise.
- Three gender-segment CTAs (SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS) reduce friction for every household segment in a single scroll.
- Subject line hits 46 chars with a concrete offer — mobile-friendly and readable at a glance.
- "STARTS NOW" gives launch energy; feels like the beginning of something rather than a routine blast.

## What's weak

- No product photography at all — a 40% off event with zero visual inventory anchor feels abstract and easy to defer.
- "Up to 40% off" with no end date gives no reason to act today vs. tomorrow; the May 26 voucher email (8/10) showed deadlines convert.
- Body copy echoes the headline almost word-for-word ("discounts up to 40%") instead of adding new information like a category callout or sale start/end window.
- Cadence pressure: this is the seventh adidas email in nine days — the sale launch competes with its own inbox noise from the FIFA jersey and heritage sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop one hero product image** — a running shoe or training short at a visible strikethrough price turns "up to 40% off" into a tangible claim and a scroll-stopper.
- 2. **Add an end date** — "ENDS JUNE 8" or a countdown anywhere in the email would convert fence-sitters. "Starts now" is half the urgency equation.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copy** from the May 25 send — "SHOP MEN'S SALE" with a category tagline (Running / Training / Football) gives repeat subscribers a new reason to click.
- 4. **Trim the body copy** — "Start the season on a high note with low prices on newly marked down favorites, including discounts up to 40%" is 21 words that repeat the subject. Replace with a category or end date.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is adidas's Summer Kickoff Event launch — "UP TO 40% OFF STARTS NOW" on a black background with a red graphic element and three gender-segment CTAs. Structurally and visually, it is the May 25 "Score 30% off" email (8/10) with a larger discount number, a seasonal event label, and a swap of "END OF MAY SAVINGS" for "SUMMER KICKOFF EVENT." The formula works, but adidas is running it for the second time in under two weeks, and the creative hasn't evolved to match the bigger offer.

The email's core problem is that "up to 40% off" without a single product image, a category spotlight, or an end date asks the subscriber to do all the work. The copy explains "newly marked down favorites" but shows nothing. At 40% off — the deepest discount in this brand history window — the email should feel like an event. Instead it reads like a reskinned blast.

The three gender-segment CTAs remain adidas's smartest structural move, and the visual hierarchy is tight. But the 40% headline deserves an anchor product or category to make it feel tangible, not just another sale banner.

## 2. What worked

- Clean visual hierarchy: "SUMMER KICKOFF EVENT" hero leads the eye directly to the offer copy, then the CTAs — no noise.
- Three gender-segment CTAs (SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS) reduce friction for every household segment in a single scroll.
- Subject line hits 46 chars with a concrete offer — mobile-friendly and readable at a glance.
- "STARTS NOW" gives launch energy; feels like the beginning of something rather than a routine blast.

## 3. What didn't

- No product photography at all — a 40% off event with zero visual inventory anchor feels abstract and easy to defer.
- "Up to 40% off" with no end date gives no reason to act today vs. tomorrow; the May 26 voucher email (8/10) showed deadlines convert.
- Body copy echoes the headline almost word-for-word ("discounts up to 40%") instead of adding new information like a category callout or sale start/end window.
- Cadence pressure: this is the seventh adidas email in nine days — the sale launch competes with its own inbox noise from the FIFA jersey and heritage sends.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Drop one hero product image** — a running shoe or training short at a visible strikethrough price turns "up to 40% off" into a tangible claim and a scroll-stopper.
2. **Add an end date** — "ENDS JUNE 8" or a countdown anywhere in the email would convert fence-sitters. "Starts now" is half the urgency equation.
3. **Differentiate the CTA copy** from the May 25 send — "SHOP MEN'S SALE" with a category tagline (Running / Training / Football) gives repeat subscribers a new reason to click.
4. **Trim the body copy** — "Start the season on a high note with low prices on newly marked down favorites, including discounts up to 40%" is 21 words that repeat the subject. Replace with a category or end date.

- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off just dropped — shop the Summer Kickoff`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on running, training, and more. Sale ends June 8.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Newly marked down favorites — find your category below.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect destination**
The click-tracking URL resolves to a relative path rather than an absolute URL:


The redirect target starts with `/g/` with no host — this will 404 or produce an unresolvable URL depending on how the click server handles relative paths. Any recipient clicking this link lands nowhere.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**
```
Text version: 0 chars
```
CAN-SPAM and many ISP filtering heuristics expect a `text/plain` alternative part. A multipart/alternative email with a 0-byte text body will trigger spam filters and fails accessibility for text-only mail agents.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were detected at the relay layer. These are required for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI and for bulk-sender compliance under Google/Yahoo's February 2024 requirements. The relay may be stripping them — needs verification at the raw SMTP level.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable via the AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com`; authentication posture for this subdomain should be confirmed directly against the receiving MTA headers.

`ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `referrer: no-referrer` meta tags are present and appropriate for email HTML.
