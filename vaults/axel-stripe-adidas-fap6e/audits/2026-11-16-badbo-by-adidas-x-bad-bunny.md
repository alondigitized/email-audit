# BadBo by adidas x Bad Bunny
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, premium product-drop email for the BadBo by adidas x Bad Bunny sneakers, and it is undercut by a Black Friday countdown banner above the hero. The two flat-lay shots on grey concrete look good. The "storm clouds to sunset" copy is more evocative than most sneaker emails. The product story is strong.
- The email never decides what it is. The black banner (02:08:50:35, "Up to 60% Off for Members") sells a discount. The body sells a limited, first-come, first-served collab drop that probably isn't discounted. A shopper can't tell whether the 60% applies to BadBo, and the footer's "supplies last" language suggests it doesn't. This is the same stacked-countdown pattern as the 2026-11-16 Care Bears send and the 2026-11-15 "Fresh looks to gear up for the big race" email. The hero product is pushed below a promo it has nothing to do with.
- The email also gives no price, no sizing, no release date and no proof of demand. Two stacked black buttons ("SHOP NOW" and "FIND STORE NOW") split the click. Verdict: a good-looking collab announcement with a confused offer and thin product detail. It will reach people who already follow Bad Bunny or adidas sneakers and will do little for everyone else.

## What's working

- The two flat-lay photos on grey concrete are cohesive, and the grey and tan-pink colorways are easy to tell apart.
- The copy ("From storm clouds to sunset", the Puerto Rico sky story) gives the product a narrative.
- The adidas and Bad Bunny co-brand lockup at the bottom is clear and credible.
- The black countdown banner is legible and has a real deadline.
- The layout is simple and uncluttered, with a single-column flow.

## What's weak

- The Black Friday banner and the BadBo drop compete. The 60% off message doesn't clearly apply to the collab shoes.
- There is no price, release date or sizing, so the reader has no concrete reason to act now.
- "SHOP NOW" and "FIND STORE NOW" are two identical black buttons stacked together, with no visual priority between them.
- The headline is plain, thin type, and the body paragraph is a dense block of small text with no emphasis on the shoes.
- The store-availability caveats are buried in small footer print ("Purchase is not guaranteed", first-come, first-served). They contradict the "Shop online" invitation.
- Neither shoe is labeled with its name or colorway next to the image, and the images aren't linked to specific products.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Either drop the Black Friday banner from this send, or say plainly that the 60% member discount excludes BadBo. The drop email should lead with the drop.
- 2. Add price, release date and a hero CTA like "Shop BadBo 1.0 Grey" and "Shop BadBo 1.1 Simple Brown", each linked to its own product page.
- 3. Make "Find store" a text link and keep one primary button. Put availability expectations ("limited quantities, select stores") above the fold instead of in the footer.
- 4. Label each colorway under its photo, with a short line for fit, sizing and materials (suede, gum sole).
- 5. Bold the headline and put Bad Bunny's name up top, since he is the main draw.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BadBo 1.0 & 1.1: Bad Bunny's new adidas`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Two new BadBos, inspired by Puerto Rico skies`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grey or Simple Brown. Limited quantities, online and at select stores.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two colorways from storm clouds to sunset. Shop the drop now.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML has a sound base: `lang="en"`, a doctype, responsive media queries and an `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta tag. The confirmed issues are a missing plain-text part (0 chars), `http://` image sources, and images without alt text. The header-based checks (unsubscribe, authentication) are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **20 tracking links were not probed.** They all use click-redirect domains such as `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`, so the final destinations are unverified.
- **Tracking asset served over HTTP.** An image on `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a1e69b4a07-.../T430CNN017n_P2WP9xobnjc861KEDaNCa1gy8BjNmDg=452` has no alt text and a non-HTTPS source. It is probably an open-tracking pixel. Clients that block mixed or insecure content will drop it, so open counts would be undercounted.
- The `http://` source and missing alt on this pixel come from the QA data. Whether it is a pixel is my inference from the URL pattern.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Insecure images.** `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` ("arrow") appears four times with an `http://` source. Some clients and proxies block or warn on these.
- **Missing alt text.**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/20b7d69a-d4d4-44a3-b434-0422396ff641?...` has no alt. If it is a content image, it is invisible with images off or to screen readers. If it is decorative, it needs `alt=""`.
  - The tracking image above also has no alt.
- **Viewport meta.** `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">` has no `width=device-width`. The media queries are written for narrow viewports, so this may cause inconsistent scaling in some mobile clients.
- **Fonts.** The `@font-face` rules for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` use only `local('Arial')`-style sources. No webfont is loaded, so these fonts always fall back to system fonts. This is not harmful, but the declarations have no effect.
- **Global `p` rule.** `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}` applies to every paragraph and could cause unintended centering in some contexts.
- **Doctype.** HTML 4.01 Transitional is legacy but acceptable for email.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The source is truncated, so this covers only the portion shown.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Plain-text part is empty** (QA FAIL: "Text version is 0 chars"). This hurts deliverability and accessibility. It is a real finding if the sent message is missing the text part rather than the relay stripping it.
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are not found.** The QA tool itself says the AgentMail relay may not capture them, so this is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `us-news.comms.adidas.com` cannot be assessed. This is a limitation of the relay.
- **Footer not visible.** The in-body unsubscribe link and physical address are cut off by truncation, so CAN-SPAM body requirements are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Unverifiable. All links are wrapped in `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, and the probes were skipped. UTM parameters, final landing URLs and campaign alignment with "BadBo by adidas x Bad Bunny" could not be confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add a multipart/alternative `text/plain` part with the key content and an unsubscribe URL.
2. Serve all images over HTTPS, including the `image.link.adidas.com` "arrow" asset and the tracking pixel on `click.comms.adidas.com`.
3. Add `alt` text to `dv.adidas.com/o/20b7d69a-...`, or `alt=""` if it is decorative. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel as well.
4. Change the viewport meta to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.
5. Check the raw message headers (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass.
6. Follow the click-redirect chains to confirm final URLs and UTM tagging. Also review the full, untruncated HTML for the footer unsubscribe link and address.
7. For the QA tool, capture the original headers and the text part. That would remove most of the "may not be captured" warnings.
