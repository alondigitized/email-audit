# Go fast and get dirty with all-day comfort
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident single-launch email for the new Agravic SL and Agravic GTX 2 trail shoes — three runners mid-stride on rocky terrain, dynamic and on-brand, sitting right below a black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" utility banner. It's a tighter, more focused execution than the thin Z.N.E. send from 2026-09-20, closer in spirit to the 2026-09-23 Adios Pro 5 launch — same playbook: one hero shot, one headline, one CTA, repeated twice.
- The problem is there's no actual offer anywhere in this email. It's pure product-launch copy ("THE AGRAVIC FAMILY'S GROWING") with zero pricing, zero specs, zero social proof, and the hero image doesn't even show the product clearly — it's three people running past a rocky trail, shoes barely visible mid-stride. For a category (trail running) that lives and dies on grip, drop, cushioning stats, this email gives a trail runner nothing to evaluate before clicking.
- Verdict: visually competent but commercially thin — a brand-awareness piece dressed as a launch email, not a converter.

## What's working

- Clean single-column layout with strong visual hierarchy: banner → hero image → headline → copy → CTA, no clutter.
- The trail hero photography is authentic and kinetic — real dirt, real incline, not a studio shot.
- Two clear "SHOP NOW" CTAs (banner + body) give redundant paths to convert.
- No render bugs — logo, image, and text all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- No offer, price, or promo of any kind — this is awareness copy, not a shopping trigger.
- The hero image doesn't clearly showcase the shoes; runners are captured mid-air/blurred motion, so the product itself is hard to actually see.
- No product specs (grip, drop, weight, GTX waterproofing callout) despite this being a trail shoe where those details drive purchase.
- Zero social proof (reviews, ratings, "best seller" badges) anywhere in the body.
- The top black banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") competes with and dilutes the Agravic launch message — it's promoting something entirely different one screen above the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "best sellers" banner or replace it with an Agravic-specific badge/pricing strip — right now it undercuts the launch focus before the reader even reaches the hero.
- 2. Add a static, well-lit product shot of the Agravic SL/GTX 2 (not just an action shot) so the shoe itself is legible — pair the lifestyle hero with a close-up.
- 3. Insert one concrete spec or price line under the headline ("GTX 2 waterproof, from $150") — give trail runners a reason to click beyond mood.
- 4. Add a review star rating or "Rated 4.7 by trail runners" line near the CTA for trust.

- One of the alt-missing images is also served over `http://` (the tracked "arrow"-equivalent redirect asset), compounding both issues on the same element.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- No issues found.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not present in the captured headers — may be an AgentMail relay capture gap rather than sender omission; cannot confirm compliance from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058, one-click unsubscribe) not found — one-click unsubscribe support unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available headers.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

7. Recommendations
- Serve the "arrow" image asset over HTTPS (`image.link.adidas.com`) to prevent client-side blocking.
- Add descriptive `alt` attributes to the two flagged images, particularly since one doubles as a tracked link.
- Confirm with the sending ESP that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually emitted (not just dropped by the AgentMail relay) to ensure RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance.
