# Up to 40% off for back to school
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is adidas's cleanest sale-mode send of the batch — a real discount ("up to 40% off"), a real deadline (July 30–August 13), and a hero image that actually sells the season instead of vibing at it: a locker hung with a Trefoil backpack, doodled sticky notes, a gold star. No models, no lifestyle mood board, just product-in-context, and it works.
- Where it falls short is specificity and hierarchy. "Up to 40%" is a range with no floor stated up front, the three CTAs (Men/Women/Kids) split intent three ways instead of anchoring on one hero product, and the adiClub Apple TV perk — genuinely interesting — is buried as a second paragraph instead of pulled into its own visual block. Compared to July 23's "Back to school in Samba" (8/10), which fused a concrete silhouette with the BTS theme, this send is thematically strong but product-agnostic; it's the fifth adidas email in eleven days and the second sale-framed BTS message, so fatigue risk is real even with a genuine offer behind it.
- Solid, shippable send — not a standout, but functional and honest.

## What's working

- Genuine, dated offer ("up to 40% off," July 30–Aug 13) with no vague "shop now" filler.
- Hero image is on-theme and well-art-directed — locker, backpack, doodles — without leaning on stock-photo teens again (a welcome break from July 25's "Style before the bell").
- adiClub perk (free month of Apple TV at $130+) is a distinctive, non-generic loyalty incentive most competitors don't have.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, legible type throughout.
- Segmented CTAs (Men/Women/Kids) give every shopper a fast path in.

## What's weak

- "Up to 40%" with no minimum stated anywhere above the fold undercuts urgency — reader can't tell if they get 10% or 40%.
- Three stacked black CTA buttons of equal weight give the eye nowhere specific to land; no single hero product or price is shown.
- The Apple TV perk is a strong hook but reads as an afterthought — same paragraph styling as the general sale copy, no visual separation.
- Preview/preheader text ("Stock up for back to school with up to 40% off") just restates the subject almost verbatim — wasted real estate.
- Fifth BTS-themed adidas email in under two weeks; no acknowledgment of prior sends (e.g., "still shopping the Samba drop? now it's on sale") to make this feel like a natural next beat rather than a repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the CTA row into a primary ("Shop the Sale") and a secondary line for Men/Women/Kids — or drop to two buttons — so there's one dominant action instead of three competing ones.
- 2. State the floor discount ("30-40% off") or lead with a specific dollar example ("Backpacks from $XX") so the offer feels concrete, not a teaser range.
- 3. Pull the adiClub Apple TV perk into its own visually distinct callout box (icon + bold headline) — it's the most differentiated thing in this email and it's currently invisible.
- 4. Add a hero product shot or price tag overlay on the locker image itself to anchor visual hierarchy on one item, not just theme.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Backpacks, shoes & more — up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off + a free month of Apple TV (adiClub)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Apple TV month for adiClub members who spend $130+`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Aug 13 — stock up before the bell rings`
