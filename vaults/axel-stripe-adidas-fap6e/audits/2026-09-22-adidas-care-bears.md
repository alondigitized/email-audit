# adidas | Care Bears
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- This is an adidas x Care Bears collab drop, and it's a genuinely fun, well-executed collection email — the pastel gradient hero with the animated-looking Care Bear perched on a Superstar sneaker is one of the more visually distinctive hero images this brand has sent this cycle, more memorable than the straight product shots in the 2026-09-18 Anthony Edwards 3 send or the 2026-09-14 Bad Bunny F50. The four-product grid does real work: two sneaker colorways plus a backpack and bucket hat, with star ratings, Prime availability, and two of four items showing a clear percentage discount.
- Where it falls short is focus and demographic clarity. The collection reads as kids'/family apparel (backpack, bucket hat, "Little Kids" naming) but the hero shoe and copy don't signal that until you're two products deep, so there's a beat of confusion about who this is for. There's also no urgency or timing cue anywhere in the email — no "new drop," no end date — which flattens the energy a nostalgia collab like this should carry.
- Solid, on-brand collab creative with real product depth, let down by a generic subject line and missing urgency.

## What's working

- The gradient hero image (Care Bear + cloud + Superstar sneaker) is distinctive and stops the scroll — clearly the strongest visual asset in recent adidas sends.
- Four real products shown with price, star rating, and review count — more product depth than the single-hero sends like 2026-09-18 or 2026-09-14.
- Two of four products show explicit percentage-off original pricing (-20%, -10%), satisfying a concrete-offer signal.
- Two distinct "SHOP NOW" CTAs (top banner + below headline) are unambiguous and high-contrast against white/black.
- Prime badge on the two sneakers is a nice trust/logistics signal for anyone comparison-shopping.

## What's weak

- Subject "adidas | Care Bears" is flat and generic — it doesn't convey collection, price, or urgency, and looks like an internal SKU label rather than a marketing subject.
- No timing/urgency anywhere in the body — no launch date, no "limited edition," no countdown, which undersells a licensed collab that should feel like a drop.
- Demographic targeting is muddled: hero implies lifestyle/adult sneaker release, but two of four products are explicitly "Little Kids" items, so the email serves two audiences without segmenting them visually.
- The top black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner is generic filler unrelated to Care Bears and competes with the actual hero for top-of-email attention.
- No social proof beyond star ratings — no "as seen on" or influencer/kid-lifestyle imagery to sell the collab concept itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to name the collection and hook nostalgia/kids-gifting angle instead of just a product tag.
- 2. Cut or replace the generic "best sellers" banner at the top — it dilutes the Care Bears theme in the first fold and adds zero information.
- 3. Add a date or "new drop" / "shop before it's gone" line near the hero to create urgency that's currently absent.
- 4. Segment the grid visually — group the two sneakers under one sub-header and the two kids' accessories under another, so parents and sneakerheads each see their section fast.
- 5. Add one lifestyle shot (kid wearing the backpack/hat, or the shoe styled on-foot) to make the collab feel tangible rather than purely e-commerce tile grid.
