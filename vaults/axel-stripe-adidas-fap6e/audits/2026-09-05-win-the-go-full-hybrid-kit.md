# Win the go full hybrid kit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine giveaway/sweepstakes email — a departure from the recent run of collab launches and sitewide discounts (Tyshawn, JENNIE, Samba, End of Summer Sale). The concept is strong for a hybrid-training persona: a flat-lay bag shot showing the Adizero Dropshoe Pro, a Polar Grit X2 smartwatch, red training shorts, and a gym bag reads as a real, covetable prize bundle, not stock photography. The itemized list under the headline does the heavy lifting the hero image alone can't.
- But it's a giveaway, not a purchase offer — there's no price, no code, no urgency, and "Sign Up" is a soft ask compared to "Shop Now." That caps how hard this can convert relative to a real discount email. There's also a distracting mismatch: the merchandising banner at top ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers / Shop Now") competes with the giveaway CTA below it, splitting attention right out of the gate. Net: creative concept is a genuine standout for this brand's recent run, execution has one clear structural fix needed.

## What's working

- The flat-lay prize shot is authentic and specific — visible Polar Grit X2 watch face, Dropshoe Pro shoe, red shorts, black gym bag all recognizable in one frame.
- The bulleted prize list under the headline removes any ambiguity about what's being won.
- Copy speaks directly to a hybrid-training audience ("race day," "new PB," "hybrid workouts") — clear persona targeting.
- Clean layout, no render bugs — logo, banner, hero image, and CTA all display correctly.

## What's weak

- Two competing CTAs stacked top-to-bottom ("Shop Now" banner, then "Sign Up" button) dilute focus — a reader skims the first CTA and may never reach the giveaway pitch.
- No entry deadline or close date visible anywhere in the body — sweepstakes without urgency are easy to skip.
- No visible odds, entry mechanism detail, or terms link near the CTA (just a generic "Sign Up") — feels a little thin for a giveaway ask, which usually needs more trust-building than a purchase CTA.
- Preview text just repeats the hero copy almost verbatim instead of adding new information.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the top merchandising banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers / Shop Now") entirely — it's the single biggest drag on this email, since it gives readers an exit ramp before they see the giveaway. Let the giveaway own the whole email.
- 2. Add a deadline line right under the headline ("Enter by [date]") — even a soft one creates urgency a giveaway otherwise lacks.
- 3. Make "Sign Up" more specific — "Enter to Win" or "Enter the Giveaway" reads more actionable than a generic account-creation phrase.
- 4. Add a one-line trust signal near the CTA (e.g., "One winner selected [date]. No purchase necessary.") to reduce skepticism about a giveaway ask.
