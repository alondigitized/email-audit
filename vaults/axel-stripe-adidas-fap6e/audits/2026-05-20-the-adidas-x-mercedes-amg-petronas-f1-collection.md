# The adidas x Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Collection 🏁
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- The adidas x Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Collection email is a well-produced collab launch — the F1 hero is bold, the "HEARTBEAT OF RACEDAY" headline lands with appropriate swagger, and the render is clean. If this were a focused, single-topic send, it would be a solid mid-tier awareness play.
- The problem: adidas glued a completely unrelated Calm meditation app campaign ("YOUR MIND IS A MUSCLE. FLEX IT.") onto the bottom half of the same email. These are two different briefs for two different audiences, and forcing them into one send splits the focus and the click. The tonal whiplash — adrenaline-charged motorsport to mindfulness meditation in one scroll — is jarring.
- Worse, this is the second adidas email arriving today. The "Unlock more rewards with the adidas app" send also hit on May 20. Two sends in a single day, right at the tail end of an onboarding sequence, is the fastest way to train a subscriber to skip. There's no offer, no urgency, no adiClub hook — just awareness marketing for full-price product. It'll move true F1 fans; everyone else scrolls past.

## What's working

- F1 hero image is strong — athletic, motorsport-coded, and on-brand without being campy.
- "THE HEARTBEAT OF RACEDAY" is a confident headline that earns the collab's name.
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout issues, no junk text visible anywhere.
- Subject line is highly specific: zero ambiguity about what this email is.
- Black "SHOP NOW" CTA is immediately visible and clearly actionable.

## What's weak

- Two unrelated campaign modules (F1 Collection + Calm meditation) share one email — these belong in separate sends to separate segments.
- No offer, no price anchor, no urgency — nothing motivates a click today vs. next week.
- The preheader slot is occupied by generic nav copy ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") that ignores the F1 subject entirely.
- This is the second adidas email on May 20 — cadence fatigue is a real risk at this point in the onboarding sequence.
- The Calm module's meditative tone directly undercuts the F1 module's high-energy positioning within the same scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the F1 and Calm modules into separate sends** — one email, one brief. This is a creative/media-buy compromise that hurts both campaigns.
- 2. **Add urgency or a member-access hook to the F1 module** — "Early access for adiClub members," a limited colorway signal, or a launch date gives a reason to act now. Tie it to the onboarding sequence that just completed (May 15–17) by calling out the adiClub angle.
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to extend the F1 subject** — the current "best sellers" banner is wasted inbox real estate; something that carries the motorsport energy would lift open rate.
- 4. **Enforce a one-send-per-day throttle** — even if both emails are valid sends, two in one day at this stage of onboarding is a deliverability and trust risk.
- 5. **Make the CTA more specific** — "SHOP F1 COLLECTION" outperforms "SHOP NOW" because it confirms destination before the click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mercedes-AMG x adidas: The F1 Collection Is Here 🏁`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Built for raceday — the adidas x AMG PETRONAS F1 drop`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Engineered for the podium. Styled for everywhere else.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The collab F1 fans have been waiting for — shop now.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two hard failures — a broken dynamic image URL and a missing plain-text fallback — plus recurring mixed-content (HTTP) image warnings and unverifiable compliance headers due to relay capture gaps. Overall QA pass rate is 38%.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken dynamic image URL**
The `dv.adidas.com` personalization endpoint is referenced with a relative path type in QA:
```
unknown url type: '/g/7e07fb28-d2a5-4524-877f-17f4157e1d90'
```
The actual `src` resolves as a full `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...` URL with a long `cp_tp` token, but the QA checker detected an internal path alias (`/g/` vs `/o/`) mismatch — likely a template variable that rendered incorrectly for this recipient. If `dv.adidas.com` returns a broken image for any cohort, the hero/personalized image will be blank.

**[WARN] 24 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirect links routed through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Not a defect, but destination URLs are unverifiable from the raw source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× HTTP image asset (same file, repeated)**
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
This arrow icon is sourced over HTTP, not HTTPS. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. The asset will silently fail to load on most modern clients. The same `src` appears 4 times (same arrow reused across CTAs).

**[WARN] Additional HTTP image**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e4698b3a0-6dacaf51-8cad-44f1-8608-a66286425cf4-000000/h7ZZTaZogE6f5N_hTEb_g6d-6...
```
A tracking pixel or image routed through `click.comms.adidas.com` over HTTP. Same blocking risk.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on 2 images**
- `7e07fb28-d2a5-4524-877f-17f4157e1d90` (the dynamic `dv.adidas.com` image — likely the hero)
- `h7ZZTaZogE6f5N_hTEb_g6d-6...` (the HTTP click-tracker image)

Both have empty or absent `alt` attributes. If images are blocked, the hero renders as a blank space with no fallback label. Fails WCAG 1.1.1.

**CSS note:** The `@font-face` declarations for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` reference only local system fonts (Arial, Helvetica, Calibri) — no remote WOFF/WOFF2 source. This is intentional for email client compatibility, but the font names are brand-specific and will silently fall back to Arial on all clients. Not a bug, but expected behavior worth documenting.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[NAME]`) are visible in the truncated source. The `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`, and `cp_cid` parameters on the `dv.adidas.com` URL suggest recipient-level personalization is in use via URL parameters rather than inline tokens — standard for this ESP stack.

The broken `/g/` path alias on the dynamic image URL (see §2) is the highest-risk personalization failure here.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is likely a relay capture gap rather than a true absence (SendGrid/Salesforce MC injects these at the SMTP envelope level), but it cannot be confirmed from available data. If missing from the actual delivered message, this violates CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) and Gmail's February 2024 bulk sender mandate.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header unverifiable for the same reason. Gmail and Yahoo require this for senders at >5k/day volume.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown via relay. Domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a subdomain — DMARC alignment depends on whether the parent `adidas.com` policy covers it.

**HTML source:** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and correct — prevents indexing and referrer leakage.

Physical address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer (not in the truncated source); assume present given adidas's operational maturity.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

No UTM parameters are visible in the raw destination URLs — all links pass through `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, so UTM injection may happen server-side at click time. This cannot be confirmed without following the redirect chain (blocked by QA relay).

If UTMs are not being appended server-side, GA4/Analytics attribution for this campaign will be lost and sessions will land as `(direct)` on adidas.com.
