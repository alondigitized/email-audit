# Wild Steals Are Here—Savings End Sunday
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a standard TSC mass-blast: gun safe, trail cam, hunting blinds, a UTV, tees, poultry treats, a utility trailer, diesel exhaust fluid, a pet bed, and dog food, all crammed into one send with no attempt at category focus. Following the July 27 "Great Savings for Every Outdoor Adventure" send that finally broke the exhausting heat-pun subject streak, this one keeps that discipline in the subject line — but the body still can't resist, with a "Beat the heat for less" banner near the footer. Old habits die hard.
- The strongest thing here is the top-of-email execution: "WILD STEALS" hero with a hard Friday–Sunday deadline, dollar-off pricing, and a tight 4-up grid of concrete deals (Winchester gun safe -$200, 25% off pop-up blinds, trail cam -$30, deer attractant at $9.99). That's a real, scannable value prop. But it's immediately undercut by stacking three competing "hero" banners in the first 400px (Unbeatable Prices bar → Wild Steals box → orange UTV/tee banner), so the eye doesn't land on one clear offer — it lands on three.
- Net: competent execution, unfocused targeting, same cadence problem the brand history keeps flagging. This is a mid-pack TSC send — better than the July 10 filler blast, weaker than the July 17 send that actually earned its 9.

## What's working

- The "WILD STEALS" hero block is well-built: hard deadline ("Friday Thru Sunday Only"), four distinct SKUs each with its own price/discount, and a clean grid layout.
- The second product row (diesel fluid, pet bed, dog food) includes star ratings with review counts (210, 51, 9, 3,848 reviews) — real social proof, a step up from the pure-price-tag rows below it.
- The TSC Credit Card offer ($20 off + $20 in rewards) and the Neighbor's Club Double Points banner give loyalty members a visible, separate reason to act.
- No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage anywhere in the render — it's clean.

## What's weak

- Three stacked "hero" banners in the first scroll (red Unbeatable Prices bar, Wild Steals box, orange UTV/tee banner) compete for attention instead of establishing one clear lead offer.
- The category spread is scattershot — gun safe, trail cam, poultry treats, utility trailer, diesel fluid, dog food, pet bed, tees, UTV — this reads as an unsegmented catalog dump, not a curated pick for any one shopper type.
- Every CTA button says "Shop Now" (or "Apply In-Store") — zero differentiation in copy even though the products are wildly different categories.
- Fine print ("Offers may vary by state, while quantities last, no rainchecks or substitutions") sits directly under the flagship Wild Steals deals, which slightly undercuts the "unbeatable" framing.
- The body still leans on "Beat the heat for less" copy near the footer — the exact seasonal-pun habit the July 27 subject line seemed to be moving away from.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut to one hero banner. Kill either the "Unbeatable Prices" strip or the orange UTV/tee banner — pick the single strongest offer (the $200 gun safe or the UTV) and let it own the top fold instead of splitting attention three ways.
- 2. Differentiate CTA copy by product — "Shop Gun Safes," "See Trail Cams," "Get the Trailer" — instead of six identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 3. Split this into 2 segmented sends instead of one everything-blast: a hunting/security-gear email (safe, blinds, trail cam) and a farm/pet-supply email (poultry treats, dog food, diesel fluid, pet bed). One-size-fits-all catalog blasts are what's driving the brand-history complaint about unsegmented mass sends.
- 4. Retire "Beat the heat for less" — it contradicts the subject line's move away from heat-pun copy and reintroduces the fatigue the July archive already flags.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Wild Steals: Up to 25% Off, Thru Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 2-for-$30 tees and $20 off your first Credit Card purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Trail cams, gun safes, and trailers — all on sale thru Sunday only.`

# Technical Audit — Tractor Supply "Wild Steals" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard ESP responsive template (Zeta/Eversend-style `ez.tractorsupply.com` click-tracking infrastructure) with sound Outlook/Gmail CSS resets, but 9 of 12 probed material links failed — including a confirmed 403 on the unsubscribe link — and standard email authentication/compliance headers were not detected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **7 timeouts** on merchandising links routed through `e.ez.tractorsupply.com/click?...` redirecting to `mi.tractorsupply.com/p/cp/{hash}/c` and `www.tractorsupply.com`: link texts "Tractor Supply Co.", "Unbeatable Banner" (×2), "Display images to show real-time content", "2026FW31UTVValueModule", "FieldandStreamTees", "ImpeckablesTreats", "Trailer". All share the same redirect chain through the `mi.tractorsupply.com` offer-routing endpoint, suggesting the failure point is that domain/service rather than isolated links (7 of 8 non-unsubscribe links failing points to a systemic redirector issue, not per-link breakage).
- **Confirmed 403** on the "Update your preferences or unsubscribe" link → `https://e.ez.tractorsupply.com/click?.../preferences.ez.tractorsupply.com/unsubscribe...`. This is a hard failure, not a timeout, and is compliance-relevant (see §5).
- Click URLs carry standard tracking payload: `utm_source=zeta&utm_medium=email`, `mi_u`, `mi_ign=073126_TSC_WKY_FW31TopDealsWeekly`, `bt_user_id`, `bt_ts`, plus per-tile `OFFERID` and `offerlocation` (e.g., `Tbanner1`, `tile_1`–`tile_4`) params for click-map attribution.
- Open-tracking pixel present: `https://e.ez.tractorsupply.com/open?...` (`s6t000120ac`), plus a secondary third-party analytics pixel at `eaAnalyticsTSC.everestengagement.com/ea/o8uREqbKQf/?e=...&c=073126_TSC_WKY_FW31TopDealsWeekly`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 3 images missing `alt` text (flagged by QA): `o.gif` (mi.tractorsupply.com tracking pixel), the Everest Engagement analytics pixel, and the `e.ez.tractorsupply.com/open` tracking pixel (`s6t000120ac`). ## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** (WARN) — may be a relay-capture artifact rather than a true absence.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (WARN) — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed supported.
- **Unsubscribe link returns 403** — this is the more serious finding: even if header-based unsubscribe exists, the in-body link recipients would actually click is broken, which is a functional CAN-SPAM risk (unsubscribe mechanism must work).
- **Authentication-Results header not found** (WARN) — SPF/DKIM/DMARC status could not be confirmed via the relay; flagged as inconclusive rather than a failure given the stated relay caveat.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Consistent `utm_source=zeta&utm_medium=email` and campaign code `073126_TSC_WKY_FW31TopDealsWeekly` across probed links, with per-asset `OFFERID`/`offerlocation` tagging for banner/tile-level attribution — structurally consistent tagging scheme.

- Landing-page alignment cannot be verified: the redirect-chain timeouts prevented QA from reaching final destination URLs, so whether tiles land on matching product/category pages is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Investigate `mi.tractorsupply.com` offer-redirector availability/latency — 7 of 8 non-unsubscribe material links time out through this single downstream endpoint, indicating a service-level issue rather than 7 isolated link errors.
2. Fix the 403 on `preferences.ez.tractorsupply.com/unsubscribe` — this is a confirmed broken compliance-critical link, not a timeout, and should be prioritized above the merchandising links.
3. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at the MTA level (vs. a relay-capture gap) and confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status via a direct header inspection outside the AgentMail relay.
