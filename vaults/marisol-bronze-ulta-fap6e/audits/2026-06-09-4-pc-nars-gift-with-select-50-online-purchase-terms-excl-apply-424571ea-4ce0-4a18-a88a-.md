# 4 PC NARS gift with select $50 online purchase** Terms & excl. apply 🪄
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Ulta's fourth gift-with-purchase email in five weeks (Rabanne 5/7, YSL 5/8, Charlotte Tilbury 6/3, now NARS 6/9), and the template fatigue is real. The execution is clean: NARS's austere, high-contrast aesthetic translates well into Ulta's email format, the multi-product showcase is well-organized, and the $50 threshold is lower and more accessible than the Charlotte Tilbury offer six days ago. The 10% off member banner stacked at the top adds a second value layer that the CT send didn't have.
- The formula is working less hard, though. There's no name personalization (compare the 5/21 send that had "Marisol" in both subject and body), no countdown urgency, and the subject line runs to ~71 characters — well over the mobile-friendly 50-char cutoff — with "Terms & excl. apply" doing real estate damage where urgency copy should live. The wand emoji doesn't compensate.
- This is a competent, mid-range GWP execution. It will convert loyal NARS buyers but won't punch above its weight at the inbox level for anyone who has already opened three near-identical sends this month.

## What's working

- **NARS hero is immediately legible** — the logo dominates above the fold and the product imagery is crisp against the white background.
- **$50 threshold is genuinely accessible** — lower barrier than the CT or YSL GWPs, which widens the addressable audience inside the list.
- **10% off member banner stacks an additional incentive** at the very top, giving a second reason to click before the GWP pitch even begins.
- **Multi-category product showcase** (blush, lip, eye, foundation) does useful work showing breadth of the NARS range for subscribers who aren't NARS loyalists yet.
- **Rewards/loyalty section at the bottom** maintains Ulta's brand consistency and gives Diamond/Platinum members a clear secondary hook.

## What's weak

- **No personalization** — "Marisol" appeared in subject and body on 5/8 and 5/21; this send drops it entirely, making it feel like a mass blast after two personalized sends.
- **No deadline urgency** — the subject and visible body copy carry zero time-bound language; the CT GWP from 6/3 had the same miss, but at least it was novel then.
- **Subject line is bloated at ~71 characters** — "Terms & excl. apply 🪄" appended to an already-full subject eats mobile preview real estate that urgency or a hook should own.
- **Fourth GWP in five weeks** — cadence this compressed trains subscribers to wait for the next GWP rather than act on this one.
- **CTA button copy is not distinctive** in the render — buttons are visible but undifferentiated across sections; no single "Shop the Gift" anchor CTA stands out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add urgency to the subject** — the most impactful single change. "Ends [day]" or a 48-hour window would move this from a passive offer to a decision.
- 2. **Restore name personalization** — Ulta clearly has the segment data (5/21 and 5/8 both ran it). Dropping it here is leaving a proven conversion lift on the table.
- 3. **Lead the hero with the scarcity angle** — "Only while supplies last" or "Limited gift" in the hero header ties the $50 threshold to FOMO and accelerates click timing.
- 4. **Consolidate to one primary CTA** per scroll section rather than multiple undifferentiated buttons — reduces decision paralysis and gives the eye a single anchor per section.
- 5. **Consider pausing GWP sends at 2-week minimum intervals** — four in five weeks compresses perceived value of each individual offer; a rest week would make the next GWP feel like an event again.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Google Fonts will not load in most email clients.** `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans...">` is stripped by Gmail and blocked by Outlook. There is no explicit CSS fallback stack for DM Sans outside the `.helvetica` class (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`). Any element using DM Sans directly will silently fall back to client default, causing layout shifts in pixel-precise designs.
- **Dead CSS payload.** The hamburger menu block (`/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu ... END Hamburger Menu */`) is commented out but the full rule set — `#mobile-label`, `#mobile-checkbox`, `#menu-wrapper`, etc. — remains in the stylesheet as commented text, adding ~3–4 KB to the `<style>` block for no render benefit.
- **`@-ms-viewport { width: device-width; }`** is a dead IE 10 Mobile hack with no effect in any current client; minor byte waste.
- **`translate="no"` on `<html>`** disables browser/client translation for all recipients. This is aggressive; non-English speakers cannot use auto-translate. Intentional only if the brand explicitly requires it.
- **`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit !important; text-decoration: none !important; }`** resets all link styling globally. In Gmail Web, this makes CTA links visually identical to surrounding body text unless each link has an explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration`. Confirm all CTAs have inline style overrides.
- **`<title>Ulta Beauty </title>`** contains a trailing space — likely a template rendering artifact; harmless but indicates incomplete token cleanup.
