# 🌊🍋🛥️ This *new* Dolce&Gabbana is waiting for you...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Ulta's fifth notable send in three weeks, and it breaks the recent GWP-fatigue cycle by pivoting to a brand spotlight — Dolce & Gabbana's new summer fragrance, led by the instantly recognizable Light Blue aesthetic (ocean water, lemons, sailboat emojis telegraphing the theme before the email even opens). The "Beauty Break" hero and the D&G feature work together as a coherent seasonal moment rather than another "here's a free sample with your order" transaction.
- The 10% off banner sitting above the hero is easy to miss because it reads like a loyalty-tier status bar rather than a primary offer. The email earns its keep as a brand-awareness send, but it leaves money on the table by not pushing a single, loud call to action around the new fragrance launch.
- Overall judgment: competent product spotlight with a buried discount. Better than the NARS GWP from yesterday in terms of creative freshness; not as conversion-driven as the 5/21 tiered-discount send.

## What's working

- **Subject-line emoji work is on-brand.** 🌊🍋🛥️ aligns perfectly with D&G Light Blue's Mediterranean DNA — the theme is telegraphed before opening.
- **"Beauty Break" hero creates seasonal permission.** Summer framing gives the email an editorial feel rather than just another promo blast.
- **D&G branding is clear and high-prestige.** The logo placement and fragrance bottle photography give the email a premium look that Ulta's house-brand GWP sends lack.
- **Star ratings appear at the footer**, adding bottom-of-email social proof for hesitant buyers.
- **10% off is visible**, even if understated — a price-sensitive reader who scrolls will find it.

## What's weak

- **The 10% offer is buried in a thin banner**, not hero-sized. A new D&G fragrance + 10% off is a strong combo that should be shouted, not whispered.
- **"This *new* D&G is waiting for you..."** The asterisks around *new* look like a formatting artifact or fine-print flag, not intentional emphasis — it reads slightly awkward.
- **No urgency signal visible in the email body.** "Waiting for you" is soft and evergreen; nothing communicates when the offer or launch window closes.
- **Cadence risk:** Yesterday was NARS GWP, today is D&G — two brand-spotlight sends back-to-back start to feel like a rotating ad wall rather than a curated inbox experience.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 10% off the hero, not a banner footnote.** Pairing "new D&G fragrance launch" with a visible discount is a far stronger CTA hook than burying it above the fold. Lead the body copy with "10% off your first bottle — try the new Dolce & Gabbana."
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "this week only" or "through Sunday" would add credible urgency and lift click rates.
- 3. **Fix the asterisk styling on *new*.** Use bold or a colored callout, not asterisks — they read as a caveat, not excitement.
- 4. **Separate sends by at least 48 hours.** Running NARS on 6/9 and D&G on 6/10 trains the subscriber to ignore daily beauty brand spotlights.
- **Subject Alt A:** `🌊 New Dolce&Gabbana + 10% off — summer's scent is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The new D&G fragrance just landed at Ulta`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Light Blue's summer sequel is finally here — shop it with 10% off today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New launch: Dolce&Gabbana's summer scent, now in stock. 10% off through Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External Google Fonts blocked by major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:...&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web + Android), all Outlook desktop builds, and Samsung Mail strip external `<link>` stylesheets. DM Sans falls back silently to the `.helvetica` rule (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`). Not breaking, but the intended typeface will not render for the majority of recipients.

**Global link-style suppression — WCAG 1.4.1 violation**
```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
}
```
This Gmail-wrapper rule removes all visual link differentiation. Any anchor without explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` will be visually indistinguishable from body text — a confirmed failure under WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color).

**`translate="no"` on `<html>` root**
```html
<html lang="en-US" translate="no" ...>
```
Suppresses Gmail/Chrome auto-translate for non-English speakers. Acceptable only if this send is scoped exclusively to English-language audiences.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Trailing whitespace in `<title>` suggests unresolved token**
```html

```
The space after "Ulta Beauty" is consistent with a merge token (e.g., `[[FIRST_NAME]]`) that rendered empty. Surfaces as the tab/preview title in some webmail clients. Verify against the ESP template source and confirm a non-empty fallback is set if the token is dynamic.
