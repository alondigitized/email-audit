# Marisol, get $10 off your $50 purchase⁺ or $20 off your $100 purchase⁺! Terms & excl. apply
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a textbook Ulta tiered-discount send — "$10 off $50 / $20 off $100" — stacked on top of a free insulated tote GWP, a 5X points offer, and a personalized rewards panel. The name runs all the way from subject through body ("Hi, Marisol"), the barcode is front-and-center for in-store redemption, and the loyalty section at the bottom gives member equity a visible moment. The mechanics are solid.
- The single biggest drag is the subject line. At ~91 characters it will truncate to roughly "Marisol, get $10 off your $50 purchase" on most mobile clients — the $20 tier, the urgency cue, and everything else disappears. That's a structural problem: this email is built around a dual-tier offer, and half of it doesn't make it to the inbox view.
- Compare to Ulta's May 8 "Marisol: This Mystery Offer ends soon 👀" send — 37 characters, open-worthy, scored 8. That send led with intrigue and let the body reveal the value. This one tries to put every data point in the subject, fails to fit, and loses the tease entirely. Better ingredients, weaker package.

- **Dual-tier offer** ($10/$20 at two thresholds) is smart — captures the casual buyer and the high-intent spender in a single send
- **Barcode in hero**: in-store redemption with zero extra steps — no need to screenshot or forward anything
- **"Hi, Marisol" in the rewards panel**: personalization that reaches into the body, not just the subject, reinforces CRM depth

- **No deadline anywhere visible**: "Summer savings: ON" is a mode, not an end date — nothing creates urgency to act today vs. tomorrow

- **Subject Alt A:** `Marisol: $10 or $20 off — your pick 🛍️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer savings unlocked: $10 or $20 off`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus free tote + 5X points this weekend only.`
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Valid through Sunday" anywhere in the hero would materially increase same-session conversion without changing anything else in the creative
- 3. **Elevate the tote GWP** — a free insulated tote in May is a summer lifestyle signal; give it a full-width module with a clean lifestyle shot between the main offer and the loyalty section
- 4. **Split the CTA into two tiered buttons** — one labeled "Shop $50, save $10" and one "Shop $100, save $20" under each offer block; this mirrors the decision the buyer is already making
- 5. **Evaluate send cadence** — this appears to be the fifth or sixth Ulta email in under two weeks; even a well-executed send starts to read as noise at that frequency; consider consolidating points/loyalty messaging into a single weekly digest

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — All links stripped of visible styling (high severity)**

```css
#MessageViewBody a {
  color: inherit !important;
  text-decoration: none !important;
  …
}
```

This rule fires in most webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo). Every `<a>` in the email body will render identically to surrounding text — no underline, no color differentiation. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 failure (use of color alone) and will degrade click rates on text links.

Same pattern repeated in `.appleLinks a`.

**Issue 2 — Google Fonts `<link>` will be silently dropped**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans…" rel="stylesheet">
```

Gmail strips `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Outlook ignores web fonts entirely. The declared fallback in `.helvetica` (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`) is valid, but any element that relies on DM Sans without also having the `.helvetica` class will fall back to the client's default serif, not the intended stack. Audit all font-family declarations in the full source to confirm fallback coverage.

**Issue 3 — `translate="no"` on `<html>` element**

```html
<html lang="en-US" translate="no" …>
```

Disables in-browser translation for all recipients. Intentional for brand-name accuracy, but blocks access for non-English speakers. If this is deliberate policy, no action needed — but it should be a documented decision, not a silent default.

**Issue 4 — Dead CSS shipped to production**

Approximately 100+ lines of hamburger-menu CSS are wrapped in a comment (`/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu … END Hamburger Menu */`) but still present in the payload. This adds bytes to every send and provides no function. Strip it at the template level.
