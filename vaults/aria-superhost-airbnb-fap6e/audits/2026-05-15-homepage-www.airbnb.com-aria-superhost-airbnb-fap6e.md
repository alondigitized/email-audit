# Homepage snapshot · www.airbnb.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I opened Airbnb and got immediately walled off by a cookie consent modal before I could see a single listing. The entire above-the-fold experience is this modal — I can't see prices, I can't see filters, I can't see if they're surfacing Superhosts or Guests' Favorites. For someone who came here with a specific trip in mind, this is an immediate friction tax before the site even shows me anything.

## What's working

- **"Start your search" search pill** — it's at the top, above the modal, and immediately tells me what to do when I dismiss this thing. That's the right instinct.
- **Tab navigation (Homes / Experiences / Services)** — I can see the product structure at a glance. The "NEW" badges on Experiences and Services are actually interesting to me; I didn't know Services was a thing.
- **Bottom nav bar** — Explore, Wishlists, Log in is clean and expected. No friction in the tab structure.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal is the entire above-the-fold experience** — I opened the app and got a legal wall. As someone booking 2 months out with a list of criteria, I came here with intent and immediately hit a speed bump.
- **"Accept all" dark pattern** — the black filled button is "Accept all," the outlined button is "Only necessary." That's a classic nudge pattern. I notice these. I don't love them.
- **Zero content visible** — no listings, no pricing, no Superhost filter, no all-in pricing toggle (which I specifically care about), nothing. I have no signal about whether this homepage is even useful to me.
- **No personalization surface** — if I'm logged in, where's my name? Where's my recent search? Where's my wishlist? All hidden.

## Recommendations

- **Defer the cookie consent or make it a banner, not a full-screen modal** — a bottom-anchored banner lets me see the homepage while still surfacing the consent. Half the industry does this. It removes the friction without hiding compliance.
- **Swap "Accept all" to an outlined style and make "Only necessary" the filled CTA** — or at minimum equalize the two. Don't dark-pattern your most engaged users on first load.
- **Surface the all-in pricing toggle above the fold** — I specifically came here because Airbnb changed how it displays prices. Show me you get it. Put the toggle in the search bar area where I can see it before I search anything.
- **If I'm a returning user, auto-populate my last search parameters** — city, dates, guest count. I've been here before. Act like it.
