# The “magic jacket” that’ll keep ’em warm when wet
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand Nano Puff catalog send, and the hero is the strongest part. A rust Nano Puff Hoody sits on teal under the headline "One Jacket to Do It All", with the "Gift Guide" tag top right. The copy is concrete: "steelhead, launching a cornice or spending the night on a belay ledge." A fly-fisherman lifestyle shot and a six-tile product grid follow.
- The subject promises a "magic jacket" that "keeps 'em warm when wet". The body never delivers on that. Nothing visible says "wet", "water" or "keeps warm when wet", so the curiosity hook is partly wasted. There is also no price, no percent off and no deadline. The only offer is the "Free Shipping on Orders over $99" strip at the top. This is a product-education and gift send, not a promotion.
- Judgement: well executed and easy to scan, but low urgency and a subject that overpromises on the "wet" claim. It is a middle-of-the-pack 5-6 send in a heavy Gift Guide cadence (flannels 11-18, Black Hole bags 11-21, snow bibs 11-21, Synchilla 11-22).

## What's working

- The hero is large, with one product and a short headline, so the eye lands there first.
- The "Explore" button is clear, and the hero copy is vivid and gift-framed ("the perfect gift for the person who does it all").
- The 2x3 product grid (Men's Hoody, Men's Vest, Women's Hoody, Women's Vest, Kids' Brick Quilt, Mitts) covers several buyers, each with a "Shop" button.
- The navy panel adds product detail: "better overhead range of motion, roomier forearms, increased hood visibility and abrasion-resistant thread."
- The "How to Find the Right Insulated Jacket" guide gives useful content and a "Read More" button.

## What's weak

- The "keeps them warm when wet" claim from the subject appears nowhere in the render.
- There is no price, sale or member benefit visible. Only the free-shipping strip carries any offer, and the footer fine print on shipping is tiny.
- The hero "Explore" button is generic, and the grid has six identical "Shop" buttons with no prices.
- The email is long and the hero is repeated: a jacket hero, then a lifestyle photo, then a grid, a panel and an article. The primary action gets diluted.
- The footer holds a lot of dense legal text, and "Read Yvon's Letter" is easy to miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the "warm when wet" proof in the hero. Add a line such as "Stays warm even when damp" with a small icon, so the subject's promise is met above the fold.
- 2. Show prices on the grid tiles, such as "From $199", and replace "Shop" with product-specific CTAs like "Shop the Hoody".
- 3. Change the hero CTA from "Explore" to "Shop the Nano Puff Hoody".
- 4. Cut the grid to four tiles, or add a clear gift deadline such as "Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery".
- 5. Move the free-shipping threshold into a visible body line.
