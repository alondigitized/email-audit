# You hit the full $11.2M spend 🏂
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a civic-engagement send, not a commerce send. Patagonia reports that its $11.2M election-season credit giveaway ($100 Patagonia credits to get out the vote) was claimed in about a week. It then pivots to a "nearly 500K people have plans to vote" milestone and asks for voter-plan sign-ups and volunteering with the Environmental Voter Project and League of Conservation Voters. The line illustration, large serif headlines and two orange pill buttons are polished and on-brand.
- For a fleece buyer, the email has almost no commercial pull. It has no product, no price and no member benefit. The subject "You hit the full $11.2M spend" also reads like a spend or receipt notice until you open it. The credits are gone, so a subscriber who missed the giveaway gets nothing but a "sign up" ask.
- It works as brand-values content and it's well made. As a send for this recipient it's low-yield. The 10/06 "Vintage styles for the mountain minded" send at least gave a product reason to click. This one gives a civic reason, and it arrives after the incentive has ended.

## What's working

- The headline "You hit the full $11.2M spend!" is huge, with generous white space, and is clearly the first thing the eye lands on.
- The "Nearly 500K people have plans to vote, thanks to you" block gives credible social proof and a communal tone.
- The hand-drawn illustrations (the fly-fishing, hiking and skateboarding crew, and the "POLL ON" circle) are distinctive and unmistakably Patagonia.
- The copy is honest. It says plainly "we're out of credits to give away" instead of dangling a dead offer.
- The layout renders cleanly, with no broken images or overlap, and the orange pill buttons contrast well on the cream background.

## What's weak

- The first button, "Act Now", doesn't say what the action is. It follows copy about credits that no longer exist, so the ask is unclear.
- There is no product, price or member benefit anywhere. It has nothing for a fleece shopper.
- Both CTAs are low-intent generic labels ("Act Now", "Sign Up"), and the second one asks for more than the first.
- The closing banner "Get your people to the polls." repeats the hero idea instead of adding a new reason to click.
- The email has no deadline, such as a registration cutoff or Election Day date, so it carries no urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the CTA specific and lead with the real next step.** Replace "Act Now" with "Make your voting plan" and put a date next to it, such as the registration deadline or Election Day. Drop the second button or make it clearly secondary ("Volunteer with EVP").
- 2. **Lead with the person, not the company's spend.** The subject should tell the reader what they accomplished or what to do next, not frame it as a budget figure.
- 3. **Add one product-adjacent tie-in** for the commerce audience, such as a small "Worn Wear / Vote" line or a link to a store-hours voting-leave policy. Otherwise accept that this is a pure values send and don't expect clicks.
- 4. **Cut the closing banner or give it a new job.** It could carry the date and the voting-plan link.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Nearly 500K people have a plan to vote. Do you?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Credits are claimed, but you can still make your voting plan in 2 minutes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find your polling place and volunteer with our nonprofit partners.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The `upn=` token format indicates a wrapped-link ESP, so click URLs are probably redirected through the same host. I couldn't confirm that because the `href`s were cut off.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`:** Some clients and the web-view version use the title. It should hold the subject line or a short description.
- **Pixel missing alt:** QA flags the `trk.na.patagonia.com/wf/open` image. It is a 1×1 tracking pixel, so the practical impact is low. Adding `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` would silence the check and keep screen readers from reading the URL.
- **Web fonts:** CopernicusEmail and RidgewayEmail load from `images.patagonia.com/eMedia/fonts/...` as woff/woff2 with `font-display: swap`. Outlook (`mso`) has its own `.Serif` and `.NoSerif` fallbacks, which is correct handling. I couldn't see the inline `font-family` stacks, so I can't verify that web-safe fallbacks are present.
- **Redundant inline CSS:** `border-collapse` is declared three times on the preheader table, and `margin` is declared twice on `<body>` (`margin-right:auto; margin-left:auto; margin:0px; ... margin:0`). This is harmless but adds weight.
- **Preheader:** `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden` with a `&zwnj;&nbsp;` padding hack is the standard pattern. No issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or the subject line (`You hit the full $11.2M spend 🏂`). The truncated body can't be fully ruled out.
