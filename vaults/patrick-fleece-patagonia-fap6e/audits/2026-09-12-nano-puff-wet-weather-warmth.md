# Nano Puff: wet-weather warmth
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia's sixth genuine product pivot in a row — no navy/cream "50% off" typography sale, just a real hero product story built around the Nano Puff. The hero shot is strong: a moody, weather-worn model in the jacket with hood up against a grey sky, on-brand and immediately legible as "wet-weather warmth" without reading a word of copy. The four-jacket grid (men's/women's Jacket and Hoody, in olive, blush, rust, and burgundy) gives real color and style choice, each with its own Explore CTA.
- It's competent but not exceptional — this is the same template as "New quilted layers" and "New fall colors and patterns," just with a different hero product. There's no offer, no urgency, no price, and the "Seventh Chance" editorial content block pulls focus away from the product push in the back half. Solid brand-consistency execution, mediocre performance email.

## What's working

- Hero image directly visualizes the subject line's "wet-weather warmth" claim — hood up, weatherproof-looking shell, moody grey backdrop.
- Clean 2x2 product grid gives clear color/style options (men's + women's, jacket + hoody) with individual CTAs per item.
- Free shipping banner is visible above the fold without being loud or spammy.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, no overlap.
- Editorial "A Seventh Chance" story reinforces brand values (climbing heritage) tied naturally to the Nano Puff's climbing origin story in the intro copy.

## What's weak

- No offer or price anywhere — pure product marketing, so click intent relies entirely on product interest, not urgency.
- "Explore" as CTA copy is repeated five times and is vague — doesn't tell the reader what happens next.
- The editorial block and "Earth is now our only shareholder" footer message dilute the product focus in the second half of the email.
- Preview/subject don't reference any timing or reason to act now — reads as evergreen content, easy to defer/ignore.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap "Explore" for product-specific CTA copy like "Shop Men's Jacket" / "Shop Women's Hoody" — raises specificity and click intent per rubric.
- 2. Add a concrete hook — even a soft one like "as seen on [route/season]" or a price point — since there's no offer to anchor urgency.
- 3. Move or trim "A Seventh Chance" into a secondary send; it competes with the product story rather than supporting it.
- 4. Test subject lines that lead with the weather use-case rather than the product name alone, since "wet-weather warmth" is doing the real work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rain-proof warmth, zero bulk`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Nano Puff, built for wet weather`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lightweight insulation that keeps performing when it's soaked.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four colors, two fits — find your Nano Puff.`
