# Backpacks and totes to ease your load
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "Backpacks and totes to ease your load" is Patagonia doing its cleanest catalog format — hero section, five stacked product tiles with capacity specs, named categories, and an editorial brand block at the bottom. - What drags it down is timing and dilution. Two days after the July 13 travel essentials send, a subscriber who opened that one is being asked to browse an adjacent category with no new hook — no seasonal peg, no back-to-school call-out, no price angle. - Hero headline "Take a Load Off" is a clean pun that earns its keep without being cutesy.

- Named shop CTAs ("Shop Technical Packs," "Shop Daypacks," "Shop Totes") are specific and verb-led, not generic "Discover" filler.

## What's weak

- Subject line is purely descriptive ("Backpacks and totes to ease your load") and buries the wordplay that already lives in the hero — a wasted asymmetry.
- The "Parenting: DISASTER STYLE" brand content block at the bottom is a tonal non-sequitur that fragments the email's commercial focus.

- **Subject Alt A:** `Take a Load Off — New Packs & Totes`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The pack that gets you there (and back)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Technical packs, daypacks, totes — find the one that fits your haul.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Terravia, Black Hole, Refugio and more — sized for every adventure.`

- 3. **Cut the "Parenting: DISASTER STYLE" block or move it to a standalone send.** It reads as an editorial CMS injection that wasn't edited for fit. It dilutes the purchase intent built by the five product tiles above it.
- 4. **Borrow the star-rating treatment from the June 29 send.** That email showed visible ratings per product; this one doesn't. Even one or two high-rated SKUs with stars would close the credibility gap caused by having no promotional offer.

Gmail (web and Android) removes `@import` rules entirely. Custom fonts will not load for Gmail recipients. The fallback stack `Helvetica, Arial` is defined inline, so visual degradation is limited — but if the custom font is brand-critical, it needs to be served via `<link>` in `<head>` with a `<!--[if !mso]>` guard, not `@import`.
