# Our new cosmic print
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia in "new arrivals" editorial mode — the email is anchored by a single print story called "Moon Tripper," a cosmic/botanical pattern applied across a women's dress and men/unisex shorts. The creative threads together the print origin story (a "Meet the Designer" section) with a color palette showcase and a sale footer — three distinct sections crammed into one scroll.
- The print storytelling is genuine Patagonia: nature-inspired, artisan-sourced, with a brief designer bio. But the email tries to do too much. It opens strong on the "New Arrivals / Explore Spring '26" hero, pivots to the Moon Tripper editorial, then drops into a six-color-swatch grid of unrelated new arrivals, then hard-cuts to a Web Specials sale banner. That's three different asks, and none of them land with full force. The sale mention at the bottom is especially jarring — Patagonia literally ran a "Up to 50% Off Web Specials" dedicated email on May 15, and this feels like a recycled append.
- Verdict: a competent content email that's diluted by module bloat. The print story is worth telling; it just needed to be the whole email.

## What's working

- The "Moon Tripper" print name is distinctive and memorable — it anchors the email with a real story hook.
- The "Meet the Designer" section adds authenticity; showing the designer's source material (the mushroom/botanical photo) differentiates this from a standard product push.
- The six-color-swatch "New Colors by Nature" section is visually efficient — readable names (Brain Bark, Bobcat Brown, Aqua Stone, Faded Magenta, Orange Peel, Basin Green) communicate the palette quickly.
- The hero CTA "Explore Spring '26" is visible and unambiguous above the fold.

## What's weak

- Subject line "Our new cosmic print" is vague — it names no product, no offer, and no benefit. Compare to the May 16 "Shorts made to move freely," which was product-specific.
- The email has four distinct sections (New Arrivals, Moon Tripper story, New Colors by Nature, Web Specials) with no connective tissue — it reads like four emails stapled together.
- The Web Specials footer rehashes the May 15 sale send; a subscriber who already opened that email will find this stale.
- The product images for the Moon Tripper SKUs (dress and shorts) are small — the print detail that makes this story worth telling is hard to appreciate at thumbnail size.
- No price, no discount, no size call-out in the hero section — the only commercial hook is buried at the very bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the Moon Tripper the entire email.** Cut the New Colors by Nature grid and the Web Specials footer. One print story, one or two larger product images, the designer section — that's a complete, differentiated send.
- 2. **Bigger product imagery.** The print is the star; show it. The thumbnail-sized Moon Tripper dress and shorts undersell the visual appeal.
- 3. **Add a price or range.** Even "starting at $79" would give the reader a commercial anchor without breaking the editorial tone.
- 4. **Surface the designer's name in the body.** The "Meet the Designer" heading is present but the designer's name isn't legible at a glance — make it the subhead.
- 5. **Drop the Web Specials footer or replace with a contextual link.** "Already in our sale? Grab it in Moon Tripper while it lasts" would be more coherent than a generic sale banner.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `<meta name="viewport">` tag** — The `<head>` contains no viewport declaration. Without it, some mobile clients default to desktop-width rendering before media queries fire, causing layout shifts or zoom-out behavior on first load.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `<body>`** — The body inline style sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`. The W3C-recommended value is `100%`, not `none`. `none` disables iOS Mail's auto-scaling entirely, which can make small text inaccessible when the email renders below intended size. The `-ms-text-size-adjust: 100%` in the same declaration is correctly set.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` renders as an empty string. Samsung Mail and some notification surfaces display this as the email identifier. Should contain a descriptive fallback (e.g., `Patagonia — New Cosmic Print`).

**Redundant `border-collapse` declarations** — The first `<table>` style contains `border-collapse:collapse` three consecutive times. Harmless but indicates a templating hygiene issue.

**`GalaxieCopernicus` font has no `.woff` fallback** — The `@font-face` declaration for `CopernicusEmail` provides only `woff2` format. The `RidgewayEmail` family correctly provides both `woff` and `woff2`. Email clients supporting woff but not woff2 (older Android native mail) will fall through to the MSO serif stack (`Palatino, Georgia`) — acceptable, but inconsistent with the Ridgeway treatment.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Correctly applied throughout the visible excerpt. No issues.
