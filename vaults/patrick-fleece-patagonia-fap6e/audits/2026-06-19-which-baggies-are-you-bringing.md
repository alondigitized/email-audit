# Which Baggies are you bringing?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Patagonia goes full-franchise mode with this Baggies roundup — a clean product-catalog send built around the brand's most iconic shorts line. After the June 12 "Best sellers that earn their keep" send (which was diffuse and editorially soft), this one has a tighter premise: the Baggies family in every variant. The "Do-Anything Shorts" hero copy lands a clear versatility message, and the family grid — Men's, Women's, Baby — signals a broad-audience play without fragmenting the creative.
- The subject line is the best thing about this email. "Which Baggies are you bringing?" breaks cleanly from Patagonia's recent run of declarative subjects and earns the open. It implies a decision (choice between variants), implies a trip (you're going somewhere), and it's 31 characters — effortless on mobile.
- The ceiling for this send is a 6 on clicks, and the reason is structural: no price move, no urgency, and the product grid cards are too small and generic to do any real selling. This is pure discovery-mode — great for subscribers who are already Baggies-curious, low yield for anyone who needs persuading.

## What's working

- Subject line breaks Patagonia's recent declarative streak ("Best sellers that earn their keep," "Our ambassadors' favorite gear") — the "Which...are you bringing?" interactive framing is the best opener in the last month of sends.
- Seasonal timing is dead-on — mid-June, shorts, outdoor lifestyle hero image.
- "Do-Anything Shorts" headline communicates versatility in three words without leaning on specs.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: full-bleed hero → featured product → family grid → accessories → editorial — the eye flows naturally.
- "Baggies Shorts for All" section broadens addressable audience (men, women, baby) without fragmenting the send into separate emails.

## What's weak

- No price signal anywhere — full MSRP with no offer, no free shipping threshold, no member pricing means this is discovery-only and click intent depends entirely on pre-existing desire.
- Grid product cards are too small and copy-free — "Shop" buttons with no differentiating detail (liner or no liner, weight, length) make the grid feel like a catalog thumbnail page, not a decision-aid.
- "Taking the Kids to Point Nemo" editorial block at the bottom is a tonal non-sequitur — it creates dead weight right when checkout intent should be peaking.
- "Adjustably Adorbs" / Baby Baggies Cap placement breaks shopping momentum before the editorial section without closing the loop on a product CTA.
- No urgency anywhere in the email body — no seasonal close date, no "limited sizes," nothing to move a passive reader to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a light urgency anchor** — "Ships before the long weekend" or a summer-window note ("The best shorts weather is here for exactly this long") would lift clicks without feeling manipulative.
- 2. **Give the Men's Baggies Lights hero card more copy** — one line on what makes Lites the Lites (1.5 oz, no liner, fastest-drying) turns a product image into a buying reason.
- 3. **Swap generic "Shop" buttons for segment-specific CTAs** — "Shop Men's," "Shop Women's," "Shop Baby" get subscribers to the right landing page faster and feel more intentional.
- 4. **Move or cut "Taking the Kids to Point Nemo"** — it's editorial content that belongs in a dedicated story send, not as a tail anchor on a product-conversion email.
- 5. **Test a variant-choice subject line** — the "which" frame is good; sharpening it to a specific decision would perform even better.
