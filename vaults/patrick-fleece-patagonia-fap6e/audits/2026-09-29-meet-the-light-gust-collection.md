# Meet the Light Gust collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia's twelfth straight non-sale send, and it's a clean product-launch layout. The "Wind Chill" hero has a struck-through "Wind", a person with binoculars, and a teal copy block with one pill CTA. Below it are three product tiles (jacket, hooded pullover, vest), each a macro detail shot followed by a flat-lay. It's easy to scan and looks polished, and the render has no visible bugs.
- It's still a catalog. There's no price, no offer and no deadline, and the only commercial hook is the "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" bar at the very top. Two days ago (09-25, "Casual jackets for mixed weather") the brand ran outerwear for shoulder season, and this send covers much of the same ground. The story tile at the bottom ("The Joy of Junky Windslop") is a nice brand touch, but it competes with the product pitch. Verdict: a solid, on-brand 6, not a send that pushes anyone to buy today.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and the hierarchy is clear. The headline, one-paragraph pitch and "Explore the Collection" pill are all visible in the first screen.
- Each product tile follows the same pattern: detail crop, flat-lay, name, then Men's/Women's buttons. That makes the send easy to scan and gives specific click targets.
- The copy names real use cases: "winter surf checks, light camping, everyday brisk-weather wear". That's more useful than generic outerwear language.
- The palette is consistent (teal, mustard, navy), and the send is on-season for late September.
- The "Joy of Junky Windslop" story tile and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" footer keep the brand voice intact.

## What's weak

- There are no prices, promotions or deadlines anywhere. The only offer is the free-shipping bar, which has an asterisk and is buried in the footer fine print.
- The hero's "Wind Chill" headline doesn't match the subject line "Meet the Light Gust collection". It also reads as cold rather than warm, and the struck-through "Wind" is a bit cryptic.
- The email is very long, with three full tiles plus a "More lightweight jackets & vests" block plus a story plus the footer. The CTAs are all small outlined pills, and the closing story button ("Read the Story") is as prominent as the shopping ones.
- There's no social proof, ratings, fit notes or "why Light Gust" detail beyond one sentence. Only the hero paragraph gives any product info, so the tiles are just names.
- The outerwear theme overlaps with the 09-25 send, and there's still no sale or news hook. That adds to a steady run of editorial sends with no reason to click now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete reason to act now: a launch date, "new for fall", a low-stock note or member early access. Show a starting price ("from $149") on each tile, since none is visible.
- 2. Cut the layout to the hero plus two products and one "shop all" CTA. Move the story into a secondary slot or a separate send.
- 3. Make the CTA buttons filled and higher-contrast, and label them with the product, for example "Shop Light Gust Jackets".
- 4. Align the hero headline and the subject. Either rename the hero to match "Light Gust", or use the "Wind Chill" idea in the subject.
- 5. Add a one-line fit or warmth note to each tile (weight, insulation, packability), plus a star rating.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Light Gust: wind-proof warmth, from $149`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The layer for brisk-weather days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Insulated, stretchy layers for surf checks and camping. Jackets, pullovers and vests.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Light Gust jackets and vests, plus free shipping over $99.`
