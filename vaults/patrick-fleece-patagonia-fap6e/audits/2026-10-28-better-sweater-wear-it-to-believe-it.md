# Better Sweater: Wear it to believe it
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, product-led catalog send for the Better Sweater fleece line. The hero is a surfer holding boards in front of a corrugated wall, and "Warmth to the Core" sits on a tan panel with one "Shop" button. Below that, the email is a long scroll of tiles: a women's quarter-zip, a vest and jacket pair, a men's crewneck, a men's jacket, another vest and quarter-zip pair, then gloves and a beanie. The design is tidy and the brand voice is intact. But nothing in it gives a reason to act today.
- The weak point is the subject-to-body match. "Wear it to believe it" promises a conviction story. The body delivers a paragraph of lifestyle copy and a lot of product tiles. The only offer is the "Free Shipping on Orders over $99" banner at the very top. The trade-in section at the bottom ("Trade In to Gear Up") is the one real value proposition, and it is buried after seven product blocks. Compared with the 2026-10-22 "Nano or Micro? Pick your puff" send, which gave one clear decision, this one is a catalog dump.
- Judgement: competent and on-brand, but it follows the same no-promotion pattern as the last week's sends (Refugio, rainwear, "New colors for fall"). Expect an average click rate, not a standout.

## What's working

- The hero image is distinctive and on-brand. A surfer with colorful boards signals "lifestyle, not discount" and fits Patagonia's tone.
- Product tiles are consistent and easy to scan. Each has a clear title, a clean photo and a trademark-correct product name.
- Mixed women's and men's sections let a broad audience find their category quickly. Each section ends with a labeled "Women's" or "Men's" button.
- The trade-in module has a real hook (credit toward new or used gear) and a distinct purple CTA, "Trade In. Get Credit."
- The footer closes on the "Earth is now our only shareholder" line, which reinforces brand values.

## What's weak

- No offer or urgency. Free shipping over $99 sits in a thin black bar at the top and is easy to miss.
- The primary CTAs are generic. "Shop" and "Women's"/"Men's" are small grey buttons with weak contrast against the tan background.
- The email is very long, about seven product blocks before the trade-in section. The strongest value prop is the last thing most readers will reach.
- There are no prices, reviews or ratings on any product, so there is no social proof and nothing to help a purchase decision.
- The hero copy is long and gives no concrete reason to buy now. It never delivers on "wear it to believe it."

## Recommendations

- 1. Lift the trade-in offer to the top, or add a one-line mention of it in the hero. It is the only concrete value in the email.
- 2. Cut the product blocks to one hero product (the quarter-zip) plus one row of men's and women's options.
- 3. Replace "Shop" with specific CTAs such as "Shop the Better Sweater" and make the buttons higher contrast.
- 4. Add a price or "from $X" line and a star rating to the featured product.
- 5. Make the free-shipping threshold larger, or tie it to a deadline.
