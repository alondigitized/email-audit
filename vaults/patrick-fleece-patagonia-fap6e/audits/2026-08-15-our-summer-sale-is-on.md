# Our summer sale is on
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth "40% off" clearance send since Aug 5, and it's the laziest of the bunch — the hero photo (two cyclists bikepacking on a mountain road) and the "Up to 40% Off Past-Season Styles**" hero block look like a near-identical crop/recolor of the Aug 12 "Up to 40% off sale continues" creative. The subject line "Our summer sale is on" is also the vaguest one yet — it doesn't even say what the sale is or by how much, forcing the open on brand trust alone.
- Where it does work is the "Where the Sale Comes Alive" retail-store block, a genuine new beat that pivots from discount fatigue to an in-person, brand-experience pitch. That's a smart wedge, but it's buried after the sale pitch and only two products (a fleece pullover, a pair of shorts) are shown, so there's very little to actually shop before the email pivots to "go to a store instead."
- Judgment: this is a tired repeat of an already-repeated promo, redeemed only by decent store CTA copy. Given the brand history, this should score at or below the Aug 12 send (5.00/10), not above it.

## What's working

- Clear, bold "Up to 40% Off" hero numeral is the first thing the eye lands on — strong visual hierarchy.
- Two distinct CTAs ("Shop the Sale" and "Find a Store") are both visible, unambiguous pill buttons.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly with no overlap or broken assets.
- "Where the Sale Comes Alive" is a fresh creative beat versus the last few clearance-only sends.
- Free shipping banner at the very top is a clean, immediate value-add with a threshold ($99).

## What's weak

- Subject "Our summer sale is on" gives zero specifics — no % off, no category, no urgency — a step down from Aug 7's "Up to 40% off starts now."
- Hero photography (cyclists on a mountain road) has no visible connection to the products shown below it (fleece, shorts) or to "summer."
- Only two products are shown before the email pivots away to a store-visit pitch — thin for a "sale" email.
- This is visually and thematically close to the Aug 12 send — third-plus repeat of the same past-season clearance in under two weeks.
- "**" and "*" fine-print asterisks on the headline offer without immediate context reads as hedged, consistent with the Aug 12 critique.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject line carry the actual offer — "Up to 40% off is on" or lead with a category, since vague sale language depresses opens after three prior clearance sends have already trained subscribers to expect a number.
- 2. Swap the hero image for one that actually features the discounted product categories (fleece, boardshorts) instead of an unrelated bikepacking scene — it undercuts the "40% off past-season styles" message directly beneath it.
- 3. Expand the product grid to 4-6 items so the email earns the "sale" framing before pivoting to the store CTA.
- 4. Move "Where the Sale Comes Alive" earlier or trim it — right now it competes with, rather than supports, the primary shopping CTA.
- 5. Retire this clearance creative for the next send — three-plus near-identical 40%-off emails in ten days risks list fatigue; rotate back to a non-sale angle per the Aug 2 fall-launch precedent.
