# Our 2026 Gift Guide
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent seasonal catalog send. The gift guide drops a bit early, on Nov 4, and it is the first Patagonia email in the recent run that reads as holiday-driven. The earlier sends (Nov 1 rainwear, Oct 30 baselayers, Oct 28 Better Sweater) were all category education. The flat-lay hero (jackets, mittens, a beanie, a duffel) is attractive, and the "Our picks to get 'em out there" headline has some personality.
- The email has no discount and no urgency. The only commercial hook is the thin "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" bar at the very top. Two near-identical "Gift Guide" buttons are the only primary CTAs. The body is a grid of eight tiles with "Shop" buttons, and none of them show a price. The middle teal block ("Gifts for getting after it") is mostly copy and activity pills, which makes the email feel long.
- Judgement: it is on-brand and renders cleanly, but it is a browse-the-catalog email. It gives a reason to visit, not a reason to act today. It is a solid 5, and it follows the same no-promotion pattern as the previous five sends.

## What's working

- The flat-lay hero is a clear, giftable image. It sets the holiday context immediately.
- The headline "Our picks to get 'em out there." carries Patagonia's voice and ties the guide to its activity audience.
- The activity pills (Trail Run, Fly Fish, Climb, MTB, Snow) let readers pick a lane.
- The tile grid mixes products (R1 Air, Fjord Flannel, Retro-X, Down Sweater) with lifestyle shots, and the Worn Wear tile ("Gifts That Keep Going / Shop Used") is on-brand.
- The gift card block near the bottom gives a clear last-minute option with a strong black button.

## What's weak

- There is no concrete offer. The free-shipping bar is tiny, and its asterisk points to fine print at the bottom.
- There is no urgency or shipping-cutoff date, which is a missed chance for a gift email.
- Two identical "Gift Guide" buttons, plus eight "Shop" buttons with no verb-plus-noun specificity, make CTA copy generic.
- No prices or reviews appear anywhere, so products give no reason to click.
- The middle teal section is a large block of copy with small pills. The hierarchy gets mushy there, and the email is long for a single message.
- The footer shareholder blurb ("Earth is now our only shareholder") and the long photo-credit text add clutter after the gift card.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a dated hook near the top, such as a shipping cutoff or a free-shipping threshold in the hero, and move it out of the micro-text bar.
- 2. Replace the two "Gift Guide" buttons with specific ones, for example "Shop Gifts Under $150" and "Shop Gifts for Climbers".
- 3. Put prices on the product tiles (R1 Air, Down Sweater) and add a star rating or a "bestseller" tag.
- 4. Trim the middle copy block and the pills into a single "shop by activity" row.
- 5. Cut the photo-credit paragraph or shrink it.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fleece, down and flannel picks, plus gift cards for last-minute shoppers.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **No broken links detected.** The QA run probed 12 material links and skipped 17, which were footer, utility and social links ranked low by click likelihood. The skipped links were not validated. No probe failures were reported.
- **Open-tracking pixel:** `https://trk.na.patagonia.com/wf/open?upn=u001.…` is on a branded tracking subdomain, which is good for deliverability. The `upn` token is long, but nothing indicates a defect.
- **Not verifiable:** The HTML was truncated before the body, so click-tracking wrapper behavior, redirect chains and the unsubscribe link target could not be checked.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`** is confirmed in `<head>`. It gives no document title for web-view or "view in browser" rendering and for some assistive tech.
- **Image missing alt:** QA flagged the open-tracking pixel (`trk.na.patagonia.com/wf/open`). This is a 1×1 beacon, so low severity. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to mark it decorative and avoid screen-reader noise.
- **Preheader:** The hidden preheader ("Give 'em what they really want.") uses `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden`, followed by the `&zwnj;&nbsp;` padding hack. This is correctly implemented.
- **Fonts:** Custom webfonts (`CopernicusEmail`, `RidgewayEmail`) load from `images.patagonia.com` with `font-display: swap`. The Outlook (`mso`) conditional block overrides them with Palatino/Georgia and Helvetica. The font-family fallback stacks in the body were not visible in the truncated source.
- **Cosmetic redundancy:** `<body>` repeats `margin` and `border-collapse` declarations, and `-webkit-text-size-adjust` is set to `none` and then `100%`. The later value wins, so there is no functional impact.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The truncated remainder was not inspected.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** (QA WARN).
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found** (QA WARN). One-click unsubscribe is therefore unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results not found** (QA WARN). SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
- **Caveat on all three:** QA notes these headers may be stripped by the AgentMail relay. They are unconfirmed rather than confirmed absent. Re-test against raw headers from a direct delivery, such as a Gmail "Show original".
- **Sender identity:** The From address `funhogs@na.patagonia.com` is on a subdomain consistent with the tracking domain `trk.na.patagonia.com`, so alignment is plausible.
- **Not verifiable:** The physical postal address and the body unsubscribe link sit in the truncated footer.
