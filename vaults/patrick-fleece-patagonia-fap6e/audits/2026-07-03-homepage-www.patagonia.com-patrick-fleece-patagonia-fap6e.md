# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-07-03
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia being Patagonia — the top bar immediately leads with their environmental accountability work, which I'd actually tap before I scroll an inch. But then the hero drops "Pack to School" on me, which is a new-purchase push dressed in mountain scenery. I'm here to check Worn Wear or read about the Supply Chain Report, not to buy a backpack for a college freshman.

## What's working

- **The top banner — "Read Our Work in Progress Report ▼"** — I'm clicking that before I even look at the hero. That's Patagonia's environmental accountability reporting, and I read every one of those. Smart placement.
- **Ironclad Guarantee callout in the hero sub-copy** — "durable, versatile designs backed by our Ironclad Guarantee" is the only reason I'd consider a new purchase. That phrase does real work for me.
- **Mountain terrain hero image** — I'm not a backpack shopper right now, but putting your product in scree fields and granite instead of a campus quad tells me you know your audience is older and outdoors-serious.
- **Recycled materials mention** — buried in the sub-copy but it's there: "recycled materials." That's table stakes for me trusting this brand, and they said it.

## What's weak

- **"Pack to School" framing** — I'm 44. My backpack is 12 years old and I repaired the zipper pull last spring. This campaign is talking past me entirely at the headline level.
- **"Explore" CTA is too vague** — For a brand that writes manifestos about honesty, "Explore" is the most corporate-generic button copy imaginable. "Shop Backpacks," "Find Your Pack" — anything more specific would help.
- **No Worn Wear entry point above the fold** — If I'm a returning customer (and I am), my first instinct is to check what's on Worn Wear before I even look at new. That path is invisible until I crack open the hamburger menu.
- **Zero pricing or offer signal** — No free-shipping threshold, no member pricing, nothing. For someone who is actively comparing new vs. used cost, this doesn't help me make any decision.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Shop Worn Wear" secondary CTA below the primary Explore button** — even a small text link would catch me and any other repair-first customer immediately.
- **Swap "Explore" for "Shop Backpacks"** — it takes two words to make a button actually useful; use them.
- **Move the Work in Progress Report banner copy to something clickable with more signal** — it's just text and a caret right now. A mini-visual or one striking stat ("X% of materials recycled") would make it a real hook, not just a nav item.
- **Surface the Ironclad Guarantee as a persistent badge or module** — it's in the hero sub-copy but it disappears. For customers like me, that guarantee is the entire brand promise. It deserves real estate.
