# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is unmistakably Patagonia — clean, mountainside-lit, no-BS layout that trusts the product and the imagery. Right now they're pushing "New Arrivals" with a young female boulderer as the hero, which is fine photography but lands about forty degrees off-axis for me. The irony of Patagonia — the company that literally ran "Don't Buy This Jacket" as an ad — opening with "New Arrivals" is not lost on a guy who has owned the same Snap-T since 2003.

## What's working

- **"Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" top banner** — I actually notice this. If there's a Snap-T colorway or a Baggies short on sale, I might dig in. Patagonia gear at half price is the one scenario where I'll buy new instead of used.
- **Hero image quality** — the bouldering shot is gorgeous. Natural rock, outdoor light, no studio fakery. This brand still knows how to shoot.
- **Clean mobile layout** — logo, search, cart, hamburger. Nothing fighting for space. My medium-tech brain doesn't have to figure out where to tap.
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — that's Patagonia copy. It sounds like something from one of their essays in the Tin Shed archives. It lands.
- **No popup, no forced modal** — I got to look at the page without being ambushed. That matters more than most brands realize.

## What's weak

- **"New Arrivals" as the hero message** — this is the one that stings. Patagonia built their reputation on telling people *not* to buy new gear. Leading with "New Arrivals" feels like they forgot who they are, or worse, decided to chase DTC metrics over ethos.
- **"Explore" CTA is meaningless** — explore what? Explore where? A button that says "Explore" on a Patagonia homepage tells me nothing. Is it menswear? Worn Wear? Activism? I'm not clicking a mystery door.
- **Zero Worn Wear visibility** — my primary relationship with this brand is through Worn Wear. It's not even hinted at in the visible viewport. That's a miss for anyone who's been a customer since the dial-up era.
- **Young female rock climber as sole representative** — I'm not saying she shouldn't be here, but she's the only signal above the fold. A 44-year-old guy in a Synchilla who hikes in the Presidentials doesn't see himself anywhere.
- **No activism hook above fold** — this company donates 1% of sales to environmental causes. They literally gave the company to the Earth. None of that is visible. It's the thing that keeps me loyal, and it's invisible.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "New Arrivals" with a message that earns the click** — something like "Built to Last. Worn Wear Inside." or split the hero between new gear and repaired gear. Give me a reason to feel good about browsing, not just buy-buy-buy.
- **Swap "Explore" for something specific** — "Shop New Arrivals" or "Find Your Trail Layer." Anything that tells me where I'm going. "Explore" is what brands write when they don't know what they're selling.
- **Add a Worn Wear tile or banner strip below the hero** — it doesn't need to be the hero, but it needs to exist above the scroll line. Even a small "Worn Wear — Give your gear a second life" link would make me exhale.
- **Drop a trust/mission line near the promo banner** — one sentence like "1% for the Planet since 1973" next to the Web Specials strip costs nothing and reminds me why I'm here instead of REI.
