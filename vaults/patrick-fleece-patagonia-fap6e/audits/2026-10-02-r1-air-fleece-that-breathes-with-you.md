# R1 Air: fleece that breathes with you
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia's fifteenth straight non-sale send, and it is a clean, product-led pitch for the R1 Air fleece. The giant "R1® Air" wordmark sits over a periwinkle-blue field, with a crew-neck fleece hero above it. The copy is concrete: it wicks sweat fast and dumps excess body heat. The Breathable Fleece texture close-up and the hoody callouts (off-shoulder seams, two front pockets, full-front zipper) show real product thinking. It reads like a product page more than a promotion.
- The weakness is the same one that runs through the last several sends. There is no offer, no price and no member benefit. The only commercial hook is "Free Shipping on Orders over $50*" in the top bar, and it is easy to miss. The email has four "Shop Men's" or "Shop" buttons plus a story CTA, so the click path is scattered. The Skintrack story block near the bottom pulls attention away from the product. Compared with 10/01 "Sun protection for sweating in", it is the same format and the same cadence. It is polished and on-brand, and a subscriber who wants this jacket will enjoy it, but it is not urgent.
- Judgement: a well-built catalog page, and clearly aimed at men. It is a pass-through for anyone who isn't already shopping for fleece.

## What's working

- The "R1® Air" wordmark is oversized and immediately tells you the product, so the hero is unmistakable.
- The "No sweat." lead-in and the copy about wicking sweat and venting heat are specific benefit language, not generic fluff.
- The Breathable Fleece texture close-up and the hoody callouts (off-shoulder seams, two pockets, full-front zipper) show fit and function details.
- The four-product grid (Zip-Neck, Jacket, Vest, Crew) gives a quick sense of range, with consistent muted colors.
- The mountain photo, the Skintrack story and the closing "Earth is now our only shareholder" line keep the brand voice consistent and trusted.

## What's weak

- There is no price, percent-off, loyalty perk or deadline anywhere. The only incentive is a free-shipping bar at the very top.
- The call to action is repeated as a generic "Shop Men's" or "Shop" four times. None of them names a product or a benefit.
- The email is very long, and the hero CTA falls below the fold on mobile. A reader has to scroll past the headline and body copy to reach the first button.
- The Skintrack story block at the bottom competes with the product push, and the dense photo-credit text under it reads as clutter.
- The product grid labels have no prices, colors or review stars. The tiles are small and the text is tiny.
- Fifteen consecutive non-sale sends in a row makes the cadence feel repetitive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete reason to act in the hero: a price ("From $149"), a restock note, or a fall-weather hook. Right now nothing says why today.
- 2. Move the primary CTA up so it sits next to the wordmark, and use a specific label like "Shop R1 Air" instead of the generic "Shop Men's".
- 3. Add prices and a review count or star rating under each product in the grid.
- 4. Cut the Skintrack story into its own send, or shorten it, so the email has one job.
- 5. Offer a women's path. The email is entirely men's, and a split CTA would widen the audience.
