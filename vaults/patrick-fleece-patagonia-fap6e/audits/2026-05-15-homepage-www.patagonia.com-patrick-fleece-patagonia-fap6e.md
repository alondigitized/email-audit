# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- This is my home turf — Patagonia — and I recognize the bones instantly. But the homepage is pitching me "New Arrivals" with prints and a young blonde climber, which is a weird flex for a brand that made its name telling people not to buy new stuff. The banner sale is a nice nod, but I'm not feeling the activist heartbeat above the fold. Feels like the marketing team had a busy quarter.

## What's working

- **The sale banner** — "Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" is the first thing I see. Even a repair-first guy checks sales when something he actually needs is finally affordable.
- **Clean layout** — Logo, search, cart, hamburger. No pop-up assault, no newsletter modal in my face. That's earned respect.
- **Rock climbing hero image** — Wrong demographic, but right world. I've been in that kind of granite overhang. The shot is technically stunning and it does anchor the brand identity.
- **The camera/visual-search icon** on the hero — low-key useful for someone like me who wants to ID a piece I saw on a trail.

## What's weak

- **"Explore" CTA is a cop-out** — After 20 years of shopping here, "Explore" tells me nothing. Is this Men's? Worn Wear? Activism? Fly fishing? Yvon's memoir?
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — This is the kind of copy that sounds nice but says nothing. Patrick doesn't need inspiration; he needs a fleece lining for his aging R2 vest.
- **No Worn Wear / repair entry point** — This is the single biggest miss for a loyalist like me. If you know your customer, you put a Worn Wear or Ironclad Guarantee link above the fold or in a sticky nav chip.
- **No activism hook** — An email from Patagonia about a dam removal campaign would get me to open a browser. This homepage doesn't follow through. The brand DNA is missing above the fold.
- **Young female climber hero on a brand with heavy Gen X male loyalty** — I'm not offended, just not targeted. There's a whole cohort of guys who have been buying here since the early 2000s and we're invisible on the landing screen.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "Explore" for "Shop Web Specials"** — the sale banner is the strongest hook; let the CTA cash in on it immediately instead of sending me into a generic browse loop.
- **Add a "Worn Wear" chip to the sticky nav or directly below the hero** — one tap, right there, for customers who repair first. Costs nothing, honors the brand promise, and captures the highest-LTV cohort.
- **Test a hero that includes a male 40+ subject using a piece of repaired or well-worn gear** — not aspirational climbing performance, but the "this jacket is 15 years old and still going" story that made Patagonia what it is.
- **Surface one activism callout above the fold** — a one-liner like "1% For The Planet since 1986" or a link to the current campaign. This is table stakes for the brand's most engaged customers.
