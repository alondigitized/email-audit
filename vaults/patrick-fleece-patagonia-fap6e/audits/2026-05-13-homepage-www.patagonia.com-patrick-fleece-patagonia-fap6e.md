# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Patagonia's doing what Patagonia does — leading with a big outdoor lifestyle shot and burying the commercial intent behind a word like "Explore." The top banner is the most interesting thing on this page to me: a "Work in Progress Report" sounds like the kind of environmental accountability content I'd actually open. But the hero is pushing new product at me, which, honestly, is exactly the tension this brand has been living in since 2011.

## What's working

- **The activism banner** — "Read Our Work in Progress Report" is exactly the kind of thing I'll tap before I tap anything else. That's Patagonia putting its values front and center in the header bar, and it earns a click from me every time.
- **Clean mobile layout** — Logo, search, cart, hamburger menu. No clutter. Loads fast. I'm not fighting the page.
- **The hero image itself** — Bouldering outdoors, big rock, natural light. Brand-consistent, no corporate photoshoot vibe. Even if I'm not the model, I get the world they're pointing at.
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — Earthy, not hustle-culture. The copy register is calm, which matches how I want to shop.

## What's weak

- **"Explore" as a CTA** — Explore what? Where does that send me? New Arrivals are for people who buy new. I'm here to check the Worn Wear section or see if my Snap-T style got a colorway update. This button tells me nothing.
- **Zero Worn Wear / repair signal above the fold** — If Patagonia's most loyal customers are the ones who fix before they buy, why is the entire hero selling new product? I have to already know Worn Wear exists to go find it.
- **The hero subject** — Younger female athlete on a boulder. Nothing wrong with that image. It's just not speaking to me specifically. I'm a 44-year-old guy who hikes, camps, and patches his gear. I don't see myself in this shot.
- **No navigation visible without tapping the hamburger** — On mobile, everything is buried. I can't even see "Men's" or "Fleece" without an extra tap. That friction adds up for medium-tech users like me.

## Recommendations

- **Swap or A/B the hero for a repair/longevity story** — Show a worn Synchilla with a Worn Wear patch badge overlay. Copy: "Still going." That earns loyalty from existing customers rather than recruiting strangers.
- **Surface Worn Wear in the top nav or announcement bar** — The activism banner slot is powerful real estate. Use it to rotate between the Work in Progress Report and a Worn Wear / Repair drop — both are on-brand and drive deeper engagement from loyalists.
- **Make the CTA honest about destination** — Change "Explore" to "Shop New Arrivals" so I know what I'm clicking into, or add a second CTA below it: "Shop Worn Wear" — two paths for two different buyers.
- **Add a men's fleece/outerwear category chip below the hero** — Even one row of tappable category pills (Fleece, Outerwear, Worn Wear) would cut the navigation friction on mobile dramatically.
