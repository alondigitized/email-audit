# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I've been wearing Patagonia since before half their current marketing team was born, and the first thing I see is a "50% Off" sale banner — which, I'll be honest, makes me feel a little weird given that the whole point of this brand is supposed to be buying less. The hero is a young woman bouldering in what looks like a desert canyon, pushing "New Arrivals" with some line about "natural abundance." Looks like Patagonia's still dressing up commerce in earth-tone language. The bones are solid; the soul is slightly muted.

## What's working

- **The "Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" banner** catches me immediately — if I need to replace something that's truly worn out, I'm going to at least look at what's on clearance before I decide whether Worn Wear has it
- **The boulder-problem hero image** is genuinely evocative — that's real rock, real movement, real outdoors; Patagonia hasn't gone lifestyle-studio on me
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — that copy register (nature-as-inspiration, not "performance-engineered") is exactly the language that keeps me loyal to this brand over Arc'teryx or TNF
- **Clean mobile render** — no jank, no layout shift, the hamburger menu icon is where I expect it; I can navigate without frustration

## What's weak

- **"New Arrivals" as the hero** — leading with new product acquisition is the wrong message for anyone who's read a Patagonia catalog from 2011 onward; I came here expecting to see some kind of environmental story or a path to Worn Wear, not the same hero a fast-fashion brand would use
- **"Explore" CTA copy is lazy** — explore what, exactly? New arrivals? All products? Bouldering gear? This button tells me nothing; I might tap it anyway but I shouldn't have to guess
- **No Worn Wear / repair entry point visible** — the feature I use most on this site is completely invisible above the fold; I have to hunt through the hamburger menu
- **Young female climber doesn't represent my demo** — I'm not saying put a 44-year-old dude in a Snap-T on every hero, but there's no one here who looks like a customer in my age bracket
- **50% Off banner creates mild cognitive dissonance** — for a brand whose most famous ad told me not to buy their jacket, leading every session with a sale percentage feels like a quiet identity compromise

## Recommendations

- **Rotate one hero variant that links directly to Worn Wear or Repairs** — even a single "Fix it before you replace it →" strip below the hero would ground the activism cred that loyal Gen X customers came here for
- **Change "Explore" to "Shop New Arrivals"** — two extra words, zero ambiguity; if they want to keep the minimalist aesthetic, fine, but "Explore" in a vacuum is not a CTA
- **Add a small "Worn Wear" shortcut badge or sticky nav link** — even a text link below the hero image like "Looking for used gear? → Worn Wear" would serve returning buyers like me without cluttering the design
- **Swap the banner copy to lead with context** — instead of just "Now Up to 50% Off," try "Web Specials — clearance before it goes to Worn Wear" or something that reframes the sale through the brand's sustainability lens rather than just screaming a discount
