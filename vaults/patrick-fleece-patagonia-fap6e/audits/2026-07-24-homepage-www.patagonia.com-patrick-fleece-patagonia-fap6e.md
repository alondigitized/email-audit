# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-07-24
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- I open Patagonia and the first thing they show me is a kid on a rock wall. I love that Patagonia makes kids' gear — genuinely — but I'm a 44-year-old guy who just sent my Snap-T back through Worn Wear for the third time. The saving grace is that black banner at the very top: "Read Our Work in Progress Report." That's the Patagonia I showed up for. Everything else above the fold is aimed at someone's eight-year-old, not me.

## What's working

- **"Read Our Work in Progress Report" banner** — that black top bar is the most Patrick-coded thing on the page. I'd tap it before I'd tap anything else. Patagonia's activism transparency is why I'm here at all.
- **Clean mobile layout** — no junk, no pop-ups, no "10% off if you sign up" modal firing before I can see the page. Respects my time.
- **"Made for outsiders" tagline** — even if it's for kids, that's Patagonia's voice. It signals I'm in the right place ideologically.
- **Photography quality** — real terrain, real climbing, no studio white backgrounds. Feels honest.

## What's weak

- **Hero is Patagonia Kids** — I came here shopping for myself or as a Worn Wear advocate. Zero above-the-fold content points me toward men's gear, repairs, or used.
- **No Worn Wear entry point** — the single most important section for a customer like me isn't visible anywhere in this viewport.
- **No navigation categories above fold** — there's a hamburger menu, but I have to tap into it to find men's. One extra tap is one extra reason to bounce.
- **Zero price or offer information** — even a free-shipping reminder would give me a reason to stay in shopping mode rather than reading-activism mode.
- **No social proof** — no review counts, no "staff favorite" badges, nothing that tells me this product earned its place.

## Recommendations

- **Rotate the hero to a men's lifestyle or Worn Wear story** — a 40-something guy fixing a fleece at a repair event is literally the Patagonia brand promise. Show it.
- **Surface a Worn Wear or repair CTA above the fold** — even a slim secondary banner: "Repair before you replace → Worn Wear" would tell me this brand still means it.
- **Add a horizontal quick-nav strip below the hero** — Men's / Women's / Worn Wear / Sale / Activism, one tap each. The hamburger isn't fast enough on mobile.
- **Pin the Work in Progress Report link but add a companion commerce hook** — "We're fixing the industry. While we work on it, shop secondhand." Connect the activism content to the actual shopping experience.
