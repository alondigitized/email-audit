# New colors for fall
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a color-story send with no promotion in it. It opens on a soft sage header, "New Fall Colors," then splits into two swatch-style blocks, Glacial Blue and Vellum Green, with an "Explore" button on each. The photography is excellent and the palette is cohesive. It looks like a brand lookbook, not a sales email.
- The weakness is that nothing here gives a reader a reason to act now. The only offer is the free-shipping bar at the top ("Free Shipping on Orders over $99*"), and the fine print at the bottom qualifies it. There is no price, no named product, and no deadline. Three different CTAs (Shop New Arrivals, Explore, Watch) compete, and two of them go to the same kind of destination. The mountain-biker image has the labels "NEW ARRIVALS / FALL 2026" and no CTA, so it is a dead scroll block.
- Compared with the 10-22 "Nano or Micro? Pick your puff" send, which helped the reader make a choice, this one asks them to browse two color families. It's pretty and on-brand but low-conversion. It belongs in the 5-6 range, in line with the recent run of catalog sends.

## What's working

- The tinted header with a centered logo and one short paragraph tells you the idea right away.
- The two 4-up photo grids (Glacial Blue, Vellum Green) show real product textures. The italic color names over each grid are a strong editorial touch.
- Pairing Glacial Blue (calm) with Vellum Green (crisp) in the body copy gives the seasonal theme a clear story.
- The "Behind the Design" video block with Nick Stockton adds brand depth, and the Watch button is clear.
- Free shipping is visible in the top bar above the logo, though the threshold is on the high side.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer, price, or named product appears anywhere in the body, and there is no urgency.
- The full-bleed mountain-biker image takes a large amount of scroll space with no CTA and only "NEW ARRIVALS / FALL 2026" labels.
- The "Explore" buttons do not say what you will find (jackets? fleece? shirts?), and "Shop New Arrivals" appears twice with the same copy.
- The "Behind the Design" video competes with the shopping CTAs, and the long free-shipping fine print and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" block add weight at the end.
- The hero copy never states a category or gives a reason to open now, so the email reads as an editorial piece without a conversion hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name specific hero products under each color block, such as "Better Sweater in Glacial Blue" with a price, so each "Explore" button leads to a product and not a vague collection.
- 2. Cut or shrink the mountain-biker image, or add a CTA to it, so the Vellum Green block sits higher.
- 3. Change the CTAs to specific verbs and nouns: "Shop blue jackets," "Shop green fleece."
- 4. Pick one primary CTA and demote the video to a text link.
- 5. Add a reason to act now, such as limited sizes in the new colors or a clear free-shipping deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Glacial Blue and Vellum Green are here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Two new fall colors, 12 ways to wear them`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Calm blue or crisp green: see the fall palette on jackets, fleece and shirts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet the colors our designers can't stop wearing this season.`
