# Leave your pants at home
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a shorts email dressed up as an action-photography email — a person leaping across a rock canyon at the top, then straight into a "No Shortage of Action" shorts grid. It's a clean, well-organized product email: two clear categories (shorts, then hoodies for "cover"), and a genuine sale banner ("Up to 40% Off Past-Season Styles") tacked on at the bottom. Compared to the Aug 5 Capilene Cool Sun send (5.00/10, product-forward but repetitive) and the Aug 7 outlet send (7.00/10, first straightforward sale), this one splits the difference — it's a category/seasonal push (shorts + layers) with the outlet sale demoted to a bottom-of-email afterthought rather than the headline.
- The subject line "Leave your pants at home" is genuinely fun and on-brand, distinct from the recent run of sun-protection and fall-launch subjects — a good sign of subject-line variety this month. But the email itself undercuts its own energy: the hero photo is dramatic, then the layout goes flat and catalog-y (four small product tiles, plain product names, no pricing, no sizing/fit info) for most of the scroll. The sale banner at the bottom is the most compelling offer in the email and it's buried below two full product grids.
- Verdict: solid, on-brand seasonal push with a great subject line, but it leads with the weakest CTA (two generic "Shop Shorts" buttons) and buries the actual discount. Move the 40% off up, and this jumps a point or two.

## What's working

- Hero image (cliff jump) is high-energy and matches the "leave your pants at home" adventure framing.
- Two clear, well-organized product sections (Shorts, then Hoodies/layers) with consistent product photography.
- Gender-split CTAs ("Shop Men's Shorts" / "Shop Women's Shorts") make navigation easy for the shopper.
- "Free Shipping on Orders over $99" banner up top is a visible, concrete offer right at the fold.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, spacing is consistent throughout.

## What's weak

- No pricing shown anywhere on the four shorts or four hoodies — a shopper can't judge the offer without clicking through.
- The actual discount (40% off past-season styles) is buried at the very bottom, after two full product grids — most opens won't scroll that far.
- CTA copy is generic ("Shop Men's Shorts," "Shop the Sale") rather than specific to a product or price point.
- Product names are technical/dense ("Men's Hydropeak Volley Shorts - 16"", "Women's Capilene Cool Sun Hoody") with no supporting fit or use-case copy beyond the shorts intro paragraph.
- "Need Clothes?" as a section header for the sale is a flat, low-energy transition after the strong opening hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "Up to 40% Off Past-Season Styles" banner up — either right under the hero or as a second module before the full-price shorts grid — since it's the strongest single offer in the email and currently gets buried.
- 2. Add price (or "from $X") under each product tile so shoppers can gauge value without a click-through.
- 3. Swap the generic CTA buttons for product-specific or benefit-specific copy, e.g. "Shop Quick-Dry Shorts" instead of "Shop Men's Shorts."
- 4. Give the hoodie section a stronger hook than "Bring a Bit of Cover" — tie it back to the adventure narrative from the hero image (e.g., layering for cold mornings/nights on the same trip).
