# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This looks like Patagonia doing what Patagonia does — clean design, real outdoor photography, earnest brand copy. But the hero they've chosen, a young woman bouldering, paired with a "New Arrivals" headline, reads like they're courting the REI Instagram crowd rather than the guy who's been wearing the same Snap-T since 2003 and actually meant it when he read the Don't Buy This Jacket manifesto. The 50% off banner is fine, but it actively undercuts the buy-less-buy-better ethos I come to patagonia.com to reinforce.

## What's working

- **"Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" top banner** — prominent, immediate, stops the thumb even if I'm skeptical of the "up to" qualifier
- **Clean, uncluttered mobile layout** — no modal ambush, no forced email capture on open; Patagonia respects my time
- **Real outdoor photography** — the bouldering shot is authentic, not a studio setup; I can see actual rock, actual light, real rope and gear
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — this is the earnest, nature-forward register I know from two decades of reading their emails; it doesn't sound like a Shopify template
- **No dark patterns visible** — I open this page without being hit in the face

## What's weak

- **Hero persona mismatch** — young female rock climber is not who I am; I'm here for fleece, retros, and gear I plan to wear for twenty more years
- **"New Arrivals" headline** — this is literally the opposite of why I use patagonia.com; I'm a Worn Wear shopper; the brand trained me to repair first and they're leading with newness
- **"Explore" CTA** — this is the vaguest possible button copy; explore what? I have no idea where this tap sends me
- **Zero Worn Wear visibility** — the program I actually use has no above-fold entry point whatsoever; it should be a persistent nav chip, not buried in a hamburger
- **No activism or environmental content** — Patagonia is the brand I chose because of the 1% for the Planet pledge and the repair ethos; none of that is visible here, which makes the homepage feel like any other outdoor retailer
- **Hamburger-only navigation** — I can't reach Men's Fleece in one tap; there are no exposed category shortcuts

## Recommendations

- **Add a persistent "Worn Wear / Repair First" entry point** — a nav chip or a secondary banner module below the hero; loyal customers like me are coming back specifically for this and right now we're invisible to the page
- **Replace "Explore" with specific CTA copy** — "Shop New Arrivals" or "See Spring Prints" sets expectations; "Explore" tells me nothing about where I'm going on a phone
- **Swap "Up to 50% Off" for a grounded offer** — "Fleece from $69" or "Sale: Jackets 30% Off" is more credible and less bait-and-switch; it matches the brand's stated transparency values
- **Test a hero that speaks to the repair/retro loyalist** — a Snap-T lifestyle shot with a 40-something in a real outdoor moment, or a "Worn Wear: Give It Another Life" module, would signal to long-tenured customers that this homepage is still for them
