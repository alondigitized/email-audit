# Shorts made to move freely
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-first email pushing Patagonia's multisport shorts line under the "Performance Foundations" umbrella — four SKUs highlighted with an active hero shot and brief selling copy. The creative is clean and seasonally on-point for May. But it's trying to do three different jobs in one send: sell shorts, publish a trail-running personal essay, and surface the Web Specials sale. None of the three gets full runway.
- The 50% off Web Specials banner — the single most conversion-relevant element — sits below both a four-product grid AND a 500-word editorial piece. A subscriber who opens, scans, and bails at the "Read the Story" section will never know there's a sale on. Yesterday's "Up to 50% Off Web Specials starts now" send had the offer as the lead; today's email buries it as an afterthought.
- The result is a pleasant brand email that undersells itself. Strong on craft, weak on commercial follow-through.

## What's working

- **Hero image and headline earn their space.** The desert running shot is aspirational and season-appropriate; "Shorts for the pursuit." is punchy Patagonia-voice copy.
- **Four-SKU grid is a solid browse prompt.** Multi Trails, Trailhead, Terrebonne, Quandary — each with its own product image — gives the reader genuine product variety to scan.
- **No render bugs.** Text, images, and layout are clean across the full scroll.
- **"Earth is now our only shareholder" closing lands on brand.** Reinforces the trust/values signal that makes Patagonia subscribers less price-sensitive.

## What's weak

- **The 50% off offer is buried below an editorial piece.** Most subscribers will bail before they reach the Web Specials banner; the conversion driver is invisible on a casual scan.
- **"Performance Foundations" is a weak CTA.** It's a category label, not an action — a reader can't tell what clicking it will do.
- **The editorial and the sale have nothing to do with each other.** "Running Led Me Home" is a good standalone story but it interrupts the purchase funnel.
- **Subject line signals nothing about the sale.** "Shorts made to move freely" is a product description; subscribers who want deals have no reason to open over yesterday's explicit 50% off subject.
- **Preheader leaks navigation/utility text.** The inbox preview shows "This is Patagonia | Learn More" rather than complementary selling copy — a wasted second impression.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with or at least surface the sale in the subject.** The Web Specials are running concurrently — give the subscriber a reason to open beyond brand affinity.
- 2. **Move the Web Specials banner above the editorial.** Better yet, merge it with the product section: show the shorts at 50% off, not as a separate section below a story.
- 3. **Replace "Performance Foundations" button copy.** "Shop Shorts" or "See All Shorts" does what a CTA should: tells the reader where they're going.
- 4. **Either cut or footnote the editorial.** Publish "Running Led Me Home" in a dedicated story email. In a product/sale email, it's friction between the browse and the click.
- 5. **Write a real preheader.** The preheader should extend the subject, not repeat it or leak nav links.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Shorts built for all day — now up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The shorts your summer needs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four styles. Fast-drying. Deep pockets. Web Specials pricing inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Multi Trails, Quandary, and more — now in the Web Specials sale.`
