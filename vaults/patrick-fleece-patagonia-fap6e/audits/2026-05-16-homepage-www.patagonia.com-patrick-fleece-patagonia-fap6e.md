# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is my brand, and I know it like I know the worn zipper pull on my '97 Snap-T. Right now Patagonia's leading with "New Arrivals" and a 50% off web specials banner — which is a little rich coming from the company whose founder once took out a full-page ad telling you not to buy their jacket. It's still clean, still them, but the homepage is playing the same retail game as everyone else today, and that dissonance hits hard when you've been repairing your gear for twenty years.

## What's working

- **The "Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" banner** is prominent and dark — I actually noticed it before the logo. If I need to replace a piece that's truly dead, I'm checking those specials first.
- **The bouldering hero image** is legitimate outdoor lifestyle — not some model in a studio. It's real terrain, real activity, and that's on-brand even if it's not my scene.
- **The CTA button** is cleanly visible — white pill against the rock face, legible, not lost in the image.
- **Mobile layout** is tight and functional: search, cart, and menu exactly where I expect them.

## What's weak

- **"New Arrivals" as the primary message** is a gut punch for someone who bought into Don't Buy This Jacket. You're leading with "buy new stuff" on a brand I trust precisely because they tell me not to.
- **"Explore" is the laziest CTA copy possible.** Explore what? Worn Wear? Activism? New fleece? I don't know what I'm tapping into.
- **Zero Worn Wear or repair signal above the fold.** For a loyal customer, that's my first instinct — is there anything used or repairable here? Nothing above the fold answers that.
- **No activism or mission content visible.** This is the brand that runs op-eds about democracy and files environmental lawsuits. None of that is surfaced where I can see it.
- **The hero model is a young woman** — that's not a problem in itself, but there's no signal that this page knows who I am as a returning buyer.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "New Arrivals" hero for a split hero or a Worn Wear callout.** Something like "Same Jacket, Second Life — Shop Worn Wear" would hit me square in the chest. Keep the new arrivals, but lead with the repair/reuse message that actually differentiates this brand.
- **Change "Explore" to something specific** — "Shop Worn Wear," "See What's On Sale," or even "Shop New Arrivals" would at least tell me where I'm going.
- **Add a Worn Wear or repair module as the second scroll-section**, right below the hero. It doesn't have to be a separate page — just a banner tile: "Repair before you replace. Worn Wear →"
- **Surface the activism calendar or a recent campaign headline** in a thin strip below the nav. Even one line — "Patagonia Action Works: See what we're fighting for" — signals to a reader like me that this brand still means what it says.
