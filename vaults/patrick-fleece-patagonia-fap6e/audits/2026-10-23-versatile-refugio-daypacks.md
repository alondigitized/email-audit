# Versatile Refugio Daypacks
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, product-led catalog send with one job: show that the Refugio Daypack comes in three sizes. The hero is a hiker on a cliff above a cloud inversion, with "Different sizes for different shenanigans." and a "Shop Daypacks" button. Below it, three stacked cards show the 26L, 30L and 32L packs, each with a one-line job: "Everyday essentials," "Pack a little extra," "Bring it all." The hierarchy is easy to follow and there are no render bugs.
- The weakness is that it gives you no reason to act now. There's no price, no deadline and no member perk. The only offer is a free-shipping bar at the top, and the "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" line is a standing offer. A pack that can run past $99 qualifies for it anyway, so it adds nothing. The back half then drifts into a second "Smart, Adaptable Storage" pack block, a surf story ("Land of the Midnight Surf") and an "Earth is now our only shareholder" letter. That dilutes the product focus.
- Compared with the Oct 22 "Nano or Micro? Pick your puff" send (6.00), this is the same pattern: a comparison-style catalog with a clear purpose. It's competent and on brand, but it won't move anyone who isn't already shopping for a pack. Judgement: solid, forgettable, and in line with the 6/10 run.

## What's working

- The three-size stack (26L / 30L / 32L) with a one-line use case for each makes the choice easy to scan.
- The hero photo is strong and on brand, and the headline is playful and sets Patagonia's tone.
- The "Shop Daypacks" CTA is a clear, visible button and is repeated under the cutaway product image.
- The interior cutaway image under "Smart, Adaptable Storage" shows the product's real feature rather than just its color.
- Nothing is broken: images load, text doesn't overlap and spacing is consistent.

## What's weak

- There is no price, deadline or member benefit anywhere, so nothing creates a reason to click now.
- Each "Shop" link is a small underlined text link on the 26L, 30L and 32L cards. It's weak compared with the hero button, and the cards don't show prices.
- The last third (surf story and shareholder letter) pulls attention away from the packs. It adds a second and third message to a one-product email.
- The free-shipping banner is a generic standing offer, and the asterisk sends you to dense fine print at the bottom.
- The size cards don't say what fits in each pack, such as a laptop size or liters in plain terms, so "Everyday essentials" is vague.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price and a detail to each pack card (for example "26L · fits 15" laptop · $89") and make "Shop" a real button. This is the biggest lift because it turns a gallery into a decision tool.
- 2. Cut the surf story and shareholder letter into a separate send, or move them below a clear divider, so the email stays on one topic.
- 3. Give a reason to buy now, such as a fall commute or travel angle, or a member perk or Worn Wear tie-in, rather than a standing shipping bar.
- 4. Lead the subject with the decision ("which size") instead of the product name. The Oct 22 "Pick your puff" angle worked better.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The Refugio Daypack, in three sizes`
