# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is unmistakably Patagonia — the logo, the bouldering hero, the earth-tone palette, and especially that black top banner pointing me to their "Work in Progress Report." That banner is the single most Patrick-relevant pixel on this screen. The rest of the hero, though? "New Arrivals" with a young woman crimping on a boulder is doing exactly the thing the Don't-Buy-This-Jacket ad was supposed to undo. Pushing product newness to the guy who repairs his Snap-T is a brand-trust tax I'm not sure they intended to collect.

## What's working

- **"Read Our Work in Progress Report" banner** — That's the first thing I see and I'm already tapping. Patagonia putting their environmental accountability front and center above the fold is the only retailer behavior I still respect. I read every one of these.
- **Clean mobile layout** — Logo, search, cart, menu — nothing overlapping, nothing broken. The hero photo loads fully, text is legible over the image. They didn't wreck the mobile experience.
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance" copy** — Not "shop the latest drops." Earthy language that sounds like it was written by someone who actually hikes, not a conversion-rate optimizer.
- **Bouldering lifestyle image** — Even if the climber isn't me, at least it's outdoors on real rock, not a studio shoot. That's the brand's visual language staying consistent.

## What's weak

- **"New Arrivals" hero** — For a brand that literally told me not to buy new jackets, leading with "New Arrivals" as the above-the-fold message is cognitively dissonant. This is a homepage choice that costs brand trust with its most loyal segment.
- **Young female climber in the hero** — I'm 44 and looking for Synchilla, Worn Wear, and maybe a Capilene base layer. The demographic signal here reads 22-year-old sport climber. Nothing wrong with her — she's just not me.
- **"Explore" CTA is a dead end** — Explore what? New arrivals I philosophically object to buying? Give me "Shop Worn Wear," "Find Your Repair Center," or even "Men's Fleece." "Explore" is a cop-out button.
- **No Worn Wear or repair shortcut above the fold** — The single biggest differentiator Patagonia has from every other outdoor brand — the Worn Wear program — is invisible. I have to dig through the hamburger menu to find it. That's a miss.
- **No navigation shortcuts visible** — The hamburger menu is the only way in. No category strip, no quick links to Fleece, Worn Wear, or Activism. One extra tap every time adds friction for a returning customer who knows exactly what they want.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "New Arrivals" hero with a Worn Wear or repair-cycle story** — Even just seasonally. A hero that says "Your old jacket has more miles in it — find out how" converts people like me and reinforces the brand's core thesis. Ship it as an A/B.
- **Change the CTA from "Explore" to something specific** — "Shop Responsibly" or "Find Worn Wear Gear" or even "Men's Fleece" would send me somewhere I want to go. Explore is noise.
- **Add a category strip below the hero** — Five tappable tiles: Worn Wear / Fleece / Baselayer / Activism / Repair. Costs one scroll row, eliminates three taps for returning customers.
- **Surface the Work in Progress Report link as a secondary module**, not just a text-only top banner — Give it a real image and a one-line pull quote from the report. That banner deserves more than 12px of black bar.
