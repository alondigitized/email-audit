# You can now shop used on Patagonia.com
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-news send, not a promo. Patagonia announces that Worn Wear secondhand gear now sits on Patagonia.com next to new product. The hero is strong: two climbers in vintage rugby shirts, with a Worn Wear badge sitting between the photo and the headline. "Shopping Used Just Got Easier" says the whole story in one line. The pitch is mission-driven and honest, and it fits the brand's values.
- It's weak on commerce. No product, price or example of savings appears anywhere. "High value. Lower price." makes a price claim with no number behind it. The second half drifts into an editorial essay ("Why Do We Keep Buying New Stuff?") and an Yvon letter, which compete with the shop CTA. A subscriber who cares about gear will like the idea, but nothing in the email tells them what they'd actually find or save.
- Next to the 2026-10-13 "New for fall" send and the 2026-10-12 Heyes Peak send, which were both product-led, this one is more distinctive but less shoppable. It's a good brand moment and an average click driver.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and on-brand. The climber photo, the Worn Wear badge and the large serif headline land immediately.
- The headline states the news plainly: used gear is now on Patagonia.com.
- The trust copy is credible. Worn Wear gear is "inspected, authenticated, cleaned" and backed by the Ironclad Guarantee.
- The vintage rugby shirts in the hero show the "rare prints and colors" the copy promises.
- Each block has a single outlined CTA, so the layout is easy to scan.

## What's weak

- No price, discount or "from $X" appears, so "Lower price" is an unsupported claim.
- No used product is shown. The second image is a hand-off of a blue puffer, and the third is a lavender down jacket whose link is an essay, not a product.
- Three CTAs go to different places (Shop Used, Explore Worn Wear, Read the Story), and the story CTA pulls traffic away from the shop.
- The Archana Ram essay block and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" footer add editorial weight to what should be a shopping email.
- The CTA buttons are small outlined pills on dark navy and sage. They are low-contrast next to the large headlines.
- No sizing, availability or condition-grade detail is visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a row of 3-4 real used items with a "from $X" price and a "was new at $Y" comparison. That one change would do the most for clicks.
- 2. Cut to one primary CTA. Make the essay a text link in the footer.
- 3. Rename the lavender-jacket block as a product (for example, a used down jacket with price and condition) instead of a story teaser.
- 4. Replace "High value. Lower price." with a concrete line such as "Used down jackets from $89."
- 5. Make the CTA buttons solid, high-contrast fills.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Used Patagonia gear, now on Patagonia.com`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Worn Wear is here: used jackets from $__`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Inspected, cleaned, and backed by our Ironclad Guarantee. Browse rare prints and classic colors.`
