# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-07-17
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I respect Patagonia — always have, probably always will. But I just opened their homepage expecting to see something about Worn Wear, or maybe their environmental work, and instead I'm looking at a kid dangling off a granite wall with a "Shop Kids' & Baby" button front and center. That activism banner at the very top ("Read Our Work in Progress Report") is genuinely the most me thing on this page — and it's six pixels tall. This brand knows what it stands for, but right now this homepage is talking to my sister, not to me.

## What's working

- **Activism banner ("Read Our Work in Progress Report")** — this is the exact thing I open Patagonia emails to read. It's tiny but it's there, and it tells me the brand hasn't forgotten why it exists. I'd tap it.
- **"Made for outsiders" tagline** — three words that still hit. That's the Patagonia I believe in. It doesn't say "performance" or "lifestyle." It says *outsiders*. That's my language.
- **Clean, no-clutter layout** — hamburger menu, wordmark, cart. No pop-ups, no newsletter interstitials, no "10% off if you sign up" banner blocking the hero. I can actually see the page.
- **Lifestyle photography quality** — the rock climbing image is real, not staged-in-a-studio. The geology is specific (looks like desert sandstone). That authenticity matters to me.
- **Bottom editorial modules starting to appear** — the arch/canyon image peeking in below suggests they're going somewhere environmental and editorial. That's promising content if I scroll.

## What's weak

- **Hero is misdirected for me** — I don't have kids, and even if I did, a kids hero above the fold means I've been immediately deprioritized as a returning adult customer. I should at least see a carousel indicator telling me men's gear is one swipe away.
- **No path to Worn Wear** — this is literally the thing that makes Patagonia different from every other outdoor brand. It's not visible anywhere above the fold. For someone who buys used-over-new, it's invisible.
- **Navigation is a black box** — I get a hamburger. That's it. I have no idea what's in there without tapping and waiting. On a small screen this means I'm one extra tap away from every category I care about.
- **No activism depth above the fold** — the "Work in Progress Report" banner is a 6-pixel-tall afterthought when it should be a headline for someone like me. They literally have a brand story that most companies would kill for and it's sitting in the tiniest real estate on the page.
- **No returning-customer recognition** — I've been shopping Patagonia since the mid-2000s. There's nothing acknowledging that. No account nudge, no "your wishlist," no loyalty balance. Feels like walking into a store you've shopped at for 20 years and the clerk acts like they've never seen you.

## Recommendations

- **Add a Worn Wear / repair CTA in the hero carousel** — even a second slide that says "Fix it first. Worn Wear." would stop people like me from bouncing. Takes one slide, zero new product, and signals the brand values I came here for.
- **Elevate the activism banner** — pull "Read Our Work in Progress Report" out of that thin black strip and give it a full-width editorial moment, maybe with a compelling visual. That banner is doing the heaviest brand-trust lifting on this entire page and it looks like a cookie notice.
- **Surface Worn Wear in the navigation tray** — not buried three levels deep, but at the top-level hamburger menu alongside Men's, Women's, and Kids. If I can't find it immediately, I go directly to wornwear.patagonia.com and the homepage loses the session.
- **Add a "returning customer" sign-in nudge in the header** — a small account icon with "Hi, Patrick" or just an active session indicator would tell me this page knows I exist. Even a subtle "sign in for member pricing" feels like acknowledgment.
