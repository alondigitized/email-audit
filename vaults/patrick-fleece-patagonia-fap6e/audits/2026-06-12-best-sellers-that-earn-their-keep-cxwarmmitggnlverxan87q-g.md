# Best sellers that earn their keep
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia in "best sellers" editorial mode — a four-product roundup headlined with "Good Taste" and a muted outdoor store lifestyle photo. The lineup: Men's Capilene® Cool Daily Shirt, Women's 1/4 Zip Fleece Jacket, Black Hole® Duffel 55L, and a Baby Micro D® Snap-T Fleece. The render is clean and on-brand, and Patagonia's visual grammar is intact. That's where the positives end.
- The fatal flaw is the scatter-shot product selection. Men's apparel, women's apparel, gear, and baby — in one scroll — maps to no single subscriber. There is no price, no promotional offer, and no urgency mechanism anywhere on the page. Four identical "Shop Now" CTAs do the least possible work. This is a filler send between stronger campaigns.
- Comparing against the June 8 "ambassadors' favorite gear" send (8/10): that email used named athletes as social-proof anchors and built narrative around specific gear choices. This one drops all of that in favor of a generic grid. It's a step down in both ambition and conversion power.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy — "Good Taste" hero transitions naturally to the four-product stack; no cognitive work required.
- Product names carry specificity ("Capilene® Cool," "Black Hole® 55L") that the CTAs fail to reinforce.
- "A versatile workhorse for all your adventures" on the Duffel is the only product descriptor that earns its space.
- Baby Micro D's "The do-anything layer for busy parents" is a sharp secondary hook for a different buyer segment.
- Render is bug-free — images load, buttons are prominent, text is legible throughout.

## What's weak

- Product mix is too broad — men's, women's, gear, and baby in one email serves no single subscriber segment well.
- No price points, no discount, no offer — the email gives no reason to buy today versus tomorrow.
- "Good Taste" hero headline is lifestyle positioning that earns zero carry into the product copy below it.
- "Shop Now" repeated four times is the weakest possible CTA for a brand known for distinctive voice.
- Environmental footer ("The Everglades of the West," Earth-as-shareholder copy) adds length that competes with the conversion path without supporting it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Narrow the product selection to a single audience segment.** Pick men's summer gear OR women's layering OR gear/bags — not all three plus baby. Depth beats breadth in a best-sellers format.
- 2. **Show MSRP on the product cards.** Even without a discount, anchoring price gives subscribers a reason to click through rather than mentally bookmarking and forgetting.
- 3. **Replace generic CTAs with product-specific copy.** "Get the Capilene," "Grab the Duffel," "Find Your Fit" — any of these beats four identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 4. **Re-inject social proof from the June 8 ambassador template.** A single line like "Worn by [Name] on every backcountry trip" under the Duffel lifts the product above grid-filler status.
- 5. **Move the environmental content or cut it.** If Patagonia keeps the Everglades block, it should open the email (sets the brand ethos) — not close it after four product pitches where it reads as an afterthought.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html

```
Some email clients (notably Outlook on Windows) surface the `<title>` value as the window title bar label. Leaving it empty is benign but inconsistent — either populate it with the subject line or remove the tag.

**Triple-duplicate `border-collapse` on main wrapper table:**
```html
style="... border-collapse:collapse;border-collapse:collapse;border-collapse:collapse; ..."
```
Redundant; no rendering impact but signals a templating/copy-paste artifact. Clean up to a single declaration.

**Conflicting `margin` declarations in `<body>` inline style:**
```html
style="... margin-right: auto; margin-left: auto; margin: 0px; ... margin: 0; ..."
```
`margin: 0` appears twice and clobbers the earlier `margin-right/left: auto` directives. The last `margin: 0` wins in all tested clients, so behavior is correct, but the redundancy is confusing and could cause unintended layout shifts if the template is modified.

**`CopernicusEmail` font missing `.woff` fallback:**
`RidgewayEmail` correctly declares both `.woff` and `.woff2` sources. `CopernicusEmail` declares only `.woff2`:
```css
src: url(https://images.patagonia.com/eMedia/fonts/GalaxieCopernicus/GalaxieCopernicus-Book.woff2) format("woff2");
```
Older Outlook Mac and some enterprise webmail clients that support custom fonts but not `.woff2` will silently fall back to the serif stack (Palatino/Georgia). Acceptable if intentional, but inconsistent with the `RidgewayEmail` pattern in the same file.

**Preheader structure — no issues found.** The `display:none` + `max-height:0` + `zwnj`/`&nbsp;` padding hack is correctly implemented.

**Responsive breakpoints — no issues found.** Dual `@media` blocks (`max-width: 600px` and `max-width: 540px`) with appropriate fluid/hide/show class overrides look correct.
