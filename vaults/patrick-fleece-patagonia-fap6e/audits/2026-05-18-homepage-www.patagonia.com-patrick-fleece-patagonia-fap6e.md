# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is my brand — no question about that — but right now it's pushing New Arrivals at me like I'm a first-timer browsing REI.com for the first time. I've been wearing the same R2 since 2014 and my Snap-T since longer than that. Patagonia knows better than to lead with "new prints" to guys like me, and yet here we are. The bones are right — the climbing shot, the clean wordmark — but the message is pointed at someone twenty years younger who hasn't yet read the Worn Wear manifesto.

## What's working

- **"Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" banner** — the top strip is the one hook that actually catches my attention. If Worn Wear gear or clearance classics are in there, I'm tapping.
- **Full-bleed climbing photography** — quintessential Patagonia visual DNA. I'm not buying new stuff, but seeing a proper outdoor athlete on real rock instead of a lifestyle influencer in a coffee shop reminds me why I trust this brand.
- **Wordmark + clean nav** — no clutter, no pop-up, no promotional overlay wrestling me for attention the second I land. That counts for a lot.
- **"Explore" CTA button** — pill shape, white, legible against the rock face. Easy enough to tap on mobile.

## What's weak

- **"New Arrivals" headline is the wrong hook for me** — Patagonia has built its entire moral authority on "don't buy stuff you don't need." Leading with "new arrivals" to a lifer customer undercuts that. Where's the Worn Wear callout? Where's the repair link?
- **Young female model only** — I'm 44. She looks 25. There's nothing wrong with that image in isolation but it signals this page wasn't built for me or anyone my age who has worn Patagonia through two decades of actual field use.
- **"Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance"** — this is soft marketing copy. Patagonia at its best writes copy that makes me feel a little guilty about not doing more for the planet. This is just flowery. It's catalog language, not manifesto language.
- **Zero Worn Wear / repair entry point above fold** — for a brand whose biggest differentiator is its repair program, that program is invisible here.
- **No free-shipping threshold, no returns mention, no trust signals** — the banner says "Web Specials" but there's nothing telling me what the catch is.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "New Arrivals" hero for a Worn Wear or repair-focused hero at least on return-visitor sessions** — a worn Snap-T with a "Still Going" headline would do more for someone like me than a new-prints push.
- **Add a "Repair before you replace" or Worn Wear shortcut to the banner strip** — literally one line: "Web Specials · Worn Wear Used Gear · Free Repairs." That strip is prime real estate and right now it's doing half a job.
- **Replace "Prints and designs inspired by natural abundance" with something activating** — Patagonia writes better than this. Pull a line from an actual Footprint Chronicles entry or an environmental campaign they're running right now.
- **Rotate the hero model/demographic** — even a second hero card in the same shoot featuring a person in their 40s+ on real terrain would signal that this brand grows with its customers, not past them.
