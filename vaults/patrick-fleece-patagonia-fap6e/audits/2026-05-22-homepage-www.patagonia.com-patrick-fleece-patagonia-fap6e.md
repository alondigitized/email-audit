# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia doing what Patagonia does — serious outdoor brand with a big sale banner slapped on top. The hero is pushing something called "Performance Foundations," which sounds like a product line refresh, not a cause. I've been buying this brand since the late '90s and I know their DNA — this feels like a selling moment more than a mission moment, which isn't what I come here for, but I'm not surprised.

## What's working

- **The "Web Specials—Now Up to 50% Off" banner** — I actually read this. Patagonia stuff doesn't go 50% off often, so when it does I pay attention. That's a real hook.
- **Three CTAs laid out cleanly** — "Explore," "Men's," "Women's" — no confusion, I can get to Men's in one tap. That's the right call for mobile.
- **"Performance Foundations" headline** — it's spare and confident. Very Patagonia. No exclamation points, no "EPIC DEALS." Respects my intelligence.
- **Clean mobile render** — logo, search, bag, hamburger. No pop-ups visible, no modal interrupting the moment. I appreciate that.

## What's weak

- **Mountain bike hero image** — I'm a fleece guy, not a downhill guy. This blurred-out wheel-and-motion shot reads as an REI ad for a 27-year-old. The brand's most loyal customers are people like me, and we want to see a worn-in Snap-T on a ridge, not a trail bike.
- **Zero Worn Wear / repair signal above the fold** — the whole reason I trust this brand is their repair-first ethos. It's completely absent from what I can see. For a guy who checks the site before deciding whether to patch or replace, this is a miss.
- **No activism hook anywhere visible** — this is Patagonia. Environmental mission is the product. I read every one of their cause emails. Nothing above the fold connects to why the brand exists.
- **"For the pursuit of your wildest days" subhead** — a little breathless. Not bad, but it's generic outdoor-ad language. Patagonia can do better than this. They have.
- **No free-shipping info** — for a $200+ average order, knowing the threshold matters before I dig in.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the mountain bike hero for a worn fleece lifestyle shot** — a weathered Snap-T hanging in a mudroom, or someone re-patching a Retool jacket. That's me. That's also the brand's actual story.
- **Add one line of Worn Wear copy to the hero** — something like "Repair it first. Shop anyway." It's honest, it's on-brand, and it converts the people who feel guilty about buying new.
- **Surface the activism hook in the banner strip** — rotate the "Web Specials" banner with a "1% for the Planet" or campaign line. Keep the offer but don't let commerce be the only message above the fold.
- **Add free-shipping threshold to the promo banner** — one small addition next to "Now Up to 50% Off" that tells me the number. Reduces friction before I even start browsing.
