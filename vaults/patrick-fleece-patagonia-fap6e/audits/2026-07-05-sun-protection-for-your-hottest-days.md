# Sun protection for your hottest days
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- "Sun Day Best" is a solid headline — the Sunday Best pun earns its keep — but this is Patagonia's **third sun-protection email in nine days** (June 26, June 28, now July 5), and the June 28 audit already called out the cadence problem. Worse: the subject line opens with the exact same two words as the June 28 send ("Sun protection for..."). Any subscriber who noticed June 28 will feel déjà vu before they open.
- Structurally, the email is clean catalog execution: hero lifestyle image, a six-product Tropic Comfort grid split across men's and women's, a mid-email "Sun Protection Essentials" section anchored by a weak "Explore" CTA, and an "Alligator Paradise" editorial block at the bottom. No pricing, no discount, no urgency. For a July 4th-week send — peak summer heat, peak purchase intent — that's a missed opportunity.
- The email looks fine and renders cleanly. The problem is strategic: Patagonia is burning subscriber patience on the same product story with minimal variation, and this one brings nothing new beyond a slightly different framing and some tights added to the grid.

## What's working

- **"Sun Day Best" hero headline** is genuinely clever — the Sunday Best wordplay gives the worn sun-protection story a fresh hook.
- **Product grid is comprehensive** — showing both tops and bottoms (tights) broadens the basket opportunity beyond prior sends.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breakage, proper Patagonia branding throughout.
- **"Alligator Paradise" editorial block** breaks the catalog monotony and gives the email a content reason-to-exist, not just a product push.
- **Subject length is mobile-friendly** at 37 characters.

## What's weak

- **Third sun-protection send in nine days** — cadence fatigue is real, and the June 28 audit already flagged this. A subscriber who gets three "sun protection" emails in under two weeks trains their finger to skip.
- **Subject line opens identically to the June 28 send** ("Sun protection for...") — this is the single most damaging creative mistake in the email. A/B testing different subject framing costs nothing.
- **"Explore" CTA is generic** — after a lifestyle hero and six product tiles, the button copy should be specific ("Shop the Tropic Line," "Find Your Sun Layer") not a bucket into which everything gets tossed.
- **No offer, no urgency** — July 4th week is a natural hook (peak summer heat, holiday shopping momentum); there's no price point, no "limited sizes," no deadline. Every signal that would push purchase intent is absent.
- **Tights in a sun-protection email is a stretch** — Tropic tights are sun-protective, sure, but leading with tops and bottoms muddles the story. The June 26 and June 28 sends both stayed focused on shirts/hoodies.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the cadence or differentiate hard.** Skip a send or reframe the story entirely — "build your full sun-protection system" (head-to-toe) would at least justify the tights and give this a distinct angle the prior two didn't have.
- 2. **Kill "Explore" — rewrite the CTA to be product-specific.** "Shop the Tropic Line" or "Build Your Sun Kit" converts the button from a dead end into a destination.
- 3. **Add a seasonal urgency hook.** "Peak UV Index week" or "hottest stretch of summer — gear up" tied to a real July heatwave is more credible than a vague "hottest days."
- 4. **Lead subject with the product angle, not the category.** The prior two sends owned "sun protection"; this one should own something else — the tights, the full kit, the specific Tropic fabric story.
- 5. **Bury or remove the "Alligator Paradise" block.** As editorial content, it's interesting. But it competes with the purchase intent built by the product grid and pushes the main CTA down.
