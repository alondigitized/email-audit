# Transition into fall trail running
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a tidy, on-brand seasonal product edit, and it's the strongest of Patagonia's recent catalog-style sends. The forest-green frame, the golden-larch mountain hero and "The In-Between Season" headline set a clear mood. The email then moves from a hero product (Houdini Jacket) to a 2x2 grid of trail gear, and closes on a rain-running story. It is easy to scan and reads as honest rather than pushy.
- It is still the sixteenth straight non-sale send, following "R1 Air: fleece that breathes with you" (Oct 2) and "Sun protection for sweating in" (Oct 1). Nothing here gives a reader a reason to act today: no price, no deadline, no member perk. The only offer is the tiny "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" bar at the very top. Every product CTA is "Shop Men's", which narrows the audience without saying so.
- Overall it is polished but passive. It works as brand and category reinforcement and does little to prompt a click this week.

## What's working

- The hero photo of runners on a ridge with fall color matches the subject and the season.
- The headline and body copy are specific about the use case: heat and sweat, then unexpected storms.
- The Houdini Jacket gets a full-width feature slot, so the eye lands on one clear hero product.
- The product grid is clean and consistent: one image, a name, a one-line benefit and a link per tile.
- The "Running in the Rain" editorial tile adds a story and ties back to the weather theme.

## What's weak

- There is no price, promotion or deadline in the body. The free-shipping bar is tiny and easy to miss.
- All four grid CTAs say "Shop Men's", which excludes women's shoppers and repeats the same button four times.
- The headline and the long paragraph under the hero push the first CTA down, so the hero button sits well past the first mobile screen.
- Product tiles have no prices, colors, sizing or reviews, so there is nothing to compare or trust before clicking.
- The footer stacks a "shareholder" letter and dense fine print after the rain story, which ends the email on a weak note.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to act now. That could be a price on the Houdini, a member perk, or a "free shipping on trail gear" callout moved into the hero.
- 2. Cut the intro paragraph to two lines and put the first CTA above the mobile fold. Make the button say "Shop Trail Running Jackets" instead of "Explore Trail Running".
- 3. Replace the four "Shop Men's" buttons with product-specific CTAs ("Get the Houdini", "Shop wind pants"). Add a Women's row or a clear Men's/Women's toggle.
- 4. Add prices and one proof point per tile, such as a star rating or "packs into its own pocket".
- 5. Move the rain story above the footer fine print and drop the shareholder block from this send.
