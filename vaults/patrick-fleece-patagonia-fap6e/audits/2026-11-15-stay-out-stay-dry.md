# Stay out, stay dry
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-and-proof send for the Torrentshell 3L Rain Jacket. The hero is a person in a green shell crouched in the rain, under a big "Good or Bad Weather" headline. Below it sit two product blocks, men's/women's and kids'/baby, each followed by a five-star customer quote. It closes on a "Keep Your Shell Well" care video and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" footer. It's a clean, on-brand piece, and the reviews are the best thing in it.
- The email has no price and no promo. The only commercial hook is "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" in the top bar, and that's easy to miss. Seven buttons point at three audiences, so there is no single primary CTA. The tone is trustworthy, but a subscriber has no reason to act today.
- Compared with the Nov 9 "New winter layers have landed" send and the Nov 11 down-jacket send, this is the same catalog pattern. Patagonia has now sent several product showcases in a row with no offer in any of them. It's competent and repetitive, so I'd score it a 5.

## What's working

- The Torrentshell is a strong anchor product, and each jacket is shown large, in clean color, with a clear hood and zipper view.
- Two real-sounding reviews, "Always dry" and "The Best of the Bernier"-style fishing-guide and hiking stories, give the email credible social proof. They are the strongest persuasion in it.
- The Men's / Women's / Kids' & Baby buttons split the audience cleanly.
- The visual rhythm of alternating teal and tan blocks reads clearly and feels on-brand.
- The "Keep Your Shell Well" care video adds a useful post-purchase angle and variety.

## What's weak

- There is no price, discount, or deadline. The only incentive is the $99 free-shipping line in a tiny top bar.
- The hero CTA, "Waterproof Jackets," is small and low-contrast, a pale outline button on a gray-green field.
- Seven outlined buttons all have the same weight, so nothing signals which one to click first.
- The detail image pair (the water-beading fabric and the blue shell) has no caption or spec, so it doesn't say what makes the jacket waterproof.
- The care video block and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" footer pull attention away from the product after the second review.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to act now, such as a price on the Torrentshell, a member perk, or a ship-by date. Put it in the hero.
- 2. Make the hero CTA a solid, high-contrast button reading "Shop the Torrentshell 3L," and link it to the product page, not a category.
- 3. Cut the buttons to one primary per audience block. Put key specs next to the jacket, such as weight, waterproof rating, and recycled content.
- 4. Move the care video lower, or send it as its own post-purchase email.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Best rain jacket I've owned." See why the Torrentshell 3L earns it.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Unclosed `<style>` in the Outlook conditional.** The block `<!--[if gt mso 9]> <style type="text/css"> .Serif {...} .winlinkwhite {...} <![endif]-->` has no `</style>` before `<![endif]-->`. Non-MSO clients treat it as a comment. Outlook desktop (Word engine) will parse it, and the unclosed tag can cause following markup to be read as CSS until the next `</style>`.
- **Empty `<title></title>`.** The title is empty, which is a minor accessibility and metadata gap in web-view contexts.
- **Missing alt on the open pixel.** See section 2.
- **Passes:** `<meta charset="utf-8">`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `lang="en"`, and responsive classes at 600px and 540px.
- **Not assessable:** `alt` coverage on content images, and fallback font stacks beyond `Georgia, serif` in the MSO block. The `Copernicus` `@font-face` uses woff2 only, so clients that block web fonts depend on inline fallbacks I can't see in the truncated source.
