# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-06-05
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is unmistakably Patagonia — restrained, mission-forward, no garbage discount banners screaming at me. The hero is pushing Baggies, which is fine, but I came here as a Synchilla guy and the brand led with shorts. The top banner asking "Why Is Patagonia Making Food?" is exactly the kind of thing I'll tap before I even look at product — that's the Patagonia I trust. Clean, confident, no gimmicks, but the homepage isn't really talking to me right now.

## What's working

- **"Why Is Patagonia Making Food?" top banner** — I am tapping that before anything else. This is the activism-forward communication style that made me a customer in the first place. Perfect hook for someone like me.
- **Four clear gender/category CTAs** — "Men's" is one tap. No hunting through a buried nav. That's earned respect.
- **Baggies hero copy** — "rugged, lightweight and quick drying" is plain-spoken, no marketing fluff. The copy register fits someone who reads Worn Wear repair guides for fun.
- **Zero clutter above the fold** — no pop-ups, no overlay modals, no "give us your email" interruption. Let me breathe.
- **Outdoor lifestyle imagery** — the rock/water setting is honest to the product. This isn't a studio shot with fake mud. I believe this person actually wore these shorts somewhere.

## What's weak

- **No Worn Wear or repair hook anywhere visible** — I specifically shop Worn Wear before I shop new. If there's a used Synchilla two taps away, I have no idea. This is leaving my wallet on the table.
- **Hero cast skews young and female** — I'm 44, male, and have owned more Snap-Ts than I can count. The hero person looks like someone's college roommate on a weekend trip. Not alienating, but not me.
- **No activism substance above the fold** — the food banner at the top is a teaser, but that's it. Where's the environmental commitment copy, the 1% for the Planet badge, anything that reminds me why I choose Patagonia over Arc'teryx?
- **No pricing or shipping signal** — I shouldn't have to scroll or tap through to a product page to know whether free shipping kicks in at $99 or $150. Put that somewhere visible.
- **Baggies as the hero for a fleece loyalist** — wrong lead for a significant segment of the Patagonia base. Seasonal, yes, but summer shouldn't erase the brand's fleece identity entirely.

## Recommendations

- **Add a persistent Worn Wear entry point** — even a small "Shop Used" pill in the nav or a second module just below the hero fold. Repair-first customers are high-loyalty; stop hiding their path to purchase.
- **Swap the hero model or add a second frame** — cast someone who looks like they've had the same jacket for fifteen years, not just discovered adventure. Gen X outdoor consumers are a huge share of Patagonia's wallet.
- **Surface the free shipping threshold and returns policy** — one thin strip below the nav. Two pieces of text. Would meaningfully reduce cart hesitation.
- **Make the activism banner more substantive** — instead of "Why Is Patagonia Making Food? ▼" as a tease, put one sentence of the answer in the banner itself. Earn the tap, don't just bait it.
