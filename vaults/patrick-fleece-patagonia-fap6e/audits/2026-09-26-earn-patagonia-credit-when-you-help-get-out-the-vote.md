# Earn Patagonia credit when you help get out the vote
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia breaking its ten-day genuine-content streak with something completely different: a civic engagement campaign, "The 11.2 Million Vote Project," built entirely on custom illustration with zero product photography. It's a real offer — $100 credit for helping three friends make a voting plan — but it's not a shopping email, and anyone opening it expecting gear (like the last ten sends) gets a civics pitch instead.
- The illustration style is distinctive and on-brand (same hand-drawn line-art aesthetic as their activism content), and the 1-2-3 "How It Works" structure is genuinely clear. But there's no product, no price, no wearable shown — this is pure brand/advocacy messaging, which caps its commercial upside even if it's a smart trust-building move.
- Verdict: strong creative execution for what it is, but a detour from the pattern this subscriber has seen all September — and for a persona shopping for outerwear, this won't drive a click today.

## What's working

- Clean, distinctive hand-drawn illustration style carries the whole email with no stock photography.
- The 1-2-3 "How It Works" breakdown is genuinely easy to scan and understand at a glance.
- Two CTAs ("Act Now" / "Learn More") are visually consistent with orange pill buttons used elsewhere in Patagonia's sends.
- The closing "Get your people to the polls" banner in bold orange gives the email a strong visual anchor before the footer.
- The $100 credit mechanic is stated plainly and specifically, not buried in vague language.

## What's weak

- Zero product is shown — no gear, no price, no fit info — so there's nothing here for someone in a buying mindset.
- Breaks the brand's own recent cadence; after ten straight product-story sends, this pivot may read as a jarring non-sequitur in the inbox.
- The opening stat ("11.2M environmental voters skipped the last election") is somber/heavy for an inbox scan — no visual hook to soften it before the ask.
- Fine print at the bottom ("no proof of registration or voting required... must be in the US to access") is small and easy to miss on first read, even though the offer itself is legitimate.
- No urgency/deadline visible beyond "through Election Day," which is vague this far out.

## Recommendations

- 1. If commerce is the goal, pair this with an actual product tie-in (e.g., "vote + gear up for the season") — right now there's nothing to click through to shop.
- 2. Move the $100 credit mention higher, above "Together, we can change that," since it's the strongest hook and currently buried mid-scroll.
- 3. Add a concrete date ("by November 3") instead of "through Election Day" for credible urgency.
- 4. Tighten the opening stat into something more actionable/less heavy for a scroll-and-tap context.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your friends need a voting plan (and you need $100)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three friends, one plan, $100 toward gear or a donation.`
