# Our best-selling gear makes great gifts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a generic gift-guide send. The hero is strong: a flat-lay of a green down jacket, mustard pants, a beanie, socks, a fleece and a duffel, under the headline "Give 'Em Our Best" in teal. It looks like Patagonia and it's clearly a holiday email. The problem is the offer. The only commercial hook is "Free Shipping on Orders over $99*" in the top bar, and it's easy to miss. Nothing else is concrete: no price, no named product, no deadline.
- Below the hero are four large category tiles (Women's, Men's, Kids & Baby, All Gifts) with white overlay text on snowy photos. They're pretty but they just sort the catalog. This follows the Nov 15 "Stay out, stay dry" (Torrentshell) and Nov 11 "We've Got This Down" sends, which each pushed one product or story. This one steps back to a broad category send, so it has less to say. The brand-values block (guarantee, responsibility, activism) is on-brand, but it sits where a product or review should be.
- Verdict: a competent, polished brand send with no urgency, no hero product and no proof. It will get opens from brand affinity and little click momentum. About a 5.

## What's working

- The hero flat-lay is clean and shows the range at a glance: jacket, pants, hat, socks, bag.
- "Give 'Em Our Best" is warm and short, and the teal headline stands out against the pale grey background.
- There is one clear primary button, "Shop Best Sellers", right under the headline.
- The category tiles give easy self-sorting by recipient, and the type is large and legible.
- The free-shipping bar at the very top is a real, visible incentive.

## What's weak

- The "best-selling" claim in the subject is never backed up. The email shows no specific best-seller, no name, no price and no rating.
- No urgency or shipping-cutoff date. A holiday gift email on Nov 16 has nothing about order-by dates.
- The free-shipping bar is tiny and its $99 threshold is footnoted with a long block of fine print.
- The white text over the busy photos is weakest on "Gifts for Kids & Baby", where the headline sits across faces and goggles. The tiles are also very tall, so the page is a long scroll of four near-identical blocks.
- The teal values block (five claims) is a lot of space that doesn't help a gift buyer decide.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace two or three category tiles with a row of three named best-sellers, each with a price and a rating. This would do the most to lift clicks.
- 2. Add a real deadline, such as "Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery", and put it in the hero.
- 3. Move the free-shipping threshold into the hero as a line under the headline, and shorten the footnote.
- 4. Cut the values block to a single line, or move it below the product proof.
- 5. Put the overlay text on a darker scrim or on the plain-snow tiles.

However, the QA run could not verify headers or links, and the source was truncated, so compliance, link integrity and UTM continuity are unconfirmed rather than confirmed good or bad.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **No link validation:** 12 material links were identified but all 12 were skipped, so no status codes, redirects or landing-page health were checked.
- **Open pixel:** `https://trk.na.patagonia.com/wf/open?upn=u001.GExYxZtjp9Jr…` is a tracking pixel from the ESP's `trk.na.patagonia.com` domain. It is flagged for missing alt text, which is low severity because it is a 1x1 beacon. Add `alt=""` anyway.
- **Click-wrapping:** the truncated source doesn't show the CTA `href`s, so I can't say whether links are wrapped in the same `trk.na.patagonia.com` redirector or what they resolve to.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>` is present but empty. This is a minor accessibility and "view in browser" issue.
- **Missing alt text:** the only QA-flagged image is the open pixel. I can't assess the other `<img>` tags because the source is truncated.
- **Fonts:** `CopernicusEmail` and `RidgewayEmail` load from `images.patagonia.com` with `font-display: swap`. The Outlook (`mso`) conditional block sets Palatino/Georgia and Helvetica fallbacks, so there is no rendering risk.
- **Preheader:** `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden` is the standard approach. Some clients ignore it and may show the 11px "We guarantee everything we make." text, but that is a known trade-off, not a defect.
- **Other:** `lang="en"` is set on both `<html>` and the wrapper `<div>`, `meta charset` is present, and `role="presentation"` is used on layout tables.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[[…]]`) appear in the visible source or the QA output. Content past the truncation point is unchecked.
