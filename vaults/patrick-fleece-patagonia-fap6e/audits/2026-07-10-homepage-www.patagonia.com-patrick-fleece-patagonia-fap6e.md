# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-07-10
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is unmistakably Patagonia — the muted palette, the rocky backcountry imagery, the word "recycled" in the third line of body copy. Right now they're pushing back-to-school backpacks, which is fine timing in July, but I came here for the brand, not a school campaign. The "Work in Progress Report" link across the top is the first thing I actually leaned into — that's the Patagonia I trust.

## What's working

- **"Work in Progress Report" announcement bar** — that link lives at the very top and it's the first thing I'd tap. Patagonia publishing its own accountability report is exactly the activism signal I keep coming back for.
- **"Recycled materials" + "Ironclad Guarantee" in sub-copy** — whoever wrote that body copy knows this audience. Those two phrases do more trust work in twelve words than any hero image could.
- **Outdoor lifestyle imagery** — the rocky terrain with adults actually using gear reads authentic, not staged catalog. The teal backpack in natural light looks like something you'd actually take into the field.
- **Clean mobile layout** — no forced modals, no flyout promo, no cookie banner eating the hero. For medium-tech-comfort me, that's a relief.

## What's weak

- **"Pack to School" hero** — I'm 44. My kids aren't school-age or I'm not shopping for school supplies. This campaign positions the landing as a student acquisition play, not a lifer retention moment.
- **CTA copy "Explore"** — vague. "Explore" could go anywhere. "Shop Backpacks" or even "See the Bags" tells me what tap is buying.
- **No Worn Wear hook** — I'm a repair-first customer. There's zero path to Worn Wear, Iron Clad repairs, or used gear visible anywhere above the fold or in any secondary module I can see. That's my actual shopping intention, and it's invisible.
- **No category rail or navigation shortcut** — the hamburger hides everything. One tap to "Men's," "Fleece," or "Worn Wear" would make this feel like the brand knows me.
- **No trust at conversion depth** — free shipping threshold, return window, pay-later — none of it visible. If I wanted to act on this, I'd be doing extra work to find out if shipping is free.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "Pack to School" hero with a dual-track module**: keep the backpack campaign, but add a secondary tile or strip that surfaces Worn Wear and the Work in Progress Report. This homepage serves two audiences — new converts and lifers like me — and right now it ignores the second group entirely.
- **Swap "Explore" CTA copy for "Shop Backpacks"** — one-word specificity increase, zero design change required, immediate clarity gain.
- **Pin a Worn Wear or "Repair First" shortcut tile directly below the hero** — even a 100px-tall strip that says "Already own it? Repair it. → Worn Wear" would stop people like me from bouncing to the nav.
- **Move the free-shipping threshold into the announcement bar** alongside the Work in Progress Report — two banners can rotate; one activates activism readers, the other activates buyers.
