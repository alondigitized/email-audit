# Homepage snapshot · www.patagonia.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is unmistakably Patagonia — clean, earnest, outdoors-forward — and right now it's pushing new performance gear at a discount. As someone who has been wearing the same Snap-T since 2009, I appreciate the visual language, but "Performance Foundations" is a new-gear pitch, and I came here half-hoping to see a Worn Wear banner or something about the Holdfast Coalition. The 50% off web specials strip at the top is the loudest thing on the page, which tells me the brand is moving inventory more than moving mountains today.

## What's working

- **The 50% off banner strip** — I buy on sale and at Worn Wear prices. Seeing a discount acknowledged immediately, not buried, respects my time.
- **"Performance Foundations" headline** — the word "Foundations" actually lands for a guy who believes in buying less, better. It doesn't scream "new drop" — it implies core gear that lasts.
- **Three-CTA layout ("Explore / Men's / Women's")** — clean pill buttons. I can get to Men's in one tap without digging through a nav. That matters on a phone.
- **Motion-blur mountain biker hero** — the movement and the terrain read authentic Patagonia. It's not a studio shot. The blurred trees say someone was actually moving fast in a real place.
- **Partially visible canyoneering image below fold** — the slot canyon shot with a climber in a blue jacket is the kind of image that makes me want to scroll. That's doing its job.

## What's weak

- **No Worn Wear or repair CTA anywhere in view** — for a persona who defines loyalty to this brand by its repair ethic, the absence of any "Repair / Worn Wear / Used Gear" entry point above the fold is a miss.
- **"For the pursuit of your wildest days"** — the subhead is vague motivational copy. It doesn't tell me *what* the Performance Foundations collection actually is. I'm a medium-tech user, not a copywriter, and I'm still reading that as filler.
- **No trust signals** — no review count, no free returns mention, no "free shipping on orders over $X." For a $250+ jacket purchase, that information matters and it's nowhere.
- **The hamburger menu** — three lines and a bag. On mobile this hides everything. If I'm looking for Worn Wear or activism content, I have to hunt.
- **No human face** — the hero is a blurred biker. The canyoneering shot below fold shows a tiny figure in a slot canyon. No one's face, no one's story. Patagonia used to put real people front and center.

## Recommendations

- **Add a Worn Wear / repair entry point above the fold** — even a small secondary link ("Shop Used | Repair My Gear") beneath the three pill buttons would signal to loyalists like me that this is still that brand.
- **Swap "For the pursuit of your wildest days" for something specific** — tie the subhead to what Performance Foundations actually *is*: "Fleece, base layers, and shells built to last decades." That's one sentence that justifies a $200+ spend.
- **Surface free shipping threshold in the banner strip** — the black bar already has my attention for the sale. "Free shipping on orders $75+" sitting right next to the 50% offer takes zero additional real estate and removes a friction question.
- **Pull one real face into the hero or the first scroll position** — a mid-40s climber or biker with visible wear on their gear would make someone like me feel seen. Blurred silhouettes are anonymous.
