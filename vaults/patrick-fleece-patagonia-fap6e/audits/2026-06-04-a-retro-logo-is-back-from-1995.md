# A retro logo is back from 1995
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia in heritage mode — a full revival of the retro oval logo design from 1995, presented through a soft, nostalgic color palette of blush, sage, and mustard. The email is clean, coherent, and a deliberate tonal shift from the last two weeks of sun-protection and sale emails. If you've been a Patagonia subscriber since before the brand went mainstream, the subject line alone is a pull.
- The core problem is simple: there's no offer. This is a product-launch email with no price anchor, no discount, no urgency hook. You're being asked to click into a full-price launch for a logo tee. That's a real ask for a list that just got a "50% off web specials" email nine days ago. The "Discover" vs "Shop" dual-CTA split in the hero also muddies the intent — pick one.
- The editorial block at the bottom ("The Joy of Junky Windslop") is quintessentially Patagonia but adds scroll distance without earning a click. This email is well-branded but conversion-light.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the star.** "A retro logo is back from 1995" is 30 characters, nostalgic, specific, and curiosity-driven — one of the stronger subjects in this recent run.
- **Color palette is on brief.** Blush hero, sage hoodie, mustard tee — the retro feel is consistent from hero to product grid.
- **Visual hierarchy is solid.** Hero hoodie → headline → CTA → product grid flows naturally; no layout confusion.
- **No render bugs.** Clean render throughout — images load, text doesn't overlap, buttons are visible.
- **Tonal reset from recent sends.** After two weeks of sun-protection and sale emails, a heritage/nostalgia angle feels fresh rather than repetitive.

## What's weak

- **No price, no offer.** A logo tee launching at full price with no anchor — no discount, no member pricing, no free shipping — gives the reader nothing to act on beyond brand affection.
- **Dual CTA in hero dilutes intent.** "Discover Oval Logo" and "Shop Oval Logo" side-by-side split the click. One CTA wins here.
- **"The Joy of Junky Windslop" editorial adds length without conversion.** The story block is on-brand but buried below the fold with no obvious link to the oval logo product.
- **No urgency or scarcity signal.** "Is back" implies it's here indefinitely — no limited run framing, no early-access hook, nothing to act now for.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a scarcity or founding-run frame.** If this is a limited reissue, say "Limited founding run" or "Original quantities" in the hero. If it's not, manufacture soft urgency — "Only in select styles."
- 2. **Collapse to one hero CTA.** Drop "Discover Oval Logo." Keep "Shop Oval Logo" — the verb is transactional and the noun is specific.
- 3. **Add a price anchor or member value.** Even "Starting at $35" or a member-early-access note gives fence-sitters a reason to click now vs. later.
- 4. **Move the editorial block to a second email or cut it.** "The Joy of Junky Windslop" is brand voice content that competes with the logo launch for attention. Save it for its own send.
- 5. **Tie the 1995 nostalgia to a founding story.** One sentence of lore ("This is the logo on the first jacket we ever sold") would deepen the heritage frame that the subject line already teased.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some screen readers and browser-based webmail (Gmail web, Outlook.com) surface the `<title>` as the tab or document label. A blank value degrades accessibility; should match subject line or brand name.

**Issue — `CopernicusEmail` missing woff fallback:**
```css
src: url(...GalaxieCopernicus-Book.woff2) format("woff2");
```
`RidgewayEmail` correctly lists woff → woff2. `CopernicusEmail` declares woff2 only. Older Outlook for Mac and some Android 4.x WebViews cannot parse woff2; those clients will silently fall back to the system serif stack rather than the intended typeface.

**Cannot audit** — alt text on images and `role="presentation"` on decorative images require seeing the full `<img>` and table markup (truncated).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) visible in the rendered portion. Cannot confirm for body content — verify full source for un-replaced tokens before send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Issue — conflicting `-webkit-text-size-adjust` values on `<body>`:**
```html
style="... -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ..."
```
The property is declared twice in the same inline style block with different values (`none` then `100%`). The latter wins per CSS cascade, so iOS will allow auto text scaling. If suppression was intentional (common in email to prevent iOS font inflation), the `none` declaration should be the only one and the `100%` value removed.

**Cannot audit** — CAN-SPAM physical mailing address, unsubscribe link presence and one-click mechanism (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post`), and DKIM/SPF/DMARC alignment for sending domain `na.patagonia.com` require either the full HTML footer or authentication headers from the received message.
