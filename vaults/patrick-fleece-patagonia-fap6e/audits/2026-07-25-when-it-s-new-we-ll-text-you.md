# When it’s new, we’ll text you
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia asking for a phone number, not selling a product — the entire ask is "text us your digits for 15% off," dressed up with a gorgeous surf photo and no actual product in sight. Compared to the last six sends in this brand history (all catalog or single-product pushes with concrete gear), this is a list-building play, and it reads as one immediately: no SKU, no price, no category signal, just a wave and a CTA to opt into SMS marketing.
- The visual is the best part — a genuinely striking barrel shot with a surfer credited by name — but it's doing double duty as "content" when there's nothing else to look at. The 15% offer is real but it's not the offer being sold; it's a bribe to get you onto another channel. For a subscriber checking email on a commute, there's nothing to click through to except a text-signup form.
- Judgement: well-produced, poorly matched to email-review purposes — this is an SMS-acquisition email wearing product-email clothes, and it will underperform any of Patagonia's actual catalog sends.

## What's working

- The hero wave photo is high-quality, dramatic, and on-brand — genuinely arresting art direction.
- Photo credit and location caption (Moona Whyte, Mentawai Islands) adds authentic editorial texture, consistent with Patagonia's voice.
- Single clear CTA button ("Get Our Texts") with no competing calls to action.
- Free shipping banner at top is a real, standing offer, not manufactured urgency.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no render bugs, overlapping text, or broken images.

## What's weak

- No product is shown or linked anywhere in the body — a subscriber who came for gear gets a wave photo and a phone-number ask instead.
- The 15% discount is buried in a double-asterisk footnote pattern and only applies "on your next order" after opting into SMS, so the real headline offer is muted.
- "Fresh Drops Coming Soon" promises new arrivals but shows none — zero preview of what's actually coming.
- The "Earth is now our shareholder / Read Yvon's Letter" block feels bolted onto an SMS-signup email — off-topic footer content that doesn't reinforce the ask.
- Coming right after four straight product/catalog sends, this is a tonal break with nothing to click through to — high risk of feeling like a wasted open.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show the actual incoming product — even a blurred/teaser shot of a fall drop — so "Fresh Drops Coming Soon" has visual proof, not just a promise.
- 2. Lead with the 15% offer in the hero copy instead of the footnote — "Sign up for texts, get 15% off now" is a stronger hook than "we'll text you when they get here."
- 3. Cut the Yvon's Letter / shareholder block from this specific send — it dilutes a single-purpose SMS-acquisition email; save it for a brand-story send.
- 4. Add a secondary link under the CTA to shop current inventory, so people who don't want texts still have somewhere to go.
- 5. Tie the surf photo to a category tease (wetsuits, boardshorts, surf accessories) so the imagery earns its place instead of being decorative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fresh drops are coming — get 15% off now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Text VIP access to new arrivals + 15% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Be first to shop new arrivals — sign up for texts and save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New season gear is landing. Get the alert (and a discount).`
