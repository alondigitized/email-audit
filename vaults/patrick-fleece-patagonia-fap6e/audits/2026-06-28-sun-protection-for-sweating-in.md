# Sun protection for sweating in
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Patagonia's second Capilene Cool Sun email in 48 hours. June 26 sent "40+ UPF for the long haul" with the same hero headline, same product, and same "Explore" CTA structure — and now here's June 28 with "Sun protection for sweating in" wrapping almost identical body content. The subject line is marginally more interesting (the "sweating in" angle emphasizes athletic performance over generic sun safety), but anyone who opened the last one will immediately recognize the repeat.
- The email itself is competently built: clean render, strong hero image with a clear performance theme, good product-feature tiles (Warp-Knit Wicking / 40+ UPF / High-Output Breathability / UPF Won't Wash Out), and an editorial tail featuring a Cochamo and Puelo Valleys field piece by Felipe Cancino. That editorial add is Patagonia's signature move — mission content piggybacking on product. But the two-section structure (product push + editorial) competes for attention rather than reinforcing a single message.
- The verdict: a well-rendered email suffering from over-cadencing. Sending the same SKU twice in a week with no price incentive and a generic "Explore" CTA is a trust tax on subscribers. This should have been one send, not two.

## What's working

- **Subject line "sweating in" angle** — sharper than June 26's headline; it implicitly signals athletic use and earns a slight curiosity bump over the previous send.
- **Product feature tiles** — the four iconographic tiles (Warp-Knit, 40+ UPF, Breathability, UPF Won't Wash Out) are clean, scannable, and specific — exactly the right format for a performance claim.
- **Hero image** — the water-action shot is high-quality and contextually appropriate; sun-on-water communicates UPF need without explaining it.
- **Editorial section** — "Connecting the Cochamo and Puelo Valleys" gives the email purpose beyond "buy this shirt" and is consistent with Patagonia's brand equity.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout bugs, Earth shareholder footer present.

## What's weak

- **Near-duplicate of June 26** — the "UPF for the Long Haul" hero headline is *identical* to the prior send; two days is too short a window to reuse the same creative.
- **No offer** — no discount, no free shipping, no member pricing — nothing to reward the person who already ignored this exact send two days ago.
- **"Explore" CTA is too soft** — appearing twice, it directs nowhere specific and creates no forward momentum; "Shop the Sun Hoody" would be materially stronger.
- **Editorial section dilutes the CTA** — Cochamo and Puelo Valleys is a great piece of content but it pulls the reader away from the product mid-email with no clear path back.
- **No social proof** — no star ratings, no reviews, no ambassador quotes despite the brand's June 8 ambassador send demonstrating they can execute social proof well.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second Capilene Cool Sun send entirely** — or wait at least 7-10 days and reframe with a new creative angle (e.g., a use-case pivot to trail running vs. water sports). Sending the same hero twice in 48 hours trains subscribers to skip the second email.
- 2. **Rename the CTA** — replace both "Explore" buttons with "Shop Men's Sun Hoody" and "Shop Women's Sun Hoody" respectively. Specific verb + noun = more clicks.
- 3. **Add a discount or threshold incentive** — even free shipping at $99 would give the repeat mailer a reason to exist.
- 4. **Separate the editorial content** — save the Cochamo field notes for a standalone mission email, or anchor it below a stronger product resolution with a "Read the Story" link, not a layout-breaking section.
- 5. **Bring in a customer quote or star rating** under the feature tiles — the June 8 ambassador send proved Patagonia's audience responds to social proof.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers in some clients (notably VoiceOver + Apple Mail) announce the title. An empty title may be read as "untitled" or skipped entirely. Recommend setting a descriptive value matching the subject line.

**Issue: Conflicting `-webkit-text-size-adjust` values in `<body>` style**
```html
style="... -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%;"
```
The first declaration (`none`) disables user text-size adjustment — an accessibility concern on iOS. The second (`100%`) overrides it within the same attribute, making the `none` declaration dead code. Clean this up: use only `100%`.

**Issue: Redundant/conflicting margin declarations in `<body>`**
```html
style="margin-right: auto; margin-left: auto; margin: 0px; ... margin: 0;"
```
`margin: 0px` and then `margin: 0` both override the preceding `margin-right/left: auto`. These are three declarations doing the same thing. Low-severity, but indicates copy-paste accumulation in the template.

**Note: GalaxieCopernicus loaded as woff2 only**
```css
src: url(...GalaxieCopernicus-Book.woff2) format("woff2");
```
RidgewaySans includes both `woff` and `woff2` fallback sources. Copernicus provides only `woff2`. Any client without woff2 support (rare but includes some older Outlook/Thunderbird builds) will fall back to the generic serif stack (`Palatino, Georgia, serif`) defined in the MSO conditional. Functional but inconsistent with the RidgewaySans approach.

**No issues found:** `lang` attribute is correctly on `<html lang="en">`, not just a child `<div>`. Preheader `display:none` pattern and `&zwnj;&nbsp;` padding hack are implemented correctly.
