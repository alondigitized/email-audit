# Ditch clothes for the polls on Election Day
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a civic-engagement send, not a sales email, and it is the most distinctive Patagonia email in the recent run. After a string of catalog sends (flannels, Nano/Micro Puff, Refugio, rainwear, "New colors for fall"), this one has no product at all. The hand-drawn illustrations are charming and on-brand: a sandwich-board hiker saying "Election Day! Back tomorrow," a face-down hiker with a mushroom, and a tiny parade of outdoor types. The "Ditch clothes for the polls." headline is a clever, on-voice take on the brand's anti-consumption stance.
- The weaknesses are in the mechanics. The email announces that US stores close on Tuesday, November 3, but the body copy only hints at the action ("There's a way you can join, too"). The first CTA, "Act Now," doesn't say what the action is. The second, "Sign Up," doesn't say what you're signing up for. The orange "Get your people to the polls." banner at the bottom is the strongest line in the email, but it isn't a button.
- For a persona that shops Patagonia gear, this is a values touchpoint with no commerce and no loyalty benefit. It builds brand equity, but it won't drive revenue. Judgement: a strong brand moment with weak CTA copy, aimed at a different goal than the previous sends.

## What's working

- The "Ditch clothes for the polls." headline is large, witty, and on-brand, and it sits clearly above the body copy.
- The illustrations give the email a distinct, human voice, unlike the photo-led catalog sends.
- Layout is clean and single-column, with generous whitespace and no render bugs.
- The orange pill buttons contrast well against the cream background and are easy to tap.
- The closing orange banner, "Get your people to the polls.", gives a strong visual end and a memorable line.

## What's weak

- "Act Now" and "Sign Up" are vague CTAs that don't name the action. The voter planning tool isn't mentioned until the second section.
- The first block says "There's a way you can join, too" and doesn't say what it is, which adds a click of friction.
- The closing banner looks like a CTA but isn't a button, so it's a missed click opportunity.
- The "three easy steps" are promised but not shown, so there's no preview of how simple the tool is.
- The footer disclaimer ("No proof of registration or voting required...") is small, and there's no social proof or mention of how many people have used the tool.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rename the CTAs to name the action: "Build your voter plan" for both buttons, or "Make your voting plan" and "Text your friends".
- 2. Make the closing orange banner a button, since it is the most compelling line in the email.
- 3. Show the three steps as small icons or numbered lines instead of just saying "three easy steps."
- 4. Lead with the tool in the first block and put the store-closure news in a single line. The closure is the hook, but the tool is the action.
- 5. Add a line on timing, such as "Takes 2 minutes," to give the urgency a credible basis.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our stores are closed Nov 3. Here's why.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Make a voting plan for your friends in 3 steps`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All US stores close on Election Day. Here's how you can help get out the vote.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Send friends where and when to vote in three easy steps.`
