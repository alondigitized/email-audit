# Get the emails you truly want
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a **preference center update email** — a retention play dressed up as a service message. Patagonia is asking subscribers to self-select into content categories (product innovations, stories & films, events) to reduce churn and sharpen their list. The intention is smart: inbox fatigue is a real problem, and giving subscribers control over what they receive is a best practice. The execution is clean, on-brand, and honest. The photography is characteristically excellent.
- The core problem is that this email has no genuine value exchange for the recipient. It asks something of you (time to update preferences) without giving anything in return — no exclusive content teaser, no incentive, no urgency. The three content category cards are vague: "Want to catch up on the latest product innovations?" tells me nothing about what I'd actually get. A reader on the fence has no reason to click.
- The outcome is a serviceable but low-performing send: opens will be decent (Patagonia brand trust is high), but click-through will be soft because the email asks for effort without offering a reward.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is bold and direct.** "Dial In Your Preferences" scans immediately and communicates the action clearly.
- **Brand voice and photography are consistent.** The outdoor action hero shot is aspirational and on-brand; no visual mismatch.
- **"Earth is now our only shareholder" footer.** This is a strong brand-values anchor that reinforces trust at the close — a differentiator almost no other apparel brand can credibly claim.
- **Clean render, no layout bugs.** The three category cards lay out correctly; no broken images or overlapping text visible.
- **Honest, non-manipulative copy.** The body explicitly acknowledges "inbox fatigue is real" — that candor lands well and earns a small trust point.

## What's weak

- **Zero value exchange for the subscriber.** The email asks for a preference update but offers nothing in return — no preview of upcoming content, no "here's what you'd get," no incentive.
- **Three category cards are too vague to motivate action.** "Want to catch up on the latest product innovations?" is a yes/no question with no supporting hook — no example product, no teaser headline, no reason to care.
- **Duplicate "Update Now" CTA adds no value.** Two identical buttons with identical copy doesn't create hierarchy; it just adds noise.
- **Subject line is generic and low-urgency.** "Get the emails you truly want" could be from any brand; it has no Patagonia-specific hook and zero time pressure.
- **No preheader visible.** Without a meaningful preheader complementing the subject, inbox display is relying entirely on a weak subject line.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a teaser in each content card.** Replace the question-only card format with a one-liner of what's coming: "We're launching the Black Hole Duffel in new colorways next week" or "The new surf film drops Thursday." Give people something to opt into, not just a category label.
- 2. **Give the email a reason to exist right now.** Tie the preference ask to a real moment: "We have a big product drop + a film premiere next month — tell us what you want to see." Without a hook, this email reads as list hygiene performed on the subscriber, not a benefit.
- 3. **Consolidate to one CTA, above the fold.** Remove the second "Update Now" and move the single button directly under the three content cards with a brief summary: "Takes 20 seconds."
- 4. **Sharpen the subject line.** The current line is forgettable.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You decide what Patagonia sends you`
- **Subject Alt B:** `What actually lands in your inbox?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick: product drops, films, or local events — or all three.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Inbox fatigue is real. Dial in what you want in 20 seconds.`
