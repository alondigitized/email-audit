# Lightweight sweatshirts for shifty conditions

- The editorial unit at the bottom — "The Joy of Junky Windslop" — pulls focus in two directions at once. By the time a subscriber scrolls to it, they've either already decided to shop or they haven't, and a surf-adjacent story doesn't resolve that either way. It reads as padding.

- **Subject line is specific and well-written** — "shifty conditions" is exactly the kind of copywriting shorthand that earns opens; 45 chars lands well under the mobile truncation zone.

- **"Earth is now our only shareholder" footer** — a quiet brand trust signal that no other apparel brand can replicate.

## What's weak

- **No pricing anywhere in the email.** Not even a "starting at $X" anchor. For a subscriber deciding whether to click, this is a blind date.
- **"Stoke the Stoke" is opaque at hero size.** A subscriber skimming fast doesn't know this is about sweatshirts until they read below the fold — the product name should be in or adjacent to the headline.

- **Zero urgency signal.** No seasonal hook ("spring's last window"), no scarcity signal, nothing. The 5/15 sale email had a hard commercial reason to click; this one doesn't.
- **Four or five emails in eight days** — cadence is getting heavy. This is the easiest of the recent batch to archive without opening.

- 1. **Put a price anchor in the hero.** "From $79" or "Under $100" next to the headline doubles click intent without compromising brand tone. Patagonia prices are credible — show them.
- 2. **Promote the product name into or directly under the hero headline.** "Stoke the Stoke — with the 85 Oval Logo Hoodie" or similar so skimmers know what they're being asked to shop.
- 3. **Cut or move the editorial section.** "The Joy of Junky Windslop" works in a monthly journal send. In a product email it dilutes the CTA cluster. Move it to a standalone content send or drop it.
- 4. **Add one seasonal urgency note.** "Built for spring weather that can't make up its mind" in the hero subhead is passive; "Grab it before the warm stretch hits" gives a reason to act today.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Affects tab label when email is opened in a browser via "view online" link. Should contain a human-readable title matching the subject line.

**Conflicting `-webkit-text-size-adjust` in body inline style**
```html
style="... -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; ..."
```
Two conflicting values in the same declaration block. Last value wins (`100%`), making `none` dead code. `none` can lock text scaling in some older iOS clients if parsing order differs — should be removed.

**`margin` shorthand overrides preceding directional declarations**
```html
style="margin-right: auto; margin-left: auto; margin: 0px; ..."
```
The `margin: 0px` shorthand resets the preceding `auto` values. Likely unintentional. Effect on email body is typically benign, but the intent is ambiguous.

**`width: 100%` declared twice in body style** — minor dead code, no functional impact.

**`border-collapse:collapse` declared three times on preheader table** — no functional impact, minor code hygiene issue.

**CopernicusEmail @font-face missing `.woff` fallback**
```css
src: url(https://images.patagonia.com/eMedia/fonts/GalaxieCopernicus/GalaxieCopernicus-Book.woff2) format("woff2");
```
RidgewayEmail includes both `.woff` and `.woff2`; CopernicusEmail declares only `.woff2`. Inconsistent and leaves no fallback format for the rare client that supports web fonts but not woff2.
