# Everyday rainwear to match the forecast
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, seasonal catalog send built around one idea: rain gear for the forecast. A hooded jacket sits under a big "Rainwear" headline, with a short line of copy and an "Explore" button. The Torrentshell 3L feature, a four-jacket grid and a running-in-the-rain story follow. The seasonal timing is right for late October. The brand voice is consistent with the rest of the recent run.
- It still has the problem that runs through the recent Patagonia sends: no price, no deal and no deadline. The only offer is the "Free Shipping on Orders over $99" strip at the top. That strip is easy to miss, and the fine print at the bottom hedges it. The email also sends readers to six or more destinations: Explore, Men's, Women's, four product tiles, the Read the Story button and the Yvon letter. That dilutes the click, and the Torrentshell feature carries the most weight.
- My verdict is a competent 5 or 6. It is well made and on-brand, but nothing here gives a reader a reason to act today.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The large "Rainwear" headline over a close-up of a hooded jacket makes the topic clear immediately.
- The copy is on-voice and seasonal: "A squall, drizzle or downpour. Whatever you call it, these everyday jackets meet the forecast head-on."
- The Torrentshell 3L feature has a specific product name and a concrete spec, "3-layer construction." A collage of three images shows the fit and the hood.
- The four-up grid mixes men's and women's jackets with consistent model photography and labels. It is easy to scan.
- The render is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text. The muted teal and slate palette is cohesive and fits the weather theme.

## What's weak

- There is no price, no promo and no deadline anywhere. Free shipping over $99 is the only incentive, and it is buried in a thin top bar.
- The email has too many CTAs competing for attention. The pill buttons are small and low-contrast, so "Explore" is ambiguous.
- The four grid tiles have only a product name. They show no price, no rating and no visible link affordance.
- The running-in-the-rain story and the "Earth is now our only shareholder" block pull attention away from the sale. Together with the small, dense photo-credit and shipping fine print, they make the bottom third feel cluttered.
- It repeats the catalog format of the 10-23 "Versatile Refugio Daypacks" and 10-22 "Nano or Micro?" sends. A subscriber seeing a third or fourth product-led email in a week has little reason to treat this one as special.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one concrete reason to act now in the hero, such as a price anchor, a rating or a delivery promise. A line like "Waterproof from $149, free shipping over $99" would do it. If Patagonia won't discount, add proof such as reviews, the warranty or Fair Trade Certified status.
- 2. Cut the CTAs to one primary button. Make "Shop the Torrentshell 3L" a high-contrast filled button, and drop the redundant "Explore."
- 3. Add prices and star ratings under each grid tile, and make the tiles clearly clickable.
- 4. Move the running story and the shareholder block below a clear divider, or drop them from this send. Keep the email to one job.
- 5. Make the free-shipping strip bigger, or repeat it near the hero button.
