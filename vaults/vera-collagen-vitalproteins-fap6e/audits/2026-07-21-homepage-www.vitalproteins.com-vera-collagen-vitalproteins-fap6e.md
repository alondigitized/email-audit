# Homepage snapshot · www.vitalproteins.com · 2026-07-21
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is immediately recognizable as a brand that knows what it's selling — the new Collagen Sparkling Water is front and center with colorful cans and a clean "Better skin starting in 30 days" promise. It feels fresh and product-launch-focused, which I get, but as someone who already subscribes to the 20-oz tub, I'm a little underwhelmed — there's zero acknowledgment that I exist as a returning customer, and no price or offer to tempt me toward this new format.

## What's working

- **"Better skin starting in 30 days"** — that headline is doing real work. It's specific, it's the exact promise I bought into with collagen in the first place, and it pairs perfectly with the sparkling water format.
- **The can hero image** — three flavors, bright colors, fruit garnishes, geometric blocks. It's playful and premium at the same time. These look like something I'd Instagram next to my coffee setup.
- **"Vital Proteins® Collagen Sparkling Water is now available in 12-packs"** — the subhead is doing informational lifting. I know exactly what format is being announced, and 12-packs matters because it signals subscribe-and-save viability.
- **"New product drop" ticker at the bottom** — subtle, but it keeps momentum going and signals the brand is actively moving.

## What's weak

- **Zero returning-customer recognition** — I subscribe to the 20-oz tub. There's no loyalty hook, no rewards callout, no "member price" badge. The homepage treats me like a stranger off the street.
- **No price anywhere** — I cross-shop Vital Proteins vs. Bulletproof on tub price every reorder. The sparkling water sounds interesting but I have no idea if a 12-pack is $30 or $60. That friction kills impulse curiosity.
- **"Shop It Here" is a generic CTA** — it's vague. "Shop 12-Packs" or "Try a 12-Pack" would have been cleaner and more specific to what the subhead just told me.
- **No trust signals whatsoever** — no review count, no star rating, no "as seen in…" badge. For a new product I haven't tried, that absence creates hesitation.
- **The top banner just says "Stay Vital"** — that's a tagline, not an offer. Wasted real estate. Even "Free shipping on orders $35+" would have converted that strip into something useful.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the "Stay Vital" banner for a free-shipping threshold or a returning-subscriber offer** — "Free shipping on orders $35+ • Subscribers save 20%" costs nothing to ship next week and immediately signals value to me.
- **Make the CTA copy match the subhead** — change "Shop It Here" to "Shop the 12-Pack" so there's zero cognitive gap between what I just read and what I'm tapping.
- **Add a star-rating chip or a social-proof line under the CTA** — even "4.8★ from 12,000+ reviews" would reassure me on a new product format I've never tried.
- **Surface a loyalty/subscribe hook** — a small "Subscribe & Save 20%" line under the CTA price anchors the value for repeat buyers like me and closes the gap against Bulletproof.
