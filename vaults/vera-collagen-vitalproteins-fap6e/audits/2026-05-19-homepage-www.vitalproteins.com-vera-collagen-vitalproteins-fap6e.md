# Homepage snapshot · www.vitalproteins.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- OK so I landed on vitalproteins.com expecting to see my Advanced Collagen Peptides front and center — maybe a "refill your tub" moment — and instead I'm getting a full court press about National Tennis Month and creatine. I know they sell creatine now, I've seen it, but that is not why I'm here. Feels like the brand is chasing a performance-sports customer at the expense of the wellness-ritual crowd that actually built them.

## What's working

- **Product visibility in the hero image:** My Advanced Collagen Peptides tubs (the teal "Strawberry Lemon" and the standard one) are actually right there in the hero shot. Even though the messaging is about creatine, I can see my product and the brand equity comes through instantly.
- **"GET 15% OFF!" sticky bar:** That bottom bar catches me on the way out. As a subscriber, 15% off would apply to my next tub order, so it's not irrelevant even if the hero pushed creatine.
- **Clean mobile layout:** The nav is tight, the logo is centered, the hero image fills the space well. Nothing is fighting for my eyes.
- **Current campaign energy:** "National Tennis Month" tells me the brand has a pulse. It's not a leftover holiday sale banner from February.

## What's weak

- **Wrong hero for me:** "Support a stronger serve here.**^"** — I don't play tennis. I stir collagen into my coffee at 7am. This entire fold is speaking to a different customer.
- **Creatine-first messaging:** The 20% off is on creatine. My product — Advanced Collagen Peptides — is in the image but gets zero copy love. It's a prop in someone else's story.
- **No shortcut to my subscription:** I'm a subscriber. There's no "manage subscription," no "reorder," no loyalty balance visible. I have to hunt for my own account.
- **No navigation categories visible:** Hamburger-only on mobile means I'm one extra tap away from even finding the collagen section. That friction costs engagement from returning buyers.
- **No social proof:** Not a single review count, star rating, or "X million servings sold" badge above the fold. For a supplement brand, trust signals matter.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the hero for a collagen-first lifestyle shot:** A 30-something woman at her kitchen counter stirring a tub into her morning cup — that's me, that's the core customer. Run the creatine/tennis module as a secondary banner below the fold.
- **Add a "Welcome back, Vera — refill your subscription" returning-customer strip** (or at minimum a loyalty balance chip in the nav) — the brand has my data, use it.
- **Surface the 15% off offer WITH a category tie-in:** Change "GET 15% OFF!" to something like "Get 15% off Collagen Peptides — shop subscriptions" so it does double duty as a CTA that routes me somewhere relevant.
- **Add one above-the-fold trust signal:** A "4.7 stars · 50,000+ reviews" badge near the hero or the sticky offer bar would close the gap for first-timers and reinforce for repeat buyers.
