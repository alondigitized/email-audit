# Homepage snapshot · www.vitalproteins.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Vital Proteins knows what it's doing aesthetically — those tubs are clean, the light blue palette is very "wellness shelf in a Soho apartment" and I'm here for it. But I opened this site for my Advanced Collagen Peptides subscription and immediately got hit with a National Tennis Month creatine push, which — I'm not a tennis person, I'm a collagen-in-my-morning-coffee person. The offer is fine, the product is technically mine, but the angle feels like it was built for a different shopper.

## What's working

- **The product trifecta shot** — three tubs, studio lighting, my exact SKU (Advanced Collagen Peptides + Creatine) is the centerpiece. I recognized it instantly, which matters.
- **The 20% off code** — "TENNIS" is memorable and the discount is front and center. Even if I don't play tennis, 20% off is 20% off and I'd type that code.
- **The sticky 15% offer bar** — annoying as a modal pattern, but if it's a sign-up discount for email/SMS, that's a real hook for a first-time visitor or for me as a returning subscriber who might log out to grab a coupon.
- **Clean rendering** — nothing broken, no overlapping text, the tubs look sharp on mobile. The brand doesn't embarrass itself visually.

## What's weak

- **"Support a stronger serve here"** — I put collagen in my coffee every morning, I don't have a serve. The copy is doing athlete-positioning when my purchase motivation is skin, joints, and hair. This feels like a different customer's homepage.
- **No returning-subscriber hook** — I subscribe to the 20-oz tub. Where is my account greeting? My rewards points? A "reorder your Collagen Peptides" shortcut? Nothing above the fold tells me Vital Proteins recognizes me.
- **Creatine is the featured product**, not pure collagen peptides — the center tub is literally just Creatine, and the subtext pushes creatine discounts. I came here for collagen. The upsell-to-creatine feels premature before you've even greeted me.
- **No trust signals** — no review count, no star ratings, no "as seen in" badge, no free-shipping callout. For a supplement brand, social proof above the fold is non-negotiable.
- **Hamburger nav on mobile** — one more tap to get anywhere. No category pill row at the top. If I want to navigate to "Collagen Peptides" specifically, I have to dig.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the hero angle for a collagen-wellness lifestyle frame** — show someone doing their morning routine (coffee, collagen scoop, quiet kitchen), not a tennis court metaphor. National Tennis Month is a fine sub-campaign, but it shouldn't be the first-screen story for a mainstream collagen buyer.
- **Add a returning-shopper bar** — a thin strip above the hero that says "Welcome back, Vera — your Collagen Peptides subscription ships in 12 days. [Manage]" would make me feel seen and reduce churn risk.
- **Surface a free-shipping threshold** — put "Free shipping on orders over $X" in the top banner or below the CTA. Right now "Stay Vital" is doing nothing for me up there.
- **Pin star ratings on the hero product** — even a small "⭐ 4.8 (12,400 reviews)" badge beneath the tub image on mobile would add massive credibility without changing the layout.
