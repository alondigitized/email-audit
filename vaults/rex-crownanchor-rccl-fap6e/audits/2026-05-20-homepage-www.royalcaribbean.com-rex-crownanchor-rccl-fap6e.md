# Homepage snapshot · www.royalcaribbean.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Royal Caribbean is clearly pushing a flash-sale message — "Buzzer Beater Savings" with up to $450 off — and they've dressed it in some kind of Arctic/Alaska adventure imagery. As someone who's sailed this brand five times a year for years, my immediate read is: this page is built for new-to-cruise shoppers, not for me. There is zero acknowledgment that I exist as a Diamond member, and "Scary Good Deals" in May makes me wonder if marketing forgot to swap the campaign.

## What's working

- **"Save up to $450 with Buzzer Beater Savings"** — I'm a deal-stacker by habit. Any headline with a specific dollar amount earns a second look. $450 is meaningful on a balcony sailing.
- **The search form is immediately actionable** — "Cruising to / Leaving from / Leaving on / Search cruises" is exactly what I need. No friction, no click to reveal, no pop-up wall.
- **Alaska/adventure imagery** — Those two figures in cold-weather gear against a blue-ice backdrop signal Alaska or a repositioning itinerary. That's the kind of sailing I'd consider as my fifth trip of the year.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — No banner ads stacked on pop-ups stacked on chat bubbles. The page respects my attention, which I appreciate.
- **Single dominant CTA** — One button, clear label. I'm not hunting for where to click.

## What's weak

- **Zero Crown & Anchor presence above the fold** — I'm a Diamond member. This homepage doesn't know I exist. No tier badge, no "Welcome back, Rex," no Diamond-exclusive offer. That's a miss. Norwegian and Celebrity both surface loyalty status on first load.
- **"Scary Good Deals" in May** — Halloween-adjacent copy in the middle of spring is jarring. It makes me question whether the rest of the deal is similarly stale or slapped together.
- **"Buzzer Beater" is wrong-gen copy** — I know what a buzzer beater is, but this phrase is aimed at someone in their 30s scrolling TikTok, not a 55-year-old Crown & Anchor Diamond who booked his next three cruises before he got off the gangway last November.
- **No ship or itinerary specificity** — I know the Oasis-class by deck plan. Show me the *Wonder of the Seas* sailing out of Port Canaveral, not a generic search form. One specific featured sailing would pull me in faster.
- **"Up to $450" is doing a lot of work** — "Up to" is the most elastic phrase in travel marketing. Is that $450 off a suite on a 14-night sailing? An inside on a 5-night? Without any qualifying context, I've learned to be skeptical.

## Recommendations

- **Surface Crown & Anchor tier state in the hero** — If I'm logged in (or the app knows me), swap the generic hero for a Diamond-addressed message: "Diamond members save an extra [X]% on Buzzer Beater sailings." I should feel recognized the moment I land.
- **Replace "Scary Good / Buzzer Beater" with seasonally accurate copy** — It's May; push summer Caribbean sailing windows, Alaska shoulder season, or fall repositioning cruises. The seasonal mismatch erodes trust in the whole offer.
- **Feature one specific sailing in the hero** — "Symphony of the Seas, 7-night Eastern Caribbean from Miami — from $899" with a deep-link directly into that itinerary. I book from specifics, not search forms.
- **Add a Diamond/loyalty bar below the search widget** — A single slim strip reading "Crown & Anchor Diamond: your exclusive rate is applied" or "You have [X] points — that's [Y] toward your next sailing" requires no redesign and makes returning high-value customers feel seen.
