# Homepage snapshot · www.royalcaribbean.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Royal Caribbean doing exactly what Royal Caribbean does — lead with a sale, stack the offers, and put a search bar front and center. The "Kickoff to Summer Cyber Sale" with up to $900 off tells me they're in full promotional mode, which I respect, but the homepage as I'm seeing it on mobile is almost entirely one giant hero and a search bar. For a Diamond member who's already logged in and ready to compare sailings, this feels like it's aimed at first-timers who need the price jolt to even consider booking.

## What's working

- **"UP TO $900 OFF" headline**: That's a number large enough to make even a veteran Diamond member pause mid-scroll. It passes the "would I screenshot this to show my wife" test.
- **Stacked offer structure**: Three bullets below the main number — 3rd/4th guests sail free and $50 off Summer Getaways. As someone who books for the family crew occasionally, the 3rd/4th guest line actually lands.
- **Search bar placement**: Putting "Search cruises" directly below the hero is smart. I know exactly what I want — I just type "Oasis April 2027" and go. One tap to utility.
- **"FINAL CALL: SAILINGS SELLING OUT NOW" urgency strip**: The hot pink banner is impossible to ignore. Even if the copy is vague, it creates just enough FOMO to make me tap before I second-guess.
- **Clean render on mobile**: No jank, no layout shift, no broken images. The page loads and looks intentional.

## What's weak

- **Zero Crown & Anchor recognition**: I'm a Diamond member with five sailings a year and the homepage treats me like a walk-in prospect. No "Welcome back," no loyalty tier badge, no link to My Account with my points balance. Carnival's app at least knows who I am when I open it.
- **No ship imagery**: The hero is an abstract aerial ocean shot — gorgeous, sure, but there's no ship. For someone who books by deck plan and knows the difference between Wonder and Icon at a glance, this is a missed emotional trigger. Show me the ship.
- **Vague urgency**: "FINAL CALL: SAILINGS SELLING OUT NOW" — which sailings? What itineraries? What dates? That copy is doing nothing for me because it's not specific. Give me "Caribbean sailings departing June–August selling out fast" and I'm tapping.
- **$50 off Summer Getaways asterisk**: That qualifier at the bottom of the hero reads like it's trying to walk back the headline. "$900 off" is the hero; "$50 off on Summer Getaways" with a terms asterisk feels like a different, lesser deal buried in the same breath.
- **No loyalty or membership entry point visible**: No "Crown & Anchor" text, no "Sign In for Member Pricing" — nothing that tells me this page knows I exist as a repeat customer.

## Recommendations

- **Inject a Crown & Anchor recognition layer at the top**: Even on mobile, a thin "Welcome back, Diamond member — your exclusive rate is applied" bar above the hero would completely change how I experience this page. Login-state personalization is table stakes at this loyalty tier.
- **Swap the abstract ocean hero for an Oasis-class ship shot**: Show me the ship — the pool deck, the Central Park, the Royal Promenade. That's what I'm buying. An aerial splash of water doesn't trigger my booking reflex the way "Icon of the Seas at sunset" does.
- **Make the urgency specific**: Replace "SAILINGS SELLING OUT NOW" with a real itinerary or sail date — "Caribbean sailings June 7–28 nearly sold out." Specificity converts Diamond members; vagueness bounces us.
- **Add a NextCruise / onboard credit callout**: I book a year out and stack BOGO + OBC + The Key. One line — "Book now, get up to $600 onboard credit" — would speak directly to how I actually shop this site.
