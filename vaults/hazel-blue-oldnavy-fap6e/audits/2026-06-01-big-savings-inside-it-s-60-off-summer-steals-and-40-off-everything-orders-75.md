# Big savings inside! It's 60% off summer steals and 40% off everything* (orders $75+)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This send marks a creative pivot away from the swim-heavy cadence that dominated the last four Old Navy emails. "Summer Steals" is broader, and the addition of a 40%-off-when-you-spend-$75 tier is a new structural move — an attempt to drive basket size rather than just volume. The hero lands cleanly: "2 DAYS ONLY!" + "60% Summer Steals" is the most legible opening frame this brand has shown all week.
- The problem is everything below the fold. The email tries to do five distinct jobs — 60% steals, 40%/$75 conditional, a bundle finder, new gear launch, and clearance — and the resulting layout reads like a catalog dump rather than a campaign. The clearance section's typographic "Clearance Clearance Clearance Clearance" repeat at the bottom undercuts the sale urgency established at the top. Clearance and "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency are tonally incompatible: one says "act now," the other says "we're liquidating."
- Verdict: stronger open frame than the last four sends, but the multi-section kitchen-sink layout diffuses the offer and the subject line wastes its first 20 characters on "Big savings inside!" filler.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency + "60%"** dominate the hero — the eye lands there immediately, no hunting required.
- **Seasonal alignment is correct** — "Summer Steals" as the June 1 pivot away from swim-only messaging makes sense and feels fresh relative to the prior run.
- **"SHOP STEALS"** is one of the more specific CTA labels this brand has used recently; verb + noun, action-oriented.
- **Product grid is dense and relevant** — multiple women's summer items (tops, shorts, dresses) visible above the fold, reinforcing the offer with real inventory.
- **"HOT! NEW GEAR" section** breaks up the promotional monotony with an energy that signals the brand is moving beyond clearance-season mode.

## What's weak

- **"Big savings inside!"** opens the subject line — pure filler that pushes the actual offer past comfortable mobile preview truncation.
- **Dual offer creates cognitive split** — the 60% steal message and the conditional 40%/$75 tier compete rather than stack; it's not obvious which to act on first.
- **The $75 minimum is buried in parentheses** in both the subject and body — that's the most important qualifier on the secondary offer and it reads as fine print.
- **Clearance section at the bottom contradicts urgency framing** — "2 DAYS ONLY!" is a scarcity signal; clearance is a desperation signal; running both in the same send muddies the brand positioning.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth Old Navy email since May 29, and the sixth in seven days. Even with the new creative angle, subscriber exhaustion is a growing risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut "Big savings inside!" from the subject entirely** — start with the actual offer. Every character burned on that opener delays the percentage numbers that actually drive opens.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 Days Only: 60% off Summer Steals + 40% off $75+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off Summer Steals ends in 2 days — plus 40% off your $75+ order`
- 2. **Surface the 2-day deadline in the subject** — the urgency lives only in the body right now; moving "2 Days Only" into the subject line makes the time-bound signal work in the inbox before the open.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your savings: 60% off steals, then 40% off when you hit $75`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer's hottest deal window — 2 days to grab 60% off`
- 3. **Strip or separate the clearance section** — either send a dedicated clearance email tomorrow or move it to a footer link. Sharing a hero frame with "2 DAYS ONLY!" and then showing a clearance grid at the bottom sends two different buying signals at once.
- 4. **Make the $75 qualifier a feature, not fine print** — if the goal is basket-building, lean into it: "Spend $75, save 40% — easy math." Call it out as a reward, not a restriction.
- 5. **Consider a send-frequency pause** — May 29–June 1 is now six emails in four days. The brand's prior high-water mark (May 29 "Today only: 50% off everything in women's") scored a 9 partly because it felt like an event. At current cadence, every email feels like noise.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken redirect chain**
The cp.oldnavy.com redirect resolves to an unknown URL type:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?mi_mid=...
  → /g/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?txn=4bfbe000-5dc3-11f1-9425-8e0ec7ba23ea
```
The destination `/g/...` is a bare relative path, not an absolute URL. This is a broken experience — the redirect target either was never set or the redirect service returned a malformed response. This same URL is also the open-tracking pixel host, so it may be doubling as both a beacon and a content link, which compounds the failure.

**WARN — 23 click-redirect links unverified**
All CTA and product links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for this domain, so destination URL correctness, UTM presence, and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed for any of them.

**Open pixel** (`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...`) — present, standard implementation, no issues.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text**
The cp.oldnavy.com tracking/content image has no `alt` attribute:
```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5d86a524-6694-47e0-8042-bbbf69b147db?mi_mid=...
```
With images blocked (default in many clients), this renders as a blank space with no fallback text.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional injects `td { line-height: 0px; }` globally for Outlook 2013+:
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
  td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This can collapse text-only cells in Outlook if a matching `mso-line-height-rule` override is not applied to every `td` that contains live text.

**Duplicate CSS rule selectors**
The stylesheet comment itself flags this: `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block. Duplicate rules are dead weight and create specificity ambiguity; the last declaration wins silently.

**No issues with:** charset declaration (UTF-8), viewport meta, VML namespace declarations for Outlook, or `display:block` on images.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

`mi_mid` and `mi_cid` parameters in tracking URLs carry populated values (`019e84c5-8e00-7000-b82a-4973d69dbe9c`, `67859151839b71d0`), indicating subscriber-level merge data fired correctly.

No unfired token placeholders (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated source. No issues found from available source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not found**
The relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders; its absence risks inbox placement and may trigger "Report spam" instead of "Unsubscribe" in supported clients.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (the machine-readable `POST` mechanism) is not confirmed present. Gmail has enforced this for senders >5k/day since 2024.

**Physical mailing address — not detected**
The QA scanner did not find a physical address in the scanned portion of the HTML. CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. This may be present in the truncated footer — confirm before flagging as a definitive violation.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC — unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Sender domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; authentication posture (SPF pass/fail, DKIM signature validity) cannot be confirmed from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 23 CTAs pass through the `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirect layer, which should append UTM parameters before forwarding to `oldnavy.com`. Because HTTP probes were skipped for this domain, UTM presence on final landing URLs is unconfirmed.

The one probed link (cp.oldnavy.com) returned a broken destination, so continuity for that specific path is broken end-to-end.
