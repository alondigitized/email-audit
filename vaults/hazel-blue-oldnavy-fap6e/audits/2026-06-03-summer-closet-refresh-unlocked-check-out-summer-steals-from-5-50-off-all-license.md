# Summer closet refresh, unlocked: Check out Summer Steals from $5 + 50% off all licensed tees & hoodies*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy sends a kitchen-sink email stuffed with five distinct value propositions: Summer Steals from $5, a "Today Only" 50% off licensed tees deal, an activewear sale, a kids & baby sale, and a clearance push at the bottom. The hero and the "Today Only" badge are the strongest anchors — two things a subscriber could actually act on immediately — but they're immediately undercut by three more sections fighting for attention below the fold. The net effect is that nothing feels urgent or decisive.
- This is the seventh Old Navy email in roughly four days. The prior sends have progressively stacked deal layers (swim → men's → summer steals + 40% threshold) and this one doesn't reset the creative or escalate meaningfully. A subscriber already desensitized by yesterday's "40% off everything + 60% summer steals" email now gets a subject with a colon AND a plus sign AND an asterisk, which signals "fine print" before the first scroll. The email isn't broken, but it's not fighting hard for attention either.
- Structurally, the clearance callout at the bottom is the most jarring non sequitur — triple-stacked "Clearance" banners read as a completely separate send grafted onto the end. This dilutes whatever coherent brand message the top half was building.

## What's working

- **"Today Only" badge with a hard 50% number** is the clearest single hook in the email — visible, specific, creates real urgency.
- **Hero visual hierarchy lands on the offer first** — the 30–60% off Summer Steals banner takes the top slot, eyes follow naturally.
- **Bright, on-season creative** — the color palette and models read as summer without ambiguity.
- **Kids & Baby section adds cross-sell depth** — for family-oriented subscribers, this is a legitimate secondary conversion path.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is 89 characters** — bloated, colon-plus-plus-asterisk construction collapses to gibberish on mobile; the "unlocked" opener is marketing filler that eats prime real estate.
- **Five competing offers dilute urgency** — Summer Steals + Today Only + Activewear + Kids Sale + Clearance is not a campaign, it's a dump.
- **The triple "Clearance Clearance Clearance" banner at the bottom** feels tacked on and visually cheap — it contradicts the curated "Summer Steals" positioning above.
- **No visible personalization or segmentation signal** — after six prior sends in four days, there's nothing here that tells a subscriber this was sent to them specifically.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the 7th email in ~4 days; the "Summer Steals from $5" framing already appeared in yesterday's send, making today's feel like an accidental duplicate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one offer and own it.** The "Today Only: 50% off licensed tees & hoodies" is the strongest differentiator in this email — it's time-bounded and specific. Strip the email to that single concept with one hero CTA. Save Summer Steals and Clearance for a separate send.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the time-bounded offer, not the umbrella.** The colon construction buries the best hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 50% off all licensed tees & hoodies`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fave graphic tees are 50% off — today only`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to extend the subject, not echo it.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop licensed styles from $10 — offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus Summer Steals from $5 while they last.`
- 4. **Cut the clearance section or move it to a separate digest.** A clearance callout at the bottom of a "Summer Steals" email trains subscribers to scroll for the discount, not to act on the hero.
- 5. **Reduce send cadence.** After 7 emails in 4 days, the marginal open rate on this send is sharply diminished. Batching today's and yesterday's offers into one better-designed send would have served both better.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — opens pixel / cp.oldnavy.com**

The Movable Ink personalization pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/333b7969...` is returning an internal redirect to a URL of type `unknown url type: '/g/333b7969...'`. This indicates the Movable Ink content block is resolving to a relative path instead of an absolute URL — the redirect chain breaks before reaching the destination page.

- Evidence: QA probe returned `unknown url type: '/g/333b7969-0f8f-46c9-bb21-ff722543d82c?txn=d4aa8580-5f55-11f1-9ec9-0690aac23d16'`
- Impact: Any click routed through this block will likely 404 or loop.
- Root cause: Movable Ink block is probably misconfigured — the `cp_tp` payload encodes a relative `/g/` path rather than an absolute `https://` destination.

**23 tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Not an error, but none were probed; destination health is unverified by this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text** on the Movable Ink image block:

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/333b7969-0f8f-46c9-bb21-ff722543d82c?mi_mid=..." alt="">
```

The `alt=""` is present but empty. If this block renders a product or promotional offer, a screen reader will silently skip it — the offer is invisible to assistive technology users.

**Open pixel** rendered in the visible DOM with no `alt`:

```html
<img src="https://click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?CB57NG7GTVKU5BSXK2M3EDEPAU..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Empty `alt=""` is technically correct for a tracking pixel (decorative), so no issue here.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors** in the mobile media query block — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different values. The second definition wins; the first is dead code. Low severity but indicates a copy-paste error in the template.

**Outlook line-height override** in the MSO conditional comment sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally. This is a standard Outlook fix, but it can collapse single-line cells that rely on inherited line-height rather than explicit values. Acceptable if tested; flag if any Outlook rendering regressions appear.
