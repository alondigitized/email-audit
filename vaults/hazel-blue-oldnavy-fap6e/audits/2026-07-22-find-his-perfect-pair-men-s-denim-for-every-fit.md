# Find his perfect pair: Men's denim for every fit
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is an editorial/fit-guide email — Old Navy is pitching the breadth of their men's denim range rather than leading with a price. That's a legitimate strategy, but it runs headlong into the brand's own recent history: the last four sends that scored 7+ all had a hard price anchor in the hero ($6 tees, 50% off jeans, 60-70% off dresses). Without a number, "Find his perfect pair" reads more like a catalog page than a reason to click today.
- The email then breaks its own promise halfway down: after the men's denim section, it pivots to a Back-to-School kids pricing module and a cozy/plaid lifestyle block — neither of which has anything to do with men's denim fits. A man who opened because of the subject line now has to scroll past content aimed at his kids to find anything relevant again.
- The subject is clean and under 50 characters, the send cadence is fine (women's activewear yesterday, men's today — good rotation), but the lack of a price hook and the kitchen-sink structure leave this as a browseable-but-forgettable send. It doesn't earn a click so much as it invites a vague scroll.

## What's working

- Men's denim hero is well-organized — multiple fit silhouettes displayed clearly, male model shown, strong visual anchor for the category.
- Fit-variety framing ("for every fit") is genuinely useful messaging for men who struggle with denim sizing.
- Good cadence rotation: after yesterday's women's activewear 50%-off send, pivoting to a men's category avoids redundancy.
- No render bugs visible — the layout appears clean throughout all sections.
- Subject line is tight at 49 characters, readable on mobile.

## What's weak

- Zero price anchor in the men's denim hero — "Find his perfect pair" without a deal is aspirational copy, not a reason to act.
- Email breaks its subject-line promise mid-scroll: Back-to-School kids pricing and "The Cozy" plaid block are irrelevant to a men's denim shopper.
- No urgency signal anywhere — no "today only," no deadline, no limited stock language.
- July 20's send ("Move that thumb!") scored 8/10 partially because it had a specific price ($6 tees, 50% off Wow jeans) anchoring every section; this send has none.
- The Back-to-School tiered pricing (the $5/$6/$7/$8 block) visible mid-email belongs in a dedicated kids send — here it dilutes the men's narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the men's denim hero** — even "from $25" or "30% off all men's denim" turns a browseable editorial into a buyable moment. Every high-scoring Old Navy send in recent history has had a number above the fold.
- 2. **Kill or collapse the off-topic modules** — strip the Back-to-School and Cozy blocks from this send entirely, or move them to a dedicated secondary module at the very bottom with a visual separator. A men's denim shopper who hits kids plaid after the hero will bounce.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — "This weekend only," "Valid through Sunday" — even a soft urgency signal materially lifts click rate on catalog-style sends.
- 4. **Make one fit the hero** — instead of showing all fits in equal weight, spotlight the bestseller (e.g., Athletic Straight) as the anchor and present others as alternates below. Specificity converts better than variety.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/30c9e989-9e1a-42a2-882e-3261cb941441?..." width="1" height="1">
```

The open-tracking pixel from `click.email.oldnavy.com` correctly uses `alt=""`. The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel omits the `alt` attribute entirely. Screen readers will read the full src URL. Fix: add `alt=""`.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS selector collision — `.MspaceTop` / `.MspaceBot`**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; } /* duplicate */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot  { padding-bottom: 5px; }
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; } /* duplicate */
```

Last-declaration wins: any element using `.MspaceTop` or `.MspaceBot` will always render at `10px`, ignoring the `5px` intent. This is a latent layout defect that will silently misfire on any element where `5px` spacing was intended.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px }`**

```html

```

This is a known Outlook fix pattern, but a blanket `line-height: 0px` on all `td` elements can collapse cells without explicit per-cell overrides. Not confirmed broken, but worth verifying in Outlook 2016/2019 renders.
