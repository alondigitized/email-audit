# Hot temps, hotter prices: 60% off all shorts & swim*, $5 dresses & half off everything* else
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a triple-deal send from Old Navy — 60% off shorts & swim, $5 dresses, and 50% off everything else — packaged under a summer heat headline. The hero is clear and the discount stack is real. Problem: this is virtually identical in structure and offer depth to the June 19 send ("Three deals, one cart: 60% off all shorts & swim*, $5 dresses and 50% off everything* else"), sent less than 24 hours prior. Old Navy is now running the same creative on consecutive days with a slightly swapped headline. That's not a campaign — it's fatigue.
- The email itself is tidy and the layout works. Offer hierarchy lands correctly: 60% off leads, then secondary offers follow. The "SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" section is the most interesting element — it signals some segmentation or personalization is happening below the fold. But the hero module is crowded with three competing offers and no single CTA to anchor against.
- Bottom line: solid execution of a format Old Navy has over-indexed on this month. The repeat creative erodes trust faster than the discounts build urgency.

## What's working

- **Three-deal hierarchy is legible** — 60% off shorts/swim reads loudest, then 50% off everything else, which is the right priority order.
- **Summer relevance is on** — shorts, swim, and the "hot temps" framing match the mid-June moment perfectly.
- **"SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" module** is the best personalization signal in the email — suggests size-filtered product rows, which increases click intent.
- **Layout is clean** — no broken images or overlapping elements visible in the render; the multi-section stack reads without confusion.

## What's weak

- **Day-over-day creative recycling** — this is essentially the June 19 send with a headline swap. A subscriber who opened yesterday already dismissed or converted; re-sending the same offer compresses the audience and risks unsubscribes.
- **Subject line is 93 characters** — it front-loads the fun ("Hot temps, hotter prices") but buries all three deals in a run-on clause. Mobile clients will truncate after ~50 chars, cutting the actual offers.
- **No single hero CTA** — three offers at the top creates decision paralysis; there's no dominant "shop now" moment to anchor against.
- **$5 dresses gets visually buried** — it's the most surprising and clickable number in the send, but it appears to be competing for equal weight with the blunter "50% off everything" frame.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give $5 dresses its own hero moment.** It's the most specific, lowest-friction number in this email and the most likely to convert casual browsers. Lead with it or give it a dedicated banner rather than third-billing.
- 2. **Break the three offers into two sends** — shorts/swim (60%) and dresses/everything else — spaced by a day. Eliminates the fatigue signal and preserves open rate for the second send.
- 3. **Trim the subject to fit mobile.** "Hot temps" is filler; drop it and let the deals lead.
- 4. **Add a time deadline.** The June 18 PowerChill send (8/10) worked because "TODAY ONLY!" created a credible close date. Nothing here signals when this expires.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$5 dresses + 60% off swim: summer's hottest prices`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three deals, one cart — ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab summer styles before they're gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**

The Marigold open-tracking pixel (`cp.oldnavy.com/o/3d7780af...`) has no `alt` attribute:
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/3d7780af-..." width="1" height="1">
```
Screen readers may announce this as an unlabeled image. Best practice is `alt=""` on purely decorative/tracking pixels.

**CSS class duplication in responsive stylesheet**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with conflicting values (5px and 10px). The second declaration wins in all conforming parsers, but the dead rule adds noise and can mask future edits:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}   /* dead */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* wins */
```

**Outlook line-height zero override**

The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a common but aggressive workaround — any `<td>` that relies on inherited line-height for spacing in Outlook will collapse. Confirm all spacer rows use explicit `height` attributes rather than padding/line-height.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent from captured headers**

RFC 2369 `List-Unsubscribe` and RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (one-click) headers were not detected. QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay capture gap, but if these headers are genuinely absent:
- Gmail and Yahoo Mail will suppress the native unsubscribe UI for bulk senders.
- As of Feb 2024 Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements, one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is mandatory for senders above 5,000/day threshold.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The automated scan did not find one in the captured portion. If the address exists below the fold in the footer (likely, given the HTML is truncated), this is a false positive. Verify the rendered footer contains the Gap Inc. / Old Navy mailing address.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to the AgentMail relay not forwarding this header. Deliverability posture cannot be confirmed from this audit. Recommend verifying `Authentication-Results` at the MX level directly.
