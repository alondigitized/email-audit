# $6 tees & tanks are going FAST
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's same-day re-engagement drop on the $6 tees / $4 tanks offer — the "going FAST" scarcity version of the morning "👕 $6 TEES & TANKS" send (which already scored 7/10 in this audit series). The creative is structurally identical: "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner at top, price anchors front-and-center, followed by a long scroll of women's summer product grids, a new arrivals section, and a clearance block at the bottom. The bones are solid and the price point is genuinely compelling.
- The problem is subscriber fatigue, not execution. Sending two emails about the same offer on the same calendar day — with nearly the same hero — turns a good deal into noise. By the time this "going FAST" version hits the inbox, a real subscriber has already seen $6 tees twice. The urgency device ("going FAST") is softer and less specific than the body's own "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner, which undercuts the subject line's only job.
- The email is competent promotional work on its own terms, but as the second same-day serve of the identical creative, it earns diminishing returns on attention.

## What's working

- **Price anchors are legible immediately** — "$4" and "$6" are the first numbers the eye lands on, no hunting required.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" banner creates honest, specific urgency** that the body copy delivers on.
- **Product variety is present** — the scroll covers tees, tanks, new arrivals, and clearance, giving different segments a reason to keep reading.
- **No render bugs** — images load, layout holds, no overlapping elements or broken gaps visible in the render.
- **Subject line is under 50 characters (30)** and reads cleanly on mobile without truncation.

## What's weak

- **Second same-day send of the same offer erodes the "going FAST" premise** — if it's really going fast, why did you have time to send two emails?
- **"Going FAST" is vaguer urgency than the body's own "2 DAYS ONLY!"** — the subject under-sells what's actually inside.
- **No visible loyalty or member pricing** — Old Navy Card holders get extra perks but nothing in the render calls that out.
- **Long scroll with multiple competing sections dilutes the hero** — new arrivals, clearance, and the main $6 tees offer are all fighting for click priority.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one daily send, not two.** If re-engagement is required, wait until the following morning (e.g., "Last chance: $6 tees end tonight"). Two same-day sends of the same creative trains subscribers to ignore the second one.
- 2. **Match the subject urgency to the body's deadline.** The email says "2 DAYS ONLY!" — the subject should say that, not the softer "going FAST."
- 3. **Lead with the specific deadline in the subject**, e.g., "Ends Sunday: $6 tees & $4 tanks."
- 4. **Surface card-member pricing or stacking** if applicable — that's a click differentiator that would separate this from a generic promo.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Sunday: $6 tees & $4 tanks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days left — $6 tees, $4 tanks, gone Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab yours before the 2-day window closes — shop women's, men's & kids'`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$6 tees + $4 tanks — deal ends Sunday. Clearance too.`
