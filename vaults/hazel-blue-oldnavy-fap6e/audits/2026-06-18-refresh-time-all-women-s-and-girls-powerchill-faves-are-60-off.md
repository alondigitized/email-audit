# Refresh time! ALL women's and girls PowerChill faves* are 60% OFF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy comes in hot on June 18 with a product-specific 60% off play — the PowerChill collection for women and girls. The hero is clean: "TODAY ONLY!" urgency, a lifestyle model in a lavender romper, and the discount badge right up top. Compared to the blunt "Summer Steals" battering-ram of June 15 (9/10), this one has more targeting precision — PowerChill is a real sub-brand with a defined audience.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. The email can't commit to a single message. After the PowerChill hero, it pivots to a Flag Tees seasonal push (a perfectly valid 4th of July setup), then drops into a triple-stacked "Clearance Clearance Clearance" block. Three separate offers means no offer owns the email. By the time you reach the clearance section, the PowerChill urgency has completely dissipated.
- The asterisk on "PowerChill faves*" in the subject does quiet damage — it signals exclusions before the reader even opens, and Old Navy's subscriber base at this point in the promo calendar is conditioned to distrust fine print. A tight, one-offer email with a clean CTA would outperform this cluttered kitchen-sink layout.

## What's working

- **"TODAY ONLY!" urgency in the hero is credible and visible** — the scarcity frame is front-loaded where it counts.
- **Product specificity beats the generic "Summer Steals" frame** — PowerChill is a named collection with a clear value prop ("soft cotton with stretch, all-day comfort"), giving shoppers something concrete to evaluate.
- **Women's lifestyle model in the hero matches the target demographic** — the lavender romper shoot fits the PowerChill aesthetic and reads as aspirational but accessible.
- **Flag Tees section lands a seasonal beat** — the 4th of July setup is timely for an email landing June 18, and Old Navy's flag tee franchise has genuine equity.

## What's weak

- **Three competing offers destroy message hierarchy** — PowerChill, Flag Tees, and Clearance fight each other; the eye doesn't know what to click.
- **The asterisk on "faves\*" in the subject erodes trust before the open** — subscribers burned by past exclusions will discount the offer before they see it.
- **The triple "Clearance Clearance Clearance" block visually overwhelms** — the repeating red-and-white stacked text is louder than the hero and undercuts the premium feel PowerChill is trying to project.
- **Subject buries the lead** — "Refresh time!" is a weak opener; the specific offer (PowerChill + 60%) doesn't land until character 15+, well past mobile preview cut.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the sixth Old Navy email audited in five days; a subscriber who opened the June 17 tees/swim/shorts send yesterday has no good reason to believe today's offer is fundamentally different.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one offer per email.** PowerChill 60% off is the story. Flag Tees and Clearance each deserve their own send. The bleed of three offers makes none of them land — and splitting them creates three opens instead of one confused one.
- 2. **Move "TODAY ONLY" into the subject line.** The urgency is hidden in the hero but absent where it drives open rate. A subject that leads with the time constraint converts faster.
- 3. **Remove or explain the asterisk.** Either drop the exclusions or surface them clearly in the hero ("select styles" is fine — a mysterious asterisk is not).
- 4. **Replace the Clearance block's stacked repetition** with a single clean CTA — "Shop Clearance →" — so it functions as a secondary option, not a competing headline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `TODAY ONLY: Women's & Girls' PowerChill — 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off PowerChill ends tonight — here's your pick`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Soft stretch fabric built for all-day wear. Sizes going fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your pick before it's gone — PowerChill faves, 60% off today only.`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy PowerChill 60% OFF

### 1. Technical Summary
One confirmed broken redirect in the tracking/pixel infrastructure causes a hard link error. Compliance headers are absent or uncapturable at the relay level, which is significant given Old Navy's send volume and current Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com tracking URL**
The campaign platform pixel URL resolves to an invalid relative path:
```
Resolved destination: /g/12937b4b-1b19-47c5-b440-aeba5bbde0a2?txn=f53baa80-70d1-11f1-a677-aea38baf8e0c
Source: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/12937b4b-1b19-47c5-b440-aeba5bbde0a2?mi_mid=019eda63-...
```
The `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect chain returns a relative `/g/` path instead of an absolute URL. This pixel appears in two places: the hidden preheader div and the open-tracker block. If this URL doubles as a click destination (not just a pixel), affected recipients hit a dead end. The `o/` → `/g/` redirect handler on cp.oldnavy.com appears misconfigured for this campaign ID.

**23 click-tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Cannot confirm final destination integrity without probing the redirect chain. No broken final URLs were detected where probing was possible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class definitions (minor)**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` is assigned to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`, and `.MspaceBot` to both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. Last-write wins, silently overriding the first definition. This is a CSS authoring bug; mobile spacing for those classes will be inconsistent.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional stylesheet sets:
```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
This is a known Outlook height-fix pattern but must be overridden on every content `<td>`. If any content cells lack an explicit `line-height`, Outlook 2013+ will collapse them to zero height.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel**
```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/12937b4b-...
```
This `<img>` has no `alt` attribute at all (distinct from `alt=""`). Screenreaders may announce the raw URL. For tracking pixels, `alt=""` is the correct pattern.

**Doctype** — XHTML 1.0 Transitional is intentional for email and not an issue.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent at relay**
Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe` (and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click, per RFC 8058) for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. These headers are not present in what the AgentMail relay captured. This may be a relay capture gap (headers stripped upstream), but if they are genuinely absent from delivered mail, Old Navy is out of compliance with current Gmail/Yahoo requirements and risks inbox demotion.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML is truncated — the address is likely in the footer. Confirm it is present in the full render before flagging as a true violation.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown at the relay level. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. For a sender of this volume, unverified authentication is a deliverability and compliance risk.
