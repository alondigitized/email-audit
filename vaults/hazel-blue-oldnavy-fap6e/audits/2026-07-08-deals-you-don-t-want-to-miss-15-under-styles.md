# Deals you don't want to miss: $15 & under styles
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's second consecutive send built around the same "$15 & under Everyday Favorites" hero — yesterday (July 7) ran "Fill that cart with $15 & under styles" with the same 2 DAYS ONLY badge and the same clearance grid. Today's send swaps that subject for the even blander "Deals you don't want to miss: $15 & under styles." That's not a refresh; that's a rename. A subscriber who opened yesterday gets the same email twice with a worse hook.
- Taken in isolation, the email is mechanically competent. The price anchor is front and center, the layout flows cleanly from hero → deep clearance → activewear → summer swim, and "$3.99 deep end clearance in your size" is a genuinely sharp second hook. Old Navy's use of "in your size" as a CTA qualifier is one of the few personalization tricks the brand reliably lands.
- The problem is the aggregate. After a week of tee-price grids, two July 4 sends, and now two identical $15-and-under sends in 24 hours, the brand is burning its list on creative that doesn't differentiate day-to-day. The $3.99 hook is strong enough to headline — burying it below a repeated hero is the single biggest missed opportunity in this email.

## What's working

- "$15 & under" price anchor dominates the hero without any hunting; price is the first thing the eye lands on
- "$3.99 Deep End Clearance" in the second block is a sharp, specific discount that adds a second reason to click
- "SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" CTA is the most specific button copy Old Navy uses — verb + noun + qualifier
- "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge anchors credible urgency that's consistent with the countdown Old Navy has been running all week
- Multi-section layout (clearance → HOT NEW GEAR → TIME TO CHILL) gives multiple entry points for browsers

## What's weak

- Near-identical creative to July 7's send — subscribers who opened yesterday see no meaningful difference
- "Deals you don't want to miss" opener is generic FOMO with zero informational value; it's weaker than yesterday's "Fill that cart"
- The $3.99 deep clearance — the sharpest price point in the email — is buried below the fold, not the hero
- No loyalty/NavyCard member differentiation visible anywhere in the render
- Four distinct promotional threads (everyday favorites, deep clearance, activewear, summer) dilute focus without a clear anchor priority

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero: lead with $3.99 deep clearance, not $15 & under.** $3.99 is the number that differentiates today from yesterday. Put it above the fold. $15 & under is fine as a secondary section.
- 2. **Write a subject that doesn't echo yesterday's.** "Deals you don't want to miss" is measurably worse than "Fill that cart" — at minimum the rewrite should front-load the sharper price point.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$3.99 clearance — your size is in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: Deep End Clearance from $3.99`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop by size before it sells out — plus $15 & under Everyday Favorites`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `2 days left on $15 & under + deep clearance from $3.99`
- 3. **Add an explicit end-date to "2 DAYS ONLY!"** — "Ends 11:59 PM Thursday" reads more credible than a floating badge.
- 4. **Consolidate to two sections maximum.** Clearance + one lifestyle category (activewear or summer, not both) would give the email a cleaner editorial spine.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**

```html

```

Empty `alt=""` on a 1×1 pixel is technically acceptable (decorative/invisible), but the QA tool flagged it. No action needed here — blank alt on a tracking pixel is correct practice.

**CSS duplicate selectors**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block (`.space-top5`/`.space-top10` both map to `.MspaceTop`; same for bot). The second rule silently overrides the first. Low impact in practice, but indicates a CSS generation bug in the template.

**Outlook conditional CSS**

```css
/* Outlook 2013+ Height Fix */
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```

This global `td` reset inside the MSO conditional block will collapse line height on all cells in Outlook 2013+. This is a known risky pattern — it requires every content `td` to re-declare `line-height` explicitly or text will render collapsed. No immediate break confirmed from source alone, but warrants Outlook 2013/2016 render testing.

**XHTML DOCTYPE**

The document uses `XHTML 1.0 Transitional` — standard for email. No issue.
