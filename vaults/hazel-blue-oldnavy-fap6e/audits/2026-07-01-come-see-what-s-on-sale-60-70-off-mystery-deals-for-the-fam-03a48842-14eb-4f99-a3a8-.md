# Come see what's on sale! 60-70% OFF mystery deals for the fam
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy leans into a "mystery reveal" mechanic for this July 1 send — a 60–70% off sale where you can't see which styles qualify until you click. The deeper discount range (up from the 50%-off drumbeat of the past week) is a genuine step up, and "TODAY ONLY!" in the hero adds real urgency. But "mystery" is a double-edged hook: it drives curiosity while breeding distrust, and after seven-plus Old Navy emails in five days, subscriber goodwill is running thin.
- The TrekTech module in the body is a recycled asset from the June 27–28 activewear blitz, which undercuts the "new deals" premise. If you just saw TrekTech headlined twice in the last four days, its reappearance here reads as filler. The email is technically clean — no broken images, no obvious layout bugs — but the creative energy is concentrated entirely in the mystery mechanic rather than in product storytelling.
- Net judgment: a competent promotional email with a gimmick that works once. At this send frequency, it needs sharper payoff.

## What's working

- **Deeper discount is visible immediately.** 60–70% off is a concrete step above the prior week's consistent 50% framing — the upgrade registers at a glance.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" urgency badge lands above the fold.** The hero hierarchy is clean: badge → discount → CTA, all without scrolling.
- **Mystery mechanic earns curiosity clicks.** The question-mark graphic and "Click to reveal" copy are a tighter creative idea than a generic product grid — it gives a reason to engage beyond price alone.
- **Product thumbnails below the hero provide immediate context.** The grid reassures subscribers that actual product exists behind the mystery, reducing bounce risk.

## What's weak

- **"Mystery" conflicts with specificity.** The subject promises "mystery deals for the fam" but offers no category anchor — apparel, activewear, kids, shoes? There's nothing to trigger category-level relevance before the click.
- **TrekTech module is recycled creative.** This feature ran June 27 and June 28. Subscribers who've been opening those emails will notice the repeat — it makes the "new deals" promise feel hollow.
- **"Discover More" app section is misplaced.** Sandwiched between the TrekTech module and the footer, it breaks shopping momentum with a brand-utility pitch that belongs in a lifecycle email, not a flash-sale send.
- **Send cadence fatigue is real.** This is at minimum the seventh Old Navy email in five days. The "TODAY ONLY!" urgency has now appeared multiple times in the brand history — credibility erodes with each repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the mystery mechanic OR commit to it.** Either reveal a category ("60–70% off kids' styles — TODAY ONLY") or lean fully into the mystery with a countdown + explicit reveal time. The current hybrid — a discount range with no category — satisfies neither instinct.
- 2. **Replace or remove the TrekTech module.** Subscribers saw TrekTech headlined June 27 and 28. Swap in a fresh category (denim, swim, kids' 4th of July) that doesn't echo last week.
- 3. **Cut the "Discover More" app section.** This is a flash-sale email. Every module below the fold should drive product clicks, not app downloads.
- 4. **Compress the subject.** At 61 characters it clips on mobile. "TODAY ONLY: 60–70% off mystery styles" is 38 characters and loses nothing meaningful.
- **Subject Alt A:** `TODAY ONLY: 60–70% off mystery styles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Mystery sale: 60–70% off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tap to reveal which styles are up to 70% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The fam's summer styles just got a lot cheaper. Reveal yours.`

One confirmed broken redirect from the cp.oldnavy.com tracking endpoint produces an unresolvable relative URL, which will cause open-tracking to fail for the affected cohort. Three compliance headers are absent or unverifiable, and the plain-text body is overwhelmingly URL content — a deliverability risk.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken open-tracker redirect**
The DV tracking pixel at:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/55dee75a-5193-4487-8582-7b0004001782?mi_mid=019f1dfa-...
```
resolves to an unresolvable relative path:
```
/g/55dee75a-5193-4487-8582-7b0004001782?txn=514a7700-7589-11f1-ad30-764a3bc6abf2
```
A relative redirect is not a valid HTTP response for a standalone domain; the client has no base URL to resolve `/g/...` against. Open-event tracking from this pixel will fail or be silently dropped.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links unverified**
All links routed through `click.email.oldnavy.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination URL integrity (404s, expired landing pages) cannot be confirmed from this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking image**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` DV pixel has no `alt` attribute at all (not even `alt=""`):
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/55dee75a-...&cp_cid=1bc829e71098f268" width="1" height="1">
```
Should be `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader announcement of the URL string.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in media query**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice under `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)` — later declaration wins, but the duplication indicates a template merge conflict and increases parse overhead.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional: `line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly`**
Applied globally to all `td` elements in the MSO 9+ conditional block. This is a known fix for Outlook ghost-spacing, but `line-height: 0px` on `td` without explicit per-cell overrides can collapse text-bearing cells in Outlook 2013–2019. Acceptable if all content cells have explicit `line-height` overrides, but cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.

**[INFO] XHTML 1.0 Transitional + VML namespaces**
Legacy but correct for broad Outlook compatibility. No issue.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**
Both `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369) and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) are missing or were not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use these headers to surface the native unsubscribe UI; their absence increases spam-folder placement risk under Google's 2024 bulk sender requirements. Confirm whether the ESP injects these at send time — if not, add them.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address in body**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email body. The provided HTML does not include one in the visible/truncated section. If it appears below the fold in the footer (common), this is a false positive from the QA probe — verify the full source. If genuinely absent, this is a legal compliance failure.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified. The `oldnavy.com` domain uses `email.oldnavy.com` as the sending subdomain (`From: oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com`) — confirm DKIM is signed for `email.oldnavy.com` and that DMARC alignment is met (either relaxed subdomain match or explicit `email.oldnavy.com` inclusion in the policy).
