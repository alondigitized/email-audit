# You deserve these! SIXTY PERCENT OFF Summer Steals + get an EXTRA 20% OFF your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy leans on the same "60% OFF Summer Steals" frame they already sent on June 15 (which scored 9/10 here), now with an EXTRA 20% stack bolted on top. The escalation is rational — stacked discounts are a real incentive — but the creative hasn't changed: same "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner, same product grid layout, same playbook. The subscriber's inbox has seen the 60% Summer Steals headline two days in a row, and the "You deserve these!" opener is the kind of pseudo-emotional lede that reads as padding rather than warmth.
- The email itself renders cleanly. The hierarchy works — 60% off hero, product grid, swimwear module, a neon "HOT NEW GEAR!" lifestyle block, then a loud clearance drum-roll at the bottom. Lots of reasons to scroll, but also lots of competing sections, which fragments attention.
- The core problem is cadence fatigue compounding creative sameness. If yesterday's 60% Summer Steals send had already converted you, you're not clicking today. If it didn't, the +20% stack is a real nudge — but the subject line is 88 characters of all-caps and exclamation overload that doesn't earn that nudge.

## What's working

- **60% hero lands immediately** — the "2 DAYS ONLY! 60% OFF Summer Steals" block is the first thing the eye hits; no ambiguity about the offer.
- **Stacked discount is genuinely additive** — 60% + extra 20% is a real escalation from yesterday and gives a subscriber who passed on June 15 a new reason to act.
- **Multiple content modules keep scrollers engaged** — swimwear, hot new gear, and clearance give different reasons to click past the hero if summer basics aren't your thing.
- **Clearance section is visually distinct** — the repeating "Clearance Clearance Clearance" type treatment in alternating colors creates strong visual contrast from the rest of the email.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder artifacts visible.

## What's weak

- **Creative is nearly identical to the June 15 send** — same layout, same "Summer Steals" frame, only the extra 20% is new. A subscriber who received both will feel like they're being shown the same email twice.
- **"You deserve these!" opener is generic** — it's not tied to any product or specific offer; it's filler that delays getting to the actual news (the +20% stack).
- **HOT NEW GEAR! section feels tone-deaf relative to the sale message** — a neon lifestyle block mid-email breaks the discount momentum and signals a different (younger, full-price) audience.
- **Subject line is 88 characters** — well past mobile truncation and stuffed with all-caps, which reads as shouting.
- **No loyalty or member pricing visible** — missed opportunity to reward Navyist cardholders with a visible exclusive, especially on a stacked-discount email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the new information** — the +20% stack is the actual news today. The subject should open with that, not repeat "60% OFF Summer Steals" which subscribers already saw yesterday.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off on top of 60% — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack it: 60% Summer Steals + 20% extra, 2 days`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The steepest the Summer Steals discount has been all month.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% was already good. Here's why today's better.`
- 2. **Drop or move the HOT NEW GEAR! block** — on a sale-focused send, a full-price lifestyle section dilutes the urgency. If it must stay, push it below clearance.
- 3. **Give the extra 20% its own visual moment** — the hero is doing all the work for the 60% off; there should be a dedicated lockup showing the math (e.g., "Take 60% off, then stack an extra 20%") to make the compounding tangible.
- 4. **Add a Navyist callout** — even a one-liner ("Navyist members: your points stack with this offer") would reward loyalty members and create perceived exclusivity.
- 5. **Cut "You deserve these!"** — replace with a concrete bridge to the offer, like "Summer Steals just got steeper."

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-layout build with responsive CSS breakpoints. One broken link constitutes a hard failure; the remaining issues are compliance gaps and deliverability signals that warrant attention before future sends.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken ConvertPipe/CDP redirect**

The pixel/tracking URL at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/2ae5fef1-81a2-4a91-a4af-c67a1d16c85e` returns an error resolving to an unknown URL type:

```
/g/2ae5fef1-81a2-4a91-a4af-c67a1d16c85e?txn=ace93c00-6c3c-11f1-be2c-0639c70ede34
```

This is embedded as a 1×1 tracking pixel in the `<body>` preheader block. While a broken tracking pixel does not block the recipient experience, it indicates a failed CDP event ingestion (likely ConvertPipe or a similar redirect service), meaning open attribution for this send is not being recorded correctly.

**[INFO] 23 tracking links skipped by QA probe**

All CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed for destination validity — downstream link rot or coupon landing page failures cannot be ruled out from the source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

The ConvertPipe pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/2ae5fef1...` has an empty `alt=""`. This is technically acceptable for a spacer/pixel, but the open-tracking pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...` also uses `alt=""`. Both are correct practice for invisible pixels, so no action required — flagging for completeness.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class bindings**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each bound to two selectors in the mobile media query:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* overwrites previous */
```

The second declaration silently wins. If `space-top5` elements are expected to render at 5px on mobile, they will not. This is a CSS authoring bug, not a rendering crash, but warrants audit against the intended mobile layout.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override**

The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a known workaround but can collapse text cells if any `<td>` relies on inherited line-height rather than an explicit value.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`, `%%first_name%%`) are visible in the truncated source. The subject line uses no personalization tokens. No issues found from available source.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The QA scan found none in the parsed text. This is either in the non-truncated footer (possible, given truncation) or genuinely absent — the sending team must confirm the full HTML footer includes a street address before treating this as a false negative.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) were detected. The QA note attributes this to possible AgentMail relay header stripping, so the headers may exist on the wire but not be visible to the checker. However:

- Gmail and Apple Mail both surface one-click unsubscribe from `List-Unsubscribe-Post`; missing it degrades the unsubscribe UX and can increase spam reports.
- Senders above 5,000 msgs/day to Gmail must support one-click unsubscribe per Google's 2024 sender requirements. This should be verified at the ESP level, not inferred from the HTML.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication result not captured**

`Authentication-Results` header was not available via the relay. Deliverability posture (SPF pass/fail, DKIM signature validity, DMARC alignment) cannot be confirmed from this audit. Must be verified via ESP delivery logs or a header-complete sample.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[INFO] 23 CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`**

The click-redirect domain was not probed for final destination URLs, so UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from this source. A full link trace (e.g., following one redirect chain to its terminal URL) should verify that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are appended to all oldnavy.com destinations.

No issues confirmed — but no confirmation of UTM coverage either.
