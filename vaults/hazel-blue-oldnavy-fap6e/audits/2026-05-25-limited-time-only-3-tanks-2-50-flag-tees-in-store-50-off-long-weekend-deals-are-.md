# ⌛ Limited time only: $3 tanks (+ $2.50 flag tees in-store!) + 50% off long weekend deals are ending
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is effectively the third consecutive send of the same $3-tank / $2.50-flag-tee / 50% off creative — the only meaningful pivot from May 24's two sends is the urgency reframe: "are ending" instead of "starts." The email itself is cleanly rendered and well-structured: the hero hits the price first, the "2 DAYS ONLY!" lock-up creates a credible deadline, and the "Your Price: $2.50" member-tier callout is a genuine value signal. The bottom third adds an editorial "3 Activewear Names To Know" section that briefly breaks the pure-promo cadence, though it arrives too late to rescue fatigued subscribers who tapped out two emails ago.
- The core problem isn't the creative — it's that Old Navy has now sent ~15 emails in a week with overlapping subject lines and nearly identical bodies. A subscriber who opened yesterday's send has no reason to open this one except the word "ending," and even that urgency has been devalued by repetition.
- Clear judgement: fine execution of a stale concept. The "ending" hook adds just enough urgency to justify the send, but the cadence damage is cumulative and this email pays part of that bill.

## What's working

- **Price-first hero** — "$3" is enormous and impossible to miss; the "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge reinforces the deadline without burying it.
- **Member pricing callout** — "Your Price: $2.50" distinguishes the loyalty tier from the rack price right in the opening panel.
- **Patriotic creative fits the moment** — star-spangled flag-tee imagery and red/white/blue palette are coherent with Memorial Day weekend and don't feel out of season.
- **Editorial break** — "3 Activewear Names To Know" near the bottom gives the email a non-purely-promotional beat that the May 24 sends lacked.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Third verse, same as the first** — the hero panel is visually indistinguishable from the May 24 sends; a returning subscriber sees nothing new.
- **Subject line is 97 characters** — most mobile inboxes truncate at ~40-50, so everything after "$3 tanks" is invisible at open; the carefully-written "are ending" urgency likely never renders on a phone.
- **No single unambiguous CTA button** — the 50% off section resolves into a grid of category tiles with no dominant "Shop Now" anchor; click intent is scattered.
- **The in-store restriction is easy to miss** — "$2.50 flag tees" is prominently sized, but "(in-store)" is small and parenthetical; online-only subscribers may click through and feel deceived.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject in half** — the double-offer subject has been used across three sends and still won't display fully on mobile. Commit to one offer: the "$3 tanks" ending angle is the sharper hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `⌛ Ends tonight: $3 tanks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — $3 tanks + 50% off`
- 2. **Write a real preheader** — the subject is so long it almost certainly crowds the preheader to nothing; a short complement like "Memorial Day weekend deals wrap up tonight" would add the second-sentence context mobile preview needs.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Memorial Day weekend deals wrap tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop now — sale ends at midnight`
- 3. **Add a single dominant CTA button below the hero** — something like "Shop $3 Tanks" directly under the hero image before the 50% off grid. Right now the eye falls off the hero and into a product tile array with no clear next step.
- 4. **Clarify the in-store restriction visually** — surface the "in-store only" language at the same font size as the price rather than tucking it in parentheses. Reduces bounce and misplaced cart frustration.
- 5. **Rest the list after this send** — this is the third "same creative, new urgency frame" in two days. The next send should either introduce genuinely new product or pause entirely; another same-day follow-up will crater open rates further.


Full pixel URL:

The pixel response appears to redirect to a relative path, which a browser will resolve but which breaks crawlers and headless checks. This suggests the pixel endpoint is returning a `Location: /g/...` redirect rather than a 200 or absolute redirect. Net effect: open-rate or engagement data may be under-counted in environments that don't follow relative redirects (some ESPs, some privacy proxies).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/97f1d43b-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Empty `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative/tracking pixels under WCAG 2.1 (the attribute is present, value is intentionally blank). Not a violation, but noted.

**[WARN] Outlook line-height override may collapse content**

The MSO conditional block sets:

```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```

This is a known pattern for fixing Outlook spacing bugs, but a blanket `line-height: 0px` on all `td` elements can collapse text in Outlook 2013–2019 if any `td` lacks an explicit `line-height` override. No confirmed visual break without a full render test, but the selector scope is broader than best practice recommends.

**CSS duplicate class selector** — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different values in the mobile stylesheet, with a comment acknowledging the duplication. The last definition wins; the earlier one is dead code. Low risk but should be cleaned.
