# Homepage snapshot · oldnavy.gap.com · 2026-05-08
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy is clearly in Memorial Day prep mode — a smiling little girl with an American flag pinwheel, all red and blue, screaming "the holiday weekend haul starts now." As a mom who basically lives for a good Old Navy clearance event, the 50% off banner had me at hello. But below those two promo strips, the page just... sits there. Cute kid, no copy, no button, nowhere to click. I'd have to work just to start shopping.

## What's working

- **The 50% off banner** — bold white text on dark blue, first thing I read. That's a number that makes me stop scrolling Instagram and open a browser tab.
- **The Encore Members free shipping strip** — "$50+ for Encore Members" tells me exactly what I need to spend to get free shipping, which as a member changes my cart math immediately.
- **The hero imagery** — a kid in a red polka-dot dress holding a patriotic pinwheel is 100% my household. I instantly picture my daughter in that dress.
- **Seasonal timing** — early May with a Memorial Day patriotic look is exactly right. They're not late, they're not early, they're on the money.
- **The Gap/Banana Republic/Athleta brand bar at top** — as a family shopper I sometimes want to hop over to Gap for adult basics, and having that one-tap switch is quietly useful.

## What's weak

- **No hero CTA button** — there is literally no "Shop Now," "Shop Girls," or "Shop the Sale" button anywhere on the hero. A full-screen image of a kid with zero actionable copy is a wasted scroll stop.
- **The credit card push leads the page** — before I see a single product or category, I'm being asked to open an Encore Credit Card. I'm here to buy my kids uniforms, not apply for financing.
- **Two competing promo strips create noise** — the credit card banner and the free-shipping banner are visually similar in height and weight, so my eye bounces between them without a clear winner. One message at a time, please.
- **No category shortcuts above fold** — a hamburger menu is the only navigation visible. On mobile, that means at least two taps just to get to "Kids." For a back-to-school loyalist like me, a "Girls," "Boys," and "Baby" shortcut row would do a lot of work here.
- **No hero copy at all** — the image tells me it's patriotic and summery, but I don't know if I'm looking at a dress campaign, a Flag Day sale, or a generic lifestyle shot. Give me a headline.

## Recommendations

- **Add a hero headline and a real shop CTA** — even just "Flag Day Faves — Shop Girls" as an overlay would give me somewhere to tap. The hero is dead real estate right now.
- **Move the credit card banner below the fold or reduce it to a slim dismissible strip** — leading with a credit card application before a first-time or casual shopper sees a single product is an aggressive ask that slows down shopping intent.
- **Add a quick-tap category row below the hero** — a single row of four icon+label tiles (Girls, Boys, Baby, Toddler) would cut two navigation taps to zero for a mom who knows exactly where she's going.
- **Consolidate the two promo strips into one** — combine "50% off with Encore" and "Free shipping for Encore Members" into a single bar. It reads cleaner and reinforces that being a member = better deal, without creating visual competition.
