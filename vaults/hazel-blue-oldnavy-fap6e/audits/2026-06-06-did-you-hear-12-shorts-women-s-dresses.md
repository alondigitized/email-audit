# 📢 Did you hear? $12 shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a same-day companion send to the morning's "Warm-weather deals: $12 shorts & women's dresses" — Old Navy is explicitly re-mailing (or segmenting non-openers) with a conversational hook subject, "📢 Did you hear?", that tries to disguise what is effectively a duplicate. The render is clean: orange hero, bold "2 DAYS ONLY!" framing, $9 and $12 price anchors in the top fold, then a stack of colorful summer lifestyle product tiles. Visually it holds up. The problem is entirely upstream — you cannot rescue inbox fatigue with a winking subject line when you've already mailed eight times in six days, twice today.
- The offer itself is fine — concrete price points, summer-relevant product, credible urgency. But stacking a second "$12 shorts & women's dresses" email on the same calendar day with a near-identical subject to what already went this morning treats subscribers like they have no memory. "Did you hear?" implies they haven't; the real question is whether they're already exhausted.
- Bottom line: technically competent send, strategically tone-deaf cadence. The "re-mail to non-openers" mechanic can work but it needs a meaningfully different angle, not a rewrapped subject and the same creative.

## What's working

- **Hero price anchors land immediately** — "$9" and "$12" are large, high-contrast, and visible in the first screen without scrolling.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency** is clearly communicated and ties the offer to a real deadline.
- **Multi-category coverage is coherent** — shorts, dresses, activewear, new arrivals, and clearance are all represented with distinct visual blocks rather than a chaotic dump.
- **Seasonal palette and model photography** are on-brand and summer-appropriate; no render artifacts visible.
- **Activewear section reinforces recent campaign momentum** — ties back to the 50%-off activewear sends from June 4–5 without feeling redundant within this single email.

## What's weak

- **Same-day duplicate of today's morning send** — the morning email already delivered this exact offer with this exact headline; re-mailing within hours compresses any perceived urgency and reads as desperate.
- **Subject "📢 Did you hear?"** is conversational cover for a repurposed send — subscribers who caught the morning email will recognize this immediately and feel managed, not delighted.
- **Women's-first framing is exclusive by design** — "women's dresses" in the subject narrows the perceived audience; there's no signal for men's shorts buyers despite $9 men's shorts appearing in the body.
- **Activewear block feels like a carryover reflex** — after two full activewear-headlining emails (June 4 and 5), including it here as a secondary module dilutes its signal value further.
- **No clearance specificity** — the bottom "Go, Go, Get Clearance" section offers no price anchor or category hook, making it the weakest block in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't same-day re-mail with a near-identical creative.** If you must re-mail non-openers, wait until tomorrow morning and give them a genuinely new hook — a fresh product, a different price tier, or a countdown ("Last chance: $12 shorts end tonight"). Today's second send looks like a mistake, not a strategy.
- 2. **Split the audience by gender on the subject line.** "Did you hear? $9 men's shorts" versus "$12 women's dresses" — same offer, segmented send. Eliminates the half-your-list problem of leading with "women's dresses."
- 3. **Give the clearance block a number.** "From $4" or "Up to 70% off" turns a dead-weight footer into a click driver.
- 4. **Retire the activewear module for at least 48 hours.** It has appeared in five of the last six emails. Frequency has destroyed its urgency signal.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click-redirect URL**
- `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/7da1d8ac-7927-4454-a9b9-f1a1bab68002?mi_mid=...&cp_tp=v3.eJy...`

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
- Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If missing at SMTP level, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024) and RFC 2369. Major ISPs use this header for the "Unsubscribe" button in the inbox UI.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
- Required for one-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058. Without it, Gmail and Yahoo cannot honor automated unsubscribe requests, increasing spam complaint exposure.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
- CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not found in the truncated source — may be present in the untruncated footer, but QA did not detect it. Needs manual verification of the full footer.

- SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown. If authentication is actually failing, deliverability to Gmail/Outlook/Yahoo inboxes is at risk, which would undercut all landing page traffic expectations regardless of link health.
