# Cross it off the list: $8 kids uniform pants + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy continues its July BTS sequencing — $6 uniform shorts (July 6), now $8 uniform pants — and the subject line is the strongest the brand has written in this streak. "Cross it off the list" borrows the mental model of a school-prep checklist, which is exactly how parents shop in July. The dual offer ($8 pants + extra 30% off clearance in-store) is ambitious and the hero delivers the price cleanly.
- The critical structural miss is the "How To Go Low" denim editorial that appears mid-email between the clearance CTA and the BTS price grid. "The Low Blue Bando" ($40) and "The Low High Loose" ($36) are fashionable denim — not school uniforms — and they sever the narrative thread just as the parent audience is warming up. It reads like a content module that was left in from the wrong template. On top of that, "in-store" buried in both the subject and the clearance section will burn anyone who clicks expecting an online promo.
- Net verdict: a strong concept with a damaging editorial detour and a channel-mismatch trap on the clearance offer. Fix those two and this is an 8+ send.

## What's working

- "Cross it off the list" is the best subject hook Old Navy has used in this BTS run — it mirrors how parents actually shop in July.
- The "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge + "$8" price anchor is visible without scrolling and creates immediate urgency.
- The **1 YEAR GUARANTEE ON ALL KIDS CLOTHING** trust seal is a smart placement directly below the hero — a real parent concern addressed before they have to click.
- The back-to-school price grid ($5 / $8 / $12) at the bottom lands cleanly and provides a clear value ladder.
- Sequential BTS narrative (July 6: $6 shorts → July 9: $8 pants) starts to feel like a coherent back-to-school campaign rather than random daily blasts.

## What's weak

- The "How To Go Low" denim editorial (Blue Bando, High Loose) is completely off-topic — it stalls the BTS narrative mid-scroll and will lose parents who aren't here for fashion denim.
- "In-store" on the 30% off clearance is a click-to-disappointment trap; most email-driven shoppers expect an online redemption path.
- The "SHOP NOW" button on the clearance section is generic — nothing in its copy signals what category or destination the click lands on.
- Nine consecutive sends in ten days; even a fresh subject line can't fully offset list fatigue at this cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or relocate the "How To Go Low" editorial module** — it belongs in a style newsletter, not a BTS uniform send. Replace with a "Complete the uniform look" module (shirts, shoes, backpacks) to extend cart value.
- 2. **Resolve the in-store friction** — if the 30% clearance is genuinely online too, say "online & in-store." If it's in-store only, pull it out of the subject line or add an honest callout so clickers aren't burned.
- 3. **Sharpen the clearance CTA** — "SHOP KIDS CLEARANCE" over "SHOP NOW" tells the clicker what they're getting before the click.
- 4. **Cadence check** — Old Navy has sent 9 times in 10 days. Consider pausing non-heroes on the list segments that opened the last 3+ sends; reward engagement instead of fatiguing it.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel URL**
The Contentsquare/Movable Ink pixel embedded in the hidden preheader div returns an unknown URL type error:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c418ffa6-b050-4cad-9837-49f9e1a64bd7?...
Error: unknown url type: '/g/c418ffa6-b050-4cad-9837-49f9e1a64bd7?txn=ca098e80-7b9a-11f1-85ae-9295eb1db778'
```
This suggests the pixel vendor's redirect endpoint is resolving to a malformed internal path (`/g/...`) rather than a valid destination. Open tracking or engagement data from this pixel may be lost for this send.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links skipped**
All body links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed, so broken destinations cannot be confirmed or ruled out. Standard pre-send link validation against the click-redirect endpoint is recommended.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c418ffa6-..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt=""` attribute present. While a 1×1 pixel has no meaningful content, the absence of an explicit empty `alt` can trigger accessibility linters and screen reader verbosity in some clients. The open-pixel `<img>` in the same div does carry `alt=""` — this one does not.

**No other rendering issues found.** The MSO conditional blocks (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`), VML namespaces, `.ExternalClass` resets, and the single `@media (max-width: 639px)` breakpoint are all correctly structured. The `td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }` Outlook fix is present.

**Minor — duplicate CSS selector targets:**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each declared twice in the responsive block with different values (10px overrides 5px silently). Not a rendering break but creates maintainability risk.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] CAN-SPAM physical address not detected in source**
QA did not find a physical mailing address in the parsed content. If it exists below the truncation point, no action needed — but it must be present in the final rendered footer.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent (or not captured)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the native unsubscribe UI. Its absence (or relay-level suppression) degrades inbox placement and user trust signals. Verify the header is present at the MTA level:
```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>, <https://...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is also absent — required for high-volume senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm authentication chain for `email.oldnavy.com`. This should be verified at the MTA or in a seed-list inbox test.
