# $6 tees and $5 tanks are yours for the taking
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy fires another price-point hero — this time $6 tees and $5 tanks with a "2 DAYS ONLY" badge — and the formula is exactly right at the top. The hero is legible, urgent, and priced aggressively enough to stop a scroll. If they'd stopped there, this would be a tight send.
- They didn't stop. Below the hero sits a spend-threshold offer, the Disney x Old Navy module (carried from the May 13 launch), outdoor lifestyle imagery, a 50% Off block, "Dream Dresses Under $37," and then a triple-stacked Clearance footer. That's at minimum six distinct value propositions fighting for attention in a single email. The subject promises tees and tanks; the body delivers a department store.
- The send is competent but it's the fifth email in six days from Old Navy, and this one looks nearly identical in structure to the May 15 "$12 shorts" and May 14 "Crinkle Gauze" sends. The hero price-point is strong enough to earn opens, but the module bloat and cadence fatigue cap how far it can go.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY" + dollar price is a clean urgency combo** — the hero communicates both the scarcity and the value in a single above-the-fold read.
- **Subject line is doing real work** — "$6 tees and $5 tanks" at 46 characters is specific, mobile-friendly, and gives the recipient a clear reason to open.
- **Disney cross-sell is timely** — carrying the May 13 collection launch into a promo email is good sequencing; it gives the collab a second life without a separate send.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, layout holds across the scroll.

## What's weak

- **Module count buries the lead** — at six-plus distinct sections, the eye has nowhere to land after the hero; each module dilutes the one above it.
- **"Dream Dresses Under $37" is a weaker echo** of May 14's "Under $25" — the price point moved up $12 in two days, which a returning subscriber will notice.
- **No urgency in the subject line itself** — "2 DAYS ONLY" lives only in the hero image; someone scanning their inbox on a Friday sees the price but not the deadline.
- **Cadence is punishing** — five emails in six days from Old Navy. Even a loyal subscriber starts filtering after three. This one will catch inbox fatigue that a Monday drop wouldn't.
- **Loyalty/member framing absent** — no NavyCard or rewards acknowledgment; misses a chance to reward loyal buyers who are most likely to redeem at this price.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the deadline to the subject** — "2 days only" or "ends Sunday" in the subject line would close the loop; right now the urgency is buried below the fold for anyone who doesn't open.
- 2. **Cut the module count to three** — Hero (tees/tanks) → one secondary offer (Disney or spend-threshold, not both) → Clearance. The rest belongs in a separate send or a landing page.
- 3. **Sharpen the Dress module or drop it** — "Under $37" after "Under $25" two days ago reads as a step backwards. Either retarget that module to a different segment or tighten the price.
- 4. **Add a loyalty hook** — even a one-liner ("NavyCard members get early access") converts high-LTV subscribers at a better rate.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $6 tee is waiting (ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up on summer basics before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$6 tees, $5 tanks — plus the Disney collab you've been eyeing`
