# Fill that cart with $15 & under styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy is back — this time swapping the multi-day tee price grid blitz for a broader "$15 & under" umbrella anchored to a "2 DAYS ONLY!" clock. The hero delivers a clean price anchor ($15 & under on Everyday Favorites) with a supporting "$3.99 & under" accessories tier below it, which actually adds useful hierarchy. That's a step up from the cloned tee sends of July 3–5. The creative does spread itself thin quickly though: after the hero pair, the email dumps into a dense mosaic of product modules — activewear, kids, lounge — with tiny product imagery and small CTA labels that blur together on mobile.
- The "$3.99 & under" accessories row is genuinely the sharpest value prop here, and it's buried in the middle. The brand history matters: subscribers just endured four consecutive tee-price emails with near-identical creative; this format shift is welcome, but Old Navy's credibility on "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency is low after the rolling "3 DAYS ONLY!" badges on July 3–5. The recipient will recognize the pattern.
- Net read: a competent clearance push with real breadth, but urgency fatigue and a too-dense lower half undercut the value. Mid-tier send.

## What's working

- **Dual price tiers create a clear hierarchy** — $15 & under hero anchored at top, then $3.99 & under accessories as a secondary drawer; the eye has a path.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" badge is visible and specific** — time-bounded urgency is at least stated; better than vague "limited time."
- **Accessories category is a useful pivot** — after five consecutive apparel/tee-led emails, dropping a $3.99 accessories tier adds category variety.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds on mobile view.
- **Product breadth** — activewear, kids, women's lounge, and accessories all represented, giving different sub-segments something to click.

## What's weak

- **Urgency credibility is shot** — subscribers received "3 DAYS ONLY!" on July 3, then again on July 4, July 5, and July 5 again; "2 DAYS ONLY!" here reads as another rolling deadline, not a real one.
- **Lower half is a grid dump** — after the hero modules, the email becomes a dense small-image mosaic with tiny labels; nothing pops as a hero product.
- **No personalization or loyalty callout visible** — unlike some prior sends with VIP/member framing, there's no member-pricing or card-holder benefit signal here.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "Shop Now" variants throughout; no specific verb-noun pairings (e.g., "Shop $15 Favorites") to reinforce the offer.
- **Multiple competing sections dilute focus** — activewear, kids, lounge, and accessories all fighting for equal visual weight means none wins.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lock the urgency to one deadline and mean it.** After the July 3–5 rolling-badge sequence, the "2 DAYS ONLY!" claim lands hollow. Either enforce the clock (show it expire) or drop the badge and lean into the dollar value — the $15 & under hook is strong enough to stand alone.
- 2. **Pick one hero product and give it a close-up.** The accessories $3.99 tier is the sharpest value hook but lives below the fold in a small image; feature one specific item (earrings, belt, bag) at hero scale to prove the claim.
- 3. **Give every CTA a verb + noun tied to the offer.** Replace "Shop Now" → "Shop $15 Tees," "Shop $3.99 Accessories," etc. A five-word CTA beats a two-word one here because the offer is price-specific.
- 4. **Collapse the lower modules.** Cut from four lower category blocks to two — pick the highest-converting sub-segments and give them room to breathe. The current grid reads as liquidation bulk, not curated sale.
- 5. **Add a loyalty / Old Navy Card teaser.** Even one line — "Card members: earn 5x today" — converts the fence-sitters who are loyalty-motivated.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Everything under $15 (yes, even that)`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tees, tanks, shorts, and accessories — all $15 & under for 48 hours.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken Criteo/CDP pixel URL**
The tracking pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/944b3892...` returns an unresolvable relative path error:
```
unknown url type: '/g/944b3892-1d68-44a2-ae55-e7858934506a?txn=50dd3080-7a0e-11f1-a4c3-d2520cded5b8'
```
This indicates the redirect target returned a relative URL rather than an absolute one. The pixel fires on open, so this silently breaks open-tracking or CDP event capture for every recipient.

**WARN — 23 click-redirect links skipped**
All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for these, so destination health is unverified. Any 404 or redirect loop on the click domain would be invisible to this audit.

The 1×1 CDP pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. Screen readers may announce the raw URL. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not absent).

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
RFC 2369 header not present. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay not forwarding it, but the risk is real: if the header is genuinely missing in delivery, Gmail and Apple Mail one-click unsubscribe buttons will not render.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe not confirmed. Gmail requires this for bulk senders (>5k/day) to suppress spam classification.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(3) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The truncated HTML does not include one in the visible source. Verify it appears in the footer of the full HTML — if absent, this is a legal violation.

**No issues found** with `title` tag or `charset` for CAN-SPAM subject-line accuracy requirements.
