# SCORE—$8 tees
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy running the exact same "$8 Tees" play it ran on 2026-08-01 — the hero art direction (pink-striped tee, pink bike shorts, "2 DAYS ONLY! Tees $6 Rompers $8") is close enough to the prior two sends that a subscriber who opened yesterday's "$8 TEES" or "Ooooh! 👀$8 tees" email will recognize this as a re-skin, not a new offer. The subject swaps in "SCORE—$8 tees" but the underlying creative, pricing, and CTA are unchanged from 24 hours ago.
- Below the fold this send earns its keep: the low-rise jeans block (Loose, Baggy Wide Leg) and the kids "Must-Haves" section (Leggings $5, Pants $6, Hoodies $7, Sweatshirts $8 plus a "1 Year Guarantee" callout) are genuinely well-organized, with clean price ladders and distinct hero shots per style. The kids sports section ("Build Their Winning Outfit," "Sporty Top Scorers," "Soft, Stretchy All-Stars") is a strong seasonal beat for back-to-school timing.
- Verdict: strong execution, weak differentiation from yesterday's send. This is a solid single email but a lazy third-in-a-row iteration on the same $8 tee hook — the brand is burning a proven creative rather than earning a fresh open.

## What's working

- Clean, unambiguous price ladder in the hero ("Tees $6, Rompers $8") that matches the subject line exactly.
- Multiple distinct, well-labeled CTA buttons throughout (Shop Tees, Shop Rompers, Shop Low Rise Jeans, Shop Kids Activewear).
- Kids section adds real specificity — per-category pricing grid (Leggings/Pants/Hoodies/Sweatshirts) plus a "1 Year Guarantee on All Kids Clothing" trust signal.
- No render bugs — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage anywhere in the scroll.
- Visual hierarchy is easy to follow: banner → hero offer → jeans → kids → clearance, each section color-blocked distinctly.

## What's weak

- Hero creative and offer are near-identical to the 2026-08-01 "$8 TEES" and "$8 tees + EXTRA 30%" sends — this reads as a repeat, not a new campaign beat.
- Multiple offers carry asterisks (30% Off Purchase*, Jeans From $22*) with no visible terms — buried fine print signal.
- The "Become an Elevated Member" banner text appears partially cut off/garbled ("Elevated Member for Exit" reads oddly) — worth a QA pass on that line.
- Email is very long (jeans → kids → clearance → footer) with no anchor nav, so lower sections (kids, clearance) will get little attention from anyone who doesn't scroll far.
- "SCORE" as an opener is generic urgency language the brand has leaned on before — it doesn't add new information the subject didn't already have with "$8 tees" alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Differentiate this send from yesterday's near-identical "$8 tees" email — either swap the hero art/model or lead with a section that wasn't already the hero 24 hours ago (e.g., promote low-rise jeans or kids-back-to-school as the lead story, tees as secondary).
- 2. Fix the "Become an Elevated Member" banner copy — it reads as truncated/broken in the render.
- 3. Add a visible deadline/time stamp near the $6/$8 tee offer in the body (not just "2 DAYS ONLY!") so urgency feels concrete rather than boilerplate.
- 4. Move a kids back-to-school teaser higher in the email — it's the freshest, least-repeated content here and is currently buried past three other sections.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tees just dropped to $6`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days only: $6 tees, $8 rompers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus low-rise jeans from $22 and kids styles from $5`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tees, jeans, kids gear — all on sale for 48 hours`
