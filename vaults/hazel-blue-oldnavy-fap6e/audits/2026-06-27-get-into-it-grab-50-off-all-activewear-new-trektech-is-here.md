# Get into it! Grab 50% off ALL activewear + new TrekTech is here
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's second email on June 27 — the brand history already logged "New moves, BIG deals! Snag 50% off ALL activewear" from this morning. This afternoon send tries to differentiate with the TrekTech product launch bolted onto the same 50%-off frame. The "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency badge is front-and-center at the top, which is the right call, and TrekTech gets its own hero module with dedicated imagery. But the email is doing two jobs simultaneously — move sale merchandise AND launch a new product line — and that tension creates a split-attention experience that dilutes both.
- The visual flow is busier than it should be. The eye bounces between the sale countdown, the TrekTech editorial block, a product grid, and what appears to be a secondary clearance offer toward the bottom (40%–70% off). That's too many messages for a single send. The TrekTech launch deserved its own clean email; instead it's sandwiched between promotional layers.
- The honest read: this is a better email than the morning's send because TrekTech is a genuine news hook, but shipping it as a second activewear email on the same day kneecaps the open rate before anyone sees the creative.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" is the very first thing you see** — the urgency frame is at the top and clear, which gives the email a reason to exist beyond the ongoing 50%-off marathon.
- **TrekTech gets a dedicated hero module** with its own model, imagery, and apparent product story — real new-product energy that breaks the discount-only pattern.
- **50% off activewear is a category-specific claim**, which is meaningfully more credible than the "50% off everything\*" messaging that dominated the prior six sends.
- **The email is clean** — no visible render bugs, broken images, or layout collapse at this render width.

## What's weak

- **Two competing hero messages** (sale + new product launch) split the email's focal point; a subscriber can't tell what Old Navy actually wants them to do.
- **Subject line is 63 characters** — too long for mobile preview, and the two-part structure ("50% off activewear + new TrekTech is here") reads like two emails merged at the seam.
- **This is the second activewear email of the day** — a subscriber who opened the morning send is already experiencing diminishing returns; the cadence is aggressive even by Old Navy's June standards.
- **No visible social proof or user reviews** tied to TrekTech, which would have given the new-product launch real credibility.
- **A secondary deep-discount offer appears lower in the email** (appears to be 40–70% off), fragmenting attention further at a point where the sale + new product have already taxed the reader.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the email.** TrekTech deserves a standalone launch email — a single hero, a clear "what is it / why do you want it" moment, one CTA. The 50%-off activewear message can carry its own vehicle the next morning. Combining them weakens both.
- 2. **Tighten the subject under 50 characters.** Lean into whichever story is primary. If it's the launch: lead with TrekTech. If it's the sale: drop "Get into it!" and the product name and put the deadline front-right.
- **Subject Alt A:** `TrekTech just dropped — 50% off to try it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days only: 50% off all activewear`
- 3. **Move the deadline into the subject.** "2 DAYS ONLY!" is the strongest urgency signal in the email but it lives in the body. Pulling it to the subject immediately earns an extra open-likelihood point.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New TrekTech is here — built for serious movers`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday. Your activewear upgrade is 50% off.`
- 4. **Cut the secondary clearance offer from this send.** The 40–70% off section at the bottom dilutes the activewear + TrekTech story. Save it for a dedicated clearance send; this one already has enough competing messages.
- 5. **Add one TrekTech product proof point** — a single line ("moisture-wicking, 4-way stretch, trail-ready") gives the new product a reason-to-believe without requiring a full-page feature.

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "50% off ALL activewear + TrekTech" (2026-06-30)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email passes basic rendering hygiene but has one confirmed broken redirect in a tracking pixel, a plain-text body that is nearly 88% raw URLs (a deliverability hazard), and three compliance headers that are absent or unverifiable at the relay. CAN-SPAM physical address could not be confirmed in the truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect on Conversant/CP tracking pixel**
The QA link probe followed `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/419057ec-a026-401e-b6e8-18394537e9d1?mi_mid=...` and received a relative redirect target of `/g/419057ec-a026-401e-b6e8-18394537e9d1?txn=e1d57880-74f1-11f1-89c3-3a70d2f5d7cf`, which is an invalid URL type (no host). The redirect chain on the Conversant `cp.oldnavy.com` open pixel is broken — the server returns a relative path that cannot be resolved from the redirect context. This is a server-side misconfiguration on the Conversant platform, not in the email HTML itself, but it will silently fail open-event attribution for this send.

**[INFO] 23 click-tracking links unprobed**
All links routed through `click.email.oldnavy.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. This is expected behavior — destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
`<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>` has no `lang="en"` attribute. Screen readers and some spam filters use this for language detection.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class targeting with conflicting values**
In the mobile `@media` block, `.MspaceTop` is targeted twice with different padding values:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }
```
The second declaration wins for all `.MspaceTop` elements regardless of intent. Same pattern for `.MspaceBot`. Any element using `.MspaceTop` alone will get `10px` top padding on mobile, not `5px`.

**[WARN] Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a blunt instrument that can collapse spacing in any `<td>` without an explicit inline `line-height` override, causing content to stack unexpectedly in Outlook desktop.

**[WARN] Invalid Schema.org markup placement**
```html
<div itemscope itemtype="http://schema.org/Organization">
  <meta content="Old Navy" itemprop="name">
  <meta content="https://image.email.oldnavy.com/..." itemprop="logo">
</div>
```
`<meta>` as a child of `<div>` in `<body>` is invalid in both HTML5 and XHTML. Gmail strips or ignores this. Use `<span>` or `<link>` with `itemprop` in body context, or move to `<head>`.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing alt attribute**
The Conversant open pixel (`src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/419057ec...`) has no `alt=""` attribute in the rendered HTML, per QA. The first pixel (`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...`) correctly carries `alt=""`. The second pixel should have `alt=""` added to suppress screen-reader noise and avoid strict-mode HTML validation failures.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent or not relayed**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were captured by the AgentMail relay. These are required by Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender policy for lists exceeding 5,000 daily sends. If Old Navy's ESP injects these at the MTA layer and AgentMail's relay strips or doesn't forward them, the absence here may be a relay artifact rather than a send-side failure — but it cannot be confirmed. The email operator should verify header presence via a direct SMTP capture or MX Toolbox header check on a live sample.

**[WARN] CAN-SPAM physical address not confirmed**
The HTML source is truncated before the footer. Physical mailing address presence cannot be confirmed. Given Old Navy's scale this is almost certainly in the footer, but the truncation prevents verification. Flag for confirmation against the full source.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because `Authentication-Results` was not captured through the AgentMail relay. Sender domain is `email.oldnavy.com` (From header) / `click.email.oldnavy.com` (tracking links). DKIM alignment should be verified against `oldnavy.com` or `email.oldnavy.com` directly.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[UNVERIFIABLE] UTM parameter coverage on 23 click links**
All destination URLs are behind `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects that the probe could not resolve. UTM parameter presence (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) and landing page alignment with the "50% off activewear / TrekTech" offer cannot be confirmed. Manual spot-check of 2–3 links recommended.

**[INFO] Dual tracking pixel setup**
The email fires both a `click.email.oldnavy.com` open pixel and a `cp.oldnavy.com` (Conversant) open pixel. The Conversant pixel's redirect is broken (see §2), so open attribution in Conversant will be undercounted for this send.
