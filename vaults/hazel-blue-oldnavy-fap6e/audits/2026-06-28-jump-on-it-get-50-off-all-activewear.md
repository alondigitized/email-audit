# 🦘 JUMP ON IT! Get 50% off ALL activewear
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's fourth activewear email in under 48 hours — following the June 27 morning send ("New moves, BIG deals!"), the June 27 afternoon send ("Get into it! Grab 50% off ALL activewear + new TrekTech"), and June 28's earlier "It's go-time" send. The kangaroo emoji and "JUMP ON IT!" are meant to convey energy but read as desperation from a brand that has been screaming the same 50%-off-activewear message on loop. The creative is familiar: same "2 DAYS ONLY" countdown badge, same 50% hero, same summer palette.
- The one genuine differentiator is the "HOT NEW GEAR!" module showcasing brightly colored bags and activewear — that product story is new and distinct from the general-discount hammering. But it's buried beneath the hero, not leading the email. The "SORRY PLUS SIZES EXCLUDED" callout is visible mid-email and handles a real limitation in the clumsiest possible way — making a design element of the exclusion instead of quietly footnoting it.
- Bottom line: a technically competent but creatively exhausted send. The offer is real, the brand is trusted, but the inbox signal is worn thin. Anyone who bought activewear yesterday — or unsubscribed from the last three sends — won't be moved by this one.

## What's working

- **Concrete offer is immediately visible** — "50% off ALL activewear" is in the hero with no ambiguity.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR!" product module** adds a product-discovery angle the previous sends lacked; colorful, high-energy creative that suits the category.
- **2 DAYS ONLY countdown badge** gives the offer a real deadline, credible since it's been consistent across multiple sends.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, layout holds across the visible viewport, no obvious display glitches.

## What's weak

- **Fourth activewear email in 48 hours** — cadence is aggressive to the point of training opens away, not toward.
- **"SORRY PLUS SIZES EXCLUDED" visible mid-email** — calling out an exclusion as a design element with an apology attached is awkward and will alienate a meaningful customer segment.
- **Subject line trips the spam signals** — "🦘 JUMP ON IT!" combines emoji, ALL CAPS, and exclamation in a way that's borderline promotional-tab fodder.
- **No social proof or reviews visible** — purely discount-led with nothing to anchor trust or desire beyond the price cut.
- **TrekTech new gear is the freshest story here but it's not in the subject line**, which wastes the one thing that genuinely differentiates this from yesterday's sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the new product story, not the repeat discount.** "HOT NEW GEAR!" is the only thing in this email subscribers haven't seen three times. Make TrekTech the subject hook; let the 50% deal be the supporting close.
- 2. **Kill the "SORRY PLUS SIZES EXCLUDED" design callout.** Move it to fine print below the CTA. The apology-as-design-element pleases no one and alienates plus shoppers who see it first.
- 3. **Rest the 50%-off-activewear cadence for 24 hours.** One skip day would make the final-day "LAST CHANCE" email land harder than another identical send does.
- 4. **Add one specific product CTA** (e.g., a single TrekTech item with a name and color) rather than linking only to the category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New TrekTech gear is here — plus 50% off activewear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: 50% off activewear + new summer drops`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hot new bags, tees, and shorts — all 50% off for 2 more days`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `TrekTech just launched. So did the 50% sale. Both end soon.`

**CSS duplicate selector warning (non-breaking)**

**Outlook MSO override**

```css

  td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```

Setting `line-height: 0px` globally on `td` for Outlook 2013+ is an aggressive hack. It suppresses phantom spacing but will collapse any `td` that relies on inherited line-height for vertical rhythm rather than explicit `height` or padding. No breakage confirmed from source alone, but flagged as a fragile pattern.
