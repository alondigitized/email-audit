# Warm-weather deals: $12 shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy lands a seventh consecutive promotional email in six days, this time trading the recent activewear-heavy creative for a warm-weather price anchor — "$12 shorts & women's dresses" in the subject. That price point is the single best decision in this send: it's concrete, low, and seasonally obvious for early June. The email body delivers on the promise with a multi-section layout: a 2-Days-Only warm-weather hero at the top, a shorts price ladder ($6/$8/$12), a continuation of the activewear sale (redundant with June 4 and June 5), a Summer Steals 50-60%-off strip, and a Kids & Baby clearance footer.
- The structure is familiar Old Navy kitchen-sink — five distinct offers competing for attention — and the activewear block in the middle is pure dead weight for a subscriber who just received two activewear-specific sends this week. The creative is clean and renders without obvious bugs, but the cadence tax is real: at this point a regular subscriber has been pitched "50% off" something every single day since June 1st.
- The warm-weather framing with specific dollar prices is a creative step forward from the percentage-stack formula, but the multi-offer dilution and cadence fatigue cap the performance ceiling here.

## What's working

- **$12 price anchor in the subject** — concrete dollar figure beats percentage-off for low-AOV items; pulls price-sensitive browsers into the open.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency badge** lands above the fold in the hero, reinforcing the time-bound nature before the first scroll.
- **Price ladder visual ($6 / $8 / $12)** — the tiered shorts pricing communicates value range at a glance; easy to parse on mobile.
- **Seasonal timing** — warm-weather apparel in early June is perfectly timed; no disconnect between subject and calendar.
- **Clean render** — no visible layout breaks, broken images, or overlapping text across the full email.

## What's weak

- **Activewear section mid-email is redundant dead weight** — two dedicated activewear emails sent June 4 and June 5; a subscriber is already fatigued on that category and won't re-engage here.
- **Multiple competing offers dilute the price anchor** — the $12 shorts hook set up in the subject is undercut before the fold by "ALL ACTIVEWEAR ON SALE," Summer Steals, AND kids clearance.
- **No single dominant CTA** — the layout encourages scrolling past multiple sections rather than driving to one action; every section has its own small button.
- **No loyalty/member pricing visible** — Old Navy's NavyCard/Rewards program is absent, missing a retention lever for loyalists.
- **Cadence is at breaking point** — this is send #7 in 6 days; "warm-weather deals" is not meaningfully differentiated from the string of promos that preceded it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the activewear block entirely this send.** It was the hero of the last two emails. Its presence here dilutes the shorts/dresses anchor and signals that Old Navy is just recycling the same inventory push.
- 2. **Make the hero a single-offer email: $12 shorts + dresses only.** Let the price ladder breathe. One scroll, one CTA, one category. The kitchen-sink structure works against the strong subject line.
- 3. **Promote the price ladder ($6/$8/$12) into the hero copy** — "From $6" above the fold is more compelling than burying it one section down.
- 4. **Add a deadline timestamp** ("ends Sunday midnight") rather than just "2 DAYS ONLY!" — specific deadlines convert better than vague day counts.
- 5. **Surface Rewards/NavyCard pricing** if applicable — a member-exclusive view reinforces retention at the end of a heavy promo run.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — open/engagement pixel**
The Conversant pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8fb52c33-1cc5-4976-a9bd-867271755870?...` resolves to a relative URL:

```
unknown url type: '/g/8fb52c33-1cc5-4976-a9bd-867271755870?txn=fe52ef00-6197-11f1-9372-4e8366552ae6'
```
A relative path returned from a cross-domain redirect is not a valid final destination; the request will fail in any HTTP client that enforces absolute URLs. This breaks engagement tracking for this send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate mobile CSS selectors (silent specificity bug)**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different values:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 5px; }
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* overwrites */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 5px; }
...
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; } /* overwrites */
```
Any element using `.MspaceTop` on mobile gets `10px` regardless of intent. The comment in source acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */` — but both declarations remain. Elements that should render with 5px top/bottom padding on mobile will silently render at 10px.

**Outlook global line-height zero**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook normalization pattern but is aggressive — any `<td>` without an explicit override will collapse line-height to zero in Outlook 2013+.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel**
```
Image missing alt text: src https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8fb52c33-...
```
The pixel has `alt=""` (empty string) rather than a null/decorative `role="presentation"`. Minor for a 1×1 pixel, but will flag in accessibility audits.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent from relay capture**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the native unsubscribe button. Its absence — whether missing at send or stripped by the relay — means one-click unsubscribe at the MUA level is non-functional.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
No `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` header present. Google's February 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this for senders over 5k/day. Old Navy almost certainly qualifies. If the header is present on the wire but the relay is dropping it, that needs to be confirmed with a raw header capture.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The QA scan found none in the captured source. Possible causes: address is in an image (non-compliant), in a footer section not captured in the truncated HTML, or genuinely missing. Needs full-source verification.

**Authentication headers unknown**
`Authentication-Results` not captured by relay — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status for `email.oldnavy.com` is unverifiable from this audit. Not a direct HTML defect, but a relay configuration gap that removes deliverability signal.
