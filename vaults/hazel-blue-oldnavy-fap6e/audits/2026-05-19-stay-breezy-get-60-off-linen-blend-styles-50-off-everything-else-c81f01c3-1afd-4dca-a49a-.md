# Stay breezy 😎 Get 60% off Linen-Blend styles + 50% off everything* else
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's ninth email in five days, and the subject finally delivers a genuine break from the "$12 + 50% off everything" loop that's defined the blitz: "60% off Linen-Blend styles" is category-specific, material-anchored, and seasonally perfect for late May. The hero executes it well — the TODAY ONLY badge reads immediately, the 60% callout is prominent, and the model in the blue linen wide-leg pants actually sells the product rather than just the discount. The render is clean top to bottom.
- The structural problem is the same one this brand repeats: one clear offer buried under two more. The linen deal competes with the perpetual "50% Off Everything*" section (subscribers have seen this block all week) and then a "SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" size-grid section that actually contains the most differentiated element in the email — a shopper-functional size/price breakdown — but arrives third when it deserves to come second. Three sections pulling three directions means no section wins.
- Against the prior blitz, this send beats last night's near-carbon-copy (scored 6/10 on 2026-05-19) and rivals the "Major news" send from 2026-05-18 (scored 8/10) purely on offer freshness. But the linen hook needed to arrive before Day 5 of hammering. At nine emails in five days, Old Navy is burning list equity faster than individual emails can recover it.

## What's working

- TODAY ONLY badge + 60% callout is the first thing the eye lands on — above-fold scannability is high.
- "60% off Linen-Blend" is specific, category-anchored, and genuinely different from the $6 tees / $12 shorts carousel that ran all week.
- Female model in the blue linen wide-leg outfit demos the product convincingly rather than just announcing a number.
- "SHOP CLEARANCE IN YOUR SIZE" size/price grid is the most functionally useful element in this email — surfaces availability by size, which reduces real shopper friction.
- Linen for late May is seasonally correct; the creative doesn't feel evergreen or recycled at the category level.

## What's weak

- Three concurrent promotional sections (60% linen, 50% everything, clearance by size) compete for attention and dilute the primary offer.
- The "50% Off Everything*" block is a direct carry-over from every prior send this week — subscribers have been conditioned to scroll past it.
- CTAs are small and dispersed; no single large button anchors the linen hero to a clear next action.
- Subject line at ~72 chars will truncate on most mobile clients — "50% off everything* else" likely never renders in the inbox view.
- Ninth email in five days; even a solid send fights cumulative unsubscribe pressure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the email to one offer**: The 60% linen deal is the hook — remove the "50% Off Everything" banner or demote it to a footer footnote. Stacking deals isn't additive; it's dilutive at this point in the blitz.
- 2. **Move clearance-by-size section to position two**: It's the most differentiated block and speaks to real shopper anxiety about fit/availability. It should follow the linen hero directly.
- 3. **Shorten the subject to under 45 chars**: The tail gets cut every time on mobile. Kill the "+ 50% off everything* else" — it belongs in the preheader, not the subject.
- 4. **Add one large anchored CTA under the linen hero**: "Shop Linen Styles" > scattered small "Shop Now" links across three sections.
- 5. **Rest the list for 48 hours**: No email tactic in this send recovers the cadence damage. The brand needs a gap more than it needs another variant.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Breezy linen, 60% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide-legs, sets, and more. Plus 50% off sitewide while it lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop by your size — clearance prices on summer's best looks.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken consent/preference link**

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8a556507-40c1-4973-97e0-338324a2f717
  ?mi_mid=019e4209-d080-7000-9fb0-11e007f0a19f&cp_tp=v3.eJx9...
```

QA reports: `unknown url type: '/g/8a556507-40c1-4973-97e0-338324a2f717?txn=40142500-53a4-11f1-bd2a-4e61e507eb6e'`. This indicates the Movable Ink redirect is resolving to a malformed relative path (`/g/...`) instead of an absolute URL. Likely a mis-configured redirect rule in the MI content block, or a stale `txn` token that no longer maps to a valid destination.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unverified**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8a556507-..." width="1" height="1">
```

No `alt=""` attribute present. Screen readers may announce the raw URL. Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not omitted).

**CSS duplicate selector conflict**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice with different `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` values (5px and 10px). The second declaration wins, silently discarding the first. Source comment acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`. Unclear if this is intentional or a template merge artifact — worth confirming against design spec.

**Outlook line-height override**

```css
/* Outlook 2013+ Height Fix */
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```

Setting `line-height: 0px` on all `<td>` elements inside the MSO conditional block is a broad reset that can collapse text rows in Outlook if any `<td>` contains inline text without an explicit override. Standard practice is to apply this only to spacer rows.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) detected in the truncated source. The personalized content appears to be rendered server-side via Movable Ink before send — consistent with the `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel pattern. No issues found beyond the broken redirect documented in §2.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Not present in the captured source. May exist below the HTML truncation point — must verify against full source.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**

Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe button. Its absence (even if present upstream) means recipients on those clients may not see the one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**

RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (>5k/day). If Old Navy sends at that volume, this is a deliverability risk, not just a courtesy gap.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected on destination URLs**

The 23 tracked links all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`, which presumably appends UTM params on redirect. However, since those redirects were not probed, it cannot be confirmed that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are being passed through to `oldnavy.com`. If the Movable Ink redirect chain that is already broken (§2) is part of the UTM passthrough path, attribution for that content block will be lost entirely.
