# Three deals, one cart: 60% off all shorts & swim*, $5 dresses and 50% off everything* else
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy goes three-for-one here: 60% off shorts & swim, $5 dresses, and 50% off everything else — all crammed into a single send. The subject line spells out all three deals explicitly, which is either a bold transparency play or a sign that the team couldn't pick a lead. In this case, it reads as the latter. The email is long, scroll-heavy, and structured as three separate mini-emails stacked vertically, each with its own hero, product grid, and CTA. That dilutes every individual offer.
- The brand history context makes this send feel predictable. After the June 15 "60% OFF Summer Steals" (9/10), the June 16 same-frame repeat with an EXTRA 20% stack (7/10), and the June 18 PowerChill push (8/10), this email is the fifth major promotional send in five days. Discount fatigue is real — and this is the first time they've tried to bundle three distinct deal frames into one email instead of leading with one clear story.
- The execution is competent but not inspired. The $5 dress section is the most arresting visual unit; it deserved to headline the whole email. Instead it's buried below a 50% off everything banner that competes with the 60% off hero for top billing.

## What's working

- **Subject-body alignment is honest** — the three deals promised in the subject are all visible and clearly signed in the scroll.
- **$5 dress section is a genuine visual standout** — the pink model image and bold price point jump out against the grid-heavy top sections.
- **Product grids are varied** — shorts, swim, dresses, activewear, and clearance all get real estate; something for every shopper.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text renders cleanly, no broken layout visible.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR" section** adds seasonal freshness to an otherwise pure-discount email.

## What's weak

- **Three competing heroes, no visual hierarchy** — the eye doesn't know where to go first; all three deal banners read at roughly equal weight.
- **Subject line is 89 characters** — it will truncate on nearly every mobile inbox, killing the "three deals" clarity entirely.
- **No urgency signal visible in any hero** — unlike the June 15 and June 18 sends, there's no "TODAY ONLY" or countdown equivalent visible in the render.
- **The email scrolls forever** — five distinct content sections plus clearance is a lot of friction before hitting the buy instinct.
- **Cadence is now working against opens** — this is the fifth+ promo from Old Navy in five days; the brand is running out of reason to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one deal to lead and subordinate the others.** The $5 dress is the sharpest anchor — lead with it, then fold the 60% off shorts/swim and 50% everything as secondary callouts in a three-column band below the hero, not three separate sections.
- 2. **Add urgency back.** The June 15 and June 18 sends both had "TODAY ONLY" banners that drove urgency. This send has none. Even "3 days only" moves the needle.
- 3. **Cut the email length by 40%.** Remove the HOT NEW GEAR section or push it to a separate send — it fights the discount story.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to fit 50 chars or under** and lead with the sharpest hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 dresses + 60% off swim & shorts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Three deals, one cart — from $5`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off everything else — today through the weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Swim, shorts, dresses — all on sale. Grab all three.`
- 5. **Restrain the cadence.** Sending a fifth distinct promo in five days is compressing the urgency of each individual offer. One rest day (or a non-discount content email) resets the perceived value of the next sale.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` attribute**
The Coherent Path pixel (`src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/64db9620-...`) has no `alt=""` attribute. While functionally inconsequential for a 1×1 pixel, it triggers accessibility linting tools and some strict ESPs flag it.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class selectors**
In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice (bound to both `space-top5`/`space-top10` and `space-bot5`/`space-bot10`). The second rule silently overrides the first — whichever `.MspaceTop` element appears will always get `padding-top: 10px`, never `5px`. Low severity but worth cleaning.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override is global**
The MSO conditional style sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a common legacy pattern but can clip single-line text cells if any `td` lacks an explicit `line-height` override.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header for the native unsubscribe button. Its absence increases the likelihood of spam complaints. Note: the QA system flags this may be a relay capture issue — confirm against raw SMTP headers before treating as a definitive send-side miss.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders ≥5k/day since Feb 2024) depends on this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. If the header is genuinely missing at send time, Old Navy is out of compliance with Gmail's bulk sender requirements.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. None was found in the truncated source. Confirm it exists in the full footer — if absent, this is a legal exposure.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not surfacing this header. Cannot confirm authentication posture from available data.
