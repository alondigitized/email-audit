# That's right! Get 50% off E-V-E-R-Y-T-H-I-N-G*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy running the identical "50% Off Everything" storewide play for at least the sixth time in under two weeks — the badge graphic and burgundy-toned family hero look like near-clones of the July 27–29 sends. What's different this time is the layout has actual structure: a clear "Our Best-of-Sale Picks" grid with concrete prices ($25 jeans, $13 hoodie, $15 fleece, $8 onesie), then a well-organized "Looking for a T-Shirt Refresh?" fit guide (Snug/EveryWear/Hue/Vintage), then a $3.99 clearance banner. This is more content-rich and better merchandised than the July 28 "$12 PJs" bait-and-switch disaster.
- The subject line "That's right! Get 50% off E-V-E-R-Y-T-H-I-N-G*" is honest to the render — the hero delivers exactly what it promises, no missing-product trap this time. But the cadence problem is severe: this is the same evergreen 50%-off-everything hook restated with a slightly cuter subject, and a subscriber who's opened three of these in nine days will recognize the wallpaper immediately.
- Solid execution on a stale campaign. The render itself has no bugs, clear hierarchy, and honest merchandising — the drag is entirely fatigue from repetition.

## What's working

- The 50% off badge and hero photo are the clear first visual anchor with a legible CTA ("SHOP FOR FREE" / shop button visible up top).
- "Our Best-of-Sale Picks" section ties concrete dollar prices ($25, $13, $15, $8) directly to product photography — no vague "shop now" filler.
- The T-shirt fit guide (Snug, EveryWear, Hue, Vintage) with a quote per style is a genuinely useful merchandising device rarely seen in this brand's recent sends.
- $3.99 clearance banner at the bottom adds a second, distinct offer tier rather than just repeating 50% off again.
- No render bugs — clean grid alignment, readable text, no overlapping or broken images.

## What's weak

- This is essentially the same 50%-off-everything creative theme running for a sixth+ consecutive send in under two weeks — a subscriber has seen this badge, this purple palette, and this "everything" claim repeatedly.
- The asterisk on "EVERYTHING*" is never resolved anywhere visible in the render — no fine-print callout on-screen, which the rubric flags as a soft trust issue even if it's technically below the fold.
- Hero copy is generic ("50% OFF EVERYTHING") with no fresh angle (no new-arrivals hook, no specific category tease) to differentiate it from the prior five sends.
- The footer app-download and "Discover More" tiles push real estate further down, delaying the T-shirt/clearance sections that are actually the freshest content.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the subject/hero with the T-shirt fit guide or the $3.99 clearance tier instead of "everything" — those are the only genuinely new hooks this brand hasn't repeated all month.
- 2. Vary the hero visual — same burgundy/purple family portrait style as recent sends; a different color story or setting would break the pattern-recognition fatigue.
- 3. Surface what the asterisk excludes in a visible one-line callout near the hero, not just in tiny footer legalese — builds trust per the rubric's "no buried fine print" signal.
- 4. Move the $3.99 clearance section higher — it's a stronger urgency play than the sixth "everything" claim and deserves more prominence.
