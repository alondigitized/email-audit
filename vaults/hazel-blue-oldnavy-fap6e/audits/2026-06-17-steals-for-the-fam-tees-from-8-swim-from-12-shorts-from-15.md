# Steals for the fam: tees from $8, swim from $12, shorts from $15
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy blinks first on the discount treadmill. After the June 15 "60% OFF Summer Steals" (9/10 here) and the week-long "$6 tees / $4 tanks" carpet-bombing, this send tries to do two things at once: launch a new activewear line called TRENTECH and run a clearance-by-size grid anchored on the subject line's "$8 tees, $12 swim, $15 shorts." Neither story gets the hero it deserves.
- The subject line is genuinely strong — three concrete price anchors across three categories is clean, specific, and represents a real creative reset from the "60% OFF" %-discount fatigue. But the body leads with TRENTECH, a product-story hero featuring models in coral athletic wear against a colorful background. Someone who opened for $8 tees lands on a product launch. The clearance-by-size grid — the email's most interesting and user-centric structural element — is buried below the fold.
- The 4th of July countdown banner across the top adds real timely urgency. The clearance grid (shop by size: XS through XXL with prices) is a smart friction-reducer for clearance browsing. But this email is trying to be two emails and ends up doing neither cleanly.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the best in this recent batch** — three category price anchors ($8, $12, $15) are specific, scannable, and don't lean on percentage-off fatigue after a week of 60%-off hammering.
- **4th of July countdown banner** lands a credible time-bound urgency that the subject line lacks — the visual timer reinforces "this is seasonal."
- **Clearance-by-size grid is genuinely user-centric** — shopping clearance filtered by your size is a real UX win and reduces the usual "find my size only to see it's gone" frustration.
- **Clean render** — no visible broken images, overlapping text, or layout bugs.

## What's weak

- **TRENTECH gets the hero, price-steals get the scroll** — the subject line's entire value proposition is buried. The above-the-fold experience contradicts the inbox promise.
- **Dual narrative dilutes both** — TRENTECH (new premium activewear launch) and clearance-from-$8 are opposite brand signals. One devalues the other.
- **TRENTECH body copy is illegible at rendered size** — the product description text under the hero is too small to read in the mobile render; the value prop is lost.
- **No was/now pricing or strikethrough** in the clearance grid — leaves lift on the table for what is explicitly a "steals" email.
- **"Fam" in the subject line is unkept** — there are no kids' items, family imagery, or kids' price callouts visible in the render, making the "fam" framing hollow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero: lead with the price anchors, put TRENTECH second.** Open with a "$8 tees / $12 swim / $15 shorts" three-up grid, then transition to TRENTECH as a "and new this week" discovery block. The inbox promise gets delivered above the fold.
- 2. **Add was/now strikethrough pricing to the clearance grid.** "Was $35, now $8" is a stronger click trigger than just "$8."
- 3. **Add a deadline copy line to the clearance section** ("while sizes last" or "through July 4") — the 4th of July timer earns it.
- 4. **Cut TRENTECH body copy to one punchy headline** or move it to its own dedicated send. At the current size it reads as filler between the header and the clearance grid.
- 5. **Honor "fam" — add a kids' category row or callout** near the clearance grid, or drop "fam" from the subject if kids aren't featured.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fam steals before the 4th — tees $8, swim $12`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance in your size + new TRENTECH drops`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop your size before July 4 — from $8`

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "Steals for the fam" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for broad client compatibility (VML/MSO conditionals present, responsive breakpoints defined), but has one confirmed broken tracking redirect, three compliance gaps, a CSS duplicate-selector bug that corrupts mobile spacing, and a plain-text payload that is 79% URLs — a meaningful deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect on Conversant tracking pixel**
The impression pixel hosted at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/f0f4f10f-b882-433d-80c0-b1633dd173e8?mi_mid=...` resolves to a relative URL:
```
/g/f0f4f10f-b882-433d-80c0-b1633dd173e8?txn=ff43b600-709d-11f1-9f87-aea38baf8e0c
```
A `Location` header with no scheme+host is an invalid redirect target. HTTP clients fail silently; the pixel does not fire. This is a tracking/attribution loss on every send, not a user-visible breakage (the pixel sits in the `display:none` preheader div), but it should be escalated to the Conversant/Epsilon team.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links not probed**
All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be verified from static analysis alone. Spot-check recommended (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS selector — mobile spacing corrupted**
Inside the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each declared twice with different padding values:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop  { padding-top: 5px; }   /* first declaration */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* wins — overrides all .MspaceTop uses */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot  { padding-bottom: 5px; }
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; }
```
Any element using `.MspaceTop` or `.MspaceBot` will always receive 10px padding on mobile regardless of intent. Intended 5px spacers are silently overridden.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing alt text**
`https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/f0f4f10f-b882-433d-80c0-b1633dd173e8?...` — `alt` attribute is empty (`alt=""`). For a hidden tracking pixel this is acceptable per WCAG (empty alt on decorative/non-content images), but screen readers in some clients will announce the filename. A null `alt=""` is technically correct here; no change required unless the client's accessibility bar requires `role="presentation"`.

**No issues found** — MSO/VML conditionals are present (`gte mso 9`), `AllowPNG` and `PixelsPerInch` are set, `ExternalClass` resets are in place, `viewport` meta is present.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Physical mailing address absent from visible source**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Not detected in the truncated source. This may exist in the full footer (source was truncated after the wrapper table open), but QA did not detect it — must be confirmed against the full send.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If actually absent from the SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk-sender requirement (>5k/day senders must include `List-Unsubscribe`). Likely a relay capture gap rather than a true omission, but must be verified against raw headers from a delivered copy.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk senders. Same relay-capture caveat applies, but absence here carries higher risk than `List-Unsubscribe` alone.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` should align with a DKIM signature on `email.oldnavy.com` and an SPF record covering that subdomain. Verify against raw headers from a delivered copy.
