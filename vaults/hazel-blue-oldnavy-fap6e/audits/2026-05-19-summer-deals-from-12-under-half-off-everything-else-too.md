# Summer deals from $12 & under + half off everything* else, too
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's eighth email in five days and a near carbon-copy of yesterday's send — same "$12 + 50% off everything" dual-offer structure, same TODAY ONLY badge, same creative formula. The subject line ("Summer deals from $12 & under + half off everything* else, too") differs from yesterday's ("$12 & under summer deals + 50% off everything else!") by exactly three words. If you're a subscriber who opened yesterday, you've already seen this.
- The email itself executes fine in isolation: the hero is clean, the $12 TODAY ONLY badge is prominent, and the 50% Off section is legible. But the middle clearance module — four large "Clearance" text tiles stacked in a 2×2 grid — reads as visual noise rather than a curated product offer. The Dream Dresses and sandals sections at the bottom are good cross-category fills, but they're buried under the clearance clutter.
- The dominant story here is cadence fatigue, not creative failure. A 6/10 email yesterday becomes a 4/10 open today because the subscriber's mental filter has already been trained: this is the same deal, again.

## What's working

- **TODAY ONLY urgency badge** is visible above the hero fold — hard to miss, creates genuine time pressure.
- **$12 hero price point** is large, legible, and anchored to a specific category ("Summer Faves"), which is cleaner than a generic "deals" frame.
- **50% Off Everything** is surfaced as a secondary offer rather than a footnote — keeps the dual-offer structure clear.
- **Dream Dresses Under $35** section adds a specific price-point anchor for a different category, breaking the single-product tunnel.

## What's weak

- **Near-duplicate of yesterday's send** — the subject, hero, and offer structure are functionally identical to the May 18 email that also scored 8.00. Zero incremental reason to open.
- **Clearance tile block** — four large "Clearance" text boxes in a 2×2 grid reads as a production shortcut, not curated product. It breaks the visual momentum from the clean hero.
- **Subject is 62 characters** — runs long on mobile, likely truncates before "else, too," which is the weakest part of the subject anyway.
- **No member or loyalty signal** — at this cadence, a Nav Cash or Navyist tier callout would at least give a segment-specific reason to open that wasn't there yesterday.
- **Asterisk fatigue** — "everything*" has appeared in the last four subjects in a row. The exclusion footnote is now a brand credibility signal, not a legal disclaimer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the send or hard-pivot the offer.** The marginal subscriber value of a near-duplicate at day 5 of a blitz is negative — it accelerates list fatigue and trains the open filter to ignore Old Navy. If the deal genuinely expires today, say that in the subject. If it doesn't, don't send.
- 2. **Replace the clearance tile block** with actual product cards (image, item name, was/now pricing). Four text-only "Clearance" boxes waste the most product-dense section of the email.
- 3. **Move the Dream Dresses module up** — it's the strongest mid-funnel hook and it's buried below the clearance clutter.
- 4. **Drop the "else, too" tail from the subject** — it adds length with no value. The offer is the lead; "too" weakens it.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: $12 summer faves + 50% off the rest`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — $12 deals end tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, shorts, tanks — all under $12 or half off. No code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab your $12 picks before midnight — plus 50% off everything* in your bag.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken consent-platform redirect**
The Cheetah Digital consent pixel (`cp.oldnavy.com/o/daf3d99c-c730-4678-addf-8c259244e2d1?...`) resolves to a redirect that the QA crawler cannot follow — it returns an unknown URL type: `/g/daf3d99c-c730-4678-addf-8c259244e2d1?txn=5d091380-5324-11f1-b5b1-4e61e507eb6e`. This is a relative-path redirect issued by `cp.oldnavy.com` that lacks a host, making it unresolvable outside the browser. Likely benign for end users (browsers handle it), but signals a misconfigured redirect on the consent platform side that should be confirmed with the Cheetah/CP team.

**[INFO] 23 click-tracking links skipped**
All CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Not an error, but means destination URL health is unverified by automated checks.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on consent/tracking pixel**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` tracking pixel at the top of the email has no `alt` attribute:
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/daf3d99c-..." width="1" height="1">
```

For a 1×1 pixel, `alt=""` should be explicitly set. Some screen readers will announce the full `src` URL when `alt` is absent.

**[INFO] VML/Office namespace declarations present** — correct for Outlook 2013+ rendering. MSO conditional comments are well-formed.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class selectors** — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` each appear twice in the responsive block with different property values. The second declaration wins; this is harmless but indicates copy-paste debt.

No issues found with charset declaration (`UTF-8`), viewport meta, or IE-compat meta.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed**
The AgentMail relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders. If the header is present at the MTA level but stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap — confirm directly against raw SMTP headers. If absent, this is a deliverability and compliance defect.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy (effective Feb 2024). Same caveat as above — verify at MTA level.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected by automated scan**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The truncated HTML does not include footer content, so this may be a scan artifact. Must be confirmed against the full email footer before flagging as a real defect. If absent, it is a CAN-SPAM violation.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTA links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects, which are not followed by the QA crawler, so UTM parameter presence on landing page URLs is unverified. No issues found with the sender domain alignment (`oldnavy.com` → `email.oldnavy.com` → `click.email.oldnavy.com` — consistent subdomain chain).
