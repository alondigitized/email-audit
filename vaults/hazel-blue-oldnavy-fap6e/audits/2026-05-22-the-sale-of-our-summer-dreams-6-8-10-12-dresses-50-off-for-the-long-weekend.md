# The sale of our summer dreams! $6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses + 50% off for the long weekend
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's latest Memorial Day weekend push — and effectively the 11th email in a five-day promotional blitz. The most notable creative shift is the subject: "sale of our summer dreams" is aspirational and a genuine tone break from the hard-offer machine that's been running since May 17. The hero executes cleanly — bold "MAJOR DRESS SALE!" headline, a woman in a white summer dress, and an expanded $6/$8/$10/$12 price ladder that gives the offer more dimension than yesterday's flat "$6 DRESSES" opener.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. Four distinct sections compete: the dress sale, a 50% off tees/tanks/shorts/dresses/tops block, a "HELLO, YELLOW!" linen moment, and a "Go, Go, Get CLEARANCE" teal outro. That's four different visual grammars and four separate offers in one email, and the dress-specific promise of the subject line quietly dissolves by the second scroll.
- The core offer is materially identical to the May 21 send — same $6 dresses, same 50% off umbrella, same long weekend peg. The aspirational subject rescues the open rate a little; it doesn't rescue the email from itself.

## What's working

- "MAJOR DRESS SALE!" hero is bold and immediate — price ladder ($6, $8, $10, $12) signals breadth without being confusing
- "Sale of our summer dreams" is the first genuine creative pivot in this blitz; softer and more evocative than seven straight hard-offer subjects
- "HELLO, YELLOW!" has a distinct identity — curated, color-themed, personality-forward; feels like a real editorial moment rather than a filler block
- White summer dress model is on-brief and creates a clean demographic anchor for the hero
- "Go, Go, Get CLEARANCE" teal block is visually bold and functions well as a hard stop/secondary hook at the bottom

## What's weak

- Core offer is a direct repeat of yesterday's May 21 send — "$6 dresses + 50% off for the long weekend" was already sent; expanded price ladder doesn't meaningfully differentiate it
- Four competing sections fragment the dress narrative; the 50% off block lists tees, tanks, shorts, and dresses alongside each other, immediately undermining the dress-first subject line promise
- Subject runs ~88 characters — the price ladder ($6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses) will be truncated on most mobile clients before the payload lands
- No loyalty or member benefit callout visible above the fold or in the hero
- "Long weekend" is vague urgency; no specific deadline ("ends Monday midnight") is visible anywhere in the email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to dresses above the fold and push everything else down.** The 50% off everything block should live below the Hello Yellow module, not immediately after the dress hero — it fragments the narrative before the reader has absorbed the lead offer.
- 2. **Cut the subject to mobile-first length.** Lead with the price hook; drop the aspirational opener to the preheader where it has room to breathe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6, $8, $10 & $12 dresses — all long weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dress sale from $6. 50% off everything else.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The summer dress edit you've been waiting for — shop before sizes go.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale of our summer dreams, ending Monday. Don't miss it.`
- 3. **Add a specific deadline.** "Ends Monday at midnight" converts "long weekend" from vague to actionable. Put it in the hero subhead.
- 4. **Consolidate or cut.** Dress hero + 50% backstop + clearance is three offers. Hello Yellow can stay as a secondary module. Four fully distinct blocks is one too many.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS class duplication — `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot`**
Both selectors are defined twice in the responsive block:
```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}   /* duplicate */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot  {padding-bottom: 5px;}
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;} /* duplicate */
```
The second definition wins in cascade; any element using `.MspaceTop` on mobile gets 10px top padding regardless of intent. This is a latent bug — low impact unless elements rely on the 5px variant.

**Outlook line-height override:**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is an intentional Outlook fix but aggressively zeroes all `td` line-heights in Outlook 2013+, requiring every content `td` to redeclare line-height inline. If any `td` is missing an inline override, text will collapse in Outlook desktop.

**Missing `alt` text on Movable Ink image:**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` content image has an empty/missing `alt` attribute per QA findings. Images-off clients (and screen readers) get no fallback description for that content block.

**No issues found** with the responsive breakpoint logic, VML fallbacks, or overall table structure.
