# Take your pick! It's 50% off everything*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's eighth consecutive promotional email in a seven-day wave, and the brand history shows the treadmill clearly. But compared to the June 19–22 sends that ran the same three-deal headline with almost no variation, this email makes a real creative pivot: "TIME TO CHILL" is a lifestyle hero rather than a deal dump, and it introduces two sections that haven't appeared before — "HOT NEW GEAR" and a visually loud clearance block. Those are constructive moves.
- The subject line is not. "Take your pick!" is a throwaway opener — it tells you nothing about what you're picking between. The June 19 send ("Three deals, one cart") was sharper because it named the deals upfront. This one buries the only signal worth reading ("50% off everything") after a phrase that adds zero information.
- The email body is a step forward; the subject line is a step backward. The clearance block in particular creates legitimate end-of-season urgency that every prior send this week lacked entirely — but no one will open far enough to see it if the inbox impression doesn't earn the tap.

## What's working

- "TIME TO CHILL" lifestyle hero is a genuine creative shift away from five consecutive deal-stack-first layouts — the right call after a week of repetition.
- "HOT NEW GEAR" section introduces a new-arrival angle that gives subscribers a reason to engage beyond discounts.
- Clearance block ("Go, Go, Go! Clearance Clearance Clearance") is visually loud and creates credible end-of-season urgency absent from all prior sends in this wave.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero commands attention, product grid follows, secondary offers nest below without fighting for dominance.
- 50% off is surfaced above the fold — the core offer is never buried.

## What's weak

- "Take your pick!" is a filler opener — delays the actual offer and adds no curiosity or urgency.
- No specific deadline visible anywhere in the body; the clearance section implies urgency but doesn't name a date.
- Category specificity collapsed: June 19–22 sends called out shorts, swim, and dresses by percentage. This email says "everything*" and leaves it there — fewer hooks for category-motivated shoppers.
- The Powersoft Chill Shorts callout in the hero area is too small to read clearly, underselling what appears to be a featured new product.
- Eight consecutive promo sends in one week — cadence has crossed the threshold where unsubscribe pressure becomes real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the offer, not the invitation.** "Take your pick!" is inert in a crowded inbox on week eight of the same promo wave.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off everything — shop new drops + clearance`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New gear just landed + 50% off everything*`
- 2. **Add a credible deadline.** The clearance block implies urgency but there's no anchor. "Ends Sunday" or "Through the weekend" would materially lift clicks on the clearance section specifically.
- 3. **Restore category callouts.** The June 19 send was more scannable because it named the deals: shorts, swim, dresses, %s. Surface the Powersoft Shorts as a named hero product with visible sizing/fit copy.
- 4. **Make the preheader work.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Powersoft Chill Shorts + clearance deals — this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hot new styles + half off sitewide — before it's gone`
- 5. **Consider a send-gap before the next promo.** A 48-hour rest after this wave would preserve list health more than any A/B test on subject line copy.

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "50% off everything" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect in a ContentPilot personalization link will silently drop traffic for affected subscribers. Compliance header gaps and a degenerate plain-text body are secondary risks that affect deliverability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken ContentPilot redirect**
The QA checker resolved `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/681f6c16-3ee4-487b-8654-7448ec2c3c6f?...` and got back an unknown URL scheme `/g/681f6c16-...`. This indicates the ContentPilot redirect chain is returning an unresolvable internal path rather than a valid `https://` destination. Any subscriber whose personalization resolves through this token will land on a dead URL.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links unverified**
All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx` (standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud redirect). These were skipped by the probe — no broken links confirmed, but no clean bill of health either.

**Open/impression pixel**
`click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?3HSGXT5CMBIETEQ4HTNNB2BFEM.60263` — structurally valid, hidden in a `display:none` wrapper as expected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class mappings**
`.MspaceTop` is bound to both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`; `.MspaceBot` is bound to both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. The second declaration wins via cascade, silently overriding the first. Mobile spacing for 5px variants will render as 10px.

**Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known pattern for fixing Outlook spacing gaps but can collapse cells with dynamic or unknown-height content. Worth validating in Outlook 2016/2019.

**Missing alt text on ContentPilot pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/681f6c16-...">` has no `alt` attribute (QA confirmed). For a tracking pixel this is low severity but technically violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 — the fix is `alt=""` (explicitly empty, not absent).

**Legacy doctype and meta**
XHTML 1.0 Transitional and `@-ms-viewport` are obsolete but non-breaking in current clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens are visible in the available HTML. The `cp_tp=v3.eJx9k...` parameter in the ContentPilot URL is a base64-encoded personalization payload — its content cannot be audited from the source alone, but the broken redirect above suggests it may be malformed or referencing a deleted template ID.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The ContentPilot layer (`cp.oldnavy.com`) is expected to append UTM params post-redirect; the broken redirect in §2 means any subscriber hitting that link also loses attribution.

**Plain-text body**: 87% of characters are raw URLs (19,134 / 22,020 chars per QA). This indicates the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from HTML without cleanup. Spam filters score this pattern negatively, and it provides no readable fallback for plain-text clients.
