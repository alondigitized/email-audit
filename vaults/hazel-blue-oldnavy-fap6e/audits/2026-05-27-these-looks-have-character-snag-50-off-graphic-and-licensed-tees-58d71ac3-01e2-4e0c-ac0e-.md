# These looks have character 😉 Snag 50% off graphic and licensed tees
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- After six consecutive sends built around dresses, tanks, and Memorial Day 50%-off messaging, this email actually attempts a creative pivot: graphic and licensed tees as the hero, with a "character" wordplay in the subject (personality + licensed characters). The "TEAM SPIRIT" section and what looks like a pop-culture licensed graphic block (the yellow panel with playful imagery) are a visual reset from the star-spangled dress-sale creative that's been running since May 22.
- The pivot is undercut by two things: Old Navy has cried "TODAY ONLY!" enough times this week that the urgency is inert, and the "Clearance Clearance Clearance" triple-red banner interrupts what could have been a tight, product-specific narrative. The email is genuinely trying to sell a specific category rather than blasting another 50%-off-everything board — that matters — but it's arriving on an inbox that has received 15+ Old Navy emails in seven days.
- This is a 6-of-10 email trying to wear a 7's outfit. The core idea is right; execution and cadence drag it down.

## What's working

- **Subject wordplay lands.** "These looks have character" doubles as personality + licensed-character tee nod — one of the better hooks in this week's Old Navy run.
- **TEAM SPIRIT block is visually fresh.** Group lifestyle shot with diverse friends in graphic tees breaks the "single model on white" formula that's dominated recent sends.
- **Category focus is real.** This is actually *about* graphic and licensed tees, not a generic 50%-off blast with subcategories buried below the fold.
- **Licensed imagery panel (yellow/pop-art block) adds visual energy** — it reads as intentional brand voice, not filler.

## What's weak

- **"Clearance Clearance Clearance" triple-banner breaks the narrative.** It reads as a separate campaign insertion — the tonal whiplash between fun character tees and a red clearance block undercuts both.
- **"TODAY ONLY" urgency is crying wolf.** Old Navy has used time-bounded urgency on essentially every send since May 22. By send 16+, it registers as ambient noise.
- **Subject line is too long at ~69 characters** — "These looks have character 😉 Snag 50% off graphic and licensed tees" truncates badly on mobile, burying the specific offer.
- **Visual hierarchy fragments** — the eye goes TODAY ONLY → Clearance → TEAM SPIRIT → yellow block in a choppy hop, rather than landing on one hero offer.
- **Loyalty or member pricing not visible** — a missed opportunity to differentiate on a product-specific email where a member-exclusive or early-access angle would strengthen the hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop or relocate the Clearance block** — it fights the "character tees" story. If it must stay, move it below the primary licensed-tee hero so the first scroll is one coherent story.
- 2. **Tighten the subject** — lose the winky-face or the front-loaded wordplay; put the offer front-and-center to survive mobile truncation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off graphic & licensed tees — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fave characters. 50% off. Today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Team spirit, pop culture, classics — all half off for 24 hours`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop licensed tees before they sell out — deal ends tonight`
- 3. **Retire "TODAY ONLY" for one send.** Use a specific clock or "ends at midnight" instead — it restores credibility the overuse has burned.
- 4. **Surface one hero licensed property** (if visible in the inventory) — a Marvel, Star Wars, or sports-league callout in the hero would make this scannable and shareable.
- 5. **Add a loyalty nudge** — even a small "NavyCard members: extra 5% back" line costs nothing and rewards the subscriber relationship.
