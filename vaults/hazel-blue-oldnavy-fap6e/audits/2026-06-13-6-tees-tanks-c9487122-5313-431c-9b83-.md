# 👕 $6 TEES & TANKS
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy swings back to an aggressive price-anchor strategy with $6 tees and $4 tanks — and it's structurally identical to the playbook they've been running all week. The top hero lands cleanly: "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner, a woman in a green tee, and the dual price points ($4/$6) are visible above the fold. That part works.
- The problem is what comes below the fold: the email stacks five or six additional offer blocks — 50-60% summer styles, Color Blocked Summer, Father's Day, All Active Gear, Clearance — all the way down to a $15 and Under section. It reads less like a curated send and more like an everything-in-the-warehouse dump. For a subscriber who got yesterday's Father's Day steal email ($16 shirts + 30% off), this feels like the same sale with lower price anchors and more noise.
- The clearance cascade at the bottom — where "Clearance" is stacked in large type four-plus times — is clearly an intentional design choice, but at this render size it reads as a visual glitch rather than bold typography. The email is doing too much, which undermines the sharp urgency of the $6 hero.

## What's working

- **$4/$6 price anchor in the hero is the strongest signal in the email** — dollar amounts that specific demand attention and communicate genuine value.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency banner** is prominent and above the fold, reinforcing the scarcity play.
- **Father's Day section is timely** — June 13 is well within the Father's Day gifting window and the man-in-black-tee visual is clean and on-brand.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, text is legible, layout holds across the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the ninth-plus email in five days from Old Navy. The $6 price point is new, but the "2 DAYS ONLY!" structure is verbatim from the last four sends. Subscribers have been trained to wait for the next one.
- **The subject says "$6 TEES & TANKS" but the hero shows both $4 tanks and $6 tees** — the disconnect is minor but creates a momentary pause: "which is which?"
- **Five-plus offer blocks below the fold dilute the $6 hero** — 50-60% off, Color Blocked, Father's Day, Active Gear, Clearance, and $15 Under all compete for the same click. None win decisively.
- **The "Clearance" stacked-type block** reads visually broken at inbox thumbnail size — looks like a layout error even if intentional at full width.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the email to one or two hero sections.** The $6 tees are the news. Drop or footnote everything else. A single focused CTA will convert better than six orphaned sections.
- 2. **Add the urgency to the subject.** "2 DAYS ONLY" is inside the email but missing from the subject line. That's a missed inbox-level lever.
- 3. **Resolve the $4/$6 ambiguity in the subject.** Pick one (the tee at $6 is the lead) or write it out: "$6 tees + $4 tanks."
- 4. **Break the "Clearance" stacked design on mobile** — test it at 375px. If it reads as broken to a human reviewer, it's broken to the subscriber.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$4 tanks. $6 tees. 2 days.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50-60% off summer styles — today and tomorrow only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack it with Father's Day — shirts from $6, tanks from $4.`
