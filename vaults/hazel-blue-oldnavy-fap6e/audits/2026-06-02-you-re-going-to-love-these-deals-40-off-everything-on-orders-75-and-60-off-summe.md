# You're going to LOVE these deals: 40% off everything* on orders $75+ and 60% off summer faves
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This Old Navy email arrives as roughly the sixth send in four days, and the structural decision to cram both the 40%-off-$75-threshold mechanic and the 60%-off-summer-steals into a single email is a double-edged move. The hero is appropriately punchy — "2 DAYS ONLY!" leads, the 60% headline is scannable — but the long scroll beneath it fractures the urgency into competing sections (women's fashion, tee shirts, kids/baby sale, clearance) that each fight for equal visual weight. Nothing dominates after the first screen.
- Compared to the clean May 29 send ("Today only: 50% off everything in women's") that scored a 9, this email is trying to do too much. The spend-gate on 40% off is a structural complexity that requires mental math before the offer clicks, and the triple-Clearance section at the bottom undercuts the "summer steals" premium feel. The demographic breadth — women's, kids, clearance in a single send — signals list fatigue management rather than genuine campaign cohesion.
- Workmanlike execution with a strong hero and credible urgency signal. But the subject line will be truncated on nearly every mobile client, and after five similar emails, subscriber fatigue is doing real damage to open rates.

## What's working

- "2 DAYS ONLY!" in the hero is immediately visible and gives a credible urgency anchor.
- The 60% summer steals headline is scannable and color-coordinated with the seasonal palette (pinks, yellows, tropical tones).
- Kids & Baby Sale section broadens household purchase relevance within a single send.
- Multiple shop CTAs throughout ensure easy exit points for click-through regardless of where a reader pauses their scroll.
- No render bugs — layout is clean and images load correctly.

## What's weak

- The subject line runs ~93 characters — it will be truncated at the first offer on virtually every mobile inbox, and the second offer (60% summer faves) is what should lead.
- The $75 spend-threshold mechanic buried in "40% off everything* on orders $75+" introduces cognitive friction at the subject-line level before the email is even opened.
- The multi-section layout after the hero creates competing visual weight — women's, tees, kids, and clearance all get similar real estate, which diffuses the "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency established at the top.
- Cadence is brutal: this is roughly email six in four days from Old Navy; loyal subscribers are training themselves to ignore these as background noise.
- The triple-Clearance footer section creates a brand dilution effect that conflicts with the "summer steals" positioning in the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the cleaner offer.** "60% off summer faves" is punchier than the $75 spend-gate. Put the threshold mechanic in the preheader, not the subject — it's a qualifier, not a hook.
- 2. **Add time specificity to the subject.** "2 days only" or "ends Wednesday" in the subject itself would lift opens without adding word count.
- 3. **Cut the email to three sections.** Women's hero → kids/family secondary → clearance footer. The current sprawl dilutes urgency across too many equal-weight offers.
- 4. **Move the $75 threshold below the fold.** Let 60% summer steals lead standalone; position the spend-more-save-more tier as a mid-email upsell module.
- 5. **Apply a send-frequency cap.** Five sends in two days accelerates churn. A 48-hour recency gate on opens would protect deliverability and list health.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This Old Navy email arrives as roughly the sixth send in four days, and the structural decision to cram both the 40%-off-$75-threshold mechanic and the 60%-off-summer-steals into a single email is a double-edged move. The hero is appropriately punchy — "2 DAYS ONLY!" leads, the 60% headline is scannable — but the long scroll beneath it fractures the urgency into competing sections (women's fashion, tee shirts, kids/baby sale, clearance) that each fight for equal visual weight. Nothing dominates after the first screen.

Compared to the clean May 29 send ("Today only: 50% off everything in women's") that scored a 9, this email is trying to do too much. The spend-gate on 40% off is a structural complexity that requires mental math before the offer clicks, and the triple-Clearance section at the bottom undercuts the "summer steals" premium feel. The demographic breadth — women's, kids, clearance in a single send — signals list fatigue management rather than genuine campaign cohesion.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Old Navy "40% off everything" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken redirect link will silently drop users before reaching a landing page. Compliance headers are unverified due to relay capture gaps, and the plain-text part is pathologically URL-heavy, raising spam-filter risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken Sailthru/Marigold redirect**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` click proxy returned `unknown url type: '/g/...'` for the following URL:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8f4e4dbf-3867-40fe-8a95-c4fd641db914?mi_mid=019e88d9-4180-7000-839f-8136fc804d79&...
```
The error indicates the stored destination URL in the ESP begins with a relative path (`/g/...`) instead of an absolute `https://` URL. The redirect engine cannot resolve it, so affected recipients hit a dead end. This link appears in both the open-pixel position and at least one content slot — verify all instances in the Marigold campaign editor.

**[WARN] 23 tracking links skipped by QA probe**
All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped, so destination URLs and UTM chains for those 23 links are unverified this run.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors (mobile stylesheet)**
`.MspaceTop` is declared twice — once for `.space-top5` and once for `.space-top10`. Same for `.MspaceBot` / `.MspaceBot` on `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`. The second declaration silently overrides the first; spacing behavior in affected modules is unpredictable on mobile.

```css
/* Both of these target .MspaceTop — second wins */
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}
```

**[WARN] Aggressive Outlook line-height reset**
Inside the MSO conditional comment:
```css
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
A blanket `line-height:0px` on all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+ will collapse text to zero height unless each cell explicitly overrides it. Any `<td>` without an inline `line-height` style will render as invisible in Outlook.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking beacon**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/8f4e4dbf-3867-40fe-8a95-c4fd641db914?..." alt="">
```
Alt is present but empty. Acceptable for a 1×1 pixel, but the image is not hidden via `display:none` — it occupies layout space if images are blocked and the broken-link error above may cause a visible broken-image icon.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source. The `mi_mid` value (`019e88d9-4180-7000-839f-8136fc804d79`) and `mi_cid` are populated, indicating subscriber context was injected at send time. No issues found in the visible markup.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header — unverified**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If this header is absent at delivery, Gmail and Yahoo will not render their native one-click unsubscribe button, and the send may fail Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirement.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe (POST method) appears unsupported. Required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). Needs verification against raw delivery headers — the relay may have stripped it.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. The truncated source does not show one. Confirm it exists in the footer of the full HTML — if omitted, this is a legal violation.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[FAIL] Broken redirect breaks continuity for affected link**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` redirect failure (§2) means that link slot has zero UTM pass-through — traffic never reaches oldnavy.com at all.

**[WARN] UTM chain unverified for 23 click links**
Because all `click.email.oldnavy.com` links were skipped by the HTTP probe, UTM parameters on final landing pages are unconfirmed. A manual spot-check of 2–3 primary CTAs (hero, top offer) is recommended to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended post-redirect.
