# GASP 😮 $5 tees
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's fourth consecutive send anchored to the identical $4/$5/$6 tee price grid — same child model in a pink tee, same rainbow of color swatches, same "3 DAYS ONLY!" badge that first appeared July 3. The subject line "GASP 😮 $5 tees" is the brand waving a white flag: they know the creative is exhausted, so they've layered an emoji gimmick on top of it hoping the theatrical reaction-face buys one more open. It doesn't. It signals creative fatigue more loudly than any fresh copy could.
- Scroll past the hero and there's actually meaningful content here: a $3.99 clearance rack that hasn't appeared before, a "NEW TRACKSTITCH" activewear launch in black, and a jeans editorial ("Why You Should Love Low Rise") that functions as real browse-bait. The problem is that none of it made it into the subject line or preview — so most subscribers have already mentally filed this one under "another $5 tee email" and moved on.
- Verdict: the body earned a 7 if evaluated standalone; the sending cadence and subject-line exhaustion drag the total package below the July 3 original.

## What's working

- **$3.99 clearance section is genuinely new** — first time this price point has appeared in the recent run, and it stands out visually.
- **"NEW TRACKSTITCH" module breaks the tee monotony** — an actual product launch with distinct editorial photography.
- **Low-rise jeans editorial** is browse-inviting content that doesn't feel like a promo, which is a welcome gear-shift.
- **Price hierarchy is still unambiguous** — the $4/$5/$6 grid is scannable at a glance even for an nth-time viewer.

## What's weak

- **Hero creative is copy-pasted from July 3** — identical child model, identical color swatches, identical badge. Four days of the same image is audience punishment.
- **"GASP 😮" is a self-aware gimmick that backfires** — it signals the brand knows the subject is tired rather than presenting fresh news.
- **The best new content (clearance, Trackstitch, jeans) is buried** — a subscriber who nearly deleted this unread never sees it.
- **No urgency specificity in subject** — "3 DAYS ONLY" is in the email body but the subject says none of that; the deadline that drove the July 3 open is invisible from the inbox.
- **Fourth send in three days on the same product** — cadence alone erodes trust in any offer, regardless of how genuine $5 tees are.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the tee hero or make it a thumbnail** — the $3.99 clearance or the Trackstitch launch should be the visual hero. Lead with what's new; footnote what's been said four times already.
- 2. **Surface the clearance or Trackstitch in the subject** — the new content is the actual news; bury nothing that gives a subscriber a reason to open.
- 3. **Kill the "GASP" gimmick** — no emoji reaction-face; just say the new thing directly.
- 4. **Add the deadline** — "ends tonight" or "last 24 hours" in the subject turns a passive scroll into urgency that's earned.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

However, the AgentMail-injected open pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx` also has `alt=""`. Both are acceptable for 1×1 pixels; no change needed.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class selectors**

`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive `<style>` block (once for 5px, once for 10px variants). The second declaration silently overrides the first. This is a minor authoring bug — whichever spacing is actually applied depends on source order, not intent. Low risk but worth cleaning in the template.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height override is aggressive**

The MSO conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known Outlook hack but can collapse spacing on text-only `<td>` elements that don't carry explicit height. Should be scoped to layout-only cells rather than applied globally.
