# Splash into 60% off all swim
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy sends a second swim-focused email on the same calendar day — earlier today's "Stay cool with 60% off all swim + 50% off all men's styles" already established the 60%-swim / 50%-men's dual offer. This send strips the men's angle from the subject and leads with swim only, which is technically a cleaner message, but any subscriber who opened the morning email will recognize the creative immediately: same "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency, same 60% number, same product grid. The cadence is the real damage here.
- Within the email itself, the structure is solid — swim hero with a clear percentage, a color-coded product grid, a secondary "It's Shorts Season" module, and a clearance strip at the bottom. The visual work is clean; no broken images, no layout collapse. But four sections compete for attention, and the swim promise in the subject never gets a dedicated, uncluttered moment before the email pivots to shorts and clearance.
- Judgment: a competent promotional email undermined by a same-day repeat offense. The offer is real and the creative is clean, but a subscriber's goodwill is finite, and Old Navy is drawing down fast.

## What's working

- **Subject is tight and concrete** — "Splash into 60% off all swim" is 29 characters, no spam signals, a specific discount percentage, and a seasonal verb that lands well.
- **Hero urgency is immediate** — "2 DAYS ONLY!" is the first thing the eye hits; the 60% number is large and unambiguous.
- **Swim product grid is well-merchandised** — colorful, varied SKUs (tops, bottoms, one-pieces) presented in a scannable tile layout; easy to spot your category.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean across the full scroll; no overlapping text, no broken image placeholders.
- **Shorts module adds incremental reach** — "It's Shorts Season" is a logical summer pairing and gives non-swimmers a reason to stay in the email.

## What's weak

- **Second swim email of the day kills open impact** — subscribers who saw the morning send will feel déjà vu the moment they open; the subject twist ("Splash into" vs. "Stay cool with") is too thin to disguise the repeat.
- **Hero is split-attention** — the 50% and 60% figures appear in close proximity near a male model; without reading carefully, it's momentarily unclear which percentage applies to which category.
- **No price anchoring or specific items called out** — the grid shows categories, not hero products with a struck-through price; harder to feel the deal's pull.
- **Clearance module dilutes the swim message** — dropping a fourth content block at the bottom makes this feel like a catch-all digest rather than a focused swim event.
- **No loyalty or member callout visible** — Old Navy has an Old Navy Money / credit card program; no sign of member-exclusive pricing or early access here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one of the two same-day sends** — if both swim emails are scheduled, consolidate or offset by 24 hours. The creative cost of subscriber fatigue exceeds any incremental open rate from a second deployment.
- 2. **Lead with a hero product, not just a percentage** — anchor the 60% on one specific swimsuit (with a struck-through price), then funnel into the grid. Price anchoring converts better than category discounts alone.
- 3. **Cut the clearance module or move it to a dedicated email** — every extra section after the primary offer erodes click-through on the primary CTA.
- 4. **Surface "2 DAYS ONLY" in the subject** — the time-bound urgency is inside the email but missing from the inbox row where it would do the most work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off swim — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your swim haul is 60% off (ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop tops, bottoms & one-pieces before the sale ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every swim style on sale — plus shorts, tees & clearance finds.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing alt text**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/d2014ab1-...&mi_mid=..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt` attribute present. While 1×1 pixels should carry `alt=""`, screen readers may announce the `src` URL fragment. Low severity but flaggable under WCAG 2.1.

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in media query**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice at `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)`. The source comment even notes this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`. The second declaration silently wins; the first is dead code. No rendering breakage observed, but it's a maintenance hazard.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional `td { line-height: 0px; }`**
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This resets all `td` line-height to zero for Outlook 2013+. Correct usage requires every content `td` to explicitly set its own `line-height`. If any `td` lacks an inline override, text will collapse. Not confirmed broken from the truncated source, but high-risk pattern.

**[INFO]** XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE + VML namespace — legacy but intentional and correct for broad Outlook support.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**
QA reports neither header was captured via the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) for one-click unsubscribe. Without it, Gmail may suppress the unsubscribe UI or route to spam. Likely a relay capture gap, but must be verified against raw SMTP headers.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Not present in the visible HTML fragment. If absent from the full footer, this is a statutory violation. Needs full-source confirmation.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` sending via `email.oldnavy.com` — alignment should be verifiable but was not surfaced by the relay.
