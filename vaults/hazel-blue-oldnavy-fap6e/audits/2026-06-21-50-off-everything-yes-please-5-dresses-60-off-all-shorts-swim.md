# 50% off EVERYTHING?* (Yes, please!) + $5 dresses & 60% off all shorts & swim*
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Old Navy send in roughly six days and it's running the exact same triple-deal stack — 50% off everything, $5 dresses, 60% off shorts & swim — that debuted on June 19. The creative swaps in new lifestyle imagery (a "Serving Looks" tennis-and-sport theme plus a clearance module at the bottom), but the discount structure is identical to the June 20 and June 21 sends already audited here. A real subscriber who opened even two of these would recognize the playbook on sight and feel like they're being spammed.
- What saves it from total creative fatigue is the "Serving Looks" sport-themed editorial block, which gives the middle section some visual freshness. The clearance grid at the bottom ("Shop Clearance in Your Size") is a smart add — it's the most action-oriented thing in the email and the only part that feels differentiated from the prior sends.
- Judgement: structurally fine, visually competent, but this send is doing almost nothing to earn another open from a subscriber who has already seen four nearly identical emails this week. Fatigue is the story here.

## What's working

- **"Serving Looks" editorial block** gives the mid-email section a distinct identity that the prior sends lacked — the tennis/sport angle is seasonally relevant and visually cohesive.
- **Hero discount hierarchy is clear** — "60% off shorts & swim" is the oversized hero claim, with secondary deals visible beneath it without scrolling on desktop.
- **Clearance module at the bottom** ("Shop Clearance in Your Size") introduces a new deal mechanic not present in prior sends; it's the freshest call-to-action in the email.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load cleanly, layout holds, text doesn't overlap.
- **Multiple CTAs mapped to distinct categories** (dresses, shorts, swim, clearance) — good for segmentation within a single scroll.

## What's weak

- **Identical deal stack, fifth consecutive send** — 50% off everything / $5 dresses / 60% shorts & swim has run June 17–21. There is nothing new here for the subscriber who opened the June 19 or June 20 sends.
- **Subject line parenthetical gimmick ("Yes, please!")** is filler — it takes up ~12 characters of mobile subject real estate without adding information or urgency.
- **No credible deadline visible in the hero** — the June 18 PowerChill send ("TODAY ONLY!") nailed urgency; this one doesn't show a hard expiry, which makes the offer feel perpetual and reduces impulse.
- **The asterisked exclusions ("*") appear multiple times but no exclusion detail is visible in the render** — subscribers have been trained by five sends to wonder what's excluded.
- **Clearance module is buried at the very bottom** — the most differentiated, action-driving element is the last thing a mobile reader sees.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface clearance or one hero product higher** — the "Shop Clearance in Your Size" module is the most differentiated CTA; move it to below the first hero block. The same deal stack needs a new hook or it's dead on arrival for repeat openers.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the hero** — even "Ends Sunday" or "Today through midnight" would manufacture the urgency that "yes, please!" fails to create.
- 3. **Trim the subject line** — drop the parenthetical and lead with the freshest element:
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 dresses + 60% off swim — weekend clearance starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance drops today: 60% off swim, shorts & more`
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader to complement, not repeat** — current preheader appears to echo the subject deal language:
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New clearance picks added — shop your size before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer refresh starts at $5 — but sizes won't last`
- 5. **Rotate the deal mechanic** — even packaging the same discount as "Build your summer kit" or a bundle story would reduce fatigue without changing the underlying offer.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's HTML is structurally sound for a legacy table-based layout, but the QA pipeline surfaced one broken tracking pixel URL, missing authentication headers, incomplete CAN-SPAM compliance signals, and multiple images without alt text. No merge token failures detected.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken tracking pixel URL**
The Cheetah Digital (cp.oldnavy.com) open-tracking pixel resolves to a redirect that produces an unknown URL type error:

```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5cd02092-0a86-4b52-bd4c-be5483edd754?mi_mid=019eec32...
→ /g/5cd02092-0a86-4b52-bd4c-be5483edd754?txn=6e86dc00-716a-11f1-a2ac-aea38baf8e0c
```

The `/g/` redirect target is unresolvable. This means open-rate data for this send is unreliable. The pixel is placed in the hidden preheader div, so no user-facing breakage occurs, but open attribution is lost.

**[WARN] 23 click-redirect links skipped**
All CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. These were not probed — destination URL validity is unverified by automated checks.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected** by QA scanner. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. Either the address is present further down in the footer (outside the truncated source window) or it is absent — must be verified against full HTML before clearance.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay artifact, but if the header is genuinely absent, Gmail and Apple Mail will not render their native unsubscribe UI. For a sender of this volume, this is a deliverability risk under Gmail's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found.** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail for bulk senders (>5k/day). If missing from the actual SMTP envelope, this is a compliance gap.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found** via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverified. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment.
