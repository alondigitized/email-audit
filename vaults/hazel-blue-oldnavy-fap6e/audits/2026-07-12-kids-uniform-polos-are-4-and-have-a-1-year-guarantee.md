# Kids uniform polos are $4 (and have a 1-year guarantee!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's **fourth polo-focused email in under 24 hours** — the July 11 morning send, July 11 afternoon send, the July 12 "Dress code, handled" send, and now this one. The brand is clearly playing volume, and at some point the inbox treats it as noise. The saving grace here is the guarantee angle: "1-year guarantee" is the only genuinely new message this email introduces, and it's a real product benefit worth leading with.
- The email itself is visually split. The hero — $4 polos, "ALL-DAY STRETCH," "1 YEAR GUARANTEE," "2 DAYS ONLY" — is tight and purposeful. Everything below the fold collapses into a kitchen-sink dump: "$3.99 clearance," new trenches, a "Late-Night Sale" section, $40 jeans, $36 items, and a generic Back-to-School shop link. That bottom half belongs to a different email entirely and drains the guarantee story of its momentum.
- Verdict: the subject line is the best thing Old Navy wrote this week on this campaign. The email behind it doesn't fully earn the click.

## What's working

- **Guarantee is a new, credible differentiator.** "1-year guarantee" — visible in both subject and hero — is the first genuinely new message in this polo streak and gives a fatigued subscriber a reason to reopen.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY" badge creates real urgency** at the top without being hyperbolic.
- **"ALL-DAY STRETCH" is a product benefit claim**, not just a price shout — it speaks to parents who care about durability.
- **Hero section is clean and focused** — price, product, benefit, and deadline are all visible above the fold.
- **No render bugs.** The email renders cleanly with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Fourth polo email in 24 hours.** Any credible urgency built up by the "2 DAYS ONLY" badge is undercut by the fact this exact product has now been the hero four consecutive times.
- **Below-the-fold is a completely different email.** Trenches, "Late-Night Sale looks," $40 jeans — these sections belong to separate campaigns and dilute the guarantee story.
- **The $3.99 clearance section is confusingly positioned** directly after the polo hero, making it read as a price correction or upsell rather than a separate offer.
- **Subject line is 56 chars** — over the 50-char mobile sweet spot; the parenthetical will likely truncate on small screens, cutting the guarantee copy.
- **No social proof anywhere** — a single star rating or "best-seller" badge would make the guarantee feel earned, not just claimed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the bottom half entirely.** The guarantee message is strong enough to carry a focused email. Every section below the polo hero cannibalizes it. Send the trenches/Late-Night content in a separate send — Old Navy clearly has the cadence for it.
- 2. **Move the "1-year guarantee" callout up into a visual badge** directly on the product hero image (like a seal or stamp), not just as body copy. Parents making a uniform decision want that front-and-center.
- 3. **Shorten the subject to fit mobile.** The parenthetical is the differentiator — restructure so the guarantee isn't the part that truncates.
- 4. **Add a single "as worn by real kids" photo or review pull-quote.** The guarantee claim lands harder with social proof behind it.
- 5. **Flag the deadline in the subject line** — "2 days only" lives only in the body badge; surfacing it in the subject or preheader would lift open rates.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$4 polos + a 1-year guarantee — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The $4 polo with a 1-year guarantee is back (but not for long)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `ALL-DAY STRETCH, school-ready colors, and a guarantee that lasts all year.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `If it wears out, we replace it. $4 kids uniform polos, 2 days only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken click destination and several compliance/deliverability warnings. The plain-text body is machine-generated junk that will harm spam scores, and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is unverifiable from the relay capture.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com tracking pixel/link**
The QA probe of the Campaign Pilot redirect URL returned `unknown url type: '/g/a23fde8d-bc2e-4081-8bd4-5977d064287d?txn=681f8200-7e43-11f1-9ec2-9295eb1db778'`. The endpoint resolved but returned an internal routing error, meaning the underlying destination path `/g/...` is unrecognized by the Campaign Pilot service. Any click that routes through this URL will land on an error.

- **Affected URL (redacted base):** `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a23fde8d-bc2e-4081-8bd4-5977d064287d?mi_mid=019f540d-...&cp_cid=1bc829e71098f268`

**23 click-redirect links skipped**
All `click.email.oldnavy.com` links were not probed. Destination reachability for the primary CTAs is unverified.

**Dual open-tracking pixels**
Both `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?...` and the cp.oldnavy.com endpoint fire on open. Double-counting opens is expected behavior for this ESP stack, but worth noting if metrics are reconciled across systems.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel image has no `alt` attribute:
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/a23fde8d-..." width="1" height="1">
```
Screen readers will announce the full src URL. Should be `alt=""`.

**Duplicate CSS class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media` block. The second declaration silently overrides the first. No rendering breakage observed, but the CSS is inconsistent and the comment `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */` in the source suggests this was noticed but not fully resolved.

**Outlook MSO line-height override**
```css
td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }
```
This blanket reset inside the `[if gte mso 9]` conditional collapses all `td` line heights to 0 in Outlook 2013+. Individual cells must explicitly set `line-height` or content will stack. Standard pattern but fragile — any `td` without an explicit override will be zero-height.

**No issues found:** XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype, VML namespace declarations, and responsive breakpoint structure are all standard and correct.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens detected in the visible HTML. `mi_mid` (`019f540d-8500-7000-b859-4bd69c0cb421`) and `mi_cid` (`67859151839b71d0`) appear to be populated subscriber-level values, not placeholder tokens.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not captured**
Both headers are absent from the relay-captured headers. If the sending MTA injects these at the SMTP layer after the AgentMail relay, this is a capture gap — not necessarily a sending gap. However, RFC 8058 one-click compliance cannot be confirmed and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements mandate it for sends at this volume.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not confirmed**
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The HTML is truncated; the address may exist in the footer. Cannot confirm presence or absence from the provided source. Must verify in the full render.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. For `email.oldnavy.com`, DKIM alignment with the `From:` domain (`oldnavy.com`) should be verified. Failure here directly affects inbox placement.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Broken cp.oldnavy.com redirect** (see §2) is the primary continuity failure — any recipient clicking a link that routes through this path gets an error page instead of a product destination.

**UTM passthrough unverifiable**
All 23 primary CTAs route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects, which were not probed. Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) survive the redirect chain to oldnavy.com cannot be confirmed from this capture.

**Plain-text body is URL-stuffed**
20,912 of 23,880 chars (87.6%) in the plain-text part are URLs. This is an auto-generated plain-text from the HTML rather than a human-readable version. Spam filters — particularly SpamAssassin's `HTML_MESSAGE` and Outlook's junk filter — penalize this pattern. Plain-text should be a stripped, readable summary with minimal links.
