# Get a jump on it: $6 kids uniform shorts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy finally pivots off the four-day tee-price-grid blitz and lands on something genuinely useful for July: a back-to-school uniform push with a "TODAY ONLY!" $6 shorts hook. The creative shift is welcome — same dollar-anchor formula, new category, real calendar relevance since uniform shopping legitimately starts in early July. The email earns its open.
- The execution is fractured, though. The hero does its job — "$6," "TODAY ONLY!," kids model, clear enough — but the email immediately sprawls into a $3.99 module, a clearance size-grid, and a "Hot New Gear" section that has nothing to do with uniforms. Four distinct messages fight for one click. The subscriber who came for $6 shorts gets routed through an Old Navy catalog instead.
- Clear verdict: the subject line is the best part of this send. Everything after the hero is noise that will cost clicks.

## What's working

- **Dollar-anchor subject line is concrete and fresh** — "$6 kids uniform shorts" breaks the tee-fatigue pattern that ran July 2–5 and gives subscribers a reason to re-engage.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" urgency is credible** — a single-day flash on a specific item is more believable than the "3 DAYS ONLY!" badge Old Navy recycled across four consecutive sends.
- **B2S timing is legitimate** — July uniform shopping is real; this doesn't feel manufactured.
- **$3.99 second module adds price-downward momentum** — two concrete price points below $10 in one email makes the value case without needing percentages.
- **Sender name "Old Navy Back to School" signals segmentation** — subscribers with school-age kids get a relevant frame before the subject line even registers.

## What's weak

- **"Hot New Gear" undercuts the uniform story** — a colorful trend/activewear section placed below a school-uniform flash sale sends mixed signals and dilutes the one-job CTA.
- **The clearance size-grid module is visually heavy and mid-funnel** — it asks subscribers to do work (find their size) before they've committed to the category.
- **Multiple competing price anchors ($6, $3.99, clearance) fragment attention** — no single offer wins the scroll.
- **CTA copy isn't readable at typical mobile scale** — button text is unresolvable in the render, which usually means it's generic ("Shop Now") rather than specific.
- **No social proof anywhere** — for a school-uniform category where fit and durability matter to parents, a single "4.7 stars · 2,400 reviews" line would move product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the "Hot New Gear" section entirely** — one job per email. The uniform/B2S frame is stronger without a trend-fashion non-sequitur at the bottom. Run Hot New Gear as its own send.
- 2. **Make the CTA button copy earn its click** — change from generic "Shop Now" to "Shop $6 Uniform Shorts" so the button reinforces the hero offer.
- 3. **Replace the clearance size-grid with a 3-product social-proof strip** — "Parents love these · ★★★★★ 2,400+ reviews" with the top 3 uniform SKUs converts the "find your size" friction into pull.
- 4. **Consolidate to two price modules max** — $6 shorts hero + $3.99 polo is enough. More dilutes.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject lead** — "Get a jump on it" is a throw-away phrase; the dollar price should come first.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6 uniform shorts — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back to school starts at $6`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shorts + polos under $4. Stock up before the rush.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Today only: $6 shorts, $3.99 polos, sizes for every kid.`

Empty `alt=""` is technically valid for decorative/tracking images, but screen readers in verbose mode will announce an unlabeled image. Low severity.

The Outlook conditional sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally, then relies on individual elements to restore spacing. This is a known but fragile pattern — any `<td>` without an explicit `line-height` or `mso-line-height-rule` override will collapse to zero height in Outlook 2013+.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent (relay artifact)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If this header is absent from the actual SMTP envelope (not just a relay capture issue), Gmail and Yahoo will not render the native unsubscribe button — a direct violation of their 2024 bulk sender requirements for lists >5k/day.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Absence means users must click through to a preference center, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. None is visible in the truncated HTML. Confirm it appears below the fold in the full source; if omitted, this is a statutory violation.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status cannot be confirmed. `oldnavy.com` has published DMARC, so a DKIM failure here would cause inbox filtering. Needs direct header inspection from the received message.
