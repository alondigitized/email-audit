# EVERYTHING* is 50% off (and its all yours) + 60% off all shorts & swim* and $5 dresses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's third consecutive send featuring the same triple-deal stack — 60% off shorts & swim, $5 dresses, 50% off everything else. Yesterday's June 19 "Three deals, one cart" send and this morning's "Hot temps, hotter prices" send (also June 20, per brand history) already ran these exact numbers. The subject line changes the wrapper ("EVERYTHING* is 50% off and it's all yours") but the offer is structurally identical, and the asterisk immediately undercuts the headline promise.
- The rendered email is a multi-section vertical stack — 60% off hero at top, $5 dresses section, a 50% off everything block, a product grid, and what looks like a new activewear section at the bottom. There's a lot competing for attention. No render bugs, but no visual focus either.
- The net read: this is a cadence-fatigued repeat send dressed in slightly different subject-line clothes. A subscriber who opened yesterday's email already knows the deal. The only meaningful addition would be if there's urgency ("today only") visible in the body — and from the screenshot, no credible deadline is apparent.

## What's working

- **60% off hero is above the fold.** The steepest discount leads, giving the eye an immediate anchor.
- **Multi-category coverage.** Three discrete deals (shorts/swim, dresses, everything else) means something is likely to connect with any subscriber segment in the list.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues across the full screenshot.
- **Summer seasonal fit.** Bright palette, lifestyle models in warm-weather outfits — the visual language matches the moment.

## What's weak

- **Third nearly-identical send in 24 hours.** The "Hot temps, hotter prices" send earlier today ran the same three deals; this is a cadence abuse that erodes urgency and trains unsubscribes.
- **"EVERYTHING*" is immediately dishonest.** The asterisk in the subject line and hero copy signals exclusions before the subscriber clicks; it reads as bait.
- **Subject line is 88 characters and incoherent.** Two separate offers crammed into one line with a "+" separator and a parenthetical filler phrase ("and its all yours") that adds nothing. Truncated on virtually every mobile client.
- **No credible deadline visible.** The 50% off block in the middle has no "today only" or expiry — with no time pressure, there's no reason to act now vs. tomorrow (when another identical send will presumably arrive).
- **Busy vertical stack dilutes every offer.** The scroll depth required to reach the activewear section at the bottom means most subscribers won't see it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one deal and make it scream.** "50% off everything" is the broadest hook — build the entire email around it. Move 60% off shorts to the sub-header, cut $5 dresses to a tertiary tile. A single headline deal converts better than three competing ones.
- 2. **Add a hard expiry, visible above the fold.** "Ends tonight at midnight" or a countdown timer would make this feel like a reason to act today instead of waiting.
- 3. **Drop the parenthetical filler in the subject.** "(and its all yours)" wastes 20 characters and adds zero persuasive value.
- 4. **Suppress to yesterday's openers.** Anyone who opened the June 19 or June 20 morning send and didn't click should get a tighter re-engagement subject, not the same email again.
- 5. **Fix the asterisk or remove it.** If "everything" has major exclusions, say "most styles" or "thousands of styles" — don't promise everything and asterisk it away.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The biggest summer sale of the week ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shorts, swim, tees, dresses — half off, right now.`

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers not captured**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were detected by the QA relay. This is flagged as a relay-capture gap rather than confirmed absence — Sailthru injects these at SMTP layer and they may be present in the actual delivered message. However, if missing from delivered mail, Gmail and Yahoo (which enforce one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders as of Feb 2024) will suppress the native unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected in scanned body**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address. The truncated HTML source provided to QA did not include a footer section where this would normally appear. Cannot confirm absence — this is likely a truncation artifact. Verify the rendered footer contains `Old Navy, 2 Folsom Street, San Francisco, CA 94105` or equivalent.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent from relay capture**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown. `email.oldnavy.com` is a Gap Inc. Salesforce Marketing Cloud (legacy)/Sailthru sending domain — DKIM signing is expected. Absence here is likely an AgentMail relay stripping issue, not a genuine authentication failure, but should be confirmed against raw headers.
