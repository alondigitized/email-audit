# JUST DROPPED — $12 PJs + 50% OFF everything*
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- The subject line promises "$12 PJs" as the headline hook, but there is not a single pajama product anywhere in this render — the hero is "TODAY ONLY! Polos $8/$10/$12," followed by wall-to-wall Baggy Jeans content. That's a bait-and-switch that will read as broken or mistargeted the second the email opens, not just a missed opportunity.
- Once you get past the subject mismatch, the body itself is a coherent, if extremely repetitive, jeans-and-fleece push: Low Rise Baggy ($18), Super Baggy ($20), Super Baggy Wide Leg ($10), Super Baggy kids ($10), then a pivot to Fleece Faves and a $3.99 clearance footer. It's Old Navy's now-familiar "50% Off Everything" wrapper (this is at least the fifth send carrying that banner since July 20) stapled onto a new jeans creative.
- Judgement: the creative execution on the jeans cascade is fine — clean product grids, clear per-item pricing, consistent purple/red brand palette — but the subject-to-body disconnect is a real defect that will tank trust for anyone who opens expecting sleepwear. Combined with fatigue from five straight 50%-off-everything sends, this lands below the brand's recent average.

## What's working

- Clear "TODAY ONLY!" urgency banner up top with itemized polo pricing ($8/$10/$12).
- Repeated "More options at 50% off" module under each jean style gives concrete swatch/style variety without feeling cluttered.
- Consistent purple-and-white "Baggy" campaign styling ties the whole scroll together visually.
- Bright red "$3.99 Clearance" band at the footer is a strong scroll-terminating offer with a legible CTA.
- No overlapping text, broken images, or lorem-ipsum — the render itself is technically clean.

## What's weak

- **The subject's entire hook — "$12 PJs" — never appears in the email.** Nothing resembling pajamas, sleepwear, or loungewear sets is shown; the $12 price point belongs to polos instead.
- Six near-identical "jeans model + product grid + more options" blocks in a row (Low Rise, Super Baggy, Super Baggy Wide Leg, Super Baggy kids) create heavy visual monotony before you even reach fleece or clearance.
- "50% Off Everything" is now the fifth consecutive send with this exact banner (July 20, 25, 26, 27, 28) — a subscriber who's opened recent Old Navy mail has seen this exact framing repeatedly.
- No hero anchor for the promised PJs means the recipient has to hunt through the whole scroll for a category that doesn't actually exist, undermining trust in future subject lines.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/body mismatch first — either add a PJs hero module or drop "$12 PJs" from the subject entirely.** This is the single biggest lift available; right now the promise is unfulfilled on open, which is worse than a mediocre subject.
- 2. Cut the jeans cascade from four back-to-back style blocks to two — lead with Super Baggy (the newest style) and fold Low Rise + Wide Leg into a single "More Baggy Styles" combined grid.
- 3. Move the $3.99 Clearance band up near the top-fold — it's the strongest, most concrete price point in the email and is currently buried at the very bottom.
- 4. Vary the campaign language from the exact "50%Off Everything!" wording used in the last four sends — even a small copy shift ("Still 50% Off — Everything") signals freshness instead of repetition.
