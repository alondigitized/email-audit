# Tees are $5 (and going fast!)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy follows yesterday's "$5 biker shorts" with the same dollar-anchor formula applied to tees. The hero delivers: a "$4 / $5 / $6" tiered price grid with a rainbow of color swatches and a "3 DAYS ONLY!" badge makes the value case in one glance. Timing against July 4 weekend is sensible and the subject is one of their tightest in weeks — price, product, scarcity, 30 characters.
- The problem is what happens mid-scroll. A "NEW TREKTECH" module drops into the middle of what should be a clean tee story — the same activewear creative that ran on heavy rotation through all of late June. A subscriber who opened for a $5 basic now has to scroll past athletic gear, then more secondary product tiles, before the email ends. The offer is real and the price is honest, but the brand keeps bolting on TrekTech like a nervous habit.
- Competent execution on a good offer, undermined by the inability to commit to one story for one email.

## What's working

- **Tiered pricing hero** — "$4 / $5 / $6" is more interesting than a flat "$5 tees" claim; the three price points expand the offer without muddying it.
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" badge** — deadline against a July 4 weekend reads as credible rather than manufactured.
- **Rainbow swatch grid** — the color variety is communicated visually in seconds; makes basics feel exciting rather than boring.
- **Subject line discipline** — 30 characters, a real price, a product noun, and a parenthetical scarcity nudge. No ALL CAPS, no exclamation stacking.

## What's weak

- **TrekTech intrusion** — the "NEW TREKTECH" module lands mid-scroll and completely breaks the tee narrative; this creative has run for weeks and belongs in a separate send.
- **Generic CTA copy** — "SHOP NOW" is lazy given the specificity of the offer; "Shop $5 Tees" would match what the subject promised.
- **No loyalty or member signal** — no Navyist callout, no card-holder benefit, no points mention; for a value-focused send this is a missed conversion lever.
- **Second consecutive $5 anchor** — yesterday was "$5 biker shorts," today is "$5 tees." Two identical dollar-anchors in two days trains subscribers to wait rather than act.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut TrekTech entirely.** This email should be one story: tees at $4/$5/$6. The module has had its run; removing it tightens the scroll and focuses the click path.
- 2. **Match the CTA to the offer.** Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop $5 Tees" or "Grab a tee" — mirrors the subject, reduces decision friction.
- 3. **Add a stock-scarcity signal.** A low-stock badge on specific colorways ("Coral — only 12 left") makes "(and going fast!)" credible instead of just a tagline.
- 4. **Surface Navyist / card-holder benefit.** One line — "Navyist members, shop early access now" — converts the loyalty base harder without changing the creative.
- 5. **Rotate off the $5 anchor tomorrow.** A third "$5 [item]" in three sends will kill the signal. Escalate to a July 4 specific frame or a different value mechanic.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `$4, $5 & $6 styles — hundreds of colors, limited time`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `3 days to get tees at their lowest price of summer`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

Old Navy follows yesterday's "$5 biker shorts" with the same dollar-anchor formula applied to tees. The hero delivers: a "$4 / $5 / $6" tiered price grid with a rainbow of color swatches and a "3 DAYS ONLY!" badge makes the value case in one glance. Timing against July 4 weekend is sensible and the subject is one of their tightest in weeks — price, product, scarcity, 30 characters.

The problem is what happens mid-scroll. A "NEW TREKTECH" module drops into the middle of what should be a clean tee story — the same activewear creative that ran on heavy rotation through all of late June. A subscriber who opened for a $5 basic now has to scroll past athletic gear, then more secondary product tiles, before the email ends. The offer is real and the price is honest, but the brand keeps bolting on TrekTech like a nervous habit.

Competent execution on a good offer, undermined by the inability to commit to one story for one email.

## 2. What worked

- **Tiered pricing hero** — "$4 / $5 / $6" is more interesting than a flat "$5 tees" claim; the three price points expand the offer without muddying it.
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" badge** — deadline against a July 4 weekend reads as credible rather than manufactured.
- **Rainbow swatch grid** — the color variety is communicated visually in seconds; makes basics feel exciting rather than boring.
- **Subject line discipline** — 30 characters, a real price, a product noun, and a parenthetical scarcity nudge. No ALL CAPS, no exclamation stacking.

## 3. What didn't

- **TrekTech intrusion** — the "NEW TREKTECH" module lands mid-scroll and completely breaks the tee narrative; this creative has run for weeks and belongs in a separate send.
- **Generic CTA copy** — "SHOP NOW" is lazy given the specificity of the offer; "Shop $5 Tees" would match what the subject promised.
- **No loyalty or member signal** — no Navyist callout, no card-holder benefit, no points mention; for a value-focused send this is a missed conversion lever.
- **Second consecutive $5 anchor** — yesterday was "$5 biker shorts," today is "$5 tees." Two identical dollar-anchors in two days trains subscribers to wait rather than act.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Cut TrekTech entirely.** This email should be one story: tees at $4/$5/$6. The module has had its run; removing it tightens the scroll and focuses the click path.
2. **Match the CTA to the offer.** Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop $5 Tees" or "Grab a tee" — mirrors the subject, reduces decision friction.
3. **Add a stock-scarcity signal.** A low-stock badge on specific colorways ("Coral — only 12 left") makes "(and going fast!)" credible instead of just a tagline.
4. **Surface Navyist / card-holder benefit.** One line — "Navyist members, shop early access now" — converts the loyalty base harder without changing the creative.
5. **Rotate off the $5 anchor tomorrow.** A third "$5 [item]" in three sends will kill the signal. Escalate to a July 4 specific frame or a different value mechanic.
