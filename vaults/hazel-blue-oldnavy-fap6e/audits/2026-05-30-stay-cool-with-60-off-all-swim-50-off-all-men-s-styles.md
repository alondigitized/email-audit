# Stay cool with 60% off all swim* + 50% off all men's styles*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Post-Memorial Day, Old Navy pivots the creative from yesterday's clean "50% off everything in women's" (which scored a 9) to a split-focus send: 60% off swim as the subject lead, 50% off men's as the hero. The strategy makes seasonal sense — swim is the natural next chapter after a long-weekend blitz — but the execution muddles the offer hierarchy. The subject puts swim first; the hero banner leads with "50%" and men's energy. If you're Hazel opening this for swim deals, the hero doesn't confirm what the subject promised.
- The email is long and covers four distinct categories: swim, women's, men's, clearance, and kids/baby. Each section is individually coherent, but together they read like a catalog dump rather than a curated send. The cadence problem is real — this is approximately the eighth Old Navy email in eight days. At this volume, even a legitimately good offer gets lost in inbox fatigue.
- The 60% swim discount is the strongest, freshest, most category-appropriate hook here, and it's buried below the fold. That's the core editorial failure.

## What's working

- **60% off swim is the sharpest offer in the email** — a meaningful step up from the 50%-off standard rate, and the timing (post-Memorial Day, peak swim season) is exactly right.
- **Swim product grid is visually rich** — multiple color options, bikini separates, and one-pieces displayed as a grid give Hazel something to scan and click into.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency is unambiguous** — the deadline is front and center in the hero, no fine print needed to find it.
- **Women's striped outfit section adds aspirational browsing appeal** — a single model in a clean look is a good break from the discount-stacked sections.

## What's weak

- **60% swim is the lead in the subject but not the hero** — the hero banner reads "50% / men's energy," creating a mismatch for Hazel who opened for swim.
- **Men's as hero is off-persona** — Hazel is a women's shopper; leading with the men's offer in the hero panel doesn't serve her path to purchase.
- **Four categories in one scroll is catalog fatigue** — swim, women's, men's, kids/baby, clearance competes for attention and dilutes every individual offer.
- **Clearance section at the bottom is noisy** — the repeated pink "Clearance" blocks feel like a separate email appended to this one, undermining the "fresh summer offer" tone.
- **Subject is 60 characters — too long for mobile preview** — the men's qualifier gets clipped on most lock screens, making it effectively a single-offer subject despite being dual.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote the 60% swim deal to the hero** — this is the better, fresher offer. Swap the hero so swim leads visually, and the men's section becomes a secondary panel below. The subject and the hero would then tell the same story.
- 2. **Cut this to two sections max** — swim + one supporting women's section. Clearance and kids/baby belong in a separate send or footer link, not a full panel. Signal-to-noise is already strained after eight sends this week.
- 3. **Add a deadline-specific timestamp to the hero** — "2 DAYS ONLY!" is good but "Ends Sunday at midnight" is better. Specificity makes urgency feel real.
- 4. **Trim the subject to under 50 characters** — the asterisk qualifiers and "all men's styles" get cut on mobile. Pick the stronger single offer or a tighter dual-offer construction.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off all swim — plus 50% off men's`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Swim up to 60% off — 2 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bikinis, one-pieces, swim shorts — all on sale this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your swimsuit is 60% off. Grab it before Sunday.`
