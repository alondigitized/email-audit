# This deal is TOO good — 60% OFF Summer Steals
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy swings to a 60% off headline — the steepest discount percentage in the audit window, escalating past the 50% off from June 9 and the $6 tees anchor that dominated June 13-14. The hero follows the exact same playbook: "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner, bright female model in a yellow summer dress, clean white background, stacked offers below. It's competent and fast to parse, but it also feels like the fifth time this week someone turned up the volume.
- The email earns its clicks with a clean visual hierarchy. The 60% off hero lands above the fold with no ambiguity, the product grid below covers multiple summer categories (women's, what appears to be a new "Tech Fit" athletic line, and a clearance sweep), and the overall render is bug-free. The design does its job.
- The liability is entirely cadence and signal exhaustion. A subscriber who also received the June 14 "$6 TEES & TANKS" double-send is now getting their sixth promotional email in four days. The subject leans on "TOO good" hype where the number alone — 60% OFF — would have been stronger. Old Navy is training its list to wait for the next deal, not act on this one.

## What's working

- **60% OFF is the lead and it's visible immediately** — the hero percentage is large, unambiguous, and the highest offer in the brand history for this audit window.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency is cleanly executed** — the red banner creates real time pressure without resorting to exclamation spam.
- **Email covers multiple summer categories** — women's dresses, tops, the new tech/athletic line, and clearance all appear in one send, giving different shopper types a reason to scroll.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, bright on-brand palette throughout.
- **The Tech Fit section creates genuine novelty** — the dark athletic-palette block breaks the summer-brights monotony and signals something new versus the "$6 tees" fatigue.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is punishing open rates** — this is the fifth to sixth promotional email in roughly 36 hours; "60% OFF" reads as desperation, not excitement, at this frequency.
- **Subject relies on hype ("TOO good") rather than the number** — "60% OFF Summer Steals" without the filler front-matter would be tighter and more credible.
- **No loyalty or Super Cash signal visible** — Old Navy's NavyHouse members are a high-LTV segment; the email doesn't surface any member-exclusive framing.
- **No single hero product** — the grid approach works for browsing but limits the impulse-click moment; there's nothing with a face or name to anchor desire.
- **Preview text is not legible / likely leaking boilerplate** — if the preheader is just "view in browser" text, that's a wasted inbox impression on top of an already fatigued list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "This deal is TOO good —" from the subject entirely.** "60% OFF Summer Steals — 2 Days Only" front-loads the number and the deadline. Hype softens specificity; specificity wins in a crowded inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% Off Summer Steals — 2 Days Only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off: summer dresses, tanks & more`
- 2. **Write a real preheader that doesn't repeat the subject.** If the preview is currently "view in browser" junk, swap it for something that does incremental work — naming a category or sealing the urgency.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, tanks, polos — all in. Ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Tech Fit + 60% off summer — two reasons to click.`
- 3. **Give Tech Fit its own send or a stronger in-email call-out.** Burying a new product line inside a clearance-and-discount email is a poor debut. It deserves its own moment or at minimum a section header that names and explains it.
- 4. **Add Super Cash or loyalty framing for members.** Even one sentence — "NavyHouse members, stack your Super Cash on top" — activates the highest-intent segment separately from price-only shoppers.
- 5. **Slow the cadence.** Four sends in 36 hours on the same $6 tees anchor, followed immediately by a new 60% off send, trains the list to wait. A one-day gap with a single subject variation would have preserved more urgency equity for this 60% offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Duplicate CSS class selectors in mobile media query**

`.MspaceTop` is defined twice — once under `.space-top5` and again under `.space-top10`. Same for `.MspaceBot`:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}   /* overridden */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* wins */
.space-bot5, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 5px;} /* overridden */
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;} /* wins */
```

Any element using `MspaceTop` or `MspaceBot` alone will always resolve to `10px` regardless of intent. Elements that need 5px mobile spacing are broken.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**

The `cp.oldnavy.com` pixel has no `alt` attribute at all (distinct from the `click.email.oldnavy.com` open pixel which correctly carries `alt=""`). Screen readers will attempt to announce the URL string.
