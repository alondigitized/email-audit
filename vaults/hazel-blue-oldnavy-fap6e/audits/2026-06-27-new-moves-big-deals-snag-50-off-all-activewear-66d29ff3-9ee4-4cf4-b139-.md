# New moves, BIG deals! Snag 50% off ALL activewear
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This send breaks the multi-week "50% off everything" marathon pattern with a category-specific hook — activewear — which is a real creative gear-shift relative to the six consecutive sends audited through June 22. The subject finally stops leaning on the entire-store discount and puts a specific department front-and-center, which makes it the most differentiated Old Navy subject line in this streak. The email body stacks multiple deal blocks: a "2 DAYS ONLY / 50%" top banner, a "40%–70% OFF DEEP DEALS" product grid (appears to feature sandals and footwear), a "Bold New Deals" clothing section, a hero activewear block, and a back-to-school Uniforms close. That's a lot of freight for one scroll.
- The activewear hero is the clearest visual moment in the email and does match the subject promise. The problem is it competes with at least three other promotion blocks above and below it — the eye has no natural resting point. The email reads like a multi-department sale masquerading as an activewear send. The urgency signal ("2 DAYS ONLY") at the top is the strongest motivating element and is visible without scrolling.
- Verdict: better subject than the prior wave, middling execution. The category specificity earns it goodwill the body doesn't fully cash.

## What's working

- **Subject specificity** — "50% off ALL activewear" is the first department-specific hook in this streak; it creates genuine differentiation from the "50% off everything" rut.
- **Urgency is front-loaded** — "2 DAYS ONLY!" banner is at the very top; deadline pressure lands before any scrolling.
- **Activewear hero is visually prominent** — lifestyle model shot in athletic wear mid-email delivers on the subject promise and has good size/contrast.
- **Deal range is clear** — the "40%–70% OFF DEEP DEALS" block with product thumbnails gives shoppers a concrete savings floor and ceiling.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no visible broken images, overlapping elements, or layout collapse; brand logo and colors are consistent throughout.

## What's weak

- **Activewear buried mid-scroll** — the category promised in the subject doesn't appear until after at least two other promo blocks; the top of the email is sandals/footwear, not activewear.
- **Too many deal tiers competing** — 50% off, 40–70% off, and the uniforms section at the bottom create discount fatigue before the primary offer lands.
- **"Bold New Deals" section is vague** — this block adds visual noise without a specific department or product hook; no clear reason to click it.
- **Uniforms close feels like a different email** — back-to-school uniforms at the bottom is a topic shift that dilutes the activewear narrative and signals Old Navy is blasting a multi-purpose template rather than a targeted send.
- **No social proof or loyalty signal visible** — no ratings, no Old Navy Cash / member pricing callout, despite prior sends in this wave having done better on that dimension.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with activewear, not sandals** — reorder so the activewear hero (or a product grid of activewear) is the first visual below the fold. The subject promises it; the body doesn't deliver it until mid-scroll. This one structural change closes the biggest promise-vs-delivery gap.
- 2. **Retire or delay the uniforms section** — it's a different audience, a different season signal, and it undercuts the "new moves" energy. Save it for a dedicated back-to-school send.
- 3. **Collapse to one deal narrative** — "2 DAYS ONLY 50% off activewear" is a complete, compelling message. The secondary "40–70% Deep Deals" block fragments the CTA. If it must stay, put it below the activewear block, not above it.
- 4. **Add a product-specific anchor** — show one or two specific activewear items (leggings, sports bra, joggers) with the price crossed out; makes the 50% promise concrete and shoppable in a single glance.
- 5. **Preheader should name a specific activewear item or the deadline**, not repeat the subject or default to boilerplate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off activewear — 2 days only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your new workout haul: 50% off all activewear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Leggings, sports bras, joggers — all half off. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before the deal ends — 48 hours of 50% off activewear.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — Missing alt text on CDP pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/f3f22718...">` has no `alt` attribute. For a tracking pixel this is low severity, but it triggers accessibility linters and some corporate mail proxies flag untagged images.

**CSS duplicate selectors**

The second declaration silently overwrites the first at the `<639px` breakpoint. This likely has no visible effect given the values are the same, but it's dead code that creates maintenance confusion.

**VML namespace declared, no VML usage detected**
`xmlns:v` and `xmlns:o` are present on `<html>`. No VML shapes appear in the truncated source. If the full email has no VML, these declarations are dead weight.

**No issues found** with baseline responsive structure — single 639px breakpoint, `max-width: 640px` wrapper, and Outlook conditional comments are all present and correct.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Major inbox providers (Gmail, Apple Mail, Yahoo) use this header to show the native unsubscribe button. Its absence increases spam complaint rates. If it exists upstream but is stripped by the relay, it needs relay configuration review; if it genuinely isn't set, this is a deliverability defect.

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender guidelines for >5k/day senders since Feb 2024) is not confirmed present. Old Navy almost certainly sends above that threshold.

**WARN — No physical mailing address detected in parsed content**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML is truncated — this may appear in the footer — but the QA scanner did not find it in the extracted text. Needs manual footer verification against the full source.

**Authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`. SPF/DKIM pass status is unknown from available data — infrastructure-level verification required.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameter coverage cannot be confirmed without probing the 23 click-tracked URLs through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. The redirect chain (ESP click tracker → destination) is opaque. If UTM parameters are appended at the ESP redirect layer rather than hardcoded in the HTML, they will not appear in source inspection but should be present on final destination URLs. **Recommend spot-checking 2–3 CTAs end-to-end to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` survive the redirect.**
