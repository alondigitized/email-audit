# Summer deals from just $2
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "Summer deals from just $2" is a deliberate gear-shift from the 50%-off-everything marathon Old Navy has been running since mid-June. The $2 price anchor in the subject is the strongest creative choice in this entire send — it's concrete, it's different from the eleven-plus identical percentage-discount lines that preceded it, and it earns a second look from a fatigued inbox. Credit where it's due.
- The email body, however, doesn't honor that promise. What the subject implies is a curated rack of genuinely low-ticket summer items; what the email delivers is a maximalist scroll — a hero banner for "UP TO 70% OFF DEEP DEALS," a product grid of sandals and apparel, a loud "HOT NEW GEAR!" back-to-school section, and a "TODAY ONLY!" section at the bottom with a $6 price point. The creative is pulling in three different campaign directions at once, and none of them land cleanly.
- The "TODAY ONLY" urgency at the bottom is real lift, but it's buried after multiple scrolls. A subscriber looking for the $2 deals teased in the subject will have to hunt. For a brand this deep into a promotional blitz, the email does just enough to survive — but it squanders the strongest subject line in weeks.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the sharpest in the brand's recent run.** "From just $2" is concrete and specific — a genuine break from the 50%-off cadence, and under 30 characters.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" urgency is present** in the lower module — a credible scarcity signal that prior sends in this wave lacked.
- **Visual variety across sections** — the colorful "HOT NEW GEAR!" module breaks the monotony of the standard product grid and signals a new campaign chapter.
- **UP TO 70% off** is a stronger headline number than the 50% that's been in every prior send; stepping up the discount level gives fatigued subscribers a reason to re-engage.

## What's weak

- **The $2 claim is invisible in the body hero.** The subject anchors to $2 but the visible hero leads with "UP TO 70% OFF" — the two messages don't connect, and the payoff for clicking is unclear.
- **Three campaigns in one scroll.** Deep deals, Hot New Gear, and a Today Only module feel like three separate emails stitched together. No single offer dominates.
- **"TODAY ONLY" urgency is buried at the bottom** — the most compelling clickdriver appears after two other sections and requires significant scroll on mobile.
- **No single hero product or category.** Previous strong sends (June 21 and June 23) at least had a clear triple-deal stack up front. This email has no equivalent anchor.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is send eleven-plus in a continuous promotional wave; even a creative pivot doesn't fully overcome the trust erosion from back-to-back-to-back promos.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the TODAY ONLY module to the top.** The single biggest lift available: put the time-bounded offer ($6 or $2 price-point items) immediately below the hero so it pays off the subject's promise before the subscriber scrolls past it.
- 2. **Build a dedicated "$2-and-up" curated grid up front.** If the subject promises "from just $2," show five or six $2–$5 items immediately — flip flops, tees, basics — so the hero earns the subject line.
- 3. **Collapse to one campaign message.** Pick deep deals OR back-to-school gear; running both creates decision paralysis. The HOT NEW GEAR module should be its own send next week.
- 4. **Give the TODAY ONLY section a visible countdown timer or end-time.** "Today Only" alone is weak — "Ends tonight at midnight" is a click trigger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$2 tees. $5 sandals. Today only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: summer deals from $2`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flip flops, tees, and more — all under $10. Grab them before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off — and some styles start at just $2. Ends tonight.`
