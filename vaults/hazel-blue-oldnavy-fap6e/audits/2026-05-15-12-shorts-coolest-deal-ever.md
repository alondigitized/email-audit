# $12 shorts? Coolest deal EVER
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy firing a textbook urgency-promo on a Thursday: "$12 shorts, TODAY ONLY" as the hero, then a layered 50%-off deal section, a "Dresses Everyone's Obsessing Over" social-proof module, and a Clearance stack at the bottom. The structure is identical to the May 14 crinkle-gauze send — same multi-promo scaffolding, same TODAY ONLY badge logic. That's not a criticism of the execution, which is clean, but it is a signal of formula fatigue for anyone who opened yesterday's email too.
- The hero works. "From $8 → $12 shorts, TODAY ONLY" is a clear, credible, specific offer, and the visual hierarchy delivers it immediately — big badge, big price, model in summer clothing. The problem is everything below it: the $75+ threshold add-on, the 50%-off grid, the dress module, and the clearance repeat all compete for the same attention. The longer you scroll, the more it feels like a circular from 2018 rather than a targeted send.
- Judgement: solidly competent, but the fifth promo in five days means it lands with diminishing urgency. The subject line is the strongest asset here — it earns the open even if the body doesn't convert with the same efficiency.

## What's working

- **Hero price is impossible to miss** — "TODAY ONLY / from $8 / $12" reads in under a second; the model and badge reinforce summer/shorts immediately.
- **Subject line earns the open** — "$12 shorts? Coolest deal EVER" is 30 characters, has a specific price, and the question-mark cadence feels conversational rather than spammy.
- **Urgency is credible** — TODAY ONLY isn't buried; it anchors the hero zone. Contrast with the May 13 Disney collection send, which had no time signal at all.
- **Seasonal alignment** — Shorts + summer palette is timely for mid-May; fits the pre-Memorial Day moment.
- **No visible render bugs** — Layout is clean top to bottom; images load, text doesn't bleed.

## What's weak

- **Multi-promo stack dilutes the hero** — The $75+ threshold modifier (Extra 50% Off When You Spend $75+) immediately undercuts the "$12 shorts" promise. The hero says cheap; the next line says "spend more."
- **Clearance triple-repeat feels desperate** — "Go. Go. Go. Clearance Clearance Clearance" at the bottom reads like markdown panic, not confidence in the offer.
- **CTA copy not legible at render size** — Buttons are present, but at mobile thumbnail scale the copy is indistinct; if the button says "Shop Now" (likely), that's a wasted specificity opportunity.
- **Cadence problem** — This is the fourth email in five days from Old Navy. Even a good offer starts feeling like noise at this frequency; the May 14 50%-off crinkle-gauze send set a high bar that "$12 shorts" only matches, not exceeds.
- **No loyalty/member signal visible** — Old Navy has a NavyCard/rewards program; nothing in the render surfaces it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the $75+ modifier from the hero zone** — Lead with the $12 shorts cleanly; move the threshold offer below the fold. Stacking a "spend more" condition directly under a "deal!" badge is self-defeating.
- 2. **Make the CTA button copy product-specific** — "Shop $12 Shorts" beats "Shop Now" or "Discover." One verb, one noun, one offer.
- 3. **Consolidate the clearance section** — One tight clearance row is fine; the triple "Clearance Clearance Clearance" reads as clutter not energy.
- 4. **Surface the loyalty angle** — Even one line ("NavyCard members: extra 5% back") gives loyal subscribers a reason to feel seen.
- 5. **Throttle cadence** — The signal would be stronger on a day where subscribers haven't already opened a 50%-off email yesterday.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Grab $12 shorts before noon (seriously)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off summer deals storewide — no code needed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shorts from $8 · dresses under $25 · TODAY ONLY`
