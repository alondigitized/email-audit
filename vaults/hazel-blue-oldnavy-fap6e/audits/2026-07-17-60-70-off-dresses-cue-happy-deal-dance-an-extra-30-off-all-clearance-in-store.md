# 60-70% OFF dresses (Cue happy deal dance) + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a straight-up re-run of yesterday's July 16 send — same 60-70% off dresses + extra 30% off in-store clearance combo, just with "Cue happy deal dance" swapped in for "Time to twirl!" Old Navy burned the dress story once and is now hoping a different subject hook gets the opens from whoever didn't bite on day one. As a tactic it's defensible; as subscriber experience it's exhausting. The email itself is well-constructed — a clearance urgency banner at the top, a strong dress hero, VIP LOUNGE member callout, then a long scroll through $9 product tiles, $3.99 clearance, and a "Shorts Season" module — but the sheer length and number of competing offers means the dress hook promised in the subject line gets lost well before the fold.
- The VIP LOUNGE layer is the most interesting thing here — it signals member-exclusive access or early pricing on the dress sale — but at this render resolution it reads as a visual badge rather than a call to action with teeth. If Old Navy has a loyalty program, this email underdoes the membership angle.
- Verdict: competent execution of a repeat deal, but the cadence is burning goodwill and the multi-module structure undercuts the single-offer clarity the subject line promises.

## What's working

- **60-70% off is a genuine hook** — the discount range is specific, credible, and lands immediately in both the subject and the hero image.
- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency banner** is prominent at the top and creates a real time constraint that wasn't in the July 16 version's subject — a small but meaningful upgrade.
- **VIP LOUNGE branding** on the hero image signals member benefits, which adds perceived value for loyalty customers.
- **Women's models throughout** match the target demo cleanly — the visual casting is consistent with the dress and shorts focus.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breakage, no obvious junk text visible.

## What's weak

- **Same deal, second day** — yesterday's send (July 16, scored 7/10 here) ran the identical 60-70% off dresses + extra 30% clearance offer. Subscribers who didn't act yesterday are now being re-pitched with a punny subject swap, not a new offer.
- **Subject line is 81+ characters** — way beyond mobile-safe ~50 chars. "(Cue happy deal dance)" is charming but burns characters that could carry the urgency ("2 DAYS ONLY") into the inbox preview.
- **Visual hierarchy reversal** — the subject leads with dresses, but the email leads with the extra 30% clearance banner. The most prominent above-the-fold message doesn't match what got you to open.
- **No price anchoring visible** — the "$9" and "$3.99" modules appear mid-scroll with no "was/now" framing visible, making the value feel asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **"Shorts Season" module dilutes the dress story** — adding a second category halfway down the email splits attention and contradicts the subject line's single-offer promise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the offer into one subject line with urgency baked in** — "2 days only" belongs in the subject, not only the banner. The parenthetical charm is fine but it shouldn't cost you the deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 days only: dresses 60-70% off + extra 30% off clearance`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dresses deal expires soon — 60-70% off, today + tomorrow only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `VIP members get first pick — shop the sale before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Prices this low don't last — 60-70% off and the clock is ticking.`
- 2. **Match the email's opening to the subject** — if dresses are the lead, the dress hero should come before the clearance banner, not after. Swap the module order so the 60-70% off hero is the first visual after the logo.
- 3. **Cut or demote the Shorts Season module** — this email should be a dress campaign with clearance support. A separate shorts story deserves its own send (and frankly, there's time in the calendar for it).
- 4. **Make the VIP LOUNGE CTA explicit** — if members get better pricing or earlier access, say so in copy ("VIP members: your exclusive early access"). Right now it's a badge without a payoff.
- 5. **Don't send the same deal twice** — if day-two re-sends are in the cadence, make the offer escalate (add a category, drop the floor price, add a free-ship threshold) rather than repackaging with a new pun. Subscribers notice.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — `cp.oldnavy.com/o/` resolves to unknown path type `/g/`**

The QA probe followed the redirect chain for:
```
https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/05e9715d-f13b-4677-b554-80f2515ef9bc?mi_mid=019f7060...
```
The final resolved URL landed on a `/g/` path, which the checker flagged as `unknown url type`. This is likely a Cheetah/Salesforce Marketing Cloud landing page that either expired, was misconfigured, or requires a valid session cookie to resolve. This link appears in the preheader/hidden tracking block, so it may be an impression pixel rather than a user-facing CTA — but it should be confirmed and either removed or pointed at a valid endpoint.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links skipped**

All click links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for these, so their final destinations are unverified in this audit. No specific link failures were reported, but destination health is unconfirmed.

**Open pixel** — `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx?QUKMOTKQ5BOEPA5SHSRPZU2RUQ.60268&d=60268&bmt=0` — standard 1×1 pixel, no issues.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS selectors**

Two media-query classes are defined twice in the responsive block, which is harmless but sloppy and can cause specificity confusion if the sheet is ever modified:
- `.MspaceTop` appears under both `.space-top5` and `.space-top10`
- `.MspaceBot` appears under both `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10`

**Outlook `line-height: 0px` override**

The MSO conditional comment sets `td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }` globally. This is a known hack for Outlook height bugs, but it clobbers any td with text that doesn't have an explicit inline `line-height`. Confirm all text-bearing tds have inline overrides.

**Missing alt text on tracked image**

```
src: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/05e9715d-...
```
This image element lacks an `alt` attribute entirely (the open-pixel has `alt=""` correctly; this one does not). If it renders visible in any client, it will show a broken-image icon with no fallback text.

**No issues** with charset declaration (`UTF-8`), viewport meta, or IE compatibility meta.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens visible in the provided HTML excerpt. The email appears to be a flat broadcast with no recipient-level personalization (no `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `{{name}}`, or equivalent). The `mi_mid` and `mi_cid` parameters in tracking URLs are message/contact IDs injected by the ESP, not content personalization — no render-time token risk.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not captured**

The relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Apple Mail use this for the native unsubscribe button; its absence degrades deliverability reputation with both ISPs. This may be a relay capture gap rather than a true omission — verify against raw SMTP headers from the ESP send log.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not captured (RFC 8058)**

One-click machine unsubscribe (required by Gmail's February 2024 bulk sender policy for lists >5k/day) is not confirmed present. Same caveat as above — check the raw headers.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. The HTML is truncated, so this may exist in the footer below the cutoff. If absent, it is a legal compliance gap, not just a best practice.

**Authentication** — SPF/DKIM status unknown; `Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm signing domain alignment with `oldnavy.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The tracking URLs use ESP-native parameters (`mi_mid`, `mi_cid`, `cp_cid`, `cp_tp`) rather than standard UTM parameters. If Old Navy's analytics stack relies on GA4 or a similar tool expecting `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign`, click traffic from this send will land as direct/none in site analytics — no campaign attribution.

The encoded `cp_tp` blob appears to be a base64+zlib payload (Salesforce MC `personalization` token), so landing page personalization may still function on the server side even without UTM params. However, cross-channel attribution in any non-Salesforce analytics tool will be blind to this send.
