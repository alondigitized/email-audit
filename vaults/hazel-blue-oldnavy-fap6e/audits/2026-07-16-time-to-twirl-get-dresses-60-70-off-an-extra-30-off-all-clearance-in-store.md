# 👗Time to twirl! Get dresses 60-70% OFF + an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy returns to full discount mode one day after the July 15 editorial break ("Behind the seams" Low Rise jean story, 7/10 here). This send pivots to dresses at 60–70% off with a stacked clearance add-on (extra 30% off), and the VIP Lounge loyalty segment makes a reappearance. The problem is the same one that dragged the July 13–14 tank/clearance sends down to 5–6/10: the email leads with the clearance header ("Extra 30% Off All Clearance / 3 DAYS ONLY!") before the dress hero, which buries the subject line's actual promise. If you opened because of dresses, the clearance banner greeting you first is a small but real friction point.
- The dress photography is genuinely appealing — light, summery, and styled for a warm-weather scroll. But the email quickly fans out into three or four distinct modules (dresses, clearance from $3.99, VIP Lounge, Hot Weather Gear, kids) and loses its through-line. This is a solid mid-tier promo send: the offer math is good, the creative is clean, the urgency is credible, and it will convert dress-seekers. It just does too many things to do any one thing memorably.
- Verdict: competent discount email, a half-step above the July 13–14 cadence fatigue because dresses are a fresh category and the 3-day clock is ticking, but it doesn't match the editorial ambition of July 15.

## What's working

- **Dress imagery is strong** — the hero model shot in a summery dress is warm, styled well, and visually earns the category.
- **Two-tier discount math is clear** — 60–70% off dresses stacked with extra 30% off clearance reads as genuinely compelling savings without confusion.
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" urgency block is visible and credible** — unlike vague "limited time" copy, a defined window gives subscribers a reason to act today.
- **VIP Lounge segment adds loyalty value** — surfacing member benefits mid-email rewards engaged subscribers and differentiates from purely transactional sends.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — all images loaded, text is legible, no overlapping or broken layout elements.

## What's weak

- **The clearance header opens the email, not the dress hero** — the subject promised dresses; the email opens on "Extra 30% Off All Clearance." Mismatched lead creates an instant friction beat.
- **Subject line is too long for mobile** — at ~80+ characters, the back half ("+ an extra 30% off all Clearance in-store") almost certainly truncates on most devices.
- **Too many modules dilute the dress story** — clearance from $3.99 → VIP Lounge → Hot Weather Gear → kids content is four pivots inside one email, each pulling attention away from dresses.
- **No product-level specificity** — no featured dress with a name, price, or linked PDP. Everything goes to category pages. Low intent signal for a click.
- **Returning to promo format after one editorial day** — the July 15 Low Rise send built narrative equity; this resets to discount mode and will feel routine to frequent openers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reorder the email so the dress hero opens first** — move the "Extra 30% Off All Clearance" ribbon below the dress hero block or fold it into the header as a secondary line. The subject promised dresses; honor that promise above the fold.
- 2. **Cut to two modules maximum** — dress hero + clearance call-out. Drop Hot Weather Gear and kids to a separate send. Every module you add halves the dress message's impact.
- 3. **Feature one specific dress** — name it, price it, link it. "The flutter-sleeve midi, now $22" drives clicks better than "dresses 60–70% off."
- 4. **Trim the subject line to under 55 chars** — the in-store tag is wasting mobile real estate.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your dress moment: up to 70% off, 3 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on $50+. Flutter hems, linen sets, maxis — all on Clearance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `VIP members: stack your extra savings on 1,000+ dress styles.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The Contentsquare/personalization pixel resolves to an internal redirect (`/g/9491c59d-31aa-40ca-916f-e4c498cd7880?txn=93de8880-...`) that returns an unknown URL type. The full URL:

This pixel also appears inline in `<div style="display:none">` — the redirect failure means open-tracking and personalization attribution for this send are broken for any recipient where this fires.

**[WARN] 23 click links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud redirect). Not a defect, but upstream click-tracking health can't be confirmed from this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on tracking pixel**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/9491c59d-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

Empty `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative/tracking images per WCAG, but the QA checker flagged it — confirm this is intentional and not a templating gap that should carry descriptive text on content images.

**No issues** with responsive breakpoint logic (`@media max-width: 639px`), Outlook VML conditionals, or the `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` fix. Desktop/mobile content toggling via `.desktop-content`/`.mobile-content` classes is correctly structured.

**Minor:** `MspaceTop` and `MspaceBot` class names are defined twice in the responsive block (with different `padding-top`/`padding-bottom` values). The second definition wins, silently overriding the first. Low risk but worth cleaning up to avoid confusion.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) visible in the truncated source. The `mi_mid` parameter in the pixel URL (`019f69f0-8c00-7000-b7c2-d1e3313df5a7`) appears to be a rendered subscriber ID, not an unfired token — consistent with correct send-time personalization.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected**

CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The QA scan found none in the captured source. Either:
- It's present in the truncated portion not provided, or
- It's genuinely missing — a CAN-SPAM violation risk.

**Verify** the footer of the full HTML contains an address block before clearing this.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers not captured**

RFC 2369 (`List-Unsubscribe`) and RFC 8058 (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`) headers are absent from what the AgentMail relay surfaced. These are almost certainly present on the actual MTA-level delivery (SFMC adds them by default), but the relay isn't forwarding them. This is a **relay configuration gap**, not necessarily a sender defect — but it means one-click unsubscribe compliance can't be verified through this pipeline.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**

SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown. Same root cause as above — relay is not forwarding authentication headers. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` sending via `email.oldnavy.com` should be covered by Gap Inc.'s SPF record and DKIM signing, but this pipeline cannot confirm it.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected** on the 23 click links — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirects. SFMC click tracking typically appends UTMs at redirect time based on campaign metadata, so this may be fine. However, if UTM passthrough isn't configured in the SFMC send definition, GA4/analytics will attribute resulting sessions to `(direct)` rather than the email campaign.

**Risk:** Cannot confirm UTM values without probing a live redirect — the 23 click links were skipped by the QA checker. Spot-check one resolved URL to confirm `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...` survives the redirect chain.
