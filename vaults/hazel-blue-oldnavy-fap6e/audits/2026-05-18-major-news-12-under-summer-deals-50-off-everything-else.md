# Major news: $12 & under summer deals + 50% off everything else!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy is now seven emails deep in a five-day blitz that has rotated through "$6 tees/$5 tanks," "50% off everything," and "$12 shorts" — and this send tries to bridge the last two: "$12 & under summer faves + 50% off everything else." The in-email execution is solid: a clean TODAY ONLY hero, a legible 50%-off section, a dress feature with a real SHOP NOW CTA, and a useful "shop clearance by your size" grid at the bottom. If this were the second email of the week instead of the seventh, it would score well top-to-bottom.
- The brand strategy problem is now fully visible in the subject line. "Major news" is what you write when you have nothing new to say. After May 17's "(yes, really!)" — a subject that essentially confessed to cadence fatigue — this send doubles down with a faux-announcement hook for the same promotional formula the subscriber has already received in five prior emails. The hook will register as noise for anyone paying attention.
- The email skews heavily female in execution: the hero copy may be gender-neutral, but the body leads with dresses and the clearance grid is exclusively petite sizing. The $12 & under angle is the strongest differentiator versus the previous sends; it's just buried under branding copy that telegraphs nothing new.

## What's working

- **TODAY ONLY hero is clean and immediate** — price point, urgency badge, and product images all visible above the fold.
- **"$12 & Under" is the freshest offer in the recent cadence** — a sharper anchor than "50% off everything" alone.
- **Shop Clearance In Your Size grid** is genuinely useful UX — presenting clickable size filters removes a step between inbox and conversion.
- **50% Off section** uses contrasting dark background to break visual monotony and create a second scroll-stop.
- **New Dresses module** has a real SHOP NOW button on a hero image, clean composition, no render issues.

## What's weak

- **"Major news" prefix is dishonest** — this is the same deal family sent since May 14; calling it news after "(yes, really!)" last email is a brand-credibility hit.
- **Subject line is 63 characters** — truncates on mobile before "50% off everything else!" even renders.
- **Clearance grid is petite-only** — excludes the majority of the subscriber base from the most personalized section in the email.
- **No loyalty or member pricing called out** — a missed hook given Old Navy Rewards exists and a member benefit would differentiate from the open-internet deal.
- **Cadence is unsustainable** — seven emails in five days with the same promotional DNA trains subscribers to wait for the next one rather than act on this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Major news" — put the time limit in the subject.** "Today only" + price + deadline is more credible than a fake announcement frame.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: $12 summer faves + 50% off everything`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$12 tees, tanks & more — TODAY ONLY + 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shorts, tanks, swim — all $12 & under. Deal ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your summer wardrobe under $12 — plus half off everything else today.`
- 2. **Open the clearance grid to all sizes, or add a "shop your size" input field** — limiting to petite immediately signals to everyone else that the most personalized section isn't for them.
- 3. **Add a Rewards/member callout** — even one line ("Rewards members earn 5× points today") converts the time-limited promo into a loyalty event and justifies the email volume.
- 4. **Merge this with the prior send or hold it one more day** — the marginal revenue from a 7th send will not offset the unsubscribe acceleration. A 48-hour gap between this and the May 17 send would have allowed "Major news" to feel earned.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on CDP tracking pixel**
`<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/7665bff9-...">` has no `alt` attribute. Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` (empty string); omitting the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to announce the full URL.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS selector in media query**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice inside `@media only screen and (max-width: 639px)`. The inline comment in the source acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`. The second rule silently overrides the first; confirm the final padding values are intentional.

**[INFO] Outlook conditional `line-height:0px` on `td`**
The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height:0px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly }`. This is a known Outlook height-fix pattern but can collapse text cells if a `td` lacks an explicit `mso-line-height-rule` override. No confirmed rendering breakage from the truncated source, but worth regression-testing in Outlook 2019/365.

**[INFO] Invalid `<meta>` inside `<div>` for schema.org**
```html
<div itemscope itemtype="http://schema.org/Organization">
  <meta content="Old Navy" itemprop="name">
```
`<meta>` is not a valid child of `<div>` per HTML spec. Gmail's schema.org parser is tolerant of this pattern, but it may fail in stricter parsers.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo (as of Feb 2024 bulk sender requirements) require this header for >5k/day senders. May be a relay capture gap, but should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Absence of this header means Gmail will not render its native one-click unsubscribe UI, degrading deliverability scores.

**[WARN] Physical mailing address not detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. The HTML was truncated; if the footer containing the address was cut off, this may be a false positive. Verify the rendered footer includes a street address.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this capture. `From` domain is `oldnavy.com`; sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`. A DKIM alignment mismatch between these two domains would cause Gmail to show a "via" qualifier, reducing trust signals.
