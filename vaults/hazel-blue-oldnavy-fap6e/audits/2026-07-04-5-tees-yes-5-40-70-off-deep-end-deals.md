# $5 TEES (Yes, $5!) + 40-70% OFF DEEP END DEALS
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy runs back the exact same tee-price hero from July 3 — same child model in a pink tee, same "$4 / $5 / $6" tiered price grid, same rainbow of color swatches — and wraps it in a new subject line with a parenthetical wink ("Yes, $5!"). The rhetorical device is smart in isolation; it signals disbelief-worthy pricing and carries a conversational tone. But anyone who opened yesterday's email will recognize this as a near-identical creative, which undercuts the freshness.
- What saves this send is the "Deep End Deals" module below the hero: a legit 40–70% off clearance rack starting at $2, with a full product grid showing women's apparel, a Pockets & Bras from $10 callout, and an All-Day Stretch activewear section. That's meaningful added value over yesterday. The summer pool metaphor ("Deep End Deals") is seasonally sharp on July 4th and differentiates it from the generic "% off everything" language that dominated Old Navy's June sends.
- Net judgement: a competent send with real value in the clearance module, let down by hero creative fatigue. If you skipped yesterday, this works well. If you opened yesterday, the déjà vu is real.

## What's working

- The "$4 / $5 / $6" price ladder is legible and arresting — dollar anchors at commodity-low levels are hard to ignore.
- "3 DAYS ONLY!" badge on the hero creates credible urgency for the tee deal.
- "Deep End Deals" is the best copy in the email — seasonal (summer pool), specific (40–70%), and anchored with a "$2 starting" floor.
- Color swatch rainbow below the tee hero is a strong visual that signals breadth without showing 30 SKUs.
- The All-Day Stretch activewear section gives the email a second content beat for a different buyer intent, extending scroll value.

## What's weak

- Hero creative is a direct repeat of July 3 — same model, same price grid, same swatches. Two-day hero fatigue is a real inbox risk.
- Subject ALL-CAPS ("TEES," "DEEP END DEALS") edges toward promotional spam styling; the parenthetical saves the tone but the caps undercut it.
- The "773 New Arrivals from + 15" module is visually crowded and the copy is truncated/unclear — "from + 15" reads as a rendering artifact.
- No visible CTA buttons at the section level in the Deep End Deals grid — relies on the user knowing to tap product images, which adds friction.
- Loyalty or member pricing is absent; no NavyCard benefit callout despite Old Navy running a co-branded card program.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Refresh the hero model/shot.** Even swapping to a different color tee or an adult model breaks the Groundhog Day effect. The price grid can stay — it's working.
- 2. **Move "Deep End Deals" to lead.** The 40–70% pool metaphor is the most seasonally differentiated idea in this email. On July 4th it earns hero position; $5 tees become the secondary module.
- 3. **Add explicit CTA buttons to the Deep End Deals grid.** "Shop $2 Deals" or "See All Clearance" below the grid removes friction for buyers who want the deep cuts.
- 4. **Fix or kill the "773 New Arrivals from + 15" module.** The truncated copy damages polish. Either render the full stat or replace with a clean "New Arrivals →" link.
- 5. **Add a NavyCard member line.** Even one sentence — "NavyCard members: extra 5% back on every order" — lifts relevance for a segment that converts at higher rates.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `A whole pool of markdowns — tops, bras, activewear — starting at $2.`
