# You heard right! You've earned some welcome deals!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's welcome series opener — triggered by a new account signup or first purchase — and it does what welcome emails are supposed to do: orient the subscriber, establish brand voice, tease the value prop, and introduce the loyalty program. The problem is it tries to do all of that simultaneously without a single clear conversion moment. "Welcome deals" is in the subject line, but the email's hero says "Welcome to the Old Navy Fam" with no offer in sight. The subscriber showed up expecting deals and the first thing they see is a brand statement.
- The product grid at the bottom is the closest thing to actionable content, but by the time you reach it you've scrolled past three lifestyle-editorial modules. For a casual, price-aware shopper — the type who responds to "earned some deals" — this email front-loads brand feelings and back-loads the actual offer. That's the wrong order.
- Serviceable welcome email. Does nothing wrong technically, but misses a clear opportunity to convert a warm, high-intent moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the actual welcome offer in the hero.** If there's a "welcome deal" (even 10% off, free shipping, a Navyist points bonus), put it in the hero with a deadline. Right now the hero is purely brand-feeling. Swap it for a hero that says "Here's your welcome offer — 20% off, 48 hours."
- 2. **Tighten the module sequence.** Three lifestyle editorial modules before the product grid is too much preamble. Cut or collapse "Deals In Our DNA" — the name promises a deal that isn't there.
- 3. **Add a visible CTA button in the hero.** The welcome hero has no immediately clear call-to-action button with specific copy. It should say "Shop Your Welcome Deals" or similar, above the fold.
- 4. **Anchor the product grid to the persona.** The grid mixes Women's, Men's, and Toddler Girl in one scroll — unfocused for a new subscriber whose category preferences aren't yet established. Prioritize one section or lead with "bestsellers."
- 5. **Subject line is too long and soft.** "You heard right! You've earned some welcome deals!" is 51 chars, uses two exclamations, and is vague about what the deal actually is. Make it concrete.

## Full review
## 1. Take

This is Old Navy's welcome series opener — triggered by a new account signup or first purchase — and it does what welcome emails are supposed to do: orient the subscriber, establish brand voice, tease the value prop, and introduce the loyalty program. The problem is it tries to do all of that simultaneously without a single clear conversion moment. "Welcome deals" is in the subject line, but the email's hero says "Welcome to the Old Navy Fam" with no offer in sight. The subscriber showed up expecting deals and the first thing they see is a brand statement.

The product grid at the bottom is the closest thing to actionable content, but by the time you reach it you've scrolled past three lifestyle-editorial modules. For a casual, price-aware shopper — the type who responds to "earned some deals" — this email front-loads brand feelings and back-loads the actual offer. That's the wrong order.

Serviceable welcome email. Does nothing wrong technically, but misses a clear opportunity to convert a warm, high-intent moment.

## 2. What stood out

The hero "Welcome To The Old Navy Fam!" is visually clean — a lifestyle shot of a woman in bold blue denim against a floral backdrop, which at least communicates the brand aesthetic immediately. The font is large and readable. Below it, the three content modules — "Jeans With A Reputation," "Deals In Our DNA," and "Perks Worth Raving About" — are well-sequenced in theory: product → offer → loyalty. But in execution each module is a lifestyle photograph plus a headline, with no visible offer attached. "Deals In Our DNA" is a module name, not a deal. The loyalty perks section ("Perks Worth Raving About") is a genuine win — surfacing the rewards program in a welcome email is smart and it's visible without excessive scrolling. The product grid near the bottom is the most concrete part of the email: product shots, category labels (Men's, Toddler Girl), and visible prices. That's useful, but it's buried. No visible percent-off callout, no "welcome 20% off" badge, no deadline in the body — yet the subject explicitly says "earned some welcome deals," which sets an expectation this email then fails to meet at the hero level.

## 3. What I'd change

1. **Lead with the actual welcome offer in the hero.** If there's a "welcome deal" (even 10% off, free shipping, a Navyist points bonus), put it in the hero with a deadline. Right now the hero is purely brand-feeling. Swap it for a hero that says "Here's your welcome offer — 20% off, 48 hours."
2. **Tighten the module sequence.** Three lifestyle editorial modules before the product grid is too much preamble. Cut or collapse "Deals In Our DNA" — the name promises a deal that isn't there.
3. **Add a visible CTA button in the hero.** The welcome hero has no immediately clear call-to-action button with specific copy. It should say "Shop Your Welcome Deals" or similar, above the fold.
4. **Anchor the product grid to the persona.** The grid mixes Women's, Men's, and Toddler Girl in one scroll — unfocused for a new subscriber whose category preferences aren't yet established. Prioritize one section or lead with "bestsellers."
5. **Subject line is too long and soft.** "You heard right! You've earned some welcome deals!" is 51 chars, uses two exclamations, and is vague about what the deal actually is. Make it concrete.

   - **Subject Alt A:** `Your welcome offer is inside — expires soon`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Welcome! Here's 20% off to get started`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Jeans, deals, and perks — your Old Navy Fam starter pack.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals + a welcome offer just for you. Don't let it expire.`
