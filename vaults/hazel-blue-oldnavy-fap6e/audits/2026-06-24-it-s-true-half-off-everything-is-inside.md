# IT'S TRUE! HALF OFF everything* is inside
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's ninth-plus send in what has become a marathon promotional wave, and the creative team is clearly feeling the squeeze. The subject line drops to ALL CAPS exclamations ("IT'S TRUE!") to manufacture urgency that the previous eight sends already burned through — a red flag for the inbox. Inside, the email actually does more than most of the prior wave: it introduces a new product line (#NewFunktech), runs the time-limited "2 Days Only / 60% off" hook, and layers in baby/toddler clearance as a secondary audience grab. That's more content variation than the June 21–22 sends.
- The problem is fatigue math. A subscriber who received all nine emails this week already knows 50% off everything is "inside." The hero doesn't need to promise a reveal — it needs to show the goods immediately. The Funktech angle is the most interesting thing in this send and it's buried beneath a banner that could have been copy-pasted from any of the previous eight.
- The "2 Days Only" urgency module is genuinely useful — credible, time-bounded, different from the omnipresent sitewide deal — but it lands mid-scroll rather than anchoring the open. That's the biggest structural miss here.

## What's working

- **"2 Days Only / 60%"** urgency module is the strongest element — specific, time-bounded, visually distinct from the sitewide offer.
- **#NewFunktech** is the first product-specific hook in the entire wave; introduces novelty that prior sends lacked.
- **Summer lifestyle imagery (TIME TO CHILL section)** is seasonally on-point and breaks up the discount wall.
- **Baby/toddler clearance block** adds audience depth and gives parents a separate reason to click beyond apparel for themselves.
- **Clean layout** — no visible render bugs, overlapping text, or broken images.

## What's weak

- **"IT'S TRUE!" ALL CAPS subject line** is a spam-signal regression; the June 23 send avoided this and scored better for it.
- **50% off hero is zero-new-information** for any subscriber who's been in this wave — this message has run eight consecutive times.
- **Funktech is buried** — the most differentiated angle in the email doesn't lead the send.
- **No visible loyalty / member pricing** despite Old Navy having an active rewards program.
- **Clearance section feels like a catch-all appendage**, not a curated curation; the baby clearance block especially looks like a different email stapled on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Funktech, not 50% off.** The new product line is the only story this send has that the previous eight don't. Put it in the hero and let the sitewide deal be the supporting offer, not the lead.
- 2. **Kill the "IT'S TRUE!" opener in the subject.** It reads desperate. The June 21 "Don't miss it" send performed better with cleaner copy.
- 3. **Move "2 Days Only" above the fold.** The time-bounded offer is the strongest click driver and it's mid-email.
- 4. **Tighten the clearance block** — either commit to a curated "best of clearance" or cut it. Baby gear + adult clearance in the same block fragments attention.
- 5. **Add a member pricing callout** — even a one-liner ("Members get early access") converts loyalty members and signals there's a reason to join.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class selectors**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the `@media` block (lines covering both `space-top5` and `space-top10`, same for bottom). The second declaration silently overwrites the first. The comment in source acknowledges this: `/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */` — but the duplicate was not actually removed. Last-write wins in CSS, so `space-top5` and `space-top10` both resolve to `padding-top: 10px`.

**Outlook conditional stylesheet**
```css
<!--[if gte mso 9]>
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
Setting `line-height: 0px` globally on all `td` elements in Outlook 2013+ will collapse any text-only cells with no explicit height. This is a known Outlook rendering pitfall — should be scoped to layout-spacer cells only, not a blanket `td` reset.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
The CP pixel `src=https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/5f68e675...` has no `alt` attribute. For a 1×1 tracking image `alt=""` is acceptable per WCAG; the absence of the attribute entirely (rather than `alt=""`) may trigger strict accessibility validators.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were captured by the relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use these headers to render the native unsubscribe button. Their absence increases spam classification risk, and Google's February 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate one-click unsubscribe for senders exceeding 5k/day to Gmail.

Note: the QA tool flags these as "may not be captured by AgentMail relay" — headers may exist at SMTP level but not be forwarded. Verify against raw `.eml` headers before treating as a hard failure.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not visible in the truncated source — confirm it exists in the footer of the full HTML. If absent, this is a statutory violation.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. Sender domain `email.oldnavy.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; DMARC alignment requires DKIM signing with a `@oldnavy.com` or `@email.oldnavy.com` `d=` value, or SPF alignment via `oldnavy.com` envelope sender. Cannot confirm without raw headers.
