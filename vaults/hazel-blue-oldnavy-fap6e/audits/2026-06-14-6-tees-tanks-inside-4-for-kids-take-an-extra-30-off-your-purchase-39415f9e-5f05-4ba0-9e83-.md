# $6 tees & tanks inside ($4 for kids) + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's fourth consecutive send on the $6 tees / $4 tanks price anchor in roughly 36 hours — and the creative playbook shows it. The email leads with a crisp "2 DAYS ONLY!" hero banner, a female model in a teal crop, and the now-familiar price points. The new value-add here is the stacked extra 30% off storewide, which is meaningfully different from the pure price-anchor sends (see: yesterday's "$6 TEES & TANKS (Yes, $6!)" at 8/10) — but that upgrade gets buried in a 76-character subject line that runs on before mobile preview clips it.
- The body is longer than it needs to be. Three modules compete for attention: the tee/tank anchor, a "Hot New Gear" section, and a clearance repeat that literally types "Clearance" four times in a row. The stacked discount — 60% off storewide + $6 price anchor + extra 30% at checkout — is genuinely compelling on paper, but the email never collapses it into one clean "here's your bottom line" statement. The result is a subscriber doing math in their head instead of reaching for the CTA.
- The cadence is the real damage here. A subscriber who opened all four of this week's Old Navy sends has now seen essentially the same creative three or four times in two days. Even if the extra 30% is new information, inbox exhaustion is the default.

## What's working

- The "2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency banner anchors the hero instantly — eye lands on offer before anything else.
- The $6 / $4 price anchor is genuinely sharp; no ambiguity about what you're getting.
- The extra 30% off storewide is a legitimate escalation from prior sends — this isn't a repeat, it's an upgrade.
- No visible render bugs: images load cleanly, layout holds, no overlapping text.
- The "Go, Go, Go! Clearance" typographic block is a visual break that stops the scroll — works better than a generic product grid would.

## What's weak

- Subject line is 76 characters — the parenthetical `($4 for kids)` and the "+" connector guarantee mobile truncation before the 30% off hook.
- Three discount layers (60% off storewide, $6 price anchor, extra 30%) run in parallel without a single "bottom line" callout — subscribers have to reconcile them mentally.
- This is the fourth near-identical send in ~36 hours; cadence fatigue erases open rate gains from the stacked discount.
- Female-forward hero creative with no male-presenting models anywhere above the fold — for a family brand pushing kids gear too, this is a demographic miss.
- No urgency in the subject itself — "2 DAYS ONLY!" lives in the body, not where the subscriber decides to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the three-layer discount into one clear statement.** The email never tells you what $6 minus 30% actually costs. Put "$4.20 tees after your extra 30%" in the hero — that does the math for you and feels like a revelation, not a puzzle.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 chars and surface urgency.** The current 76-char subject buries the best hook (extra 30% off) past where mobile clips it.
- 3. **Test male or family models above the fold.** You're selling kids gear and unisex basics — the hero should show that audience, not just a women's crop.
- 4. **Retire the clearance triple-repeat or give it its own dedicated send.** Running "Clearance Clearance Clearance" as a third module dilutes the tee/tank and new gear stories. If clearance is the hook, lead with it.
- 5. **If there's a fifth send on this theme, start with a fresh hook.** The only move left on this cadence is "last call" with a hard countdown — or a full creative reset.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6 tees + extra 30% off — 2 days left`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack it: $6 tees & 30% off everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kids tanks from $4. All orders take extra 30% at checkout — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus hot new arrivals — extra 30% off applies to everything.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken cp.oldnavy.com redirect**

The pixel URL at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c8caeb5b-...` resolves with an internal redirect to a relative path:

```
unknown url type: '/g/c8caeb5b-3faf-40f3-a39f-2d9e6a2fce47?txn=9ade9080-6ab8-11f1-95a0-ca2f212c8bca'
```

This is a relative-path redirect bug on the `cp.oldnavy.com` tracking/preference server — it returns a `Location` header with `/g/...` instead of an absolute URL. The HTTP client cannot follow it. If this is a tracking pixel, open-rate data for this send is likely incomplete or corrupted.

**[WARN] 23 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe**

All outbound links pass through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. The QA runner skipped HTTP probing on these, so broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed or ruled out from this audit alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Tracking pixel missing `alt` text**

The `cp.oldnavy.com` open-tracking `<img>` has no `alt` attribute:

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c8caeb5b-..." width="1" heigh…
```

Screen readers will attempt to read the raw URL. The `alt=""` attribute should be explicitly set on all tracking pixels (the `click.email.oldnavy.com` open pixel in the same `display:none` div has `alt=""` correctly set — this one does not).

**[WARN] Truncated `height` attribute**

The same pixel has `heigh…` — the HTML source provided is truncated, but if the production source is also truncated, the `height="1"` attribute is malformed and some clients may render the pixel at default height.

**CSS duplicate selector collision**

In the responsive `@media` block, `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each declared twice with different values:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}  /* overwrites above */

.space-bot5, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 5px;}
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot {padding-bottom: 10px;}  /* overwrites above */
```

The latter declaration wins in all cases, so `.MspaceTop` elements that were intended to get 5px padding will receive 10px on mobile. The comment in the source acknowledges this (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`) but the fix was not applied — both declarations remain.

**Outlook line-height zero override**

The MSO conditional block sets `td { line-height: 0px }` globally for Outlook 2013+. This is a known workaround for ghost spacing but will collapse any `<td>` that relies on inherited line-height for vertical rhythm. Only safe if every `<td>` with text content has an explicit `line-height` inline style or class — worth verifying in Outlook rendering.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent**

`List-Unsubscribe` was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header for their native unsubscribe UI. Its absence means:
- Gmail may show a "Report spam" prompt instead of a managed unsubscribe link.
- Without `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058), one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules for >5k/day senders) is not supported.

This is likely a relay capture issue rather than a send-side omission, but should be verified against raw headers in the ESP's send log.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected in parsed body**

CAN-SPAM §7(a)(3) requires a valid physical postal address. The HTML source is truncated — the address may exist in the footer below the truncation point. Confirm it is present in the full source. If absent, this is a legal violation.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**

`Authentication-Results` header was not captured. Sending domain is `email.oldnavy.com`; the `From` domain matches. Cannot confirm DKIM signature validity or SPF alignment from this audit. Verify via ESP send log or a direct header capture.
