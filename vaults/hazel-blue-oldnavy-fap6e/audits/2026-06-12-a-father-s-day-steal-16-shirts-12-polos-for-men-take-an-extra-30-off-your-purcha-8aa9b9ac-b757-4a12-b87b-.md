# A Father's Day steal: $16 shirts & $12 polos for men + take an EXTRA 30% off your purchase
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy pivots to Father's Day with a legitimately new hook — price anchors on men's shirts ($16) and polos ($12) plus an extra 30% off stacked on top. The hero lands cleanly: "TODAY ONLY!" banner, a man in a white polo, and the two price callouts are the first things your eye hits. The Father's Day angle is a welcome creative break from the relentless 50%-off women's-apparel drumbeat that defined the last eight sends in five days.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. The email immediately collapses into the same multi-section clearance dump — women's summer dresses, activewear, a weekend "Fri, Sat, Sun" clearance event — that has appeared in every recent Old Navy send. The Father's Day frame is credible for about one scroll, then it dissolves into the usual everything-on-sale chaos. The men's gift-buyer or male recipient is left wondering whether this email was actually for them.
- The stacked-discount construction ("$12 polos + EXTRA 30% off your purchase") is classic Old Navy discount theater. Technically generous, but so layered that the real price isn't obvious at a glance. Clear judgement: best Old Navy send in the recent run, but still structurally cluttered.

## What's working

- **Father's Day hook is timely and specific** — the June 12 send with a June 15 deadline window is credible urgency, not manufactured.
- **Hero price callouts are immediate** — "$12" and "$16" are the first numbers the eye catches; no hunting required.
- **Men's model in the hero** matches the stated audience; rare discipline for a brand that defaults to women's apparel even on gender-targeted sends.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" + concrete prices** combines urgency with specificity in a way the recent 50%-off sends didn't — a specific dollar amount anchors faster than a percentage.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is 89 characters** — gets truncated on virtually every mobile client before reaching "EXTRA 30% off your purchase."
- **Email defocuses within two sections** — Father's Day men's framing evaporates as soon as women's summer dresses and clearance rows appear below the hero.
- **Stacked discounts are opaque** — "$12 polo + extra 30% off" means different things depending on which items qualify; no clarification is visible.
- **Weekend clearance event at the bottom ("Fri, Sat, Sun!")** cannibalizes the "TODAY ONLY!" urgency set up at the top — can't be both today-only and a weekend event.
- **No loyalty/member callout** — Old Navy Card or NavyCredit benefits are absent, a missed retention signal given the member pricing stacks nicely with 30% off.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to under 55 chars** — the back half is invisible on mobile. Lead with the steal angle and drop "your purchase."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day steal: $12 polos & $16 shirts for men`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: Dad's shirts from $12 + extra 30% off`
- 2. **Rewrite the preheader to add the deadline** — Father's Day is Sunday; the clock is a real conversion driver that the current send doesn't surface.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab his gift before Sunday — prices this low don't last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ships in time for Father's Day. Shop men's from $12 today.`
- 3. **Remove or quarantine the women's/clearance sections** — either send two separate emails or push those below a hard visual divider with its own CTA. Mixing them undermines the men's gift frame and splits click-through intent.
- 4. **Resolve the urgency contradiction** — pick "TODAY ONLY!" or "Fri, Sat, Sun clearance." Both in the same email trains subscribers to ignore both signals.
- 5. **Add a concrete deadline for the extra 30%** — "ends tonight at midnight" or "through Father's Day Sunday" converts the vague offer into a real decision driver.

### 1. Technical Summary

One hard-broken redirect (broken experience) and three compliance gaps (List-Unsubscribe headers + missing physical address) are the critical issues. The plain-text part is also nearly unreadable to spam filters at 82% URL content by character count.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com Movable Ink pixel**

The QA probe followed `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/61bea2e7-1163-4cce-ba7b-d1423e9df113?mi_mid=019eb9c5-ab80-7000-a724-5346e628b4b6&cp_tp=v3.eJyN...` and received a redirect to a relative path `/g/61bea2e7-...` — not a valid absolute URL. This breaks the Movable Ink personalization render pipeline; the dynamic image or content block backed by this token will fail to load for affected recipients.

- **Root cause:** The Movable Ink `o/` (open) endpoint is redirecting to a `/g/` (generate) path without a host, which is invalid as a redirect target. Likely a misconfigured Movable Ink campaign or an expired/malformed `cp_tp` payload.
- **Blast radius:** Every subscriber who opens this email will get a broken dynamic image/content block, not just edge cases.

**[INFO] 23 tracking links skipped**

All click-redirect links route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for these (expected behavior for click-tracking domains). No evidence of broken destinations, but cannot confirm landing page UTMs without manual spot-check.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS selector definitions (minor)**

In the `@media (max-width: 639px)` block, both `.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are defined twice on different rules:

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 5px;}
...
.space-top10, .MspaceTop {padding-top: 10px;}   /* overwrites previous */
```

The second definition silently overrides the first. `MspaceTop` will always resolve to `padding-top: 10px` on mobile; any elements relying on `5px` via that class will render incorrectly. Same pattern for `MspaceBot`.

**Outlook `td { line-height: 0px }` override (moderate)**

Inside the `<!--[if gte mso 9]-->` conditional block:
```css
td { line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly }
```
This is a global zero-line-height reset applied to all `<td>` elements in Outlook 2013+. If any `<td>` contains live text that isn't individually overridden, text will collapse. Safe only if every text-bearing cell has explicit `line-height` inline styles.

**Missing alt text on dynamic image**

The Movable Ink dynamic image (`cp.oldnavy.com/o/61bea2e7...`) is flagged with no `alt` attribute. For the open/tracking pixel at `click.email.oldnavy.com/open.aspx`, `alt=""` is present and acceptable. The dynamic content image should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off render environments.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated HTML source. Personalization parameters (`mi_mid`, `mi_cid`, `cp_cid`) appear populated in the Movable Ink URLs. The broken experience is a redirect resolution failure, not a token substitution failure.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers absent**

Neither `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369) nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were captured by the AgentMail relay. Note these may be present on the actual SMTP envelope and not captured — however:

- Google and Yahoo's bulk sender requirements (enforced since February 2024) mandate `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for senders above 5k/day. Old Navy almost certainly exceeds this threshold.
- If these headers are genuinely missing, this creates inbox placement risk with Gmail and Yahoo Mail, which use the absence of one-click unsubscribe as a negative signal.

**Action:** Verify headers are present on the raw SMTP message (not just as forwarded by relay). If missing, ESP must inject them at send time.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**

CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The HTML source is truncated so the footer may contain it — but if the QA check did not find it in the full HTML parse, the address is either absent or rendered as an image (which fails the requirement). Must be live text.

**[INFO] Authentication-Results header not captured**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the `Authentication-Results` header. Old Navy's sending domain (`email.oldnavy.com`) should be fully authenticated — cannot confirm from this sample.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot fully verify UTM coverage** — all 23 destination links are behind the `click.email.oldnavy.com` redirect and were not probed. Manual spot-check of 2-3 final landing URLs is needed to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` parameters are appended.

**Broken Movable Ink block** (see §2) means any CTA or product image served dynamically from `cp.oldnavy.com` will not render for openers, creating a gap between the email design intent and what recipients actually see. If the broken block is a hero image or primary CTA, click-through rates will be suppressed.

**Plain-text part is 82% URLs by character count** (21,581 / 26,343 chars)

The plain-text alternative appears to be a raw URL dump rather than human-readable text. This is a deliverability signal — spam filters score plain-text parts that are predominantly URLs as low-quality or suspicious. The plain-text version should mirror the email's readable content with URLs only where needed.
