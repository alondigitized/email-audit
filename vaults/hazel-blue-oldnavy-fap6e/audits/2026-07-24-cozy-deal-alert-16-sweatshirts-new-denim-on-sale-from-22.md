# 📣 COZY DEAL ALERT: $16 sweatshirts + new denim on sale from $22
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's **second email of July 24** — and both sends feature the exact same $16 sweatshirt deal. This morning's send ("*The comfiest* layers. Shop $16 sweatshirts," 7/10) already made that case; this afternoon's "COZY DEAL ALERT" is the same creative argument in a different envelope. Structurally it's a five-block kitchen sink: new jeans from $22, a TODAY ONLY sweatshirts block ($12/$16), a VIP LOUNGE athleisure section (lifted directly from the July 19 PowerChill send), a second category block, and the $3.99 clearance grid that also appeared July 20. Nearly every module here has run in the past five days.
- The hero conflict is the most actionable miss: the subject line opens with "$16 sweatshirts" but the first scroll is denim. Subscribers who opened for the sweatshirt deal have to hunt past a jeans editorial to find the thing that got them in. "COZY DEAL ALERT" in late July compounds the problem — "cozy layers" is a September positioning, and it undersells a legitimate summer denim story that was already being built in last week's editorial emails.
- **Verdict:** Technically clean, prices are real, urgency is present — but same-day duplication, recycled creative blocks, and a subject/hero mismatch make this feel like scheduled batch-and-blast rather than a deliberate campaign beat. A subscriber who got both emails today is likelier to unsubscribe than to shop twice.

## What's working

- **Concrete prices are prominent throughout** — $22, $16, $12, and $3.99 are all in-email without hunting
- **TODAY ONLY urgency** on the sweatshirts block is clear and creates a genuine decision point
- **VIP LOUNGE branding** maintains continuity for loyalty subscribers who associate it with that sub-brand
- **Wide price ladder** ($3.99 clearance through $22 jeans) gives multiple intent levels an entry point

## What's weak

- **Second email, same deal, same day** — the $16 sweatshirts already headlined the morning send; a real subscriber who got both notices, and not in a good way
- **Subject/hero mismatch** — subject leads with sweatshirts, email opens with jeans; creates disorientation on open
- **"COZY DEAL ALERT" in ALL CAPS mid-July** — mild spam-signal AND seasonal mismatch; "cozy layers" belongs in September, not during peak summer
- **Recycled creative blocks** — the VIP LOUNGE section and $3.99 clearance grid have both appeared in the past five days; this reads as copy-paste, not campaign architecture
- **Kitchen-sink dilution** — five competing blocks give no single product enough room; the new denim story being built through last week's editorial sends gets buried here as a secondary block

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the same-day duplicate.** If the sweatshirts TODAY ONLY was already sent this morning, don't send again. If a second email is genuinely justified, lead with something that wasn't in the morning send — make this a denim-only drop, lean into the new arrivals angle.
- 2. **Align hero to subject.** If the subject says "$16 sweatshirts," sweatshirts must be the first scroll — not jeans. Either flip the block order or rewrite the subject to match what's above the fold.
- 3. **Drop "COZY" and lead with denim.** "New denim from $22" is a cleaner seasonal hook for late July; sweatshirts become a secondary urgency block below, not the hero framing.
- 4. **Sentence-case the subject** — "Cozy deal alert" is less spammy than "COZY DEAL ALERT" and reads more like human copy.
- 5. **Cut two blocks** — VIP LOUNGE and $3.99 clearance have both run this week. Removing them gives jeans and sweatshirts room to breathe and shortens an email that's competing with itself.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New denim from $22 — plus $16 sweatshirts, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Denim just dropped to $22. Grab a $16 sweatshirt while you're here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `TODAY ONLY: cozy layers from $12 while you browse the new jeans`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $16 sweatshirt is waiting — jeans on sale too, from $22`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/c52e8bbd-..."> <!-- no alt attribute -->
```
The Cordial pixel has no `alt=""`. While invisible pixels don't need descriptive alt text, the attribute must be present (even empty) to pass WCAG 2.1 and avoid screen-reader noise.

**[INFO] Outlook line-height conflict**
The MSO conditional style block sets `td { line-height: 0px; }` globally for Outlook 2013+, then overrides per-element. This is a known pattern but aggressive — any `td` without an explicit `mso-line-height-rule` override will collapse to zero height in Outlook. Confirm in Litmus/Email on Acid under Outlook 2016/2019.

**[INFO] `MspaceTop` / `MspaceBot` class collision**
In the responsive stylesheet, `.space-top5` and `.space-top10` both map to `.MspaceTop`, and `.space-bot5` and `.space-bot10` both map to `.MspaceBot`. The second declaration silently overrides the first — elements using the earlier class get the wrong spacing on mobile.

**[INFO] XHTML doctype with emoji subject**
The HTML is declared as `XHTML 1.0 Transitional`. The subject line contains `📣` — this is fine at the SMTP/header layer but note the HTML `<title>` also contains the emoji, which is valid in UTF-8/XHTML.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (≥5k/day) as of 2024. If the header exists at the ESP layer but was stripped by the AgentMail relay, it needs to be confirmed at the raw SMTP envelope level.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. The `POST` variant must accompany `List-Unsubscribe` with a `mailto:` or HTTPS endpoint. Absence here is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The truncated source does not show one; it may appear in the unrendered footer. This must be verified in the full HTML — failure is an FTC violation.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the relay. SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status for `email.oldnavy.com` cannot be confirmed from this capture. Gap is in the relay pipeline, not necessarily a sending-side failure — but the audit trail is incomplete.
