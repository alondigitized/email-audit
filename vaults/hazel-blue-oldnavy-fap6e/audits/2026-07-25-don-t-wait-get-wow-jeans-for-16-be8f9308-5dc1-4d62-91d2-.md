# DON'T WAIT ⏰ Get Wow jeans for $16
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Old Navy's third "Wow Jeans" push inside a week — the deal first appeared July 20 in the "$6 tees and Wow jeans are 50% off" kitchen-sink send (8/10), and now it's back as the sole hero story at a flat $16. The compression from "50% off" to a flat price is a smart reframe (a fixed number is easier to process than a percentage), and the "2 DAYS ONLY!" badge plus clock emoji in the subject gives it real urgency. The hero itself is clean — blue background, bold pricing, single product focus.
- Where it falls apart is everything after the hero. This immediately sprawls into a five-section kitchen sink (Wow Jeans → Jeans from $22 → TrekTech activewear → Clearance from $3.99 → Discover More footer), and there's no single unmissable "Shop Now" button under the headline offer — just a row of category chips (WOMEN, GIRLS, TODDLER BOYS, TODDLER GIRLS). For an email whose subject line screams urgency about one specific deal, the body diffuses that urgency across four unrelated promotions before the reader even gets to the second jeans-price section.
- Solid, on-brand, but running the same "Wow Jeans" hook for the third time in five days with no fresh creative — cadence fatigue is starting to show.

## What's working

- The hero communicates the deal instantly: "2 DAYS ONLY!" with clear Kids & Toddler $9 / Adults $16 pricing, no scrolling needed.
- Flat-dollar pricing ($16, $9) is easier to parse at a glance than the July 20 send's "50% off" framing.
- The Encore Member free-shipping banner up top adds a real loyalty incentive without derailing the hero.
- Product grids are well-organized by category (Women, Girls, Boys, Toddler) with visible price call-outs, making browsing easy once you're in the email.
- No visual bugs — images render cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together through a long scroll.

## What's weak

- No single, unambiguous primary CTA button under the hero — just a row of category text links, which splits intent instead of driving one click.
- The email is a five-section kitchen sink (jeans, jeans again, TrekTech, clearance, footer) that buries the urgent 2-day jeans deal under unrelated promotions.
- "Select styles only. Exclusions Apply. See Details" sits directly under the hero price — visible fine print undercutting the "$16" headline.
- This is the same Wow Jeans deal as the July 20 send, just reframed from "50% off" to "$16" — subscribers who opened that email will recognize the recycled hook.
- No product detail (fit, sizing, wash) beyond a small "Boot Cut" label — thin for a jeans-first send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one clear "Shop Wow Jeans" button directly beneath the price callout in the hero — right now the category chips make the reader do the work of picking where to click.
- 2. Cut the email to two sections max (Wow Jeans + Jeans from $22) and move TrekTech/Clearance to a separate send — the current structure dilutes the urgency the subject line promises.
- 3. Move or shrink the "Exclusions Apply" fine print so it doesn't sit immediately under the headline price.
- 4. Since this is the third Wow Jeans mention in five days, differentiate the creative — new model shot, new angle — so it doesn't read as a repeat of July 20.
- **Subject Alt A:** `⏰ 48 hours: Wow Jeans, $16`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — Wow Jeans drop to $16`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Adults $16, kids $9 — ends Sunday night`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Boot cut, straight, skinny — all $16 for 2 days`
