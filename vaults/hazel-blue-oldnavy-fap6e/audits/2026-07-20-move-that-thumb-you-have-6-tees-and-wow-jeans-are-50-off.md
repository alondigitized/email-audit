# 📲 Move that thumb! You have $6 tees and Wow jeans are 50% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic Old Navy kitchen-sink send — five distinct offer blocks stuffed into a single email: $6 tees (TODAY ONLY), 50% off Wow jeans, $3.99 clearance, new Run Shorts launch, and a Back-to-School deals module at the bottom. The subject delivers on the two loudest promises ($6 tees, Wow jeans 50% off), but the body immediately fragments into a discount pile-up that dilutes every individual offer. Compared to yesterday's PowerChill "VIP Lounge" concept, which committed to a single lifestyle angle, this send abandons editorial ambition entirely.
- The email gets credit for price specificity — $6, $3.99, $5/$8/$12 tiers are all visible without hunting — and the Wow jeans section uses a solid blue lifestyle photo that earns its real estate. But burying a NEW product launch (Run Shorts) as the fourth section of a clearance email is a near-criminal misuse of newness. That story deserved its own send.
- Judgment: Old Navy's cadence (at least 6 emails in 5 days including two on July 19) is doing real damage to open rates. This email does nothing to break the pattern; it accelerates it. A dedicated, single-offer email — either the Run Shorts launch OR the Wow jeans 50% moment — would outperform this on every metric.

## What's working

- **Price specificity is excellent** — $4, $6, $3.99, $5, $8, $12 are all visible and concrete; the subscriber doesn't have to click to understand what they're getting.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" hero creates real urgency** — the red text block at top communicates a hard deadline immediately.
- **Wow jeans lifestyle photo** — the blue-background couple shot is the strongest creative in the email; the model pairing signals broad adult relevance.
- **Seasonal coherence** — clearance + Back-to-School at the bottom correctly reflects the July inventory flush moment.
- **Run Shorts color shots** — the teal product-on-white row shows the color range clearly; the product visuals are the execution highlight.

## What's weak

- **Five competing heroes kill visual hierarchy** — "TODAY ONLY tees" vs. "50% off Wow jeans" vs. "$3.99 clearance" vs. "NEW RUN SHORTS" vs. "BTS Deals" each demand attention; the eye has nowhere to commit.
- **A new product launch is buried as section four** — Run Shorts deserves its own email, not fourth billing under clearance pricing.
- **"Move that thumb" opening feels try-hard** — the mobile scroll metaphor reads as copy written for social, repurposed awkwardly into email; it adds no offer clarity or urgency.
- **"You have $6 tees" is pseudo-personalization** — "You have" implies account-specific inventory or a loyalty perk, but this is a broadcast. A subscriber who reads it closely will feel misled.
- **Cadence fatigue is compounding** — July 19 had two Old Navy sends; this is the sixth promo in five days. The discount level has to escalate (it's at $3.99 clearance now) to maintain the same open rate, which is the definition of a race to the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two emails** — a clean Wow jeans 50% off hero one day, Run Shorts launch the next. Kitchen-sink sends depress click rates because subscribers can't pick a lane.
- 2. **Retire "Move that thumb"** — replace with a subject line that leads with the single strongest offer and lets the verb do the work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$6 tees, today only — plus Wow jeans just went 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $6 tees expire tonight (Wow jeans too: 50% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab them before midnight — no code, prices already cut.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tees start at $4. Wow jeans drop to half price. Both end tonight.`
- 3. **Promote Run Shorts to a standalone send tomorrow** — lead with the lifestyle model shot, make color the hero, price-anchor at $5. That's a compelling email on its own.
- 4. **Drop or collapse the Back-to-School module** — yesterday's two BTS sends already owned that story. Repeating it here dilutes both the BTS campaign and this send.
- 5. **Add a loyalty callout** — even a single line "NavyCard members: early access ends at 10am" would give members a reason to open faster and differentiate the offer from a pure broadcast.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — cp.oldnavy.com tracking URL**
The Cordial engagement-tracking pixel resolves to an invalid relative path:
```
Error: unknown url type: '/g/0bb8938d-fd88-46bb-8a4b-b76185b85524?txn=84c14880-8447-11f1-bdc0-7685877ed2a2'
Source URL: https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/0bb8938d-fd88-46bb-8a4b-b76185b85524?mi_mid=...&cp_cid=1bc829e71098f268
```
The `/o/` endpoint is returning a redirect to `/g/` with a `txn` parameter that the checker cannot follow — likely a Cordial open-tracking redirect misconfiguration or a dead transaction ID. This pixel appears twice in the `display:none` preheader block.

**[WARN] 23 click-tracking links unverified**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Missing alt text on tracking pixel**
```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/0bb8938d-fd88-46bb-8a4b-b76185b85524?..." width="1" height="1">
```
No `alt` attribute present. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to satisfy strict XHTML 1.0 Transitional validation and suppress screen-reader noise.

**[INFO] Duplicate CSS class definitions**
`.MspaceTop` and `.MspaceBot` are each defined twice in the responsive block (the source even comments on the duplication). Last-write wins, so behavior is correct, but the dead declarations add noise and complicate future edits.

**[INFO] `td { line-height: 0px }` Outlook conditional**
The MSO-conditional style sets `line-height: 0px; mso-line-height-rule: exactly` on all `td` elements. This is a known Outlook height fix but can clip single-line cells if any content `td` lacks an explicit line-height override.

No missing responsive breakpoint issues detected in the truncated CSS; `@media (max-width: 639px)` covers the standard mobile stack.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent at relay**
QA could not confirm the header via AgentMail. Gmail and Yahoo enforce one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe-Post`) for bulk senders; absence at the relay layer may mean the header is stripped or was never injected by the ESP.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
Required for one-click unsubscribe compliance under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender rules. Must accompany a `List-Unsubscribe: <https://...>` mailto-less header.

**[WARN] No physical mailing address detected**
CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the message body. The truncated HTML ends before the footer — if a Gap Inc. address block exists below the fold this may be a false positive, but it must be confirmed.

**[INFO] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown at the relay. `oldnavy@email.oldnavy.com` should be covered by Gap Inc.'s SPF record and DKIM signing on `email.oldnavy.com`, but relay interception prevents verification here.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 23 CTAs pass through `click.email.oldnavy.com` — the click-redirect layer is responsible for appending UTM parameters before forwarding to `oldnavy.com`. Because probing was skipped, UTM injection cannot be confirmed from this audit. Known risk: if the redirect chain omits `utm_source` / `utm_campaign`, site analytics will attribute sessions to direct rather than email.

The Cordial `cp_tp` payload (base64 + deflate-encoded) carries campaign context internally but is not a substitute for URL-level UTM parameters visible to GA4/Adobe.
