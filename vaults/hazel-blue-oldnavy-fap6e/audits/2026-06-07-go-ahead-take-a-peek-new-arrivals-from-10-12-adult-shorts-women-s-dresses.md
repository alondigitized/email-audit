# Go ahead, take a peek 👀 New arrivals from $10 + $12 adult shorts & women's dresses
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Old Navy swings back with a third consecutive email anchoring on the same $12 shorts and women's dresses offer that dominated yesterday's two sends (the 9 AM straightforward send and the afternoon "📢 Did you hear?" re-engagement). Today's creative attempts a reframe — "Go ahead, take a peek 👀 New arrivals from $10" — bolting a curiosity hook onto a price offer, while the email body actually opens with "2 DAYS ONLY!" and a $9 price point. That subject-to-hero mismatch ($10 in subject, $9 in email) is sloppy and erodes the already-thin trust of a subscriber who opened yesterday's identical offer.
- The email is structurally a five-section kitchen sink: hero sale pricing, a price ladder ($6/$8/$12), a plus-size CTA module, an activewear-on-sale band, and a triple-Clearance closer. Each section is individually readable, but the cumulative effect is exhaustion — there is no single reason to click. The "HOT NEW GEAR!" badge on the activewear section is the most credible contrast point to yesterday's sends and deserved to be the hero.
- Bottom line: this is a re-hash dressed in curiosity language. For a subscriber who has already seen two near-identical sends in 24 hours, the creative pivot isn't enough to reset fatigue.

## What's working

- **"2 DAYS ONLY!" urgency is immediate** — the time-bound framing lands in the first scroll stop without any hunting.
- **$9/$12 price anchors are bold and legible** — the hero typography makes the value proposition instantly scannable.
- **Plus-size CTA band** is a thoughtful inclusivity signal that stands out in Old Navy's recent run of standard-model imagery.
- **"HOT NEW GEAR!" badge** on the activewear section adds a genuine novelty cue that the last few sends lacked.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-hero price mismatch** — subject says "from $10," hero shows $9; minor but looks like a versioning error.
- **Third send on the same $12 shorts theme in 24 hours** — any subscriber who opened yesterday's "Did you hear?" will feel déjà vu before they scroll past the hero.
- **Five discrete offer blocks with no clear winner** — the email asks the reader to prioritize five things simultaneously (hero deal, price ladder, plus sizes, activewear, clearance), which effectively makes none of them primary.
- **"Go ahead, take a peek" curiosity hook is a cliché** — it delays the offer without building real intrigue, and on mobile the subject truncates before the actual price information.
- **Activewear section (the freshest content) is buried mid-scroll** — the most differentiating content from yesterday's sends is the fourth fold, not the first.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the "HOT NEW GEAR" story, not the recycled $12 shorts.** The new arrivals angle is the only thing that genuinely differentiates this send from yesterday; bury it and you've wasted the subject line's "new arrivals" promise.
- 2. **Cut to three sections max.** Hero new arrivals, one sale anchor (shorts/dresses at $12), and clearance. Consolidate or drop the plus-size module and the activewear band — or send them as their own emails on a less exhausted cadence.
- 3. **Fix the subject/hero price alignment.** If the email shows $9, say $9 in the subject — it's a stronger hook anyway.
- 4. **Kill the teasy opener.** "Go ahead, take a peek" wastes the first ~22 characters of subject real estate on a phrase that adds no information.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals from $9 — shorts, dresses & hot activewear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$9 new arrivals just dropped — 2 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus $12 shorts & dresses + all activewear still on sale — shop before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New gear, summer pricing: deals from $9 through Sunday only`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one confirmed broken redirect in the tracking infrastructure and three compliance header gaps; all other core rendering logic is structurally sound. The plain-text part is a spam-signal liability due to near-total URL saturation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect chain — `cp.oldnavy.com` open-pixel**

QA probe of the Contentsquare/Movable Ink pixel returned a relative path instead of an absolute URL:

```
Resolved path: /g/afbaf220-5177-4133-8667-2fbb9d338953?txn=bea40a00-6260-11f1-b68a-4e8366552ae6
Expected:      https://cp.oldnavy.com/g/...
```

The pixel at `https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/afbaf220...` issues a redirect to a bare `/g/...` path. Any client that follows the redirect without re-attaching the origin will receive a 404/invalid resource. This breaks open-tracking and any engagement metric derived from this pixel.

**[INFO] 23 click-tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.email.oldnavy.com`. Not probeable without live recipient session cookies; treat as untested.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate CSS class definitions (mobile stylesheet):**

```css
.space-top5, .MspaceTop  { padding-top: 5px; }   /* line ~93 */
.space-top10, .MspaceTop { padding-top: 10px; }  /* line ~96 — overrides above */
.space-bot5,  .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 5px; }
.space-bot10, .MspaceBot { padding-bottom: 10px; }
```

The source even contains an inline comment acknowledging the duplication (`/* MspaceTop was duplicated, kept one */`) but the duplicate was not removed. On mobile, any element using `.MspaceTop` will always receive `padding-top: 10px` regardless of intent — the 5px variant is effectively dead.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixel:**

```html
<img src="https://cp.oldnavy.com/o/afbaf220-..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```

QA flagged this pixel for missing alt. An empty `alt=""` is acceptable for decorative/tracking pixels per WCAG — confirm the attribute is present in the untruncated source (the HTML cuts off before closing the tag). If the attribute is absent entirely, screen readers will read the full `src` URL aloud.

**No issues** with Outlook VML conditional blocks, mobile breakpoint logic (639px), or `ExternalClass` Outlook resets — these are correctly implemented.
