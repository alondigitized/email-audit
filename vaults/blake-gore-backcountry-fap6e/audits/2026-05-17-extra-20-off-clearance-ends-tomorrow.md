# EXTRA 20% off clearance ends tomorrow!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the urgency close on the same Extra 20% off clearance offer Backcountry launched yesterday — the May 16 "EXTRA 20% off clearance is ON 🔓" email was the launch; this is the "ends tomorrow" push. The structure is logical: a repeating ENDS TOMORROW ticker for immediate pressure, a clean hero with the clearance offer, a "SALE ON SALE ENDS SOON" product grid spotlighting specific Patagonia pieces, then a SHOP CLEARANCE button before pivoting to a separate Memorial Day sale section.
- The core offer is well-executed — "sale on sale" is genuinely compelling framing because it signals compound savings, and the Patagonia product grid grounds the abstract discount in real merchandise. The render is clean and the visual hierarchy holds.
- The main weakness is structural: this email is doing two jobs. The clearance close competes for attention with the Memorial Day 30% off section in the lower half. Anyone who came in for clearance might get confused or bounce before clicking. For a last-day urgency email, you want a single hard push — not two promos fighting for the close.

## What's working

- **"SALE ON SALE ENDS SOON" framing** is clear and communicates stacked savings without math — exactly right for a clearance close.
- **Specific Patagonia product grid** (four named pieces with product photos) makes the clearance offer tangible, not just a discount floating in space.
- **SHOP CLEARANCE mid-email CTA** is more specific than a generic "Shop Now" and breaks up the scroll nicely.
- **Top urgency ticker** ("TOMORROW! ENDS TOMORROW!") is aggressive but on-brand for a last-day push and front-loads the deadline signal.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum; the email is visually tight.

## What's weak

- **Two competing offers in one email** — clearance at extra 20% off and Memorial Day sale at up to 30% off are distinct promotions; the lower section undermines the urgency of the close.
- **Generic hero CTA** — the hero button reads "SHOP NOW" with no object, which wastes the primary conversion point. "SHOP CLEARANCE" appears only mid-email.
- **ENDS TOMORROW ticker is visually repetitive** — the horizontally scrolling/repeated text ("TOMORROW! ENDS TOMORROW! ENDS TOMO...") feels frantic rather than confident.
- **No price anchors in the product grid** — the four Patagonia items show no original or discounted prices, so the "sale on sale" claim is unverifiable at a glance.
- **Heavy cadence** — five emails in two days from this sender, three of which hit on May 17. Even with this being a legitimate sequence close, inbox fatigue is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Memorial Day section entirely or push it to a separate send.** For a last-day urgency email, every second of attention belongs to the clearance close. The Memorial Day section is a conversion leak.
- 2. **Add price anchors to the product grid.** Show original price crossed out and final sale-on-sale price. "Was $249 → $79" closes faster than a naked product name.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero CTA** from "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Clearance — Ends Tonight" — verb + noun + deadline in one button.
- 4. **Calm the ticker.** "Last day: Extra 20% off clearance" as a single clean banner line reads more authoritative than the looping "TOMORROW!" repetition.
- 5. **Swap subject to drop the all-caps EXTRA**, which reads as a mild spam signal and makes the subject feel like a markdown error rather than intentional emphasis.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: extra 20% off all clearance`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance ends tonight — Patagonia, TNF + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale on sale means 50-70% off already. Stacks through midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These Patagonia pieces won't come back cheaper. Last day.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External font CSS — single point of failure**
```
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
This is a render-blocking external stylesheet loaded from Bluecore's CDN. If that CDN is unavailable at open-time, fallback fonts activate silently — no error surfaced to senders. Low probability, but zero mitigation in the source.

**Tracking domain — no issues found**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<html>` tag missing `lang` attribute** — confirmed absent in the opening tag. Screen readers and accessibility validators will flag this. Required fix:
```html
<html lang="en">
```

**CSS selector bug — `.md-fluid-row .col-9` never matched**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` (should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`). As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives two conflicting width rules (66% from the correct rule above, 74.25% from this one — last-write wins at 74.25%), while `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no responsive width override at all. This silently breaks the 9-column fluid layout on mobile for `md-fluid-row` containers.

**No `<html>` `dir` attribute** — minor; combined with missing `lang`, affects bidi and accessibility tooling.

**Layout tables** — standard Bluecore-generated emails omit `role="presentation"` on structural tables. Not confirmed absent here (source is truncated), but flag for QA if screen-reader compliance is required.

**External font load** — if `partner-fonts.bluecore.com` is blocked (corporate mail proxies, aggressive DNS filtering), no `font-family` fallback stack is defined in the embedded `<style>`, so clients fall back to the browser/client default rather than a controlled system font.
