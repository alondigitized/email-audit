# New year, new adventure 🧑‍🎓🎒🚲✏️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Backcountry pivots away from the sale-cycle frame that dominated its last six sends (July 13–22, all structured around percentage-off urgency) into a back-to-school theme — a legitimate seasonal beat for late July that the brand hasn't hit recently. Execution is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent black-button CTA styling throughout, and a tidy stack of category banners (Backpacks, Water Bottles, Kids/Men/Women's Clothing) that reads like a well-organized department store flyer.
- But the send undercuts its own focus. The subject line "New year, new adventure 🧑‍🎓🎒🚲✏️" reads like a January resolution email, not a late-July back-to-school one — the phrase and the emoji-cluster are fighting each other. Inside, the email never commits to one offer: back-to-school essentials, then a Woom kids'-bike cross-promo, then a Sock Bundle deal, then a 40%-off Summer Sale banner, all stacked vertically with no visual signal for which one actually matters. That's four asks in one email.
- Solid production values, muddled message. This is a mid-pack Backcountry send — better than nothing new, but a step down from the sharper single-offer urgency closes earlier in July.

## What's working

- The category-banner format (Backpacks, Water Bottles, Kids'/Men's/Women's Clothing) is scannable and gives every likely shopper a fast lane to their section.
- Summit Club+ cash-back callout sits in the very top utility bar, so loyalty value is visible before any scrolling.
- The hero image (tree-climber in Cotopaxi-style layered fleece) and the Woom bike-riding shot both land the right seasonal, lifestyle-forward tone without looking stocky or generic.
- Button styling (solid black, white text, "SHOP ___" copy) is consistent across all seven CTAs — no visual confusion about what's clickable.

## What's weak

- Subject line doesn't match the email: "New year, new adventure" is a January mental model dropped into a late-July back-to-school send — it undersells what's actually inside.
- No single offer wins the email. Back-to-school essentials, Woom bikes, a Sock Bundle, and a 40%-off Summer Sale all compete for the same scroll, none clearly primary.
- Zero urgency or deadline anywhere — no "ends Sunday," no stock language — despite Backcountry using hard urgency closes ("FINAL HOURS," "ends TMRW") just two weeks earlier in this same brand history.
- Every banner links to a category, not a specific product — there's no single hero SKU to anchor a click.
- No sizing, fit, or availability information anywhere, which matters for backpacks and kids' bikes specifically.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to match the content.** Drop "New Year" entirely — it's borrowed language that doesn't belong in a July send. Lead with back-to-school + a concrete hook.
- 2. **Pick one hero offer and demote the rest.** Either back-to-school essentials or the Summer Sale 40% off should be the single above-the-fold ask; push Woom bikes and the Sock Bundle to a "you might also like" module below.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "Shop before the first bell rings" or a specific date gives the send urgency it currently lacks entirely.
- 4. **Surface a real number in the hero.** "Up to 40% off" is buried near the bottom — pull a specific stat (backpacks from $X, or "Save 20% on back-to-school bundles") into the top third.
- 5. **Link at least one banner to a specific product**, not just a category page, to shorten the path from click to cart.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school gear, sorted 🎒✏️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New backpacks, new bikes, new year of adventure`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, water bottles, and kids' bikes — all in one stop before the bell rings.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: Buy 3 socks, save 20% — while summer gear's still on sale.`
