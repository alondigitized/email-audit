# Full-price just went 20% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- Backcountry leads with a genuinely clean, well-designed hero: "3 DAYS ONLY / 20% OFF FULL-PRICE ITEMS" is legible, high-contrast, and backed by an on-brand lifestyle shot of runners on a trail. The subject line's "full-price just went 20% off" reversal framing is a nice hook — it signals a real markdown on items that normally never discount, which is a stronger pitch than another clearance push.
- But this send is doing three jobs at once. Below the hero product grid, it pivots into "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE" — the exact same offer this list saw in the 2026-07-31 "4 days only: extra 20% off clearance" email — and then pivots again into "UP TO 50% OFF SUMMER SEMI-ANNUAL SALE," a callback to 2026-07-31's "Up to 50% off SALE" send. Three concurrent promotions crammed into one email dilutes the "full-price 20% off" headline that got the open in the first place, and reading this against the brand history, it's the fourth major promo email in three days (7/30 teaser → 7/31 sale → 7/31 extra-20%-clearance → this). That's sale fatigue territory, not urgency.
- It's a well-built email that will perform fine on clicks because the hero and product grid are strong, but the cadence and the offer-stacking are working against the brand's own credibility.

## What's working

- Hero headline hierarchy is excellent — "20% OFF" is the first thing the eye hits, oversized and high-contrast against the trail photo.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA button appears three times (hero, product grid, clearance/sale banners), so there's no scroll-to-find-the-button friction.
- Four-product grid (Filson backpack, Outdoor Research pants, Osprey tote, Black Diamond climbing shoe) gives real, specific, clickable merchandise instead of a generic "shop all" link.
- Summit Club+ 10% cash-back banner at the very top surfaces a loyalty benefit immediately for members.
- No render bugs — images load, no overlapping text, layout is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- Three stacked offers (20% off full-price, extra 20% off clearance, up to 50% off semi-annual) compete for attention and blur which deal the subject line actually promised.
- The clearance offer is a verbatim repeat of the 2026-07-31 "extra 20% off clearance" send — a subscriber who opened that email sees no new value here.
- No preview/preheader text is present in the render — that's a wasted second line of inbox real estate.
- No sizing, fit, or stock-availability detail on any of the four featured products, so a shopper still has to click through blind.
- Cadence: this is the fourth big promotional email in three days from this brand, which risks list fatigue and unsubscribes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one offer per send. Lead with "full-price 20% off" alone and hold the clearance/semi-annual sale content for a separate email later in the week — this alone would sharpen the CTA and likely lift CTR more than any copy tweak.
- 2. Add a real preheader that names the specific offer window, since none is currently rendering.
- 3. Drop the repeated clearance block entirely — subscribers who saw 7/31's clearance email get zero incremental reason to open this one for that section.
- 4. Add one line of product detail (e.g., "Men's & Women's sizes available") under at least the hero product row to reduce click hesitation.
- 5. Space this out — after three promos in three days, consider skipping a day before the next full-send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rare: full-price gear is 20% off (3 days)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your favorite full-price picks, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No code needed — discount applies automatically at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Filson, Osprey, Black Diamond and more — all full-price, all 20% off.`
