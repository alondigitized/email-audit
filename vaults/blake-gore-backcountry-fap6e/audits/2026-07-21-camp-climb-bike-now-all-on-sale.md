# Camp, climb, bike now all on sale ✓
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's second category-sale email in five days. The July 16 "Summer's categories now all on sale" send organized outdoor activities into a sale framework; this one does the same — camp, climb, bike, 40% off, six curated product picks, sport-specific section CTAs. Structurally, it's a well-executed send: the three-category architecture maps cleanly to the subject line, the named-brand product picks (Black Diamond, Osprey, Mammut, POC) add credibility, and the Summit Club cash-back banner rewards members with a visible upside right at the top.
- The problem is cadence, not execution. Five days after an 8/10 category-sale send, the signal-to-noise ratio for a subscriber's inbox collapses. This email re-teaches a list that already has "Backcountry Summer Sale — outdoor categories, up to 40% off" locked in. The structural quality is real, but its lift is heavily discounted by fatigue.
- The single biggest fixable miss: no deadline anywhere in the email. No end date, no countdown, no "through Sunday." "All on sale" without a closing mechanism is an invitation to defer and forget.

## What's working

- **Category coherence:** Subject line ("Camp, climb, bike") maps exactly to the three email sections — extremely clean information architecture with zero ambiguity.
- **Named-brand product picks:** Black Diamond, Osprey, Mammut, Yakima, POC — actual product names and brand callouts build trust vs. a generic banner.
- **Section CTAs are specific:** "SHOP CAMP ON SALE," "SHOP CLIMB ON SALE," "SHOP BIKE ON SALE" are meaningfully better than three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- **Summit Club banner placement:** 10% cash back at the very top of the email — right where a member's eye lands first — is well-timed value signaling.
- **Layout renders cleanly:** No broken images, no overlapping text, fast scan in under 10 seconds.

## What's weak

- **Near-repeat of July 16:** "Summer's categories now all on sale 🚵 🧗🏾" → "Camp, climb, bike now all on sale ✓" is the same concept with a different checkmark. Five days is too tight a recycle.
- **No urgency mechanism:** No sale end date, no deadline on any section, no "while supplies last" scarcity hook. The sale exists in permanent ambiguous time.
- **Hero image is a small collage:** The lifestyle montage reads cluttered at email width — it doesn't deliver the single emotional pull that earns a hero image slot.
- **No price anchors on any product:** Six products shown, zero price strike-throughs. "Up to 40% off" stays abstract when you can't see $199 → $119 on the Osprey pack.
- **Zero social proof:** Not one star rating, not one "best seller" badge on any of the six featured items — especially damaging for climbing ropes and helmets where reviews reduce safety-purchase friction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the hero and each section header** — "Sale ends Sunday, July 27" turns passive browsing into an action decision. This is the single highest-lift change available.
- 2. **Show at least one price strike-through** — e.g., "~~$219~~ $129" on one featured pack makes the 40% claim tangible rather than a banner abstraction; the other products benefit by association.
- 3. **Lead with a freshness signal when re-running the same framework** — "New markdowns just added" or "Just landed in the sale" tells a subscriber why this email is different from the one five days ago.
- 4. **Replace the hero collage with one strong lifestyle shot** — a single climber, cyclist, or camper image at full width would outperform the montage on emotional pull.
- 5. **Add one social-proof badge per section** — even a "★ 4.7 · 412 reviews" on one product per category would meaningfully cut click-to-cart friction.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External stylesheet will be stripped (confirmed issue):**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), Apple Mail, and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags from email `<head>`. This font load silently fails for the majority of recipients. Custom fonts must be declared via an inline `@font-face` block inside a `<style>` tag to have any chance of rendering (still only in Apple Mail/some webmail).

**CSS selector typo — `col-9` rule targets `col-8` instead (confirmed issue):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets two conflicting width rules (16.5% from its own rule, then 74.25% from this one — last-write wins at 74.25%). Any 9-column responsive cell under `.md-fluid-row` will have no mobile width rule applied.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
`none` (not `100%`) completely disables iOS text scaling for all elements. This is an accessibility regression; `100%` is the correct value to prevent auto-sizing while preserving user zoom.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html>
```
No `lang="en"` declared. Screen readers cannot infer language for pronunciation; also required for WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1.
