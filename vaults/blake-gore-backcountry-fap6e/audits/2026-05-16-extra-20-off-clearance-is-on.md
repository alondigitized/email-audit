# EXTRA 20% off clearance is ON 🔓
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a workhorse sale-blast: clean hero, recognizable brand, and a concrete offer (Extra 20% off clearance) front-and-center. The Memorial Day timing gives it seasonal legitimacy, and the render is tight — no broken images, no layout issues. On those mechanics alone it clears the bar for a competent promotional send.
- The problem is offer dilution. By the time you scroll through a clearance extra-20% deal, a "SALE ON SALE" product grid, a Memorial Day up-to-30%-off section, and a sunglasses buy-2-save-20% section, none of the three promotions feel urgent or prioritized. The email is doing what a lot of outdoor retailers do — blast every active promo into one email and hope something sticks. It mostly doesn't.
- The timing is also awkward. Backcountry sent a welcome email on the same day (15% off next purchase). New subscribers getting a second email the same day — especially one that leads with clearance — risks training them to expect constant discounting before they've even made a first purchase.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate and specific.** "Extra 20% off clearance" with a clear SHOP NOW button is visible at a glance — no scrolling required.
- **Memorial Day hook is credible.** The seasonal tie-in is timely and gives the send a natural urgency peg (weekend = real deadline, even if unstated).
- **Named brands in the product grid.** Burton, The North Face, Outdoor Research — brand-name callouts in the clearance grid add credibility that generic product cards wouldn't.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text anywhere in the email.

## What's weak

- **Three competing offers with no hierarchy.** Extra 20% clearance, up to 30% Memorial Day, and sunglasses buy-2-save-20% are each asking for attention independently. No single offer wins.
- **No prices on the clearance product cards.** "Extra 20% off" only feels meaningful if you can see what you're starting from. None of the four featured products show an original or sale price.
- **"SALE SALE SALE" is visual noise.** The triple-stacked type treatment in the Memorial Day section looks aggressive and carnival-y — it undercuts Backcountry's aspirational outdoor positioning.
- **No deadline on the clearance offer.** The subject says the deal "is ON" but doesn't say when it ends. Memorial Day implies a natural end date, but the hero section doesn't make that connection.
- **Second email on the same day as the welcome.** This cadence signals volume over targeting and risks annoying brand-new subscribers who are still evaluating whether to stay opted in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one headline offer, one deadline.** Pick the Memorial Day clearance angle and merge the two discount mechanics: "Extra 20% off clearance, ends Monday." Remove the sunglasses section or push it to a dedicated send.
- 2. **Show at least one price.** Even one "Was $149 → Now $89 + extra 20%" in the hero grid makes the savings concrete and motivates clicks more than a percentage alone.
- 3. **Kill the "SALE SALE SALE" block.** Replace it with a single clean Memorial Day banner — a photo, the offer, one CTA. The triple repeat reads as desperation.
- 4. **Add an explicit countdown or deadline phrase.** "Ends Sunday night" or "Memorial Day weekend only" in the hero transforms a vague "is ON" into actionable urgency.
- 5. **Suppress from same-day welcome recipients.** A CRM suppression for the welcome cohort (or at minimum a 24-hour buffer) protects early trust.
