# Extra 15% off clearance! Only while supplies last
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's most damaging send of the cycle — not because the email itself is badly executed, but because of what it means in context. Three days ago subscribers received "EXTRA 20% off FINAL HOURS." Today they get "EXTRA 15% off clearance." The timeline is a flashing neon sign: the deadline was fake, the urgency was manufactured, and the new offer is actively worse than what supposedly just expired. That's not a campaign — it's a trust burn.
- Inside the email itself, the execution is actually solid. The personalization is the standout: a "JUST FOR YOU" framing with a named exclusive code (`EXTRAOFFSALE3EBIM3EWO`), a coherent men's-only product grid with real markup-to-sale pricing (Merrell hiking shoes at $73 down from $226 is a genuine deal), and a clean visual hierarchy that doesn't ask much of the reader. The bones are good.
- The verdict: this email would score 8-9 in isolation. In context, every signal it sends — urgency, exclusivity, limited supply — has been devalued by the prior three sends. Backcountry is conditioning its list to wait, not buy.

## What's working

- **Personalization is real**: "JUST FOR YOU" header + named exclusive promo code signals segmentation, not batch-blast.
- **Product grid is coherent**: All six products are men's outdoor gear; no cross-category noise or filler.
- **Pricing is honest and visible**: Crossed-out original prices next to sale prices — Merrell at $73.48 from $225.95 is a hard number that earns attention.
- **Clean visual hierarchy**: Hero → offer → code → CTA → product grid → category grid. The eye follows a logical path.
- **No render bugs**: Images load, prices display, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Offer regression destroys credibility**: The July 13 "FINAL HOURS" email promised 20% off was ending. Today's 15% offer proves that deadline was false — a worse deal appeared three days later.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is wasted**: A generic imperative on a segmented, personalized send. "Shop Men's Clearance" would take 30 seconds to write and convert better.
- **No social proof**: Six products with strong markdowns and zero reviews, ratings, or "X sold" signals to push hesitant buyers.
- **"Only while supplies last" urgency rings hollow**: After three false deadlines in a week, scarcity language has lost all punch with this list.
- **Category grid is filler**: The SKI and SNOWBOARD categories at the bottom of a mid-July clearance email are pure template inertia.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Acknowledge the discount honestly, or don't send.** A "supplies are moving fast — grab what's left" framing would be more credible than implying a new, independent event. The subscriber knows what was in their inbox on July 13.
- 2. **Make the CTA product-specific**: Change "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Men's Clearance Deals" — it matches the grid content and signals the subscriber they're going somewhere relevant.
- 3. **Add a review signal to the top product**: One star rating or a short pull quote under the Merrell shoe (the biggest discount) would reduce friction for the click.
- 4. **Kill SKI / SNOWBOARD from the category grid in July.** Replace with "FOOTWEAR" or "LAYERING" — categories that match the product grid and the season.
- 5. **Break the urgency loop**: The next send should be editorial (new arrivals, gear guide, brand story) to reset list fatigue before the next promo lands.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's clearance is moving — 15% extra with your code`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Merrell, Scarpa, Black Diamond — shop before sizes sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use EXTRAOFFSALE3EBIM3EWO at checkout — exclusive to you`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector mismatch (bug):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The selector pairs `.fluid-row .col-9` with `.md-fluid-row .col-8` — the second selector targets the wrong column class (`.col-8` instead of `.col-9`). On mobile, any element using `.md-fluid-row .col-9` will not receive its intended width. This is a copy-paste error in the responsive grid CSS.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixel:**
```
src: https://trk.b.backcountry.com/ss/o/u001.s4HNEC6Qw5Htp4aPAN5Gdw/4sd/UyHSNFUHR3ylyhhVkvnm4w/ho.gif
```
`ho.gif` has no `alt` attribute. For a tracking pixel this is typically `alt=""` (empty, intentional), but the absence of the attribute entirely triggers accessibility linters and some spam filters. Should be `alt=""` explicitly.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** is applied globally to `div, p, a, li, span, td`. The `none` value prevents user-initiated text scaling, which is an accessibility regression on iOS. `100%` is the correct value to prevent automatic adjustment while still allowing user-initiated zoom.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text alternative: 0 characters**
The MIME message contains no text/plain part. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a plain-text part, but its absence is a significant deliverability signal — Gmail, Outlook, and most commercial spam filters weight multipart/alternative heavily. A 0-char text body also breaks rendering in text-only clients and assistive technologies.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not found**
QA relay did not capture this header. If it is present upstream but stripped by the AgentMail relay, it should be verified at the raw SMTP layer. If genuinely absent, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (effective Feb 2024) for senders exceeding 5,000 msgs/day.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (POST mechanism) is required by Gmail for bulk senders. Without it, Gmail's unsubscribe button triggers a mailto fallback, which degrades UX and increases spam complaints. Evidence: QA header scan, no `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` value found.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header absent via relay**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. Sending subdomain is `b.backcountry.com`; DKIM should align to `backcountry.com` or `b.backcountry.com`. Cannot confirm without raw headers — flag for verification against Bluecore's DKIM signing configuration.
