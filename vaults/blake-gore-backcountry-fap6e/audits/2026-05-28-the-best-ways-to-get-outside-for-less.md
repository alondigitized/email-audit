# The best ways to get outside for less
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- After a week that delivered personal promo codes, countdown timers, and headlines like "Up to 40% off" and "Summer sale is ON," Backcountry pivots hard to a generic category-browse email with zero discount specificity. The layout is clean and the category tiles are well-executed, but "ON SALE" labels without a single percent or dollar figure attached is a significant step down from the precision this brand showed in the Memorial Day run. A subscriber who received the "Your 15% off code ends today!" send earlier this same day — and acted on it — has no obvious reason to open or click here.
- The Spring New Arrivals section at the bottom actively works against the sale framing at the top. The email can't decide if it's a sale email or a new-arrivals email, so it reads as neither. The hero copy ("We all summer a little different, but the essentials always remain the same") is brand-safe boilerplate that adds nothing.
- Clean render, trusted brand, relevant categories — and still a missed opportunity because there's no number to anchor the offer. This is an evergreen content slot dressed up as a promotional send.

## What's working

- Five category tiles (Footwear, Bike, Camp, Climb, Travel) give a clear, scannable browse path — a user with intent will find their lane fast.
- Brand logo strip (Merrell, Outdoor Research, +MORE) adds immediate credibility and signals product depth.
- Zero render bugs — lifestyle photography is sharp, text is crisp, buttons are clear.
- "Spring New Arrivals" photography at the bottom is high quality and visually distinct from the sale tiles above it.

## What's weak

- No specific discount percentage or dollar figure visible anywhere in the email — "ON SALE" labels without a number are the weakest possible call to action in this category.
- Subject line "The best ways to get outside for less" is 100% generic and a sharp regression from this brand's recent specific-offer subjects; nothing here creates open urgency.
- "Spring New Arrivals" at the bottom contradicts the sale positioning at the top and confuses the email's single job.
- This is the second Backcountry email on May 28 — same-day stacking after the personal promo code closer ("Your 15% off code ends today!") adds to subscriber fatigue without adding value.
- Hero copy is soft, brand-safe filler with no offer hook; the promotional intent isn't confirmed until the first category tile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a headline discount to the opening block.** "Up to 40% off" or "Our top 5 categories — all on sale now" above the category tiles would immediately justify opening. The tiles should inherit a displayed %-off, not just an "ON SALE" badge.
- 2. **Choose one job for this email.** Cut the Spring New Arrivals section entirely, or send it as its own email. Mixing sale and new-arrivals messaging dilutes both.
- 3. **Make the subject line earn its open.** The current subject is indistinguishable from a generic outdoor retailer drip; this brand has proven it can do better.
- 4. **Space out same-day sends.** Sending the promo-code closer and a category-browse in the same inbox day makes both feel cheaper.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off gear: footwear, camp, climb & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer kit is on sale — shop by category`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Merrell, Outdoor Research + top brands — all discounted now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your category. Save on everything from trail shoes to tents.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector typo — col-9 width miscalculation**
In the `<style>` block:
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` elements receive no explicit width at this breakpoint (they fall through to whatever the base table layout provides), while `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets a conflicting 74.25% rule on top of the correct 66% already declared three lines earlier. Any 9-column mid-breakpoint layout block will mis-render.
