# 20% off full-price items ends TODAY 🚨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is the close-out send on the 20% off full-price campaign that Backcountry first deployed on June 30 and has been keeping alive as a secondary offer through the 4th of July run. The urgency is real — the sale genuinely ends today — but the creative is showing its age: same offer, same "SHOP NOW" structure, same brand grid. The one fresh element is the Summit Club exclusive at the bottom (free Wasatch Merino Tee on orders $80+), which gives members an actual reason to act that goes beyond the discounted price.
- The bigger structural problem is the email runs two simultaneous closing offers — "20% off full-price items ends TODAY" in the hero, and "UP TO 60% OFF / 4th Of July Sale / ENDS TONIGHT!" in a separate section below. These are not the same thing, and the email doesn't explain how they coexist. A subscriber who reads this carefully may wonder whether their cart even qualifies for the 20% off if sale items are already 60% off. The dual urgency is a dilution.
- For an outdoor gear subscriber this email is relevant — North Face, Black Diamond, La Sportiva, Osprey, Outdoor Research, and Nemo are exactly the right brand names — but the execution is a sale-fatigue send dressed up with "ends TODAY" urgency that Backcountry has already used multiple times this week.

## What's working

- **Hero urgency is immediate.** "20% OFF / SELECT FULL-PRICE ITEMS / ENDS TODAY!" reads fast and the outdoor lifestyle imagery anchors the brand without confusion.
- **Brand grid is well-curated.** North Face, Black Diamond, La Sportiva, Nemo, Outdoor Research, Osprey — this is a credible shortlist that outdoor shoppers will recognize and trust.
- **Summit Club exclusive adds a pull tactic.** The free Wasatch Merino Tee (orders $80+) is a loyalty reward that makes the close-out feel like a genuine incentive for members, not just another nudge.
- **No render bugs.** The email loads cleanly — no broken images, no truncated text, no overlapping elements.
- **Subject line delivers.** "20% off full-price items ends TODAY 🚨" is concrete, under 40 characters, and the urgency is credible.

## What's weak

- **Dual-offer confusion.** The 20% off full-price hero and the separate "UP TO 60% OFF" 4th of July Sale section compete without explanation. Two different discount mechanics, two different urgency phrases ("ends TODAY" vs. "ENDS TONIGHT!"), zero bridge copy.
- **CTA copy is generic wall-to-wall.** Every button says "SHOP NOW" — the hero, the text block, the category grid, the July sale section. None of them link to a specific product or brand.
- **Category grid adds no targeting value.** "Men's Clothing / Women's Clothing / Men's Footwear / Women's Footwear / Hike & Camp" is the same structure any Backcountry email could send to any subscriber on any day.
- **Offer fatigue is real.** This is the seventh Backcountry email in seven days, and the 20% off full-price deal has now appeared in three of them. The urgency is worn down.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill one offer, commit to the other.** Either this is the 20% off full-price closer or the 4th of July Sale closer — not both. The hero should own one mechanic and the email should deliver on that one thing crisply. The split dilutes both.
- 2. **Swap generic CTA copy for brand-specific links.** Instead of five "SHOP NOW" buttons, give each brand tile a "Shop The North Face" / "Shop Black Diamond" link. Lift is immediate.
- 3. **Bring the Summit Club exclusive up.** The free Merino Tee is the freshest creative element; bury it last and most subscribers never see it. Move it to just below the hero.
- 4. **Add at least one specific hero product.** Pin one jacket or boot that's actually 20% off today. A named product with a specific discount converts better than a category promise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off The North Face, Osprey & more — ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: full-price gear 20% off + free merino tee`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop The North Face, Black Diamond, La Sportiva and more before the sale ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summit Club members: free Wasatch Merino Tee on $80+ orders — today only`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — External `<link>` stylesheet stripped by major clients:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip `<link>` tags in email `<head>`. The font load will silently fail, falling back to system defaults. Custom font declarations should be inlined via `@font-face` inside a `<style>` block if brand font consistency matters.

**Issue — CSS selector typo in responsive grid:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, any `.md-fluid-row .col-9` element receives no width override and will render at whatever the table default is; `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets a double-applied width rule (66% from its own rule *and* 74.25% from this one — last-write wins, so 74.25% applies, making 8-column cells too wide on medium viewports).

**Issue — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html>
```
Should be `<html lang="en">`. Affects screen reader language selection and can increase spam scoring with some filters.

**No issues found** — `charset=utf-8`, `viewport`, and `format-detection=telephone=no` are all correctly declared.
