# Gear questions? We’re here to guide you!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- After six blasts in five days — two of them landing on May 20 alone — Backcountry pivots here to its editorial content format, framing the send around Gearhead® expert picks rather than a marquee discount. The email does three things at once: merchandises four seasonal categories, drops a 15% promo code mid-scroll, and cross-sells the Summit loyalty program. That's too many masters for one send. But the execution is clean, the brand voice is coherent, and the format change is earned after the Memorial Day sale barrage.
- The email's biggest liability is its own subject line. "Gear questions? We're here to guide you!" hides everything valuable inside — the 15% code, the seasonal category edit, the Summit offer. After a week of concrete discount subjects (Extra 20% Off, 30% Off, Memorial Day Sale), this soft service-framing will bleed opens from subscribers conditioned to Backcountry's promotional envelope. The body outperforms what the subject promises.
- Verdict: a competent cadence reset that earns its place structurally, but the subject squanders the content inside.

## What's working

- **Gearhead hero is immediately legible** — the dramatic mountain-hiker image and "Gearhead® Picks" headline establish editorial authority at a glance.
- **Four category tiles are seasonally coherent** — Bike, Travel, Camp picks map directly to Memorial Day long-weekend purchase intent.
- **15% Off offer is concrete and visible** — mid-email promo code section gives subscribers a transactional reason to click even if the editorial tone doesn't hook them.
- **Summit Cash Back section adds a second conversion path** — loyalty upsell closes the email with real member value rather than a generic footer plug.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — text, images, and layout all intact.

## What's weak

- **Subject is pure service-speak, zero offer signal** — the 15% code and seasonal picks are invisible from the inbox preview; there is no reason to open.
- **Too many competing CTAs** — four "SHOP NOW" buttons, one "GET 15% OFF," and one "JOIN NOW" leave no single conversion priority.
- **Category tiles, not products** — "Bike Picks" with a SHOP NOW link is a much lower-intent click than a specific item with name, image, and price.
- **No expiry on the 15% offer** — after a week of time-boxed sales, a deadline-free discount reads as evergreen and therefore less urgent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 15% code in the subject** — it's the clearest reason to open and it's completely hidden. This is the single change with the most impact.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 15% off code is inside — Gearhead picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Bike picks, camp essentials, 15% off — shop the edit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Gearhead edit for Memorial Day weekend, plus a discount code to use right now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four categories. One code. Shop what our experts actually reach for.`
- 2. **Promote the 15% Off section to hero position** — move the discount above the category grid so the transactional value lands before the editorial browse.
- 3. **Add a hard expiry date to the promo code** — "Valid through May 26" is costless and restores the urgency mechanics the subject completely abandoned.
- 4. **Swap category tiles for one specific hero product per category** — a real item with a name, original price, and sale price converts at a meaningfully higher rate than a label.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] CSS selector typo — `.md-fluid-row .col-9` missing, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives conflicting width**

In the responsive `<style>` block:
```css
/* Line as written: */
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. Consequence:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` now has two competing mobile widths (`66%` from its own rule, `74.25%` from this rule — the later rule wins, so col-8 renders at 74.25% instead of 66%).
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no mobile override and will retain its desktop width on small screens.

**[MEDIUM] External font stylesheet will silently fail in most clients**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Samsung Mail strip `<link>` tags. Custom typefaces will not load for the majority of recipients; fallback font stack behavior depends entirely on inline styles not visible in the truncated source.

**[LOW] `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` applied too broadly**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
Disabling auto-sizing on all text elements prevents iOS Mail from upscaling small text. Any font size below ~13px will remain unreadable on small-screen iPhones. Scope this to specific elements where you explicitly control size.

**[LOW] `format-detection` only suppresses telephone**

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
iOS Mail will still auto-link addresses and dates. Add `address=no,date=no` to prevent unintended tap targets wrapping physical address text in the footer.

**[INFO] Trailing non-breaking space in `<title>`**

```html
<title>Gear questions? We&#8217;re here to guide you!&#160;</title>
```
`&#160;` (NBSP) at end of title is benign but unnecessary noise.
