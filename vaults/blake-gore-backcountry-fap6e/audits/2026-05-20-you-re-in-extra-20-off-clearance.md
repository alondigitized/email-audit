# You’re in! EXTRA 20% off clearance
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the "VIP Early Access" wrapper on the same Extra 20% off clearance offer Backcountry has been running since May 16 — the same offer that explicitly "ended today" in the May 18 close email. The creative is clean and competent: strong hero, unambiguous offer, four named products with individual CTAs, and a well-timed Memorial Day teaser slotted in the footer. The "You're in!" subject and the VIP EARLY ACCESS banner at the top work hard to frame this as a member-exclusive.
- The problem is credibility. Any subscriber who received the "ends today" email two days ago and chose not to buy is now being told the same deal is back — just rebranded as "early access." That's not exclusivity; it's fake urgency retroactively exposed. The framing might land on subscribers who missed the original run, but for anyone who's been in the inbox all week it reads as an extension wearing a costume.
- The execution is competent — layout is clean, products are real, the Memorial Day tease is smart forward-planning — but the story wrapped around the offer is the thing doing the most damage.

## What's working

- Hero is immediate: "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE" is the first thing you see, zero ambiguity.
- "You're in!" plus the VIP EARLY ACCESS banner in the nav creates a genuine exclusivity signal before the email even renders.
- Four specific named products (two Patagonia, one Cotopaxi, one Free People) give the body browsing value rather than a generic category dump.
- Memorial Day Sale teaser in the footer is good forward-planting for the next send without overwhelming the current offer.
- No render bugs, clean layout, consistent Backcountry brand voice end-to-end.

## What's weak

- Credibility is the core miss: this offer "ended" on May 18 and is back 48 hours later as "VIP Access" — which makes all prior deadline language feel fabricated.
- No visible deadline in the body copy — "early access" implies scarcity but there's no "through Sunday" or "window closes tonight" to actually close the loop.
- Free People "Framed Cable Sweater" breaks product cohesion hard — this is Backcountry, and that slot should be a trail layer, boot, or baselayer, not a fashion brand.
- All four product CTAs read "Shop Now" — identical copy across different products is a missed conversion lever.
- The Memorial Day footer competes with the clearance hero for campaign identity; a subscriber can't tell which one is the point of this email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a real deadline in the body.** "Your VIP window closes Sunday at midnight — after that, we open this to everyone" converts the vague "early access" promise into actual urgency and makes the exclusivity claim true rather than just labeled.
- 2. **Make the product CTAs specific.** "Shop This Vest," "Shop This Pack" — takes 30 seconds to swap and adds intent-matching that generic "Shop Now" cannot.
- 3. **Cut the Free People sweater.** Replace it with a hard outdoor product (trail shoe, shell, tent) to keep the persona tightly outdoor. Free People in a Backcountry clearance block reads like a remnant from a wrong segment.
- 4. **Acknowledge the re-open honestly in copy.** Instead of "get the best before the rest," write: "As a VIP member, you get first crack before we open this to the full list Monday." It turns a credibility problem into a benefit statement.
- 5. **Isolate the Memorial Day tease to a footer or remove it.** Two campaign stories in one email dilutes both; let the clearance email own the moment.

- **Subject Alt B:** `VIPs first: 20% off clearance before everyone else gets in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First pick on clearance before the general sale opens Monday — your window closes Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two Patagonia picks, a Cotopaxi pack, and more — 20% off, your window first.`

## 1. Overview

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo:**

```css
/* Line in mobile media query */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` elements receive **no mobile width override** (they fall through to 100% with the catch-all rule, which may or may not be correct depending on layout intent).
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets **double-targeted** — matched by both the `.col-8` rule (66%) and this rule (74.25%); the latter wins due to selector specificity tie + source order. This could cause unintended column widths in any two-column `.md-fluid-row` section.

**Redundant media query wrapping:**

```html
<style type="text/css" media="only screen and (max-width: 600px)">
  @media screen and (max-width: 600px) { ... }
</style>
```

The outer `media` attribute and the inner `@media` block are redundant. Harmless in practice (browsers handle it), but adds unnecessary parsing overhead.

**`display: none !important` on `.mobile-hide`:** Elements hidden this way are removed from the accessibility tree. If any `.mobile-hide` elements contain meaningful content (e.g., alt text, links), screen reader users on mobile won't reach them. Cannot fully assess without knowing which elements carry this class.
