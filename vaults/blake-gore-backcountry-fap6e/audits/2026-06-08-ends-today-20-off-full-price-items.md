# Ends today! 20% off full-price items 🚨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth distinct send in Backcountry's second arc of "20% off full-price items" — a campaign that already ran a full five-email arc May 29–June 1, was briefly displaced by a Summer Sale on June 2–3, then relaunched June 5 with "VIP early access" framing. "Ends today!" is the third distinct deadline on this exact offer: June 2 said "ends tonight," June 7 said "ends TOMORROW," and now June 8 says "ends today." Subscribers who've been paying attention have been trained that these deadlines are performative. Trust cost is real and cumulative.
- The render itself is technically clean. Visual hierarchy works — the 20% headline hits first, the partner logos reinforce authority, the product tiles and category grid give people things to click. The Summer Sale callout at the bottom adds confusion, not depth: the subscriber is now looking at two simultaneous promotions (20% off full-price vs. up to 40% off sale) in the same scroll, and neither is clearly dominant.
- The email is competent creative serving a broken strategy. The execution doesn't save the fatigue.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is strong.** "20% OFF FULL-PRICE ITEMS" lands immediately and big; the "ENDS TODAY" ticker above it reinforces the deadline without obscuring the offer.
- **Partner brand logos earn trust.** The North Face, Osprey, and one other visible logo anchor credibility for the discount.
- **"Last Chance to Save on These Styles" product tiles** give specific gear to consider, moving beyond pure category browsing.
- **Top Categories grid is well-executed.** Six lifestyle-photo tiles with labeled categories (Men's Jackets, Women's Jackets, Camp & Hike, Travel, Footwear) give natural entry points for different intents.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks.

## What's weak

- **"Ends today!" is the third fake deadline on this offer.** At this point it reads as a dark pattern, not urgency. The June 2 send said "ends tonight." June 7 said "ends TOMORROW." This send says "ends today." The deadline has zero credibility.
- **Primary CTA copy is counter-intuitive.** The hero button reads "BUY FULL PRICE →" — which communicates the opposite of a savings offer. A subscriber scanning quickly may misread this as confirmation they'll pay full price.
- **Two overlapping promotions in one email.** 20% off full-price (hero) and Summer Sale up to 40% off (footer) compete with each other. The subscriber doesn't know which deal to take or which is the better value.
- **No urgency reinforcement in the body.** The ticker says "ENDS TODAY" but the body copy doesn't repeat the deadline or give a cut-off time (midnight PST, end of day, etc.), making the urgency feel hollow.
- **Cadence is visibly fatiguing.** This is the third email in three days on the same offer. A real subscriber has mentally filed this as background noise by now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or kill the fake deadline.** If the offer is genuinely ending today, add a specific time ("midnight PST tonight") and honor it. If it's not, drop the "ends today" framing entirely — you've already spent down the credibility of that deadline twice this week.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** Replace "BUY FULL PRICE →" with "SHOP 20% OFF →" or "SAVE ON FULL-PRICE GEAR →". The current copy actively undermines the value prop.
- 3. **Collapse to one promotion per email.** Drop the Summer Sale footer block from this send. Pick one offer, go deep on it. The dual-promo layout dilutes both offers and makes the email harder to act on.
- 4. **Add a cut-off clock or at least a time stamp.** "Ends today at midnight PT" beats "ends today" by a measurable margin for conversion — it's specific and auditable.
- 5. **Rotate or retire the creative.** The hero image, layout, and product tiles are nearly identical to June 6 and June 7. If you're going to send a fourth arc email, give it a different visual entry point.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Last call: 20% off The North Face, Osprey + more"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Tonight only: 20% off full-price outdoor gear"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Your cart items just got cheaper — through midnight PT."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"The North Face, On, Osprey: full-price gear, 20% less."`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — duplicate CSS selector causes wrong column width on mobile:**

```css
/* Rule 1 */
.fluid-row .col-8, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }

/* Rule 2 — selector typo: .md-fluid-row .col-8 should be .md-fluid-row .col-9 */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` appears in both rules. Because Rule 2 is declared last, it wins — `.md-fluid-row .col-8` renders at **74.25%** instead of 66% on mobile. Separately, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives **no mobile width rule at all**.

**Issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` on broad selector:**

```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

The value `none` fully disables font-size adjustment, which can prevent users who rely on browser/OS text-scaling from adjusting type. Correct value is `100%`.

**Issue — `<link>` stylesheet from external CDN:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```

Most email clients (Gmail web, Outlook, Apple Mail in some configurations) strip `<link>` tags. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. This is expected behavior for email but warrants verified fallback font stacks in inline styles.
