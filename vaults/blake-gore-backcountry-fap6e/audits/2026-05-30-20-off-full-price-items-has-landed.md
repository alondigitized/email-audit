# 20% off full-price items has landed
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the public launch activation for the 20% off full-price items event that Backcountry teased to VIP subscribers yesterday ("Early access! 20% off full-price items"). The two-email tease-then-launch sequence is coherent — "has landed" is a logical follow-up to "early access." The email itself is clean: bold white discount headline over an outdoor action hero, specific brand badges (NEMO, TNF, OR) calling out scope, a hard end date, and four relevant product cards beneath. It follows the established Backcountry visual playbook without deviation.
- The problem is that this subscriber has now received at least eight Backcountry promotional emails in roughly four days. The creative is competent but the cadence is corrosive. "Has landed" is also the softest urgency language Backcountry has used all week — after countdown timers, 😬 emojis, and "ends TONIGHT" closers, this subject feels flat.
- Judgement: Solid execution of a planned sequence, but arriving in a fatigued inbox with no new hook beyond activation. A middling-impact send that does the job without doing anything memorable.

## What's working

- **Two-step cadence pays off** — yesterday's "early access" framing makes "has landed" feel earned rather than random.
- **Hero is immediately scannable** — "20% OFF FULL-PRICE ITEMS" + NEMO / TNF / OR badges communicate scope in a single glance.
- **Explicit deadline** — "Deal ends 6/1" sits directly below the offer; no fine-print hunt required.
- **Both men's and women's product cards** — the grid shows relevant apparel for both personas without feeling padded.
- **Summit Club loyalty bar** at the top gives members a stacking incentive (10% cash back) the subject line doesn't mention.

## What's weak

- **"Has landed" carries zero urgency** — after a week of countdown timers and "ends TONIGHT," this reads like a product page notification, not a call to act now.
- **Four product cards with four identical "Shop Now" CTAs** — no single hero product to anchor the click; focus is diluted.
- **No prices shown on product cards** — the entire value proposition of this email is a discount, yet the cards show no original price, no sale price, no "save $X."
- **The Cotopaxi shoulder bag** (Trico-XL Cargo Sac) is a fashion accessory in an email that leads with Osprey and Outdoor Research — it muddies the outdoor-first positioning signal.
- **Cadence is at subscriber-list-burn territory** — send 8+ in four days; even a legitimate, well-designed email loses lift here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add prices and savings to product cards.** "Was $89 → $71" is the single highest-impact change. The email's whole reason to exist is a discount; hiding the math forces subscribers to click just to know if it's worth it.
- 2. **Tighten urgency in the hero copy.** Swap "Deal ends 6/1" for "Ends Sunday" or add a three-day countdown badge — dates require mental math, days remaining don't.
- 3. **Pin one hero product.** Feature one aspirational item (Osprey pack, TNF jacket) with a before/after price and make it the dominant click target; let the product grid be secondary.
- 4. **Replace the shoulder bag** with a hard-use outdoor item (footwear, pack, baselayer) to stay on-brand and reinforce the "gear" positioning the brands section signals.
- 5. **Pull cadence back.** One fewer email this week would protect list health more than any creative tweak.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `NEMO, Rab, On, Outdoor Research all included — full-price items only, ends 6/1.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack with your Summit Club cash back — deal window closes Sunday night.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED BUG] CSS selector typo — col-9 fluid layout broken on `md-fluid-row`:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. This duplicates the col-8 width rule on `md-fluid-row` containers and leaves `.md-fluid-row .col-9` unstyled. Any 9-column block inside a `md-fluid-row` will render with incorrect width on mobile.

**[RISK] External font dependency — single point of failure:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
If `partner-fonts.bluecore.com` is slow or unavailable at render time, the font stack silently degrades. No `font-display` strategy is available in email context. Acceptable if Bluecore SLA is trusted, but worth noting for deliverability-time performance.

**[RISK] Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag:**
The opening `<html>` tag has no `lang="en"` attribute. Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) will not correctly infer language, and some spam filters flag this. Evidence: `<html>` appears without a lang attribute in the source.

**[RISK] Overly broad `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
Setting `none` (not `100%`) on all text elements disables iOS auto-scaling entirely, which can make small text inaccessible on older devices. `100%` is the correct value to suppress auto-scaling while preserving legibility.
