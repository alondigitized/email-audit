# More brands on sale! 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a Memorial Day Sale expansion email — Backcountry is announcing that new brands (Blue Ice, Cotopaxi, and a handful of others visible in the "More Brands on Sale" grid) have been added to the ongoing 30%-off sale. The structure is a vertical brand-by-brand catalog: each brand gets a hero image block and a "SHOP NOW" button. It covers a lot of ground — mountaineering gear (Blue Ice), colorful lifestyle packs (Cotopaxi), camp bundles, hike bundles, a sun-and-swim bundle — and then, at the very bottom, the zombie returns: the EXTRA 20% OFF clearance banner, the same mechanism that "ended" on May 18, was resurrected May 20, blasted again May 21, and is now making yet another appearance.
- The fresh content here — specifically Blue Ice and Cotopaxi being added to the sale — is genuinely useful to an outdoor subscriber. But the email is trying to do two things at once (Memorial Day Sale expansion + clearance extension), and it does both at medium volume rather than either at full force. The subject, "More brands on sale! 👀," is light: the 👀 emoji is playful but contributes nothing specific, and "more" is a relative word that requires a subscriber to remember prior context.
- Verdict: solid mid-tier promotional execution with real offer content and no visual breakage, but the clearance tail undermines the Memorial Day lead, and the subject undersells the actual news (named brands, up to 30% off).

## What's working

- The brand-by-brand structure is clean — each section has a logo, product images, and a dedicated "SHOP NOW" button, so a subscriber can jump straight to their brand.
- Blue Ice and Cotopaxi are category-relevant picks for a core Backcountry subscriber; adding named brands to the Memorial Day frame is a stronger hook than generic sitewide-sale language.
- The orange-on-white button styling is consistent and easy to spot — CTAs don't get lost in the layout.
- The bundle blocks (Hike / Camp / Sun & Swim, each at 20% off) are a useful format change mid-email — they offer a curated angle rather than just more products.
- No visible render breakage: images load, text is legible, spacing is consistent throughout.

## What's weak

- The subject line "More brands on sale! 👀" is one of the weakest possible frames — "more" implies you already know what brands were on sale before, and the 👀 adds nothing if Blue Ice and Cotopaxi aren't named.
- The EXTRA 20% OFF clearance section at the bottom is back again — after being declared over May 18, resurrected May 20, and blasted May 21. Its presence here is now noise, not urgency.
- No explicit deadline is visible anywhere in the email — Memorial Day implies "before Monday" but the email never says it, which softens urgency meaningfully.
- "SHOP NOW" repeated across every brand section is generic; with five or six of them stacked, the CTAs blur together and create decision fatigue rather than action.
- The catalog format rewards browsing, not buying — no single hero product or moment anchors the email and gives the reader one obvious place to land.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the brand names in the subject.** "Blue Ice & Cotopaxi just joined the Memorial Day Sale" beats "More brands on sale!" by a mile — you're naming things the subscriber already has opinions about.
- 2. **Kill or quarantine the clearance tail.** The EXTRA 20% OFF clearance section has appeared in every send this week. Either feature it prominently as the main story or drop it entirely; tacking it to the bottom of a Memorial Day Sale email just confirms it's not a real event.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline in the hero copy.** "Up to 30% off — ends Monday" is one line. Without it, nothing in this email creates action today vs. tomorrow vs. never.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTA copy per brand.** "Shop Blue Ice" and "Shop Cotopaxi" cost nothing to write and make the catalog scannable without reading every image.
- 5. **Pick one hero product per brand.** Rather than a product grid per section, one strong hero image (a specific pack, a specific jacket) with price would give the subscriber a concrete starting point.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Blue Ice crampons, Cotopaxi packs, and more — up to 30% off through Memorial Day`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New brands just dropped into the sale. Shop before Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — CSS selector bug:**

```css
/* In the mobile media query: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. This is a copy-paste error in the Bluecore template. At `max-width: 600px`, any `md-fluid-row` 9-column element will incorrectly inherit the 8-column (66%) width rule rather than this rule, and the 9-column rule is never applied to `md-fluid-row`. Impact is layout-width drift on any row using `md-fluid-row col-9`.

**Confirmed issue — missing `lang` attribute:**

```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```

Screen readers cannot determine language without this. Required for WCAG 2.1 Level A (Success Criterion 3.1.1). Should be `<html lang="en">`.

**No issues found:** viewport meta, charset declaration, format-detection, and media query structure are all correct.
