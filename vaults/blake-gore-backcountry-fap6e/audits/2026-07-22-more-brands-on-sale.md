# More brands on sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's third structurally identical sale-organizing email in six days. The July 16 "Summer's categories now all on sale" and July 21 "Camp, climb, bike now all on sale ✓" both used the exact same frame — category tiles, brand tile grid, trending product row — and this email reproduces that template without alteration. The only freshness peg is "MORE BRANDS ADDED," which is a legitimate reason to re-ping a sale subscriber, but the execution is so close to the prior two sends that the incremental lift over just not sending is marginal.
- Within the email itself the production holds up. The hero is clean, the dual-message (UP TO 40% OFF / MORE BRANDS ADDED) reads fast, and the brand tile section — Patagonia, Black Diamond, Suunto, Castelli, Cotopaxi — does real name-recognition work. The Run Bundle cross-sell at the bottom is the most interesting creative choice in the email: a second purchase mechanic (Buy 2+, save 20%) that breaks the monotony of straight-discount framing.
- The subject line is the hard problem. "More brands on sale!" has no %, no brand name, no category, no deadline. It could have been sent by any outdoor retailer on any summer day. After seven-plus sends in ten days, Backcountry subscribers scanning their inbox have strong pattern-matching incentives to skip this one without opening.

## What's working

- "MORE BRANDS ADDED" callout in the hero gives a genuine re-engagement hook — dynamic inventory sales do warrant a re-ping
- "UP TO 40% OFF" is large, prominent, and readable in the hero before any scrolling
- Brand tile grid earns its keep — Patagonia and Black Diamond on sale is news worth showing
- Run Bundle cross-sell (Buy 2+, save 20%) is the most differentiated creative beat in the email vs. the prior two sends
- Product grid puts specific merchandise (shoes, dress, shorts) in front of the reader immediately below the hero

## What's weak

- "More brands on sale!" is the weakest subject line in this audit series — no %, no brand, no category, no urgency
- Template is near-identical to July 21 and July 16 sends; no creative variation to interrupt the pattern for fatigued subscribers
- No visible deadline anywhere in the email — "more brands added" implies a live, dynamic event but there's nothing telling me when it closes
- "SHOP NOW" and bare category arrows (Camp →, Climb →) have no specificity; nothing makes one tile more compelling than another
- The "low inventory" mention in body copy is buried in a long sentence and doesn't land as a scarcity signal

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject line.** Naming a brand or anchoring to a specific % would immediately separate this email from inbox clutter. The July 21 subject "Camp, climb, bike now all on sale ✓" was more specific than this; "More brands on sale!" is a step backward.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Patagonia, Black Diamond + more just joined the sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New brands added — still up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `More inventory means another shot at what you passed on last week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bestsellers disappear fast — here's what's newly on sale today.`
- 2. **Give the hero a deadline or a "live sale" mechanic.** "More brands added" implies this sale is dynamic; back that claim with "updated daily through July 25" or a visible timestamp. Without it, urgency is theoretical.
- 3. **Name a brand in the hero creative itself.** "Patagonia just joined the sale" or a brand logo in the hero image does more work than the generic "MORE BRANDS ADDED" text banner.
- 4. **Differentiate the category tile CTAs.** "Camp →" is invisible. "Camp deals — up to 35% off →" or a single featured product image per category gives the click a reason.
- 5. **Vary the template structure.** Three near-identical emails in six days trains subscribers to scroll past without engaging. A curated "editor's picks" row, a different color treatment, or collapsing the category tiles in favor of a single featured brand story would reset the pattern.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **External font load** — `https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css` is fetched at render time. Email clients that block remote resources (Outlook desktop, many corporate proxies) will drop the custom font silently. No inline `font-family` fallback stack is present in the `<head>`, so affected recipients fall back to the client's default serif, which may break layout metrics.
- **Sending subdomain** — `b.backcountry.com` is Bluecore's standard sending domain; see Compliance section for authentication implications.
- HTML is truncated — full link-level UTM and redirect-chain audit is noted under §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in responsive grid:**
```css
/* line in <style media="only screen and (max-width: 600px)"> */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, `.col-9` columns inside `.md-fluid-row` containers receive no mobile width override and fall through to the inherited desktop width. `.col-8` gets `74.25%` applied redundantly (its own rule already sets `66%`).

**Widget IDs in use** — six Bluecore widget blocks are present (`widget_e24c3988...`, `widget_677ee7...`, etc.). Each scopes its own mobile overrides, which is correct pattern, but the number of `!important` overrides per widget increases CSS specificity debt and makes future template edits fragile.
