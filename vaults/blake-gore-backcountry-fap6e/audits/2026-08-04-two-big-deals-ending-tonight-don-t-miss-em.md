# Two big deals, ending tonight - don’t miss ‘em
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a hard pivot from the last four Backcountry sends in this same "two deals ending" cycle — no hero numeral, no lifestyle photography, no product shots at all. It's a plain-text "personal note from Gearhead Alex" format: a paragraph of copy, a small circular headshot, and a black text-only "SHOP THE SALE" button, followed by three dense blocks of legal fine print. Compared to the 7/31–8/3 sends that scored 7-9 on the strength of big bold hero graphics and lifestyle imagery, this one strips away every merchandising visual in favor of a folksy, low-production "one last nudge" email.
- The strategy makes some sense as a fatigue-breaker — this is the fourth or fifth near-identical "final call" combo email this week, and a plain-text personal note is a legitimate way to cut through inbox blindness to the same offer. But execution-wise it sacrifices the thing that made prior sends click-worthy: a legible, scannable hero. There's no product to visually anchor the offer, no urgency countdown treatment, nothing but paragraphs of text before a generic button.
- Net: credible urgency and honest copy, but a visually flat send that will underperform the brand's stronger hero-driven emails on click-through, even if the personal-note gimmick helps it stand out in a fatigued inbox.

## What's working

- Deadline is explicit and credible — "Both deals wrapping tonight at 11:59pm MT" gives a real, specific cutoff.
- The two offers are both named with actual numbers (extra 20% off clearance, 20% off select full-price) rather than vague "big savings" language.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- The personal-note format (headshot + signed-off "Catch you on the mountain, Gearhead Alex") is a deliberate change of pace from the standard hero-banner format and could cut through repetition fatigue.

## What's weak

- Zero product imagery anywhere in the email — no gear shots, no lifestyle photo, nothing to visually sell the sale.
- No visual hierarchy: it's a wall of body copy before you even reach the CTA, so the eye has nowhere obvious to land first.
- CTA is fully generic ("Shop the Sale") — doesn't point to a category or specific product, despite the brand having Men/Women/Bike categories in the nav.
- The bottom third of the email is three separate blocks of dense legal terms, which visually dominates more space than the offer itself.
- This is now the 4th-5th consecutive send this week hammering the identical two-offer combo (20% off full-price + extra 20% off clearance) — cadence fatigue is real and this send does nothing to disguise the repetition, it just repackages it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Bring back a visual hero — even a simple banner stating "20% OFF + EXTRA 20% OFF" with one product shot would out-click plain text; the personal-note tone can stay in the body copy below it.
- 2. Make the CTA category-specific where possible (e.g., "Shop Clearance" or "Shop Men's Gear") instead of the generic "Shop the Sale."
- 3. Move or condense the three fine-print blocks — collapse into one shorter combined terms block so the offer content dominates the visual weight of the email.
- 4. Given this is deal #4-5 in the same cycle, this would be a good moment to try a genuinely different offer frame (bundle deal, specific category spotlight) rather than another "ending tonight" variant on the same two discounts.
