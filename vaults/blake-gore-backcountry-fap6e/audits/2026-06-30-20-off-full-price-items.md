# 20% off full-price items 👀
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a quiet, low-stakes "keep the full-price offer alive" send dispatched on June 30 — the same 20% off full-price deal that appeared as the secondary offer buried inside the June 19 campaign opener. Backcountry is essentially recycling a de-prioritized offer and giving it its own send, while the Summer Sale at the bottom is presumably the active promotion. That strategic conflict is the defining problem here: the hero says "20% off full-price," but the email closes on "Up to 40% off Sale." A subscriber who scrolls past the hero finds a better deal at the bottom. Why would they pay full price?
- The creative is clean and functional — hero image is attractive, the category grid works, there are no render bugs — but the offer framing is muddled. "Full-price items" is the weakest possible discount hook: you're asking someone to pay retail and feel good about 20% off, while simultaneously telegraphing that 40% sale items exist. The editorial picks ("Explore Top Picks For You") add some interest but lack any personalization signal.
- This send does nothing wrong visually, but it does nothing strategically right either. It's a B-team email running while the A-team promo (the Summer Sale) sits a scroll away.

## What's working

- Hero creative is visually strong — outdoor action image with clear white overlay text, no render bugs
- 20% offer is legible and prominent in the hero without scrolling
- "Shop Now" CTA is unambiguous and appears above the fold
- Category grid (Men's Clothing, Women's Clothing, Men's Footwear, Women's Footwear, Hike & Camp) gives subscribers clear navigation lanes
- Summer Sale banner at the bottom adds urgency that the hero alone lacks

## What's weak

- The Summer Sale 40% off tile at the bottom cannibalizes the hero — it's a strictly better deal and undermines the 20% full-price message
- "Explore Top Picks For You" editorial section has no visible personalization signal — feels like a generic product grid dressed up as a recommendation
- The hero copy text is small and low-contrast on mobile — offer details feel dense for a quick read
- Subject line "20% off full-price items 👀" is a near-verbatim repeat of the June 19 offer that was already buried as a secondary message
- No loyalty or member pricing callout visible, which Backcountry has used effectively in prior sends

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the hero/footer conflict.** If the active campaign is the Summer Sale (40% off), make that the hero. If this email is intentionally about full-price 20% off, cut the sale tile at the bottom. Running both in the same email teaches subscribers to scroll past the hero to find the better deal.
- 2. **Anchor the 20% offer to a specific category or curated edit** — "20% off our picks for your summer kit" is a stronger hook than generic full-price. Tie it to a season or activity.
- 3. **Add credibility to the "Top Picks For You" block** — a single visible star rating or bestseller callout would justify the "for you" language.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Your 20% off window closes tonight — full-price picks inside`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `Still full price? Not for long — 20% off this week only`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab the gear that sells out before the sale even starts.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off items that never hit the sale rack — shop the full-price selects.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — mobile CSS selector typo:**
```css
/* Line in mobile @media block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets overridden to `74.25%` on mobile (stomping the correct `66%` set in the prior rule), while `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives no mobile override at all. This will break any two-column layout using those classes on mobile viewports.

**External font dependency risk:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
`<link>` tags in email `<head>` are stripped or blocked by Gmail (web), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. If the custom font fails to load and no `font-family` stack fallback is defined in inline styles, text will render in the client's default serif/sans. Cannot confirm fallback presence without seeing full widget inline styles.
