# Win a $20k adventure + tons of gear ⛰️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a co-branded sweepstakes email from Backcountry x EF Adventures dangling a $20k adventure trip plus $2,000 in gear. After a week of back-to-back Flash Sale and 4th of July promotional emails, this send does exactly what it needs to on cadence: it breaks the discount fatigue loop. The creative is clean, the partnership framing is legitimate, and the prize values are front-and-center — no guesswork.
- The problem is structural. This is a sweepstakes email with no visible entry deadline, no purchase incentive, and a secondary prize described as "tons of gear" in the subject when the actual value is $2,000. Without a closing date anywhere in the body, the default subscriber response is "I'll enter later" — which means never. Compare this to the June 25 "Up to 70% off ends TMRW" closer, which used a single urgency lever (tomorrow) to max out its conversion window. This email has no equivalent.
- The "Gear Up For Your Future Trip" product section is the right secondary mechanic — use the sweepstakes to merchandise real products — but it's underdeveloped. Four products with minimal detail and no prices doesn't close anyone.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong** — outdoor explorers on rock with natural light; co-branding with EF Adventures reads as a real partnership, not an ad buy
- **Prize values are concrete and prominent** — "$20k Adventure" and "$2,000 worth of gear" are both surfaced above the fold, no fine-print hunting required
- **Co-brand explanation is efficient** — the "Two Experts, One Direction" copy block earns the partnership in three sentences without overselling
- **Cadence variety is earned** — the sweepstakes format is a genuine break from the Flash Sale / 4th of July discount loop; this doesn't feel like a fifth-consecutive-promo
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no overlapping text, visual hierarchy holds on this device

## What's weak

- **No entry deadline anywhere in the email** — the single highest-converting lever in any sweepstakes is a close date; its complete absence makes "enter later" the path of least resistance
- **"Tons of gear" undersells a $2,000 prize** — the secondary prize has a real dollar value in the body; use it in the subject
- **CTA copy is object-less** — "ENTER NOW" appears twice and never says what you're entering; the button reads like boilerplate
- **Product grid is under-merchandised** — four products shown with no prices visible, no sizing callout, no "this is part of the gear prize" badge; it floats without purpose
- **No social proof** — sweepstakes convert better with a past trip photo or one community signal; there's nothing here that makes the $20k trip feel real

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an entry deadline to the hero, the body, and the subject** — "entries close July 4" transforms this from ambient awareness into a genuine action window; this is the single change that lifts the email most
- 2. **Kill "tons of gear" — use the dollar amount** — "$22k in prizes" or "$20k trip + $2,000 in gear" is specific where the subject is currently vague
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Enter to Win the $20k Trip" makes the object of the action explicit and sounds less like placeholder copy
- 4. **Badge at least one product as "part of the gear prize"** — blurring the line between sweepstakes prize and shop-now product is the entire point of that grid; right now it reads like a generic editorial module
- 5. **Add one past-winner or past-trip image** — even a single landscape shot from an EF Adventures trip with a caption like "Past winners explored Norway with EF" makes the prize feel attainable rather than aspirational
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win a $20k EF adventure trip — enter by July 4 ⛰️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$22k in prizes: enter the Backcountry x EF giveaway`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$2,000 in gear included. No purchase necessary. Entries close July 4 at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Small-group hiking and biking across Europe — plus the gear to get you there. One winner.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — CSS selector typo: `.md-fluid-row .col-9` missing, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` duplicated**

In the mobile `@media` block:
```css
.fluid-row .col-8,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }  /* BUG */
```
The second rule should target `.md-fluid-row .col-9` but instead targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` again. Net effect on mobile:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` resolves to **74.25%** (cascade override) instead of 66%
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives **no mobile width rule**, falls back to desktop width

Any fluid-row column grid using 9-column spans on mobile will be broken.

**Issue — External font stylesheet will be stripped by major clients**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (webmail and app), Outlook desktop, and Yahoo Mail strip `<link>` tags entirely. The Bluecore-hosted font will not load in these clients. Fallback font behavior depends on the inline `font-family` stack specified in the body — cannot confirm without full source.

**Issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
Using `none` instead of `100%` completely disables iOS automatic text size adjustment across all elements. `100%` is the correct value to prevent unwanted scaling while preserving legibility accessibility. `none` can cause very small text to remain unreadably small on older iOS devices.
