# Thousands of styles added 🎰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's Memorial Day sale expansion email — the subject line promises "Thousands of styles added" while the body delivers a densely-packed blast: 30% off the main sale, 70% off two bundles, and a persistent extra 20% clearance reminder. The hero is strong, the brand is trusted, and the Memorial Day timing is legitimate. But the slot-machine emoji subject line ("Thousands of styles added 🎰") is a notable misfire — it reads like a fast-fashion drop, not an outdoor gear retailer. For someone who got three Backcountry emails yesterday, the inbox fatigue is real.
- The email suffers from offer fragmentation. By the time you've scrolled past the hero sale, the "TOP PICKS FOR YOU" grid, the brand logo row, two bundle callouts, the "ENDS TOMORROW" urgency ticker, and a second clearance push, the message has diluted itself. Each individual offer is compelling; stacked together, they compete for attention rather than directing it.
- Net verdict: a solid execution of a familiar format, let down by a subject line that doesn't earn the click and a body that tries to do too much at once.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and immediate** — "Memorial Day Sale / UP TO 30% OFF" with the "SHOP SALE" CTA is visible without scrolling and communicates the value prop instantly.
- **"TOP PICKS FOR YOU" personalization** — surfacing individual product picks mid-email shows Backcountry is doing behavioral targeting, not just blasting a catalog.
- **Brand logo strip** (Patagonia, On, Osprey) adds credibility and signals premium merchandise range.
- **"ENDS TOMORROW" urgency ticker** is eye-catching and creates real time pressure without being dishonest.
- **70% off bundle callouts** are high-contrast and specific — "CAMP BUNDLE" and "SUN & SWIM BUNDLE" tell you exactly what's on offer.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is off-brand** — "Thousands of styles added 🎰" sounds like H&M or SHEIN, not a trusted outdoor gear retailer. The slot machine emoji undermines the brand entirely.
- **Four emails in two days is too many** — the 5/16 clearance, the 5/16 brand-sale, the 5/16 welcome, and now this. Inbox fatigue is a real risk here.
- **Too many competing offers** — 30% off sale, 70% camp bundle, 70% swim bundle, extra 20% clearance, plus brand-level discounts. No single hero offer wins.
- **The second text block ("Our Memorial Day Sale just got better…")** introduces a second narrative after the hero has already made its point — it breaks momentum.
- **No visible loyalty pricing or member-exclusive hook** despite Backcountry having a Gearheads/pro program.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line entirely** — the current subject loses the brand identity and the offer. Lead with the discount and the timing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day: 30% off just got bigger`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Thousands of styles, up to 30% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Patagonia, Osprey, On — plus your personal picks. Ends tomorrow.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your TOP PICKS just dropped in price. Plus 70% off camp + swim bundles.`
- 2. **Pick one hero offer and one secondary** — the 30% sale is the hero; pick either the bundle deals OR the clearance extension as the secondary, not both. Remove the duplicate urgency strips.
- 3. **Lean into the personalization** — the "TOP PICKS FOR YOU" section is the most differentiated part of the email. Move it above the brand logo strip and give the products more room to breathe (bigger thumbnails, visible pricing).
- 4. **Rest the clearance messaging** — the top banner AND the bottom module both push the extra 20% clearance that was already its own email yesterday (5/16). It's noise at this point.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo:**
```css
/* Line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
`.md-fluid-row .col-8` appears in two rules — once correctly at `66%` and again here at `74.25%`. The cascade gives `.md-fluid-row .col-8` a width of `74.25%` (later rule wins), while `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no mobile width rule at all. This will break any 9-column grid cell on mobile. The selector should read `.md-fluid-row .col-9`.

**External stylesheet — blocked by most email clients:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web and app), Outlook, and Samsung Mail strip `<link>` tags. The font load will silently fail and fall back to system fonts. If brand typography is load-bearing for layout (e.g., oversized headlines affecting line wrapping), this can cause reflow. Mitigation: inline `@font-face` declarations inside the existing `<style>` block.

**Aggressive text-size override:**
```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Applying `none` (rather than `100%`) to all text elements prevents users from scaling text, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Change to `100%` to allow scaling while still suppressing iOS auto-inflation.

**Missing `initial-scale` on viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Best practice is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"` to ensure consistent zoom behavior on first render in mobile webviews and Apple Mail.
