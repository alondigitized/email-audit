# Our bestselling sun hoodies! ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the editorial palate-cleanser Backcountry needed to send after a bruising two-week promo cycle — but the timing is awkward. Today is the same day as the "EXTRA 20% off FINAL HOURS" urgency close, meaning subscribers likely received two Backcountry emails in the same inbox session: one screaming about a deadline and one calmly showcasing full-price sun hoodies. That split creates friction — readers who just saw "FINAL HOURS" will reasonably wonder whether these sun hoodies are included in the deal or not. The email itself says nothing.
- Stripped of context, the send is well-executed. The hero image is clean outdoor lifestyle, the two featured products (Backcountry Tahoe Pro LT and Patagonia Capilene Cool) are specifically named with color swatches and gender splits, and the subject line — "Our bestselling sun hoodies! ☀️" — is a clear, quiet counterweight to the urgency hammering of the past 10 days. The brand grid at the bottom gives discovery depth without feeling like a dump.
- The core problem: this email asks for a full-price click from subscribers who've been trained by weeks of 20-40% off signals to wait for a deal. There's no offer, no price anchor, no urgency, no social proof beyond "bestselling" in the subject. It will earn opens from engaged subscribers and browser tabs from shopping-minded ones, but conversion will underperform what the clean creative deserves.

## What's working

- Hero copy ("Sun's Out Adventures On") nails July timing — sun hoodies in peak UV season is a credible editorial hook, not a forced promo.
- Both featured products are specifically named (Backcountry Tahoe Pro LT Sun Hoodie, Patagonia Capilene Cool Sun Hoodie) with color swatches — genuinely useful for a shopper comparing options.
- Gender-split CTAs ("SHOP WOMEN'S" / "SHOP MEN'S") reduce click friction more than a generic "SHOP NOW" would.
- Subject line is clean, short (~30 chars), spam-signal-free, and a welcome tonal shift after the "EXTRA 20% off FINAL HOURS" cadence.

## What's weak

- Zero offer: no % off, no price anchor, no free shipping, no member pricing — the email asks for a full-price click with nothing to sweeten it.
- Same-day collision with the urgency close creates subscriber confusion: are these sun hoodies included in the "EXTRA 20% off" deal or not? The email is silent.
- The hero CTA is "SHOP NOW" — generic, buried by the better "SHOP SUN HOODIES" button one section lower; these should be swapped.
- "Bestselling" is claimed in the subject but never substantiated in the body — no star ratings, no "Gearhead Pick" badge, no review count.
- The multi-brand grid at the bottom (Mountain Hardwear, QR Outdoor, Black Diamond, Hester) dilutes the focused sun hoodie narrative into a general shop browse.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the discount ambiguity first.** If these sun hoodies are included in the same-day "Extra 20% off" campaign, say "These bestsellers qualify — Extra 20% off ends today." If not, add any hook — member pricing, a "From $X" price anchor, or a Gearhead rating — to justify a full-price click.
- 2. **Promote the secondary CTA to primary hero.** Replace "SHOP NOW" in the hero with "Shop Sun Hoodies" — the email already writes this copy below; use it at the top.
- 3. **Substantiate "bestselling."** Add a star rating or "Top-rated by our Gearheads" badge on the Tahoe Pro LT and Capilene Cool cards. One number earns more trust than the adjective alone.
- 4. **Collapse or move the brand grid.** The bottom brand block undercuts the focused narrative. Move discovery to a nav-style footer row, not an image grid competing with the product showcase.
- 5. **Add a preheader.** The email's first readable text below the nav is likely leaking as preview text. A deliberate preheader copy line like "Lightweight, breathable, and built for your longest days out" would set expectations before the open.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **External stylesheet via `<link>` will not load in most email clients.** `<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">` is stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Custom fonts from this tag will silently fall back to system fonts — rendering depends entirely on the fallback stack defined elsewhere.

- **CSS copy-paste bug in responsive grid:**
  ```css
  .fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
  ```
  The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no width override on mobile, and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets the wrong width (74.25% instead of 66%). Any 9-column sub-layout inside an `md-fluid-row` will break on mobile.

- **Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag.** Screen readers and translation tools use this; its absence is a WCAG 3.1.1 failure.

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` applied globally** to `div, p, a, li, span, td`. This disables iOS's user-configured text size accessibility feature across the entire email body, not just controlled layout zones.

- **`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">` is missing `initial-scale=1`.** Incomplete viewport declaration; may cause inconsistent zoom behavior on some mobile clients.
