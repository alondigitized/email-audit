# Last chance! EXTRA 20% off clearance
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is at minimum the seventh activation of Backcountry's "Extra 20% off clearance" campaign — a deal that was declared finished on May 18, resurrected, re-declared "last chance" on May 22, and declared "closes TODAY" on May 23. Today's send has the exact same framing with a "FINAL HOURS" ticker banner at the top. That ticker is a lie the subscriber has seen before, and that's a trust problem no amount of clean creative fixes.
- The email packs four distinct promotional tracks into one send: the clearance hero, a product thumbnail carousel, a Memorial Day Sale expansion section (up to 30% off), and three category bundle rows at 20% off (Hike, Camp, Sun & Swim). The bundles are actually the freshest, most actionable content here — but they're buried below two competing sections that exhaust attention before the subscriber gets there.
- The execution is technically clean, but the strategy is fatigued. "Last chance" has now cried wolf for six days straight. Future urgency-driven sends from this brand will open less.

## What's working

- Hero renders immediately and legibly — "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE" headline and "SHOP NOW" button are unambiguous above the fold.
- "FINAL HOURS" ticker bar at the top is a credible visual urgency device — the problem is brand history, not the execution.
- The Hike / Camp / Sun & Swim bundles are a smart category-editorial angle that actually adds new value to what's been a monotone clearance drum.
- Memorial Day Sale secondary section correctly anchors to a real seasonal moment and adds a second pricing rationale.
- Product thumbnail grid is clean — readable product images, logical layout.

## What's weak

- False urgency is compounding: yesterday's email said "closes TODAY," this one says "FINAL HOURS." Subscribers remember.
- Four competing offer tracks (clearance + carousel + Memorial Day + bundles) fragment attention; none gets adequate real estate.
- Every CTA across every section reads "SHOP NOW" — zero differentiation across clearance, Memorial Day, and three distinct bundles.
- The bundles are the freshest content but live below the fold, behind two sections the subscriber has already seen versions of this week.
- Preview text mirrors "last chance" from the subject instead of adding an incremental hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop the false urgency or actually enforce it.** If clearance ends tonight, end it. If it's extending, say "Extended through Monday" honestly. Subscribers notice repeat "last chance" signals and stop clicking.
- 2. **Give the bundles the hero slot.** Hike / Camp / Sun & Swim bundles are new, seasonally sharp, and specific. They deserve a send — or at minimum, the lead position in this one.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** "Shop Hike Bundle," "Shop Camp Bundle," "Shop Sun & Swim Bundle" will outperform three "SHOP NOW" buttons — especially for click attribution and testing.
- 4. **Pick one discount story per send.** Clearance AND Memorial Day Sale are fighting for the same inbox moment. Alternating them across sends preserves freshness; stacking them in one email dilutes both.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hike, Camp, and Sun & Swim sets — grab the one that fits your summer`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector bug (confirmed)**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row` half of this rule targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. On medium breakpoints, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` elements will fall through to an incorrect width rule, collapsing layout for any 9-column grid row on that class.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` (problematic)**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Using `none` instead of `100%` prevents iOS from scaling text even when the user explicitly zooms. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and can cause readability issues on small screens. The correct value is `100%`.

**`alt` attributes** — Cannot audit from truncated source; no `<img>` tags visible.
