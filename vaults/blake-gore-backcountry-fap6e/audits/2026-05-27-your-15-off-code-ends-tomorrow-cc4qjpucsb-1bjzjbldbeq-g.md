# Your 15% off code ends tomorrow ⏳
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean pivot from the Memorial Day blast template. Where every previous send this week screamed SALE in orange blocks and countdown timers, this one lands quietly with a personal promo code, a single hero, and a "Top Picks For You" shelf. It's a different lever — cart-engagement or loyalty drip — and the tonal shift is the right call after seven consecutive sale emails. The code is front and center, the deadline is legible, and the render is mostly clean.
- The one visible crack is a recommendation engine failure: Klymit Pillow X Camp Pillow appears twice in the four-slot "Top Picks" grid. On a send that's supposed to feel personal and curated, a duplicate product undercuts the trust signal immediately. It looks like the system ran out of items and backfilled with the same one. That's an operational miss that a subscriber will clock.
- Cadence is the bigger structural problem. This is Backcountry's eighth email in roughly four days. Even a well-executed drip loses credibility when subscribers are swimming in your brand.

## What's working

- **Code is impossible to miss.** `BCNTF20Q1G5OYDJB` is in large, centered type directly below the hero — no hunting required.
- **"Extra 15% Off" + "Expires Tomorrow" creates a tight, honest urgency loop.** The clock emoji in the subject reinforces it without shouting.
- **Tonal contrast lands.** After days of orange countdown blocks and "SALE SALE SALE" headers, the understated hero image (mountain biker, muted palette) reads as a personal note, not a blast.
- **Hero and code are fully above the fold** on mobile — the subscriber gets the entire offer before scrolling.

## What's weak

- **Duplicate product in "Top Picks."** Klymit Pillow X Camp Pillow fills both the top-left and bottom-left slots. The grid looks like a recommendation engine error and kills the "curated for you" premise.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the only CTA, and it's generic.** After surfacing four specific products, landing on a single undifferentiated button wastes the personalization.
- **No cart context.** The copy says "all eligible full-price items in your cart" but nothing in the visual anchors this to the subscriber's actual cart or a specific item they browsed. It reads like a generic code blast.
- **Eighth email in four days.** The cadence fatigue erodes whatever goodwill the tonal pivot earns.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the duplicate product immediately.** This is an operational error — ship a 5-product row or pull a different item. Leaving two identical Klymit pillows in a "personalized" grid undermines every trust signal in the email.
- 2. **Swap the single "SHOP NOW" for per-product CTAs.** Each product tile should have its own "Shop" or "Add to Cart" button. That's the natural action off a curated grid, and it prevents the scroll back to find one button.
- 3. **Anchor the copy to a specific cart item if data allows.** "You left the Osprey Ace 50L in your cart — use this code" converts 2–3× better than a generic "items in your cart" line.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency.** The next Backcountry email shouldn't go out for 3–5 days minimum. Prior sends: the "ends in a couple hours" email on May 27 (this morning) already told the subscriber the Memorial Day sale was closing — a new code the same day looks like an admission the sale didn't close.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in mobile breakpoint:**

```css
/* Line in <style media="only screen and (max-width: 600px)"> */
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The selector `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is wrong here — it should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Consequence: on mobile, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` never receives `74.25%` width (it falls through to the earlier 66% rule), and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` has conflicting specificity from two rules — the correct 66% rule and this erroneous 74.25% rule, with the latter winning due to source order. Any 9-column `md-fluid-row` section will render narrower than intended on mobile.
