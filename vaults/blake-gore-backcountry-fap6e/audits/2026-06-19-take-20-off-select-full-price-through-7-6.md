# Take 20% off select full-price through 7/6 👀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent-looking email with a fundamental strategic self-sabotage problem: Backcountry's hero offer is 20% off select full-price items, while the same email contains a "4th of July Sale — UP TO 40% OFF" banner at the bottom. A subscriber who opened yesterday's July 4th campaign launch would immediately notice the downgrade — why buy full-price stock at 20% off when sale stock is 40% off in the same scroll? The email undermines its own hero CTA before the reader even hits the first "SHOP NOW."
- The personalization section ("PICKED FOR YOU") is the standout element. Four specific outdoor products — sleeping pads from Exped, NEMO, and Klymit plus a Katadyn water filter — signal real behavioral targeting, not just category-level guessing. The Summit Club loyalty header is a solid persistent signal that keeps the relationship feel alive.
- That said, after the high-intensity Flash Sale arc (June 13–15 at up to 70% off) and the July 4th launch on June 19, landing a 20%-off-full-price email in the same day as the sale announcement reads as either a segmentation error or a confusing multi-offer strategy. The cadence is fatiguing and this send is the weakest offer in the arc.

## What's working

- **PICKED FOR YOU personalization is specific and on-brand.** Named products (Exped MegaMat Duo, Katadyn BeFree microfilter) tell the reader the algorithm knows what they look at — a real differentiator from generic "here are deals" emails.
- **Summit Club header creates a persistent loyalty framing.** The 10% instant cashback mention at the very top is correctly placed and compounds the offer without cluttering the hero.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** "20% OFF SELECT FULL-PRICE ITEMS" reads instantly; no competing above-the-fold noise.
- **The TOP BRANDS tile grid is well-executed.** North Face, La Sportiva, Marmot, Black Diamond, NEMO — credibility through association, no explanation needed.
- **Email renders cleanly.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- **The 4th of July Sale banner in the footer actively cannibalizes the hero.** A reader will prefer the 40% off sale items over 20% off full-price; the email argues against itself.
- **"Select" is doing a lot of heavy lifting with no clarification.** Which categories qualify? The hero gives no signal — a click is required just to learn the offer scope.
- **Three consecutive "SHOP NOW" buttons with zero variation in copy.** One each on the hero, product section, brand section — all identical and generic. Zero pull toward a specific category or product.
- **No deadline reinforcement inside the body.** "Through 7/6" is in the subject but never restated in the email itself; a subscriber who opened without reading the subject wouldn't know this is time-bound.
- **After a 70% Flash Sale, 20% off full-price feels like a step backward.** The June 13–15 arc set an aggressive value ceiling. This send doesn't reset perception — it deflates it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove or reframe the July 4th Sale banner** — it competes directly with the hero. Either make this a dedicated full-price-items email with no sale crossover, or restructure to put the two offers side-by-side with a clear "OR shop the 4th of July Sale" secondary path. Don't let them cannibalize each other.
- 2. **Name the categories that qualify for 20% off.** "Select full-price items" is the vaguest possible qualifier. One line like "20% off new arrivals in hiking, climbing + trail running" would pull a specific buyer segment instead of hedging for everyone.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with verb+noun CTAs.** "Shop Sleeping Pads," "Shop Trail Running," "Browse Full-Price Hiking Gear" — each CTA earns its click instead of relying on volume.
- 4. **Restate the deadline in-body.** "Offer ends July 6" in the footer or near the hero would reinforce urgency for readers who skimmed past the subject.
- 5. **Write a real preheader.** The Summit Club cashback banner is leaking as preview text. A designed preheader like "New arrivals, no promo exclusions — 20% through 7/6" would pair cleanly with the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals at 20% off — hiking, trail & climbing gear through 7/6`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Full-price = fair game. 20% off through July 6 👀`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Gear that just landed — no exclusions, no hoops, ends July 6`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New drops + 20% off: North Face, Marmot, Black Diamond and more`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo (mobile stylesheet, line ~34):**

```css
/* BUG: second selector says .col-8 instead of .col-9 */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

Effect: `.md-fluid-row .col-9` columns receive no mobile width override; `.md-fluid-row .col-8` columns match two rules (66% from its own rule AND 74.25% from this misnamed rule) — the 74.25% wins by source order, so 8-column cells render ~8.25% too wide on mobile.

**External font `<link>` tag:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```

Gmail and Outlook strip external `<link>` stylesheets at render time. Custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. This is expected behavior in email, but the design should be verified with fallback fonts active.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly:**

```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

The value `none` (vs. `100%`) blocks user-initiated text zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 requires text to be resizable up to 200% without assistive technology. Prefer `100%`.
