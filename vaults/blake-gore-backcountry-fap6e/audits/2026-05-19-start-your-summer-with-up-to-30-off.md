# Start your summer with up to 30% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent but unremarkable Memorial Day sale blast — the fifth or sixth Backcountry send in three days. The hero is loud and on-message ("SALE SALE SALE" stacked three times over an outdoor scene, 30% off visible immediately), and the category/brand grid below gives the subscriber a clear navigation path. It does its job.
- Where it falls short is differentiation. The category tiles and brand logo rows are nearly identical to the May 19 brand-showcase send ("Brands our Expert Gearheads can't go without") and the May 17 "Thousands of styles added" blast. If you opened either of those, you've essentially already seen this email. The subject line — "Start your summer with up to 30% off" — is functional but forgettable; no urgency, no Memorial Day hook, no single compelling product to anchor the scan.
- The Summit Club exclusive (a free cashmere blanket) is a smart loyalty layer but oddly placed in a summer outdoor-gear sale. It feels appended rather than integrated. Net result: a solid 6 for a subscriber who's been following the sale sequence, but nothing that breaks through cadence fatigue.

## What's working

- Hero offer is unmissable — "UP TO 30% OFF" in the banner plus the triple "SALE" stack is bold and instantly legible.
- Orange "START SAVING NOW" CTA is high-contrast and clearly the primary action.
- Category tiles (Men's/Women's Apparel, Men's/Women's Footwear) make it easy to self-select and click into the right section.
- Brand logo row (Patagonia, TNF, Black Diamond, Salomon, Outdoor Research, Osprey) is a strong trust signal for the outdoor persona.
- Summit Club exclusive deal adds a loyalty incentive layer that generic blast emails skip.

## What's weak

- Subject line skips the Memorial Day deadline entirely — this is a three-day sale and the subject reads like evergreen seasonal copy.
- No single hero product with a price or specific callout — every CTA points to a category page, not a product.
- Cadence is genuinely fatiguing: this is the 5th+ Backcountry send since May 17 with near-identical creative, which erodes trust in each incremental open.
- The "Free Cashmere Blanket" Summit Club perk feels off-genre — a camp blanket or sleeping bag liner would have landed better thematically.
- Too many competing CTAs (SHOP NOW hero → START SAVING NOW → SHOP MEMORIAL DAY SALE → 4× SHOP NOW in categories → SHOP ALL → JOIN NOW) dilutes the hierarchy below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the subject.** "Memorial Day" in the sender name doesn't count — the subject itself needs it. "Ends Monday" or "Last chance" turns a browseable subject into an action item.
- 2. **Lead with one hero product, not a category grid.** Pick the single best-performing item from the sale (e.g., a Patagonia shell at a specific price) and anchor the hero to it. Category tiles can follow.
- 3. **Integrate the Summit Club CTA into the hero** rather than burying it at the bottom — "Summit Club members: free blanket with any order" is a genuine differentiator that deserves above-the-fold real estate.
- 4. **Collapse the CTA count.** One primary CTA ("SHOP MEMORIAL DAY SALE"), four category tiles with links, one Summit Club CTA. Cut the redundant "START SAVING NOW" and "SHOP ALL" to reduce noise.
- 5. **If you must send daily**, rotate the frame: Gearhead Alex personal voice (like the May 18 send) works far better on day 3–4 of a sale sequence than a repeat blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends Monday: up to 30% off Memorial Day Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last weekend for 30% off — don't wait`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top picks from Patagonia, The North Face, Salomon — all on sale through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summit Club members: free blanket with your order this weekend.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo:**

```css
/* line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This is a copy-paste error. On mobile, a 9-column element inside `.md-fluid-row` will not get the correct 74.25% width — it falls through to an unconstrained width and will likely collapse or overflow.

**External font stylesheet — Gmail will strip it:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```

Gmail (web and Android) strips `<link>` tags from the `<head>`. If fallback `font-family` stacks are not declared on inline `style` attributes, custom fonts silently fail and render as the client default. Verify all text elements have inline `font-family` fallbacks.

**`viewport` meta — missing `initial-scale`:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```

Best practice: `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Without `initial-scale=1`, some iOS clients may render at a scaled-down zoom level on first load.

**`format-detection` is incomplete:**

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

Only suppresses phone auto-linking. Add `address=no, email=no` to prevent iOS Mail from auto-linking addresses and email strings inside body copy, which can disrupt layout.

**Alt text** — truncated source prevents a full audit. Confirm all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, especially hero and product images.
