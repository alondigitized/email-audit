# EXTRA 20% off FINAL HOURS
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's third urgency close on the same "Extra 20% off clearance" campaign — the July 12 send literally said "ends TMRW," making today's "FINAL HOURS" subject line the third deadline in four days. That false-urgency pattern is now fully exposed, but there's a worse problem hiding in the body: the subject screams "FINAL HOURS" while the body copy says "This sale on sale ends **tomorrow**." Same email, directly contradictory deadlines. A subscriber who notices that contrast won't just distrust this send — they'll discount every future urgency claim from this sender.
- The structural execution is solid: the hero is clean, the 20% off message lands immediately, and the category tiles add useful depth. But Backcountry is stacking a second campaign (Summer Sale, up to 40% off) under the clearance close, muddying the offer and diluting the urgency of the primary CTA. An email can't be a "FINAL HOURS" send and a "Summer Sale launch" send at the same time.
- The Summer Club cash-back banner at the very top is the most interesting element Backcountry has sent in this stretch — 25% instant cash back is a real loyalty hook — but it's rendered so small it's functionally invisible on mobile. A genuine member benefit buried in a 12px top strip is a missed conversion driver.

## What's working

- Hero 20% off clearance message is immediate — offer is legible within one second of opening
- "Sale on sale" framing is genuinely compelling if the subscriber understands the mechanism (clearance prices minus another 20%)
- Category tiles (Men's/Women's Footwear) provide targeted navigation depth without cluttering the hero
- Summer Club cash-back banner, however small, is the first loyalty signal in this entire campaign run

## What's weak

- Subject says "FINAL HOURS" but body copy says "ends tomorrow" — directly contradictory deadlines in the same email
- This is the third urgency close on the same campaign; the "ends tomorrow" promise from July 12 clearly didn't hold, and credibility is now fully spent
- Two separate sale campaigns (Extra 20% off clearance + Summer Sale up to 40% off) compete for attention in a single send — neither gets the focus it deserves
- "SHOP NOW" CTA copy is identical across every send in this series — no specificity, no category pull
- Summer Club 25% cash-back benefit is confined to a tiny top banner; a strong loyalty offer deserves a full content block

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/body deadline contradiction before anything else.** "FINAL HOURS" and "ends tomorrow" cannot coexist. Pick one, commit, and mean it. If the sale truly ends tonight, remove "tomorrow" from the body. If it ends tomorrow, don't say "FINAL HOURS."
- 2. **Stop extending this deadline.** One more extension and Backcountry's urgency language is permanently devalued for this list segment. If the sale needs to run longer, reframe it — don't just keep saying "ends tonight" on different nights.
- 3. **Split the two campaigns into separate sends.** The clearance finale and Summer Sale are different products for different intents. Stacking them kills both.
- 4. **Make the CTA copy earn its spot.** "Shop Clearance — Prices Rise Tonight" does real work. "SHOP NOW" does nothing.
- 5. **Give Summer Club a mid-body feature block.** 25% instant cash back is the best member offer in this campaign run — it deserves a full-width moment with a real CTA, not a 12px top strip.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance closes tonight — prices reset at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: extra 20% off clearance ends today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Once this sale closes, these prices don't come back. Shop now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer Club members earn 25% cash back on every clearance pick.`

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback — 0 characters**
The MIME envelope contains no plain-text part. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a text part, but many spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) penalize HTML-only messages, and RFC 2822 best practice requires a `text/plain` alternative. This is the only hard fail in the QA run.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to render the native "Unsubscribe" button above the message. If the header exists in the original SMTP envelope but is stripped by the AgentMail relay, the QA warning is a relay artifact — confirm by checking raw headers at the MTA level. If it is genuinely absent, this risks suppressed Gmail placement for bulk mail.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header missing (RFC 8058)**
Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (as required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policies for >5k/day senders) is not supported. Gmail will downgrade to a redirect-based unsubscribe flow. Bluecore should be adding this automatically — its absence suggests a configuration gap on the ESP side.

SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from available headers. The sending domain `b.backcountry.com` should have SPF and DKIM records aligned with the Bluecore envelope. Verify at the MTA level or via a tool like MXToolbox.
