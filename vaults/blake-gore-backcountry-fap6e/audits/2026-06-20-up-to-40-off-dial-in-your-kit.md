# Up to 40% off | Dial in your kit 🚨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 2 of Backcountry's 4th of July Sale — following the June 19 campaign opener ("To 250 years of adventure") — and the execution is competent. The hero is clean, the category grid gives subscribers six immediate entry points, and the trending-gear section adds product-level specificity that the opener lacked. The visual hierarchy works: 40% off lands first, categories funnel interest, trending products invite the click.
- The strategic wound from June 19's "Take 20% off select full-price" email is still bleeding here. The same 20% off full-price banner reappears at the bottom of this email — right underneath a sale that goes up to 40% off. For a subscriber who already saw that conflicted June 19 email just yesterday, this is the second consecutive send that hedges its own headline. The mixed-message problem isn't fixed; it's compounded by repetition.
- As a standalone email it scores well. As the second touch in a sale campaign that keeps undermining itself with a competing offer, it's a missed opportunity to build urgency cleanly.

## What's working

- **4th of July Sale hero is immediate** — the rafting imagery + "UP TO 40% OFF" read in one glance; no ambiguity about what the email is selling.
- **Category grid is functional** — Hike & Camp, Men's Footwear, Women's Footwear, Bike Apparel, Climb, Travel with individual SHOP NOW buttons covers the subscriber's likely entry point without requiring scrolling to find it.
- **Trending Gear on Sale section** — actual product tiles (The North Face, Columbia) with struck-through prices and red sale prices provide specific proof of the discount; a good upgrade from the opener's category-only approach.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken UI visible in the render.
- **"Dial in your kit"** — the secondary headline is brand-voice appropriate; speaks to a gear-focused subscriber without being generic.

## What's weak

- **20% off full-price banner returns** — same conflicting offer called out in the June 19 audit; placing it directly below a "SHOP THE SALE" CTA asks subscribers to reconcile two different discounts in the same scroll, and the inferior offer (20%) follows the superior one (40%).
- **No explicit deadline in the email body** — the subject uses 🚨 for urgency but the hero and category grid carry no "ends July 4th" countdown or date anchor; the sale could feel perpetual.
- **Six categories = no single hero product** — the grid distributes attention evenly across every category rather than spotlighting one timely item that creates a must-have pull.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing all the CTA work** — every button says the same thing; "Shop Hiking Gear" / "Shop Men's Footwear" would segment clicks and reduce choice paralysis.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or relocate the 20% full-price banner.** This is the single highest-impact change. It actively dilutes the 40% off sale message two sends in a row. Move it to a dedicated email or a footer footnote — don't let it compete with the hero.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** Even a small "Sale ends July 4th" line under "UP TO 40% OFF" converts the urgency emoji in the subject into a concrete reason to act today versus tomorrow.
- 3. **Feature one hero product with a price.** Pick the top seller from trending gear (the North Face piece already in the email) and give it a real billboard moment — product name, was/now price, and a direct link — before the category grid.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTA copy per category.** Replace generic "SHOP NOW" with category-specific verbs on at least the top two tiles to improve click attribution and reduce cognitive load.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4th of July Sale ends soon — up to 40% off 🔥`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 40% off | Sale ends July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Trending gear from The North Face, Columbia, and more — all on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hike, climb, bike: your 4th of July kit is on sale`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `lang` attribute (confirmed)**
```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```
Screen readers cannot infer language for pronunciation/hyphenation. WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 failure.

**Issue 2 — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` applied globally (confirmed)**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
`none` (not `100%`) prevents users from overriding text size on iOS. WCAG 2.1 §1.4.4 violation. Should be `100%`.

**Issue 3 — CSS selector defect in fluid-row col-9 (confirmed)**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important;}
```
The `.md-fluid-row .col-9` selector is missing — it was written as `.col-8` instead. Any `md-fluid-row` nine-column element will not receive its intended 74.25% width on mobile, collapsing to 100% via the catch-all instead.
