# Deals ending tomorrow! Two you can’t miss 🫣
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry running its now-familiar two-offer combo — 20% off full-price plus an extra 20% off clearance — the same structural play that scored 9.00 on both August 1 sends. The execution is clean: a legible "TWO WAYS TO SAVE" hero, a red urgency ticker, and two well-organized product grids with named items and visible discount percentages. No render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- Where this send loses ground versus its recent siblings is clutter. Instead of one clean offer, this email stacks three: 20% off full-price, an extra 20% off clearance, AND a bottom-of-email "up to 50% off Summer Semi-Annual Sale" banner that reads like a completely different promotion bolted onto the same send. A subscriber scrolling to the bottom is left wondering which discount actually applies to what — that's the kind of internal inconsistency that erodes trust over a fast cadence of near-identical sale emails.
- Also worth flagging: per brand history, this is at minimum the sixth sale-cycle email in about two weeks (7/25, 7/30, 7/31 ×2, 8/1 ×2, now 8/2). The creative execution earns real credit, but the cadence is starting to feel like fatigue rather than a fresh urgency beat.

## What's working

- The hero "TWO WAYS TO SAVE" + big 20%/20% numerals is immediately legible and states the offer without ambiguity.
- Brand-name product callouts (The North Face, Osprey, Mountain Hardwear, La Sportiva) under the hero add instant credibility and category signal.
- Product grids show specific items with names, discount percentages in red, and individual "Shop Now" links rather than one generic CTA.
- The red "ENDS TOMORROW!" ticker bar reinforces urgency visually right where the eye lands after the hero.
- Loyalty benefit ("10% instant cash back with Summit Club+") is visible at the very top for members.

## What's weak

- A third, larger offer ("up to 50% off Summer Semi-Annual Sale") appears at the bottom, undercutting the 20%/20% headline and creating confusion about which discount is real.
- CTA copy is the generic "Shop Now" everywhere — no differentiation by product or category, a missed specificity opportunity given how specific the product grid already is.
- No pricing, sizing, or rating/review information on any product tile — just name and discount percentage.
- This is at least the sixth sale-themed email in two weeks per brand history; the format is starting to repeat rather than escalate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the bottom "up to 50% off" banner or move it to its own send — running 20%+20% and "up to 50%" in the same email confuses which number a shopper should trust, and it's the single biggest drag on this send's clarity.
- 2. Swap generic "Shop Now" CTAs for product-specific verbs ("Shop Bibs," "Shop Wetsuits") to lift click intent from the grid.
- 3. Add a star rating or "X reviews" line under at least the hero products — Backcountry has this data and it's a proven click lever.
- 4. Vary the creative pattern from the last two 8/1 sends — same red/gold urgency banner + lifestyle hero + product grid structure risks blending into the prior two "9.00" emails and losing distinctiveness.
