# Here's what we'd grab that’s up to 40% off 👀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's mid-campaign editorial pivot in their 4th of July Sale run — following the category grid send on June 20 ("Up to 40% off | Dial in your kit 🚨") and the campaign opener on June 19. The creative refresh is legitimate: instead of another "here's the sale," this send leads with "here's what our Goathead Experts would actually grab." That framing gives fatigued subscribers a real reason to open when they've already seen the discount ceiling twice.
- The execution mostly holds up. The hero kayaking image is seasonally sharp, the "PICKED FOR YOU" product grid with four specific SKUs at visible sale prices is the closest thing Backcountry has sent to genuine editorial curation, and the visual flow is clean. If you're going to send a third email in a 4-day campaign window, this is the right creative direction.
- The persistent drag: the 20% off full-price items banner reappears at the bottom — the same internal contradiction flagged in the June 19 audit. If the 40% off sale is the reason to act, the 20% full-price offer sitting below it dilutes urgency and raises a comparison question the email doesn't answer.

## What's working

- **Expert curation framing** is a genuine differentiator from prior sends — "these are the picks our Goathead Experts would actually grab" is more compelling than "sale is live."
- **Four specific products with visible sale prices** in the "PICKED FOR YOU" grid ($17.47, $40.46, $44.80, $30.86) — specific SKUs convert better than category banners.
- **Kayaking hero image** is on-brand and seasonally accurate for a July 4th outdoor send.
- **Destination + Tahoe Buy 2+, Save 20% modules** add layering — subscribers who've already decided to buy get an incremental incentive.

## What's weak

- **Dual-offer confusion returns**: the 20% off select full-price items footer banner sits below a 40% off sale. The message conflict was flagged on June 19; it's still unresolved.
- **No sale deadline visible** in the email body — "4th of July Sale" implies July 4, but the date isn't stated, which softens urgency.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout** are generic; the product-level clicks would lift if the buttons said something specific like "Shop Columbia" or "See All Sale Gear."
- **Women's board shorts in a mixed expert picks grid** — if this is personalized, the gender mix isn't signaled; if it's not, the "picked for you" copy overclaims.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or re-sequence the 20% full-price banner.** It's a different audience segment. Either send it separately or gate it behind the sale CTA — don't put a worse offer below your best one.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline date.** Change the hero sub-copy to include "Through July 4" — one line that converts "sale" to urgency.
- 3. **Specificity in CTAs.** Replace generic "SHOP NOW" on product tiles with brand/category names: "Shop Flylow →" or "Get This Shirt →."
- 4. **Signal the segmentation.** If this is a curated picks list, a brief "based on your past purchases" or "for hikers and paddlers" line under the headline would validate the "PICKED FOR YOU" claim.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Experts picked these 4 — all up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `What our Goathead team is buying in the 4th of July Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends July 4 — these are the actual buys worth grabbing`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Columbia, Flylow, ALPS and more — here's what's worth it at 40% off`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector typo — col-9 / col-8 collision (confirmed bug)**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The intended rule is `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` inherits 74.25% (should be 66%)
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has no mobile width override at all

Any layout section using `md-fluid-row` with 8- or 9-column cells will render broken on mobile.

**Missing `initial-scale` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Standard practice is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Without `initial-scale=1`, some Android WebViews may default to zoomed-out rendering.

**Overly broad `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
Applying `none` (rather than `100%`) globally disables the user's ability to zoom text in accessibility settings on iOS. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern and affects all clients that honor the rule.

**`<title>` matches subject line**
This is acceptable for preheader but provides no diagnostic value if the email is opened as a file — minor, not a blocker.
