# 20% off full-price items ends TOMORROW
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's "ends TOMORROW" send for the same 20% off full-price offer that has been running in some form since May 29 — and that already declared itself over on June 2 ("ends tonight"). The creative itself is tightly executed: a prominent hero, a repeating urgency ticker, four real product picks, brand logos, and a clean SHOP NOW CTA. But the brand has a credibility problem baked in at the inbox level, because anyone paying attention knows this offer doesn't actually end.
- The more damaging issue is internal: buried beneath the urgency push is a competing Summer Sale block promising "UP TO 40% OFF." If 40% off exists, why would I act on 20% off? The two offers cannibalizing each other undercuts both. The email looks professional but has been undermined by its own campaign architecture.
- The cadence on this arc is now exhausting. Seventh-ish email, same offer, same partner logos (Osprey, Black Diamond, Marmot, Columbia), same hero copy. Even the repeating marquee ticker — "ENDS TOMORROW! ENDS TOMORROW! ENDS TOMO…" — reads as the brand nervously shouting into the void.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible**: "20% OFF FULL-PRICE ITEMS" is the first thing the eye lands on, above the fold, no ambiguity.
- **Repeating urgency ticker** is an attention-grabbing technique; the animation effect works visually even if the deadline's credibility is gone.
- **Real products, real categories**: Bear Vault, Exped MegaMat, Kelty tent, women's fleece — this is a coherent outdoor assortment, not random stock photos.
- **Top Brands grid** (Osprey, Black Diamond, Outdoor Research, Marmot, Pearl Izumi, Columbia) adds purchase-decision credibility without needing to name a single SKU.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Competing offer kills urgency**: The Summer Sale "UP TO 40% OFF" block at the bottom makes the 20% off push incoherent — why act on the smaller offer?
- **Deadline is not credible**: "Ends tonight" was the subject on June 2. Subscribers remember this. "ENDS TOMORROW" in an all-caps ticker lands as a broken promise, not urgency.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the laziest possible CTA**: Three separate "SHOP NOW" buttons and zero product-specific copy ("Shop Tents," "Shop Sleeping Pads") means clicks scatter to the homepage.
- **No social proof**: No reviews, no star ratings, no "bestseller" tags on any of the four featured products.
- **Subject line carries the full weight with nothing underneath it**: No preheader strategy visible; the email relies entirely on the subject to earn the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the competing offer, or invert the hierarchy**: Either promote the Summer Sale (40% off) as the hero and reference the 20% off as a separate angle, or drop the Summer Sale block. Presenting both in one send makes neither persuasive.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with category-specific CTAs**: "Shop Tents," "Shop Sleep Systems," "Shop Women's Layers" — one click, one destination, lower bounce.
- 3. **Add social proof to the product grid**: Even a star count ("★★★★☆ 312 reviews") next to the Kelty tent makes it a real recommendation instead of a catalog listing.
- 4. **Retire the offer and relaunch clean**: The 20%-off arc is credibility-depleted. A hard stop followed by a fresh subject line (even a 1-day reset) would outperform another "ends tomorrow."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bear Vault, Kelty tents, Exped pads — 20% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: 20% off The North Face, Osprey, Marmot`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six brands, one discount. Shop before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tents, sleep systems, and layers at 20% off — shop the full list.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in mobile media query:**

```css
/* Line as written: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }

/* Should be: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-9 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The `.md-fluid-row .col-8` selector is a copy-paste error. Effect: any `.md-fluid-row .col-9` element gets no mobile width rule (inherits 100%), and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets 74.25% instead of the correct 66% (defined one rule earlier). This misaligns any two-column or multi-column product grid in `md-fluid-row` containers on screens ≤600 px.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:** `<html>` has no `lang="en"` attribute. Screen readers cannot infer language; WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 violation.

**`format-detection` meta present:** `telephone=no` is set correctly — suppresses unwanted phone-number auto-linking in iOS Mail.

**Viewport meta present:** `width=device-width` — correct.
