# Don’t miss up to 70% off | Last chance 🚨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's second Gearhead Alex personal-note email on May 25 — the first ("You're not going to want to miss this one") scored a 4.0 earlier today. The format executes cleanly: short body copy, Gearhead Alex photo sign-off, one oversized CTA. But the "genuine friend flagging something for you" brand voice is being destroyed by volume. Three Backcountry emails in a single calendar day, all pointing at the same sale, make the earnest Gearhead persona feel like a persona rather than a person.
- There's not a single product image in this email. The entire click argument is "up to 70% off" — an offer that's been in subject lines since May 24 — and a SHOP NOW button that goes somewhere. No proof, no pull, no differentiation from the send that arrived an hour earlier.
- The email renders cleanly and the CTA is physically unavoidable. But the cadence damage is the real story. By this point a real subscriber has been told "ends tomorrow" on May 24, "closes today" twice on May 25, and now "final hours" again. The send shouldn't have happened.

## What's working

- FINAL HOURS marquee banner creates instant temporal urgency before the first line of body copy
- Body copy is exactly two sentences — no padding, no detour, hits the offer immediately
- SHOP NOW button is oversized, high-contrast black, impossible to miss
- Gearhead Alex photo + "Catch you on the mountain" sign-off is a consistent and recognizable brand beat
- Fine print with specific sale dates (VALID 5/24–5/25) is visible and grounds the "final hours" claim

## What's weak

- Second Gearhead Alex personal-note email today — the earnest, one-friend-to-another voice stops working when it fires twice in eight hours
- 🚨 in the subject directly contradicts the plain-text "here's a note from me" tone the body sells
- Zero product proof — no tiles, no slashed prices, nothing showing what 70% off actually looks like
- SHOP NOW is the least specific CTA possible: no category, no destination, no reason to believe this click leads somewhere different from yesterday's SHOP NOW
- Footer copyright reads ©2024 — two years stale, a small but visible credibility nick

## Recommendations

- 1. **Skip this send or consolidate.** The second Alex note on the same day is net-negative for the brand relationship. If you have two sends planned, pick one and cut the other.
- 2. **Show 2–3 product tiles with crossed-out prices** above the CTA — prove the 70% claim visually instead of just asserting it again.
- 3. **Drop the 🚨 emoji entirely.** The body copy is doing the urgency work. The emoji signals "promotional blast" and blows the personal-note premise.
- 4. **Write a specific CTA.** "Shop Summer Apparel" or "See All Clearance Deals" gives the click a destination and a reason.
- 5. **Update the copyright year** in the footer template — ©2024 is two years stale.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The gear you'll actually reach for this summer — still up to 70% off until tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `I'd hate for you to miss this. A few pieces I'd grab before midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector collision in mobile stylesheet:**

```css
/* Line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important;}
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` appears **twice** in the responsive rules — once correctly at `66%`, then again here at `74.25%`. The intended selector `.md-fluid-row .col-9` is **missing entirely**. On mobile, any `.md-fluid-row .col-9` cell will receive no mobile override (defaults to 100% via the `.row` catch-all instead of the intended 74.25%), and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` cells will have conflicting cascade rules.

**Correct form should be:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-9 { width: 74.25% !important;}
```

**External font dependency:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web), Apple Mail with remote content blocked, and several corporate clients strip `<link>` tags. Bluecore likely accounts for this with system-font fallbacks, but verify the fallback stack renders acceptably in Gmail web.
