# 4 days only: extra 20% off clearance
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is the same clearance-extra-20% frame Backcountry has run repeatedly this cycle, and it lands right after the July 30 teaser / July 31 "Up to 50% off SALE" delivery pair — meaning this send is either a third beat in the same 48 hours or a slight rewind to the clearance angle instead of the semi-annual sale. The hero is clean and on-brand: gorgeous climbing photography, bold "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE" type, four recognizable outdoor brand logos (The North Face, Marmot, Mountain Hardwear, OR), and a real "4 DAYS ONLY!" urgency cue.
- The execution is solid — no render bugs, clear CTA stack, logical category grid — but the strategy is where this falls apart. The email buries its own bigger offer: after "SHOP NOW" and a full six-tile category grid, it drops a second full-width banner for "Up to 50% off Summer Semi-Annual Sale," which is a materially better deal than the 20% off clearance headlining the email. A subscriber skimming the top gets the weaker offer; only someone who scrolls past two CTAs and a category grid finds the stronger one.
- Given the brand history — this is at least the fourth or fifth sale-structured send in about two weeks — cadence fatigue is a real risk, and burying the 50% off banner under the weaker 20% clearance pitch is a self-inflicted miss.

## What's working

- Hero art is high-quality and on-brand — real climbing photography, not stock-looking filler.
- "4 DAYS ONLY!" handwritten-style callout gives credible, visible urgency right in the hero.
- Four recognizable brand logos (The North Face, Marmot, Mountain Hardwear, OR) build trust and specificity in the offer.
- Category grid (Men's, Women's, Camp & Hike, Bike, Climb, Travel) gives multiple clear entry points beyond the single hero CTA.
- No layout bugs, broken images, or overlapping text — clean render throughout.

## What's weak

- The stronger offer (up to 50% off Summer Semi-Annual Sale) is buried below the weaker 20%-off-clearance hero and full category grid — wrong offer leads.
- Two competing sale narratives in one email (20% off clearance vs. 50% off semi-annual) dilute focus and force the reader to reconcile which one applies to them.
- This is another near-identical sale-structured send in a cycle that's already run five-plus of these in two weeks (per brand history), risking promo fatigue.
- Fine print at the bottom excludes full-price styles, in-store purchases, and stacking — standard, but it's the only place the real scope of "clearance" is explained.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the bigger number.** Swap the hero and the 50%-off banner — if 50% off semi-annual is live and valid, that should be the top-of-email hero with its own CTA; extra 20% off clearance becomes the secondary banner. Leading with the smaller number is leaving open rate and click value on the table.
- 2. **Collapse to one sale narrative per send.** Two full hero treatments for two different sales in one email splits attention — pick the stronger one as the single message and let clearance be a category tile, not a second hero.
- 3. **Trim the category grid or move it below both offers.** Six black tiles between the two heroes adds scroll distance to the second (better) offer — either shrink it to 2-3 tiles or move it to the very bottom.
- 4. **Tighten urgency copy to match brand history.** "4 days only" is good, but pair it with a specific end date/time near the hero (the fine print already has it: 08/03/2026 11:59 pm MT) so the urgency is scannable without reading legal copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off clearance — ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off + extra 20% off clearance, 4 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The North Face, Marmot, OR, and more — final markdowns before Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack savings: semi-annual sale plus extra clearance markdowns, thru 8/3.`
