# Our first plans for the 4th?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**

- This is Backcountry's July 4th morning send — a text-only editorial dressed as a personal note from Gearhead Alex. After the July 2 "Thrashing through your most-used gear?" mailer that earned an 8/10 with a personalized product grid, this one steps backward — no product imagery, the offer buried below the CTA in fine print, and "up to 40% off" landing after weeks of "up to 70% off" flash sale messaging without any acknowledgment of the shift. - **Gearhead persona is on-brand.** Alex's photo, casual voice, and personal sign-off are consistent with Backcountry's expert-advisor positioning — it doesn't feel like a form letter.
- **Subject line is mobile-friendly.** "Our first plans for the 4th?" at 28 characters earns a curiosity click without spam signals.
- **CTA is clean and unambiguous.** "SHOP 4TH OF JULY SALE" in a dark full-width button is impossible to miss.
- **Smart cadence break.** After flash-sale saturation, a soft editorial feel is the right tone for the holiday morning slot.

## What's weak

- **Zero product imagery in an outdoor gear email.** No tent, no cooler, no pack — nothing visual to create a "I want that for this weekend" moment.
- **"exploreyear-round" is a visible typo.** The missing space between "explore" and "year-round" is live in the render — a basic proofreading miss.
- **Offer is buried below the CTA.** "Up to 40% off" appears in small text *under* the button — subscribers should see the deal before they see the button to act on it.
- **40% off follows weeks of 70% flash sale messaging with no explanation.** Long-time subscribers will notice the offer regressed and wonder what happened.
- **Subject is all charm, zero offer.** Someone skimming a crowded July 4th inbox won't know there's a sale without opening.

- 2. **Move the offer into the hero body.** "Up to 40% off, runs through Sunday" should appear in the first paragraph or as a callout line, not below the button in fine print.
- 3. **Fix "exploreyear-round" before the next send.** If click volume justifies it, send a corrected version.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — CSS selector typo (confirmed):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row` selector targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. Result: any `md-fluid-row col-9` column will not receive its 74.25% mobile width, causing incorrect layout on responsive mobile render. The line above it correctly handles `.col-8` at 66%, creating an uncovered gap at this breakpoint.

**Issue — External `<link>` stylesheet will be blocked:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail strip or ignore `<link>` tags in email `<head>`. Custom fonts loaded this way silently fail in the majority of commercial inboxes. Web fonts in email must be inlined inside a `<style>` block using `@import` or `@font-face`, not via a `<link>` tag. Currently, the font fallback stack (undefined from truncated source) is what most recipients see.
