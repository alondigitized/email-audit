# Ends tomorrow! Up to 70% off ⏰
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's Day 2 urgency follow-up to the Flash Sale launched June 13 ("Up to 70% off! 3 days only 🔥"). The creative is nearly identical to yesterday's launch email — same FLASH ⚡ SALE hero, same 70% ceiling, same The North Face / OR / +MORE brand strip — with one new element: the "ENDS TOMORROW!" ticker repeating across the top banner. The formula is correct for a day-2 reminder. What makes this email worth opening is the product grid, not the hero: four named products with honest, specific discounts (54% off a Kelty tent, 34% off Black Diamond pants, 40% off Norda trail shoes, 41% off a Mountain Equipment sleeping bag) give a subscriber something to act on.
- The genuine problem is cadence. Anyone who opened the June 13 double-send — Flash Sale launch *and* the bestsellers pivot — is seeing this as their third Flash Sale email in roughly 36 hours. The creative holds up; the frequency is building resistance. Backcountry's list has been pushed hard since the 20% off arc wound down on June 8, and the goodwill earned from that creative refresh is being spent fast.
- Competent execution of a tired playbook. Gets the job done without being memorable.

## What's working

- **Urgency ticker is impossible to miss**: The repeating "ENDS TOMORROW!" black bar lands the deadline in the first 200px — no scroll required.
- **Hero hierarchy is immediate**: FLASH SALE / UP TO 70% OFF dominates above the fold; there's zero cognitive friction.
- **Product grid is doing real work**: Four specific items with named brands, gendered variants, and honest percentage discounts — this is more persuasive than a second "SHOP NOW" hero.
- **Lifestyle photo is on-brand and seasonal**: Mountain biking trail shot reads immediately as June outdoor gear, not a stock-photo misfire.
- **Category nav shortcuts at the bottom**: Six clean arrow-links (Men's, Women's, Camp & Hike, Bike, Travel, Ski Clothing) give a non-buyer a fast alternative path to browse.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic**: The hero button copy tells the subscriber nothing about what they're clicking into. "Shop the Flash Sale" or "See 70% Off Deals" would be meaningfully more specific.
- **Zero social proof**: No star ratings, no "bestseller" badges, no popularity signals — particularly glaring given the June 13 "Bestsellers are in the Flash Sale" send that presumably surfaced exactly this kind of data.
- **Brand-logo strip fragments the hero**: The North Face / OR / +MORE strip under the headline is a launch-day differentiator. By day 2, the subscriber already knows which brands are in the sale — this strip just adds visual noise.
- **Preview text leaks the ticker**: The topmost visible text in the email is "ENDS TOMORROW!" from the ticker banner. If no dedicated preheader is set, inbox clients will surface that as preview — an exact echo of the subject line, squandering the slot.
- **Sender name "Backcountry | Limited Time Event" is cluttered**: The sub-label adds nothing on day 2 of a known campaign and makes the from-field harder to scan at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the hero CTA**: "SHOP NOW" → "Shop All Flash Sale Deals" or, better, split into two category CTAs ("Shop Camping Gear" / "Shop Apparel") to lift click relevance.
- 2. **Add a badge or social signal to each product tile**: "Bestseller," "Low Stock," or a star rating under the Norda shoes (40% off trail shoes is a strong deal — let the subscriber know others agree).
- 3. **Set a real preheader**: The ticker is almost certainly leaking as preview. A dedicated hidden preheader with specific deal copy ("Kelty tents, Norda shoes, Black Diamond — ends midnight tomorrow") is a quick win.
- 4. **Drop the brand-logo strip from the hero**: Reserve that for launch-day context. Day 2 benefits more from a tighter, faster-reading hero.
- 5. **Trim the sender name**: "Backcountry" or "Backcountry | Flash Sale" — the "Limited Time Event" tag is noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Flash Sale ends tomorrow — your top picks are still in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last full day: up to 70% off Kelty, Black Diamond & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `54% off tents. 40% off trail shoes. Sale ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kelty, Norda, Black Diamond — all discounted until tomorrow night`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in mobile media query:**

```css
/* Line 1 — correct */
.fluid-row .col-8, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }

/* Line 2 — typo: second selector should be .md-fluid-row .col-9, not .col-8 */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

Effect: `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is declared in both rules; the second declaration wins (74.25% instead of 66%). Any `md-fluid-row` two-column layout using `col-8` will render ~8% too wide on mobile, causing overflow or wrapping. `.md-fluid-row .col-9` is never defined, so those columns receive no responsive width override.

**Additional observations:**
- `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` applied globally to `div, p, a, li, span, td` — suppresses iOS auto-scaling for all text, including small print. Acceptable for marketing email but means tiny legal/footer copy won't auto-scale up on small screens.
