# Homepage snapshot · www.backcountry.com · 2026-07-23
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- I opened Backcountry and got walled by a bot-check interstitial — orange header, "Let's confirm you are human," a "Begin" button, and a language dropdown. That's literally the entire screen. No product, no hero, no nav, no deals. This isn't a homepage review, it's a friction-wall review, and the friction is severe.

## What's working

- The "Begin" button is high-contrast orange on white — technically unmissable if you actually need a CAPTCHA solved.
- Language selector is present, which at least signals some global-audience awareness.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** I came to browse trad gear and got a bot-check wall. This is the worst possible first screen for a returning customer.
- **No brand context whatsoever.** Backcountry's logo, category nav, or any product signal is completely absent above (and below) the fold.
- **"Let's confirm you are human" is hostile copy.** It implies I'm suspicious. Not exactly the vibe for a brand whose whole identity is "we're your Gearhead."
- **Zero personalization or recognition.** A logged-in account or cookie should bypass this — if it didn't, the session detection is broken.
- **No bypass or alternative path visible** — if I were on slow trail-head cell service, this could be a dead end.

## Recommendations

- **Fix bot-detection thresholds for mobile user agents.** A real customer on an iPhone shouldn't be hitting this wall — tighten the signal targeting so legitimate mobile sessions pass through.
- **If a CAPTCHA wall is unavoidable, brand it.** Drop the Backcountry logo and a one-liner ("Your gear is waiting — quick check first") so the experience feels intentional, not adversarial.
- **Add a "Sign in to skip" shortcut.** Returning customers with valid sessions should never see this screen. Surface a sign-in CTA so loyal shoppers can jump the queue.
- **Measure and monitor CAPTCHA intercept rate by device/channel.** If mobile homepage bounce rate is spiking, this is almost certainly why.
