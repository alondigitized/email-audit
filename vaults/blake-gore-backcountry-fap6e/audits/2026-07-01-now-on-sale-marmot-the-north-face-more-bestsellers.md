# Now on sale: Marmot, The North Face & more bestsellers
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Brand-by-brand spotlight — Patagonia, The North Face, Marmot — is the right creative pivot from Backcountry's last two weeks of generic category-grid and percentage-off fatigue. Naming three beloved outdoor labels directly in the subject is the smartest hook this brand has used since the June 22 "Here's what we'd grab" editorial send (which earned a 9). Subscribers with brand loyalty have a direct "aisle" to walk down.
- The credibility problem is harder to ignore: Backcountry told this exact list on June 25 that "up to 70% off ends TMRW" — and here's the hero on July 1 screaming "SUMMER BESTSELLERS UP TO 70% OFF" again. That's the fourth event in 12 days claiming the same depth. Subscribers who noticed the June 25 "last call" email now face a reasonable question: was the sale ever real, or is this brand just always "up to 70% off"?
- The layout itself is clean and functional — hero → brand triptych → category grid → loyalty Merino Tee close. But the category grid is dead weight after a brand-focused open, and no product prices are legible in the brand sections. Solid craft, weakened strategy.

## What's working

- Naming all three brands in the subject ("Marmot, The North Face & more bestsellers") gives inbox browsers a concrete, brand-specific reason to tap — far stronger than the June 30 subject's vague "20% off full-price items."
- Brand-by-brand section architecture (Patagonia / TNF / Marmot each with their own visual header and "SHOP NOW" CTA) turns the email into a navigable catalog rather than a wall of products.
- The Free Wasatch Merino Tee loyalty CTA with "JOIN NOW" is a genuinely high-value membership acquisition hook layered at the close.
- The "20% off" loyalty/member block stacks a second offer tier for subscribers who want full-price items — smart segmentation logic even if it's buried.
- Clean render — consistent section styling, clear button placement, no visible layout breaks.

## What's weak

- "UP TO 70% OFF" in the hero is a zombie from the June 23–25 Flash Sale that was explicitly declared over June 25 — it weakens deal credibility, not enhances it.
- Subject carries no discount signal: "Now on sale" is the flattest possible urgency hook when you're allegedly running 70% off three top outdoor brands.
- No price callouts or "was/now" pricing visible on any product in the brand sections — subscribers can't evaluate the actual deal at a glance.
- The generic six-category grid (Men's Clothing, Women's Clothing, Footwear…) is a copy-paste bolt-on that adds length and friction after a brand-specific entry point.
- This is the seventh promotional email in 12 days; cadence fatigue is now the biggest unsubscribe risk this list faces, and nothing in this email acknowledges or rewards subscriber patience.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the discount and a real deadline in the subject** — anchoring "up to 70% off" and a July 4th cutoff date directly in the subject line would lift opens meaningfully on its own.
- 2. **Add "ends July 4th" (or the real date) to the hero copy** — resurrecting the 70% claim only holds if there's a new, credible anchor event. July 4th gives you that.
- 3. **Show sale prices on every product tile** — "Was $249, Now $129" is the single change that would most immediately lift click-to-purchase conversion inside the brand sections.
- 4. **Swap the category grid for curated brand picks** — instead of "Men's Clothing / Women's Clothing," show 2–3 specific hero products per brand with names and saved-$ callouts.
- 5. **Elevate the Free Merino Tee loyalty offer** — move it immediately after the brand triptych, not buried after the category grid; it's the most differentiated offer in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Patagonia, TNF & Marmot — up to 70% off through July 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fave outdoor brands are on sale — Patagonia, TNF & Marmot`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real savings on the brands that last — shop before the holiday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: get a free Wasatch Merino Tee when you join Gearhead`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bugs:**

- **CSS selector typo** — In the responsive `<style>` block:
  ```css
  .fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
  ```
  The right-hand selector targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. On mobile, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives no matching rule and falls through, while `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets the wrong (74.25%) width. Fix: `.md-fluid-row .col-9`.

- **Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">` should be `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` for consistent baseline rendering on iOS/Android.

- **No `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — `<html>` has no `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot determine language; some spam filters score this negatively.

- **Overly broad `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important`** — Applied to `div, p, a, li, span, td` globally. This suppresses iOS automatic text scaling on all text elements unconditionally. Standard practice is to apply only to `body` or omit it where not needed.
