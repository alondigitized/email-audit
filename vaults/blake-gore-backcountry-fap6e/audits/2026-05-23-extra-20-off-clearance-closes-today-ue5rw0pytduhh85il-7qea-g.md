# EXTRA 20% off clearance closes TODAY
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth (or more) activation of a clearance campaign Backcountry declared over on May 18. The email renders cleanly and the hero hits immediately — "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE / ENDS TODAY!" is the first thing you see and it's legible. But the urgency is completely spent. A subscriber who saw "EXTRA 20% off clearance ends TMRW" yesterday and "EXTRA 20% off clearance is ON" the day before that will not feel the "closes TODAY" hook — they've been trained that the deal resurfaces.
- The email is also doing two jobs at once: close out the clearance AND launch Memorial Day bundle deals (Hike, Camp, Sun & Swim). That pivot is structurally sound but buried — the Memorial Day "SALE SALE SALE" block and bundle offers sit below the fold, meaning most mobile readers won't reach them. If the Memorial Day bundles are the real revenue play going forward, this email hides the lead.
- Verdict: competent send, wrong strategy. The clearance urgency no longer converts because it's been burned out. The Memorial Day pivot needed its own send, not a footer appearance.

## What's working

- Hero banner is clean — bold "EXTRA 20% OFF CLEARANCE" on an outdoor photo, "ENDS TODAY!" copy, and a visible "SHOP NOW" CTA all within the first screen.
- Product cards in the "Extra Savings Ending Soon" grid show specific brand names (The North Face, Nemo, Norrona) and concrete percentage discounts (40% off), which is scannable and credible.
- Top Categories nav grid is useful wayfinding — six category chips let a reader route directly to what they care about.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact on both halves of the email.

## What's weak

- Urgency is completely eroded. "Closes TODAY" after five prior sends using identical language reads as noise, not action trigger.
- Two campaigns, one send. The clearance close and the Memorial Day bundle launch compete for attention; neither gets full focus.
- Bundle deals ("20% OFF HIKE BUNDLE") are below the fold and underexplained — "purchase two or more eligible items, save 20%" tells you nothing about what's in the bundle.
- Memorial Day "SALE SALE SALE" graphic is visually loud but contextually thin — no specific brand or product is called out to anchor the deal.
- CTA copy across the entire email is generic "SHOP NOW" — no button differentiates clearance from bundles from Memorial Day.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Let the clearance die and lead with Memorial Day bundles.** This email should have been a clean handoff: "Clearance is done — here's what's next." The bundles are the fresh offer; lead with the Hike Bundle or Camp Bundle at the hero, with specific eligible products named.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs.** "Shop Hike Bundle" and "Shop Camp Bundle" instead of three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons. A reader should know at a glance which button does what.
- 3. **Put one specific product in each bundle block.** Even one line like "Includes eligible Big Agnes tents, MSR stoves, and more" makes the 20%-off offer feel real rather than vague.
- 4. **Kill the Memorial Day "SALE SALE SALE" graphic or make it the hero.** Three repetitions of "SALE" in oversized type looks like a template that wasn't finished — it has no product context.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance is done. Memorial Day bundles are live.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External font CDN dependency — single point of failure**
```
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
If `partner-fonts.bluecore.com` is unreachable (timeout, CDN outage), the email renders in client fallback fonts. No `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the visible inline CSS. Many corporate mail clients (Outlook, Gmail in restricted environments) block external stylesheets outright, meaning this font will silently fail for a significant share of recipients.

**Tracking pixel / redirect chains** — Not assessable from truncated source. Bluecore typically wraps all links in `https://t.bluecore.com/...` redirects; confirm click-tracking redirects resolve correctly and return `200` rather than `301` loops.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS `col-9` selector targets wrong class**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The right-hand selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This means:
- On `md-fluid-row` layouts, any `col-9` cell gets no responsive width override (falls through to whatever width is set inline or via another rule).
- `col-8` inside `md-fluid-row` now has two competing `!important` width rules: `66%` (from the `col-8` rule) and `74.25%` (from this incorrect rule). Cascading order determines which wins, but the intent is broken.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html>
```
No `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot determine the document language, which affects pronunciation and WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 (Level A) compliance.

**Incomplete `viewport` meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Missing `initial-scale=1`. Some mobile browsers will render at default zoom rather than 1:1, which can override the responsive breakpoint behavior the media query is targeting.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` only**
```css
-webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important;
```
Standard `text-size-adjust: none` is absent. Modern non-WebKit engines (Firefox on Android) will not respect this, potentially causing auto-font-size inflation on small viewports.
