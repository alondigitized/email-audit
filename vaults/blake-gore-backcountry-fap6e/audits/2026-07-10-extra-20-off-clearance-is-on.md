# EXTRA 20% off clearance is ON 🔓
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's clearance re-entry play following the extended 4th of July sale run that finally closed July 6. The pivot is smart — the brand has burned through its seasonal urgency twice (the "ends tomorrow" / "ends TODAY" loop), so opening a new clearance channel with an EXTRA 20% off hook is a logical next move. The hero message is clean: clearance is on, this is a limited-time bonus discount, get in now.
- The structure is functional but thin. Below the hero you get a small grid of top clearance styles (sling bag, a shirt, sandals), a category nav block, then a full-bleed trail/hike banner leading into a "Summer Sale Up To 40% Off" module. That bottom Summer Sale block is confusing — it reads like a completely different campaign has been stapled onto the end, and the 40% off framing undercuts the hero's EXTRA 20% off clearance positioning rather than reinforcing it.
- Net judgement: this is a decent transition email from a heavy sale cadence into a new clearance window, but the body loses focus quickly and the product tiles are too generic to drive strong click-through.

## What's working

- The hero banner is unambiguous — "EXTRA 20% Off Clearance" in large type with a bright yellow/gold accent; the offer is legible at a glance.
- "LIMITED TIME SALE / LIMITED" ticker/banner at the top signals urgency without resorting to ALL-CAPS spam.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA appears twice above the fold — once in the hero, once in a secondary line — minimizing scroll friction.
- The subject line lands the key offer clearly and the lock-unlock emoji adds a fresh creative beat after days of 🚨 sends.

## What's weak

- The "Summer Sale Up To 40% Off" module at the bottom directly conflicts with the hero offer — two different discount frames in one email with no editorial bridge.
- Top clearance product tiles are tiny and lack pricing context — no "was $X, now $Y" visible, which is the strongest clearance conversion signal.
- Category nav (Men's Footwear, Women's Footwear, Men's Accessories, Women's Accessories) is too narrow a cut — clearance shoppers expect more categories.
- No deadline or end date is visible anywhere in the render, which makes "limited time" feel vague after a week of specific countdown language.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove or reframe the Summer Sale footer block.** The 40% off module undercuts the hero — pick one offer per email. If both exist simultaneously, lead with the clearance + extra 20%, mention the Summer Sale as "also on" in a single line below, not a full-bleed banner.
- 2. **Add "was / now" pricing to the product tiles.** Nothing converts clearance browsers like seeing a $160 jacket for $72. Even a small "now from $X" line under each tile would lift CTR.
- 3. **Add a specific end date to the hero.** After the overdone "ends tomorrow / ends today" cycle, a concrete "through Sunday" or "48-hour window" is actually more credible now, not less.
- 4. **Expand clearance categories in the nav.** Footwear + Accessories only leaves out apparel, camping gear, and packs — the broader SKU depth is the whole point of a clearance event.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — External stylesheet will be blocked (high severity)**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```

Gmail (web and Android), Apple Mail (privacy relay), and most corporate Outlook environments strip or block external `<link>` tags. Custom fonts will silently fall back to the client's default serif/sans-serif. If the design depends on specific font weight or letter-spacing, this will degrade noticeably. Font CSS must be inlined or the design must be validated against system font fallbacks.

**Issue 2 — CSS selector collision in mobile media query (medium severity)**

```css
/* Line A — col-8 at 66% */
.fluid-row .col-8,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }

/* Line B — col-9 at 74.25%, but also re-targets .md-fluid-row .col-8 */
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` is declared twice with different widths. Because Line B appears later, it overrides Line A — `.md-fluid-row .col-8` columns render at 74.25% instead of 66% on mobile. Any two-column row using `.md-fluid-row` will have a broken width ratio on screens ≤600px.

**Issue 3 — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly (low severity)**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

Applying `none` (not `100%`) to all text elements prevents iOS Mail from adjusting small text for readability. The accepted practice is `100%` on the `body`, not `none` on all block elements.

**Issue 4 — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` (low severity)**

```html
<html>
```

No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers cannot infer language for correct pronunciation, and some spam filters treat this as a signal of low-quality HTML.
