# Ends today! 20% off full-price items 😨
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth email in a four-day campaign sequence — VIP early access (5/29), public launch (5/30), day-two reminder (5/31), ends-tomorrow (5/31), and now this final-day closer. The email does exactly what a last-day send should: big hero with the offer front-and-center, "ends today" urgency, Summit Club loyalty layer at the top, and a tight set of product thumbnails to drive immediate clicks. Mechanically, it is well-executed.
- The problem is creative déjà vu. From the hero layout to the "ENDS TODAY" typography to the brand logo strip (NEMO, The North Face, Outdoor Research), this is near-identical to yesterday's "ends tomorrow" send. Anyone who opened that email will recognize this on sight. The product grid (fishing rod, ski box, men's shorts) is also a strange final edit — not a curated "last chance on summer's big-ticket items" moment, just a random assortment pulled from catalog.
- This will convert fence-sitters who've been tracking a specific item. It will not reactivate the roughly two-thirds of subscribers who ignored the previous four sends. Good enough for the job; not better than it needed to be.

## What's working

- **Summit Club loyalty banner** — the 24% cashback callout in the top strip layers a genuine second incentive on the 20% base offer, giving members a reason to re-engage even if they've seen this before.
- **Hero is instantly legible** — "20% OFF FULL-PRICE ITEMS" in large block text, with the scrolling "TODAY! ENDS TODAY!" ticker, leaves zero ambiguity about the offer and deadline.
- **"SHOP FULL-PRICE →" CTA** — clear verb+noun, black button, high contrast; it doesn't say "Discover" or "Explore."
- **Top categories and top brands grid** — useful if the hero products don't resonate; gives subscribers multiple entry points.
- **Sender display name** — "Backcountry | Limited Time Event" correctly signals this isn't a regular promo and provides instant context.

## What's weak

- **Zero creative refresh from yesterday's email** — layout, hero, brand strip, and CTA are essentially a copy-paste of the 5/31 "ends tomorrow" send. A subscriber who opens this immediately after yesterday's gets no new signal.
- **Product grid is incoherent** — fishing spinning rod, ski roof box, and men's casual shorts don't form a "last chance summer gear" story. The edit should work harder on a final-day send.
- **Emoji mismatch** — 😨 (fearful/sweating face) reads as anxious on behalf of the sender, not the customer. Urgency emojis like ⏰ or 🔥 would fit better.
- **No visible price anchors in the product tiles** — the thumbnails show "Shop Now" with no before/after pricing. On a 20% off event, showing original price → sale price would increase urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Refresh the product grid with a curated "last chance summer kit" edit** — swap random SKUs for three to four high-value bundles or hero products (tent, pack, footwear) with struck-through original prices. This is the biggest missed lift on a final-day send.
- 2. **Add price anchors to product tiles** — even "Was $199 → $159" under each thumbnail converts browsers into buyers on the last day.
- 3. **Swap the hero image** — yesterday's send used the same outdoor adventure shot. Use a different hero image (a different activity, a different model, a tighter product close-up) to signal this is a new message.
- 4. **Fix the emoji** — replace 😨 with ⏰ or 🔥 to align tone with urgency rather than distress.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: 20% off full-price gear ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours — North Face, Osprey + more at 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summit Club members stack an extra 4% back — shop before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Big-ticket summer gear. Final day. Don't regret waiting.`

**CSS copy-paste selector bug in responsive block.** The `.md-fluid-row .col-9` selector is missing — the rule incorrectly repeats `.col-8`:
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
/*                  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ should be .md-fluid-row .col-9 */
```
Effect: at ≤600px, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives 74.25% width (col-9's value) instead of 66%, while `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no override. Any 9-column fluid grid block in this email will not stack correctly on mobile.

If this request fails or is blocked by a client's firewall/privacy filter (common in enterprise mail clients), the email falls back to system fonts with no visible degradation control. No `font-display` fallback is defined in the inline styles.
