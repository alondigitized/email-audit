# Up to 70% off ends TMRW
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the "last day" closer for Backcountry's second Flash Sale event in under two weeks — a near-identical replay of the June 13–15 run that closed with a perfect-10 "This is the last call" email. The creative is competent: red urgency ticker, clean mountain-biker hero, "UP TO 70% OFF" headline, a grid of four ending deals, and a Summit Club loyalty offer at the bottom. The bones are solid.
- The problem is déjà vu. Subscribers have now seen "UP TO 70% OFF FLASH SALE" — from Backcountry — three times in 12 days, across two overlapping sale cycles. The June 23 Flash Sale relaunch was already the second act; this is the third. "Ends TMRW" is a real deadline, but it reads like the same wolf howling again. The creative does nothing to acknowledge the repeat or give the subscriber a new reason to care.
- The Summit Club exclusive — a free Wasatch Merino Tee on $300+ orders — is the most interesting thing in this email and it's buried below the fold, below a category-tile wall. That's the wrong priority stack for a last-day closer.

## What's working

- Red scrolling ticker ("TOMORROW! ENDS TOMORROW!") lands urgency before the hero even renders — a smart structural choice.
- "DEALS ENDING SOON" grid names real brands (Columbia, POC, Original Retro Brand) giving the reader a browse target rather than just a vague discount.
- Summit Club section adds a loyalty layer the June 15 closer didn't have — free Merino tee at $300 is a meaningful incentive.
- Six category tiles give the scan-and-bail subscriber a direct path to their interest (Bike, Camp & Hike, Women's Footwear, etc.).
- Layout is clean with no render bugs — hierarchy flows ticker → hero → products → categories → loyalty.

## What's weak

- Hero screams "UP TO 70% OFF" but the four visible products show 45% off — the ceiling headline and the actual deal shown are misaligned, which erodes trust.
- The sunglasses bolt-on ("Extra 10% Off Sunglasses") breaks the Flash Sale urgency frame — it reads like a campaign stapled onto a campaign.
- Summit Club exclusive is the last section in the email — the strongest new hook is buried below four category tiles and a sunglasses ad.
- No editorial voice or curation angle: four products in a grid is browsing, not a recommendation. The June 22 email ("Here's what we'd grab") executed editorial selection visibly better.
- Third "up to 70% off" email in 12 days with identical framing — the creative does zero work to address fatigue or differentiate this run.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Summit Club perk** — "Last day + free Merino tee at $300" is a genuinely new hook Backcountry hasn't hammered. Put it in the hero or immediately below it.
- 2. **Replace the grid with one editorial hero product at a real price** — "The POC Ventral Air MIPS is 45% off. Ends midnight." converts better than a browse grid with ceiling pricing.
- 3. **Cut or separate the sunglasses section** — it dilutes the Flash Sale close. If it's worth sending, send it as its own email after the sale.
- 4. **Move Summit Club above the category tiles** — the loyalty hook belongs in the first scroll, not the last.
- 5. **Give the repeat Flash Sale some acknowledgment** — even a subtle "back by popular demand" or "our biggest markdown window of the summer" reframes the déjà vu and re-earns attention.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Flash Sale closes tonight — Summit perk inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Wasatch Merino Tee on $300+ orders — today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four deals ending at midnight, one free tee`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed Bug — CSS selector mismatch (mobile breakpoint):**

```css
/* Line in media query: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is declared twice (it already appears in the col-8 rule at `66%`), and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no mobile override. Any 9-column `md-fluid-row` block will collapse incorrectly on screens ≤600px.

**External font dependency:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```

Custom web fonts via `<link>` are blocked or ignored by Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Samsung Mail. No fallback stack is visible in the truncated source — confirm a system-safe fallback (`Arial`, `sans-serif`) is declared on all font-family properties.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

`none` fully disables iOS auto-scaling on all text elements. The standard safe value is `100%` (prevents unwanted scaling while allowing user zoom). Using `none` can cause accessibility issues on small-viewport devices and may trigger Apple Mail rendering quirks.
