# Up to 30% off ends in a couple hours 😬
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's seventh Memorial Day-adjacent send in four days, and it arrives on May 27 — two days after the actual holiday and the morning after yesterday's "ends TONIGHT" closer. The 😬 emoji in the subject is the most honest moment in the email: even the brand seems embarrassed about flogging this sale one more day. The "Final Hours!" ticker, the product grid, the bundle bars — it's the exact same creative kit as every prior send, down to the identical 20% off Hike/Camp/Sun & Swim bundle blocks.
- There is a genuine credibility problem visible in the render: the subject line promises "up to 30% off," but the hero copy appears to show "up to 20% off." That's a number mismatch the subscriber will catch immediately. Combined with the serial broken urgency (this is at least the third "last chance" claim), trust in the offer is functionally gone for anyone who's been paying attention.
- The email is competently assembled and renders cleanly. But it has crossed from urgency into fatigue, and the downgraded discount — from "70% off flash sale" last week to whatever 20–30% is now — reads like the real deal is over and Backcountry is clearing remnant inventory on reduced terms.

## What's working

- **Final Hours ticker is immediately visible** — the red repeating bar at the top communicates urgency before the hero even loads.
- **Brand-name social proof** — The North Face and Outdoor Research logos in the hero add legitimacy to the sale.
- **Bundle bars are scannable** — three distinct use-case bundles (Hike, Camp, Sun & Swim) each with a clear CTA give the eye a clear path if the hero doesn't convert.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken elements.

## What's weak

- **Subject vs. hero discount mismatch** — subject says "30% off," hero appears to show "20% off." That's a trust-breaking inconsistency visible to any engaged subscriber.
- **Broken urgency chain** — yesterday's email said the sale "ends TONIGHT." Today's says "ends in a couple hours." A subscriber who saw both will not believe either.
- **Offer step-down kills momentum** — previous sends led with "up to 70% off flash sale." This send leads with 20–30%. The real deals are clearly gone; this feels like scraps.
- **Zero new creative** — the product grid, bundle bars, and brand tile layout are unchanged from multiple prior sends. There is nothing new to see here.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic** — four instances of "SHOP NOW" with no specificity; the bundle bars are the only contextual differentiator.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the sale or own the extension explicitly** — if the sale genuinely runs through May 27, say "extended through end of day" rather than a third consecutive "final hours" claim. Subscribers aren't fooled, and the 😬 emoji signals even the sender knows it.
- 2. **Fix the 20% vs. 30% discrepancy** — either the subject or the hero is wrong. Pick one number and use it everywhere. This alone tanks click confidence.
- 3. **Lead with the sharpest remaining deal** — if 30% is real on specific categories, name them. "Up to 30% off The North Face" is infinitely more clickable than a generic headline.
- 4. **Retire or rotate the bundle bars** — they've appeared in every email this week. Either make them feel fresh with a product callout or drop them.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED BUG] CSS grid selector copy-paste error:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector incorrectly targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Any `col-9` element inside an `md-fluid-row` will render at 66% (the `col-8` width) rather than 74.25%. The correct rule should be:
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-9 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

**[CONFIRMED] `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important` applied globally:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
This suppresses user-initiated font scaling on iOS, blocking an accessibility accommodation. The value should be `100%` or this declaration should be removed; `none` is not a valid spec value and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**[CONFIRMED] External `<link>` stylesheet in `<head>` will not load in most clients:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web and app), Outlook desktop, and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags from email `<head>`. This font stylesheet will silently fail for the majority of recipients. Fonts must be declared inline via `<style>` blocks or fallback stacks must be sufficient on their own.

**[CONFIRMED] Missing dark mode declarations:**
No `<meta name="color-scheme">` or `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` present. Apple Mail and Outlook for iOS will apply automatic dark-mode color inversion without these, which can invert product image backgrounds and text on colored containers.

**[CONFIRMED] Missing Apple reformatting suppression:**
No `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">`. Apple Mail may reflow narrow layouts unpredictably without this tag.
