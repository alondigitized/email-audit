# Final chance for up to 40% off current-season styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's campaign wrap email — a warm, text-forward personal note from Gearhead Alex signaling that both the 4th of July Sale (up to 40%) and the 20% off full-price items offer end tonight. The structure follows the same editorial-persona playbook as the July 4th "Our first plans for the 4th?" send: minimal product imagery, Gearhead Alex voice, single CTA. That formula earned an 8/10 on July 2nd when it had a strong editorial hook; here it arrives as the third Backcountry email today with no new hook, which blunts its effectiveness considerably.
- The core problem is deployment order. Two other Backcountry sends already said "ends today" this morning for the same sale — once with 🚨 emoji. This one strips the drama and delivers a flat restatement. There's also a visible typo: "stellarso far!" is missing a space, a small but trust-eroding slip in an otherwise carefully voiced send. The dark hero image is on-brand but shows zero products, so anyone who made it through the text block has nothing product-specific to click toward.
- Verdict: a competent campaign closer that arrives too late in a saturated day. The Gearhead Alex persona works; the over-cadence and lack of product proof in the body work against it.

## What's working

- The personal opener ("Gearhead Alex here, Hopefully this message finds you on your way back from an epic trip…") is consistent with the brand voice and avoids the screaming urgency of the two earlier sends today — a tonal gear-shift that could re-engage fatigued openers.
- Both active offers are hyperlinked inline ("4th of July Sale" and "20% off full-price items"), giving a low-friction path to both deals without forcing a scroll to the CTA.
- "SHOP THE SALE" is a clean, unambiguous button — one action, no competing CTAs.
- The dark hero section with the mountain silhouette is visually cohesive with Backcountry's brand and contextually appropriate for a summer/outdoor send.

## What's weak

- "stellarso far!" — missing space is a visible typo in the closing line; in a short, personal email like this it reads as sloppy.
- No product imagery anywhere — compared to the July 2 editorial that included a multi-brand spotlight grid (Outdoor Research, Black Diamond, Osprey, Smith), this send gives a subscriber nothing specific to be drawn to.
- The dark hero section puts the CTA below the text block, meaning the button is likely below the fold on mobile before any urgency is established.
- This is the third Backcountry send today about the same offers; the "final chance" framing lands hollow when the morning email already said "ends today 🚨" for the same sale.
- "CATCH YOU ON THE MOUNTAIN, GEARHEAD ALEX" above the CTA is brand-flavored signoff copy, not an offer reinforcement — a missed chance to echo the 40% savings figure at the click point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix "stellarso far!"** — ship no personal-tone email with a visible typo. One word before deploy: find/replace "stellarso" → "stellar so."
- 2. **Insert 2–3 product tiles between the text block and the hero.** The July 2 send showed this works — product cards for a tent, a jacket, and a backpack (each with % off) would give the subscriber something concrete to click, not just a vague "SHOP THE SALE."
- 3. **Move the CTA closer to the offer mention.** Inline "Shop the sale →" immediately after "both end tonight" catches the intent at peak urgency rather than making the reader scroll to the dark hero.
- 4. **Echo the discount in the CTA block** — rewrite the hero call-out from "CATCH YOU ON THE MOUNTAIN" to "UP TO 40% OFF — FINAL HOURS" so the value prop travels all the way to the click point.
- 5. **Trim cadence.** Sending three "ends today" emails in one calendar day for the same sale trains subscribers to ignore all of them. A single well-constructed closer (ideally this one, with product tiles) would outperform three.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: up to 40% off — sale ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: 40% off gear you've been watching`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heads up — 4th of July Sale and 20% off full price both expire tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This is the real last call. Grab gear at up to 40% off before it resets.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — CSS selector bug:**

```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` (a duplicate of the `.col-8` rule above it) instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Any `md-fluid-row col-9` column will receive no mobile width override and will likely collapse to 100% via the catch-all `.row .col-*` rule — breaking 9-column fluid layouts on mobile.

**Minor — viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```

Best practice is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Omitting `initial-scale` can cause zoom issues on some Android WebView clients.

**Preheader padding technique:** The preview text uses `&#160;&#8204;` (NBSP + zero-width non-joiner) runs to suppress inbox preview bleed. This is standard practice; no issue, but `&#8204;` (ZWNJ) is occasionally rendered as a visible space in older Samsung Mail. Low risk.
