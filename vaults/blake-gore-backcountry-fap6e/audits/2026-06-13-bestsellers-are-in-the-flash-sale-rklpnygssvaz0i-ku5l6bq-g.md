# Bestsellers are in the Flash Sale 🔥
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Backcountry's second send on June 13, landing in subscribers' inboxes on the same day as the Flash Sale launch email ("Up to 70% off! 3 days only 🔥" — scored 9/10 in the prior audit). The pivot to a "bestsellers are IN the sale" angle is a smart creative differentiation: it frames this as a curated discovery email rather than a repeat blast. The hero execution is sharp — mountain biker against forest light, "FLASH ⚡ SALE / UP TO 70% OFF" in high-contrast type, and a clean 2×2 product grid anchored by recognizable brand names at real discounts.
- The problem is the same-day double-send. Any subscriber who opened the morning Flash Sale launch is now seeing near-identical hero creative, the same 70% off ceiling, and overlapping SKUs. "Bestsellers" saves this from being a pure duplicate, but it strains the credibility of the "3 DAYS ONLY" scarcity narrative — running an event and filling it with multiple sends on day one signals either desperation or poor segmentation hygiene.
- The creative itself earns its keep. The timing does not. If this hit non-openers only, it's a defensible B2 send. If it hit the same full list twice in one day, that's a mistake that compounding Flash Sale fatigue makes worse.

## What's working

- "FLASH ⚡ SALE / UP TO 70% OFF" lands immediately in the hero — no ambiguity about the offer.
- The 2×2 product grid shows four named items (Black Diamond, Mountain Hardwear, The North Face) at real 50% off discounts, substantiating the headline claim rather than hiding behind "up to."
- "3 DAYS ONLY!" ticker in the header nav adds scarcity without polluting the subject line.
- "10% Cash Back" loyalty callout in the top nav gives members a compounding reason to click beyond the sale discount.
- Brand logos in the hero (The North Face, Outdoor Research) serve as credibility anchors for a multi-brand event.

## What's weak

- Second send on June 13 with near-identical hero creative as the same-day Flash Sale launch — subscriber tolerance was already spent.
- "SHOP NOW" on the hero CTA is generic; "SHOP BESTSELLERS" (the lower black button) is more specific and should be the hero button.
- Product tiles show only name and discount — no colorways, size availability, or "only 2 left" signal that would close undecided shoppers.
- No social proof anywhere despite the "bestsellers" framing — no star ratings, review counts, or "top pick" badges on individual products.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress to non-openers only.** If this hit the same list as the same-day launch, that's the root problem. The single change with the highest lift is proper audience segmentation before send — non-openers of the morning email only, or hold it for Day 2 with "still going + here's what to grab" framing.
- 2. **Put a specific % or brand in the subject.** "Bestsellers are in the Flash Sale" is passively framed. Naming a concrete discount or brand cuts through harder.
- 3. **Swap hero CTA.** Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop Bestseller Picks" — the email has earned a specific button.
- 4. **Add a stock-scarcity signal** to at least one product tile ("Only a few left," "Going fast"). The 50% off justification is already there; a supply signal closes the loop.
- 5. **Add bestseller badges.** Overlaying a "Top Pick" or "#1 Bestseller" badge on each product tile earns the email's own positioning and creates individual product urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off our bestsellers — Flash Sale ends in 3 days`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Flash Sale: Black Diamond, TNF, MHW now 50% off 🔥`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bestselling jackets and pants from top brands, now half price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `3 days to grab the picks that always sell out first.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**CSS selector typo — mobile column mis-sizing**
```css
/* line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` never receives its mobile width → falls back to whatever cascades in (likely `100%` from the `.row .col-*` rule), breaking any 9-column grid layout on mobile.
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives `74.25%` *instead of* its correct `66%` (defined two rules earlier), overriding the intended width.

**External `<link>` stylesheet stripped by major clients**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/backcountry/backcountry_fonts_1769208036.css">
```
Gmail (web + Android), Yahoo Mail, and AOL strip all `<link>` tags. Brand custom fonts will not load for the majority of recipients; system fallbacks (Arial, sans-serif) will render instead. Font CSS must be inlined into a `<style>` block if brand typography is required.

**Overly broad `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Disabling text-size adjustment on every element prevents iOS Mail from scaling small text to readable sizes. The standard practice is to apply this only to the `<body>` or outer wrapper, not all content elements.
