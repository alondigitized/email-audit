# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Classic Warby Parker aesthetic — the two stacked frames in the hero are legitimately beautiful and they've clearly still got the tortoiseshell DNA that made me a fan in the first place. But landing on this homepage felt like getting buzzed into a building and then having someone immediately hand you a clipboard of paperwork: a cookie modal eats the entire lower half of the screen before I can see a single word of hero copy, a price, or a CTA. For a returning customer who's done four home try-ons, that first friction is a bad omen.

## What's working

- **The hero frame photo** — two stacked pairs, navy tortoise on top, deep burgundy below, clean warm-beige background. That's a mood board, not just a product shot. It arrested my scroll immediately.
- **Top navigation tabs** — "Eyeglasses / Sunglasses / Contacts / Style quiz" in one thumb-friendly bar. I can get to Eyeglasses in one tap, which is exactly where I'd go.
- **Style quiz tab** — as a home try-on obsessive, I live in that flow. The fact that it's a top-level nav item rather than buried in a hamburger menu shows they know their customer.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal** — it fires immediately and swallows the bottom 40–50% of the viewport, covering whatever hero copy, CTA, and promo info was there. For a returning customer on mobile, this is a reflex-close situation, but I still had to deal with it before seeing the actual page.
- **Zero pricing signal above the fold** — the $95 starting price is literally Warby Parker's main competitive advantage over legacy optical. Not a trace of it here. That's the number that hooks skeptical Millennials; hiding it is a miss.
- **No home try-on mention** — my primary reason for coming back year after year. Nothing above the fold acknowledges the feature that differentiates them from every DTC glasses brand that launched since 2020.
- **No human being in the shot** — I'm trying to imagine how these frames look *on a face*, not as a still life. Lifestyle imagery with an actual person would do more conversion work than the product-only composition.

## Recommendations

- **Kill or delay the cookie modal on return visits** — if I'm logged in or have a cookie already set, skip the consent wall. A first-party cookie check costs nothing and removes the single biggest friction point I experienced.
- **Lock "$95. Free home try-on. No exam required."** into the hero copy** — three value props, one line, visible without any scrolling. That's the brand promise and it's invisible right now.
- **Swap the still-life hero for a lifestyle shot of someone actually wearing the frames** — ideally a 28–35-year-old in a WFH or coffee-shop setting. Make me see myself in these glasses.
- **Add a "Back for your annual try-on?" returning-customer hook** in the nav or a sticky bar — a small nudge like "Your last try-on was X — ready to refresh?" would get a home try-on devotee into the funnel in one tap.
