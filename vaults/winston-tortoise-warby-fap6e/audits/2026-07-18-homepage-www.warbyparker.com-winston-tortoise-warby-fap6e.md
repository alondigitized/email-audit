# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-07-18
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Warby Parker's homepage is trying to be a clean, minimalist eyewear brand — and in the half-second before the cookie modal detonated across my entire screen, I caught a glimpse of what looked like a genuinely gorgeous tortoise-templed red frame in the hero. But I can't actually *see* the homepage because a massive cookie consent overlay is sitting on top of literally everything. Classic own-goal — this is a brand I've ordered from four times and they're leading with legalese before they even say hello.

## What's working

- **The frame in the hero**: Red acetate with unmistakable tortoise temples, shot close-up and crisp — this is exactly the kind of detail that makes me want to do a try-on round. Whoever picked this product for the hero understands their customer.
- **Navigation bar**: Clean, simple — Eyeglasses, Sunglasses, Contacts, Style Quiz. I can see exactly how to get to what I want in one tap if I dismiss the modal.
- **Style Quiz link**: That's still up there in the nav and I'd actually use it to narrow down this season's frames. Smart persistent placement.

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal as first impression**: This is the entire above-the-fold experience. There's zero brand messaging, zero offer, zero CTA that moves me toward glasses — just a wall of privacy text and a "Manage Preferences" button. On a return visit from a loyal customer who's done four try-ons, this feels insulting.
- **No visible offer or home try-on hook**: Home try-on is literally Warby's signature differentiator and I can't see it anywhere. That should be one of the first things I see.
- **No returning customer recognition**: No "Welcome back," no loyalty status, no "pick up where you left off" — nothing that acknowledges this isn't my first rodeo.
- **Hero copy is completely hidden**: I have no idea what the current campaign message or seasonal push is. The modal ate it entirely.

## Recommendations

- **Minimize the cookie prompt to a slim banner at the bottom of the screen** — not a full-bleed modal that buries the entire homepage. Every major retailer has solved this; Warby is behind here.
- **Make "Home Try-On" the primary above-fold CTA** — a "Try 5 frames free" button should be unmissable for a brand whose trial program is the core conversion lever.
- **Add a returning-customer module**: If I'm logged in (or even if my browser has a cookie), show me "Continue your try-on" or "Your last order: [frame name] — see what's new in tortoise."
- **Keep the tortoise close-up frame shot — but add the product name and a "Try it free" button overlaid on the hero** so that beautiful image actually works as conversion real estate.
