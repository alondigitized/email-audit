# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-06-06
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see when I land is a cookie consent wall sitting on top of what looks like a pretty nice tortoiseshell frame shot — which, honestly, is the most Winston Tortoise thing Warby Parker could put in their hero, so props there. The brand still reads clean and considered: navy nav, minimal logo, the classic four-tab layout. But I can't actually interact with any of it because the modal is eating the entire screen. As someone who cycles through home try-on every single year and knows this site well, I'm dismissing the modal immediately — a first-timer might just bail.

## What's working

- **Tortoiseshell hero frame** — I see two pairs of glasses, one clearly tortoise-patterned. That's not an accident and it's directly in my wheelhouse. If I can get past the modal, this image will pull me in.
- **Eyeglasses tab in top nav** — one tap to my category, no hunting. That's the right call on mobile.
- **Clean nav design** — navy bar, icon-only cart/account, four clear categories. No clutter, no mystery meat navigation.
- **"Style quiz" tab** — for someone on their fifth annual home try-on, I skip it, but it signals Warby Parker still cares about the discovery funnel.

## What's weak

- **Cookie consent modal** — it covers literally everything below the nav fold. No hero copy, no CTA, no offer — all invisible. This is the single biggest friction point on the page for a first-timer.
- **Zero returning-customer signal** — I've ordered from Warby Parker multiple times. There's nothing acknowledging I exist: no account welcome, no "pick up where you left off," no home try-on status.
- **No offer is surfaced** — I'm curious whether the $95 tier has gotten any play or whether the push is toward the $145 premium. I'd want to see that distinction immediately. Right now I see nothing.
- **No blue-light coating callout** — I add it every single time. If it's not surfaced on the homepage, it's a missed upsell on someone who converts 100% of the time.

## Recommendations

- **Auto-dismiss or minimize the cookie modal after 3 seconds** — or at minimum, make the hero copy and one CTA visible above the modal by reducing its height. The tortoise frame shot underneath is good; let it breathe.
- **Add a "Home Try-On" CTA to the top nav or hero** — for returning annual cyclers like me, that's the front door. Eyeglasses → HTO should be one tap, not buried deeper in the flow.
- **Surface the $95 price point as a hero message** — "Frames starting at $95, including prescription" would be the single most trust-building thing Warby Parker could put above the fold for a price-sensitive millennial.
- **Blue-light coating badge or callout in nav/hero** — even a small "blue-light add-on available" chip would nudge people like me toward a faster conversion.
