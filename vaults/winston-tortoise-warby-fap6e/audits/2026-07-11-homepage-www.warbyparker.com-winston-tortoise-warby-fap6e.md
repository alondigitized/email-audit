# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-07-11
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Warby Parker comes in looking sharp — literally, those bold rectangular dark-red frames with tortoiseshell temples in the hero are *exactly* my frame DNA — but then a cookie consent modal parks itself on top of everything before I can read a single word of copy or find the home try-on CTA I came for. It's a clean, confident brand that knows its aesthetic, but right now I'm staring at a preference-management popup instead of a glasses lineup.

## What's working

- **The hero glasses themselves** — dark burgundy rectangular front with amber tortoiseshell temples is genuinely compelling product photography for someone like me who owns four frames and actively hunts for that look. I almost want to tap it even with the modal in the way.
- **Top nav tabs** — Eyeglasses, Sunglasses, Contacts, Style quiz are all above the fold, immediately scannable, and "Style quiz" is a nice low-commitment on-ramp for someone cycling through home try-on annually.
- **Brand wordmark + glasses icon** in the header is clean and confident — this reads premium-but-approachable, exactly the $95-floor vibe I trust.

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal is the entire first interaction** — before I see a headline, a CTA, or any pricing signal, I'm managing preferences. On mobile this is full-screen disruption. It's a bounce risk for anyone impatient.
- **No home try-on callout visible** — that's the reason I come back to this site every year, and there's no hook for it anywhere in the above-fold experience.
- **No price anchoring** — I care deeply about the $95 tier. Zero pricing visible anywhere. That's a miss for returning shoppers like me who use price-awareness as a trust signal.
- **No blue-light coating mention** — it's my default add-on and a margin driver for them; not surfacing it above the fold is a lost upsell moment.
- **No model or lifestyle context** — the frame shot is a clean product close-up floating on a light background. Beautiful, but no human wearing them, no desk-and-laptop scene that says "this is your work-from-home upgrade."

## Recommendations

- **Dismiss the cookie modal or shrink it to a bottom banner** — even a slim bar at the bottom with a single "Accept" tap would let the hero content breathe and stop blocking conversion intent on first load.
- **Add a "Home try-on — free, ships in 1 day" callout as a persistent sub-banner** — that's the single biggest reason repeat customers like me choose Warby over a competitor; it should be impossible to miss.
- **Surface a price anchor in the hero area** — something like "Frames starting at $95, prescription included" is the trust signal that pulls returning shoppers back into the funnel immediately.
- **Swap the floating product shot for a lifestyle close-up** — same frames, but on a face (preferably a male in his late 20s–early 30s at a coffee shop or desk), which makes the jump from "cool glasses" to "that's me" a lot shorter.
