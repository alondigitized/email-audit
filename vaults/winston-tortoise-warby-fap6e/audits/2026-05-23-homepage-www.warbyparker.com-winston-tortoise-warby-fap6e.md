# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Warby Parker has always felt like *my* brand — the whole $95-glasses-delivered-to-my-door thing was practically invented for me — but right now I'm staring at a cookie consent wall that's swallowed most of the homepage before I've even seen what they're pushing this season. The brand still reads clean and confident (that group selfie vibe is very "my friends on a rooftop"), but I can't shop through a GDPR modal, so first impressions are rough.

## What's working

- **Top nav strip** — "Eyeglasses / Sunglasses / Contacts / Style quiz" in one tap. That's exactly where I need to go and it's right there above the fold, above even the cookie modal. As someone who goes straight for eyeglasses, I can bypass the whole mess.
- **Hero photography** — the partial glimpse of four people in frames, shot from below against a blue sky, feels aspirational without being pretentious. The group energy and age range lands for me.
- **Brand logo confidence** — "WARBY PARKER" in centered sans-serif on that navy bar is unmistakably them. No identity confusion.
- **Cart + account icons in header** — as a returning customer who orders every year, those are immediately useful touchpoints.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal** — it covers maybe 65% of the viewport. I can't see a single line of hero copy, any CTA button, any pricing callout, or any home-try-on hook. For a brand whose whole thing is frictionless digital eyewear shopping, opening with a wall of legal text is a serious own-goal.
- **No home try-on mention visible anywhere** — that program is why I keep coming back year after year. It should be in the hero or at minimum a sticky strip. I see zero reference to it above the fold.
- **No price anchoring** — the $95 starting price is legitimately a competitive weapon and it's nowhere. My first impression has no reason to feel good about value.
- **Sunglasses-first framing (implied)** — the hero image is all sunglasses, but I'm here for eyeglasses. The nav saves me, but the visual lead doesn't.
- **No trust signals or review counts** — nothing saying "4.8 stars" or "10M+ frames sold" or "free returns" before I even see product.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the cookie modal or auto-dismiss it after 3 seconds** on mobile — alternatively, collapse it to a slim bottom bar so the hero is actually visible. Losing the entire above-the-fold on a cookie banner is a conversion killer.
- **Add a persistent "Home Try-On" strip** directly under the nav — something like "Try 5 frames free at home → Ships in 1 day." That's the brand differentiator and it's invisible right now.
- **Put the $95 starting price in the hero copy** — "Eyeglasses starting at $95, including lenses" should be a headline, not something I have to hunt for.
- **Swap hero image to show someone actually *wearing* eyeglasses** (not just sunglasses) — or split the hero by season-category with eyeglasses prominent for returning shoppers who came for frames, not shades.
