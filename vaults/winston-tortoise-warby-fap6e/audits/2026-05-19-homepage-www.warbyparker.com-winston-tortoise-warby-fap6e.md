# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see when I load Warby Parker? A cookie consent wall sitting on top of whatever they actually want to show me. The brand DNA is still there — those stacked tortoise and navy frames in the hero are basically made for me — but the homepage is leading with a legal modal instead of a story. It reads like a clean, confident optical brand that got interrupted mid-sentence.

## What's working

- **Frame imagery in the hero:** The stacked tortoiseshell + dark navy frames are genuinely beautiful — close-up, tactile, product-forward. As someone who owns four frames and cycles home try-on annually, that shot makes me want to touch the screen.
- **Top navigation:** "Eyeglasses / Sunglasses / Contacts / Style quiz" — four clean tabs, no ambiguity, Eyeglasses is first and that's exactly where I'm headed.
- **Style quiz tab:** Having "Style quiz" in the primary nav is smart for me specifically. I use the home try-on loop; a quiz entry point signals they understand how I shop.

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal on top of the hero:** The single biggest fail here. I land on the page and the first thing I'm asked to do is manage privacy preferences, not shop. My hero is completely occluded — I have no idea what the headline says, what the CTA is, or what they're pushing right now.
- **Zero pricing signal above fold:** Warby Parker's $95 entry price is genuinely compelling — it's the thing that pulled me in years ago. Nothing on screen reinforces it. A returning customer like me is re-evaluating every year; I want to see "Frames from $95" somewhere instantly.
- **No returning-customer hook:** No "welcome back," no rewards, no "your last try-on was X" nudge. Warby has my email and my order history. This homepage treats me like a first-time visitor.
- **No urgency or seasonal hook visible:** I can't tell if there's a spring campaign, a limited colorway, a new collection — nothing. The modal ate all of that context.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress or collapse the cookie banner on repeat visits.** If I've been to the site before (cookie or login signal), don't re-wall me. And even on first visit, use a slim persistent banner at the bottom — not a full-screen overlay eating my hero.
- **Add a persistent "$95 starting price" badge or strip above the fold.** Even a one-line banner — "Frames start at $95, including prescription lenses" — would immediately anchor the value and reassure the tier-skeptic in me.
- **Surface a home try-on CTA above the fold.** "Try 5 frames at home, free" is the thing that makes Warby different from every other optical site. It should be prominent in the hero, not buried after a scroll.
- **Add a returning-customer login state that shows something personal** — last frame tried, loyalty status, "your next eye exam is due" — anything that signals the brand knows me.
