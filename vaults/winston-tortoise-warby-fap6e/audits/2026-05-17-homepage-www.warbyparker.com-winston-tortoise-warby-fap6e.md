# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see when I land? A cookie consent wall eating the entire viewport — the hero, the CTA, any offer they might be running, all of it buried. What I *can* see is a navy nav bar, the classic Warby category tabs, and a moody close-up of frames that look like tortoise and dark acetate. Warby looks premium and deliberate in its brand presentation, but right now it's leading with legalese instead of the product I came to shop.

## What's working

- **Frame photography** — the close-up of layered tortoise and dark acetate frames is actually perfect for me. That's the kind of tactile, "I want to hold these" shot that makes me want to browse.
- **Category nav** — "Eyeglasses," "Sunglasses," "Contacts," "Style quiz" is exactly the right four tabs. No clutter, no guesswork. I can tap "Eyeglasses" the second I dismiss the modal.
- **Brand wordmark** — clean, confident, navy. Still feels like the premium-but-not-luxury brand I trust at the $95 tier.

## What's weak

- **Cookie consent modal occupies 80%+ of the viewport** — this is the entire experience above the fold. I can't see a hero headline, a CTA, a promo, nothing. The modal body text is a dense legal paragraph — not a friendly "we use cookies, cool?" nudge.
- **Zero pricing or offer signal** — as someone who orbits the $95 tier and is skeptical of the $145 "premium" upsell, I want price anchoring fast. I get none of it.
- **No home try-on hook visible** — HTO is literally the reason I keep coming back every year and it's nowhere in what I can see.
- **No social proof or trust signal** — no review count, no "4.8 stars," no "free shipping + free returns" badge. All hidden.
- **"Manage Preferences" as the only CTA** — for a returning customer who just wants to browse frames, this is friction before the first step.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the full-page cookie modal with a slim bottom banner** — a single-line "We use cookies. OK / Manage" strip lets the hero breathe and doesn't bury the entire experience for a user who just wants glasses.
- **Surface the Home Try-On CTA in the hero** — "Order 5 frames. Try them at home. Ship back free." That one sentence is why I'm loyal. It should be the hero headline or a persistent badge.
- **Add a price anchor strip below the nav** — a thin persistent bar reading "Frames starting at $95 · Blue-light lenses included" would immediately speak to returning shoppers like me who know the pricing tiers and want confirmation nothing changed.
- **Show a lifestyle shot with the product close-up** — a millennial guy actually wearing the tortoise frames in a real-world setting would land harder than frames on a table, however moody the lighting is.
