# Homepage snapshot · www.warbyparker.com · 2026-07-04
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- My first impression landing on Warby Parker is... a cookie wall. Almost the entire above-the-fold experience is eaten by a "Website Cookies" consent modal, with only the tiniest sliver of what looks like a gorgeous pair of dark green frames peeking out behind it. The brand telegraphs clean, premium minimalism through that navy header, but I can't actually experience any of it right now. This feels like showing up to try on frames and being handed a legal form before you even touch the glasses.

## What's working

- **Nav bar categories** — Eyeglasses, Sunglasses, Contacts, Style quiz is exactly the right four options for a returning shopper like me. I can tap "Eyeglasses" immediately without digging.
- **Dark green frames in the hero** — what little I can see of the hero image is compelling. That's a rich, jewel-toned frame that catches my eye instantly. Strong product photography, at least from the sliver visible.
- **Warby Parker brand trust** — navy header, clean wordmark, no visual clutter. Even in a degraded state, the brand reads as credible and well-produced.

## What's weak

- **Cookie modal covers the entire hero** — the consent dialog takes up roughly 80% of the viewport and blocks every signal that could hook me: the hero copy, the CTA, any offer. This is a critical UX failure for mobile.
- **No offer visible anywhere** — I'm a returning home try-on shopper; I'm price-conscious about the $95 vs. $145 tier. Not seeing a single price signal, promo, or even a "start home try-on" hook above the fold.
- **No returning-user recognition** — no "welcome back," no recently viewed, no loyalty hook. WP knows me but the homepage treats me like a stranger even before the cookie wall.
- **"Manage Preferences" as the dominant CTA** — the biggest, boldest interactive element on my screen right now is a consent management button. That's backwards.

## Recommendations

- **Shrink the cookie banner to a bottom toast** — a slim 60px strip at the bottom with "Accept" and "Manage" keeps consent compliant without nuking the hero experience. Every major retailer does this; WP should too.
- **Surface the home try-on CTA in the nav or a sticky strip** — I tap into this site specifically to start a try-on. A "Try 5 frames free" button in the nav or a sticky top strip would catch returning shoppers like me immediately.
- **Show a price anchor above the fold** — even something like "Frames from $95, including prescription" in the sub-header or hero callout would immediately validate I'm in the right place and not about to get hit with $200 frames.
- **Add a "Welcome back" returning-user state** — if I'm logged in, swap the generic hero for a "Ready for your next try-on?" prompt. That's a one-week ship for a logged-in variant and it would dramatically improve returning-shopper conversion.
