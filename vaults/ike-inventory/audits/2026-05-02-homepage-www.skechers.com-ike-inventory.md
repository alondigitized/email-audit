# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a comfort-and-lifestyle brand running a celebrity-spokesperson campaign for their Hands Free Slip-ins line. The page above the fold is split between two promo banners and a dual-celebrity hero — Brooke Burke on the left, and what looks like a male celebrity (name tag too small to read clearly) on the right. Nothing here is pointed at kids, boys' footwear, or anyone doing inventory work — this is a 45-year-old active-adult play, front and center.
- **2/10**

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF banner** — top-of-screen, hard to miss, auto-applied at cart. That's a clean mechanic for a consumer who already knows what they want. If I were here for adult product, this would move me.
- **Slip-ins branding** — "HANDS FREE Slip-ins" is big, legible, and product-specific at the bottom of the viewport. At least I know what the hero SKU family is.
- **Dual-promo stacking** — two separate banners (BOGO + 25% off apparel) visible before I even touch the scroll. High promotional density above the fold.

## What's weak

- **Zero boys' content visible** — I came to audit boys' shoes. Nothing in this viewport signals kids exist on this site.
- **Celebrity pairing skews old** — Brooke Burke reads 40s-lifestyle-wellness. The male celebrity reads the same demo. This is not a homepage talking to parents buying back-to-school kicks or me looking for boys' SKU coverage.
- **No navigation shortcuts above the fold** — the only nav is a hamburger MENU. No visible category tabs, no "Kids" or "Boys" shortcut. I have to go hunting.
- **Apparel promo is noise** — I'm a footwear auditor. A "Spring Apparel Sale" strip is irrelevant and burns real estate.
- **Hero is a split lifestyle scene, not a product shot** — I cannot see the actual product clearly at scale. The shoes are incidental props on seated celebrities.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Kids / Boys" quick-link in the sticky nav bar** — even one tap-target next to MENU would route footwear buyers to the right lane immediately.
- **Swap the hero or add a second hero card for Kids Slip-ins** — if the Slip-ins franchise has a boys' version, put a kid wearing them in that blue section below, not just adult celebrities.
- **Replace the apparel sale banner with a category-row strip** — four icons: Men / Women / Kids / Sale. Takes the same vertical space, routes every buyer segment faster.
- **Make celebrity name tags legible at mobile resolution** — currently both names are unreadable thumbnail text. If you're paying endorsement fees, make the name readable.
