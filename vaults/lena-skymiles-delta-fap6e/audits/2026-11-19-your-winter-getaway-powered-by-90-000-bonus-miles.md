# Your Winter Getaway, Powered by 90,000 Bonus Miles

- This is a clean, single-offer card acquisition send. The gold Amex SkyMiles card sits under a "YOUR SPECIAL OFFER: EARN 90,000 BONUS MILES" hero. A "+ EARN 2X MILES ON DELTA PURCHASES" line and a red "VIEW DETAILS" button follow. The eye lands on the offer first, and the personalized line ("Lena, with your special offer, earn 90,000 bonus miles after you spend $5,000 on your Card in the first 6 months") states the terms.
- The email then stacks a "Boost Your Miles Balance" table (90,000 earned, 0 current balance, 90,000 total) and a "Your Benefits Are Waiting" section. That section covers a free checked bag, a $120 rideshare credit and 15% off Award Travel. A Business Owner upsell sits near the bottom, and an enormous legal block runs below that. The copy is repetitive. The 90K claim appears in the hero, the body and the table, and there are two CTAs ("View Details", "Learn More") before the benefits.
- The offer is strong, and it follows Delta's 7-8/10 sends on Nov 13 and Nov 2. It is a rerun of the same Amex pitch that Lena has seen roughly ten times since August. The subject is seasonal ("Winter Getaway"), but the body has no winter or destination imagery. The subject promise and the body don't connect.

## What's working

- The hero is unmistakable: a large "90,000 BONUS MILES" over the card art, with a single red button.
- The personalized line sets out the spend threshold ($5,000 in 6 months) and the offer end date (January 27, 2027) near the top.
- The balance table makes the offer concrete: 90,000 earned, 0 current balance, 90,000 total.
- The three-benefit section (free bag, rideshare credit, 15% off Award Travel) reads as lifestyle value, and the photos support it.
- The Delta header and footer keep the brand voice consistent and trustworthy.

## What's weak

- The "Winter Getaway" subject has no payoff in the body. There are no winter or destination visuals, and the offer is the same card pitch as before.
- Two stacked CTAs ("View Details", "Learn More") before the benefits section dilute the primary action.
- The terms block is very long, about half the render. It is dense, tiny text, and "$5,000 spend" is the key condition. That condition is stated in small type under the hero, not in the headline.
- The cadence is stale. This is at least the tenth Amex acquisition send to Lena since late August, including the Nov 13 "Earn 90K Bonus Miles" email, with little change in creative.
- The Business Owner banner is off-topic for this recipient and adds noise right before the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero match the subject. Use a winter destination image with "90,000 miles ≈ a round trip to X" so the getaway promise pays off, and keep one CTA.
- 2. Put the spend condition ($5,000 in 6 months) and the deadline next to the headline number instead of in the small type.
- 3. Cut the second CTA and the Business Owner banner. Keep the benefits section to three items, each linking to its own detail.
- 4. Suppress or space this send. A near-duplicate of the Nov 13 email arrives six days later, so vary the creative or the angle.
- 5. Move the legal block to a linked page, or collapse it to a short summary with a link.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- The truncated source doesn't expose the link hrefs, so I can't confirm any broken or malformed links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
Confirmed from source:
- **Missing alt text:** `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` has no `alt` attribute (QA WARN). Screen readers will announce the filename or skip the image.
- **Non-HTTPS image:** the same asset loads from `http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png`. Clients may block it or flag mixed content. The `image.o.delta.com` host should serve it over HTTPS.
- **Duplicate root element:** `<html xmlns=... lang="en" dir="ltr">` is immediately followed by a bare `<html>`.
- **Content before the DOCTYPE:** a `<div style="font-size:0px; line-height:1px;">` and several comments come before `<!DOCTYPE html>`. This can trigger quirks mode in some clients.
- **Block elements inside `<head>`:** `<!-- BEGIN CSS BLOCK --> <div style="font-size:0px; line-height:1px;"> <style>…` puts a `<div>` in `<head>`, which is invalid. Parsers may close `<head>` early and push the `<style>` blocks into `<body>`.
- **Non-standard viewport:** `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; user-scalable=yes"` uses semicolons instead of commas. Most clients tolerate it, but it isn't spec-compliant.
- **Non-standard X-UA-Compatible:** `content="IE=9; IE=8; IE=7; IE=EDGE"` is also malformed and has no effect in modern clients.
- **Ineffective CSS selector:** `ReadMsgBody { width: 100%; }` is missing the leading `.`, so it targets a nonexistent element instead of the Outlook.com class.
- **Unclosed meta tags:** the `<meta name="viewport">`, `format-detection` and `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` tags are not self-closed. This is only a problem if the doc is treated as XHTML, which the `xmlns` declaration implies.
- The 06/02/2025 comment says aria tags were added, but none are visible in the truncated source, so I can't verify them.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the truncated source. The lookup and variable scripts are in comments and appear to have been processed before send. The body past the truncation point wasn't inspected.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` over `https://` and add a descriptive `alt` (or `alt=""` if it's decorative).
2. Remove the nested `<html>` tag and move the lookup-script `<div>` and comments after the DOCTYPE, or into `<body>`.
3. Take the `<div>` wrapper out of `<head>` and leave only `<style>` and `<meta>` there.
4. Change the viewport meta to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`. Remove or fix the X-UA-Compatible value.
5. Change `ReadMsgBody` to `.ReadMsgBody`.
6. Re-run QA against raw SMTP source, or from a seed inbox, to capture `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Then confirm or clear the compliance warnings.
7. Run the 43 tracking links through a redirect-following check that allows the click domains, to validate destinations and UTM carry-through.
