# Homepage snapshot · www.delta.com · 2026-08-13
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- Delta's homepage still knows exactly who I am the second I land — flight search front and center, "BUR" already populated like it remembers I fly out of Burbank. But the hero real estate is spent on an Amex card acquisition pitch, not on anything that speaks to me as a Diamond Medallion who already books direct. It reads like a bank ad wearing a Delta skin.

## What's working

- The flight search module up top, pre-filled with "BUR" — feels like it recognizes a frequent booker and gets out of my way.
- "Find Flights" is a real button with real intent, not a mushy "Explore" CTA.
- SkyMiles and Delta AMEX Cards are both one tap away in the nav bar — my loyalty ecosystem isn't hidden.
- The fine print on the mileage offer is small but present, not buried three scrolls down.

## What's weak

- The hero is an Amex welcome offer, not anything about the Delta flying experience — as a Diamond Medallion I'm not the target for a "welcome offer," I already have the relationship.
- "As High As 80,000 Miles*" — that asterisk-and-"as high as" combo is exactly the kind of soft bait copy that makes me skeptical rather than curious.
- No operational reliability messaging, no on-time performance stat, nothing about Delta One or Comfort+ — the stuff I actually scan for isn't here.
- The lifestyle photo (woman on salt flats, flowing skirt) reads vacation-editorial, not "business traveler who values consistency."

## Recommendations

- Swap the Amex welcome-offer hero for something rooted in the flying experience — a Delta One cabin shot or an on-time performance callout — and move the card offer to a secondary module.
- Add a "Welcome back" or status-tier acknowledgment near the search bar for logged-in Medallion members instead of a generic "Log in" prompt.
- Surface a Choice Benefits or SkyMiles balance teaser above the fold — something that rewards loyalty rather than pitches new acquisition.
- Tighten "As High As 80,000 Miles" to a real number or clearer tiered breakdown so it doesn't read as vague.
