# Get The Most Out Of Your SkyMiles® Membership
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a membership-education email — Delta's play to convert a freshly-enrolled Lena from passive accumulator into active miler. The cadence gap is healthy: 49 days since the June 5 Amex card push, so this doesn't feel spammy. The concept is right — "here's how to actually earn miles in your daily life" is exactly the email a new SkyMiles member needs. But the execution is diffuse. The email tries to cover too many earning categories at once (Uber, presumably dining, shopping, hotels, etc.) with no single dominant offer or urgency hook to drive a click right now.
- The hero banner lands "MEMBERSHIP" in large type, which is brand-correct but not action-oriented. There's a CTA button visible early, which is good, but without a concrete number attached — no "Earn 3X" or "Get 1,000 bonus miles this week" — it reads as a brochure, not a conversion driver. The Uber co-brand module is the strongest hook in the email, but it's buried below the fold.
- Verdict: a serviceable mid-funnel engagement email that maintains the relationship but leaves meaningful click-through lift on the table. Compared to the June 5 Amex send (8/10), this one is softer and less actionable.

## What's working

- **Delta brand recognition is instant** — logo, blue palette, and clean layout are unmistakably on-brand from the first pixel.
- **Uber co-brand module is a real-world hook** — Uber is a daily-life brand; connecting miles to rides is a concrete earning path Lena can use today.
- **Cadence is respectful** — 49-day gap since the last send avoids fatigue and makes this feel like a meaningful touchpoint, not noise.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout holds, images load, no broken containers or overlapping text.
- **Footer social icons and legal** are present and complete, covering compliance without eating into the content zone.

## What's weak

- **No concrete number in the hero** — "Membership" as a hero headline is a missed opportunity; a specific earning rate or bonus offer would give the eye somewhere to land.
- **Multiple modules with no clear hierarchy** — at least three distinct earning categories compete for attention; the email lacks a single dominant CTA path.
- **No personalization visible** — "Lena" doesn't appear in the body (at least not above the fold); the May 16 welcome used her name, but this resets to generic.
- **No urgency mechanism** — nothing time-bounded is visible; without a deadline, there's no reason to click now vs. never.
- **Extremely long fine print** — the disclaimer block at the bottom is visually overwhelming and signals friction to a casual scanner.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific number to the hero** — "Earn 3X Miles on Every Uber Ride" or "Unlock 1,000 Bonus Miles This Month" turns the hero from brand communication into an actionable offer. This is the single change that lifts both open-rate perception and CTR.
- 2. **Pick one earning category as the lead** — the Uber module should be the star; consolidate other categories into a secondary "also earn with" grid below. One story per email.
- 3. **Add a deadline or limited window** — even "earn bonus miles on Uber through August 15" creates the click-now impulse this email currently lacks.
- 4. **Reintroduce personalization** — opening with "Lena, you've earned X miles so far — here's how to earn more" would re-anchor the member relationship and make the content feel tailored.
- 5. **Trim the fine print block** — link to a dedicated terms page rather than rendering 500+ words of legal in-line; it damages perceived value and trust below the fold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn miles on your next Uber ride, Lena`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 ways to earn more SkyMiles® this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your membership unlocks miles on rides, dining, and more — here's how to start`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Uber, hotels, dining — every day is a chance to earn`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a well-structured ESP production template (Delta global master, last updated 2025-06) with one confirmed broken URL, six images missing alt text, one mixed-content image, and compliance header gaps that conflict with Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements. The malformed document structure (duplicate `<html>` tag, misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>`) is a latent rendering risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken link (404)**
QA detected: `https://www.hteumeuleu.com/2021/fixing-gmail-dark-mode-css-blend-modes/--`
The trailing `--` is the HTML comment-close sequence leaking into the URL string — the reference URL ends at `/` but the parser consumed `--` as part of the href. This is a developer comment citation, not a subscriber-facing link, but it registers as a hard 404 and will drop the pass rate. Fix: move the reference URL outside the `<a>` tag or into a code comment that cannot be parsed as a link.

**WARN — 27 tracking links unverified**
All click-redirect URLs (domain: `o.delta.com` or similar) were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination validity and UTM parameter correctness cannot be confirmed without unwrapping the redirects. (See Section 6.)

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed document structure**
Two `<html>` opening tags are present — one before `<head>` and one after. The `<!DOCTYPE html>` declaration appears after a populated `<div>` block (the lookup scripts wrapper), violating the HTML specification. Browsers and email clients are lenient, but Outlook's Word rendering engine can misinterpret a misplaced DOCTYPE and trigger quirks mode.

**Mixed-content image (HTTP, not HTTPS)**
`3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` is served over `http://`:
```
src: http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png
```
Modern mail clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) block mixed-content images. This image will be broken for the majority of recipients. Change the scheme to `https://`.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header not detected**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Since February 2024, Google and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Delta is unambiguously in scope. If the header is present on the actual SMTP envelope but stripped by the relay, this is a relay configuration issue. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance defect.

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe-Post header not detected (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Google's 2024 bulk-sender policy. Absence means Gmail will surface a more friction-heavy unsubscribe path, increasing spam complaints.

**WARN — Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `o.delta.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; it should have its own DKIM key aligned to `delta.com`. Cannot confirm without the full header.
