# For A Limited Time: Earn Up To 90K Bonus Miles With This Offer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a Delta SkyMiles American Express card acquisition email — a logical next step in Lena's onboarding sequence. The May 16 welcome, May 17 miles nudge, and May 30 benefits overview all built familiarity with the program; this is the monetization play. The hero does its primary job: a stunning Mediterranean coastal image pairs with a gold card and a large "90,000" that reads instantly. Delta knows what they're selling and shows it clearly.
- The execution is solid but the email's biggest liability is visible and damning: the legal disclaimer block at the bottom dwarfs the actual offer content. It runs longer than the entire marketing body of the email. Any subscriber who scrolls past the CTA is immediately confronted with a wall of dense legalese that erodes the aspirational frame the hero worked hard to build. That asymmetry — gorgeous Italy on top, contract terms on the bottom — undercuts the trust the brand has been building over three weeks.
- The "Up To" hedge in the subject combined with the fine print mass makes the 90K promise feel conditional before the recipient even opens. It's still a compelling offer, but it's dressed in enough asterisks that a skeptical reader will discount it.

## What's working

- **90,000 is the hero number and it lands immediately** — the oversized typographic treatment against the coastal backdrop makes the offer scannable in under a second.
- **The credit card visual is well-placed** — the gold card appears naturally against the travel imagery, connecting the aspirational (the trip) to the vehicle (the card).
- **Aspirational imagery is on-brand** — Mediterranean cliffs are exactly what a SkyMiles email should look like; the scene sells the dream before any copy fires.
- **Benefit icons break up the mid-section cleanly** — the three-column structure with the "15" accelerator and supporting icons makes the card's day-to-day value case at a glance.
- **The sequence timing is correct** — four weeks in, having primed Lena on the program's value, this is the right moment to introduce the card product.

## What's weak

- **The fine print wall is enormous** — it visually competes with the marketing content and occupies what looks like 35–40% of the total email height; no other element does more damage to open trust.
- **"For A Limited Time" without a concrete deadline** — "limited time" in the subject and body with no expiration date reads as perpetual urgency, which subscribers have learned to ignore.
- **"Up To 90K" is doing quiet damage** — "up to" is hedge language; the subject line plants doubt before a click happens. The actual threshold miles should be the lead.
- **No personalization** — four emails into a named-member onboarding sequence, "Lena" doesn't appear in the subject or visible hero copy; the May 30 send at least had her name in the subject.
- **Second CTA competes with the first** — having two separate blue buttons with slightly different copy creates a decision fork where one clean CTA would convert better.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the double CTA — make one button, "Apply Now," the sole action** and move it below the consolidated benefit section. The apply flow is the only conversion that matters here.
- 2. **Add a real deadline** — "Offer ends June 30" is more credible and more clickable than "For A Limited Time." If it is actually limited, prove it.
- 3. **Replace "Up To 90K" with the exact earn structure** — e.g., "Earn 60K miles after $3K spend + 30K more at 6 months" converts better than hedged top-line numbers because it shows the path.
- 4. **Inject Lena's name into the hero** — "Lena, your next trip could be on us" or similar costs nothing and uses the personalization the onboarding sequence established.
- 5. **Collapse the fine print** — Move legal text behind a "Terms & Conditions" accordion or link; what's visible should be the minimum disclosure required, not the full card agreement.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lena — 90K Miles, Apply By June 30`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your SkyMiles can take you to Italy. Here's how.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 60K miles at $3K spend, then 30K more — here's the full path.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited-time offer for SkyMiles members. See the card that gets you there.`
