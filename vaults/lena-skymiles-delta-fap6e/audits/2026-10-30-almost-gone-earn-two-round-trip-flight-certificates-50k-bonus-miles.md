# Almost Gone: Earn Two Round-Trip Flight Certificates + 50K Bonus Miles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh pass at Delta's two-certificate, 50K-mile Amex pitch to Lena. The only new element is a real countdown timer (about 6 days left) and a "LAST CHANCE" banner, with the offer ending November 4, 2026.
- The render is clean. The timer sits above the beach hero with the card art. The headline "EARN TWO ROUND-TRIP FLIGHTS AND 50,000 BONUS MILES" sits on a navy block, and a single red "VIEW DETAILS" button follows. The four destination tiles (San Juan, St. Thomas, Los Angeles, New York) add a second, more tangible hook, and the "VIEW OFFER" button below them gives a second way to click.
- The weaknesses are fatigue and fine print. Subscribers have seen "limited time" six times, so the new deadline only works if it feels real. The email also ends in a very long wall of terms. The $10,000 spend in 6 months is only a short line under the CTA. The email works, but it is the same creative with a countdown added.

## What's working

- The countdown timer at the top is the first element the eye hits and gives the urgency a visual anchor.
- The "LAST CHANCE" banner and "Offer ends November 4, 2026" give a concrete deadline that matches the subject.
- The hero headline is big and simple: two flights plus 50,000 bonus miles.
- The single red "VIEW DETAILS" button contrasts well and is easy to find.
- The destination tiles (San Juan, St. Thomas, Los Angeles, New York) make the certificates feel usable. The "SkyMiles® Member" tag in the header signals the email is meant for Lena.

## What's weak

- This is the seventh near-identical send in about two months, so "Almost Gone" has little credibility.
- The $10,000-in-6-months spend requirement is buried in small body text under the CTA instead of near the headline.
- The terms block is enormous, dense and tiny. It makes the email feel long and legalistic, and the headline offer feels less honest next to it.
- The destination tiles are labels only. They show no prices, no sample routes and no detail on the "no blackout dates" claim.
- The subject is about 70 characters and will truncate on mobile, so the deadline and the 50K miles may get cut off.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the spend requirement ("after $10,000 in purchases in 6 months") in the hero copy, so the offer reads as honest and Lena can self-qualify before clicking. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Lead with the deadline and put the personalization in the subject. Lena has seen the offer six times, so the date is the only news.
- 3. Trim or collapse the terms block, or link out to it, so the email ends on the CTA and not on legalese.
- 4. Add one concrete use case to the destination tiles, such as "Round trip to San Juan from [home airport]". Vary the creative from the six earlier sends.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The template carries structural HTML defects (a doubled `<html>` element, a `DOCTYPE` that isn't first, and block elements inside `<head>`). Automated QA found one image with no alt text, one `http://` image, and no captured compliance or authentication headers. The source was truncated, so the footer, unsubscribe link, and body links were not available for inspection.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **28 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains, and expiry are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Insecure image host:** `http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` loads over plain HTTP. Clients can block it or show mixed-content warnings, and the request can be tampered with in transit.
- No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` (the same image as the HTTP one above) has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will read out the filename, and nothing displays when images are blocked.
- **Duplicate `<html>`:** `<html xmlns=… lang="en" dir="ltr"> <html>` opens two root elements. The second is invalid markup that parsers have to correct.
- **`DOCTYPE` not first:** `<!DOCTYPE html>` comes after comments and a `<div style="font-size:0px; line-height:1px;">` block. Content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some clients.
- **`<div>` inside `<head>`:** the `<!-- BEGIN CSS BLOCK -->` section wraps `<style>` tags in a `<div>`. This is invalid, and browser-based clients may move or close `<head>` early, which can drop the styles that follow.
- **Non-standard viewport syntax:** `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; user-scalable=yes"` uses semicolons where the spec calls for commas. Most clients tolerate it, but it is not guaranteed.
- **Global `img { display:block }`** and `.dark-img { display:none !important }` rely on dark-mode toggling that is not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm the dark-mode swap works.
- **Positives:** `lang="en"` is set, the Outlook `DontUseAdvancedTypography` and `PixelsPerInch` blocks are present, and `color-scheme` meta tags are declared.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `${…}`) appear in the visible source.
- **Template scaffolding is exposed in the delivered HTML**, including `<!-- DRAG IN "78329_TMPL_CSS_Custom_Fonts_shared" TO ADD WHITNEY & LATO FONTS -->`, `<!-- SET LOOKUP SCRIPTS -->`, `<!-- DATE AUTOMATION SCRIPT -->`, `<!-- OMNITURE TAGGING -->`, and the version changelog comments. These are empty placeholders and harmless to render, but they leak internal asset IDs and add payload weight. The two empty scaffolding `<div>`s add noise as well.
- The `<title>` is the generic "Delta Air Lines", not the subject line or a personalized value. This is minor.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found.** QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly stripping it, so it is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe is unverified, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status could not be assessed. The From domain `o.delta.com` and the image domain `image.o.delta.com` are aligned subdomains, which is a good sign but not proof of authentication.
- **Physical address and in-body unsubscribe link:** not verifiable because the HTML was truncated before the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 28 links were routed through tracking redirects and skipped, and the truncated source doesn't show the final URLs. No UTM parameters or landing-page mismatches could be confirmed. The `OMNITURE TAGGING` comment suggests tracking is handled by the redirect layer, but that is an inference.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change the image `src` to `https://` and add a descriptive `alt` (or `alt=""` if the image is decorative) for `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png`.
2. Remove the nested `<html>`, move `<!DOCTYPE html>` to the first line, and move the `<style>` blocks out of the `<div>` and directly into `<head>`.
3. Change the viewport meta to comma-separated values: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.
4. Strip authoring and template comments (font-asset IDs, "DRAG IN…") in the ESP's production render.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on the original message, not the relayed copy, and check SPF/DKIM/DMARC from the raw headers.
6. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Then resolve a sample of the 28 tracking links to confirm final URLs, UTM parameters, and landing-page alignment.
