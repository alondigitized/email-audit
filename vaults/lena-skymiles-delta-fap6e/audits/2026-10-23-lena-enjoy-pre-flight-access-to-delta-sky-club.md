# Lena, Enjoy Pre-Flight Access To Delta Sky Club®​
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- Delta sends Lena another acquisition pitch for the Amex SkyMiles Reserve card, this time under a Sky Club banner. The subject sells lounge access. The hero follows with "Unlock Sky Club Benefits, up to $950 in annual value" and a View Details button. Directly under that sits the same "Earn two round-trip flights + 50,000 bonus miles" block from the Oct 8 and Oct 16 sends. A "Say Hello To More Benefits" grid follows, with 15% off award travel, upgrade priority and a plus-one benefit. A "View All Benefits" button comes next, then a very long legal block.
- The strongest part is the angle. Sky Club access is a tangible, aspirational perk, and the $950 figure gives the hero a number to anchor on. The weakness is that this is the same card pitch Lena has now seen about seven times since Aug 28. It also arrives two days after the Oct 21 Starbucks/eCredits send and a week after Oct 16, so it feels like a repackage of the old pitch. The email has two competing headline offers (lounge value vs. flights plus miles) and at least three buttons, so the eye has no single place to land.
- My read is that this is competent but fatiguing. The Sky Club framing is fresher than another "first time ever" push, but the body undercuts it by falling back on the old certificate offer.

## What's working

- The Sky Club angle is a real change from the Aug–Oct certificate subjects. It is the first send in this run that sells lounges rather than flights.
- The subject names Lena and stays under 50 characters.
- The "up to $950 in annual value" hero gives a concrete figure.
- The "Say Hello To More Benefits" grid breaks the perks into short, scannable cards.
- The Delta branding is clean, and the render shows no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The hero offer ($950 lounge value) and the second offer (two flights plus 50K miles) compete for attention. The second reads as a leftover from earlier sends.
- Three or more buttons ("View Details", "Take a Look", "View All Benefits") all do roughly the same job. None of the labels names what you get.
- "Up to $950" is vague, and the fine print under it hedges the claim further.
- The legal and disclosure block runs for roughly half the email's total height. It buries the footer and makes the render feel heavy.
- The cadence is poor. This is the third Delta send in eight days (Oct 16, Oct 21, Oct 23), and the card pitch is the same one Lena has seen since late August.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one offer. Lead with Sky Club access and drop the certificates and miles block, or reverse the order and let Sky Club be the supporting perk. That change would lift the email more than any other.
- 2. Use one primary button with specific copy, such as "See Sky Club Access". Make the others text links.
- 3. Replace "up to $950" with a breakdown Lena can verify, for example "Sky Club access + 15% off award travel + upgrade priority".
- 4. Move the long disclosures to a linked page, or collapse them behind a short "Terms apply" line.
- 5. Space the card sends out. Skip a week after a non-card send like the Oct 21 Starbucks email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Skip the gate crowd: Sky Club access, Lena`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lounge access, 15% off award travel and upgrade priority, all on one card.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two Comfort certificates + 50K miles when you spend $10K in 6 months.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Delta's global-template email has structural HTML problems (duplicate `<html>`, content before the DOCTYPE) and one insecure, alt-less image. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the AgentMail relay not exposing headers, so they aren't confirmed defects. The HTML was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and link-level checks couldn't be made from source.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **49 tracking links were skipped** by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Their destinations, redirect chains and expiry are unverified.
- **Insecure image:** `http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` loads over HTTP and may be blocked or flagged as mixed content. Serve it over `https://`.
- No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** the same `3f473969-…png` has no `alt` attribute. Add a descriptive `alt`, or `alt=""` if it's decorative. Delta's own comment (06/02/2025) says accessibility enhancements were added, yet this image slipped through.
- **Invalid document structure** (visible in the source):
  - A `<div style="font-size:0px; line-height:1px;">` and several comments precede `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some clients.
  - There are two opening tags, `<html xmlns=… lang="en" dir="ltr">` followed by a bare `<html>`.
  - A `<div>` containing `<style>` blocks sits inside `<head>` (`<!-- BEGIN CSS BLOCK -->`), which is invalid and may be stripped or reparented by parsers.
- **Malformed viewport meta:** `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; user-scalable=yes"` uses semicolons instead of commas. Some clients ignore it.
- **Leftover template comments** remain in the shipped HTML: "DRAG IN '78329_TMPL_CSS_Custom_Fonts_shared'…", "ADD EMAIL-SPECIFIC SCRIPTS / CODE HERE", and the "SET LOOKUP SCRIPTS" and "OMNITURE TAGGING" placeholders. They add weight and expose internal ESP block names. Whether the font block was actually included can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- Positives: `lang="en"`, `dir="ltr"`, `color-scheme: light` and the Outlook `DontUseAdvancedTypography` setting are present.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The subject token resolved correctly ("Lena, Enjoy Pre-Flight Access…").
- **Subject anomaly:** the subject ends in `®` followed by a zero-width character (`Sky Club®​`, likely U+200B). It's invisible, but it can cause string-match and dedupe quirks and trips some spam heuristics.
- No unresolved merge tokens were visible in the truncated source. The rest of the body is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found** and **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA note says the relay may not capture them. This is unconfirmed, and one-click support should be verified in the original message headers.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found,** so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown. The relay is the expected cause. The sender is `DeltaAirLines@o.delta.com`, a dedicated sending subdomain.
- The footer (physical address, in-body unsubscribe link) was not in the truncated HTML, so CAN-SPAM body requirements are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters or landing URLs were visible. Links are wrapped in tracking redirects, and 49 were skipped by the probe.
- Landing-page alignment is unverified because the redirect targets were never resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Change the image `src` to `https://` and add `alt` to `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png`.
2. Fix the document skeleton: put `<!DOCTYPE html>` first, keep a single `<html>`, and move the `<div>`/`<style>` wrapper out of `<head>`.
3. Change the viewport meta to comma-separated values.
4. Strip the ESP and template comments from the production render.
5. Remove the trailing zero-width character from the subject.
6. Re-run QA against raw `.eml` headers to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Also resolve the 49 tracked links to check destinations and UTMs.
