# Lena, Enjoy 70+ Lounges Worldwide with the Delta SkyMiles® Reserve Card​
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a Reserve Amex acquisition send. It has a real hook, but it is the weakest Reserve email Delta has sent Lena this cycle. The subject sells lounge access ("70+ Lounges Worldwide"). The hero then leads with "Unlock Sky Club Benefits, up to $950 in annual value" and a **View Details** button. Below that, a second banner pushes "Earn Two Round-Trip Flights + 50,000 Bonus Miles" with a **Take a Look** button. That is two offers and two CTAs competing in one email. Since the September 9 Sky Club send ("Thinking Lounge Before Take Off?"), Delta has largely repeated the same Reserve pitch.
- Cadence is the bigger problem. Lena got the "First Time Ever" certificate push on August 28, September 4, September 18 and September 25. This send folds that same certificate and 50K-mile stack into a lounge email, so it reads as the fifth variation on one offer. A real subscriber would see the repetition before anything else.
- My read is that the render is clean, the offer is concrete, and the dollar-value anchor is strong. But the email splits focus, and about 80% of its length is legal and terms copy. This is a 6–7 send that is competent but tired.
- Caveat: I reviewed a 680×10,395 image shown at about 131px wide. I could read the headline blocks, CTA labels and section headings, but not the fine print or the exact preheader.

## What's working

- The "up to $950 in annual value" headline gives a concrete dollar anchor for the lounge pitch.
- Both CTA buttons render clearly and are easy to spot, with no broken images or overlapping text.
- The "Say Hello To More Benefits" tile row (15% off award travel, upgrade priority, a Plus One) breaks up the benefits into scannable pieces.
- The deadline on the certificate banner (a November date) adds some time-bound pressure.
- Delta branding is consistent and instantly recognizable, and the subject uses Lena's name.

## What's weak

- The subject promises lounges. The hero leads with Sky Club, and the certificates and 50K miles then take over the middle of the email, so there is no single message.
- Both CTAs are generic ("View Details", "Take a Look") and say nothing about the card or the benefit.
- The very long terms and disclosure block runs for most of the email. It buries the offer and makes the whole thing feel heavy.
- This is the fifth near-identical Reserve push in about five weeks, with no new creative angle.
- The 70+ lounges claim is never shown visually. There is no map, list or lounge photo near the top, so the subject's main promise is unsupported.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one offer.** Make this a pure lounge email, and either drop the certificate banner or save it for a different send. The lounge angle is new relative to the August–September certificate sends.
- 2. Put a lounge visual, a count or a short list of airports directly under the hero so it delivers on "70+ lounges".
- 3. Rewrite the CTAs as verb plus noun: "See Sky Club Access" and "Compare Lounge Perks".
- 4. Move the terms into a collapsed "Offer details" link and keep only the required APR and fee line near the CTA.
- 5. Pause the Reserve cadence for at least two weeks, or switch to an educational, non-acquisition send, as with the July 24 SkyMiles membership email.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on Delta's global template. The truncated source and the QA data show one image with no alt text, one image served over plain `http://`, and malformed document structure in the head. The compliance and authentication warnings look like relay capture gaps, not confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- Because the source is truncated, I can't confirm whether any link is broken or malformed.
- No other issues found.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the filename or skip the image. With images off, it leaves a blank gap.
- **Non-HTTPS image:** the same asset loads from `http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png`. Clients that proxy or block mixed content may not render it. The same host should serve it over `https://`.
- **Malformed document structure:**
  - A `<div style="font-size:0px; line-height:1px;">` containing the lookup scripts sits before `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Content ahead of the doctype can push some clients into quirks mode.
  - A second, nested `<html>` tag follows `<html xmlns=... lang="en" dir="ltr">`.
  - A `<div>` wraps the `<style>` blocks inside `<head>` (the `<!-- BEGIN CSS BLOCK -->` section). `<div>` is invalid in `<head>`, and parsers may close the head early and treat the styles as body content.
- **Viewport meta:** `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; user-scalable=yes"` uses semicolons instead of the standard commas. Most clients tolerate it, but it is non-standard.
- **Leftover template comments:** the source still contains authoring notes such as `<!-- DRAG IN "78329_TMPL_CSS_Custom_Fonts_shared" ... -->` and `<!-- ADD EMAIL-SPECIFIC SCRIPTS / CODE HERE -->`. They are harmless but add weight and expose internal template names.
- **Accessibility positives:** the Outlook `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` setting is present, and `lang="en"` is set.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The subject renders correctly ("Lena, Enjoy 70+ Lounges…"), with no unresolved tokens.
- The subject ends with a zero-width or invisible character after "Card" (`Card​`), which looks like a stray character. It is harmless in most clients, but it is worth trimming.
- No unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `%%[ ]%%` tokens appear in the visible portion of the source. The rest was truncated, so I can't confirm the body.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are flagged missing. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. Check the raw message at the original receiving inbox before treating it as a sender defect.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain `o.delta.com` and the image host `image.o.delta.com` are consistent, which is a mild positive.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address:** these fall outside the truncated source, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The 49 skipped tracking links hide the final landing URLs and any UTM parameters. Continuity can't be verified from the available data.
- The template references Omniture tagging in the script block. No click-through destination or campaign parameters are visible in the truncated source.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add a descriptive `alt` to `3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png`. Use `alt=""` if it is purely decorative.
2. Serve that image over `https://image.o.delta.com/...`, and audit the other assets on that host for `http://` sources.
3. Fix the document structure:
   - Move the lookup-script `<div>` out of the pre-doctype area.
   - Remove the duplicate `<html>` tag.
   - Move the `<div>` wrapper out of `<head>`, so only `<style>` and `<meta>` elements remain there.
4. Strip authoring and template comments before send.
5. Check the original message headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. If they are present there, add a relay-aware exception to the QA check.
6. Resolve a sample of the skipped tracking links, or follow the redirect chain with a non-probing method. This confirms the landing pages and UTM parameters.
7. Change the viewport meta to the comma-separated form: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.
