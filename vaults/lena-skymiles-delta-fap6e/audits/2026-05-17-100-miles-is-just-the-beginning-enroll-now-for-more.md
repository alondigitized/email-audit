# 100 Miles Is Just The Beginning, Enroll Now For More
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a fast follow to yesterday's SkyMiles welcome email — sent one day later, designed to convert a freshly enrolled Lena from passive member to active earner. The hero does its job: "SECURE YOUR 100 MILES" connects directly to the subject line and frames the ask as protecting something she already has, rather than a cold pitch. The milestone ladder (500 → 1,000 → 2,000) is a reasonable structural choice for a new-member onboarding email.
- What kills it is the back half. Roughly half the email scroll is a dense wall of legal fine print — unbroken, small-type, visually overwhelming. It doesn't just bury the CTA; it actively signals that the "offer" comes with enough conditions to require a legal department. For a consumer trying to understand what she's actually enrolling in, this is a trust deflator.
- The secondary ask — "Tell us about Your Experience as a New SkyMiles Member" — is also a mistake at this stage. She's 24 hours in. She has no experience to report. It dilutes the enrollment CTA and adds friction with no benefit.

## What's working

- **Hero copy earns its place.** "SECURE YOUR 100 MILES" is possessive framing — makes the ask feel like protecting an asset rather than a sales pitch.
- **Subject-to-hero continuity.** "100 Miles Is Just The Beginning" in the subject connects cleanly to what the hero is saying; no bait-and-switch.
- **Delta brand recognition.** Sender is immediately identifiable; no credibility lift needed.
- **Milestone ladder is instructive.** The 500/1,000/2,000 tier structure gives a new member a quick mental model of how earning works.

## What's weak

- **Fine print dominates the scroll.** Terms and conditions appear to account for at least 50% of the email body — a visual signal that the offer is complicated or restrictive.
- **Survey ask is premature.** Asking a 24-hour member to review their experience competes with the enrollment CTA and has zero conversion logic.
- **Subject line is two commands stitched together.** "100 Miles Is Just The Beginning, Enroll Now For More" reads like two separate thoughts separated by a comma — weakens both hooks.
- **"More" is vague as a destination.** The subject promises "more" without naming what — more miles, more status, more benefits?
- **No urgency or deadline.** "Enroll Now" is imperative but there's no time-bound reason to act today vs. next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the fine print above the fold; collapse or footnote it.** Move terms to a linked disclosure page or accordion. The legal wall is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Drop the survey ask entirely.** It splits attention from the enrollment CTA. Bring it back in a 30-day email when she actually has experience.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject as one tight idea.** Two commands joined by comma feel breathless and unfocused.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 100 Miles Are Waiting — Here's How To Multiply Them`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New member perk: turn 100 miles into 2,000`
- 4. **Add a specific deadline to the enrollment window.** Even "offer valid through May 31" would lift click urgency materially.
- 5. **Name what she's enrolling in.** The CTA should say exactly what happens at the other end — "Start Earning With SkyMiles Shopping" beats "Enroll Now."
- **Preheader Alt A:** `You've got 100 miles — here's how to reach 2,000 faster`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your enrollment window is open. See how far 100 miles can go.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed HTML structure**
The document contains two `<html>` opening tags and a `<!DOCTYPE html>` declaration placed mid-body (after content `<div>` elements). Compliant parsers will ignore the second declaration; permissive parsers may behave unpredictably in Outlook or older Android clients.


**Empty script placeholder comments**

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not detected. This header is required by Google and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024) for senders above 5,000/day. Absence will suppress the one-click unsubscribe prompt in Gmail and Yahoo Mail UIs, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
Without the `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` header, one-click unsubscribe (as required by Google/Yahoo policy) is not supported regardless of the `List-Unsubscribe` header value.
