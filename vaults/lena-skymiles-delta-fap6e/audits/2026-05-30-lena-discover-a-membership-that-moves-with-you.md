# Lena, Discover A Membership That Moves With You
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the third email in Delta's SkyMiles onboarding sequence — the welcome landed May 16, the miles enrollment nudge followed May 17, and now this benefits overview drops 13 days later. The cadence is reasonable and the arc is coherent: Delta is correctly sequencing Lena from "freshly enrolled" to "engaged member" by surfacing what the program actually unlocks beyond flights.
- The execution is solid but safe. The "MEMBER BENEFITS" hero is clean and on-brand, the layout renders without issue, and the three content tiles below the fold carry lifestyle imagery that fits the travel context. What this email does not do is create pull. There is no specific offer, no deadline, and no single CTA that makes Lena feel she is leaving something on the table if she closes the tab.
- This email educates. It does not convert. For a new member two weeks into her SkyMiles relationship, that is a missed window — Delta had Lena's attention and gave her a brochure instead of a reason to act. One concrete perk (a partner miles bonus, a first-upgrade path, a welcome reward) anchored in the hero would have transformed this from catalog to conversion.

## What's working

- **MEMBER BENEFITS hero is unambiguous** — the headline instantly telegraphs the email's purpose and connects to Lena's recent enrollment action.
- **Delta brand recognition is immediate** — the blue palette, mark, and clean typography carry trust from the first pixel.
- **Onboarding arc is coherent** — this send doesn't repeat the welcome or the miles enrollment pitch; it advances the story logically to "here's what you now have access to."
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, imagery loads, no overlapping elements or placeholder text visible anywhere in the render.
- **Name in subject** — "Lena" signals this isn't a mass blast, even if the body doesn't carry that personalization through.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer in the hero** — "MEMBER BENEFITS" is a category, not a hook; a specific perk would give Lena something to evaluate rather than something to browse.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism** — nothing in the visible render suggests this offer expires or that waiting has a cost.
- **CTA is fragmented across content tiles** — three separate content blocks dilute attention; there is no single dominant action to take.
- **Subject is generic aspirational filler** — "Discover A Membership That Moves With You" could belong to any loyalty program on earth; it promises nothing specific.
- **Dense legal block at the bottom** — the wall of disclaimer text is unavoidably prominent and makes the email feel heavier than a benefits-overview send warrants.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with one specific, concrete perk.** Swap "MEMBER BENEFITS" for a single benefit Lena can act on — e.g., "Your SkyMiles gets you free upgrades, 2x miles on hotels, and more." Give her something tangible to evaluate, not a category to explore.
- 2. **Consolidate to one primary CTA.** Pick the highest-value benefit, build the email around it, and put one focused button above the fold. Move secondary benefits below.
- 3. **Add a lightweight deadline.** Even "Activate your first partner perk before June 30" turns this from an awareness email into a conversion email.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to name a specific benefit.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lena, here's what your SkyMiles actually unlocks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your member perks are live — here's what to claim first`
- 5. **Write a preheader that teases the most specific benefit in the body.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free upgrades, partner deals, and bonus miles — all yours as a member.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `You're in. Here's what to redeem this month.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] Pre-DOCTYPE content injection**
Content (a `<div>`, CSS `<style>` blocks, and script comments) appears before the `<!DOCTYPE html>` declaration in the rendered source. This forces browsers and some email clients into quirks mode, which can break table layouts and CSS specificity. The content block order in the raw source is:

```
[script/div content]
[CSS blocks]
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html ...>
```

This is a template-level structural bug, not copy-specific.

**[FAIL] Duplicate `<html>` tag**
The source contains two `<html>` opening tags — one inside the VML namespace declaration block, one bare. Most clients silently ignore the second, but it is invalid markup.

**[WARN] One image served over HTTP**
```
src: http://image.o.delta.com/lib/fe5815707c6201787313/m/10/3f473969-95d8-43bc-8d81-7bdb8c2d4dd8.png
```
Mixed-content HTTP on a predominantly HTTPS page. Modern clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) will block this image silently. Should be `https://`.

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 5 images**
- `3f473969-...png` — content image, no `alt` attribute
- `2x2_trans_spacer.png` — appears 4 times, no `alt=""` (spacers require explicit empty alt, not omitted alt)

Screen readers will announce file names or skip unpredictably.

**[INFO] Dark mode implemented**
`color-scheme: light dark` and `.dark-img` CSS toggle class are present. No issues detected.

**[INFO] `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible">` value**
`content="IE=9; IE=8; IE=7; IE=EDGE"` — semicolon-separated values in a single `content` attribute are non-standard; only the last token (`IE=EDGE`) is honored. No functional impact on modern clients, but the legacy values are dead weight.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
The QA relay did not capture this header. If truly missing, Gmail and Apple Mail will suppress the native one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; a footer link satisfies the letter of the law, but missing this header is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024) depends on this header being present alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Its absence means Delta's email does not meet current bulk-sender requirements for Gmail/Yahoo. This is a significant compliance gap.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unknown from this relay snapshot. Cannot confirm deliverability posture. This is a relay capture gap, not necessarily a sending-side failure — but it should be verified against raw headers from a delivered sample.
