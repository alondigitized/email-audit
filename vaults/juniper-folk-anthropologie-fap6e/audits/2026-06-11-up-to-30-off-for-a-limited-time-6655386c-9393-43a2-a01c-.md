# Up to 30% OFF for a limited time!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Anthropologie's launch email for a "Up to 30% off Summer Faves" promotional period, sent June 11 — the day after the "Extra 50% off sale!" blast (June 10, 8/10). The hero is clean and on-brand: the 30% off callout lands immediately, the category nav sits right below it, and the editorial sub-sections (Tassel Kids, Jetty Bandana, Beach Bag Season, Going Analog) give the email enough content to scroll through. No render bugs visible.
- The problem is structural: this email is cannibalizing itself. Yesterday's email headlined 50% off; today's hero leads with 30% off — but there's still an "EXTRA 50% OFF SALE" banner inside the body. For the subscriber who got both sends, the instinct is "wait, did the deal get worse?" before they realize these are two different promotional tiers co-existing. The email never clearly explains the two-offer architecture.
- The formula also runs deep: this is essentially a summer promotional blast identical in structure to what scores 7-8 every time. It works but it doesn't surprise. Compared to the tight single-product focus of the June 9 maeve logo tank email (8/10), this is a blunt instrument — broad summer coverage with no hook strong enough to feel essential.

## What's working

- Hero hierarchy is clear — "UP TO 30% OFF" lands above the fold with a direct "SHOP NOW" button, no hunting required.
- Category nav (Dresses, Swim, Bottoms, Beauty, Shoes, Accessories) immediately below the hero is a smart shortcut for intent-driven openers.
- Editorial sub-sections (Beach Bag Season, Going Analog, Summer Activities) add browsability and make the email feel curated rather than purely transactional.
- Summer framing is seasonally on-point for a June 11 send.
- The Anthropologie aesthetic — soft palette, editorial photography — is consistent and trusted.

## What's weak

- Two competing discount levels (30% off in the hero, 50% off in the body banner) send mixed signals to anyone who got yesterday's send; the email never explains the two-tier structure.
- "For a limited time" is soft urgency — no date, no countdown, nothing that creates a real reason to act now vs. tomorrow.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is maximally generic; it points to a broad summer sale, not any specific editorial section or product.
- No loyalty or member pricing visible — a missed signal for a brand with a rewards program.
- Cadence is aggressive: this is the second Anthropologie promotional email in 24 hours. A subscriber who got yesterday's 50% off send may tune this out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Separate the two offers or kill one from the hero.** If 30% off summer faves and 50% off sale items are running simultaneously, name both explicitly: "Summer faves: 30% off. Sale: extra 50% off." One clear sentence. Don't bury the 50% off banner below the fold — it's actually the bigger deal for bargain-hunters.
- 2. **Add a real deadline.** "Through June 15" or "Ends Sunday" turns "limited time" into actual urgency. The June 17 "ENDS TONIGHT" send proves the deadline creative works — front-load that to the launch email.
- 3. **Link specific editorial sections to their own landing pages.** "Beach Bag Season" and "Going Analog" are compelling editorial hooks; give each its own CTA button rather than funneling everything to SHOP NOW.
- 4. **Space out the cadence.** Back-to-back sale emails on June 10 and 11 train the subscriber to wait for a deeper cut.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer faves: 30% off (+ extra 50% off sale)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off summer. Plus, extra 50% off sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, swim, beauty & more — shop the edit before it ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two ways to save this summer — here's the breakdown.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed MSO conditional CSS** (`<!--[if mso]>` block):
```css
body, table, td { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important; }
```
Two defects on the same line:
1. Double comma produces an empty font-family entry (`Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'`) — invalid CSS, parsers will skip or error on it.
2. `!important` is placed **after the semicolon**, outside the property value — the directive is ignored entirely. Outlook will still render Arial as the first declared font, so visual impact is low, but this is a latent bug that will break if font order is ever changed.

**Duplicate CSS rule** — `[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` (Open Sans) is declared twice: once under the `/* BEHOLDEN */` comment and again under `/* TERRAIN */`. The second declaration wins; the duplication indicates copy-paste drift in the shared template and may cause unexpected overrides if the rules ever diverge.

**`.yfix{}` empty class declared three times** — once per brand button section (UO, FP, Anthro). No functional impact but confirms template is not deduplicated.
