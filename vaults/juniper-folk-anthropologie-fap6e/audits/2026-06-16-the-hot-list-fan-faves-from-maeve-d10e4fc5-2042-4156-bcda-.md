# The Hot List: fan faves from maeve
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is maeve's third standalone send in roughly three weeks — following "Ahoy, maeve here" (May 26) and "kick it in sporty new arrivals" (June 15). The format has shifted from that sporty editorial hero into a curated product-grid dump: a "Hot List" that's meant to telegraph "people love these items, come see." The hero image — a model in a cream oversized cardigan, breezy and summery — is confident and on-brand. But the email below the hero collapses into a dense product grid with no editorial point of view anchoring it, and the social proof hook ("fan faves") goes completely unsubstantiated. There's no review count, no "bestseller" tag, no urgency.
- The bottom third introduces a "NEW SALE STYLES" module, which is a meaningful offer — but burying a discount below a long product scroll is the wrong hierarchy. If there's a sale, lead with it or give it a full section; don't tack it on as an afterthought. As a whole this send is a step backward from the tighter editorial discipline of the June 15 sporty send.

## What's working

- **Hero image is clean and seasonally right.** The cream cardigan on a bright-sky background reads immediately as summer and communicates the maeve aesthetic without overexplaining.
- **The "Hot List" framing is culturally legible.** "Fan faves" is social-vernacular shorthand that lands in a single read.
- **No render bugs visible.** Layout is intact, images load, text doesn't overlap.
- **Sub-brand voice is consistent.** The typography and white-space approach matches the May 26 introduction and June 15 follow-up — maeve is building a recognizable look.

## What's weak

- **"Fan faves" is asserted, never proven.** No star ratings, no "X people love this," no bestseller badge — the social proof hook is a subject-line claim that the body doesn't back up.
- **The product grid is too dense to feel curated.** A "Hot List" implies editorial selectivity; this reads like a full catalog scroll. The blue/white ceramic plate mid-email is visually jarring — it breaks the fashion editorial rhythm without context.
- **"NEW SALE STYLES" is buried below the fold.** The one concrete commercial offer in this send is the last thing a mobile reader would see. That's backwards.
- **No CTA hierarchy.** Every product appears to have a small text link; there's no single primary call-to-action that anchors the email's intent.
- **No urgency anywhere.** The "Hot List" label implies these items are trending — but nothing communicates scarcity, limited sizes, or a deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the sale in the hero or position it as a clear second module.** "Fan faves — now up to X% off" combines the social proof hook with a commercial reason to click. The current structure makes the sale feel like a clearance footnote.
- 2. **Back up "fan faves" with a single social proof signal.** Even "★★★★★ — 847 reviews" beneath one hero product would validate the premise. The claim without evidence is just marketing copy.
- 3. **Edit the grid ruthlessly.** A "Hot List" should have 6–8 items max, not a full catalog scroll. Pull the ceramic plate only if it has a editorial callout (e.g., "obsession of the week").
- 4. **Add one time-bounded element.** "This week's picks" or "selling fast" on two or three flagged items turns a browse into a reason to act now.
- 5. **Sharpen the CTA.** One primary button — "Shop the Hot List" or "See What's Trending" — above the grid, not twelve equal text links.

**CSS syntax error in Outlook conditional block:**

```css
body, table, td { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important; }
```
Two issues on this line: double-comma in the font stack (`Helvetica, ,`) produces an invalid value, and `!important` is placed outside the property declaration (after the semicolon), making it inert. Outlook will silently ignore both and fall back to its default font.

**Duplicate CSS rule:**

```css

```
This selector appears twice in `<style>` (once under "FREE PEOPLE", once under "TERRAIN"). The second declaration wins; the duplicate is dead code but could mask future specificity bugs.

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Result | Evidence |
|-------|--------|----------|
| List-Unsubscribe header | **WARN** | Not captured by AgentMail relay — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) and RFC 2369 require this |
| List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | Absent — one-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (≥5k/day) |
| SPF/DKIM/DMARC (Authentication-Results) | **WARN** | Not present in relay-captured headers — authentication status unverified |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header is the highest-priority compliance gap: Google and Yahoo have enforced one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence can trigger inbox demotion or bulk folder delivery regardless of sender reputation.

Note: all three warnings may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay stripping headers rather than the ESP not setting them. Confirm against raw headers from a seed inbox before treating as an ESP-level failure.
