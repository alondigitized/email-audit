# Ends tonight: Up to 30% OFF getaway, jewelry, home & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This send is functionally a rerun of the July 29 "Up to 30% off Celandine, beauty, home & more" email — same percent-off mechanic, same category-grid template, just two days later with an "ends tonight" urgency badge bolted on. The subject line promises "getaway, jewelry, home" specifics, but the actual hero banner just says generic "SUMMER ESSENTIALS" — nothing in the creative pays off what the subject teased.
- Execution-wise the layout is clean: big yellow "30% OFF" hero, unambiguous "SHOP NOW" button, a tidy six-category grid, then editorial tiles, then a personalized product grid at the bottom. No render bugs, nothing broken. But it's the fourth or fifth iteration of a template this brand has been running non-stop since July 20, and a stray Halloween tile mid-scroll on July 31 makes it feel like the creative team is stretching a template rather than building something intentional for this specific sale.
- Verdict: well-built but low-effort — a competent execution of a mechanic Anthropologie is now clearly on autopilot with.

## What's working

- "Ends tonight" badge top-left creates immediate, credible urgency.
- Hero visual hierarchy is strong — the "30% OFF" is the first and only thing the eye lands on.
- Six-color-coded category grid (Clothing, Dresses, Shoes, Accessories, Beauty, Entertaining & Gifts) gives multiple clear entry points.
- "Handpicked for you" personalized product grid at the bottom adds a relevance signal.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "getaway, jewelry, home" but the hero just says generic "Summer Essentials" — the specific categories teased in the subject aren't reflected anywhere prominent in the creative.
- Near-identical structure and offer to the July 29 send ("Up to 30% off Celandine, beauty, home & more") just two days apart — minimal creative differentiation.
- A Halloween-themed tile appears mid-email on July 31, which reads as out-of-season filler in a summer-sale email.
- "Up to 30%" plus small-print exclusions at the very bottom is a classic soft bait pattern — most items likely don't hit the headline number.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero actually match the subject — either swap the hero copy to reference getaway/jewelry/home items directly, or change the subject to say what the creative actually shows ("Summer Essentials").
- 2. Differentiate this send from July 29 — vary the mechanic (tiered spend threshold, early access, category-specific %) instead of rerunning the same percent-off grid three days later.
- 3. Cut the Halloween tile — premature for a July 31 summer-essentials sale; save it for closer to season.
- 4. Lead with one hero jewelry or travel product shot since the subject specifically name-checks those categories — right now nothing in the visible creative backs that promise up.

# Technical Audit: Anthropologie — "Ends tonight" Promo Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard URBN/Anthropologie multi-brand ESP template (MSO/VML conditionals present) with two confirmed compliance gaps (missing unsubscribe headers) and one unresolved personalization token; core rendering markup appears otherwise standards-compliant.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- Two third-party tracking pixels are present: `login.dotomi.com/profile/visit/px/1_0` (Dotomi/CJ affiliate tracking) and `pxl.mon-trk.com/95bb6f26-9cf5-4254-8a2d-39742e856443/073126_AN-US_SummerEssentialsEndsTonight/003a700000uTzaxAAC` (appears to be a Salesforce Marketing Cloud journey/campaign pixel, ID embedded in path). No issues with presence of these pixels themselves.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `<meta name="viewport" ... user-scalable=0>` disables pinch-to-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) for users who need to zoom.
- Web fonts are loaded via `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?...')` inside a `<style>` block — `@import` is unreliable/stripped in a number of major email clients (notably older Outlook and some webmail sandboxes); fallback stacks (`Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` / `Times New Roman, serif`) are correctly declared, so degradation should be graceful, but the primary fonts (Playfair Display, Nunito Sans, etc.) may not render in-client.
- Custom `@font-face` declarations for Clearface/Brandon Grotesque (`community.urbn.com/static/fonts/...`) are correctly scoped inside `@media screen`, preventing Outlook desktop from attempting to load them — good practice, no issue.
- 5 unique images missing `alt` text (2 appear duplicated in QA output):
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg`
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg`
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`
  - Dotomi tracking pixel (`login.dotomi.com/.../1_0`)
  - mon-trk tracking pixel (`pxl.mon-trk.com/...`)
  
  The three content banner images lacking `alt` text is a real accessibility/deliverability issue (screen readers and image-blocked inboxes will show nothing); the two 1×1 tracking pixels are low-priority for alt text but ideally use `alt=""` to be explicitly marked decorative.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The Dotomi pixel URL has an empty personalization parameter: `dtm_email_hash=` with no value appended. This indicates an unresolved/failed merge token in the tracking pixel — worth confirming whether this is expected (e.g., anonymous fallback) or a broken personalization insert, since an empty hash could affect downstream attribution matching.
- No other merge-token placeholders (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{...}}`) were visible in the provided source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found. Note QA caveat that this may be a capture limitation of the AgentMail relay rather than confirmed absence in the raw send — recommend verifying against raw SMTP headers before treating as confirmed. If genuinely absent, this is a gap against Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements (which mandate a one-click List-Unsubscribe header for senders at this volume), independent of CAN-SPAM's own (looser) requirement for a visible unsubscribe link in the body.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058)**: not found, meaning one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed supported even if a List-Unsubscribe header exists elsewhere.
- **Authentication-Results (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)**: not found in the captured headers; same AgentMail-relay caveat applies — cannot confirm authentication pass/fail status from this data.
- No CAN-SPAM body-level violations (physical address, unsubscribe link presence) could be assessed — not visible in the truncated HTML source provided.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Not assessable: the provided HTML source is truncated within the `<head>`/style block, before any body content or CTA `href` attributes are reached. No UTM parameters or landing-page URLs are visible in the supplied excerpt, and the 38 tracking links noted by QA were skipped rather than resolved to final destinations. Recommend re-running the audit against the full HTML body to validate UTM consistency and landing-page alignment.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Confirm directly against the raw SMTP headers (bypassing the AgentMail relay) whether `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` are actually present — if genuinely absent, add both to meet Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
2. Same direct-header verification for `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) before concluding authentication is misconfigured.
3. Add `alt` text to the three content banner images (`pencilline_1`, `pencilline_2`, `1e313de8-...png`); add `alt=""` to the two tracking pixels to mark them decorative.
4. Investigate the empty `dtm_email_hash=` parameter in the Dotomi pixel to confirm it's not a broken personalization insert.
5. Re-run link/UTM validation against the full (non-truncated) HTML body, since 38 tracking links and all landing-page URLs were unverifiable from the source provided here.
6. Consider whether `user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag is necessary — removing it would resolve the WCAG 1.4.4 zoom-accessibility gap.
