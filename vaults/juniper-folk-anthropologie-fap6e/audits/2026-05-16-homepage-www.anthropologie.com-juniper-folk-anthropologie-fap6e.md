# Homepage snapshot · www.anthropologie.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Oh no. I opened Anthropologie on my phone and got hit with a wall — literally just the logo and "Access is temporarily restricted." No hero, no products, no sale banner, nothing. The brand I know for dreamy linen dresses and candles I definitely don't need just served me a plain-text error page with a bot-detection explanation and an incident ID. It feels less "curated bohemian lifestyle" and more "you are a suspect."

## What's working

- **The logo loads.** At least I know I'm on Anthropologie and not some phishing page. That's genuinely the only positive thing I can pull from this screen.

## What's weak

- **The access restriction wall.** The entire homepage is replaced by "Access is temporarily restricted" — zero product content, zero brand experience.
- **Cold, clinical copy.** "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" followed by a numbered list of bot-detection reasons is aggressive and accusatory language directed at what is supposed to be a welcomed returning shopper.
- **The IP address is exposed in plain text.** Showing `75.140.9.0` to a regular consumer is confusing and slightly alarming — this reads like a system diagnostic screen, not a branded experience.
- **No recovery path.** There's no "Tap here to continue shopping" or "Verify you're human" button. The only escape is "Submit Feedback About Experience," which is a dead end for anyone who just wants to buy a dress.
- **Blank void.** The center third of the screen is completely empty white space — even the error messaging is sparse and offers no reassurance or next step.

## Recommendations

- **Add a branded soft-block screen.** If bot detection fires on a real user's session, show an on-brand interstitial — styled with Anthro's typography, a warm image, and a CAPTCHA or "I'm not a robot" tap. Don't dump them into a bare system error page.
- **Replace the clinical copy with human language.** "We want to make sure it's really you — tap below to continue" is infinitely better than a four-item numbered list of bot-detection reasons.
- **Hide the IP address.** Showing the raw IP to consumers adds zero value and feels like a data privacy overstep for a shopper who just wants boho dresses.
- **Provide a real CTA to retry.** A "Try Again" or "Continue to Anthropologie" button that refreshes or routes back to the homepage gives the user a path forward instead of a dead end.
