# Romantic new arrivals (with an edge)
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- This is editorial-mode Anthropologie, and it's a clean break from the discount-hammer cadence this brand has run for six straight weeks (30% Off Fall Looks 9/17, Ends TONIGHT 9/20, Extra 50% Off run three times through 9/27). No hero discount badge, no urgency banner — just "Romantic new arrivals (with an edge)" and a long, lifestyle-driven scroll through florals, utility pants, ruffles, and cowboy boots. It reads more like Maeve's 9/21 "personality pieces" send than the parent brand's usual playbook.
- The execution is polished — moody product photography, consistent serif script overlays, clear section breaks (jewelry/belts, home/beauty) — but it buries the lede. A small "NEW SALE STYLES" banner doesn't show up until roughly two-thirds of the way down, after five or six full product blocks. Anyone scanning on mobile for an offer will bounce before they ever see it.
- Verdict: gorgeous, on-brand, and completely non-transactional until far too late. Fine as an occasional brand-building send, but risky as a pattern — this brand's list has been trained on 30-50% off hooks, and a cold switch to pure lifestyle copy with no upfront value prop will hurt open/click rates for exactly the segment (deal-seekers) that's been engaging.

## What's working

- Strong, consistent visual identity — soft script typography over lifestyle shots feels premium and cohesive top to bottom.
- Clear category anchors (jewelry, belts, home, beauty) with simple "shop" links make browsing easy once a reader is engaged.
- No render bugs — every image loads, text is legible, layout holds together cleanly across the long scroll.
- Smart top utility bar ("FURNITURE, RUGS & MORE HOME. SHIP FREE") gives at least one concrete hook above the fold.

## What's weak

- No discernible primary CTA — six-plus "shop X" links compete with no single hero action, unlike the brand's usual single-offer emails.
- The one offer ("NEW SALE STYLES") is buried mid-scroll, after multiple full lifestyle sections — most subscribers won't scroll that far.
- Subject promises "romantic... with an edge" but the hero image (utility trousers, boots) doesn't visually deliver the "romantic" half until several screens later.
- Extremely long single-column scroll with no early "jump to sale" link risks high early-exit/low-click behavior on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the sale banner (or at least a "new arrivals + sale styles" combined CTA) into the first screen.** This is the single biggest lift — right now the offer is invisible until deep scroll, and this list has been offer-trained for six weeks straight.
- 2. Pick one hero CTA for the top module ("Shop New Arrivals") and make it visually dominant — right now the eye has no clear first stop.
- 3. Align the hero image with the subject line — lead with a romantic/floral piece, not utility trousers, so subject and hero don't fight each other.
- 4. Add a lightweight urgency or specificity signal somewhere above the fold (even "New this week") since the brand's recent high-scoring sends (8/10 on 9/19) leaned on credible urgency.
