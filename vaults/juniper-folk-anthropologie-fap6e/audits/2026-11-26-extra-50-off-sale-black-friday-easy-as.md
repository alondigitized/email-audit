# EXTRA 50% OFF SALE! Black Friday. Easy as 🥧
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventeenth Anthropologie Black Friday send I've audited in about four days. The creative is the same plaid-edged gift-tag hero as the 11/25 "NOW, EXTRA 50% OFF SALE for Black Friday!" email. It leads with "NOW, EXTRA 50% OFF SALE," then "+ 30% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING ELSE" with three red buttons. This time the subject matches the hero, which was the mismatch in the 11/25 "30% OFF Pilcro, Maeve & MORE 🫶" and "It's time: 30% off" sends. The pie emoji and "Easy as 🥧" nod to Thanksgiving, but the line is a pun and carries no information.
- The body is a clean stack of image-and-red-bar tiles: sweaters, holiday decor, shoes, sleepwear, furniture, jewelry, kitchen, beauty, wedding and Terrain. It's tidy and on-brand, but it's a catalog tour with no hero product and no ranking of what matters. A recommendations grid with "Top Rated" tags follows, then the footer.
- Verdict: competent but redundant. This subscriber has now seen the same offer structure about 17 times, so the only fresh element is the pie pun. The 50%-off sale framing is credible, but fatigue is the real problem.

## What's working

- The subject and hero both lead with "EXTRA 50% OFF SALE," so the promise is consistent.
- The hero stacks two offers clearly: a 50% sale tier and a 30% tier for everything else. The two big numbers are easy to read.
- The red "SHOP NOW" button is high-contrast and sits directly under the headline.
- The category tiles are consistent, with white-on-red labels and good editorial photography.
- The "Top Rated" tags in the recommendations grid add some social proof.

## What's weak

- The hero is unchanged from the previous send, so this reads as the 17th near-identical email in four days.
- The hero has three more CTAs, in the 30% block, and then about 10 category tiles. There is no single focal product.
- The double asterisks on the offer lines and the "excludes select items" note read as fine print. The offer is layered, and "EXTRA" on top of "NOW" is confusing about what the 50% stacks on.
- The marquee strip, "THE YEAR JUST GOT BETTER · THE BEST DEALS OF THE YE…", is cut off at the edges.
- The recommendations grid has no visible prices or product names, so there's little product detail to drive a click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Collapse the hero to one offer and one button. Sale gets "Shop the 50% off sale," and the 30% line moves below as secondary copy.
- 2. Vary the creative day to day. Rotate the hero to a single product or category, or use a countdown, so the subscriber sees something new.
- 3. Cut the category grid from about 10 tiles to 4–6 and put prices or "from $X" on each.
- 4. Add prices and ratings to the recommendation tiles.
- 5. Reduce the cadence. Send one email per day with a specific reason, such as "last day" or a new category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 50% off sale ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sweaters, shoes & decor, now 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 30% off everything else. Free shipping on $100+.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Top-rated sweaters and holiday decor, marked down again.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML has a few real code defects: a malformed Outlook font rule, quoted generic font keywords, a viewport tag that blocks zoom, no `lang` attribute, and three banner images without alt text. Header-level compliance and authentication checks couldn't be confirmed, because the QA run reports the headers as not captured via the AgentMail relay. The source is also truncated, so footer and unsubscribe markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **44 tracking links were not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status codes and redirect chains are unverified.
- **Third-party pixel with an empty parameter.** `https://login.dotomi.com/profile/visit/px/1_0?dtm_cid=2466&dtm_cmagic=7b41bf&dtm_fid=103&cli_promo_id=99&dtm_email_hash=` ends with an empty `dtm_email_hash=`. This could be a failed merge or just QA truncation. Verify it against the raw message.
- **Plain-text part is 75% URLs** (8528 of 11365 chars). That points to long tracking-wrapped URLs in the text alternative. It is a minor spam-filter heuristic risk.
- The `pxl.mon-trk.com` pixel path embeds what looks like a CRM contact ID (`003a700000uTzaxAAC`). It is a normal tracking pattern, but it is a persistent per-recipient identifier in a third-party URL.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed Outlook CSS.** `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;` has an empty list entry (`, ,`). The `!important` is orphaned after the semicolon, so it isn't applied to the declaration. The Outlook font override is therefore unreliable.
- **Quoted generic keywords.** The `[style*="Font-Normal-*"]` rules use `'sans-serif'` and `'serif'` in quotes. Quoted, they are treated as family names rather than generic fallbacks. Fallback then relies on Helvetica, Arial or Georgia alone.
- **Viewport blocks zoom.** `maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 failure on mobile.
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns=... xmlns:v=... xmlns:o=...>` has no `lang` attribute, and `<title></title>` is empty.
- **Missing alt text on content images** (QA-confirmed):
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg`
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg`
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`
- **Missing alt on tracking pixels.** The dotomi and mon-trk pixels are also flagged. If they are decorative they should carry `alt=""`; I can't tell whether it is absent or empty.
- **Minor CSS issues.**
  - The `[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` rule is declared twice.
  - A comment precedes the `<!DOCTYPE>`, which can trigger quirks mode in some legacy clients.
  - The Google Fonts `@import` is stripped by Gmail and others. The fallback stack covers this.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the truncated source.
- The only suspect item is the empty `dtm_email_hash=` value noted above.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA tool notes this may be a relay capture artifact. Confirm in the original message headers. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `anthropologie@s.anthropologie.com` are unverified.
- The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source, so I can't assess them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- UTM parameters can't be assessed. The visible links are tracked redirects and 44 of them were skipped, so I can't confirm UTM presence or landing-page alignment.
- The pixel path includes the campaign name `112526_AN-US_PromoBlackFridayDeeperDiscountReminder3_PM`, which matches the send date, but that is the only continuity signal.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the MSO rule to `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif !important;` and use unquoted `sans-serif` and `serif` in the other font stacks.
2. Remove `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1.0` from the viewport tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a meaningful `<title>`.
4. Add descriptive alt text to the three banner images, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixels.
5. Check the raw message for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. If they are present, mark the QA warnings as relay artifacts.
6. Check whether `dtm_email_hash=` is empty in the raw HTML or only truncated.
7. Resolve a sample of the 44 skipped tracking links to confirm final URLs, status codes and UTM parameters.
8. Consider a more balanced plain-text alternative, with fewer or shorter URLs.
