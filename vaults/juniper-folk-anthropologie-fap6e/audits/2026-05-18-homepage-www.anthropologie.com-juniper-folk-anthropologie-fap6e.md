# Homepage snapshot · www.anthropologie.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay so I opened Anthropologie and... I got a wall. Literally zero homepage — just their logo, "Access is temporarily restricted," and a bot-detection error message citing my IP. This isn't a homepage experience at all; it's a hard block page. For someone like me who shops Anthro constantly and has AnthroPerks, being greeted with "we think you're a bot" is genuinely off-putting.

## What's working

- **The Anthropologie wordmark** rendered correctly in its serif all-caps style — at least I know I'm in the right place.
- **"Submit Feedback About Experience"** link is present, which is a small grace — at least there's a path out.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage** — nothing loaded. No hero, no nav, no products, no promos, no loyalty hooks.
- **The block message copy** — "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" with a public IP address (`75.140.9.0`) printed on screen is alarming and clinical. For a brand that sells $300 dresses and leans into warmth, this reads like a cease-and-desist.
- **No recovery path for a real customer** — there's no CAPTCHA, no "prove you're human," no alternative to just... being locked out.
- **The blank white expanse** between the logo and the error text makes the page feel broken before you even read the message.

## Recommendations

- **Add a CAPTCHA or "I'm not a robot" challenge** instead of a dead end — let real customers prove themselves and land on the actual homepage without calling your IT team.
- **Rewrite the block copy in brand voice** — something like "Looks like we hit a snag — tap below to continue shopping" instead of IP addresses and developer-tool accusations.
- **Surface a minimal nav or search bar** even on the error state so that a legitimate shopper can still reach a category page directly.
- **Test bot detection thresholds** on mobile user-agents — this looks like it's tripping on normal mobile browse behavior ("rapid taps" is listed as a reason, which is literally how people scroll on phones).
