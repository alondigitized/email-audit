# Up to 40% off ends TONIGHT!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a HOME sub-branded email — a meaningful departure from the last four sends, which were all anchored to apparel and the "Fashionably Early Fall Event." The hero is a clean ornamental badge delivering "UP TO 40% OFF*" in an instant, and the structured six-section product grid (The Total Set, Living Botanics, Summer Sundries, Outdoor Thinking, Don't Stop Basics, Going Strong) gives subscribers multiple browse entry points. Visually, it's the most coherent HOME-dedicated creative Anthropologie has sent in this stretch.
- The problem is self-inflicted: the email buries its own lead by jamming a mid-scroll band for "30% off: fashionably early fall event" — a competing, lower-discount, different-category promotion — into the same send. Yesterday's email told this same subscriber that the fall event was "LIVE" at 30% off. Today they open to find a HOME sale at up to 40% off that "ends TONIGHT." Two sale narratives, two SHOP NOW buttons, no clear hierarchy between them.
- The cadence is now genuinely damaging. This is the twelfth-plus promotional send in roughly two weeks. The subscriber just weathered "FINAL HOURS" twice and "ends TONIGHT" at least twice. Another "ends TONIGHT" — on a *different* sale from a sub-branded sender — will land as noise rather than urgency, regardless of how clean the creative is.

## What's working

- **HOME sub-brand is explicit from the first frame** — "ANTHROPOLOGIE HOME" at the top tells the subscriber their category before they read a word of copy.
- **40% badge is the undisputed visual anchor** — large type, ornamental framing, and white space make it scannable in under a second.
- **Six editorial sections create multiple entry points** — lifestyle photography and named category tiles let different subscribers self-select into what pulls them.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout failures visible anywhere in the scroll.
- **Subject line is short and deadline-clear** — 28 characters, no emoji stacking, one exclamation point.

## What's weak

- **Dual-offer confusion undermines both promotions** — "Up to 40% off" on HOME and "30% off: fashionably early fall event" on apparel share the same email with competing CTA buttons; the subscriber can't easily tell which sale takes priority or which to trust.
- **"SHOP NOW" on every single button** — six product sections plus the hero all use the same generic CTA; no guidance toward a specific entry point, no category-specific verb.
- **Cadence is at a breaking point** — the subscriber just read "30% off Fashionably Early Fall Event is LIVE" yesterday; landing in their inbox the next day with a *different* sale "ending TONIGHT" reads as inconsistent or even manipulative.
- **No loyalty or member framing** — the fall event was launched as an AnthroPerks exclusive; now it appears mid-scroll with no member acknowledgment, stripping the prior "early access" framing of meaning.

## Recommendations

- 1. **One email, one sale story.** HOME deserves its own dedicated send; the fall event secondary band only muddies the value proposition and competes for the click. Separate them — they're different categories, different discounts, different audiences.
- 2. **Replace every "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific verb.** "Shop Furniture," "Shop Lighting & Decor," "Shop the Home Sale" — each one gives the subscriber a clearer reason to move.
- 3. **Add specificity to the deadline.** "Ends at midnight tonight" or "Ends July 9" is more credible than the repeated "TONIGHT" pattern subscribers have now seen at least three times this week.
- 4. **Surface member pricing if it exists.** If AnthroPerks members get an additional stack on the HOME sale, say so — it's the one loyalty hook missing from an otherwise good HOME send.
- 5. **Rename the subject to lead with the category.** HOME gets lost in "Up to 40% off" when the subscriber's mental model is that all Anthropologie sales are apparel-first.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off home ends tonight — furniture, decor & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: Anthropologie Home up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, bedding — this deal ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six home categories, one price drop. Shop before it's gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — Invalid CSS in MSO conditional block:**
```css
body, table, td { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important; }
```
Two defects on one line:
1. Empty font-family slot: `Helvetica, ,` — a dangling comma produces an invalid token that some parsers reject entirely, discarding the whole declaration.
2. `!important` placed after the closing semicolon — it is outside the property value and is syntactically illegal. The `!important` has no effect in Outlook rendering.

**Bug — Duplicate CSS rule:**
`[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` (`Open Sans`) is defined identically in both the "ANTHRO" and "TERRAIN" comment sections. The second declaration is redundant but harmless; the first one wins due to cascade order.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not detected** (QA WARN). This header is required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender compliance (enforced Feb 2024). Its absence in the captured message may be an AgentMail relay artifact (header stripped in transit) rather than a send-side omission — but it cannot be confirmed without raw SMTP headers from the receiving MTA.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not detected** (QA WARN). One-click unsubscribe (required for Gmail/Yahoo senders >5k/day) depends on both headers being present together. If the `List-Unsubscribe` header is genuinely missing at send time, Gmail will downgrade unsubscribe handling and may apply deliverability penalties.

**`Authentication-Results` header — not detected** (QA WARN). SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown for this capture. Anthropologie sends from `s.anthropologie.com` (a Salesforce Marketing Cloud subdomain); DKIM alignment to the `anthropologie.com` organizational domain should be verified independently.
