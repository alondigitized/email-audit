# Psst. Up to $100 could be yours!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Anthropologie sends the spend-threshold reward mechanic a second consecutive day — "Up to $100 could be yours!" echoes yesterday's "Get up to $100 off just for shopping" almost verbatim. Where July 22 felt like a fresh pivot away from the Extra 50% Off saturation cycle, today's send reverses that progress: the Extra 50% Off Sale resurfaces mid-email, sandwiched between the spend-tier hero and a product editorial grid. The result is two competing promotions, two mental models, and one confused subscriber.
- The hero execution itself is solid — a clean two-tier offer ($50 off $150 / $100 off $300) with a visible SHOP NOW CTA lands immediately on open. The colorful category nav below it is a useful shortcut for intent-driven browsing. The photography is editorial and brand-consistent throughout.
- The problem is strategic, not executional: this email asks you to hold two incompatible offers in your head simultaneously. Is the Extra 50% Off Sale still live? Or should I be calculating whether I'll hit $150 or $300 to trigger the reward? That friction suppresses clicks. Worse, the subject-line teaser ("could be yours") is softer and more conditional than yesterday's cleaner "just for shopping," so the email opens weaker than its predecessor and delivers a messier body.

## What's working

- **Two-tier hero is legible at a glance** — "$50 off $150 / $100 off $300" with a visible SHOP NOW button lands clearly before any scroll.
- **Category nav is colorful and functional** — quick-tap access to specific departments sits right below the hero, useful for intent-driven shoppers.
- **No render bugs** — images load, layout holds, text doesn't overlap.
- **Brand photography is editorial quality** — models, lifestyle shots, and the product grid maintain Anthropologie's aesthetic consistently.
- **Subject line is mobile-friendly** — 32 characters, no ALL CAPS, no spam markers.

## What's weak

- **Extra 50% Off Sale reappears mid-email** — the same mechanic that has run four-plus times since July 15 undercuts the freshness of the spend-tier offer and creates two competing CTAs.
- **"Could be yours" is hedge language** — yesterday's "Get up to $100 off just for shopping" was direct and earned; today's phrasing introduces doubt and feels like a tease rather than a promise.
- **No deadline visible for either offer** — neither the spend-tier reward nor the Extra 50% Off Sale shows an expiry, removing the one lever that would resolve the "which offer is more urgent?" confusion.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic** — with two active promotions in the email, the CTA should anchor to the specific mechanic ("Earn Your $100 Reward" or "Shop the Sale"); "SHOP NOW" commits to nothing.
- **Same mechanic, back-to-back days** — cadence-aware subscribers who opened July 22 will feel re-sold, not rewarded.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one promotion per send.** Drop the Extra 50% Off Sale module entirely — if the sale is still live, it gets its own send tomorrow. Running two mechanics in one email halves the persuasion of each.
- 2. **Harden the CTA copy to the offer** — change "SHOP NOW" to "Earn Your $100 Reward" or "Start Earning — Shop Now" so the action is anchored to the incentive.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even a soft "through Sunday" on the spend-tier banner closes the loop the subject line deliberately leaves open.
- 4. **Rework the subject to be as direct as July 22** — "could be yours" loses the directness that made yesterday's send work. If this is a retransmit to non-openers, the subject must meaningfully differ, not just soften.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$50 or $100 off — here's how to claim it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $150, get $50 back. Spend $300, get $100.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two tiers, one reward — the more you shop, the more you save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your reward unlocks automatically at checkout — here's what to know.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**MSO/Outlook CSS syntax error** in the `<!--[if mso]>` conditional block:
```css
body, table, td {
  font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;
```

Two bugs on the same line: a semicolon before `!important` terminates the declaration early (Outlook ignores `!important`), and the double comma `Helvetica, ,` inserts an empty font-family token. Font override will silently fail in all Outlook versions — the MSO block exists specifically to fix Outlook rendering and is now a no-op.
