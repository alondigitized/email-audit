# Want to win a $250 gift card?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a survey solicitation with a sweepstakes hook — Anthropologie asking subscribers to weigh in on the brand in exchange for a chance at a $250 gift card. The creative execution is charming and unmistakably on-brand: "a penny for your thoughts!" in that signature coral script, followed by an honest two-column "what's in it for you / us" frame that respects the reader's intelligence. Anthropologie has done harder things worse.
- The problem is structural. This is a form email, not a shopping email, and the click path leads to a survey — a massive drag on conversion intent. The "START YOUR WISH LIST?" product block bolted below the CTA feels like filler to justify the email's length, not a coherent content strategy. And the sweepstakes entry has no visible deadline, which kills whatever urgency the $250 prize might have generated.
- The cadence compounds everything: the brand history shows a "kick it in sporty new arrivals" maeve email also dated June 15. Two sends in one day after a recent editorial run is the kind of thing subscribers don't consciously notice but subconsciously punish at unsubscribe time.

## What's working

- "A penny for your thoughts!" is warm, witty, and unmistakably Anthropologie — a big step up from the rote "Extra 40% off" language that dominated May.
- The two-column "What's in it for you / What's in it for us" frame is transparent and builds goodwill; this is rarer than it should be in brand surveys.
- The coral "let's get acquainted →" CTA button is visible and on-brand; no visual confusion about where to click.
- The 20% off loyalty module (5+ reviews per season) is smart secondary placement — reinforces the review program without making it the lead.

## What's weak

- "Want to win a $250 gift card?" reads like a generic sweepstakes blast that could come from any retailer — the Anthropologie brand doesn't appear in the subject line at all.
- Zero deadline on the sweepstakes entry; a credible close date would have been the single cheapest lift available.
- "Let's get acquainted →" is vague — a subscriber doesn't know they're clicking into a survey until they land on the page.
- "START YOUR WISH LIST?" secondary content is tonally and functionally disconnected from a survey email; it dilutes the one ask.
- Same-day send as the June 15 maeve "sporty new arrivals" email — double-tapping subscribers in one day after a busy May is a cadence misstep.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the sweepstakes** — "Survey closes June 30" costs nothing to add and is the fastest path to more entries.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to name the action** — "Take the 2-min survey →" tells subscribers exactly what they're doing; "let's get acquainted" hides it.
- 3. **Put the brand name in the subject** — Anthropologie has enough equity to carry it; using it also differentiates from the dozens of generic giveaway emails in any inbox.
- 4. **Hold the send for a standalone cadence slot** — Don't double-send on the same day as a sub-brand email. This deserved its own day.
- 5. **Cut or reframe the wish list block** — Either tie it to the survey ("telling us about your wishlist helps us improve") or drop it entirely to keep the one-ask structure clean.

- **Subject Alt B:** `2 minutes. Your thoughts. $250 on us.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A short survey. A chance to win. Closes June 30.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us what you love — we'll pick up the tab (maybe).`

## Technical Audit — Anthropologie "Want to win a $250 gift card?"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a shared URBN multi-brand template with several technical defects: a CSS syntax error in the Outlook conditional block, 8 images missing alt attributes, and missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers that put this send out of compliance with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements enforced since Feb 2024.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**27 tracking links skipped by QA probe** — all click-redirect domains were excluded from HTTP probing, so destination URL integrity (broken links, redirect chain depth, final landing page) is unverified for the majority of CTAs.

**Tracking pixel missing alt text:**
- `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/95bb6f26-9cf5-4254-8a2d-39742e856443/AN-US_WelcomeSeries_TP9/003a700000uTzaxAAC` — tracking pixels must carry `alt=""` to suppress broken-image indicators in image-blocking clients.

**User ID inconsistency in Evergage URLs:**
Evergage recommendation image URLs use a 15-char Salesforce ID (`userId=003a700000uTza`) while the monitoring pixel uses the 18-char form (`003a700000uTzaxAAC`). Both forms are valid Salesforce IDs, but the mismatch suggests two separate merge systems are inserting the ID with different length conventions. Verify the Evergage system is resolving the correct record consistently.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS syntax error in Outlook conditional block:**
```css
<!--[if mso]>
body, table, td { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important; }
```
Two defects on the same line:
- Double comma: `Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'` — empty slot in the font-family list, invalid CSS.
- `!important` is placed *after* the closing semicolon, making it syntactically dead. The declaration falls back to browser default in Outlook.

**Duplicate CSS selector (minor):**
`[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` is defined twice — once under the BEHOLDEN block and again under TERRAIN — with identical declarations. Harmless but indicates template drift.

**Multi-brand CSS payload bloat:**
The `<style>` block includes full font definitions and button class sets for Urban Outfitters (`.link-uo-*`), Free People (`.link-fp-*`), Beholden, Terrain, and Community/Vendor Relations — none of which apply to this Anthropologie send. This inflates the CSS payload with dead rules on every send.

**8 images missing `alt` text:**

| Image | Domain |
|---|---|
| `summary.png` (×3) | `urbanoutfitters.us-1.evergage.com` (personalization recs) |
| `e6a722ed-….png` (×2) | `image.s.anthropologie.com` |
| `1e313de8-….png` (×1) | `image.s.anthropologie.com` |
| `003a700000uTzaxAAC` (tracking pixel) | `pxl.mon-trk.com` |

Content images need descriptive alt text; decorative/tracking images need `alt=""`.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND** — This is required for bulk senders under Google and Yahoo's February 2024 policies (>5k/day to Gmail). Absence risks deliverability penalties and inbox demotion.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058): NOT FOUND** — One-click unsubscribe (required by the same Feb 2024 policies) is not signaled. Gmail shows its own unsubscribe UI when this is missing, which can bypass the sender's list management and suppress tracking of unsubscribe events.

**Authentication-Results: NOT CAPTURED** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the header. This is an audit gap, not a confirmed failure, but SPF/DKIM status must be verified at the ESP level before concluding the send is authenticated.

The physical address and unsubscribe link are assumed present in the email body (CAN-SPAM §5) but could not be confirmed from the truncated HTML provided.
