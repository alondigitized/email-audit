# 30% OFF, just in time for dessert 🍰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the twentieth Anthropologie Black Friday send I've audited in about four days. It reuses the plaid-edged gift-tag hero from the 11/26 "🥧 Dig in! 30% OFF 1000s of styles" email almost unchanged. The subject sells "30% OFF, just in time for dessert", but the hero's biggest, first-read line is "NOW, EXTRA 50% OFF SALE". The 30% offer sits second, below a pink plus sign. Anyone who opens on the subject's promise lands on a bigger number than the one they were promised.
- Below the hero, the email is a clean, well-organised grid of teal-captioned category tiles: top-rated dresses, holiday decor under $50, sweaters under $100, sneakers, jewelry, denim, ILIA beauty gift sets, wedding and guest styles. It ends with a "Might we recommend…" product strip. The structure is easy to scan and the photography is strong. The tile captions are the real hooks, because they put price points up front.
- My judgement is competent but repetitive. It is a good template, run again on another day with a different subject line. A subscriber who got the 11/25 "30% OFF Pilcro, Maeve & MORE 🫶" and the 11/26 "Now we're cooking" sends has no new reason to open this one.

## What's working

- The hero is a clear two-tier offer: 50% off sale and 30% off everything else, with separate CTAs for each.
- The three 30% buttons ("Shop Clothing", "Shop Home & Furniture", "Shop Gifts") route shoppers by category.
- The tiles with price caps ("under $50", "under $100") give concrete, budget-friendly entry points.
- The teal caption bars and consistent tile grid make the layout easy to scan on mobile.
- The photography is warm and seasonal, and the plaid edge and ribbon tag feel on-brand and festive.
- The "Might we recommend" strip adds star ratings, which is social proof.

## What's weak

- The subject promises 30% but the hero leads with 50% off. The mismatch is the same one I flagged in the 11/25 "30% OFF Pilcro, Maeve & MORE" send.
- Hero hierarchy is muddled. Two percentages, a plus sign and four CTA buttons compete, so the eye has no single landing point.
- The "dessert" and pie theme shows up nowhere in the hero beyond the earlier "Dig in!" send, so the subject's hook is lost in the render.
- The top banner marquee ("ANKSGIVING! | HAPPY THANKSGIVING!") is cropped mid-word, which looks like a clipped strip rather than a designed one.
- The exclusion footnotes ("excludes select items", double asterisks) are tiny, which weakens how honest the offer feels.
- The email is long, with eight or more tiles, and the "Might we recommend" items have no visible prices.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject and hero tell the same story. Lead the hero with whichever percentage is the real headline, and put the other in a subline.
- 2. Cut the hero CTAs to one primary button, with the category links moved down into the tiles.
- 3. Show prices on the recommended products to lift click-through.
- 4. Fix the cropped greeting banner so it reads as a full phrase or remove it.
- 5. Space out near-identical sends this week, or vary the creative for each.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 50% off sale + 30% off the rest`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call before the Friday rush: 30–50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, denim, and gifts under $50 — all marked down through tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Top-rated sweaters under $100 are going fast. Shop before they sell out.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a multi-brand URBN template and its tracking is intact. The confirmed defects are in the source: 3 content images with no alt text, a malformed Outlook font rule, and a viewport meta tag that blocks zoom. Compliance headers and authentication could not be verified because the relay didn't capture them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 44 tracking links went through click-redirect domains and were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and status codes are **unverified**.
- The Dotomi pixel `https://login.dotomi.com/profile/visit/px/1_0?...&dtm_email_hash=` has an empty `dtm_email_hash` value. Either the hash wasn't populated for this recipient or the token failed to resolve. See section 4.
- The pixel `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/95bb6f26-.../112626_AN-US_PromoThanksgivingBlackFridayReminder2_PM/003a7…` is a third-party tracker. It adds an extra request and a third-party data flow, but no failure is observed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on content images (confirmed):**
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg`
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg`
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`

  If these carry the offer or navigation, image-blocking clients and screen readers lose that content.
- **Tracking pixels (dotomi `1_0`, mon-trk `003a700000uTzaxAAC`):** both lack `alt`. Low severity, but they should have `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0"/>` stops pinch-zoom on clients that honor it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Malformed Outlook font rule:** `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;` has an empty font-family entry (`, ,`). The `;` before `!important` also makes that declaration invalid, so the Outlook override likely doesn't apply.
- **Empty `<title></title>`.**
- **No `lang` attribute:** the `<html>` tag in the visible source has none, only the xmlns declarations.
- **Web fonts via `@import`:** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/...')` is ignored by Gmail and several other clients. The fallbacks are defined, so this is expected degradation rather than breakage.
- **Template bloat:** the CSS carries class and font blocks for UO, Free People, Beholden and Terrain in an Anthropologie send. This adds bytes and risks Gmail's ~102KB clipping. I can't confirm clipping because the source was truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The only evidence is the empty `dtm_email_hash=` in the Dotomi URL. This is probably a recipient-hash merge that didn't resolve, or a deliberate blank for a test or seed address. I can't distinguish the two from the source.
- No unresolved `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[[…]]` tokens appear in the visible portion. The remainder was truncated and not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA tool notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unverified, not a confirmed defect**. If they are truly absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both.
- `Authentication-Results` was not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `s.anthropologie.com` are unknown.
- The footer unsubscribe link and physical address are not in the truncated source, so they can't be assessed.
- The plain-text part is 8,528 of 11,371 characters URLs (75%). That is above the 70% threshold and a spam-filter signal. The alternative part is probably a tracked-link dump rather than readable text.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- All 44 tracked links are redirect-wrapped, so UTM parameters and landing pages can't be checked from the HTML. No continuity issues are confirmed, but nothing was verified either.
- The `PromoThanksgivingBlackFridayReminder2` token in the mon-trk path indicates campaign naming. No source-side `utm_*` values were visible.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the three banner and content images. Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.
2. Remove `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1.0` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Fix the Outlook rule to `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'Font-Normal' !important;`, with no empty entry and no stray `;`.
4. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a meaningful `<title>`.
5. Check whether `dtm_email_hash` populates on a real subscriber send. If it is blank for live recipients, fix the ESP merge.
6. Re-test with raw `.eml` headers, not the relay copy, to verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
7. Generate a human-readable plain-text part and cut the URL ratio below 70%.
8. Resolve redirect chains (follow with a tracking-aware probe) to confirm landing pages and UTMs.
9. Trim unused brand CSS and confirm the final HTML stays under 102KB.
