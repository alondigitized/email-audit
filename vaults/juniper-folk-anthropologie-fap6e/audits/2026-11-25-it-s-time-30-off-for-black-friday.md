# It’s time: 30% off for BLACK FRIDAY 🛍️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixteenth Anthropologie Black Friday send I've audited in about four days, and the subject has drifted from the hero again. The subject says "30% off for BLACK FRIDAY 🛍️". The hero now leads with "NOW, EXTRA 50% OFF SALE" and only then shows 30% off everything else. That is the same plaid-edged gift-tag creative as the 11/25 "NOW, EXTRA 50% OFF SALE for Black Friday!" and "30% OFF Pilcro, Maeve & MORE 🫶" sends. The subject undersells the best offer in the email.
- The render is clean and the offer stack is easy to read. A "Shop Now" button sits under the 50% off sale, and three buttons (Shop Clothing, Shop Home & Furniture, Shop Gifts) sit under 30% off. Below that is a long run of red-banner category tiles and a "Might We Recommend…" product grid. The category tiles are well shot and seasonal. But the email is long, the top has two competing offers, and nothing is personalized.
- Verdict: competent, on-brand and well rendered. It's also one more near-identical Black Friday blast. The subject doesn't match the strongest hero claim, and the cadence is fatiguing.

## What's working

- The hero stacks two offers clearly: "EXTRA 50% OFF SALE" and "30% OFF almost everything else".
- The "Shop Now" button and the three category buttons are visible above the fold.
- The red banner tiles (sweaters, holiday decor, sneakers, furniture, beauty) have strong editorial photography and consistent labels.
- The "Might We Recommend…" grid with "Top Rated" tags adds some social proof.
- The footer has useful secondary paths: New Arrivals, E-Gift Card, TikTok and Pick Up in Store.

## What's weak

- The subject promises 30% off, but the hero leads with 50% off sale, so the strongest offer isn't in the subject.
- The "Black Friday" ticker strip is cropped at the edges ("THE YEAR JUST GOT BETTER • THE BEST DEALS OF THE YE"). It reads as a cut-off marquee.
- The hero has four buttons across two offers. The eye has to work out which one is the primary action.
- The email is very long, with about ten category tiles and a nine-item product grid. Product detail, prices and reviews are thin.
- The "**" fine print on the offers is easy to miss at that size, and the exclusions only show in the footer and a small line.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the strongest offer in the subject and make the hero match it, so the subject, preheader and hero all say the same thing. If the extra 50% off sale is the headline, lead with it.
- 2. Collapse the hero to one primary button ("Shop the 50% Off Sale") and make the 30% off a secondary text link.
- 3. Cut the category tiles from about ten to five or six, and add prices or "from $X" to the product grid.
- 4. Fix the ticker so the text isn't clipped.
- 5. Segment or personalize the hero. Show women's clothing or home depending on browse history.
- 6. Throttle cadence. Sixteen near-identical sends in four days trains people to ignore them.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday is here: up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale styles just dropped again. Sweaters, sneakers and holiday decor inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 50% off sale, plus 30% off almost everything. Ends soon.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard ESP-generated (Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style) table template. The automated checks show a 50% pass rate (0 errors, 4 warnings), and the HTML source adds a few code-level defects. Compliance headers and authentication couldn't be verified because the relay didn't capture them. The HTML was also truncated, so footer and link markup is unchecked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **44 tracking links weren't probed.** The QA run skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **Tracking pixels have no `alt` attribute.**
  - `https://login.dotomi.com/profile/visit/px/1_0?dtm_cid=2466&dtm_cmagic=7b41bf&dtm_fid=103&cli_promo_id=99&dtm_email_hash=`
  - `https://pxl.mon-trk.com/95bb6f26-.../112526_AN-US_PromoBlackFridayDeeperDiscountReminder2_PM/003a700000uTzaxAAC`
  - Both should carry `alt=""` and explicit 1×1 dimensions so screen readers don't announce the filename.
- **The Dotomi pixel's `dtm_email_hash=` is empty.** This is either an unresolved merge field or a deliberate blank. If it should be populated, the retargeting pixel isn't getting its identifier.
- **The mon-trk pixel path ends in `003a700000uTzaxAAC`.** This looks like a Salesforce Contact ID, an identifier exposed in a third-party pixel URL.
- **Two third-party pixel domains, `dotomi.com` and `mon-trk.com`, load in addition to the first-party `image.s.anthropologie.com`.** Clients that block remote content will suppress them, so open and retargeting data will be incomplete.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Three content images lack alt text** (QA-confirmed):
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg`
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg`
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`
  - They may be decorative, but they need an explicit `alt=""` if so. A missing attribute isn't the same as an empty one.
- **`<title></title>` is empty**, which is an accessibility and web-view issue.
- **The viewport meta disables zoom:** `maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0` (WCAG 1.4.4 concern).
- **The Outlook-only conditional CSS is malformed:**
  `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;`
  It has an empty font-family entry and a stray `;` before `!important`. The `!important` is dropped, and the empty entry makes the font stack unreliable.
- **An HTML comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE html>`.** This triggers quirks mode in some older IE/Outlook rendering engines.
- **Web fonts load via `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/...')` inside `<style>`.** Gmail and several other clients strip or ignore `@import`, so the fallback stacks do the work there. The `[style*="Font-Normal-xx"]` attribute selectors depend on matching inline styles, which is fragile.
- **The plain-text part is mostly URLs:** 8528 of 11371 characters (75%). The long tracking URLs inflate it, and this can raise spam-filter scoring.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`) appear in the truncated source.
- The empty `dtm_email_hash=` is the only suspicious blank (see section 2).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) weren't found.** The QA notes say the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers are really absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements aren't met.
- **`Authentication-Results` wasn't captured**, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain `s.anthropologie.com` is aligned with the image host `image.s.anthropologie.com`, which is a good sign, but alignment isn't proof.
- **The visible unsubscribe link and the physical postal address couldn't be checked** because the truncated HTML cuts off before the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters and landing-page status can't be verified.** All 44 click links go through tracking redirects that weren't resolved, and none were visible in the truncated source.
- Resolving the redirect chain would show whether UTM parameters survive to anthropologie.com.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-run the QA against the raw `.eml` to capture `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Confirm the headers actually exist before treating them as findings.
2. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to the Dotomi and mon-trk pixels. Add `alt=""` to the decorative `pencilline` banners and the `.png`, or real alt text if they carry meaning.
3. Check why `dtm_email_hash=` is empty in the Dotomi pixel.
4. Fix the Outlook CSS to `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif !important;`.
5. Remove `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1.0`, and populate `<title>`.
6. Move the comment after the DOCTYPE, or remove it.
7. Shorten the plain-text version, for example by using shorter or unwrapped links.
8. Follow the redirect chains on a sample of the skipped tracking links, with a headless resolver rather than a plain HTTP probe. Verify UTM carry-through and that landing pages return 200.
