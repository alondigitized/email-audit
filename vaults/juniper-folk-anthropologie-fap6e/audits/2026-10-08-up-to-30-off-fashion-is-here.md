# Up to 30% off fashion is here!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, offer-first Anthropologie sale blast, and the third version of the same promo in three days. The boxed hero stacks three colour-blocked offers: 30% off fashion (online only), 30% off home, and an extra 50% off sale. The hierarchy is easy to read, and the subject matches the top block. But the hero is a tidy menu of discounts, not one reason to click. The seasonal framing is also thin, since "Feels like fall sale" doesn't pull toward anything specific.
- Below the hero it turns into a category directory. Six editorial tiles (Trend Hub, Holiday Party Shop, Cashmere Shop, Mantel Décor, Watches, Beauty Gift Sets) link to broad shops, followed by a nine-product "These are so you, too" grid with no prices, names or sale markers. The long asterisked fine print, which has a separate footnote for each of the three offers, undercuts the "limited time" tone.
- Judgement: competent and on-brand, but it is a rerun. Compared with the Oct 7 "Extra 50% off sale won't be here for long" and the Oct 6 sends, there is no new beat. The only change is that fashion now leads. It will perform on brand trust alone.

## What's working

- The hero is a bold colour-blocked stack, with burgundy, pink and olive each carrying one offer. The eye lands on "30% OFF" first.
- The subject's offer ("up to 30% off fashion") matches the first hero block, so the open-to-click message is consistent.
- Each offer notes where it applies ("online only" or "online & in stores"), which is useful channel clarity.
- The editorial tiles have good photography, and the Holiday Party Shop and Cashmere Shop tiles fit the season.
- The "Even more to love" footer gives a useful secondary path: denim recycling, Klarna, inclusive sizing and the A-List.

## What's weak

- Three offers compete in the hero, and the arrow text links don't read as buttons. There is no single dominant CTA.
- "Limited time!" gives no end date, so the urgency isn't credible. It is also the same deadline-free pressure as the Oct 7 send.
- The nine-product grid has no prices, names or sale badges. A shopper can't tell what the discount actually gets them.
- Six category tiles plus a product grid plus a footer module make a very long scroll with no priority. It reads as a directory, not a curated pick.
- The fine print is heavy, with three separate asterisk footnotes plus the denim-recycling terms. The "extra 50% off sale" fine print carries exclusions that dilute the headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer. Lead with fashion, since that's the subject, and demote home and sale to a slim secondary strip. Use a real button, "Shop 30% off fashion".
- 2. Add a real end date ("ends Sunday" or a "ends 10/11" line). Credible urgency is the cheapest lift available, especially given the Oct 6–7 repeats.
- 3. Put sale prices and names on the product grid, for example "$98 → $68", and link each tile to its product page. Right now the grid is decoration.
- 4. Cut the tile list to the three most seasonal shops (Holiday Party, Cashmere, Trend Hub) and drop watches and mantel décor from a fashion send.
- 5. Vary the creative from the Oct 6–7 sends, or space the sends out. Subscribers will read a fourth near-identical promo as noise.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Cashmere, coats & party dresses: up to 30% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus extra 50% off sale and 30% off home. Online through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our holiday party edit and cashmere shop are included.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt (QA-confirmed):**
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg` (appears twice)
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg` (appears twice)
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`
  - The `login.dotomi.com` and `pxl.mon-trk.com` tracking pixels also lack `alt`. Adding `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` would fix the pixels. The three content images need real alt text or an explicit `alt=""` if decorative.
- **Malformed Outlook CSS:** `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;` has an empty font-family entry (`, ,`). The stray `;` before `!important` makes that declaration invalid, so the Outlook font override likely doesn't apply.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0"/>` disables pinch-zoom on mobile clients that honor it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns=... xmlns:v=... xmlns:o=...>` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the correct language.
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>` is empty.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** `<!-- ANTHROPOLOGIE TEMPLATE -->` comes before `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Some older clients render in quirks mode when anything precedes the doctype.
- **Duplicate rule:** the `[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` selector is defined twice, which is harmless but redundant.
- **Web fonts:** the `@import` of Google Fonts will be stripped or ignored by several major clients (Gmail, for example). The fallback stacks exist, so this is expected degradation, not a defect.
