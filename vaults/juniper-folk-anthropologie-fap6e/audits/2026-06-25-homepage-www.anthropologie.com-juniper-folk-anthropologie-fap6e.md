# Homepage snapshot · www.anthropologie.com · 2026-06-25
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- I opened Anthropologie on my phone and literally got a wall of text telling me I'm blocked. No hero. No dresses. No nothing — just "Access is temporarily restricted" and a list of reasons why I might be a bot. This isn't a homepage experience; it's a bouncer at the door telling me to leave.

## What's working

- The Anthropologie wordmark at the top at least confirms I'm at the right URL — brand recognition is instant.
- The "Submit Feedback About Experience" link is technically a CTA, and I appreciate that they give me somewhere to go rather than a dead end.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is replaced by a bot-detection block — I see zero product, zero imagery, zero offers.
- The error message reads like an IT helpdesk ticket ("JavaScript disabled or not working," "Automated (bot) activity") — completely off-brand for a boutique lifestyle retailer.
- No fallback content, no "try again" button, no path to the actual shop — I'm just stuck.
- Listing my IP address publicly on the page (`75.140.9.0`) is a weird, clinical touch that makes me feel surveilled, not welcomed.
- A real shopper hitting this on their phone would immediately close the tab — the friction is total.

## Recommendations

- **Add a graceful fallback page** — if bot detection fires, show a simplified, cached version of the homepage (no dynamic personalization needed) rather than a blank error wall. Even a static "Here's what's new" layout preserves the brand experience.
- **Strip the technical diagnostic copy** ("JavaScript disabled," IP address) — replace it with warm brand language: "Hmm, something went wrong on our end — tap here to reload" keeps the Anthro voice and removes the clinical chill.
- **Add a prominent "Reload / Try Again" button** styled to brand so shoppers don't just bounce.
- **Test bot-detection thresholds on mobile** — "rapid taps or clicks" is exactly how a real person browses on a phone; the trigger is too sensitive and is actively blocking paying customers.
