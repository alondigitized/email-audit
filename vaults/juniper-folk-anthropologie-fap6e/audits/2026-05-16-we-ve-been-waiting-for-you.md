# We’ve been waiting for you.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a loyalty program introduction email from Anthropologie, anchored around the ANTHRO+ membership program, framed as the start of a "new Relationship." The romantic, script-heavy art direction is completely on-brand — botanical illustrations, warm palette, bohemian cursive — and the brand identity holds together visually. But the email is structurally undisciplined: it opens on a loyalty onboarding pitch, then pivots to Kids, then Weddings (BHLDN), then Terrain (the garden brand), then a product grid. That is five different emails stuffed into one send.
- The subject line "We've been waiting for you." is evocative and clean at 28 characters, but it buries the actual offer. A subscriber scanning their inbox gets zero signal about what's inside — no offer, no product, no program name. The 20% off hook in the ANTHRO+ section is genuinely motivating once you're inside, but the path to that CTA is cluttered by competing sections that dilute the loyalty message.
- The email is prettier than it is effective. It communicates brand feel fluently but fails to deliver the one thing a loyalty introduction email must do: get the subscriber to join right now.

## What's working

- ANTHRO+ benefits are broken into three scannable tiers (PERKS, BONUS, GIVE BACK) with iconography — easy to parse at a glance
- 20% off is a concrete, specific offer that gives an actual reason to sign up
- Spring/botanical imagery is seasonally current and consistent with Anthropologie's aesthetic
- "join up today →" is clean and action-oriented relative to most luxury brand CTAs
- Brand voice stays consistent throughout — romantic, aspirational, never aggressive

## What's weak

- Subject line "We've been waiting for you." provides zero offer or product signal — it could be from any brand about anything
- Email fractures across five unrelated sections (ANTHRO+, Kids, Weddings, Terrain, product grid) with no unifying narrative
- No visible deadline or expiry on the 20% offer — nothing drives urgency to act today rather than next week
- The INTRODUCING KIDS section is jarring in what should be a focused loyalty welcome email
- Visual hierarchy collapses after the hero — the eye has nowhere clear to land after the opening section

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip this to one story.** Cut Kids, Weddings, and Terrain entirely. This should be a single-purpose ANTHRO+ onboarding email. Each of those cross-brand sections deserves its own send.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the offer.** Atmospheric subjects sacrifice open rate from subscribers who need a concrete reason to click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Join ANTHRO+: 20% off your first order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your benefits are ready — join ANTHRO+ free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free perks, birthday bonuses, and 1% giveback. No fees ever.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn rewards on everything you love. Join free today.`
- 3. **Add an expiry to the 20% offer.** Even "for the next 7 days" turns a passive offer into an action trigger. Without a deadline, subscribers defer indefinitely.
- 4. **Replace the hero CTA arrow with a real button.** "there's so much more where that came from →" is weak. Replace with `Claim My 20% Off` or `Join ANTHRO+ Free` as a full-width button directly under the ANTHRO+ benefits block.
- 5. **Anchor the loyalty pitch to one specific product.** Show a single bestseller at member price — "Start with this at 20% off" — turns abstract program value into a concrete purchase moment.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is a loyalty program introduction email from Anthropologie, anchored around the ANTHRO+ membership program, framed as the start of a "new Relationship." The romantic, script-heavy art direction is completely on-brand — botanical illustrations, warm palette, bohemian cursive — and the brand identity holds together visually. But the email is structurally undisciplined: it opens on a loyalty onboarding pitch, then pivots to Kids, then Weddings (BHLDN), then Terrain (the garden brand), then a product grid. That is five different emails stuffed into one send.

The subject line "We've been waiting for you." is evocative and clean at 28 characters, but it buries the actual offer. A subscriber scanning their inbox gets zero signal about what's inside — no offer, no product, no program name. The 20% off hook in the ANTHRO+ section is genuinely motivating once you're inside, but the path to that CTA is cluttered by competing sections that dilute the loyalty message.

The email is prettier than it is effective. It communicates brand feel fluently but fails to deliver the one thing a loyalty introduction email must do: get the subscriber to join right now.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Strip this to one story.** Cut Kids, Weddings, and Terrain entirely. This should be a single-purpose ANTHRO+ onboarding email. Each of those cross-brand sections deserves its own send.

2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the offer.** Atmospheric subjects sacrifice open rate from subscribers who need a concrete reason to click.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Join ANTHRO+: 20% off your first order`

3. **Add an expiry to the 20% offer.** Even "for the next 7 days" turns a passive offer into an action trigger. Without a deadline, subscribers defer indefinitely.

4. **Replace the hero CTA arrow with a real button.** "there's so much more where that came from →" is weak. Replace with `Claim My 20% Off` or `Join ANTHRO+ Free` as a full-width button directly under the ANTHRO+ benefits block.

5. **Anchor the loyalty pitch to one specific product.** Show a single bestseller at member price — "Start with this at 20% off" — turns abstract program value into a concrete purchase moment.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Anthropologie "We've been waiting for you." (Welcome Series TP1)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

This is a URBN shared-platform email (template serves Anthropologie, Free People, Urban Outfitters, Terrain, Beholden) deployed via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`s.anthropologie.com`) with Evergage (Interaction Studio) personalization. Two compliance headers mandatory for Gmail/Yahoo bulk senders are absent, and a Salesforce Contact ID is exposed unmasked in image request URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — FAIL**
QA reports 8,932 of 12,508 plain-text characters are URLs (71.4%). Spam filters treat plain-text bodies that are predominantly URLs as a strong signal for bulk/spam classification. The plain-text alternative needs real text content, not a URL dump.

**23 click-redirect links unverified**
All CTA links route through a click-tracking redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped HTTP resolution, so destination URLs, UTM parameters, and landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit alone. A separate link-resolution pass is needed.

**Tracking pixel domain: `pxl.mon-trk.com`**
Open/render pixel fires to a third-party domain (`pxl.mon-trk.com/95bb6f26-9cf5-4254-8a2d-39742e856443/AN-US_WelcomeSeries_TP1/003a700000uTzaxAAC`). See §4 for PII concern with the contact ID in this path.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Images missing alt text — 8 instances**

| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| `503296307-23-welcome-series_tp1_brand_04.gif` | `image.s.anthropologie.com` |
| `summary.png` (×3) | `urbanoutfitters.us-1.evergage.com` (recommendation slots) |
| `e6a722ed-...` (×2) | `image.s.anthropologie.com` |
| `1e313de8-...` | `image.s.anthropologie.com` |
| Tracking pixel | `pxl.mon-trk.com` |

Content images must have descriptive `alt` text for screen reader accessibility and for clients with images disabled. Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` (empty, not absent).

**CSS syntax errors in MSO conditional block**
```css
/* Line in <!--[if mso]> block */
font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;
```
Two issues: double comma in the font-family list (empty family slot), and `!important` placed *after* the semicolon — outside the property value entirely. Invalid syntax. Outlook's Word-based renderer may silently drop this rule or produce unexpected fallback behavior.

**`@import` in `<style>` for Google Fonts**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Playfair+Display&...');
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is unsupported in the majority of email clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail in some modes). The eight font families loaded here will silently fall back everywhere it matters. Not a breaking bug, but the `@import` approach is wasted HTTP overhead for email.

**Duplicate `.yfix{}` declaration**
The empty `.yfix{}` rule appears three times in the stylesheet (once per brand section). Harmless but indicates copy-paste template maintenance debt.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Salesforce Contact ID exposed in Evergage URLs — PII RISK**

Three product recommendation image URLs embed the raw Salesforce 15-char Contact ID:
```
https://urbanoutfitters.us-1.evergage.com/api/dataset/anus_prod/campaign/7p6Ep/GxBX1/1/summary.png?userId=003a700000uTza
```
The full 18-char ID (`003a700000uTzaxAAC`) also appears unencoded in the `pxl.mon-trk.com` tracking pixel path. If this email is:
- **Forwarded** — the recipient's browser fires image requests with the original subscriber's Contact ID
- **Cached by a proxy or email scanner** — the ID is logged at that third-party infrastructure

The identifier should be hashed or replaced with an opaque session token before being placed in image URLs. This is a CCPA/GDPR data minimization concern.

**No broken merge tokens visible** in the truncated HTML, but the full template could not be verified end-to-end.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — MISSING (CRITICAL)**
Required since February 2024 for Gmail and Yahoo bulk senders sending >5,000/day. Its absence risks inbox demotion or bulk-folder routing. This header must be set at the MTA/ESP level, not in HTML.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — MISSING (CRITICAL)**
One-click unsubscribe (machine-actionable POST endpoint) is mandatory alongside `List-Unsubscribe` under the same 2024 Gmail/Yahoo policy. Both headers are absent.

**`Authentication-Results` — NOT CAPTURED**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment status are unknown because the AgentMail relay did not forward the authentication results header. The sending domain `s.anthropologie.com` must be covered by the DMARC policy for `anthropologie.com`. Cannot confirm without raw headers from a delivered copy.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Cannot verify presence in truncated HTML; must be confirmed in the full template footer.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters — unverifiable**
All 23 CTAs route through click-redirect tracking URLs. Until those are resolved, UTM campaign/source/medium/content values cannot be audited. Expected UTM pattern for a TP1 welcome send: `utm_campaign=welcome_series&utm_medium=email&utm_source=anthropologie&utm_content=tp1`.

**Evergage dataset ID in URLs: `anus_prod`**
The production Evergage dataset identifier (`anus_prod`) appears verbatim in all recommendation image URLs. While functionally harmless, this string will appear in browser network tabs, server logs, and any URL-scanning tools — worth an internal naming review with the Evergage team to avoid accidental flags.
