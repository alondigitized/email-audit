# unexpected combos that feel right.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-voice editorial send from the maeve sub-label. It's a lookbook with almost no commerce. Four tall street-style images with lowercase captions ("unexpected combos very good ideas," "stripes + sequins surprisingly compatible") carry the whole top of the email. It looks good and the voice is consistent, but it reads like a magazine page, not a sales email.
- The only offer is a small orange "30% OFF Fall Entertaining, Candles & Decor" banner. It sits about a third of the way down, below the fashion content, and it's for home goods, not maeve clothing. A subscriber who opened for the "combos" story won't connect that banner to what they just read. Since Oct 6–12 the Feels Like Fall Sale was hitting the inbox almost daily, so I'd call this a brand-building break from that cadence. It's a welcome change in creative, but it leaves clicks on the table.
- Verdict: strong mood, weak conversion path. It works as a top-of-funnel brand touch and underperforms as a revenue send.

## What's working

- The "maeve" wordmark hero with a model reading on a ledge sets the brand identity immediately.
- The lowercase, deadpan captions give the email a distinct, consistent voice.
- The images are tall, styled and cropped consistently, and I see no broken images or layout bugs.
- It's a visible creative change from the near-identical sale blasts of Oct 8–12 (e.g. "Last call! Up to 30% off Maeve, Pilcro & more").
- The "More to explore" tiles (Holiday Party Shop, Maeve Dresses, Pants, Shoes) give clear category entry points.

## What's weak

- The only price offer, 30% off, is for home and entertaining, not the fashion content that fills the top of the email.
- The offer banner sits well below the fold, after four full-height images.
- The editorial images have no visible product names, prices or "shop this look" labels, so the content doesn't tell the reader what to buy.
- The only visible button is the small "Shop Now" in the orange banner.
- The email is very long, with "More to explore," a recommendation grid and a long footer stacked on top of the lookbook.
- Nothing creates urgency: no deadline, no code and no stock cue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Tie each look to product. Add a "shop the look" link and a price under every caption, and make each image tappable to that outfit.
- 2. Move the offer to the top. If 30% off applies to maeve, state it in a strip above the hero. If it's home-only, drop it from this send or label it clearly as a separate offer.
- 3. Replace the generic "Shop Now" with a specific button, such as "Shop the layering edit."
- 4. Cut the length. Keep the four looks and one category row, and drop the recommendation grid.
- 5. Add a deadline or a reason to act now, such as a seasonal "cold-weather layers" angle.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Links not verified.** 41 tracking links were skipped as click-redirect domains. Destination status, redirect chains, and expiry are unknown.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 9,111 of 11,821 characters (about 77%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. The plain-text part is dominated by long tracking URLs, which can add spam-filter weight and makes the text version hard to read.

  Add `alt=""` to both.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on content images.** These were flagged by QA:
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg` (twice)
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg` (twice)
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`

  Screen readers may announce the filenames, and nothing displays when images are blocked. If these are decorative, they need `alt=""`.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="... maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0"/>` blocks pinch-zoom on supporting clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 problem.
- **Empty `<title>`.** `<title></title>` leaves nothing for screen readers or web-view tabs.
- **Malformed Outlook font rule.** The `<!--[if mso]>` block contains `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important;`.
  - The empty list item makes the declaration invalid, so the browser drops it.
  - The `;` before `!important` also detaches the flag.
  - The intended Arial override for Outlook likely does not apply.
- **Quoted generic font fallbacks.** The stacks use `'sans-serif'` and `'serif'` in quotes, for example `'Nunito Sans', Helvetica, Arial, 'sans-serif'`. Quoted, these are read as font names, not generic keywords, so the generic fallback never applies.
- **Web fonts via `@import`.** The Google Fonts `@import` is stripped by Gmail and some other clients. These clients fall back to Helvetica or Arial.
- **Duplicate CSS rule.** `[style*="Font-Normal-os"]` is declared twice. This is harmless.
- **Truncated source.** Table structure, `role="presentation"`, `lang`, and dark-mode handling can't be assessed from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- **Identifier in a tracking URL.** The mon-trk pixel path ends in `003a700000uTzaxAAC`, which looks like a CRM contact ID (Salesforce `003` prefix). It is a subscriber-level identifier exposed in a third-party URL.
- **No visible merge tokens.** No unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens appear in the visible portion.

- **Not assessable.** The visible source is truncated before the footer, so the body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address can't be checked.
- **From identity.** `"maeve by Anthropologie" <anthropologie@s.anthropologie.com>` is a consistent sending subdomain. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment are unknown.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC from the raw message. The relay may be hiding them.
2. Add `alt` to the 5 flagged content images (descriptive, or `alt=""` if decorative). Add `alt=""` to both tracking pixels.

5. Populate `<title>`.

7. Re-run the QA with a resolving link checker (follow redirects on the click domain) and a full, untruncated HTML capture. That would cover the footer compliance elements and the UTM/landing-page continuity checks.
