# Extra 50% off sale breaks the internet, not the wallet 🙌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the same plaid-edged gift-tag hero Anthropologie has run all week, here with a new "breaks the internet" subject. The tag stacks three tiers: EXTRA 50% OFF SALE, 40% OFF OUR MOST-LOVED STYLES, and 30% OFF ALMOST EVERYTHING ELSE. Each tier has its own pink or red CTA. The hierarchy is clear and the hero reads fast. But three discount tiers and five buttons make the shopper decide before they've seen a product.
- Below the hero is a long scroll of eight category tiles in a 2-up grid. They cover sweaters, decor, shoes, dresses, sleepwear, furniture, jewelry, kitchen, beauty, wedding and Terrain. Each has a red caption bar. The imagery is polished, but nothing shows a price, a product name, or which discount tier applies. The "Might we recommend…" grid has product photos and star ratings but no names or prices.
- Judgment: a competent, on-brand Black Friday send. It's also close to a copy of the last 23. A subscriber who has seen this hero repeatedly has little reason to click again, so the novelty is gone even though the execution is clean.

## What's working

- The hero offer is instantly legible. "50% OFF" is the biggest type on a dark maroon tag against pink.
- The tiered stack separates sale, most-loved, and everything-else offers with different colors, so each reads as its own block.
- Category tiles are fashion-forward and cover a broad range, so most shoppers will find something relevant.
- The red caption bars keep the tile labels consistent and readable.
- "Online & in stores" is stated directly under the offer.

## What's weak

- The top banner text is cut off at both edges ("THE YEAR JUST GOT BETTER · THE BEST DEALS OF THE YE"). It's a marquee-style crop and looks sloppy.
- The hero has five CTAs: Shop Now, Shop Clothing, Shop Home & Furniture, Shop Gifts, plus tier labels. No single action stands out.
- The category tiles carry no price, discount tier, or product name, so a shopper can't tell what 40% versus 30% applies to.
- The "Might we recommend…" products have stars but no names, prices, or sale prices. That's wasted social proof.
- The email is very long, with a heavy fine-print block at the bottom. The asterisks on the offers ("40%**", "30%**") hint at exclusions that aren't explained in the visible hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Collapse the hero to one dominant CTA, such as "Shop 50% off sale," and make the 40% and 30% tiers a secondary line instead of three more buttons.
- 2. Put the discount tier on each category tile, for example "Sweaters · 40% off."
- 3. Add product name and sale price to the recommended grid. The ratings are already there, so show the price too.
- 4. Fix the marquee banner so the text fits inside the width or loops cleanly.
- 5. Change the creative from day to day. After a week of the same gift tag, a new hero for the final weekend would lift attention.

- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off sale, 40% off favorites, 30% off the rest`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sweaters, shoes, and sleepwear from 30% off. Online and in stores.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our best Black Friday deals are live. Shop before your size is gone.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, 3 content images** on `image.s.anthropologie.com`:
  - `482199364-emailbanner_pencilline_1.jpg`
  - `482199362-emailbanner_pencilline_2.jpg`
  - `1e313de8-0c8e-41c2-97a7-2d33a8cff42e.png`
- **Missing alt text, 2 tracking pixels:** the Dotomi and mon-trk pixels above. They should carry `alt=""` plus explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Malformed Outlook rule:** `body, table, td { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, , 'Font-Normal'; !important; }`.
  - The empty list item `, ,` and the stray `;` before `!important` make the declaration invalid or ignored in MSO.
  - The Outlook font override therefore may not apply.
- **Quoted generic keywords:** `'sans-serif'` and `'serif'` appear in quotes (e.g. `[style*="Font-Normal-ns"] { font-family: 'Nunito Sans', Helvetica, Arial, 'sans-serif' }`).
  - Quoted, they are treated as font names rather than generic fallbacks.
  - If the web font and Helvetica/Arial are unavailable, the generic fallback never fires.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0">` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>`.
- **No language attribute:** `<html xmlns=… xmlns:v=… xmlns:o=…>` has no `lang`.
- **Comment before the doctype:** `<!-- ****** ANTHROPOLOGIE TEMPLATE ****** -->` precedes `<!DOCTYPE html>`. This can push old IE and Outlook-web engines into quirks mode.
- **`@import` for Google Fonts** inside `<style>` is ignored by Gmail and some clients. The fallback stacks above matter because of this.
- **Template bloat:** the `<head>` carries CSS for other brands (UO, Free People, Beholden, Terrain). The `Font-Normal-os` rule is declared twice, and developer comments ship in the production HTML. This adds weight, which matters given Gmail's ~102KB clipping threshold. Total size isn't visible from the truncated source.

## 7. Recommendations

2. **Generic fonts:** unquote `sans-serif` and `serif` in all `Font-Normal-*` stacks.
3. **Alt text:** add descriptive `alt` to the 3 content images. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to the Dotomi and mon-trk pixels.
4. **Accessibility basics:** add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and fill in `<title>`. Remove `user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1.0`.
5. **Template cleanup:** move the template comment below the doctype. Strip dev comments and unused brand CSS, and check the total size against Gmail's clip limit.
6. **Headers:** confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` plus SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment from a raw-header capture, such as a direct Gmail "Show original", rather than the relay.
7. **Empty hash:** confirm `dtm_email_hash` populates on real sends. If it's empty only in the relay capture, no fix is needed.
