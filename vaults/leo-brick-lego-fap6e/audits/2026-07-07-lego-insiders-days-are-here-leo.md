# LEGO® Insiders Days are here, Leo!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is LEGO's campaign-launch send for Insiders Days (July 7–12), and it does the basics right: it's personalized, it lists real benefits, and it has a logical information hierarchy. But compared to the two emails that came just days before — the Koenigsegg Megacar reveal and the pinball machine drop — this one trades sharp narrative for a scattergun product grid. Those emails had a single hero product and a reason-to-care that was inseparable from the visual. This one leads with "6 days of extra special treatment" — language so generic it could belong to any loyalty program on earth.
- The product curation compounds the problem. Toy Story Slinky Dog Bookends alongside the Fast & Furious Toyota Supra MK4 alongside the S.H.I.E.L.D. Helicarrier is not a curated collection — it's a sample of inventory. For a campaign send that is supposed to feel like "your VIP head start," the product grid undercuts the promise the hero makes.
- The fundamentals hold: the email renders cleanly, the 2× points callout is legible, the member points balance is visible in the header, and the "3 ways to celebrate" benefit triptych is clear and scannable. This is competent. It is not the level of craft Leo got two days ago with the Koenigsegg teaser.

## What's working

- **Personalization is threaded throughout** — "Leo" appears in the subject, the footer member panel, and the points-balance header, making the member relationship feel real rather than decorative.
- **"3 ways to celebrate" triptych is the best section** — the 2× points / Insiders Price / Gifts with purchase cards are visually clean and give a member three distinct reasons to open the app or shop now.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — every product image loads, buttons are legible on the dark green hero, and the layout holds across the full email length.
- **Specific products with prices** — rather than a vague hero CTA to "the sale," four named sets with visible price tags give Leo something concrete to evaluate.

## What's weak

- **Hero headline is generic** — "6 days of extra special treatment — just for members" could run for any loyalty program. The email's actual offer (2×points + Insiders pricing + gift with purchase) is far more compelling than this opener communicates.
- **"Explore now" CTA is the weakest possible button copy** — after the Koenigsegg "flip it first" / "build the Megacar" specificity, a two-word generic CTA feels like a step backward.
- **Product grid is incoherent** — Toy Story bookends next to a Marvel Helicarrier next to a Harry Potter set next to a F&F Supra signals inventory clearance, not a curated Insiders Days showcase.
- **No deadline urgency in the hero** — the "6 days" window is mentioned in the subhead copy but doesn't appear on or near the CTA. A member who skims the hero and taps "Explore now" has no anchoring reason to act today vs. tomorrow.
- **Preview text repeats "Insiders Days"** — the body copy opens "It's a great time to be a member with the return of LEGO® Insiders Days," which re-lands the subject line's main phrase rather than extending it with a concrete hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the hero headline to lead with the actual offer.** "2× points on every Insiders Days purchase" is a specific, numerical, scannable promise. "6 days of extra special treatment" is not. The number should be in the hero, not buried in a grid below the fold.
- 2. **Put a deadline on the CTA button.** "Shop now — ends July 12" or "Shop 2× points — 6 days left" turns a passive invitation into a timed action without adding copy anywhere else.
- 3. **Pick one product hero, then grid.** The Koenigsegg and pinball emails worked because they led with a single object that made Leo want to keep reading. Lead with the set most likely to resonate with an adult collector Leo (the Supra or the Helicarrier), make it full-width, then grid the rest. One anchor product > four equals.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader so it doesn't echo the subject.** Lead with the concrete benefit and the end date.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Leo, 2× points start today — 6-day Insiders window`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Insiders Days head start: 2× points + exclusive pricing`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Double points on every purchase + Insiders pricing on hundreds of sets — now through July 12.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `6 days only: your 2× points window opened. Shop the Supra, Helicarrier, and more at Insiders prices.`

## Technical Audit — LEGO Insiders Days (2026-07-07)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a systemic accessibility gap (25 images missing `alt` text) and compliance flags around unsubscribe headers and physical address that represent deliverability and legal risk. The two BROKEN EXPERIENCE link failures are false positives from the QA probe hitting `<link rel="preconnect">` roots rather than actual broken content URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives — not real failures:**
- `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` are flagged as 404. These are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints, not fetchable URLs — the probe is hitting the domain root. The actual font stylesheet requests (with `?family=` query strings) are what matter.

**Real issue — redundant font loading:**
The email loads Noto Sans Display via three separate requests:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700&display=swap">
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans&display=swap');
```
The first `<link>` is a strict subset of the second and should be removed. The `@import` duplicates the weight-400 load via a slower CSS-parse-time request. This adds ~200–400ms render latency in clients that honor web fonts (Apple Mail, Outlook.com).

**Tracking links:** 31 click-redirect links skipped — destinations and UTM param integrity unverifiable from this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

Decorative images (`GreyBrick.png`, separator) should use `alt=""`. Content images (hero GIF, product cards, rewards image, logos) need descriptive alt text. In image-blocking clients (Outlook on Windows), all content rendered via these images is invisible with no fallback text.

**Mobile image swap pattern:** The hero GIF appears twice under `.mj-image-swap-mobile` / `.mj-image-swap-desktop` classes — both instances lack alt text, so the swap works but accessibility fails on both paths.

**Positive signals:** `<html lang="en">` present; `<meta name="viewport">` present; `<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` present; Outlook MSO conditional comments included.

### 5. Compliance

**High severity:**

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — flagged as not captured by AgentMail relay. Gmail has enforced one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (>5k/day) since Feb 2024. Missing this header risks inbox placement degradation and Gmail bulk sender policy violation.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — required for one-click unsubscribe compliance alongside the above.
- **No physical mailing address detected** — CAN-SPAM §7 requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not present in the captured HTML. If it exists below the truncation point, this clears; if not, it's a legal compliance gap.

**Medium severity:**

- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm `e.crm.lego.com` is authenticated. Sending domain should show `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass` in relay-forwarded headers.
- **Plain-text body is 82.7% URLs** (15,215 of 18,387 chars) — the plain-text alternative is effectively a URL dump, not human-readable. Some spam filters penalize this pattern.
