# Leo, we’ve been expecting you
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-built Early Access send. The Downton Abbey brick build sits in a diamond-patterned black-and-gold frame, and the headline "The castle doesn't open its doors to just anyone" is a good line. It makes Early Access feel like an invitation. After the broken sends on 9/23 and 9/25, it's good to see a clean render. It also stays on theme, with gold Art Deco dividers and a "More sets at your service" section that matches the period tone.
- It's a close cousin of yesterday's Star Wars Early Access send (10/1) and of the PlayStation send earlier today. That's three Insiders "you've been chosen" emails in about 48 hours, and the novelty is wearing thin. The subject line is also much weaker than the creative. "We've been expecting you" is charming but says nothing about LEGO, Downton Abbey, or a deadline until the person opens it.
- Overall this is a strong, honest, well-targeted send held back by a vague subject and thin offer detail. Four product tiles show piece counts and Insiders points but no prices. Everything below the hero is generic "Shop now."

## What's working

- The hero is clear and eye-catching. The Downton Abbey build is large, centred, and framed so the eye lands on it first.
- The headline and body copy tie the Early Access status to the recipient: "you've been granted Early Access… until the 3rd of October."
- The Downton Abbey styling carries through the gold dividers, the lifestyle strip and the minifigure line-up. It feels like one campaign.
- The product tiles show piece counts and "+Insiders Points" values, which is useful to a points-minded builder.
- The Insiders card and app-download block at the bottom reinforce the loyalty relationship.

## What's weak

- The subject line is cryptic. It names no product, brand moment, or deadline.
- No prices appear anywhere, and the points values are tiny and easy to miss. The only "offer" is access, with nothing concrete like double points or a gift with purchase.
- The four "More sets at your service" tiles (Tower Bridge, Bookstore, Shopping Street, Tudor Corner) have nothing to do with Downton Abbey. They read as filler, and every button just says "Shop now."
- The member card at the bottom shows "Points 0 / Value $0.00." That is a dead-end, mildly discouraging stat to show a subscriber.
- The cadence is heavy. This is the third Insiders early-access style email in two days (Star Wars 10/1, PlayStation 10/2, now this).

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to name the set and the deadline. The Downton Abbey hook is the best thing in the email and it's hidden.
- 2. Put a concrete perk in the hero, such as bonus points on this set, with the price and points value next to the CTA.
- 3. Make the button copy specific, for example "Get Downton Abbey early" instead of "Shop now."
- 4. Drop or relabel the four generic tiles. Show them as "More Icons-style builds for the Downton fan" or cut to one or two, each with a price.
- 5. Suppress the "0 points / $0.00" card for zero-balance members, or turn it into a prompt such as "Earn 2,000+ points with this set."
- 6. Space out the Early Access sends. Hold this one back, or fold it into a single weekly Insiders digest.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, MJML-style build with `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag, and dark-mode meta tags. The main confirmed defects are widespread missing alt text and an `http://` image. The compliance and authentication findings are unverified because the headers weren't captured and the HTML source was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **58 tracking links were not probed.** They are on click-redirect domains, so destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **Mixed content:** the "Insider Barcode" image loads over plain HTTP: `http://cl.s50.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABp7ImQiOjUwMTcs…`. It appears twice. Clients and proxies that block non-HTTPS images may hide the barcode, which is functional content. Serve it over `https://`.
- **Plain-text part:** 10,752 of 13,056 characters (about 82%) are URLs. This is a consequence of the long tracking URLs. It is a spam-filter and readability risk for the text alternative.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on about 30 `<img>` instances.** The QA check can't distinguish an absent `alt` attribute from an intentional `alt=""`. Affected sources, all under `https://image.e.crm.lego.com/lib/fe3b117175640479761271/m/1/`:
  - `LEGOInsiderLogowhitelog.png`
  - `01-Project-Spider-EarlyAccess-Email-202610-Logo-Large.png` and `…Logo-Small.png`
  - `02-…Hero-Banner-Tall.*` (×2) and `04-…Hero-Banner-Short*` (×2)
  - `05-…Hero-Banner-Short*` (×2)
  - `GreyBrick.png` and `Footer-Products_GenericPoints_Colour.png` (×8 each)
  - `Footer_TopSeparator_Desktop_Member-Non-Member_Light.png` (×3)
  - `Logo+Container+logo+white.png`
- **Images that carry content:** the hero banners and logos likely carry the message. With images off, a screen reader or image-blocking client gets nothing. Add descriptive alt text.
- **Decorative images:** `GreyBrick.png`, the footer points graphic and the separators should use an explicit `alt=""`.
- **Duplicated images:** the Tall/Short hero banners are each used twice, which fits desktop/mobile swap blocks (`.mj-image-swap-mobile` / `.mj-image-swap-desktop`, `.hide-on-desktop`, `.show-on-desktop`). Both copies are in the DOM, so screen readers may announce the content twice unless the hidden copy is `display:none`. This is unconfirmed from the truncated source.
- **Web fonts:** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/…')` inside `<style>` and `<link>` font tags are stripped by Gmail and some other clients. The fallback stack (`'Noto Sans', Cera Pro, Arial, sans-serif`) is present, so this is low risk. The proprietary `LEGO Typewell` face will not load in most clients.
- **`<title>`:** the value `LEGO` is generic and has no accessibility value.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The subject renders as "Leo, we've been expecting you", and no unresolved merge tokens are visible in the supplied source. The source is truncated, so the footer and body can't be fully checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed. Verify at the sending ESP (the `exct.net` / `e.crm.lego.com` infrastructure suggests Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **Physical address:** not detected in the supplied HTML. The footer was cut off by truncation, so this needs a check against the full source.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain `e.crm.lego.com` aligns with the image and link domains, which is a good sign but not proof.
- **Unsubscribe link in body:** not verifiable in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. All 58 click-tracking links were skipped, and no UTM parameters or final landing URLs appear in the available data. A manual click-through is needed to confirm that the CTAs ("Project Spider" early access) land on matching pages and carry campaign parameters.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the logos and hero banners, and use `alt=""` on decorative images (`GreyBrick.png`, footer graphics, separators).
2. Serve the Insider Barcode image over HTTPS (`cl.s50.exct.net` → `https://`).
3. Confirm at the ESP that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are sent, and that the physical address is in the footer. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml` to see the headers.
4. Check the raw headers for SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
5. Hide the duplicate mobile/desktop image copies from assistive tech (`display:none`, `aria-hidden`) and give the title a meaningful value.
6. Reduce the URL weight in the plain-text part, for example with shorter tracking URLs.
7. Re-run the link probe with a redirect-following resolver for the 58 tracked links, and record the landing URLs and UTM values.
