# Leo, we saved you a slice 🍰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a birthday trigger email — one of LEGO's highest-ROI automations — and it earns its send. The three-act structure works cleanly: celebrate the subscriber, stoke nostalgia with a real vintage set they'd remember (the 2005 Star Wars Death Star II, item 10143, 3,449 pieces), then close with a birthday discount code gated at $75+. The personalized subject line and the "we saved you a slice" cake metaphor running through hero-to-CTA is the best creative continuity this brand has shown in recent sends.
- The main drag is execution tension, not concept. The promo code ("4NXVWURMKA") lives below two full content blocks — subscribers have to scroll to find the offer the subject line promised. The nostalgia Star Wars block is charming but inert: it shows a retired 2005 set with no buy link, no "back in stock" hook, and no bridge to anything Leo can actually purchase today.
- One timing note: this lands on June 29, the same day as the double-points deadline email ("Ready to double the fun?"). Two LEGO sends in a single day — even when both are individually justified — compounds inbox pressure and dilutes the birthday moment.

## What's working

- **Subject line wordplay is genuinely good** — "we saved you a slice" is a double entendre (birthday cake + savings) that earns the open without being clickbait.
- **Personalization runs all the way through** — "Leo" in the subject, the hero copy written to you, and the birthday minifig feel cohesive rather than bolted on.
- **Star Wars nostalgia block is emotionally resonant** — showing the Death Star II with exact specs (2005, 3,449 pieces, item 10143) signals LEGO knows its adult audience and respects their collector identity.
- **Promo code is clean and visible** — large, legible code with clear $75 minimum threshold; no buried-asterisk fine print visible.
- **"Your cake's waiting for you" CTA section** ties the birthday metaphor to the actual offer, making the CTA feel earned.

## What's weak

- **Offer is buried below the fold** — the promo code doesn't appear until the third content block; subscribers who disengage early never see the payoff the subject promised.
- **Nostalgia block leads nowhere** — the 2005 Death Star II is a retired set. There's no link, no "see what's available now," no bridge to a purchasable product. Charm without conversion.
- **Discount amount is not clearly legible** — the exact savings figure in the offer block is too small to read at mobile screenshot scale; "save" with a $75 minimum threshold but no stated $ or % off is weaker than it should be.
- **Same-day cadence collision** — sending two emails on June 29 (this + double-points deadline) undermines the birthday send's specialness and inflates opt-out risk.
- **"Get yours" CTA copy is soft** — for a birthday moment with a clear promo code, "Claim your birthday gift" or "Use your birthday code" would be more specific and more urgent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the offer above the nostalgia block** — or at minimum add the code and minimum threshold to the hero section so the payoff matches the subject's promise without scrolling.
- 2. **Make the discount amount unmissable** — whatever the actual $ or % off is, it should be in large type inside the CTA block, not buried in sentence-case fine print.
- 3. **Give the nostalgia block a forward link** — "This set retired in 2007 — see what's out now" or a link to the current adult-collector lineup converts an emotional moment into a click.
- 4. **Strengthen the CTA copy** — replace "Get yours" with "Claim your birthday code" to match the subject's promise and create a closed loop.
- 5. **Space birthday sends away from transactional deadline emails** — if the double-points send had to go June 29, queue this birthday send for June 30 or the actual birthday day.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Leo, your birthday gift is ready 🎁`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Happy birthday, Leo — here's something from us`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your exclusive code + a trip down memory lane`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use code 4NXVWURMKA — no strings, just birthday perks`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with proper responsive scaffolding, dark mode meta tags, and Outlook conditional comments. The primary actionable issues are a duplicate Google Fonts request, two images missing `alt` attributes, and unconfirmed compliance headers (unsubscribe, physical address, authentication) that may be suppressed by the AgentMail relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Likely false positives — not real breaks:**
The QA tool flagged `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` as 404s. These are CDN root domains, not fetchable pages; the link checker is probing the apex rather than an actual stylesheet URL. The `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display...">` stylesheet paths are what matter and are not flagged broken.

**Real issue — duplicate font request:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Two separate requests load Noto Sans Display. The second request already includes `wght@400;700`, making the first redundant. This adds a needless round-trip on load.

**25 tracking links skipped:**
Click-redirect domains were not probed. Destination URLs and UTM parameter consistency are unverified (see Section 6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (2 instances — same image, appears twice):**
```
src: https://image.e.crm.lego.com/lib/fe3b117175640479761271/m/1/
     02_Lifecycle_Birthday-Journey_1_50x50_Offer_v2.png
```
No `alt` attribute is present on either instance. Given the filename (`50x50_Offer_v2`), this is likely a decorative offer badge or icon. If it conveys offer value (e.g., a discount percentage), the missing alt text breaks screen reader accessibility and fails WCAG 2.1 AA. If purely decorative, `alt=""` should be set explicitly.

**Positive findings:**
- `<html lang="en">` is set correctly
- `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">` are present for dark mode
- `<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">` prevents unwanted iOS auto-linking
- Outlook conditional CSS (`mj-outlook-group-fix`) is in place
- Responsive breakpoint at 599px with appropriate mobile overrides

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**List-Unsubscribe / RFC 8058 (one-click):**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers were captured by the QA relay. LEGO is a high-volume sender; Gmail and Yahoo Mail now require one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) for bulk senders. These headers must be present at the envelope level — confirm they exist in the raw SMTP headers before delivery, not just the HTML.

**Physical address:**
No postal/mailing address was detected in the visible HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the body. Confirm it appears below the fold in the untruncated source (common in footer); if absent, this is a compliance violation.

**Authentication-Results:**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status was not captured via the AgentMail relay (`Authentication-Results` header missing from QA output). Sender domain is `e.crm.lego.com` (ESP subdomain). Verify DKIM is signed on this subdomain and DMARC policy on `lego.com` covers it — particularly important given Gmail's 2024 bulk sender enforcement.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The 25 click-redirect links were not probed, so UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit. The following should be verified manually or via a dedicated link-expansion pass:

- All CTAs carry consistent UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` at minimum)
- Birthday offer CTA lands on a page that reflects the same offer presented in the email (no generic homepage redirect)
- No tracking redirect chains that could inflate redirect hop count or trigger spam filters
