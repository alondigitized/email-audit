# These sets are trending
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- "These sets are trending" is a standard product-curation email that executes the fundamentals competently — four adult-collector sets laid out clearly with individual "Shop now" CTAs and a wish-list nudge at the bottom. The set selection is genuinely interesting: SEGA Genesis, Shrek, Fast & Furious Toyota Supra MK4, and Water Lilies hit four distinct collector fandoms in one send.
- The email's problem is that it's a digital leaflet with no offer, no urgency, and no editorial spine. It asserts these sets are "trending" but shows no evidence: no social signal, no sold-out badge, no member add-to-cart count. Compared to the June 1 "best sets for adults" send — which led with a clear editorial angle and IP-licensed lineup — this one arrives empty-handed and feels like inventory push.
- Critically, this lands on June 25 with Father's Day barely in the rearview and summer gifting season wide open. That cultural moment goes completely unused.

## What's working

- **Set selection hits four distinct adult collector niches.** SEGA Genesis (nostalgia gaming), Shrek (pop culture animation), Fast & Furious Supra (automotive), and Water Lilies (botanical) — a defensible, interesting shortlist.
- **Product cards are clean and scannable.** Each has image, set name, star ratings, price indicator, and a "Shop now" button. No guessing.
- **Visual hierarchy holds from top to bottom.** The Indiana Jones-style minifigure hero anchors the eye, the yellow "Right this way!" transition banner reframes as discovery, products follow, then membership tip closes.
- **No render bugs.** All images load, layout is intact, text is readable throughout.
- **Wish-list membership tip is a smart passive-retention touch** for subscribers who aren't ready to buy today.

## What's weak

- **No offer at all.** No discount, no points multiplier, no member-exclusive access. The June 1 adults email justified opens with strong editorial framing; this one offers no equivalent pull.
- **"These sets are trending" is the weakest subject in recent LEGO history.** It names no product, no brand, and gives no reason to open today vs. deleting. It's a step backward from even "LEGO® secrets enclosed..." from May 25.
- **Four products, zero editorial hierarchy.** No "our pick," no "bestseller," no distinction between a $50 botanical set and a $250 console. The SEGA Genesis is the cultural anchor here — it should be the undisputed hero.
- **No seasonal hook.** Father's Day just passed, summer is peak collectibles-gifting season, and none of that is in the email. Feels like a scheduled blast, not a campaign.
- **"Shop now" ×4 is inert.** Identical CTA copy across four products collapses all signal into noise.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the SEGA Genesis the hero.** Build the above-the-fold story around one set — close-up, piece count, cultural nod ("It even has working cartridge slots") — and move the other three to a secondary strip. One product in, one click out.
- 2. **Add a member offer.** "Earn 2× Insiders points on these sets this weekend" converts the email from browsing prompt to action trigger. The June 20 wrap-up showed each member's point balance — reference it here.
- 3. **Replace the trend claim with observable proof.** "X Insiders added this to their wish list this week" or a "Back in stock" badge on the Supra makes the "trending" premise land. Right now it's an assertion with no evidence.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTA copy per product.** "Build the Genesis" / "Shop the Supra" / "Shop Botanicals" beats four identical buttons and makes the email feel curated, not templated.
- 5. **Add a summer gifting frame.** Even a one-line "your summer project starts here" or "treat-yourself season" header would have made this feel timely rather than evergreen.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — "These sets are trending" | LEGO® News

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a well-structured MJML-style HTML build with solid responsive scaffolding, but has meaningful compliance gaps (missing CAN-SPAM physical address, unverifiable unsubscribe headers) and a pervasive accessibility problem across 22 images lacking alt text. The QA pipeline's false-positive 404s on Google Fonts CDN endpoints are a tooling artifact, not real breakage.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Google Fonts false positives** — The QA checker flagged `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` as 404s. These are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints, not fetchable HTML pages; a HEAD/GET to the bare domain returns a non-200 status by design. This is a probe methodology issue in the QA tool, not broken email infrastructure.

**Redundant Google Fonts requests** — The `<head>` loads `Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700` twice via two separate `<link>` tags, then reimports `Noto+Sans` via `@import` in `<style>`. That's three separate font requests for what could be one:
```
https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700
https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700   ← supersedes above
@import 'https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans'
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks rendering until the sheet resolves and is the slowest possible font loading strategy. In email clients that honor web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail), this adds measurable load latency.

**32 tracking links skipped** — QA correctly deferred probing click-redirect domains. No broken destinations confirmed, but they cannot be cleared without a live probe through the ESP's redirect chain.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on 22 images** — Every repeated instance of the following images has an empty or missing `alt` attribute:

| Image | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| `Generic_Novelty_Hero_Template-3_Option-1.png` | 2 |
| `GreyBrick.png` | 8 |
| `01_Email-Footer_Member_Transactional_Points-Icon.png` | 8 |
| `MembershipTip_1.png` | 2 |
| `00_Email-Footer_Member_Marketing_Top.png` | 2 |

`GreyBrick.png` and the footer icons appear to be decorative spacers/icons — those should carry `alt=""` explicitly. Hero and membership tip images are content-bearing and require descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients.

**Commented-out CSS** — Two `NotoB` font-family rules are commented out:
```css
/* [style*="NotoB"] { font-family: 'Noto Sans Display', sans-serif; } */
```
If any live elements use `style="...NotoB..."` as a selector hook (which this CSS pattern implies), those elements silently lose their bold font in clients that don't support the inline `LEGO Typewell` font. Needs audit against live element `style` attributes.

**Duplicate `@media (max-width: 599px)` blocks** — Three separate `@media screen and (max-width: 599px)` blocks exist in `<style>`. While technically valid, this increases parse time and makes maintenance error-prone. No confirmed rendering breakage, but worth consolidating.

**`input { display: none !important; }`** — Hides all `<input>` elements globally. If any interactive fallback elements are present (dark mode toggles, checkbox hacks), they are suppressed unconditionally.

### 5. Compliance

**Physical address — FAIL** — QA found no physical mailing address in the email body. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. If it exists only in an image (e.g., footer graphic), it will be invisible to image-blocked recipients and does not satisfy the requirement. Address must appear as live text.

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — UNVERIFIABLE** — QA reports the header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. This is likely a relay artifact (headers stripped before capture), not necessarily absent from the original send. However, if genuinely missing: Gmail and Yahoo Mail require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders as of February 2024; absence risks inbox demotion or bulk folder placement.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — UNVERIFIABLE** — Same relay caveat. One-click unsubscribe is now required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. If absent from original headers, this is a deliverability risk.

**Authentication-Results — UNVERIFIABLE** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown due to relay stripping. The sending domain `e.crm.lego.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain (Salesforce Marketing Cloud pattern); DKIM alignment to `lego.com` parent domain should be confirmed directly with the ESP.
