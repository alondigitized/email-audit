# Ready to double the fun?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a last-day loyalty-drive email — its one job is to convert Insiders members who haven't yet shopped during the double-points window before it closes tonight, June 29. The "Seeing double, Leo?" hero framing is the email's best move: it surfaces the 2x offer immediately, uses the member's first name, and gives a reason to act right now. The four product tiles below may be wish-list recommendations, which would be genuinely smart personalization if true.
- Where the email fails is on urgency transparency. The promo ends today, but the subject line says nothing about it. "Ready to double the fun?" is a pleasant pun — it is not a last-day alarm. Subscribers who skim the header copy will miss that the offer closes in hours. Compare this to the June 20 send ("Leo, check out this month's membership wrap-up"), which put the member name directly in the subject; this email drops that advantage with no urgency to replace it.
- The product mix also works against the email. Two Toy Story sets, a Cosmos Flowers botanical, and a FIFA World Cup 2026 trophy share equal tile weight below the hero. Unless these are truly personalized to Leo's browsing history, the lack of curation cohesion undercuts the "that set you've had your eye on just got more tempting" promise in the hero copy. The June 1 "best LEGO sets for adults" send had a clear editorial angle; this one reads like a recommendations-engine dump.

## What's working

- **"Seeing double, Leo?"** is the sharpest line in the email — personal, punny, and tied directly to the offer in one read.
- **The 2x badge** overlaid on the hero image makes the offer scannable without reading a single word of copy.
- **"Win Your Wish List" purple block** adds a second engagement hook — a sweepstakes feel that rewards subscribers just for having a wish list.
- **FIFA World Cup 2026 Official Emblem set** is culturally timely; including it earns seasonal credibility the rest of the email lacks.
- **Clean render throughout** — all four product images load, layout holds on mobile, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Subject says nothing about the deadline.** A last-day promotional email with "Ready to double the fun?" in the subject is a missed opportunity — the most urgency-driving detail (the offer expires today) is buried in body copy.
- **"Leo" disappeared from the subject.** The June 20 send opened with "Leo, check out this month's membership wrap-up" and scored an 8. This regression to generic copy is unexplained.
- **Four identical "Shop now" CTAs** give the subscriber no decision help; every product has the same button copy and the same visual weight.
- **Product cohesion is weak.** Toy Story × 2, botanical flowers, and a soccer trophy share the same section header ("Get a new set at your fingertips") with no editorial logic tying them together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the deadline.** This is a last-day email. The subject should say so.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Leo, last day to earn double points`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2× points expire tonight — shop now`
- 2. **Set a preheader that sells the deadline, not the vibe.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Insiders double-points window closes tonight, June 29.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every purchase counts double until midnight — pick your set.`
- 3. **Add a visual countdown or bold "TODAY ONLY" callout in the hero.** The dates "06/19–06/29" appear in body copy but don't read as urgent. A countdown timer or a red "Last Day" pill on the hero button would convert deadline-aware shoppers.
- 4. **Break one product out as a featured hero recommendation.** Instead of four equal tiles, surface one set as "Your Most-Wished Item" with a larger image and richer copy — gives subscribers a single default click target rather than a decision paralysis grid.
- 5. **Differentiate the CTA copy per product.** "Build the Trophy" / "Shop Cosmos Flowers" beats five identical "Shop now" buttons and captures what's actually in the catalog.

## Technical Audit — LEGO "Ready to double the fun?"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no broken content links, but carries compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) that will degrade inbox placement with major providers, widespread missing alt text that breaks the fallback experience when images are blocked, and a redundant/partially-broken Google Fonts loading strategy. 33% pass rate on automated checks confirms real structural debt.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives — do not action:**
- QA flagged `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` as 404. These are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints, not navigable URLs. A HEAD/GET probe against a preconnect origin will return 4xx — this is expected and not a bug.

**Real issue — 31 tracked links unverifiable:**
- All 31 CTA links pass through a click-redirect domain and were skipped by the prober. Destination URLs, UTM parameters, and landing page health cannot be confirmed from the source alone. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts — triple load, two methods incompatible with major clients:**

The email loads Noto Sans via three separate declarations:
1. Two `<link>` tags in `<head>` (one redundant — both load `Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700`, second adds `wght@400`):
   ```html
   <link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap" ...>
   <link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700;400&display=swap" ...>
   ```
2. An `@import` inside `<style>`:
   ```css
   @import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans&display=swap');
   ```

Outlook strips `<link>` tags from `<head>` entirely. Gmail strips `@import`. The fallback stack (`LEGO Typewell, 'Noto Sans', Cera Pro, Arial`) handles graceful degradation, but the first `<link>` tag is redundant and should be removed; the `@import` is dead code in most clients.

**`NotoB` font-family rule commented out:**
```css
/* [style*="NotoB"] { font-family: 'Noto Sans Display', sans-serif; } */
```
The `NotoR` rule is live, `NotoB` is disabled. If any element uses `style*="NotoB"` in the HTML body, it will receive no custom font. Verify no elements rely on this selector.

**Three duplicate `@media (max-width: 599px)` blocks:**
The `<style>` block declares the same breakpoint three times. While browsers/clients merge them correctly, it increases parse time and creates maintenance risk (specificity conflicts are harder to trace). Consolidate into one block.

**Missing alt text — 23 images affected:**

Every decorative and functional image in the send lacks `alt` attributes. When images are blocked (common in Outlook/corporate environments), the email renders as a blank white canvas. Affected images include the hero, product shots, footer icons, and dividers. The two most critical:

- `01_20260615_Q2_2xPoints_Reveal_NonInsiders_Hero.png` — hero (appears twice, suggesting desktop/mobile swap)
- `01_Email-Footer_Member_Transactional_Points-Icon.png` — appears 7× in footer rows

Decorative spacers (`GreyBrick.png`) should use `alt=""` (empty, not missing). Functional images need descriptive text.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — FAIL for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 requirements:
- `List-Unsubscribe` not detected by AgentMail relay capture
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) also absent

Gmail requires both for senders exceeding 5,000/day to Gmail addresses. Absence will suppress inbox placement and may trigger bulk folder routing. This is the highest-priority fix.

Note: Headers may be injected by the ESP at send time and not present in the raw HTML source — confirm with the ESP (Salesforce Marketing Cloud based on the `e.crm.lego.com` send domain) that these headers are present in actual delivered messages.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown:**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured via AgentMail relay. Confirm DKIM signing is active on `e.crm.lego.com` and SPF record covers the sending IP range. Without verification, deliverability risk is unquantifiable.

**Plain-text body is 85.5% URLs** (11,635/13,600 chars):
The multipart plain-text alternative is nearly entirely tracking/image URLs. Spam filters treat high URL-density plain text as a signal. The plain-text part should contain readable content mirroring the HTML, not a URL dump.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from truncated source — confirm a physical mailing address appears in the footer.
