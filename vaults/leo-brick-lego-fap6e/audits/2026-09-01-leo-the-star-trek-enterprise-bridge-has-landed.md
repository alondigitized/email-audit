# Leo, the Star Trek Enterprise Bridge has landed
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is LEGO's new-set-drop email for the Star Trek Enterprise Bridge, timed to "Star Trek 60" anniversary branding, and it's the cleanest, most confident send in this recent LEGO stretch. One hero product, one clear scene-setting image, a genuinely fun tagline ("Live long and prosper"), and specific narrative detail (captain's chair, turbo lift doors, eight minifigures including Kirk, Spock, Uhura) — that's real copy, not filler. It reads like a proper launch, not a catalog dump.
- The catch: there's no price, no piece count on the hero set itself, no discount, and no urgency — this is pure new-release awareness, not a conversion push. For a $250+ impulse-resistant set, that's a real gap; the "other builders love these sets" cross-sell grid (also all full-price, no offers) compounds it. Compared to the 2026-08-12 Ministry of Magic teaser (7/10, which had a code and countdown), this send trades urgency for pure product theater — good creative, weak commercial mechanics.
- Verdict: strong creative, on-brand IP tie-in, clear single CTA — but zero purchase incentive drags it down from what could've been the best send of the batch.

## What's working

- Single, well-composed hero image of the Bridge set with visible minifigures (Kirk, Spock, Uhura) sets the scene immediately.
- "Live long and prosper" as the lead-in is an on-brand, recognizable hook for the Star Trek audience.
- Copy is specific — captain's chair, turbo lift doors, eight minifigures — not generic "new set alert" filler.
- Clear single primary CTA ("Shop now") sits right under the pitch, no scroll needed.
- "STAR TREK 60" anniversary lockup gives the send a credible occasion/reason-to-buy beyond "new toy exists."

## What's weak

- No price or piece count shown for the hero set itself — a real gap for a big-ticket adult collector purchase.
- Zero discount, bundle, or points multiplier — nothing that rewards Insiders members specifically, despite the loyalty card footer.
- Cross-sell grid ("Other builders love these sets") is just more full-price product with no offer, diluting the single-hero focus that made the top half strong.
- No urgency or release-window framing (pre-order? in stock now? limited?) — the read is "this exists," not "act now."

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price and piece count directly under the hero CTA — buyers of a $250+ set need that before clicking, and its absence is the single biggest drag on click-through.
- 2. Give Insiders members a concrete reason to act now — a bonus-points multiplier or early-access window tied to the anniversary framing, not just a points balance display.
- 3. Cut the four-item cross-sell grid to one or two directly Star Trek/sci-fi-adjacent picks (the Enterprise NCC-1701-D is a fit; Tintin Moon Rocket and Project Hail Mary dilute the theme) to keep focus on the hero.
- 4. Add a release-status line ("Available now" / "Pre-order ships [date]") to convert awareness into urgency.
