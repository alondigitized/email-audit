# Leo, celebrate the new LEGO® PlayStation™
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the best-looking LEGO send in the recent run. It leads with a clean grey PlayStation 1 brick build in a diamond-patterned hero. The "Early Access" key badge and "Gamers: Uncover your next build!" make the gaming angle obvious within a second. Everything renders: no broken images, no empty tiles. That makes it a clear step up from the 2026-09-25 and 2026-09-23 sends, which both landed at 3/10 because of broken price tiles.
- It also tries to do too much. The email carries three separate offers: Early Access, an Astro Bot gift with purchase, and a PlayStation Plus trial for 400 points. A second row then pushes Donkey Kong and Pinball sets. The Astro Bot gift is the real hook, but it sits more than halfway down the first scroll and needs a $150 spend. The hero never shows a price or piece count for the PlayStation set itself.
- Overall it's a strong gaming send with a muddled offer stack. Leo, a points-collecting Insider, gets real member value here. One clear offer above the fold would take it from good to excellent.

## What's working

- The hero is on-theme and eye-catching: a large PlayStation brick build, and the Early Access badge signals Insider status.
- Personalized copy ("Leo, ready for gaming excitement?") ties the send to the recipient's Insider membership.
- The Astro Bot gift-with-purchase has a credible window (10/01–10/07, while supplies last) and a strong blue panel.
- The member value is explicit: the 400-point PlayStation Plus trial, and +1300 / +1495 points on the product tiles.
- The layout is clean and renders without breakage. The closing Insiders card with barcode reinforces the loyalty frame.

## What's weak

- Three competing offers dilute the message, and the strongest one (Astro Bot) is buried below the hero.
- The hero "Shop now" is generic, and the PlayStation set shows no price, piece count, or points value.
- The Insiders points widget shows "Points 0 / Value $0.00", which is a deflating message for a loyalty-driven pitch.
- "Early Access starting the 10/01" arrives on 10/02, so the urgency reads stale.
- The $150 threshold and "while supplies last" sit in small text on the Astro Bot panel, with an asterisk to the footer.
- The Donkey Kong and Pinball tiles are off-topic for a PlayStation send. They read as filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Astro Bot gift into the hero: "Free Astro Bot with $150+ through 10/07." It's the only concrete offer, so lead with it.
- 2. Show the PlayStation set's price, piece count, and Insider points in the hero. Change the CTA to "Build the PlayStation set".
- 3. Cut the PlayStation Plus trial or fold it into a single reward line. Keep the second product row to two or fewer tiles, and only if it's PlayStation-related.
- 4. Hide the "Points 0 / $0.00" widget when the balance is zero. Replace it with "Earn 1,300+ points on this set".
- 5. Send the Early Access email on launch day, or reword it to "Now available".

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Early Access to LEGO® PlayStation™ is open`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $150+ by 10/07 and Astro Bot ships free with your order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Insiders earn bonus points on the PlayStation build. Shop now.`

# Technical Audit: "Leo, celebrate the new LEGO® PlayStation™"

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`exct.net`) build with responsive CSS, dark-mode meta tags and a merged first name in the subject. The QA run flags widespread missing image alt text and two insecure `http://` image sources. The compliance and authentication warnings are most likely capture limits of the relay, not defects. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, unsubscribe and body-link checks are limited to the QA data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 35 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are on tracking domains. Their destinations are unverified, so this is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- The QA run reports 0 broken links. It only probed non-tracking URLs, so that is not evidence the tracked links resolve.
- The plain-text part is more than 70% URLs (6685 of 8985 chars). This is consistent with long tracked URLs and can add to spam-filter scoring. It is not a rendering defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** QA flagged about 20 `<img>` instances. Distinct assets:
  - Hero banners: `01_Project-P26-EA-0110-Hero-Banner-Tall.gif`, `04_Project-P26-EA-0110-Hero-Banner-Short.png`
  - Reward block: `05_Project-P26-EA-0110-Reward-ContentBlock.png`
  - Product images: `72051_Prod.png`, `blte7ecfac1aa09d7c5-11374_Prod_en-gb.png`
  - Footer: `LEGOInsiderLogowhitelog.png`, `Logo+Container+logo+white.png`, `GreyBrick.png` (×4), `Footer-Products_GenericPoints_Colour.png` (×4), `Footer_TopSeparator_Desktop_Member-Non-Member_Light.png` (×3)
  
  The hero, reward and product images carry the content, so with images blocked they render with no text equivalent. The footer separators and bricks may be decorative. If so, they should use an explicit `alt=""`, which the check cannot distinguish from a missing attribute.
- **Non-HTTPS image:** The "Insider Barcode" image loads from `http://cl.s50.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=...` (appears twice). Gmail proxies images, but Outlook and other clients may block or warn on mixed content. The barcode is a personalized, functional element.
- **Font loading:** The `<style>` block uses `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/...')` and `<link>` tags to Google Fonts. Gmail and some other clients strip these. The stack falls back to `Arial, sans-serif`, so this is low risk.
- **Hide/show rules:** Mobile and desktop show/hide rules rely on `!important` and `max-height`. These are standard, and I found no problems in the visible portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The subject renders "Leo," with no unresolved tokens. The truncated HTML shows no `%%`, `{{` or `[FIRST]` tokens, but the body past the CSS was not available. The barcode image, which is a LiveContent personalized asset, can't be confirmed for this recipient from the source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is unconfirmed, so it is not a verified defect. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both headers, so check them against the raw original message.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA note attributes this to the relay.
- **Unsubscribe link and physical address:** Not verifiable, because the footer was cut off in the truncated source.
- **From address:** `Noreply@e.crm.lego.com` is a no-reply sender on a dedicated CRM subdomain, so replies are not possible. No technical failure.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Product images link to `www.lego.com/cdn/cs/set/...?canvas=200:125`. These are CDN asset URLs, not landing pages.
- UTM parameters cannot be assessed. The links are wrapped in tracking redirects (35 skipped) and the destination URLs were not exposed in the QA data or the truncated source. No continuity issues are confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero banners, reward block and product images. Use `alt=""` explicitly on purely decorative footer images (`GreyBrick.png`, separators).
2. Serve the Insider barcode over HTTPS (`https://cl.s50.exct.net/...`) and add alt text such as "Insider barcode".
3. Check the raw `.eml` for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, plus SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment for `e.crm.lego.com`, before treating the compliance warnings as real.
4. Re-run the audit on the full HTML, or the final rendered links, to verify the unsubscribe link, physical address and UTM parameters.
5. Optionally, trim the plain-text part so it has more readable text and fewer long tracking URLs.
