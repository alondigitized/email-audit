# The best LEGO® sets for adults
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a cleanly executed product-curation email that leans hard into the adult LEGO fan segment — and correctly so. The "Adults Welcome" hero framing, the IP-licensed lineup (Mandalorian, Jurassic Park Jeep, DC-3 Pan Am Airliner, Mario Kart/Luigi), and the member-callout section all reinforce a coherent positioning. The render is clean, the product grid is easy to scan, and the brand presentation is on-model.
- The critical miss: there is no price, no discount, and no member-exclusive benefit anywhere in the email. After five LEGO Insiders emails in 17 days, recipients have learned this brand's pattern — another browse-and-discover send without a compelling offer will produce diminishing returns. The May 25 wish-list push was soft; this June 1 follow-up needed to convert harder.
- The wish-list module at the bottom is copy-pasted directly from the May 25 send. It undercuts the adult-curation narrative and reads as filler. Cut it or replace it with a member-price hook.

## What's working

- "Adults Welcome" hero framing immediately validates the segment and justifies opening.
- IP selection is excellent — Mandalorian, Jurassic Park, Pan Am Airliner, Mario Kart/Luigi all track authentic adult collector interests.
- Purple product-grid section creates a strong visual break that makes the four sets easy to scan.
- "Members can't get enough of these sets" is a soft social-proof signal that reinforces Insiders identity.
- Clean render: no broken images, no overlapping text, product shots are crisp.

## What's weak

- No price, no discount, no member-exclusive offer — there is nothing to act on except "shop anyway."
- "Discover now" hero CTA is vague: discover what, exactly? It competes with four "Shop now" buttons below it and loses.
- Wish-list module appears verbatim from the May 25 send — templated recycling that dilutes the editorial feel of the adult curation angle.
- No product detail visible (no piece count, no pricing, no "members pay X") — a set like the DC-3 PAN AM Airliner deserves context to close the gap between interest and purchase intent.
- Five sends in 17 days is aggressive cadence; without a tangible offer payoff, this one risks training subscribers to ignore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a member offer.** Even a "Double points this week on adult sets" or a visible price on the hero product would give this email a reason to exist beyond content marketing. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Replace "Discover now" with a specific CTA** — "Browse sets for adults" or "See the full lineup" anchors the action.
- 3. **Cut the wish-list module.** It was in the May 25 send. It has no connection to the adult-curation angle. Replace with a piece-count or "Available now" label under one of the four sets.
- 4. **Add one product detail per set** — piece count or MSRP under each item lifts purchase intent without requiring extra copy.
- 5. **Personalize the subject** — even segment-level ("For LEGO Insiders") would lift open rate.

## Technical Audit — LEGO "Best Sets for Adults" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders with solid structural hygiene (HTML 4.01 Transitional, `lang="en"`, dark-mode meta tags), but has three confirmed compliance gaps in transport headers, two accessibility deficits, a duplicate font load, and a plain-text body composition that will score poorly with spam filters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives (QA artifact):**
- `fonts.googleapis.com` and `fonts.gstatic.com` flagged as 404 are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints, not navigable URLs. HTTP probing a preconnect host directly returns non-200; the QA checker treats these as broken links. No real breakage — but the checker should exclude preconnect/DNS-prefetch hrefs from link probes.

**Confirmed gaps:**
- 27 click/redirect links were skipped — final landing page URLs and UTM parameters are unverified. If any redirect chain is broken, it won't surface here.

**Duplicate font load:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Two separate requests for `Noto+Sans+Display`. The first (weight 700 only) is a strict subset of the second. Sends two HTTP requests where one suffices; minor performance overhead.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 3 images flagged:**

| Image filename fragment | Role |
|---|---|
| `…0924_Divider.png` | Decorative divider |
| `…0924_Hero_Wish-li…` (×2) | Hero/product image |

Decorative dividers should carry `alt=""` to silence screen readers. Hero/product images must have descriptive alt text for accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1) and for rendering in image-blocked clients.

**Triplicate media query blocks:**
Three separate `@media screen and (max-width: 599px)` blocks exist in the `<style>` tag. They merge fine in practice but indicate template assembly issues and add unnecessary CSS payload.

**`input { display: none !important; }` global rule:**
Hides all `<input>` elements globally. In most email clients this is harmless, but it would suppress any AMP for Email interactive inputs or survey embeds if ever used in this template.

### 5. Compliance

**Critical — transport headers missing:**

| Header | Status | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` | Not captured | Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe badge won't render; Yahoo/Google bulk sender requirement (Feb 2024) |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` | Not captured | RFC 8058 one-click POST mechanism absent; major inbox providers require this for >5k/day senders |
| `Authentication-Results` | Not captured | SPF, DKIM, DMARC pass/fail status unknown via AgentMail relay |

The QA tool notes these may be relay artifacts (AgentMail may strip or not forward headers). Verify headers on a raw SMTP capture directly from `e.crm.lego.com` before treating as sender-side failures.

**Plain-text body composition:**
81% of plain-text characters (4,897 / 6,021) are URLs. This is a strong Bayes/spam-filter trigger. The plain-text part is likely auto-generated by stripping HTML and retaining link hrefs inline, resulting in URL soup. A human-readable plain-text alternative should be authored separately.

**Body compliance (CAN-SPAM §5):**
HTML source is truncated — physical postal address and unsubscribe mechanism in the email footer could not be verified from the provided excerpt. Must be confirmed in full source.
