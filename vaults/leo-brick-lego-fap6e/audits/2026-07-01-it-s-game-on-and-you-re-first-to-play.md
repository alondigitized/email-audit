# It’s game on, and you’re first to play
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is LEGO's early-access reveal email for what appears to be their first brick-built pinball machine — a genuinely anticipated product drop positioned as an Insiders-exclusive "flip it first" window. The creative earns its send: the "You get to flip first, Leo" hero line is both personalized and perfectly themed, the product hero is striking (dark background, Classic Space aesthetic, retro Spaceman figure front and center), and the early-access framing gives Insiders members a real reason to open over a regular subscriber.
- The body copy is punchy and specific — "the very first brick-built pinball machine," "fully playable with digital scoreboard," and a price anchor of "from £110" — which is exactly what an adult collector needs to decide whether to click. The secondary section offering a spaceman poster for 900 Insiders points is a nice loyalty touch that reinforces the Insiders positioning.
- The one jarring note is structural: "Your LEGO® Insiders points total to: 0" renders in the reward section. Whether this is a personalization merge failure or genuinely correct, it undercuts the poster redemption CTA — a subscriber with 0 points can't redeem anything, and after the June 29 double-points push, seeing a zero balance will feel wrong to any engaged member who thinks they earned points that week.

## What's working

- **Personalized hero line lands immediately** — "You get to flip first, Leo" uses the name naturally, not as a prefix gimmick.
- **Product reveal copy is specific and believable** — "fully playable with digital scoreboard and retro Spaceman and space baby" gives collectors exactly the details they want.
- **Early Access framing creates real member value** — the "Early Access for Members" badge at the top and the explicit "you get to flip it first" language makes Insiders feel like a privilege, not just a points balance.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — dark hero image → headline → body copy → "Shop now" is a straight line, no distraction.
- **Classic Space/retro Spaceman aesthetic is on-brand** for the adult collector segment LEGO has been cultivating since the June 1 send.

## What's weak

- **"Points total to: 0" is a liability** — renders immediately below the hero in the reward section; whether a data failure or accurate, it makes the 900-point poster redemption feel inaccessible and undermines the loyalty narrative built since the June 29 double-points send.
- **No urgency anchor in the email body** — "Early Access" implies a window, but there's no end date, countdown, or "available until X" copy. The exclusivity promise has no teeth.
- **"Shop now" CTA is generic** — for a product this novel, "Flip it first" or "Get early access" would carry the campaign theme through to the click.
- **Subject line is clever but opaque** — "It's game on, and you're first to play" works *after* you know it's a pinball machine; in the inbox, before open, it reads as a vague gaming metaphor that doesn't name the product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix or suppress the 0-points merge before this send.** If the user has 0 points, hide the reward section entirely or offer an alternative ("earn points today"). Sending a redemption CTA to someone who can't redeem is friction, not benefit.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the Early Access window** — even "Available to Insiders through July 7" would transform the hero copy from a nice benefit into an action trigger.
- 3. **Replace "Shop now" with "Flip it first"** — carries the campaign metaphor to completion and is specific enough to feel earned.
- 4. **Name the product in the subject line** — the curiosity hook is solid, but a pinball machine mention would cut through for collectors who've been tracking the rumor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your early access to the LEGO® Pinball Machine is open`
- **Subject Alt B:** `First to flip: the LEGO® Pinball Machine is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The very first brick-built pinball machine — yours before anyone else. From £110.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fully playable, digital scoreboard, retro Spaceman. Insiders get it first.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound but has two compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) that violate current Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements, plus six missing alt-text attributes and redundant font-loading requests that add unnecessary overhead.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives in QA output:**
The two "broken link" FAILs for `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` are not navigable links — they are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints. The checker probed the bare domain root, which returns non-200. These are not real broken links.

**Duplicate Google Fonts requests:**
Three separate font-load calls exist for the same family, creating redundant HTTP round-trips:
- `<link href="...Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700">` (line ~12)
- `<link href="...Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700">` (line ~13)
- `@import url('...Noto+Sans')` inside `<style>` (line ~18)

The first `<link>` (weight 700 only) is fully redundant — the second covers both weights. The `@import` adds a render-blocking request after the `<link>` tags have already established the preconnect.

**Unverified tracking links:**
37 click-redirect links through `e.crm.lego.com` were skipped. Destination URLs and UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without sampling (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 6 instances across 3 images:**

| Image file | URL |

| `01_01072026_Q3_Project+Wizard_EA_Hero.png` (×2) | `https://image.e.crm.lego.com/lib/fe3b117175640479761271/m/1/01_...Hero.png` |
| `03_01072026_Q3_Project+Wizard_EA_Reward.png` (×2) | `https://image.e.crm.lego.com/lib/fe3b117175640479761271/m/1/03_...Reward.png` |

Each appears twice (desktop/mobile swap pattern). Hero and reward images are likely the primary CTA-bearing content — missing alt text means no fallback when images are blocked (default in Outlook, many corporate clients).

**Unloaded custom fonts:**
`LEGO Typewell` and `Cera Pro` appear in the `body` font-family stack but have no corresponding `@font-face` declaration or CDN link. All rendering will fall through to `Noto Sans` / `Arial`. This is expected behavior for email but is worth noting as intentional vs. oversight.

**Commented-out CSS:**
```css
/* [style*="NotoB"] { font-family: 'Noto Sans Display', sans-serif; } */
```
The `NotoB` selector is commented out while the `NotoR` selector is active. If any elements carry `style="...NotoB..."`, they will not receive the intended font binding.

**Duplicate media query blocks:**
Two separate `@media screen and (max-width: 599px)` blocks exist in `<style>`. This is functional but increases parse overhead and risks specificity confusion during maintenance.

### 5. Compliance

**Critical — List-Unsubscribe headers missing:**

Both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo have required one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`) for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Absence of these headers:
- Suppresses Gmail's native unsubscribe UI
- Risks inbox placement degradation
- May constitute a CAN-SPAM compliance gap depending on volume

**Action required:** Confirm whether headers are set by the LEGO CRM platform upstream and are being stripped by the AgentMail relay, or whether they are absent from the send entirely.

**Authentication-Results not captured:**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain (`e.crm.lego.com`) is a dedicated ESP subdomain that should have both configured. This needs to be verified directly against message headers in a full raw header capture — the AgentMail relay gap may be the cause.

**Plain-text URL density:**
75.8% of the plain-text version is URLs (4,117 of 5,432 chars). This is a spam filter signal. The plain-text alternative appears to be auto-generated from tracked link expansions rather than human-readable copy.

---
