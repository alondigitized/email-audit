# Celebrate SpongeBob day Leo!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- SpongeBob SquarePants Day (July 14 = franchise anniversary) gives LEGO a legitimate cultural calendar hook for a product launch, and they take it. The Bikini Bottom set reveal is the centerpiece, the IP photography is vibrant, and the structure is clean. This is a competent send with a strong premise.
- The execution has one crippling defect: "Launching on ##01" — an unfilled date merge token — sits in the body copy at exactly the moment a reader would need the launch date to decide whether to pre-order. For a brand that just ran a technically polished five-email Insiders Days sequence (July 7–12), this is an embarrassing QA failure. Coming the day after the July 13 birthday email ("Leo, the party's not over yet"), this is the third consecutive LEGO send, and cadence fatigue is starting to accumulate.
- The LEGO Insiders Offer — BOGO LEGOLAND Festival ticket — is a good member benefit, but it belongs in its own send. Bolted onto a SpongeBob product launch, it splits the email's job and weakens both messages.

## What's working

- SpongeBob Day is a real cultural moment and the timing of a product launch announcement around it is genuinely smart.
- Personalization ("Leo!") in the subject line is consistent with every email in this campaign arc — it reads as intentional, not incidental.
- Product photography is immediate and nostalgic — pineapple house, Patrick's rock, and Squidward's house are recognizable icons that land without a caption.
- Live Insiders points balance in the header reinforces loyalty value with zero extra copy.
- The BOGO kid's ticket benefit has clearly stated dates (July 20 – August 31, 2026) and clean eligibility fine print — no bait-and-switch.

## What's weak

- "Launching on ##01" is a live merge token failure in the email body — the launch date is missing and the broken token is visible to every recipient.
- No price shown for the SpongeBob sets — pre-orders without price anchoring create hesitation, especially for a new product line.
- Two separate offers (SpongeBob pre-order + LEGOLAND Festival ticket BOGO) dilute the hero message and give the email two competing jobs.
- The "Pre-order now" CTA appears twice with identical copy and no differentiation — unclear if both link to the same destination.
- Third email in three days (last-chance July 12, birthday July 13, SpongeBob July 14) — cadence is accumulating into subscriber fatigue territory.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix and resend to fix the ##01 merge token** — patch the template and send a corrected version. No other change matters more. A pre-order CTA with no launch date is stranded.
- 2. **Add a price anchor** — "Starting at $XX.99" under the hero copy or beside the CTA would dramatically reduce click hesitation on a pre-order.
- 3. **Cut or defer the LEGOLAND Festival offer** — SpongeBob Day is a clean story; the BOGO ticket interrupts it. Send the Festival offer stand-alone later this week.
- 4. **Differentiate the two CTAs** — "Pre-order the Bikini Bottom Set" and "Explore the full collection" rather than identical "Pre-order now" buttons.
- 5. **Add a SpongeBob Day deadline line** — even "Today only: celebrate SpongeBob Day by being first in Bikini Bottom" gives the cultural hook more momentum and lifts urgency without false scarcity.
- **Subject Alt A:** `SpongeBob Day is here — pre-order his new home, Leo`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Happy SpongeBob Day, Leo — Bikini Bottom just got a LEGO makeover`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pineapple house, Patrick's rock, Squidward's place — all new. Pre-order today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brand-new SpongeBob sets just announced — plus a free kid's ticket for Insiders.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — LEGO "Celebrate SpongeBob Day" Email (2026-07-14)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email's core rendering structure is sound, but it has meaningful accessibility gaps (11 images lack alt text including the hero), compliance risk from missing unsubscribe headers visible to inbox providers, and a plain-text body that is 77% raw URLs — a material spam-filter risk. Two QA "broken link" failures are false positives.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False positives in QA output:**
- `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` are flagged as 404s. These are `<link rel="preconnect">` resource hints, not navigable URLs — direct HTTP probes correctly return non-200. Not a real issue.

**Cannot verify 28 tracked links:**
- All click-redirect URLs (domain: `click.e.crm.lego.com` or equivalent) were skipped. Underlying destinations and UTM integrity are unverified.

**Redundant font loading:**
- `Noto Sans Display weight:700` is fetched twice via separate `<link>` tags (once as `wght@700`, once as `wght@400;700`). The first import is a subset of the second — the standalone `wght@700` request is redundant and adds an unnecessary render-blocking round-trip.

**Commented-out font import still active:**
```css
/* @import url('...Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700...'); */  ← commented out
```
…but the equivalent `<link>` tag remains live. The `NotoB` CSS selector that would consume this font is also commented out, meaning the bold display font is loaded and never used.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text (6 unique assets, some appear as desktop/mobile pairs):**

| Image | URL (truncated) |
|---|---|
| Hero banner (tall) | `.../01_Project-Bubble_Preorder_20260714-LI-Hero-Banner-Tall.png` |
| Hero banner (standard) | `.../03_Project-Bubble_Preorder_20260714-LI-Hero-Banner-Standard.png` |
| Content card | `.../05_Project-Bubble_Preorder_20260714-ContentCard.png` |
| Footer separator (×3) | `.../Footer_TopSeparator_Desktop_Member-Non-Member_Light.png` |
| Logo (mobile) | `.../Logo+Container+white+mobile.png` |
| Logo (desktop) | `.../Logo+Container+logo+white.png` |

The hero banner and both logo images are the highest-priority fixes — screen readers will announce nothing, and images-off rendering (common in Outlook) will show blank boxes with no context.

**Three separate `@media (max-width: 599px)` blocks** in `<style>`. While not broken, duplicate media query blocks create maintenance risk and rule ordering surprises. Should be consolidated.

**`input { display: none !important; }`** globally suppresses all `<input>` elements. If any embedded preference center or feedback widget is added later, this rule will silently break it.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No issues found. The first-name token in the subject line (`Leo!`) resolved correctly. No unresolved `{{token}}` or `%%token%%` placeholders visible in the truncated HTML.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not detected:**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. As of February 2024, Google and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders. LEGO's list volume almost certainly exceeds the 5,000/day threshold. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, that must be confirmed; if genuinely absent, this is a deliverability violation.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058) — not detected:**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method variant) is required by Google for high-volume senders. Absence here means Gmail will not show the native one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**Authentication-Results — not found:**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status for `e.crm.lego.com` is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not forward this header. Cannot confirm authentication chain. This is a relay configuration gap, not necessarily a sending-side failure — but it means the QA pipeline has no visibility into auth status.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Plain-text body is 77% URLs (9,487 of 12,371 characters):**
This is the most actionable deliverability finding. A plain-text alternative that is nearly entirely raw URLs (with minimal human-readable prose) is a known spam-filter signal and provides zero value to plain-text clients. The plain-text version should mirror the email's actual copy with URLs inline, not be auto-generated as a URL dump.

**Tracked link destinations unverified:**
28 click-redirect links were not probed. UTM parameter presence (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) on destination URLs cannot be confirmed. If UTMs are injected at the redirect layer rather than in the HTML, this is acceptable — but should be documented.
