# Aren’t LEGO® bricks so much better when they click?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a LEGO Insiders welcome/onboarding email for newly enrolled member "Leo," sent two days after the May 15 activation-code send. Its two jobs are: confirm membership and collect preference data to drive future segmentation. Both are handled cleanly — Leo's name appears twice, the preference survey ask is light-touch, and the "Members are loving..." section adds commercial weight without feeling like a hard sell. This is not a promo email; there's no discount, no deadline, no urgency. LEGO is deliberately playing the long game.
- The subject line is the weakest link. "Aren't LEGO® bricks so much better when they click?" is warm brand copy that does nothing in the inbox — no member name, no program hook, no signal of what's waiting inside. A fresh Insiders signup will open it on brand trust alone, which may explain why LEGO didn't try harder. But that's a free open being squandered on a soft subject.
- Verdict: a solid, brand-appropriate onboarding sequence that earns its keep through relationship-building rather than conversion craft. The survey CTA is genuinely clever; the subject line is a missed opportunity.

## What's working

- **Heavy name personalization:** "Leo" appears in the hero and again in the preference section, making it feel like a direct conversation rather than a mass send.
- **The "I'm looking for..." survey CTA:** Low-friction, high-value ask framed as curiosity rather than data collection — a smart segmentation mechanism dressed as a favor to the subscriber.
- **"Members are loving..." carousel:** Series 29 and Jurassic Park Jeep® Wrangler give the email immediate commercial utility; two specific products with "Shop now" buttons are more than most onboarding emails bother with.
- **Clean visual hierarchy:** Hero → account CTA → survey → products → fun fact → social is a logical onboarding flow with no layout confusion.
- **No render bugs:** Images load, text doesn't overlap, nothing is broken across the visible render.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is brand fluff, not inbox bait:** No name, no program mention, no offer, nothing that says "open me now" to a cold scroll.
- **Insiders perks remain abstract:** "Perks and rewards are about to stack up" is aspirational filler; one concrete benefit (e.g., "Earn 3 points per $1") would make the program feel real.
- **"Your account" CTA is inert:** Sends a brand-new member to a generic account page rather than a specific onboarding milestone — "Claim your first points" would do more.
- **Products ignore the survey:** Recommending Series 29 and a Jurassic Park set *before* Leo has answered the preference survey makes the "Members are loving..." section feel like it defeats the purpose of the ask above it.
- **Fun-fact section dilutes focus:** The LEGO name etymology is charming but competes with the email's actual conversion goal at scroll depth.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name the program and tease a specific benefit** — the brand-nostalgia play doesn't work when you need to earn an open against a crowded inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Leo, your LEGO® Insiders perks just unlocked`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your first Insiders reward is closer than you think`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tell us what you love — we'll make every send worth opening.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn points, get early access, and shop what actually fits your collection.`
- 2. **Make one perk concrete in the hero.** Replace "perks and rewards are about to stack up" with a scannable one-liner: "Earn 3 points per $1. Redeem for exclusive sets." One sentence, one number.
- 3. **Replace "Your account" with an action-framed CTA.** "Complete your profile" or "Claim your first points" gives new members a habit-forming next step rather than a dead-end account page.
- 4. **Reorder: survey first, products after — and acknowledge the sequencing.** Say "While you fill in your preferences, here are some fan favorites to browse" — that makes the algorithmic product picks feel like a bridge, not a contradiction.
- 5. **Cut or relocate the fun-fact section.** Move it to the post-survey confirmation email where it lands as a reward for engagement, not a non-sequitur at the bottom of a welcome.

## Technical Audit — LEGO® Insider Welcome Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound with proper HTML4 doctype, viewport meta, and MSO conditional comments, but the QA pipeline is producing false-positive 404s on Google Fonts preconnect hints, and two compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) are not being captured through the AgentMail relay — which needs verification at the relay level rather than in the email HTML itself.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**False-positive 404s — preconnect hints probed as URLs**
The QA checker is treating `<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">` and `<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>` as navigable links and probing them with HTTP HEAD/GET, causing spurious 404 failures. These are not clickable links — they are browser hints. The broken-link check should filter `rel="preconnect"` and `rel="preload"` elements.

**25 tracking links skipped**
All 25 click-redirect URLs on `click.e.crm.lego.com` (or equivalent redirect domain) were skipped. Destination reachability is unverified. A redirect-following probe should be added to confirm final landing pages return 2xx.

**Duplicate Google Fonts `<link>` tags**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Both load Noto Sans Display; the first is a strict subset of the second and is redundant. Additionally, `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans&display=swap')` in the `<style>` block loads a third variant, making three separate font requests where one would suffice. Minor load-time penalty; no functional breakage.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not captured** (QA WARN)
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is required by CAN-SPAM and, since February 2024, by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies for senders at LEGO's volume. Root cause is likely relay-level header stripping — the header is almost certainly present in LEGO's actual outbound envelope but not forwarded by AgentMail. **Action: configure AgentMail to pass through or log raw headers before relay.**

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not captured** (QA WARN)
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header also absent from captured headers. Same relay-stripping root cause likely applies. Gmail enforces this for bulk senders; missing it can increase spam folder placement.

**Authentication-Results header not captured** (QA WARN)
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown because the relay is not preserving the `Authentication-Results` header injected by the receiving MTA. This makes it impossible to audit deliverability signal from the pipeline. **Action: log raw headers at ingest, before any relay transformation.**

**Plain-text body is 76% URLs** (4,897 / 6,415 chars)
A plain-text alternative that is majority URLs is a spam filter signal. The plain-text part should be a human-readable summary of the email content, not an auto-generated URL dump.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

25 tracking links were skipped by the QA prober — UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed from this audit. The redirect domain (`click.e.crm.lego.com` or equivalent) should be probed with redirect-following to verify:
- Final destination returns HTTP 200
- UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present on all CTAs
- No redirect chains exceed 2 hops (latency + link rot risk)

No issues found in the HTML source itself regarding UTM structure — this gap is solely a QA tooling coverage limitation.
