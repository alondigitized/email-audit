# Ready for new bricks, Leo?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a "new sets of the month" grid email — four fresh product tiles (Mayor Manor, Star Trek Enterprise Bridge, SpongeBob Bikini Bottom, Ministry of Magic Collector's Edition) under a "New bricks for your basket" hero, followed by an Advent Calendar cross-sell and a favorites/app-download block. Compared to the LEGO stretch I've been tracking — the Member Day perk emails on 2026-09-09, the countdown urgency on 2026-09-06, the rerun "Rediscover the joy of building" on 2026-09-08 — this one is actually a legitimate new-product beat, closer in spirit to the 2026-09-01 Star Trek Enterprise solo-drop, just bundled into a grid instead of a single hero.
- The execution is clean and render-bug-free, but it's unfocused: four completely unrelated properties (a horror-themed haunted house, Star Trek, SpongeBob, Harry Potter) crammed into one grid with no unifying theme or offer, plus a fifth Advent Calendar pitch bolted on below. There's no discount, no urgency, no segmentation — just "here's stuff." For Leo (assuming a build-focused, sets/points-driven persona), the "favorites" strip (Star Wars, Icons, Harry Potter, Ideas) is the most relevant personalization signal in the whole email, but it's buried near the bottom.
- Verdict: solid production value, zero render bugs, but it's a generic monthly catalog blast dressed up as a personal recommendation — no offer, no urgency, and a scattershot product mix keeps this in "skim and maybe click one thing" territory.

## What's working

- Clean 2x2 product grid with consistent card layout — image, title, piece count, points value, CTA — easy to scan.
- Points/value module at top and the physical membership-card footer are on-brand and reinforce the Insiders loyalty angle.
- "Here are your favorites" strip surfaces category personalization (Star Wars, LEGO Icons, Harry Potter, Ideas) which is the closest thing to true targeting in the email.
- No layout breakage, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.
- App download CTA block is a nice secondary conversion path without competing with the main grid.

## What's weak

- No discount, promo code, or time-bound offer anywhere — it's pure catalog browsing, weakest lever in the whole rubric.
- The four hero products span wildly different franchises (haunted house, Star Trek, SpongeBob, Harry Potter) with no organizing theme, diluting visual focus.
- Subject "Ready for new bricks, Leo?" is vague — doesn't hint at any specific set or offer, so it undersells what's actually a decent product lineup.
- The Advent Calendar section interrupts the "new sets" narrative with an unrelated seasonal pitch, muddying the email's single message.
- This is the third distinct "check out new/rediscover sets" framing in eight days (2026-09-08 rerun, 2026-09-01 Star Trek solo drop, now this grid) — cadence is starting to blur together even though the content itself isn't a rerun.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the single strongest hero (the Ministry of Magic Collectors' Edition at 3491 pieces / +2925 points is the standout) as one big feature block, then drop the other three into a smaller "also new" strip — right now equal-weighting all four buries the best product.
- 2. Cut or move the Advent Calendar block to a separate send — it competes with the "new sets" message rather than supporting it.
- 3. Move the "Here are your favorites" personalization strip up near the hero — it's the most relevant signal for a specific subscriber and currently gets no visual weight.
- 4. Add a concrete hook to the subject/preview (specific set name or points multiplier) instead of a generic tease.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Leo, the Ministry of Magic just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 new sets, Leo — including 3,491-piece Ministry of Magic`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus Star Trek, SpongeBob, and a haunted Mayor Manor — see what's new`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn up to 2,925 Insiders points on this month's new arrivals`
- 5. Add even a soft incentive (double points on new releases, free gift over $X) to give a reason to act now rather than "someday browse."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard MJML-generated LEGO Insiders promotional email (ESP: Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget domains `e.crm.lego.com`, `s50.exct.net`). Renders responsively but fails several compliance and accessibility checks per automated QA (38% pass rate).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 56 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by HTTP probe (expected behavior for SFMC click-tracking wrappers — not itself a defect, but means link-destination validity is unverified).
- One image asset loads over plain HTTP: `http://cl.s50.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5OTh9...` ("Insider Barcode", dynamic AMPscript-driven loyalty barcode). Mixed content on an otherwise HTTPS-served email risks the image being blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading (Gmail, Outlook.com proxy).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 18 `<img>` tags missing `alt` text, including functional/content images (product shots: `blt21fb8295f9caaf81-11383_Prod_en-gb.png`, `76476_Prod.png`, hero banner `02-Novelty-September_2209-Hero-Banner-RB-Short.png`) and repeated decorative assets (`GreyBrick.png`, `Footer-Products_GenericPoints_Colour.png`, `Footer_TopSeparator_Desktop_Member-Non-Member_Dark.png`, `Footer_Mobile_LegoInsidersLogo.png`). With images blocked by default in most clients, this leaves the entire hero/product grid and footer with no fallback text.
- Uses legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if lte mso 11]-->`) — standard for Outlook desktop compatibility, no issue.
- Web font loading via `<link>` `@import` (Google Fonts `Noto Sans` family) with `Arial`/`sans-serif` fallback chain in the `body` rule — acceptable degradation path, no issue.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Subject line uses a first-name merge token ("Ready for new bricks, Leo?") — resolved correctly in the sample, no raw token artifacts (`%%FirstName%%`, `{{first_name}}`, etc.) visible in subject or truncated body.
- No issues found in visible HTML for merge-tag leakage.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not detected. Note the QA tool flags this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not surfacing the header rather than the sender omitting it — cannot confirm sender-side absence from this data alone.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header (RFC 8058)**: not detected — if genuinely absent, one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules) is not supported.
- **Physical mailing address**: not detected in the truncated HTML/QA scan. CAN-SPAM requires a postal address in every commercial email; this needs confirmation against the full footer HTML (truncation may simply have cut it off before the footer address block).
- **Authentication-Results (SPF/DKIM)**: not present in the captured headers — status unknown, likely also an AgentMail relay capture gap rather than a sender misconfiguration, but unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot assess: all outbound CTA/product links are wrapped in SFMC click-tracking redirects (`cl.s50.exct.net` style) that were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destination UTM parameters and final landing-page URLs are not visible in the provided data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 18 flagged images, particularly the product images and hero banner (content-bearing, not decorative).
2. Replace the HTTP `LiveContent.aspx` barcode image reference with an HTTPS equivalent to prevent mixed-content blocking.
3. Confirm directly with the ESP/SFMC send definition (outside this HTML snippet) that `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and a physical address are actually present in the raw send — the QA warnings here may reflect relay capture limitations rather than true absence.
4. If full HTML/headers are available, re-run link-destination and UTM-continuity checks by resolving the SFMC redirect wrappers rather than skipping them.
