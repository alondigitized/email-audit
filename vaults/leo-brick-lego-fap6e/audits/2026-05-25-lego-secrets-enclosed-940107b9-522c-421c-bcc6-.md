# LEGO® secrets enclosed...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth LEGO Insiders email in ten days (following May 15, 17, and 21 sends), and the fatigue is starting to show. The email has two jobs — drive downloads of the LEGO Builder app and introduce the wish list feature — plus a product recommendation block at the bottom. The design is clean and on-brand, with a clear visual hierarchy that walks you from feature adoption down to commerce.
- The core problem is the subject line. "LEGO® secrets enclosed..." sets an expectation of exclusive insider knowledge or a hidden deal. What you actually get is: here's our app, here's our wish list tool, here are four sets. No secrets. That bait-and-switch isn't egregious enough to feel deceptive, but it is the kind of gap that quietly erodes trust over time.
- The wish list section ("Rescue yourself from unwrapping another year of socks") is holiday-framed evergreen copy dropped into a late-May send. In isolation it works; in context it reads as copy that wasn't written for this moment. This email needed either a concrete offer or a genuinely novel piece of Insiders intelligence to justify the "secrets" promise.

## What's working

- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Yellow → purple → white creates strong section breaks and the eye moves naturally from Builder app to wish list to products.
- **Product selection is on-brand and fan-aligned.** Batman Logo, Jurassic Park Jeep Wrangler, and Mario Kart Luigi sets all signal adult collector/fan targeting, which is appropriate for an Insiders member.
- **No render bugs.** All four images load, buttons are readable, no layout breakage visible.
- **"Members can't get enough of these sets"** is a subtle but effective social-proof headline that activates Insiders identity.

## What's weak

- **Subject line promises secrets and delivers none.** The content is a feature tour and product grid — nothing exclusive or insider-feeling.
- **No offer anywhere.** Not one price, discount, or member-exclusive perk is visible. For the fourth email in ten days, there's no escalating value to the subscriber.
- **"I wish..." CTA is too cute.** Paired with no deadline or specific product link, it reads as low-urgency decoration rather than a true conversion driver.
- **Wish list in late May is seasonally adrift.** The "year of socks" framing is holiday copy — dropped seven months before Christmas with no bridging context (like a birthday campaign angle).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the "secrets" promise or rewrite the subject.** The easiest fix is replacing the subject with something that matches the actual content (see alts below). The harder-but-better fix is adding one piece of genuine Insiders intelligence — an early-access product, a members-only discount code, or a tip the general public doesn't see.
- 2. **Add at least one offer to the product block.** Even "Insiders members earn 2× points this week" or a free-shipping threshold turns a browse email into an action email.
- 3. **Rewrite "I wish..." to "Build your wish list."** Verb + noun, unambiguous, still playful.
- 4. **Cut the holiday socks copy or contextualise it.** Either anchor it to a real gifting moment (Father's Day is in two weeks — that's a layup) or replace it with a year-round framing like "the one list your family actually wants to shop."
- 5. **Rest the list.** The May 21 send was already the third onboarding email. This one would benefit from a pause or a material reason to be in the inbox.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — LEGO® "secrets enclosed" Welcome Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Well-structured HTML4 email with solid Outlook/mobile conditional logic, but has 6 images missing alt text, unresolvable font preconnect URLs triggering false-positive 404s, and compliance headers that are either absent or not surfacing through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font preconnect false positives (QA FAIL)**
- `https://fonts.googleapis.com` and `https://fonts.gstatic.com` are flagged as 404s by the link checker, but these are `<link rel="preconnect">` hints — not fetchable endpoints. The HTTP probe is incorrectly treating them as navigable URLs. **Not a real broken link**, but the QA tool should skip `rel="preconnect"` tags.

**26 tracking links skipped**
- All 26 click-redirect links (via `e.crm.lego.com` or equivalent) were skipped by the probe. Destination URL integrity (landing pages, UTM params) is unverified for this send. Manual spot-check of CTA destinations is needed.

**Duplicate Google Fonts `<link>` tags**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Display:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Two separate requests for the same family — the second subsumes the first. The `wght@700` request is redundant; consolidate to the `wght@400;700` request only.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**6 images missing `alt` text (QA WARN)**

| Filename fragment | Type |
|---|---|
| `01_…Hero_Builde` (×2 — desktop + mobile swap) | Hero image |
| `02_…Divider.jpg` | Decorative divider |
| `03_…Hero_Wish-L` (×2 — desktop + mobile swap) | Secondary hero |
| `04_…Divider.png` | Decorative divider |

Hero images lacking alt text break the email for image-off rendering (Gmail Promotions tab, corporate firewalls). Decorative dividers should use `alt=""` explicitly to signal intentional emptiness to screen readers.

**`<title>` is generic**
```html
<title>LEGO</title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>`. Should be descriptive (e.g., `LEGO® Welcome — Your secrets await`).

**No issues** with table-based layout structure, MSO conditional comments, viewport meta, or color-scheme dark mode declarations.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture (QA WARN)**
- Header not detected by AgentMail. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe UI. If it is present at the ESP level but stripped by the relay, it needs to be confirmed via raw header inspection of a delivered copy. If genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM risk and a Gmail deliverability risk (Gmail deprioritizes bulk mail without it).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` / RFC 8058 one-click absent (QA WARN)**
- Required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders). LEGO at this volume almost certainly exceeds that threshold. If one-click unsubscribe is not wired at the ESP, it must be added.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured (QA WARN)**
- SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the relay. Given the sending domain is `e.crm.lego.com` (a subdomain likely aligned to a CRM ESP), DKIM alignment should be verified against the `lego.com` DMARC policy. If `e.crm.lego.com` is not covered by the DMARC record, spoofability is a concern.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs (QA WARN)**
```
4543 / 5616 chars are URLs
```
This indicates the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from the HTML rather than authored. Spam filters penalize this pattern. A short human-readable plain-text version should be authored separately.

---
