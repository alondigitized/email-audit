# Home Office Collection
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Home doing what Zara Home always does — a pure lifestyle lookbook with almost zero product information. "Home Office Collection" is a moody editorial shoot of a red-lacquered desk, an anglepoise lamp, a wall-mounted pinboard, a chair, and a leather ottoman, stacked in a long vertical scroll with barely any copy. There is exactly one CTA button ("The New") buried two-thirds of the way down, no price anywhere, and a random "Home Fragrances" cross-sell tacked onto the bottom that has nothing to do with the subject line.
- This is a brand-building send, not a conversion send. If you already love Zara Home's aesthetic and have the budget to shop it sight-unseen, it's a pleasant scroll. If you're actually furnishing a home office and want to know what a desk costs or how to buy it, this email tells you nothing and makes you work for it.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent art direction — warm, moody, editorial tones carried through every shot (red lacquer, wood, brown leather).
- Clear thematic focus: every image is genuinely home-office-adjacent (desk, lamp, chair, pinboard, ottoman).
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken layout.
- Section is clearly labeled ("HOME OFFICE COLLECTION" title card early in the scroll).

## What's weak

- No visible pricing, product names, or fabric/material details anywhere in the render — every image is uncaptioned.
- Only one CTA in the entire email ("The New"), and its copy doesn't even reference "home office" or link to a specific product.
- The email ends with an unrelated "Home Fragrances" block that breaks the stated theme of the subject line.
- No urgency, no offer, no price — nothing that would move a reader from "pretty photo" to "buy."
- Extremely long, image-heavy scroll (over a dozen full-bleed photos) before any actionable link appears.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add product name + price captions under at least the hero desk and lamp shots — right now there's no way to know what anything costs or is called.
- 2. Move a "Shop Home Office" CTA button to appear right after the hero, not two-thirds down the scroll.
- 3. Cut the email length by half — 6-7 images with captions would convert better than 15 uncaptioned lifestyle shots.
- 4. Drop or clearly separate the "Home Fragrances" block — it dilutes the home-office focus and reads like a mismatched insert.
- 5. Give the CTA specific copy tied to the category, e.g. "Shop the Desk Edit," instead of generic "The New."
