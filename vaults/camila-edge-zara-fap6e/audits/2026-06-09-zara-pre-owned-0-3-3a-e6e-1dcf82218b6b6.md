# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's seasonal edition email for their pre-owned resale platform — the "Summer Vibes Edition" of ZARA PRE-OWNED. The concept is smart: dress the resale platform in the same editorial confidence Zara uses for its mainline seasonal sends. The headline "NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" is the single best copy line in recent Zara emails reviewed — it disarms the secondhand stigma directly and with personality.
- The execution is structurally familiar: hero editorial, cascading category sections (The Trendé Linen, Terracotta Club, Men Collection, Kids Collection), product thumbnail rows, branded hashtag footer. It all renders cleanly. But the email asks the reader to do everything — browse a platform, download an app, consider donating — without giving them a concrete reason to act *now*. No price signal, no inventory urgency, no "sell yours too" hook.
- Compared to the June 5 "House of Marisa Berenson" send (which built a cinematic world around a single editorial anchor), this one feels more like a catalog preview than a campaign. It's competent Zara. It's not compelling Zara.

## What's working

- **Hero headline lands.** "NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" is confident, anti-stigma, and on-brand voice — the best copy beat in this send.
- **Seasonal frame is credible.** "Summer Vibes Edition" makes the pre-owned platform feel curated, not clearance. Smart framing.
- **#ZARAPREOWNED + Repair/Donation footer.** Grounds the platform in a sustainability ecosystem beyond just shopping — adds brand value at no layout cost.
- **Multi-category coverage.** Women, Men, Kids all visible — signals the platform has depth.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout breaks, no obvious overlap issues.

## What's weak

- **No price signal whatsoever.** Pre-owned implies cheaper, but the email never says it. Even "From €X" on one hero item would convert browsers.
- **CTA button is undersized and easy to miss.** The teal button sits in a tight space between the headline and the first section — it blends into the editorial rather than demanding attention.
- **No "sell your Zara" angle.** The donation link is buried in the footer; the biggest reason a current Zara subscriber would engage with a pre-owned platform is the two-way economy. It's invisible here.
- **Cadence fatigue risk.** This is at least the 6th email in ~11 days from Zara. The pre-owned angle is differentiated, but subscribers who've already deprioritized the FIFA Classics and CARAMEL LONDON sends may never get here.
- **Product thumbnails are too small to evaluate.** The mini-grid rows near the top are so compact that items are unidentifiable on mobile — they create visual noise without driving consideration.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor in the hero.** "From €12" or "Up to 70% off retail" immediately tells me why to click over the mainline store. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Make the CTA button bigger and rewrite it.** Change from generic "SHOP PRE-OWNED" to something specific like "Shop Summer Pre-Owned" — and increase button size/padding so it reads on mobile without squinting.
- 3. **Surface the "sell yours" proposition.** Add a second CTA block mid-email: "Have Zara pieces to sell? List them in 3 steps." This opens the two-way marketplace value prop that a resale platform subscriber actually wants to hear.
- 4. **Cut the mini-grid thumbnails at top or make them larger.** They're illegible at current size and add friction before the editorial hook lands.
- 5. **Pull the Donation/Repair services up.** Move "Repair" and "Donation" out of the footer and into a visible mid-email block — it's a genuine differentiator that currently has zero visual weight.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` tag (blocked in most clients):**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip external `<link>` tags. The font will silently fall through to the browser/system default. Either accept this (if system fallbacks are defined) or load via `@import` inside `<style>` — though `@import` support is also limited. Confirm fallback stack is acceptable.

**Issue — `user-scalable=no` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale=1.0`.

**Issue — Invalid CSS value `line-height: auto`:**
```css
.heightmobile { height: auto!important; line-height: auto!important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `line-height` (valid values: `normal`, unitless numbers, lengths, percentages). Replace with `line-height: normal !important`. Some clients may ignore the entire rule or behave unpredictably.

**Issue — Preheader suppressed for Outlook:**
```html
<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <td ...><span style="display: none !important; ...">New second-hand arrivals&zwnj;...</span></td>
</div>
```
Both the MSO conditional comment and `mso-hide:all` prevent Outlook from rendering this node. Outlook's preview pane will fall through to the first visible body text instead. If the first visible body content is an image (likely, given this layout), Outlook users will see no preview text or an image filename.

**Cannot audit alt text** — no `<img>` tags present in truncated excerpt. Verify all images have descriptive `alt` attributes for screen readers and image-blocking clients.
