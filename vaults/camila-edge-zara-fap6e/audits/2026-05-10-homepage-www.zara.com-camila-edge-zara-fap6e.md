# Homepage snapshot · www.zara.com · 2026-05-10
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-10**
## Executive summary

- I didn't even get to the homepage — what loaded is a location-gate interstitial: the Zara wordmark, a country/language selector, and a "CONTINUE" button. That's it. No imagery, no campaign, no product, no reason to feel anything. It reads like a site that doesn't trust me yet, which, coming from Zara, feels like a miss — I've been buying SRPLS drops here for years and it still can't remember where I live.

## What's working

- **Clean render:** The location selector itself renders without bugs — the dropdowns and button are legible and functional.
- **"Remember selection" checkbox:** At least the option is there to not see this gate again, which is the one small grace note for repeat visitors.
- **Logo presence:** The oversized Zara wordmark is sharp and on-brand for the aesthetic.

## What's weak

- **Zero product or campaign content above the fold:** I opened Zara to browse drops and I'm staring at a blank white page with a country picker. This kills any shopping momentum instantly.
- **No session recognition:** As a returning shopper I've clearly been here before — there's no cookie-based skip or "Welcome back" flow. Mobile browsers persist cookies; this gate should not be shown to me.
- **"CONTINUE" is a dead-end CTA:** The only interactive element that advances me forward is a form submit. There's no product image, no teaser of what's inside, nothing to create desire before I'm even let through the door.
- **"DO NOT SELL OR SHARE MY PERSONAL INFORMATION" at the bottom:** Slapping a California privacy link as the sole footer content on what should be a brand moment is tonal whiplash.
- **No imagery whatsoever:** The whole screen is white. For a brand that lives on visual identity — runway photography, editorial stills, SRPLS campaign shots — this is a completely wasted opportunity to establish mood before the gate clears.

## Recommendations

- **Auto-detect and skip the gate for return visitors:** If a geolocation or existing cookie signals the user is in the US (and has visited before), bypass this screen entirely and land directly on the homepage. Every millisecond of friction here is a bounce risk on mobile.
- **Add a background editorial image behind the gate form:** Even a full-bleed campaign still or a current collection shot would make this feel intentional rather than broken. Keep the form centered on top.
- **Replace the blank white footer with a subtle campaign hint:** Something like a cropped product image at the bottom of the gate — half-visible, creating pull — so I'm already wanting to get past it.
- **Surface a "welcome back" state:** If a session or cookie is detected, skip the gate and drop me on the homepage. If a soft gate is legally required, at minimum personalize it: "Welcome back — you're shopping in the United States."
