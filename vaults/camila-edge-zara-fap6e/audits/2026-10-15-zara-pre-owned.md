# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's second-hand programme sent as a lookbook, and the creative is strong. The black-and-white wordmark header, the plaid-blazer hero and the lace "Dark romance" editorial look expensive. The line "The poetry of tailoring. Not new but still cool!" explains the concept in one go. It is a better fit for a style-led persona than the recent Kids and Origins sends.
- It still isn't a sales email. There is no price, no percent off, no button, and no stated condition or sizing for the items. The numbered grids (001 BLAZER to 006 COAT, 001 and 002 for sweaters) carry no prices. The only real CTAs are the two small tiles, "Secondhand Man Collection" and "Secondhand Kids Collection", near the bottom. Pre-owned is the strongest hook in the email, and it appears only as a title. Nothing says how much cheaper it is or how limited the stock is.
- Verdict: good brand and sustainability content, weak commerce. It's a 5 or 6 for the effort and a 4 for sales.

## What's working

- The "ZARA PRE-OWNED" header and hero set the concept immediately, and the plaid-blazer shot fits the autumn tailoring story.
- "The poetry of tailoring. Not new but still cool!" reframes second-hand as fashion instead of a discount.
- The numbered six-item grid (blazer, pants, shoes, shirt, belt, coat) is easy to scan, and the items are cleanly cut out.
- The "Dark romance" and "Knitwear, rediscovered" sections give the email a sense of seasonal pacing.
- The Repair and Donation links under "More services" extend the circular-fashion story.

## What's weak

- No prices or savings appear anywhere, so there is no offer. The shopper can't tell what "pre-owned" saves them.
- There are no visible buttons. The product grids look like images, with only the tiny "001 BLAZER" labels as hints.
- The "Dark romance" section has a layout gap. A lone black dress floats to the right of a large lace photo, with big blank space around it, and it reads as a misaligned block.
- The most obvious CTAs, the Man and Kids tiles, sit at the very bottom, and they point away from the women's content the email leads with.
- The email gives no information on condition, sizing or availability.
- The sections are long and airy, so the email takes a lot of scrolling to reach any action.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or saving to the hero, for example "From $15" or "Up to 70% off retail". It is the biggest gap in the email.
- 2. Add a visible "Shop pre-owned" button under the tagline, and show prices beneath each numbered item.
- 3. Fix the "Dark romance" layout so the dress sits in a proper grid or next to the model, with a shop link.
- 4. Add trust details: condition grading, authenticity, sizes, and "limited, one of each".
- 5. Move the Man and Kids tiles up and give them real button copy.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Not new, but still cool: shop pre-owned`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tailoring, knitwear and lace, one of each. Shop second-hand Zara today.`
