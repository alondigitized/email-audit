## Executive summary

- This is Zara's weekly menswear lookbook scroll, built around "Archive AW26," "Street Style," and "Evening." The black-and-white Paris-quay hero with the oversized ZARA wordmark is striking. The rest of the scroll is well paced, moving from tailoring to Amsterdam street casting to evening wear. It is a good magazine and a poor sales email.
- There is no price, no offer, no product name, and no urgency anywhere in it. The only calls to action are tiny "VIEW" links and one-word category labels (Knitwear, Coats, Jackets, Jeans, Trousers, Accessories, Blazers). A subscriber has to decide where to tap with nothing to go on. - Judgement: a beautiful brand-building send with weak commerce mechanics. It will get opened out of habit and will convert below its potential.

## What's working

- The hero is distinctive. The black-and-white image with the cropped ZARA wordmark gives instant brand recognition.
- The visual rhythm varies: large hero, two-up, three-up grids, and full-bleed street shots. It keeps the scroll from feeling repetitive.
- The three headlines (ARCHIVE AW26, STREET STYLE, EVENING) are bold and give clear section breaks.
- The category labels under images (Knitwear, Coats, Jackets, Jeans) give scannable entry points.
- The footer has a clear "Download our app" block with social links.

## What's weak

- No offer, price, or product detail appears anywhere, so there is no reason to click now.
- The "VIEW" links are tiny and the "Discover the collection" text is small. None of them reads as a button.
- The "EVE NING" headline is split mid-word across two lines, which looks like a layout glitch.
- The 3x3 grid has no labels or links I can see. It is unclear what is shoppable.
- The "Newsletter, online version" line is small grey text at the very top and adds friction.
- The send is very long, with the eye wandering through repetitive male-model shots.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a single concrete commerce hook near the top, such as one hero product with a name and price, or "New in: outerwear from $X". That would lift click-through most.
- 2. Turn "VIEW" into real, larger buttons with specific copy, such as "Shop the coats".
- 3. Fix the "EVE NING" break so the word sits on one line.
- 4. Cut the grids to one and label each tile with product and price.
- 5. Add a time or season cue, such as "Cold-weather essentials, this week".

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The HTML itself is Responsys-built XHTML 1.0 Transitional with Outlook (MSO) fallbacks. It has widespread missing image alt text and a viewport setting that blocks zoom. The HTML source was truncated, so only the head and the start of the body were visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- The tracking pixel `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=...` has no alt text (flagged as `as`). Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`.
- The HTML comment `CAMPAIGN_CODE:zara;2026_11_07_Norte_man_weekly_07_11;US_en` and the Spanish-language macro comments (`Macros para el contenido`, `PAISES_SKIP`, `PAISES_ENVIO`) ship in the delivered source. This leaks internal campaign and segmentation metadata. Strip comments at send time.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text fallback [FAIL]:** The text version is 0 chars. This is a deliverability and accessibility risk, because multipart/alternative has no usable text part.
- **Missing alt text [WARN]:** 19 content images have none: `img_01`–`img_04`, `img_05A`–`img_05H`, and `img_06`–`img_12`, all under `https://news.zara.com/assets/responsysimages/industria007/.../2026_11_07_Norte_ma...`. With images blocked or a screen reader, the email would be blank or unintelligible. The `.imagemobile` class sets fallback alt styling (`font-size:16px; text-align:center`), but this only works if alt text exists.
- **Viewport meta:** `content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;"` uses semicolons instead of commas, and `user-scalable=no` / `maximum-scale=1.0` disable pinch-zoom. That is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Missing `lang`:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick a language.
- **Link colour:** The global `a { color: #999898; }` is about 2.8:1 against white, which fails WCAG AA (4.5:1) for any link that doesn't override it inline. I couldn't see the inline overrides in the truncated source.
- **Web font:** The Google Fonts `<link>` in `<head>` is stripped by Gmail and several other clients. The MSO block provides `HelveticaNeue, Helvetica, Arial`, but a non-Outlook fallback stack wasn't confirmed.
- **XHTML:** `<link ... >` isn't self-closed under an XHTML doctype. This is minor and harmless in email clients.
