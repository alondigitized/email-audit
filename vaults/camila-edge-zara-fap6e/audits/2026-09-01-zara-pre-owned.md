# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Pre-Owned, and for once it's a genuinely different program from the usual "Woman | This week's selection" grind — resale/secondhand is a real point of differentiation, not another lookbook reskin. The numbered "001 TRENCH," "002 POLO TOP" grid with small labeled thumbnails is a smarter layout than the giant unbroken hero-then-category-block pattern this brand has run five times running in recent sends. It actually reads like a catalog you could shop.
- But it still commits Zara's cardinal sin: zero prices anywhere. On a resale program specifically, price is the entire pitch — "is this actually a deal?" — and the email never answers that question. It also buries a decent hook ("not new but still cool") under a huge, hard-to-read orange all-caps wordmark that reads more like a warning label than a header.
- Net: better structural idea than Zara's typical sends, undermined by the same missing-price problem that's dogged every recent audit, plus a genuinely fatiguing amount of scroll (six product grids, two collection banners, a services section) before you hit the footer.

## What's working

- The "001/002/003" numbered grid format is more scannable than Zara's usual undifferentiated category-block scroll.
- Pre-owned/resale is a distinct campaign concept, not a rehash of the same "explore our newest collections" template.
- "Repair" and "Donation" services plus "#zarapreowned" signal a genuine sustainability program, not just a marketing wrapper.
- Clean section breaks (Highlights, Skirts, Secondhand Man/Kids Collection) give the email actual navigable structure.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere — on a resale email this is the single most important missing data point.
- The giant orange "ZARA PRE-OWNED" headline is visually harsh and reads more like an alert banner than a brand mark.
- Extremely long scroll — six numbered product tiles, an editorial mid-section, a skirts subsection, then two more collection banners — well past what a mobile reader will get through.
- No visible discount, bundle offer, or loyalty tie-in; "curated selection" is the entire value prop, with nothing concrete backing it up.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices (or at least "up to X% vs. new") next to every product tile — this is the one change that would most improve both credibility and click-through for a resale email.
- 2. Shrink or restyle the hero wordmark — smaller, or paired with the product imagery instead of blocking it, so the eye lands on the clothes, not a traffic-cone-colored logo.
- 3. Cut the email in half — lead with the numbered grid (the best asset here), move Skirts/Man/Kids into a "Shop by category" strip instead of three separate full-width sections.
- 4. Add one clear CTA button ("Shop Pre-Owned") near the top instead of relying on implicit product-tile clicks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pre-loved Zara, up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drop: one-of-a-kind pre-owned pieces`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Trenches, denim, and loafers with a past — priced to match.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Curated secondhand, restocked weekly. Shop before it's gone.`
