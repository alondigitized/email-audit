# Back to it. New looks for the return
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Man's back-to-office send, and it's the same category-grid skeleton the brand runs everywhere else — hero editorial shot, section header, then a dense wall of nine product-category tiles (Blazers, Trench Coats, Jeans, Jackets, Trousers, Suits, Polo Shirts, Bags, Accessories) each with just a text link underneath. There's no price, no discount, no "shop the edit" CTA button — just moody black-and-white lifestyle photography of men in offices, doing office-adjacent things (typing, gesturing, carrying a briefcase).
- The concept — "Back to Work" tailoring for the return from summer — is timely and well-executed visually; the hero shot (a sharp guy in a blazer against a NYC skyline with a giant ZARA wordmark) is genuinely striking. But there's zero commercial urgency anywhere in this email. It reads as a lookbook, not an offer, which is consistent with every Zara send in this brand history — the score keeps landing in the same 3-5 band because the format hasn't changed regardless of theme.

## What's working

- Hero image is strong — high-contrast editorial photography, clear seasonal hook ("Back to Work") tied to actual back-to-school/return-to-office timing in late August.
- Section is well-organized into nine distinct, clearly-labeled category tiles, making it easy to jump straight to a specific product type.
- Visual consistency (all black-and-white, office-setting photography) gives the email a cohesive, premium editorial feel.
- No render bugs — images all loaded, text is legible, layout is clean and evenly spaced.

## What's weak

- No offer of any kind — no price, no % off, no promo code anywhere in the email.
- No primary CTA — nine equal-weight text links with no visual hierarchy telling the eye where to go first.
- Category labels are the only clickable copy; there's no product detail (price, fabric, fit) visible anywhere.
- No social proof, loyalty messaging, or urgency — this could run any week and nothing would need to change.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a single hero CTA button ("Shop Tailoring" or similar) directly under the hero image instead of relying on nine flat category links — right now nothing tells the reader where to click first.
- 2. Put at least one price or offer signal in the hero band (e.g., "New Season Tailoring" is fine as a header, but even a "Suits from $99" line would give this commercial teeth).
- 3. Cut the grid from nine tiles to 4-5 — Blazers, Trench Coats, Suits, Jackets, Bags is plenty; Trousers/Jeans/Polo Shirts/Accessories dilute focus and this is the same over-segmentation problem flagged in the Woman/Kids sends this month.
- 4. Add a caption line under 1-2 hero looks with a specific product name or price to give browsers a reason to click into a garment page instead of a generic category page.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sharp layers for the season's return to the desk`
