# ZARA TIMELESS
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a different animal from the last several Zara sends I've flagged — no lookbook grid-scroll, no "Discover the new collection" filler copy. "ZARA TIMELESS" leans into a full editorial campaign: a black-and-white hero of kids and a dog piled into a truck bed, sentimental sibling-bond copy ("The Bond We Share With Our Siblings, And The Memory That, Once, We Were All Kids"), and warm family-in-nature imagery before it ever gets to product.
- The problem is that it commits fully to brand storytelling and gives almost nothing back to a shopper. There's no price, no offer, no size/fit info, and the only call-to-action in the entire email is a single small text link — "Discover The Collection" — buried after four screens of mood photography. If I'm a parent scanning my inbox for a kids' clothing deal, this reads more like a magazine spread than a marketing email.
- Verdict: beautifully shot, on-brand, and genuinely distinct from Zara's recent formulaic sends — but it under-performs as a commercial email because it asks for a lot of scroll and gives almost no reason to click.

## What's working

- Real, cohesive editorial concept (sibling nostalgia, truck/lake/dog imagery) instead of the recycled category-grid skeleton seen in the 2026-09-13 "Kids | The New" and earlier sends.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage anywhere in the scroll.
- Knitwear color story (green, pink, blue, mustard, orange) is visually appealing and consistent with a fall assortment.
- Sender is instantly recognizable and the black-and-white truck hero has strong scroll-stopping visual hierarchy.

## What's weak

- Zero price, discount, or offer anywhere — nothing gives a reason to act now versus later.
- Only one CTA in the whole email ("Discover The Collection"), small, plain-text, and placed after roughly 4 full screens of mood imagery.
- Subject line "ZARA TIMELESS" gives no hint this is a kids' collection or what's actually for sale.
- "NEWSLETTER: ONLINE VERSION" junk text sits at the very top of the render, the first thing visible before any real content.
- No product-level detail (sizing, price, availability) anywhere in the body — pure lookbook, not a shoppable email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a single price/value anchor near the top ("Kids Knitwear from $XX") — this alone would double the reason to click, since right now there's nothing transactional in the entire send.
- 2. Turn "Discover The Collection" into a real button, and repeat it after the emotional copy block (screen 2) instead of making readers scroll through the full editorial before any CTA appears.
- 3. Make the subject line specific to the category — this is clearly a Kids campaign but the subject hides that.
- 4. Move or shrink the "NEWSLETTER: ONLINE VERSION" line so it's not the first thing a recipient's eye hits.
- 5. Add at least one shoppable product callout (e.g., "The Knitwear Edit") with a direct link, not just a link to the general collection.
