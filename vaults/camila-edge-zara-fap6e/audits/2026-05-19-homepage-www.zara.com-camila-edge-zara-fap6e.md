# Homepage snapshot · www.zara.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened Zara on my phone and got a wall. Not a hero, not a drop teaser, not even a campaign image — just a location selector staring at me like I'm filling out a customs form. For a brand that moves like Zara does with SRPLS and Studio drops, landing on this gate before you even see a single product is genuinely baffling. Right now this homepage looks like an airport kiosk, not a fashion brand.

## What's working

- **The ZARA wordmark** — the oversized serif logo is gorgeous and brand-confident. At least I know where I am.
- **"Remember selection" checkbox** — small UX courtesy; if I check it, I don't have to deal with this gate again next visit.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no visual noise, no pop-ups fighting the gate. The gate itself is technically well-executed.
- **"DO NOT SELL OR SHARE MY PERSONAL INFORMATION" footer** — visible privacy signal, which I appreciate as a US user who cares about data.

## What's weak

- **The entire viewport is a gate, not a homepage** — I came to shop, possibly to catch a new Studio or SRPLS drop, and I'm being asked to fill out a form before I see a single product.
- **No hero image or campaign teaser** — Zara's entire brand equity is editorial imagery. There's not one pixel of it here.
- **No seasonal or editorial context** — it's May and I have zero idea what Zara is pushing right now. Summer linen? A new collab? No clue.
- **"CONTINUE" is ambiguous to a first-time visitor** — continue to what? The button doesn't hint at what's on the other side.
- **Zero loyalty or account hook** — I've shopped Zara before. Nothing acknowledges that.

## Recommendations

- **Run the "Remember selection" checkbox as checked by default** for returning visitors — or skip this gate entirely for known US traffic via geo-IP, so repeat shoppers land directly on the homepage.
- **Add a background editorial image behind the form** — even a single campaign still would make this feel like Zara and create anticipation for what's behind the "CONTINUE" button.
- **Surface a teaser headline over the gate** — something like "NEW IN: Studio SS26" floated above or below the form so I know what I'm continuing *to*.
- **Replace the "CONTINUE" button label with something destination-aware** — "Shop United States" or "Enter US Store" gives me a reason to tap.
