# This week’s selection
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a Men's editorial drop — five editorial sections stacked vertically, all male models, finishing at a "Download Our App" footer — structurally indistinguishable from the July 11 "Man | Explore our newest collections" send scored 7/10. The creative quality is high: full-bleed photography, cohesive warm tones, and a summer-editorial visual thread running top to bottom. But the email commits two recurring Zara sins in one send: a subject line so generic it erases all division signal ("This week's selection" could be anything from Home to Kids), and zero commercial offer in the body.
- The preheader situation is the quiet killer here. Without a custom preheader, whatever junk text leaks from the email header will appear in the inbox next to "This week's selection" — the most anonymous subject Zara has sent in the audited batch. Combined with the heavy cadence (this is the seventh brand email in nine days), the inbox open environment is as difficult as it gets for this sender.
- Content-wise, there are some stronger section headers visible than in prior sends — "SUMMER STIRS: CHAPTER 14 IN HIGH SERVICE" is more evocative than "Discover what's new" — but "PRINTED BOY" with a bare "SEE" CTA underneath doesn't give a reason to click.

- No offer anywhere in the email — pure editorial with no price, no promotion, no urgency hook.
- "SEE" as the primary hero CTA is the weakest possible verb — no noun, no destination.

- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA from "SEE" to something with a destination.** "Shop the look" or "Explore printed styles" is minimal lift for a meaningful click signal.
- 3. **Write a real preheader.** Even one line — "Five summer looks worth having" — beats whatever navigation text is leaking.
- 4. **Add one time-bounded hook.** "New in for the week of July 18" works without requiring a discount; it earns the open on freshness alone.
- 5. **Vary the scaffold.** After two Women's editorials, a Men's, a Kids, a Home, and a Pre-Owned in nine days all using the same vertical stack, consider leading with a single-product hero rather than five stacked sections.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with correct `role="presentation"` usage, but contains two conflicting campaign code comments (A/B variants co-present in the same send), an invalid DOM nesting error in the preheader block, and a viewport directive that disables zoom — a hard accessibility failure. ### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Dual campaign codes (high confidence — confirmed in source comments):**

```html
<!-- CAMPAIGN_CODE:zara;2026_07_18_Norte_man_weekly_testb_18_07;US_en-->
...
<!-- CAMPAIGN_CODE:zara;2026_07_18_Norte_man_weekly_18_07;US-->
```

Two distinct campaign codes are embedded in the same rendered HTML — the base variant (`Norte_man_weekly_18_07`) and the B-test variant (`testb_18_07`). If both are parsed by the ESP's analytics layer, click/open attribution will be split or double-counted. This is consistent with a template merge error where both variant blocks were included rather than exclusively one.

**External Google Fonts request:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700..." rel="stylesheet">
```
This request will be blocked or stripped in Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail in certain privacy modes. No explicit `font-family` fallback is declared in the stylesheet — the `a` rule and body rule lack a `font-family`, meaning rendering will silently fall back to client defaults rather than a specified fallback stack.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=no` + `maximum-scale=1.0` (confirmed):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
This combination prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). iOS 10+ ignores `user-scalable=no` in Safari, but it remains a compliance flag and affects Android WebView-based clients.

**Semicolon delimiters in viewport meta:** The `content` value uses semicolons (`;`) as separators instead of the spec-required commas. Widely tolerated in practice but technically malformed.

**Invalid DOM nesting in preheader block (confirmed):**
```html
<table ...>
  ...
  <!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
  <div style="mso-hide:all;">
    <tr>   ← div directly wraps tr, outside any tbody/thead
      <td ...>
```
A `<div>` wrapping `<tr>` directly is invalid in both HTML and XHTML. Most rendering engines will recover, but this can cause preheader content to be misplaced or duplicated in strict parsers (Litmus notes this pattern causes issues in older Yahoo Mail).

**`role="presentation"` on tables:** Correctly applied. No issue.
