# ZARA SRPLS. COLLECTION 17
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's SRPLS collection 17 lookbook, and it is a catalog, not a sales email. The "ZARA SRPLS" wordmark with a red square is a strong header. Below it is a tight, cohesive editorial scroll: a black funnel-neck coat hero for WMN, a faux-shearling bomber for MN, and a kids pair for KDS. Each section has a hero image, two rows of four full-length looks, and a block of small uppercase copy.
- Visually it is clean and consistent, and it is a clear step up from the generic "Discover the new collection" sends of 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-06. It still has the usual Zara problem. There is no offer, no price, no product names and no CTA button. The only links I can see are the social row and "Download our app". A subscriber who opens it gets a nice scroll and no reason to act.
- Verdict: good editorial, weak commerce. SRPLS is a niche sub-line, so the audience is narrower than a main Woman or Man send. Even that audience is given nothing to click.

## What's working

- The "ZARA SRPLS" wordmark with the red square is an instantly recognizable, distinctive header.
- The three sections (/ WMN, / MN, / KDS) are clearly labeled and structured the same way.
- The photography is consistent: warm neutral backdrops, full-length looks in rows of four, and a close-up hero for each section.
- The faux-shearling bomber with the chunky burgundy knit is a strong, seasonal hero image.
- The kids section extends the send to families without cluttering it.

## What's weak

- There is no visible offer, price, or loyalty or member benefit.
- There is no CTA button. Nothing says "Shop women" or "Shop men", and the "Download our app" line at the bottom is the only prominent action.
- The product images give no names, prices or sizes, so a reader can't tell what to buy.
- The body copy is tiny, wide-tracked uppercase, which is hard to read on mobile and says nothing a shopper can act on.
- The email is very long, about 5,000px, and has repeated look rows with no CTA anywhere along it.
- The subject is shouty and gives no concrete reason to open. "COLLECTION 17" means little to most subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a clear "Shop SRPLS women / men / kids" button under each hero. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Tag each look with a product name and price, so the images become shoppable.
- 3. Put the "Faux-shearling bomber" or "Funnel-neck coat" in the first screen as a named hero.
- 4. Increase the body copy size and cut it to one line per section.
- 5. Trim the second row of looks in each section to shorten the scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Utility meets tailoring: new SRPLS`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Faux-shearling, funnel-neck coats and layered knits for women, men and kids.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the new SRPLS pieces, from outerwear to knitwear.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Responsys-sent (`news.zara.com/pub/as`, `responsysimages`) image-heavy template. The one hard QA failure is a missing plain-text part (0 chars). Every content image, including all hero images, has no alt text. The compliance and authentication warnings come from header capture through the relay and can't be confirmed from the data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 material links were probed and 2 were skipped (ranked by click likelihood). No broken, redirecting or erroring links were reported, so there is no confirmed link failure.
- The skipped links aren't identified, so their status is unverified.
- The open-tracking pixel `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=X0Gzc2X…` has no `alt`. It is a 1×1 beacon and should carry `alt=""`. This is a minor accessibility-lint issue, not a functional one.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 16 `<img>` flags (15 distinct images):** `img_01.jpg`, `img_02_en.jpg`, `img_03.jpg`, `img_04_en.jpg`, `img_05.jpg`, `img_06b_en.jpg` (campaign content), `latredes142.jpg` (appears twice), `rrss1b`–`rrss6b.jpg` (social icons), `download_en_b.jpg`, and the `/pub/as` pixel.
  - The campaign images (`.../campaigns/2026/11.noviembre/2026_11_12_Norte_al…`) appear to carry the entire message. With images blocked, or in a screen reader, the email would render as the preheader and footer only.
  - The social icons have no accessible name, so the links they wrap are unlabeled.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;">` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4). It also uses `;` separators instead of `,`.
- **Invalid markup:** `<div style="mso-hide:all;">` is placed directly inside `<table role="presentation" width="640">`, wrapping a `<tr>`. A `<div>` is not a valid child of `<table>`, so browsers and some clients will hoist or drop it. The `mso-hide` also won't apply as intended.
- **Web font:** `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed…&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext">` is stripped by Gmail and several other clients. It loads a Cyrillic subset for a `US_en` send. The stack needs a fallback, and I couldn't see whether the visible font-family declarations include one.
- **Hidden preheader:** The preheader pads with `&#8199;&#847;` repeats. This is a standard technique and is fine.
