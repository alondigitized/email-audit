# This week’s selection
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Men's "This week's selection" — another straight editorial grid, structurally identical to the "Edition AW26" send I audited on 2026-08-01 (4.00/10) and the general pattern seen across every Zara vertical this summer (Women's, Kids, STUDIO). It's a street-style hero image bleeding into five category sections — Street Fits, New Collection, Everyday Rhythm, Zara Athleticz — each just a photo grid with underlined product-type labels (KNITWEAR, SHORTS, JACKETS, TROUSERS, FOOTWEAR). There is zero price, zero offer, zero copy beyond category names.
- This is a lookbook, not a commerce email. It photographs beautifully and reads like a magazine spread, but for a subscriber deciding whether to click there is nothing here to click toward — no deal, no new-arrival urgency, no sizing or availability info, just "here are some categories, go browse." Given the brand history of near-identical sends scoring 4-5/10 repeatedly, this is Zara running the same low-conversion playbook on the men's line now too.
- **Judgement: a well-shot, poorly-converting email.** Visually competent, commercially inert.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent visual system — full-bleed photography, consistent underlined labels, generous whitespace, no render bugs or broken images.
- Hero image is striking and on-brand (two models in coordinated streetwear against an urban backdrop).
- Section labels (STREET FITS, NEW COLLECTION, EVERYDAY RHYTHM, ZARA ATHLETICZ) give some narrative structure instead of just raw category tiles.
- Footer app-download CTA and social icons are clearly present and unbroken.

## What's weak

- No offer of any kind — no price, no % off, no "new" badge, nothing to justify opening now versus any other week.
- No single primary CTA — six section headers each with 2-3 unlinked-looking product labels means the eye has nowhere obvious to land.
- Subject "This week's selection" is maximally generic — could describe literally any Zara send, any week, any brand.
- Product labels (KNITWEAR, SHORTS, T-SHIRTS) give zero product specificity — no hero item, no "shop this look."
- "ZARA ATHLETICZ" is an odd, jarring stylization (extra Z) with no explanation — reads like a typo on first glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and lead with it.** Instead of six equal-weight category grids, feature one item (e.g., the tan jacket or the sneaker) with a real CTA — "Shop the Jacket" — above the fold. Category browsing can stay lower in the email.
- 2. **Add a reason to click now.** Even a soft one ("New drops every Thursday" or "Just landed") beats zero temporal signal.
- 3. **Give the subject a concrete anchor** — a product, a collection name, or a season cue instead of "selection."
- 4. **Fix or explain "ZARA ATHLETICZ"** — it reads as a typo, not a sub-brand name, without any supporting copy.
- 5. **Add real preview text.** Right now there's nothing complementary to see in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: Street Fits for August`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The jacket everyone's asking about`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `This week's street style, shot in NYC`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Knitwear, jackets, and the bag we can't stop wearing`
