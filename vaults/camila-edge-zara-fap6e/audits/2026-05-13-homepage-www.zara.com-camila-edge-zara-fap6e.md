# Homepage snapshot · www.zara.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- What I got instead of a homepage is a location gate — the full viewport is occupied by a country/language selector before I can see a single product or campaign image. For a brand that built its reputation on drop culture and editorial storytelling, this is a surprisingly cold first impression. It feels like a bouncer checking IDs at the door of a store whose window display is blacked out.

## What's working

- **Clean rendering:** The page itself is technically bug-free — logo is sharp, the dropdowns and button are legible, nothing is broken.
- **"Remember selection" checkbox:** Small detail, but it at least acknowledges I might come back — saves one click on future visits if I check it.
- **CCPA footer link:** "DO NOT SELL OR SHARE MY PERSONAL INFORMATION" is right there at the bottom. Trust signal for privacy-conscious shoppers, even if it's legally mandated.

## What's weak

- **Zero product, zero campaign, zero editorial:** I came to check if the SRPLS drop landed and I'm staring at a form. This is a dead end for engagement.
- **No personalization bypass:** I've definitely been to zara.com before. There's no cookie recognition that skips me past this gate — the site is treating me like a first-time visitor every single time this screenshot was captured.
- **CONTINUE is the only CTA and it goes nowhere useful yet:** Tapping it just gets me to the actual site. It's a delayed bounce risk — any friction before the content increases drop-off on mobile.
- **No brand energy whatsoever:** The ZARA wordmark is big and bold but it's floating above a utility form. No image, no copy, no vibe. This is Zara — the brand invented the idea that fast fashion could feel editorial, and this opening screen communicates nothing about that.
- **Lower half of the viewport is completely blank:** Large dead white space below the form. On a mobile screen that real estate is precious and it's wasted.

## Recommendations

- **Cookie-detect returning visitors and bypass the gate automatically.** If I've selected United States / English before, skip straight to the homepage. This is standard practice and Zara is leaving engagement on the table by not doing it.
- **Layer a campaign teaser behind the selector form** — a blurred or partial editorial image in the background would make the gate feel like it's part of the brand, not a dead stop. Even a full-bleed SRPLS shot with the form overlaid would retain brand energy.
- **Replace the blank lower half with a micro-preview rail** — three product thumbnails from the current drop, visible but locked/blurred until location is confirmed. Creates immediate pull to complete the gate.
- **Add a one-line campaign hook beneath the logo** — something like "NEW IN: SRPLS 09" directly under the wordmark costs nothing and orients drop-watchers instantly.
