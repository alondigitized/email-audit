# 40% OFF selected items
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's Black Friday teaser. It is the first send in the recent run with a real offer: "40% OFF SELECTED ITEMS" in huge red type, with online timing of 5PM ET today and stores on 11/27. The earlier sends (Studio Kids on 11/22, the Woman weekly edit on 11/20, Ski on 11/19) were all lookbooks with no offer. This one finally gives the subscriber a reason to act.
- The execution is thin, though. "Selected items" never says which items. There is no product, no price and no button. The only links are a text nav strip (WOMAN / MAN / KIDS / BEAUTY / MASSIMO DUTTI) and a "DOWNLOAD OUR APP" line. The "starting time may vary a few minutes due to technical reasons" note is a bit awkward, but it is honest. The tri-panel hero of a woman, a man and a kid in coats and a puffer, under a cropped red ZARA wordmark, is strong and on-season.
- Overall it is a clear announcement, not a conversion email. It beats the lookbook run on relevance. It is still a countdown poster with a nav bar, and it asks the subscriber to do all the work after the click.

## What's working

- The "40% OFF" headline is the clear focal point. The large red type on white reads instantly.
- The tri-panel monochrome hero (woman, man, kid in outerwear) fits late-November weather and covers every segment.
- The timing is specific and credible: 5PM ET online, 11/27 in stores.
- The red-on-white palette and the ZARA wordmark keep the brand look consistent and uncluttered.
- The note that the start time may vary is honest and sets expectations.

## What's weak

- There is no button. Nothing says "Shop now" or "Set a reminder," and the only way in is the thin text nav.
- "Selected items" is vague. No product, price or category is shown, so nothing shows what the 40% applies to.
- The "starts today at 5PM ET" offer is not live yet. Someone opening before 5PM has nothing to click and no way to be reminded.
- The nav strip is cramped, and "MASSIMO DUTTI" looks clipped at the right edge.
- There is a large empty gap above the nav. "DOWNLOAD OUR APP" is a bare heading with no badges or link visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a primary button below the headline, such as "Shop the 40% edit" or "Remind me at 5PM ET." It should land on a live sale page or a reminder signup.
- 2. Show 3–4 hero products with sale prices under the headline, so "selected items" becomes something concrete.
- 3. Replace the text nav with larger category tiles (Woman, Man, Kids) that each link to that sale.
- 4. Fix the nav spacing so Massimo Dutti isn't cropped, and add real App Store and Google Play badges.
- 5. Tighten the vertical whitespace so the nav sits higher.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a Responsys-built XHTML 1.0 table layout with a mobile media query and an MSO fallback. QA flagged one FAIL (no plain-text part) and 4 warnings. Nine images lack alt text and there are markup and accessibility defects. The HTML was truncated, so links, footer, unsubscribe and UTM parameters could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable.** The source is cut off after the preheader, so I can't audit CTA hrefs, redirect wrappers or the footer links.
- **Tracking pixel has no alt.** `https://news.zara.com/pub/as?_ri_=X0Gzc2X%3D...` is the Responsys open pixel. QA lists it as image "as" with no alt. This is low severity, but it should have `alt=""` and explicit `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Tracking token in URL.** The `_ri_` parameter is a long recipient-scoped token. Forwarding the email exposes it.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid table structure.** `<div style="mso-hide:all;">` wraps a `<tr>` directly inside `<table width="640" class="width90">`. A `<div>` is not a valid child of `<table>`, so clients may reflow or drop the preheader row. Use `<tr><td><div style="mso-hide:all">…` instead.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;">` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4. The semicolon separators are also non-standard; commas are the spec.
- **Missing alt text (9 images, per QA):**
  - `image_01b.jpg`
  - `image-woman-en-us-us_0.jpg`
  - `menu_v5d_01b_en.jpg` through `menu_v5d_05b_en.jpg`
  - `download_en_b.jpg`
  - the `pub/as` pixel

  The `menu_v5d_*` images look like navigation, so with images blocked or a screen reader the nav is unusable.
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, although the campaign is `US_en`.
- **Low link contrast.** The global rule `a { color: #999898; }` on a `#ffffff` background is about 2.9:1, below the 4.5:1 AA minimum. Inline overrides could not be checked because the source is truncated.
- **Tiny mobile text classes.** `.font5` (5px), `.font7` (7px) and `.font8` (8px) are defined in the media query. I couldn't confirm where they're applied.
- **Web font via `<link>`.** The Google Fonts `<link>` (Roboto Condensed) is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. The Outlook-only `font-family: Arial` override covers MSO, but no fallback stack is visible for other clients. The `<link>` is also not self-closed under the XHTML doctype.
- **Internal comments left in production HTML.** Examples are `CAMPAIGN_CODE:zara;2026_11_25_norte_promo_bf_prewarming;US_en` and the Spanish macro comments. This is low severity and adds payload and leaks internal naming.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens such as `{{ }}`, `%%`, `$!` or `[[ ]]` appear in the visible source. The macro comments are inert, though the truncated body could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance

- **[WARN] No physical address detected.** CAN-SPAM requires one. This is unconfirmed because the footer is truncated, so re-check against the full HTML.
