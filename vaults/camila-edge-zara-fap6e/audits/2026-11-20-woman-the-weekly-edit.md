# Woman | The weekly edit
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's women's weekly lookbook, and it's a catalog with no offer. The scroll runs through "The Projects AW26", "Velvet Nights", "TRF Collection", "The Golden Edit" (makeup), "Leather Edit" (shoes) and a Fifth Avenue flagship news item. The imagery is strong and the moody dark palette is on-brand. The oversized black sans headlines are confident but cut up by forced word breaks ("PRO / JECTS", "CO / LLEC / TI / ON", "LEA / THER").
- The email does a lot of brand-building and very little selling. I see no price, no promo, no deadline and no button. The only calls to action are tiny "VIEW" text links and small category labels like "ACCESSORIES" and "COATS". The sections don't follow one story either: velvet, coats, makeup, boots and a store opening all get equal weight. Compared with the 2026-11-19 "Ski Collection" send, which had one clear seasonal idea, this one is scattered.
- Judgement: a good-looking, forgettable send that works for people already shopping Zara and does little for anyone else. It's average, around 4-5.

## What's working

- The dark, editorial photography is cohesive and clearly in the AW26 mood.
- The big ZARA wordmark in the hero makes the sender instantly recognizable.
- Category labels under the image pairs (Jackets, Shirts, Coats, Blazers) give shoppers a way to browse.
- The Golden Edit makeup section adds variety and a cross-sell beyond clothes.
- The footer is clean, with an app download prompt and social links.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, or urgency anywhere in the email.
- CTAs are tiny "VIEW" text links and small caption labels, with no real buttons.
- The forced hyphenation in the headlines ("CO LLEC TI ON", "LEA THER") hurts readability and looks like a layout accident.
- Too many unrelated themes dilute the message. Nothing in the hero tells you what to do.
- The wordmark is cropped on the right edge ("ZARA" clipped to "ZAR"), which reads as a render flaw.
- The photos carry no product names or prices, so the reader gets no product detail.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero story, for example "Velvet Nights", give it a real button ("Shop velvet") and put it first.
- 2. Cut the module count from about seven to three or four, and link to specific products instead of broad categories.
- 3. Replace the forced word breaks with headlines that fit on one line.
- 4. Add at least one anchor: a few featured pieces with prices, or a clear member or app benefit.
- 5. Fix the cropped wordmark so the full "ZARA" shows.
