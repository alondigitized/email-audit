# Woman | Discover what’s new
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's standard women's editorial drop — four content sections (ZW Collection, Seasonal Updates, TW Collection, Selected Pieces), full-bleed photography, minimal copy, zero discount. Visually immaculate in Zara's house style: muted earth tones, aspirational lifestyle shots, sparse labels. There's nothing broken and nothing exciting.
- The single biggest issue is invisible to the casual eye but obvious to anyone checking their inbox: the subject line is **word-for-word identical** to the June 19 send scored 7/10 in this audit. "Woman | Discover what's new" ran two weeks ago. Running it again on July 3rd — the eve of a US holiday sale weekend where competitors are flooding inboxes with 4th of July deals — makes this email disappear rather than compete. For a brand that has been almost exclusively editorial (no discount sends since the June 24 sale launch), this is the worst time to recycle a subject.
- The content itself is fresh — ZW Collection, Seasonal Updates, TW Collection, and an accessories row (espadrilles, bag, sandals) are seasonally appropriate. But without a single CTA button, a price point, or any urgency signal, this email is asking a subscriber to actively decide to browse without giving them any reason to. It's a bet that Zara's brand pull alone carries the click.

## What's working

- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout artifacts. The four-section editorial grid is crisp on this render.
- **Seasonally appropriate** — summer whites, espadrilles, and "TAN" / "SUMMER HITS" labels land right for early July.
- **Zara logo hero** — the opening full-bleed with the Zara wordmark anchors brand recognition immediately; no one is confused about who sent this.
- **Accessories section** — "Selected Pieces" with espadrilles, bag, and sandals is the most product-specific section in the email and the most actionable, giving a clear shopping hook.

## What's weak

- **Exact duplicate subject line** — "Woman | Discover what's new" ran June 19. Repeating it verbatim 14 days later trains subscribers to ignore the send.
- **No CTA buttons** — every action requires clicking a photo. No labeled buttons, no "Shop now / Shop dresses / Shop the edit" prompts. The intent is editorial; the result is vague.
- **Zero urgency or offer** — July 3rd is a prime sale-weekend moment. Nothing in this email acknowledges it, and the brand hasn't discounted since June 24.
- **Section labels are opaque** — "ZW Collection" and "TW Collection" mean nothing without explanation. A new subscriber has no idea what either line stands for.
- **"Download Our App" footer CTA** — the only explicit CTA in this email is an app download ask buried at the bottom, not a shop link.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop recycling the subject line** — this is the single highest-lift change. Even minimal variation avoids the repeat-signal trap in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Woman | New this week: summer whites + espadrilles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Zara Woman | The ZW Collection is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four edits: ZW Collection, seasonal updates, TW, and accessories.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New dresses, denim, and summer-ready sandals — all just dropped.`
- 2. **Add one shop button per section** — a single labeled button (e.g., "Shop Dresses" under ZW Collection) converts browsers who won't click a photo but will click a button. Doesn't break the editorial aesthetic.
- 3. **Lean into the accessories section** — espadrilles, bags, and sandals are the most concrete and purchase-ready products in the email. Move this section up or give it a stronger header and it becomes the natural click magnet.
- 4. **Name the collections** — replace "ZW Collection" / "TW Collection" with short descriptors ("The White Edit" / "The Tailored Edit") for subscribers who aren't Zara insiders.
- 5. **Acknowledge July 4th weekend** — even an editorial-first brand can gesture at the season. "Long weekend dressing" or similar copy acknowledges the moment without discounting.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 3a — Invalid div nested inside table (HIGH)**
```html
<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <tr>
    <td ...>
```
A `<div>` cannot be a direct child of `<table>` (outside `<td>`/`<th>`). This is malformed markup. Gecko and WebKit browsers auto-correct it by moving the div outside the table, which breaks the intended MSO hide logic and may cause layout drift in Gmail web, Apple Mail, and Samsung Mail. The correct pattern is to wrap the `<tr>` in `<!--[if !mso]><![endif]-->` conditional comments instead.

**Issue 3b — Viewport meta uses semicolons as separators (MEDIUM)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;">
```
The HTML spec requires commas as separators for viewport content values. Some parsers (notably older Android WebView) may misparse semicolon-delimited values, breaking responsive scaling on those clients.

**Issue 3c — `user-scalable=no` violates WCAG 1.4.4 (MEDIUM)**
The `maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no` combination prevents users from pinching to zoom. This fails WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked by iOS 10+ Safari regardless, making the directive both an accessibility violation and a no-op on the most common mobile client.

**Issue 3d — Invalid CSS value on `.heightmobile` (LOW)**
```css
.heightmobile { height: auto!important; line-height: auto!important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `line-height`. Valid values are `normal`, a unitless number, a length, or a percentage. Browsers interpret this as `normal`, but it may produce unexpected behavior in Outlook for iOS and should be corrected to `line-height: normal`.

**Issue 3e — External Google Fonts blocked by major clients (LOW)**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Yahoo Mail block external `<link>` stylesheets. Roboto Condensed will silently not load for the majority of recipients. This is an accepted limitation of email rendering, but if brand-accurate typography is required, embed the critical `@font-face` declarations inline in `<style>` instead.
