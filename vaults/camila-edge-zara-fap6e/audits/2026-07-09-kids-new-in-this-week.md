# Kids | New in this week
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Kids' standard new-arrivals editorial — "Kids | New in this week" — and it's structurally identical to the July 2 "Kids | This week's arrivals" send (scored 6/10 five days ago). Same vertical editorial grid, same zero-discount posture, same minimal copy under each photo block. The visual execution is clean: summery palette, diverse child models, a range of silhouettes from dresses and overalls to beachwear in the final shot. It's competent and on-brand but completely interchangeable with the prior send.
- The core problem is cadence collapse. A parent who opened the July 2 kids drop opens this and sees an almost identical email five days later. There's nothing to differentiate the two sends — not a thematic hook, not a seasonal urgency beat, not a single price signal. That "new in" framing does almost no work when it's competing against a send from last week that said essentially the same thing.
- Clear judgement: a passable editorial refresh that Zara's design team can produce in their sleep, but one that will train subscribers to stop opening kids emails.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered grid layout with no render bugs or broken images — Zara's template execution is consistently solid.
- Diverse child models across multiple age groups and ethnicities give the email broad appeal within the kids category.
- Summer-appropriate clothing (beachwear finale, stripes, denim overalls) signals seasonal relevance without needing explicit copy.
- Section structure allows quick visual scanning — a busy parent can read the whole email in under 10 seconds.

## What's weak

- No differentiation from the July 2 send — same format, same no-discount approach, five days apart. Cadence fatigue is real.
- No thematic editorial hook — the July 7 Zara Home metallic send at least had a material story; this is just "new stuff."
- No visible CTA button — section links are present but small, with no single dominant action to drive clicks.
- "This week" urgency framing is hollow when it arrives five days after a nearly identical kids email.
- No price, no offer, no loyalty signal — zero transactional pull for a parent actually looking to buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the cadence or differentiate harder.** Two near-identical kids drops in five days is too much. Either skip this send and roll it into a bi-weekly digest, or give each send a distinct editorial theme (e.g., "Back to Play" for outdoors, "First Day Ready" for school). The theme should appear as a hero headline, not just in the subject line.
- 2. **Add one dominant CTA button** — "Shop New In Kids" centered above the footer would capture the intent of every parent who made it to the bottom.
- 3. **Surface a price signal or member exclusive** — even "From $12.90" under the lead section sets a transactional anchor without compromising editorial tone.

- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh arrivals for kids · Shop before it goes`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `New silhouettes just dropped — from beach-ready to everyday essentials.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `Dresses, denim, and swim — this week's new-in for kids is here.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=no` in viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0;
  maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
Prevents users from zooming. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Also, the separator used is `;` — the spec requires `,`. Browsers tolerate it, but it is malformed.

**`line-height: auto` — invalid CSS value:**
```css
.heightmobile { height: auto!important; line-height: auto!important; }
```
`line-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value (`normal` is the keyword; `auto` is silently ignored). Any client applying this class gets no `line-height` override.

**Invalid DOM nesting in preheader block:**
```html
<table ...>
  <!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
  <div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <tr>
    <td ...>
```
A `<div>` is injected as a direct child of `<table>` (between `<table>` and `<tr>`), which is invalid HTML. Non-MSO clients will attempt to error-correct this, with unpredictable results depending on the client's parser. The conditional comment pattern here (`<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->`  with an unclosed `<div>`) is also structurally risky.

**External Google Fonts request:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700
  &display=swap&subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext" rel="stylesheet">
```
The majority of email clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail on some configs) block or ignore external `<link>` stylesheet requests. The font will silently fall back to system fonts with no visual warning. The `subset=cyrillic,cyrillic-ext` parameter is irrelevant for a `US_en` send and suggests a shared template that was not locale-trimmed.
