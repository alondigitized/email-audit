# Woman | Explore our newest collections
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the exact same skeleton I've flagged in every "Woman | The New" send since July — hero editorial shot, then a stack of category blocks (ZW Collection, Nineties, TRF Collection, Back To Office, The Item, Selected Pieces) with zero prices, zero offers, and zero copy beyond category labels. "Explore our newest collections" is a slightly different subject wrapper on the identical creative pattern I scored 4.00/10 on 2026-08-14 and 4.00/10 on 2026-08-07.
- The photography is competent — moody, cohesive, on-brand — but this is a lookbook, not a marketing email. There's no CTA button anywhere, no discount, no urgency, nothing that differentiates this send from just browsing the Zara homepage. "Back to office" is a mildly seasonal nod (late August) but it's buried as one grid module among six, not a hero theme.
- Judgement: another pure-brand-awareness send with no commercial hook. Fine for a loyalist who already intends to shop; does nothing to convert or re-engage anyone on the fence. Same category, same score band as the last four Zara sends I've reviewed.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean and on-brand, no render bugs, full-bleed and legible at both header and footer.
- Consistent visual rhythm — image pairs + underlined text link under each pair — makes the email easy to scan quickly.
- "BACK TO OFFICE" module has a specific seasonal-relevant hook (return-to-office dressing) that ties to late-August timing.
- No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- Zero price or offer anywhere in the email — nothing to click "for" beyond curiosity.
- No primary CTA button; only small underlined category text links (SHIRTS, DRESSES, etc.) buried under each image.
- Subject "Explore our newest collections" is generic filler — indistinguishable from the last three sends' subjects.
- Seven separate category modules with no hierarchy — the eye has nowhere obvious to land first.
- "THE ITEM" module has a distracting double-exposed wordmark overlay on the model image that reads as a visual glitch, not intentional design.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product + one clear CTA button ("Shop Back to Office") instead of seven flat category grids — pick the strongest seasonal story and commit to it above the fold.
- 2. Add real merchandising info: at least one price point, new-arrival count, or "just landed" framing to give a reason to click now.
- 3. Fix or remove the ghosted double-wordmark treatment on "THE ITEM" hero image — it currently looks like a rendering error.
- 4. Replace the underlined-text-only links with visible tap targets (buttons) — critical for mobile thumb-tap accuracy.
- 5. Vary the subject line meaningfully from prior sends to avoid list fatigue (four "Woman" sends in three weeks with near-identical language).
