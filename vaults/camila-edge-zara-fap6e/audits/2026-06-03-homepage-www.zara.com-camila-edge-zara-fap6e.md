# Homepage snapshot · www.zara.com · 2026-06-03
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- I didn't even make it to the homepage — what loaded is a location/language gate. Logo, two dropdowns, a "CONTINUE" button, and a checkbox. That's the entire screen. For a brand I already know and specifically navigated to, having to confirm I'm in the United States (in English, obviously) before seeing a single product is friction I genuinely did not need. It tells me nothing about what Zara is pushing right now.

## What's working

- **The "CONTINUE" button** is clean, high-contrast, easy to tap on mobile — no hunting required.
- **"Remember selection" checkbox** is a small but real UX courtesy — I won't have to do this again next visit if I check it.
- **No render bugs** — the gate itself is technically clean, loads fast visually, no layout chaos.

## What's weak

- **The gate itself is the problem** — I already know I'm in the US, I already know I want English. This is dead air between my intent and Zara's content.
- **Zero brand presence above the fold** — nothing about what's new, what's dropping, what collection is live. The ZARA wordmark is doing all the heavy lifting and it's doing zero persuasion.
- **No skip or auto-detect** — Zara almost certainly has my locale from prior sessions or browser signals. Forcing me to confirm anyway feels like a design decision that chose caution over experience.
- **"DO NOT SELL OR SHARE MY PERSONAL INFORMATION"** sitting at the very bottom in small caps is a weird note to end on — legally required but placement makes it oddly prominent on an otherwise empty screen.

## Recommendations

- **Auto-detect location and language** from browser/IP and pre-fill the gate, or skip it entirely for returning users (cookie already set from last session — use it).
- **Put a hero image behind the gate** — let the new collection bleed through as a blurred/dimmed background so I know what I'm clicking through to. The anticipation would make the gate feel like a reveal rather than a roadblock.
- **Swap "Remember selection" to on by default** — most users want this; make them opt out, not in.
- **Add a "Continue as Guest in United States" auto-route** that fires after 2 seconds if no interaction, or a "Skip" link for signed-in users.
