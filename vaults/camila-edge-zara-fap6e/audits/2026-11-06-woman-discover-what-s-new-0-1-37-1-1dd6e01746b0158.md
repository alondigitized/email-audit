# Woman | Discover what’s new
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's usual image-first lookbook scroll, this time built around "The New Party Edit." It opens on a black-and-white trio with the oversized ZARA wordmark, then moves through party dressing, a "Laila Gohar & Paz" holiday-table story, "The Wardrobe Collection," "Evening," and a closing "The Item" bomber jacket. The photography is strong and the typography is bold. As a seasonal mood piece for holiday dressing it works.
- It has almost no commerce. There is no price, no offer, and no button, only small "VIEW" text links and category labels (Jackets, Dresses, Shirts, Knitwear, Blazers, Coats, Trousers). The 2026-11-06 send also gives a subscriber nothing new next to the 2026-11-01 Kids fleece edit or the 2026-10-30 "Woman | This week at Zara" send. - The verdict is a well-crafted, low-urgency catalogue. It will please people already in a Zara mood and do little for anyone who needs a reason to click today.

## What's working

- The party edit matches the season. Sequins, satin camisoles and tuxedo-style blazers suit early-November holiday shopping.
- The black-and-white hero with the giant ZARA wordmark is striking and unmistakably on-brand.
- The category rows (Jackets/Dresses/Shirts, Knitwear/Blazers, Coats/Trousers) give a quick way to browse.
- The "Laila Gohar & Paz" family story adds warmth and variety to an otherwise austere scroll.
- The closing "The Item" pairing of a model shot and a flat-lay of the frog-fastening bomber puts a single product in focus.

## What's weak

- There are no real buttons. The "VIEW" links are tiny black text beside huge headlines, so the primary CTA is easy to miss.
- No price, offer, loyalty benefit, shipping message or deadline appears anywhere, so there is no reason to act now.
- Several headline treatments break awkwardly: "THE WARD ROBE CO LLEC TI ON" and "EVE NING" are chopped mid-word. It looks deliberate but hurts readability.
- Category labels such as "JACKETS" and "KNITWEAR" are very small and sit under the images, so tap targets look tiny on mobile.
- The body is very long for an email with no hierarchy of offers. Women's party wear, a kids/family story and menswear-looking evening looks (the "Evening" hero shows men) all sit in one scroll with no clear lead.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one visible, button-style CTA under the hero, such as "Shop the party edit", and make the "VIEW" links full-width buttons.
- 2. Put the hero product (the white satin top or the tuxedo dress) first, with a name and price, so the opening image leads somewhere specific.
- 3. Add a time or availability hook, such as "Party season: order by Dec 15 for delivery" or "Online now, in stores this week."
- 4. Make the category labels larger and clearly tappable, and fix the mid-word breaks in the headlines.
- 5. Cut the scroll. Lead with the party edit and drop or defer the secondary stories.
