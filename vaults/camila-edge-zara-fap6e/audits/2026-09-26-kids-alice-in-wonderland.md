# Kids | Alice in Wonderland
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Kids doing a Disney "Alice in Wonderland" collaboration, and it's the most conceptually distinct send I've seen from this brand in weeks — a genuine licensed-IP capsule instead of another generic lookbook scroll. The editorial is striking: high-contrast color grading (yellow, red, blue tints), the Cheshire Cat hoodie, the Queen of Hearts dress, the classic Alice blue-dress-and-apron look. It reads as a real creative swing, not a template fill.
- But there's zero commerce information anywhere in this render. No price, no "shop now" button, no product names, no single tappable CTA on any image — it's a pure lookbook of moody photography with a "Download our App" footer as the only real action. For a marketing email this is style over substance: gorgeous, but it asks nothing of the reader except to scroll and admire.
- Judgement: strong creative differentiation, weak commercial mechanics. It'll get looked at, but it won't convert without a clear next step.

## What's working

- Distinct, on-theme visual concept (Disney x Alice in Wonderland) breaks the Zara lookbook-fatigue pattern flagged repeatedly since August.
- Color-tinted portraits (yellow Mad Hatter, red Queen of Hearts, blue "Cheshire" close-up) create a memorable, cohesive mood board.
- Character-specific product moments are legible at a glance — bunny-ear Alice costume, striped Cheshire Cat hoodie, checkerboard Queen of Hearts dress.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken assets.
- Halloween teaser strip near the footer (pumpkin, black cat, spider) hints at a next campaign without cluttering this one.

## What's weak

- No visible price, discount, or offer anywhere in the email — this is closer to a mood film than a retail email.
- No CTA buttons on any product image; the only clickable-looking element is "Download our App," which isn't category-relevant to a Disney kids capsule.
- No product names or "shop the look" labels, so a parent can't tell what's actually for sale versus styled-for-photo.
- Extremely long vertical scroll of full-bleed images with no section breaks or shop links compounds the "lookbook, not storefront" feel.
- Halloween costume-like styling (bunny ears, cat hoodie) could read as literal costumes rather than everyday kidswear, creating ambiguity about what's actually purchasable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a persistent "Shop the Collection" button after the hero and repeated every 2-3 image blocks — right now there's no path to purchase at all.
- 2. Overlay or caption product names/prices directly on or under each character look (e.g., "Queen of Hearts Dress — $49.90") so parents can identify sellable items instead of guessing.
- 3. Lead with a concrete hook in the subject/preheader — "New arrivals" energy, but this deserves urgency or specificity given it's a limited licensed capsule.
- 4. Trim the scroll — 8+ full-bleed images with no CTA is a lot of asks-nothing real estate; cut to 4-5 hero looks each paired with a shop link.
- 5. Clarify costume vs. everyday-wear positioning so the capsule doesn't get mentally filed as "Halloween costume email" and skipped by parents shopping for regular clothes.
