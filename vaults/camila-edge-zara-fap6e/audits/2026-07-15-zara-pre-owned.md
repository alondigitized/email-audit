# ZARA PRE-OWNED
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's Pre-Owned platform send — a meaningful format departure from the relentless new-arrivals editorial drumbeat that has dominated their July cadence (three division drops in three days, July 9–11). The hook is smart: crochet is inherently vintage-coded, so leading with "CROCHET, REVISITED. NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" is self-aware positioning that earns its cleverness. The hero image — a woman in a bold patterned knit sitting in what reads as a curated vintage setting — is visually arresting and tonally distinct from Zara's usual full-bleed product lookbook shots.
- The problem is execution depth. After the strong hero, the email sprawls: Women → Shoes & Accessories → Summer Shouts → Mini Collection → Kids Collection → Repair | Donate footer. That's five category blocks and a services pitch compressed into a single send. None of them carry a price anchor. Pre-owned implies a savings proposition but the email never makes it concrete — not even a "from €X" in the subhead. It's brand storytelling dressed up as a commerce email.
- Verdict: a 6. Better than Zara's recent formulaic editorial sends in concept, weaker in conversion mechanics. The crochet editorial gives it a genuine campaign identity; the lack of any price signal or urgency trigger leaves real lift on the table.

## What's working

- **Hero tagline lands.** "CROCHET, REVISITED. NOT NEW BUT STILL COOL!" is self-aware and genuinely on-trend; crochet's vintage associations make pre-owned the natural home for it.
- **Distinctive hero image.** The styled editorial shot (model in patterned crochet, period-esque setting) looks nothing like Zara's standard white-background or street-editorial norm — it stops the scroll.
- **Platform comprehensiveness signal.** Five category blocks (Women, Shoes, Summer, Mini, Kids) communicate that Pre-Owned is a real breadth platform, not a token capsule.
- **Sustainability ecosystem reinforced.** The "More services: Repair | Donate" footer elevates Pre-Owned from resale to circular fashion — a credibility layer competitors rarely make visible.
- **#ZARAPREOWNED hashtag.** Visible, branded, and creates UGC framing without feeling forced.

## What's weak

- **No price anchor anywhere.** Pre-owned's core promise is value; "from €15" or "up to 60% off retail" in the hero would immediately answer the subscriber's implicit question: why buy second-hand Zara instead of new?
- **Subject line is a label, not a hook.** "ZARA PRE-OWNED" tells you what it is; it says nothing about why today is the moment to click.
- **Five category blocks kill momentum.** Women's picks → Shoes → Summer Shouts → Mini → Kids is too much inventory breadth with no editorial through-line after the crochet hero — focus collapses at scroll depth 3.
- **CTA button copy unreadable at mobile scale.** The button below the hero is too small to confirm its copy in the render; if it says "DISCOVER" or "EXPLORE," that's a wasted verb where "SHOP CROCHET PICKS" would do real work.
- **Zero social proof.** No ratings, no "X items listed this week," no "top picks this season" framing — nothing that validates the Pre-Owned inventory as curated and fresh.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the hero subhead.** A single line — "From €12 · Authenticated by Zara" — below the tagline would transform this from editorial to shopping intent. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject line to include a hook.** The current subject is a directory label. Give it a reason-to-open today.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pre-owned, but make it crochet`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Secondhand Zara — prices that make sense`
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** If it's leaking footer junk or repeating the subject, swap it for copy that complements.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Women, Kids, Shoes & Accessories — all authenticated, all pre-owned`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The crochet trend, sourced from closets like yours — from €12`
- 4. **Cut to two featured categories.** Lead with Women (the crochet anchor) and one curated "Staff Picks" cross-category block. The five-section sprawl undermines the editorial discipline that makes Zara's brand sends work.
- 5. **Add a freshness/urgency signal above the product grid.** "1,800+ new listings this week" or "These 12 crochet pieces were just added" creates implicit scarcity without a fake deadline.
- 6. **Make the CTA copy specific.** "SHOP CROCHET PRE-OWNED" or "BROWSE NEW LISTINGS" > any variant of "DISCOVER."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Google Fonts stripped by major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Roboto+Condensed:400,700&display=swap..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip external `<link>` tags. No system-font fallback is declared alongside this request. Recipients on those clients will render in the client's default serif/sans-serif — not Roboto Condensed. Fix: add a CSS fallback stack (`'Roboto Condensed', Arial, sans-serif`) in inline styles or a `<style>` block declaration.

**[HIGH] Preheader hidden from all Outlook versions**
```html
<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->
<div style="mso-hide:all;">
  <span style="display: none !important; ...">New second-hand arrivals…</span>
```
The `<!--[if !mso 9]><!-->` conditional excludes Outlook 2007 (mso 9) entirely. For all other Outlook versions, the `mso-hide:all` on the wrapper div then suppresses it. Net effect: **no Outlook client renders the preheader preview text.** Outlook users see a blank preview pane. Fix: move the preheader `<span>` outside the `mso-hide` div, or use a standard MSO-safe preheader pattern without the `mso-hide` wrapper.

**[MEDIUM] `user-scalable=no` violates WCAG 1.4.4**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width; initial-scale=1.0; maximum-scale=1.0; user-scalable=no;" />
```
Disabling pinch-to-zoom on mobile fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale=1.0`.

**[LOW] Viewport meta uses semicolons as separators**
The same tag uses `;` between properties instead of `,`. Technically invalid per spec; most parsers tolerate it, but it should be corrected.

**[LOW] Invalid HTML — `<div>` as direct child of `<tr>`**
The preheader `<div>` is placed directly inside a `<tr>` with no `<td>` wrapper. This is invalid HTML and may trigger quirks-mode rendering in strict parsers. Wrap in `<td>`.

**[LOW] Duplicate CSS rule**
In the `@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)` block, `.bot40 {padding-bottom: 40px!important;}` is declared twice. The duplicate is dead code — remove one.
