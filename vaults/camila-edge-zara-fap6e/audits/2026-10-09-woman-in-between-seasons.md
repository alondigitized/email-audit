# Woman | In Between Seasons
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-shot Zara lookbook that works as brand content and does little as a sales email. The park-setting hero with trench coats and the "In Between Seasons" framing match the October weather. The category tiles for dresses, blazers, jackets, knitwear, T-shirts and jeans give a woman shopper plenty of ways in.
- Nothing here asks for the click, though. There's no price, no offer and no deadline. The only CTAs are tiny "VIEW" and category captions under the photos, and none of them is a button. It's the same grid-scroll skeleton Zara has run since August (see 2026-09-19 "Discover the latest arrivals" and 2026-09-25 "This week at Zara"). It's less bloated than the 09-25 mega-send, but it still doesn't have the single idea that made 2026-09-20 "ZARA TIMELESS" and 2026-10-01 "Re{form} Zara. Vol. 1" stand out.
- Verdict: pleasant and on-season, but a skim-and-leave email. It's a mid-tier send, not a bad one.

## What's working

- The hero is strong and seasonal: two models in trench and wool coats, with a large ZARA wordmark.
- "In Between Seasons" fits early October layering, so the email feels timely and not stale.
- Bold black display type against clean white gives a clear editorial hierarchy and an unmistakable Zara look.
- The category tiles (dresses, blazers, jackets, knitwear, T-shirts, jeans) cover the whole womenswear range and make browsing easy.
- "The Fold-er" and "Shop with Sophia" add a human, styled-by-a-person angle that breaks up the product grid.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or urgency, so nothing gives a reason to click today.
- The CTAs are tiny "VIEW" and caption labels under the photos. There are no buttons and no verb-led copy like "Shop the blazers."
- The "TRF COLLECTION" headline breaks into stacked fragments ("TRF / CO / LLEC / TI / ON"), which looks like a layout error and is hard to read.
- The ZARA wordmark is cropped at the right edge of the hero, so the logo reads as cut off.
- "Travel Mode Miami" at the bottom is an off-topic tail that dilutes the Woman focus.
- The email is long, with nine or so modules and no single hero product. That is the same lookbook-scroll fatigue as earlier sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product or category and a real CTA button, such as "Shop the trench coats," instead of spreading attention across nine modules.
- 2. Fix the TRF headline so it sets on one or two lines, and make sure the wordmark isn't cropped.
- 3. Replace "VIEW" and the bare captions with verb-plus-noun labels like "Shop blazers" or "Shop knitwear."
- 4. Cut the Miami travel module, or move it to a separate send, so the email stays on Woman.
- 5. Add some product specificity, such as a price, a "new in" flag or a featured item name, so there's something concrete to click.
- 6. Give the preview slot real copy. Right now the top line is "NEWSLETTER. ONLINE VERSION."
