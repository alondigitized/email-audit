# Homepage snapshot · www.zara.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I opened Zara's URL and got hit with a location/language gate before I could see a single product, campaign, or hero. The entire above-the-fold experience is a white screen with a logo, two dropdowns, and a "CONTINUE" button — zero editorial content, zero merchandising, zero reason to be excited. It reads as a functional tollbooth, not a fashion brand's front door.

## What's working

- **The logo** is large, clean, and instantly recognizable — no ambiguity about what brand this is.
- **"Remember selection" checkbox** is a small UX grace note; if I check it I won't see this screen again, which is the only saving grace here.
- **"United States / English" pre-selected** means the dropdowns don't require any interaction for a US English speaker — I can tap CONTINUE immediately.

## What's weak

- **The entire screen is a gate, not a homepage** — I'm a returning Zara customer who already knows where I am, and I'm being asked to confirm my country before I can see anything.
- **No campaign imagery whatsoever** — Zara usually leads with high-fashion editorial. Right now I'm looking at white space and two form fields. Dead on arrival for someone in browse mode.
- **Lower half of the viewport is completely blank** — the form sits in the top 60% and the rest is empty, which reads like a rendering failure on mobile.
- **Zero personalization signal** — no "welcome back," no sign-in prompt, no hint that the brand knows I've been here before.
- **"DO NOT SELL OR SHARE MY PERSONAL INFORMATION"** is the last thing my eyes land on — a legal disclaimer as the exit note of the screen. Not a vibe.
- **No urgency, no offer, no season** — I'm in a fashion drops mindset; this screen gives me nothing to be excited about.

## Recommendations

- **Remember the session and skip this gate for returning visitors** — anyone who's been to zara.com before (which is most of the traffic) should land directly on the editorial homepage. Geo can be inferred from IP; show it only as a passive "Shopping in: US" footer toggle.
- **If the gate is required, put a campaign image behind it** — a full-bleed editorial shot with the form overlaid keeps the brand energy alive while the user confirms location.
- **Add a sign-in prompt on this screen** — a small "Sign in" link in the top corner would let logged-in users skip or auto-confirm, reducing friction and surfacing personalization faster.
- **Move the CCPA disclaimer to the footer** — it's legally required somewhere, but making it the visual anchor of the landing screen is a conversion killer; put it where it belongs.
