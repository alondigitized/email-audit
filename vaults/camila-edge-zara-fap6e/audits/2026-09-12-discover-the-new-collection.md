# Discover the new collection
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Man running the identical lookbook skeleton that's been flagged repeatedly across Woman, Man, Kids, and Home sends since early August: a hero editorial shot, then an unbroken vertical scroll of category blocks — ZARA ORIGINS → NEW ARRIVALS → STREET FITS → BAREFOOT STYLES — with zero prose, zero offer, and zero differentiation from the last five "Discover" sends.
- The photography is competently shot (moody, brown-toned AW26 menswear) and the product spread is broad, but the email does the same thing every recent Zara send does: no discount, no urgency, no specific product callout beyond a category label under each image, and a subject line so generic it's interchangeable with three prior sends in this history. There's nothing wrong on the render side — no broken images, no overlap — the failure is entirely strategic: this is a lookbook PDF wearing an email's clothes.
- Judgement: a clean, competent execution of a strategy that isn't working — same score band as the last six Zara sends, because it's the same email with different photos.

## What's working

- Hero image is well-composed and on-brand — clear model, clear logo lockup, no clutter.
- Category sections are cleanly labeled (SHIRTS, SUITS, BLAZERS, JACKETS, TRENCH COATS, JEANS, SNEAKERS) so the eye can at least scan for a category of interest.
- No render bugs — images all loaded, text is legible, layout is consistent top to bottom.
- Reasonably broad product coverage in one send (outerwear, tailoring, footwear) without feeling cluttered.

## What's weak

- Zero offer of any kind — no %, no $, no free shipping, nothing to click for beyond "browse a category."
- Subject "Discover the new collection" is the same non-specific pattern as "Discover what's new" (2026-09-04, 2026-09-11) — this brand keeps recycling the identical vague verb.
- No prose anywhere in the body — a reader gets a name-a-category label and nothing else, no fabric, fit, or pricing detail.
- CTA is implicit (click the photo) rather than an explicit button — nothing tells the reader what happens next.
- This is the sixth near-identical structural send in five weeks per the brand history — cadence fatigue is real at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one offer or one hero product, not eight category blocks.** Pick the strongest item (the leather jacket under JACKETS) and build the top of the email around it with a price or a "just landed" story — then let the rest of the lookbook follow below.
- 2. **Add real subject-line specificity tied to what's actually new** — AW26 origins collection, leather jacket, whatever the actual hero product is — instead of the fourth "Discover" subject this month.
- 3. **Give each section one line of copy** — fabric, fit note, or styling tip — so it reads like a curated edit rather than a folder of thumbnails.
- 4. **Add an explicit CTA button** ("Shop the Edit") under the hero at minimum, so click intent isn't purely image-hover dependent.
