# ZARA & Aaron Levine Capsule
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's designer capsule collaboration email — "ZARA & Aaron Levine" (aaronlevine sub-brand), the third installment of this collection per the body copy. It's a moody, cinematic lookbook: dim interiors, warm lamplight, vintage cars, worn leather and denim on male models, styled like a film-noir editorial rather than a retail push. Visually it's the most distinctive and highest-production-value send in the Zara brand history I've reviewed — a real step up from the flat "This week's selection" grids (2026-08-08, 4.00/10) and repetitive category blocks that have defined every other Zara vertical this month.
- But distinctive doesn't mean effective as a commerce email. There's exactly one CTA — a plain "VIEW" button buried after a dozen-plus stacked photos — no price, no specific product callout, no urgency, no clear entry point to shop. It reads like a fashion magazine spread, which is on-brand for a designer capsule but does nothing to move someone toward a click quickly. Judgment: gorgeous, cold — style over conversion.

## What's working

- Strong, consistent visual identity — same aaronlevine wordmark/blue-tag treatment repeated three times reinforces the collab branding.
- No render bugs: images load cleanly, text is legible, no overlap or broken assets.
- Body copy paragraph gives real narrative texture ("bustling city escapes... worn fabrics aged over the course of time") rather than generic sale language — feels premium and intentional.
- Product grid near the bottom (2x2 outfit tiles) at least offers some shoppable structure after the pure lookbook section.

## What's weak

- Single, generic CTA ("VIEW") with no product or category specificity — could link anywhere.
- Extremely long scroll (image is ~8400px tall) before any actionable link appears — high mobile abandonment risk.
- Zero pricing, zero offer, zero urgency — this is pure brand storytelling with no reason to act now.
- No visible sizing, availability, or product names next to the outfit grid — just numbered tiles.
- All-male cast in a subject/campaign that doesn't specify "Men" — if this hit a broader Zara list, it's a mismatch for female or kids-segment subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real CTA above the fold — "Shop the Capsule" or "Shop Aaron Levine x Zara" directly under the hero image, not just "VIEW" after 15 scrolls of lookbook photography.
- 2. Label the outfit grid tiles with product names/prices so the 2x2 section is actually shoppable, not just decorative.
- 3. Trim the lookbook — 6-8 near-identical moody shots before any CTA is too much; cut to 3-4 and move a CTA up.
- 4. Segment send to Men's list only, or add "Men's" to subject for clarity if list isn't segmented.
- 5. Add urgency or scarcity — capsule collabs sell out fast; a "limited release" line would fit the narrative and drive faster clicks.
