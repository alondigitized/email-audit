# ZARA | Into The Process IX
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara Man's ninth installment of "Into The Process" — a full-scroll black-and-white editorial short film dressed up as an email, built around dancer Callum Linnane and a craftsman's hands working leather. It's the most aesthetically ambitious Zara send I've seen in this stream, with real narrative intent (dancer movement, artisan process shots, product close-ups), but it commits the same sin as every other Zara email in this brand history: zero prices, zero visible CTA buttons, and a product legend (Piece 1: Oxford, Piece 2: Loafer, etc.) crammed into tiny caption text at the very bottom that most subscribers will never scroll to.
- Compared to 2026-08-18's "Home Office Collection" (4/10, pure lookbook) and 2026-08-16's "The Fold-er" (4/10, influencer content play), this is another content-marketing send rather than a commerce send. It's better produced than most of the recent Home/Kids sends, but it's an even longer scroll — I count roughly 15+ full-bleed images before any product identification appears — which makes it worse for someone opening on mobile hoping for "what's new and how much."
- Judgement: gorgeous, on-brand, and completely useless as a driver of a click-to-purchase. This is a lookbook, not a marketing email, and it should be labeled and measured as one.

## What's working

- Strong, consistent black-and-white art direction with a named dancer (Callum Linnane) gives it more editorial credibility than the generic "Kids" or "Woman" lookbook sends.
- Product close-ups (Oxford shoe, loafer, tote, crossbody) are shot cleanly enough that texture and hardware are visible.
- No render bugs — every image loaded, text blocks are legible, spacing is even throughout the long scroll.
- The footer does eventually name the six pieces shown, which is more product transparency than the "Home Bedroom Collection" send (2026-08-26) offered at all.

## What's weak

- No price is visible anywhere in the entire email — not even a "from $X" on the Piece 1-6 legend at the bottom.
- No clickable-looking CTA button anywhere in the render — no "Shop Now," no "Discover the Collection," just static imagery.
- The product legend (Piece 1: Oxford / Piece 2: Loafer / etc.) is set in small, low-contrast caption type and buried after roughly 15 screens of scroll — almost no one reaches it.
- Hero copy ("Shoes and Bags / Contemporary Essentials Zara Man") is generic enough that it's interchangeable with any past "Contemporary Essentials" send.
- The email is exceptionally long for what it delivers — high production cost per pixel of purchase intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a persistent or repeated CTA button ("Shop Contemporary Essentials") near the top and again near the product legend — right now there is no click target visible anywhere in the render.
- 2. Move the Piece 1-6 product legend up near the top third of the email, right after the hero, and pair each with a price — this is the single highest-impact fix since it's the only commerce information in the entire send.
- 3. Cut the scroll roughly in half — 6-8 hero/process images make the same emotional case as 15+, and a shorter email converts better on mobile.
- 4. Give the subject/preheader a concrete hook (a product or price) instead of relying entirely on "Into The Process IX," which means nothing to anyone who hasn't followed the series.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: The Oxford, Loafer & Tote — Zara Man Essentials`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Behind the Craft: 6 New Pieces, Zara Man`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six wardrobe essentials, made to last — shop the edit`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From the workshop to your closet: this season's key pieces`
- 5. Add loyalty/member pricing or a "just landed" urgency cue if one exists — nothing in the render signals timeliness or scarcity.
