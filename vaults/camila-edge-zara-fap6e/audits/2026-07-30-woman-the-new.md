# Woman | The New
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Zara's Women's "New" edit — seven vertically stacked category blocks (ZW Collection, Stripes, TRF, Lingerie, Accessories, Selected Pieces) with zero copy beyond section labels and product-type links. It's the same skeleton as the July 24 "Woman | The weekly edit" send, just re-shuffled sections and swapped imagery — Stripes and Lingering are the only blocks that read as genuinely new versus that prior send.
- The photography is clean and on-brand, but there's no offer, no price, no urgency, and no single hero moment — it's a lookbook, not a marketing email. The Accessories block (a 5x2 grid of face crops) is visually confusing on first glance and doesn't clearly link to a product category the way the others do.
- Judgement: competent editorial execution, weak as a commerce email. This is a "browse when bored" send, not a "click now" send — consistent with Zara's pattern of near-identical weekly edits with no discernible campaign structure.

## What's working

- Clean masonry-style two-image grid per section keeps visual consistency throughout.
- Stripes Collection has the strongest hero shot — high contrast, editorial mood, distinct from anything in recent brand history.
- Section labels (ZW Collection, TRF Collection, Lingerie Edit) and sub-links (Dresses, Co-ord Sets, Tops, Shirts) give clear category navigation.
- No render bugs — images all loaded, text is crisp, no overlap.

## What's weak

- No offer, price, or promotional hook anywhere in the email — pure lookbook.
- Subject "Woman | The New" is nearly indistinguishable from July 24's "Woman | The weekly edit" — same brand, same cadence, no differentiation for a repeat subscriber.
- Accessories Collection block (10 face-crop thumbnails) is visually cluttered and doesn't clearly signal "click to shop accessories."
- No hero CTA button anywhere — every link is a small underlined text label, easy to miss on mobile.
- Seven full sections before reaching the footer is a lot of scrolling for zero urgency payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete offer or seasonal hook to the top hero (e.g., "New In: Free Shipping This Week") — this email currently gives a subscriber zero reason to act now versus browsing later.
- 2. Redesign the Accessories grid — swap face crops for product shots (bags, jewelry, sunglasses) so it reads as shoppable rather than a model casting sheet.
- 3. Add a bolded CTA button under the top hero image ("Shop Women's New In") instead of relying on underlined text links throughout.
- 4. Trim to 3-4 sections max, or move Selected Pieces/Accessories below a "See More" fold — seven full-width sections is excessive scroll for a no-urgency send.
- 5. Differentiate the subject line from the July 24 send so repeat subscribers don't file this as a duplicate.
