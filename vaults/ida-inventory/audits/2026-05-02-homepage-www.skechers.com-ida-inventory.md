# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Adult lifestyle brand, full stop. The above-the-fold real estate is owned by two adult celebrities — Brooke Burke and Tony Hawk — pushing the Hands Free Slip-ins line to grown-ups. Nothing on this screen signals kids, girls, school shoes, or anything that belongs on my clipboard. From where I'm sitting, this is a homepage built for 35–55-year-old adults, and it doesn't apologize for it.
- **2/10**

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF sticky banner:** Pinned at the very top in high-contrast blue. I see it, I register it, and it would move a parent making a multi-kid shoe run — that's at least one adjacent win for my use case.
- **Dual-celebrity split layout:** Structured, readable on mobile, no visual clutter. The page isn't broken. It loads clean.
- **Hands Free Slip-ins callout below the fold:** Slip-ons are the single biggest girls' category crossover SKU Skechers has. That message *could* funnel down to kids if I kept scrolling — but it's adult-positioned here.

## What's weak

- **Zero kids' content above the fold:** Not a thumbnail, not a nav callout, not a "Shop Girls'" strip. My entire category doesn't exist in the first screen.
- **Celebrity talent is adult-coded:** Brooke Burke and Tony Hawk mean nothing to a girl shopping for her own shoes, and they don't signal "mom, here's where to start" either. Wrong spokesperson selection for my segment.
- **25% OFF Apparel banner:** I audit shoes. Apparel noise on a shoe retailer's homepage is inventory confusion, not a hook.
- **No navigation shortcut visible:** The MENU hamburger is there, but there's no quick-tap row for Women / Men / Kids above the hero. I have to dig.

## Recommendations

- **Add a persistent "Girls' / Kids'" quick-link strip** directly below the logo bar — one tap, no menu excavation needed.
- **Swap one panel of the celebrity split** for a back-to-school or playground lifestyle shot featuring a girl — even 50/50 adult/kids split recovers half my attention.
- **Repurpose the apparel banner slot** for the top-N girls' categories (Twinkle Toes, Uno, Sport) — that's load-bearing shelf space going to a category I don't track.
- **Surface size-availability cues** for high-velocity girls' styles in the hero or directly beneath — "Now in wide widths" or "Toddler–Big Kid sizes" tells me the inventory is there before I click in.
