# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Skechers is running a Hands Free Slip-ins Glide-Step push with two stacked promos — BOGO 50% and a Spring Apparel Sale. The site looks competent, current, and clean for a mass-market footwear brand. For my purposes — systematic audit of the girls' shoe section — this viewport tells me essentially nothing about the kids' floor.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% Off banner** — pinned at the very top in high-contrast dark blue, impossible to miss; this deal likely covers kids' shoes and is the strongest reason to stay
- **Category nav tabs (Women / Men / Kids' / Recently Viewed)** — "Kids'" is right there at the bottom of the viewport, one tap from my workday; that's efficient
- **"Recently Viewed" tab** — for a daily auditor doing repeat walks of the same category pages, this is genuinely useful UX
- **Seasonal coherence** — Spring Apparel Sale copy, floral dress, pool backdrop, May date — the campaign actually matches the calendar, which tells me this site is being maintained

## What's weak

- **Hero is adults-only** — both models are in their 20s wearing adult Slip-ins; I see zero kids' product, zero kids' imagery in this viewport
- **"SHOP NOW" is a dead-end CTA** — generic destination unknown; no indication it routes to kids', women's, or Slip-ins specifically
- **No free shipping threshold visible** — for someone doing test purchases to verify size availability, that threshold matters, and it's not surfaced here
- **No trust signals** — no review count, no "free returns," no "120-day comfort guarantee" — nothing that would reassure a first-time quality checker on a test order
- **25% Off Apparel is noise for me** — second promo takes up real estate and is completely irrelevant to a footwear audit

## Recommendations

- **Replace or supplement the hero with a kids' seasonal story** — a "Back to School Preview" or "New Spring Girls' Styles" panel would give auditors and parents a direct on-ramp instead of routing through adult lifestyle
- **Make the "Kids'" tab a CTA button, not just a nav tab** — bump it visually so it reads as a destination, not a filter; even a small label chip ("New Arrivals") would help
- **Surface the BOGO exclusions in plain text** — if kids' shoes are excluded from the BOGO, say so at the banner level, not buried in the "details" link; saves auditors a wasted click
- **Add a free-shipping threshold line** to the secondary banner strip — one line, no design effort, immediate utility for anyone validating checkout flows
