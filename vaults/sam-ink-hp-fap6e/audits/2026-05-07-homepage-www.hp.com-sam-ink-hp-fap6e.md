# Homepage snapshot · www.hp.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- Well, they caught me at the right moment. I've been an Instant Ink guy for a few years and honestly the subscription has had its weird phases — price bumps, cartridge compatibility headaches — so a pitch like "Printer problems? We've got a plan for that" lands pretty directly. HP is pushing their All-In Plan hard right now, and the whole vibe of this hero is "we know your printer life is annoying, let us fix it." That's a brand that knows its customer.

## What's working

- **The hero headline** — "Printer problems? We've got a plan for that." This is exactly what I think about every six months when my Instant Ink shipment is late or I'm getting low-ink warnings before a big print job. They're speaking my language.
- **The imagery** — Guy in a dress shirt standing on a pile of destroyed printers with a baseball bat. Gen X dark humor. I get it immediately. I've *felt* this way about printers.
- **The price anchor** — "$7.99/month" in the body copy gives me a concrete number to react to instantly. I don't have to click to find out if this is in my range.
- **Clean mobile layout** — No clutter, no pop-ups, no cookie banner obscuring the content. Loads and presents clearly.

## What's weak

- **"Learn" CTA is doing zero work** — If you've already told me there's a plan for my printer problems and quoted me a price, why are you sending me to "learn"? Say "See the Plan" or "Get Started" — something with direction.
- **No HP Rewards callout** — I'm in HP Rewards. I've bought multiple printers direct from HP. There is no acknowledgment I exist as a returning customer. No balance, no "member exclusive," nothing. That's a miss for loyalty retention.
- **No navigation shortcuts above the fold** — The hamburger menu is the only way into the site. If I want to look at printers, ink, or accessories, I have zero direct entry points visible without opening that menu.
- **No trust scaffolding** — For a subscription pitch asking for $7.99/month recurring, there's nothing here about cancellation policy, what happens to the printer if I leave, or any social proof. That's a gap for someone like me who's been burned by ink subscriptions before.

## Recommendations

- **Replace "Learn" with "See the Plan"** — One word swap, immediate clarity about destination. "Learn" reads like a homework button.
- **Add a small HP Rewards acknowledgment** near the nav (e.g., "Hi, Sam — You have 1,240 points") — even a guest-mode "HP Rewards members save more" strip would signal that returning customers are seen.
- **Add a one-line trust note under the CTA** — Something like "Cancel anytime. Printer stays yours." That's the objection I'm holding in my head when I see a monthly subscription pitch.
- **Surface a secondary nav link or icon** for "Printers" or "Ink" — I came to hp.com for a reason. If the All-In Plan doesn't hook me in the first 3 seconds, give me a fast path to browse without the hamburger.
