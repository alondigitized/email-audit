# Homepage snapshot · www.hp.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- HP's opening move is a direct gut punch to anyone who's ever rage-quit an ink subscription — a dude in office clothes standing on a mountain of smashed printers with a baseball bat. Hard to look away. The whole page is pushing one thing: the HP All-In Plan, a $7.99/month bundle of printer, ink delivery, and 24/7 support. As an Instant Ink subscriber who's replaced two printers specifically because the subscription got weird, this landing feels personal in a way most homepages don't.

## What's working

- **The hero image**: Guy in business attire standing on demolished printer hardware holding a baseball bat is visceral and funny. It earns attention. I actually stopped scrolling to look at this.
- **"Printer problems? We've got a plan for that."**: Short, plain-spoken, Gen X plain talk. Doesn't over-explain. Doesn't feel like it was written by a 26-year-old.
- **Price specificity**: "$7.99/month" in the subhead is doing real work. I know immediately whether this is in my range without clicking anything.
- **Benefit clarity**: "Get a new printer, ink delivery, and 24/7 support" — three things, one sentence, no ambiguity.

## What's weak

- **"Learn" is a cold CTA**: I'm already interested. Tell me to "Subscribe," "Get Started," or "See Plans" — not "Learn." Learn feels like I'm being sent to a PDF.
- **No HP Rewards hook**: I have an HP Rewards account. Nothing on this screen acknowledges that. A returning customer sees the same page as a first-timer.
- **No navigation shortcut to printers/ink**: The nav is a hamburger. If I'm not sold on the All-In Plan and just want to browse printers or ink cartridges, there's no one-tap path visible.
- **No trust reinforcement**: No review count, no "as seen in," no "X million subscribers" — just the pitch. For a subscription product, I want at least one data point that people like it.

## Recommendations

- **Change "Learn" to "See Plans" or "Get Started for $7.99/mo"**: Close the gap between interest and action — make the button do the convincing, not just invite more reading.
- **Add a loyalty/returning-member nudge**: Even a simple "HP Rewards member? Sign in to apply points" line under the CTA would make me feel seen instead of anonymous.
- **Surface one social proof signal**: "Over 5 million subscribers" or a star rating strip under the subhead — anything that confirms other people have made this bet and don't regret it.
- **Expose a secondary nav link**: Put a visible "Shop Printers" or "Buy Ink" text link below the hero for people who aren't ready to subscribe — don't make them find the hamburger menu.
