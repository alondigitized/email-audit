# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I opened Saks on my phone expecting to see the Pre-Fall drop I've been watching — and instead got a cold-shoulder "WE'RE SORRY" block page. This is not a homepage experience, this is a security wall, and for a SaksFirst Diamond cardholder who shops frequently and aggressively, it reads as the brand calling me a bot. The only thing being communicated right now is that I cannot access the site.

## What's working

- The typography on "WE'RE SORRY" is clean and on-brand serif — at least the font system didn't break.
- The explanation copy is legible and at least tells me *why* I'm blocked (unusual activity, possible bot flag).

## What's weak

- **The entire experience:** I land on a dead-end with no way forward — no retry button, no "continue to site" CTA, nothing.
- **The framing is accusatory:** listing "Automated bot activity" and "Use of developer or inspection tools" as reasons tells a loyal customer she looks suspicious. That is not luxury-brand language.
- **No escape hatch:** there is zero navigation, no link to the app, no customer service number, no fallback for a legitimate shopper.
- **Zero brand content:** I can vaguely see a street-scene photo bleeding through behind the modal — that's the only hint that a homepage exists beneath this.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Continue Anyway" or CAPTCHA option** so a real shopper can clear the flag without calling customer service — this is a one-week ship.
- **Replace accusatory bullet copy** ("bot activity on your network") with softer security language: "To keep your account safe, we need to verify this visit." Luxury brands do not accuse their best customers.
- **Surface the SaksFirst phone number or live chat link** on this error screen — if I'm locked out, give me the concierge route immediately.
- **Auto-redirect to the app store** or the Saks app deep link as an alternative so the visit isn't a complete dead end.
