# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I opened Saks on my phone and got hit with a wall: a bot-block page. No hero, no product, no offer — just "WE'RE SORRY" and a list of reasons why they think I'm a robot. This isn't a homepage experience, it's a rejection. Whatever the brand is pushing right now, I have no idea, because I never got to see it.

## What's working

- The error copy is legible — serif headline "WE'RE SORRY" is readable and the body text explaining the block is clear.
- The blurred background hints at a lifestyle/street photo, so the brand's aesthetic DNA is partially intact even on the error screen.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is replaced by a security block — I see zero product, zero navigation, zero offers.
- The bullet list of "reasons" (rapid taps, developer tools, bot activity) reads accusatory toward a legitimate mobile shopper. As a SaksFirst Diamond member, being told I look like a bot is genuinely off-putting.
- No recovery path — there's no "retry," no CAPTCHA, no "tap here to verify you're human." I'm just stuck.
- The IP address (75.140.9.0) is exposed in plain copy, which feels technical and cold for a luxury brand.
- No brand logo, no nav bar — the Saks identity is completely absent. This could be any site's error page.

## Recommendations

- Add a one-tap verification option ("Tap to confirm you're human") so a legitimate mobile user isn't hard-stopped.
- Replace the raw IP address display with something human: "We detected some unusual traffic — tap below to continue" keeps brand voice intact.
- Ensure the SaksFirst loyalty cookie/session token can suppress bot-detection blocks for authenticated members — my Diamond status should short-circuit this entirely.
- Style the error page with the Saks wordmark and brand palette so that even in failure state, the luxury brand experience doesn't evaporate.
