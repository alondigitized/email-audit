## What's working

- The sans-serif typography on "WE'RE SORRY" is at least legible and consistent with Saks' clean aesthetic — small mercy.
- The blurred street-scene background image partially visible behind the card is the only suggestion that there's a luxury brand behind this wall.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is replaced by a bot-detection interstitial — zero product, zero navigation, zero brand equity visible.
- The copy is clinical and accusatory: "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" followed by a bullet list that includes "Automated (bot) activity on your network (IP 47.41.15.19)" — my IP address is printed on screen. That feels invasive and off-brand for a luxury retailer.
- There is no CTA or recovery path — no "Try Again," no "Contact Us," no link back to the homepage. I'm just stranded.
- The design of the interstitial is generic white-card-on-blur — it reads like a bank error page, not a Saks moment.
- "Rapid taps or clicks" listed as a suspicious behavior is ironic given that mobile-first shoppers like me navigate quickly. The fraud net is catching the exact behavior it should want.

## Recommendations

- Add a one-tap "I'm not a robot / Retry" CTA with a CAPTCHA challenge so real customers can immediately recover without abandoning.
- Replace the IP-address exposure with a softer message — printing "47.41.15.19" on screen is unnecessary and feels like a security / trust violation, not a safety feature.
- Style the interstitial to match the Saks brand: black and white palette, editorial type, a real product or store image — if I have to see this page, at least make it feel like Saks.
- Recalibrate the mobile tap-rate threshold — fast tapping is standard behavior on a phone and should not trigger the bot screen for known SaksFirst cardholders.
