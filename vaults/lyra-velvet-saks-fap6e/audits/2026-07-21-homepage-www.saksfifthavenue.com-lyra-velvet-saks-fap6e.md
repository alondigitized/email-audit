# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-07-21
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- I opened saksfifthavenue.com and instead of their homepage, I'm staring at a white modal that says "WE'RE SORRY" in large serif type. The page is a bot-block — Saks detected "unusual activity" from IP 47.229.200.33 and refused to serve me the site at all. Whatever they're selling today, I have no idea, because I never got past the door.

## What's working

- The "WE'RE SORRY" heading is typographically consistent with a luxury brand — clean serif, generous white space. If nothing else, the error screen doesn't look cheap.
- The blurred background suggests a street/lifestyle scene, which at least hints at brand aesthetics.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage** — it didn't load. I see an error interstitial, not a shopping experience.
- **Bot block is over-triggering** — the page lists "use of developer or inspection tools" and "JavaScript disabled" as possible causes. For a mobile-first shopper like me opening the site normally, getting hit with this wall is a five-alarm UX failure.
- **No recovery path** — the screen gives me reasons but zero CTA: no "Try again," no "Continue to site," no CAPTCHA to pass. I'm just stuck.
- **IP flagging exposed to the user** — showing `IP 47.229.200.33` in the error message is unnecessary and slightly unnerving. I don't need to know my IP address while shopping for a Bottega bag.

## Recommendations

- **Add a recovery CTA immediately** — even a simple "I'm not a robot — take me to the site" button with a CAPTCHA would let real customers through instead of bouncing them.
- **Tune the bot-detection threshold** — if a standard headless browser screenshot hits this wall, so does a real customer on a corporate or shared network. The false-positive rate is a revenue leak.
- **Strip the IP address from the error copy** — showing raw network data to a luxury shopper erodes the premium brand feel instantly.
- **Add a fallback graceful experience** — at minimum, surface a phone number or the SaksFirst concierge contact so a locked-out customer has somewhere to go.
