# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Saks didn't even let me in. Instead of their homepage, I'm looking at a bot-detection wall — "WE'RE SORRY, We've noticed some unusual activity" — with a list of reasons including rapid taps, disabled JavaScript, and bot activity. This isn't a shopping experience; it's a locked door. As a SaksFirst Diamond member who opens this app routinely on mobile, getting blocked like a scraped IP is embarrassing for the brand.

## What's working

- The page at least renders the error in clean, legible typography — nothing is overlapping or broken within the error card itself.
- The copy explains *why* access was blocked (bot detection list), which is more informative than a generic 404.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is absent.** I'm a SaksFirst Diamond cardholder on mobile and I'm getting treated like a scraper. That's a loyalty-destroying moment.
- **No fallback or recovery path.** There's no "try again," no sign-in CTA, no link to reach a stylist or CS — the page is a dead end.
- **The IP-based blocking feels blunt and punitive** — listing "47.41.15.19" on screen is technical noise that means nothing to a shopper and signals "we built this for ops, not for you."
- **Zero brand expression.** No logo, no color palette, no seasonal campaign — the Saks identity is completely absent.

## Recommendations

- **Add a soft-retry CTA immediately** — a "Tap to continue to Saks" button with a simple CAPTCHA or challenge, so real customers aren't permanently blocked.
- **Swap the error copy for brand-appropriate language** — "We want to make sure it's really you" in Saks' editorial voice, not a technical readout about network IPs.
- **Surface a loyalty sign-in prompt as the fallback** — if the bot wall is going to fire, at least give me a chance to authenticate as a SaksFirst member and bypass the block.
- **Test the bot detection threshold on mobile Safari user-agents** — mobile-first shoppers hit rate limits faster; the sensitivity needs a separate policy for app-like browsing patterns.
