# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-05-08
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- I opened Saks on my phone and didn't get a homepage — I got a wall. A full-screen bot-verification gate ("IS IT YOU?") with a slider CAPTCHA blocked the entire experience before I could see a single product, campaign, or editorial image. For a brand charging $800 for a tote, this is a jarring first impression that feels more like a government portal than a luxury retailer.

## What's working

- The CAPTCHA page is at least legible — the "IS IT YOU?" headline and slider instruction are clear and readable in plain English.
- A brief explanation ("Why is this step needed?") is provided, which is minimally reassuring over a blank error page.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is gated.** I am a known SaksFirst Diamond customer on mobile — this is exactly the demographic most likely to be browsing at odd hours from a known device type, and I got flagged as a bot.
- **"IS IT YOU?" as a headline** is anxiety-inducing, not welcoming. For a luxury brand the tone should be warm, never accusatory.
- **No fallback content.** There's no editorial, no brand reassurance, no "here's what's trending while we verify you" — just a cold security form over a blurred background.
- **The IP flag is exposed in plain text** ("IP 47.41.15.19") — surfacing a user's IP address in a consumer-facing error message is a trust and optics failure.
- **Slider CAPTCHA on mobile** is a clunky interaction pattern; the touch target and slider mechanism are notoriously finicky on small screens.
- **Background imagery is blurred and unidentifiable** — I can vaguely see a street scene but nothing that signals Saks, luxury, or fashion.

## Recommendations

- **Allowlist SaksFirst card + loyalty program IPs/device fingerprints** so known Diamond-tier customers never hit a bot gate — my spending history should be my passport.
- **Replace the IP-exposure copy** — never show a customer's raw IP address on a consumer page; swap for a generic "unusual network activity detected."
- **Soften the copy register** — "IS IT YOU?" reads like a phishing alert. Try "One quick check" or "Just a moment" to stay in brand voice.
- **Add a brand-consistent fallback strip** beneath the CAPTCHA — even a static logo, tagline, and blurred hero would keep the luxury context intact while verification runs.
