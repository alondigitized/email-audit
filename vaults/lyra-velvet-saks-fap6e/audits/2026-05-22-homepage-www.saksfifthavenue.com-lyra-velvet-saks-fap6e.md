# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened Saks on my phone and got a wall of text telling me I'm a bot. Not a hero image, not a new Pre-Fall drop — a "WE'RE SORRY" block screen accusing my device of suspicious behavior. This is not a homepage experience; this is a bouncer turning away a Diamond cardholder at the door. The brand completely disappears behind its own security infrastructure.

## What's working

- The typography is clean and legible — at least I can read the bad news clearly.
- The error copy is in plain English without jargon, so I understand what happened (even if I shouldn't be seeing this at all).

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is missing.** I see a bot-detection block instead of any brand content — this is catastrophic for a returning SaksFirst Diamond member.
- **"WE'RE SORRY" as a headline** is brand-damaging. That phrase in a luxury context reads as apologetic failure, not security confidence.
- **IP accusation is visible and specific** ("IP 75.140.9.0") — surfacing network internals to a customer is technically jarring and makes Saks feel like a firewall, not a fashion house.
- **No escape route for legitimate users.** There's no "I'm a real person" button, no CAPTCHA alternative, no redirect to a customer service path. I'm just stuck.
- **Zero brand presence.** No logo, no color palette, no imagery — a blurred streetscape in the background is the only visual signal that this was ever a retail site.

## Recommendations

- **Add a verification path immediately** — a simple tap-to-confirm CAPTCHA or "Continue shopping" button so real customers aren't permanently blocked. Ship this next week, not next quarter.
- **Replace the block page headline** — "We're keeping your account safe" with the Saks wordmark visible is infinitely better than "WE'RE SORRY."
- **Suppress the raw IP address** from customer-facing copy — it reads as a technical error, not a polished security message.
- **Whitelist mobile browsers more aggressively** — a SaksFirst Diamond member on Safari/iOS should never hit a bot wall on a direct site visit; tune the detection thresholds for logged-in or high-LTV sessions.
