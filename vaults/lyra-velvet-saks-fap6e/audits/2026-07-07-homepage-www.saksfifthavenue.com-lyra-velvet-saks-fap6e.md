# Homepage snapshot · www.saksfifthavenue.com · 2026-07-07
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- I opened Saks on my phone and instead of Pre-Fall arrivals or a designer handbag moment, I got a bot-wall. The page is showing a "WE'RE SORRY" interstitial citing "unusual activity" from my IP — no product, no brand expression, no nothing. This is the entire above-the-fold experience: a plain white card with an apology and a bullet list of reasons why I might be flagged as a bot.

## What's working

- The typography on "WE'RE SORRY" is clean and on-brand — at least they didn't use Comic Sans for the block.
- The copy is direct and explains *why* I'm being blocked, which is marginally better than a generic 403.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is a bot-wall.** A real customer with a Bond No. 9 habit and a SaksFirst Diamond card just got told she looks like a script.
- **No recovery path visible.** There is no "Continue to Saks," no CAPTCHA, no retry button — literally nothing to tap to get past this screen.
- **IP 75.140.9.0 is exposed in the error message.** Broadcasting the flagged IP in the UI is unnecessary and slightly unsettling to a consumer.
- **Zero brand presence.** No logo, no nav, no color, no product — this could be any site's error page.
- **"Rapid taps or clicks" listed as a reason** is insulting to someone shopping on mobile — of course I'm tapping quickly, that's how phones work.

## Recommendations

- **Add a visible "I'm not a robot / Continue Shopping" button** — even a simple CAPTCHA tap-through so a real customer isn't just stranded.
- **Inject a Saks logo and brand-consistent color** into the error template so it at least reads as Saks, not a generic CDN block page.
- **Remove the raw IP address** from the consumer-facing message — move it to a support reference code instead.
- **Tune bot-detection thresholds for mobile traffic** — "rapid taps" is a normal mobile browsing pattern and should not trigger a hard block for a known loyalty-tier IP.
