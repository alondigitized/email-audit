# Homepage snapshot · www.costco.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Costco doing what Costco does — functional, no-frills, warehouse-energy straight from the jump. The top banner is screaming at me that savings end tomorrow, my Burbank warehouse is already pinned, and the member-only pricing is front and center before I even scroll. For me personally, this reads like a utility app more than a shopping experience, which honestly is fine — I'm not here for inspiration, I'm here to reload the pantry.
- **8/10**

## What's working

- **Location pin showing "Burbank — Open until 7PM"** — I didn't ask for this and it's already there. That's exactly how I plan my Saturday run. This is useful.
- **Urgency banner "Select In-Warehouse and Online Savings End Tomorrow"** — That's the first thing I see and it works on me. I check savings cycles the way other people check their horoscope.
- **"Member-Only Savings & Everyday Values" block** — Confirms the membership is paying off before I've done anything. I use the 2% reward like a quarterly bonus, so this framing lands.
- **"This Week's Featured Items" tile** — That's the tab I'd open first on a Saturday anyway. It mirrors my mental model of the warehouse run.
- **Search bar immediately accessible** — I'm usually coming in with a list. Being able to type "Kirkland olive oil" in two seconds is the right UX for me.

## What's weak

- **"What's New" tile feels low-priority for me** — I'm not a discovery shopper at Costco. I know what I want. That tile earns real estate I'd rather see go to reorder history or the Gold Star deal strip.
- **Carousel dots (four of them)** — I can see there are four panels in this carousel but I have no idea what's in panels 2, 3, or 4 without swiping. On my phone while I'm standing in the parking lot, I'm probably not swiping — those deals are invisible.
- **No above-the-fold product photography** — The tiles look like app icons, not merchandise. Costco's strength is the physical abundance of the warehouse. A visual of a jumbo pallet of Kirkland paper towels would actually convert me faster than an icon.
- **Delivery Location "91201" shown as just a zip code** — I have to mentally decode that. Show me "Delivery to Burbank, CA" and I feel confident. A zip code feels like a debug field.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the carousel icon tiles with a single scrollable product strip** — show me the top 4–6 featured items as actual product cards with prices. I don't need a doorway to the features, I need the features.
- **Add a "Your Usuals" or "Buy Again" row directly below the warehouse pin** — I buy the same 30 items on rotation. If I can see that Kirkland Signature Wild Alaskan Salmon is in stock and on sale, I tap it in 10 seconds.
- **Surface the savings end date with a specific timestamp** — "Ends Tomorrow" is vague on a phone at 6pm. "Ends Sunday at midnight" is actionable and creates real urgency without feeling like a manipulation pattern.
- **Swap the zip code delivery display for city + state** — a one-word fix that makes the confirmed delivery location feel human, not technical.

## 8. Promotional & Urgency Cues

- **Active promos:** "Select In-Warehouse and Online Savings," "Member-Only Savings & Everyday Values"

- **Loyalty hooks:** "Member-Only Savings" banner with the red ribbon treatment; the warehouse pin itself implies active membership status is recognized
- **Honesty check:** "End Tomorrow" without a time is a soft manipulation — "end of day" is ambiguous enough to create anxiety without being explicit. It's not outrageous for Costco but it's a pattern worth noting.
