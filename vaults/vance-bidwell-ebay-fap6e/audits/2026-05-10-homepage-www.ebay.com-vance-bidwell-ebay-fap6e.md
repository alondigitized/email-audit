# Homepage snapshot · www.ebay.com · 2026-05-10
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-10**
## Executive summary

- I've been on eBay since the dial-up days, and this is what greets me — a Ralph Lauren apparel sale on a bright green background. If I didn't know better I'd think I accidentally opened a Macy's app. eBay is pitching fashion to a platform where half its users are hunting grails in Collectibles and Electronics, and yet there's zero above-the-fold acknowledgment that auctions, watch lots, or trading cards even exist. Classic eBay move: treat the homepage like a department store circular instead of the secondhand market it actually is.

## What's working

- **The offer is legible immediately.** "Save up to 70% off Ralph Lauren" — I read that in half a second. Bold white-on-green with a hard number does the job.
- **"Shop now" button** has good contrast against the dark pill shape; I know exactly what tapping it does.
- **Top nav bar** — "Saved" is right there in the shortcut strip, which for someone like me who watches 30+ items at a time is genuinely useful muscle memory.
- **Search bar is prominent.** For a power user like me who never browses — I search — having "Search for anything" front and center is the right default.
- **Clean render, no junk.** The carousel, arrows, and pause button work as expected. No modal popped over the page, no cookie wall blocking my view.

## What's weak

- **Wrong category entirely for my use case.** I'm here for a Rolex Submariner dial lot or a PSA-9 Charizard. Ralph Lauren polos are not why I opened eBay on my phone.
- **No sign-in personalization.** I have over 1,200 feedback and a watchlist with dozens of live auctions ending tonight, and the page greets me with "Hi! Sign in" like I'm a tourist. eBay knows exactly who I am — it should prove it.
- **No auction-time urgency.** eBay's single biggest differentiator from every other retailer is live auctions ending in hours. There's no countdown, no "ending soon," no "X items in your watch list ending today." That's the heartbeat of the platform and it's invisible here.
- **No eBay Bucks or loyalty hook.** My rewards balance should be right there nudging me to spend. Nothing.
- **Product tiles show apparel only.** A plaid shirt, a striped dress, a green hoodie. If I'm a first-time visitor, I have no idea eBay sells vintage timepieces, sealed Pokémon booster boxes, or basically anything collectible.
- **"Iconic styles to elevate any wardrobe"** is department-store copywriting — it has zero eBay voice, no secondhand angle, no uniqueness. Every brand says this.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the hero for a logged-in, personalized state.** If I'm already a signed-in eBay user, the hero should surface my watchlist activity — "3 items you're watching end in the next 4 hours" with a direct link. That's an instant engagement driver for high-value users like me.
- **Add an auction countdown strip below the nav.** A thin bar reading "Hot Auctions Ending Soon — Watches · Trading Cards · Sneakers" with live timers would anchor the platform's identity and pull collectors immediately toward their categories.
- **Rotate the hero carousel to include a Collectibles or Trading Cards slot.** One of those four carousel dots should feature something from the top-grossing secondhand categories — a PSA-graded card, a vintage watch, a sealed console — not four apparel items in a row.
- **Surface eBay Bucks balance above the fold** for signed-in users. Even a small "You have $12.50 in eBay Bucks" chip next to My eBay would drive immediate action from rewards-stack players.
