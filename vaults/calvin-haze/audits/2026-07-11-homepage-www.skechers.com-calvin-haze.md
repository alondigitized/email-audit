# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-07-11
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- First tap in and I'm immediately hit with a BOGO 50% off banner and a hero that actually looks like something I'd see on my Instagram explore page — streetwear-coded couple, Skechers HQ as the backdrop, very campaign-shoot energy. Skechers is clearly swinging at a younger, style-aware demo right now and pushing hard on the deal angle. This doesn't feel like the comfort-shoe brand from a decade ago; it reads like they're chasing the sneaker-culture crowd.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF top banner:** Impossible to miss in that blue bar. Before I even see a shoe I know there's a deal. That's the right order of operations for a deal-aware shopper.
- **Slip-ins in the quick nav:** It's the first tab. One tap from anywhere on this screen to the category I actually came here for. That's exactly how mobile should work.
- **Hero casting:** The guy in the beanie and wide-leg pants with the Skechers chunky sneakers — that's aspirational but attainable. He looks like someone I'd see at the coffee shop, not a runway model. It pulls me in.
- **Secondary apparel offer:** "25% OFF Apparel" signals they've got more than shoes, which opens up a basket-building opportunity if Martha wants something too.

## What's weak

- **No hero headline copy:** The hero image is all vibe, zero words. I don't know what line of shoes this is, what the campaign is, or why I should care beyond the aesthetic. There's a soccer ball icon on the Skechers logo — is this a collab? A World Cup tie-in? Say something.
- **No visible button CTA:** "Shop" and "Details" are plain text links. On mobile with a thumb scroll, those are easy to miss or misfire. Give me a pill button.
- **Zero trust signals above the fold:** No "4.8★ from 200k reviews," no "Free shipping," no "Easy returns." For a first-time Skechers visitor, there's nothing that builds confidence before the ask.
- **Men's category not called out separately:** The quick nav says "Slip-ins" which I like, but there's no "Men's" disambiguation. If I tap it, am I landing on a mixed-gender page? That's a friction point for a focused shopper.

## Recommendations

- **Add a two-to-three-word headline on the hero:** Even something like "New Drop. No Laces." or the campaign name. The image is doing 90% of the work but it's carrying zero information.
- **Swap the plain "Shop" text link for a pill button:** Dark fill, white text, positioned low on the hero — make it thumb-sized and unmissable on mobile.
- **Pin one trust signal beneath the quick nav:** A single line — "Free Shipping Over $75 · Free Returns · 4.8★ from 200k Reviews" — would do a lot of conversion work before I even scroll.
- **Label the quick nav tabs by gender on mobile:** "Men's Slip-ins" instead of just "Slip-ins" removes ambiguity and tells me the page is built for me, not just the product category.
