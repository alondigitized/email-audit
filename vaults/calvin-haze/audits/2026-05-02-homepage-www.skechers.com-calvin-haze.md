# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Skechers is clearly pushing Hands Free Slip-ins hard right now, which honestly is the right move for me — that's exactly what I came here for. The homepage reads like a mid-tier mall brand trying to ride celebrity endorsements, but that Slip-ins headline below the fold is the thing that actually hooks me. Two promos stacked up top before I even see a shoe feels a little aggressive, but the BOGO is hard to ignore.
- **7/10**

## What's working

- **"HANDS FREE Slip-ins" hero copy** — this is the exact tech I've been watching on TikTok. Seeing it front and center tells me I'm in the right place without having to dig.
- **BOGO 50% OFF banner** — applied at cart automatically, which means no code hunting. That's frictionless and I noticed it immediately.
- **Celebrity lifestyle imagery** — the split panel with Brooke Burke and Tony Hawk in real-world seated poses (not standing on a white backdrop) actually makes the shoes feel lived-in and wearable, not just athletic.
- **Mobile nav is clean** — MENU pill, search, cart all immediately accessible, no clutter. Feels native to how I browse.

## What's weak

- **Two promo banners before the hero** — I have to scroll past a BOGO strip AND a "25% OFF Apparel" strip before I see any product imagery. On mobile that's real estate I'm burning before the brand even shows me anything.
- **Apparel sale feels off-mission** — I came for shoes. "25% OFF Apparel" as the second thing I see dilutes the Slip-ins focus. Feels like a different campaign got bolted on.
- **Celebrity names are tiny** — I can barely make out "Brooke Burke" and "Tony Hawk" overlaid on the image. If the whole point of using these two is credibility, the callout isn't landing.
- **No product name or CTA above the fold** — the Slip-ins headline only appears at the very bottom of the viewport. I have to trust the scroll to get there; a less patient shopper bounces.

## Recommendations

- **Collapse the two promo banners into one rotating strip** so the hero image appears fully above the fold on mobile — the Slip-ins message is the hook, don't bury it.
- **Add a "Shop Slip-ins" CTA button** directly on or below the celebrity image, not just the headline — I want one tap to the product grid, not a hunt.
- **Swap the apparel sale banner for a men's Slip-ins callout** — if you know I've been on Instagram watching sneaker content, lead with "New colorways just dropped" or a men's-specific filter shortcut.
- **Make the celebrity names bigger or badge them** — "As seen on Tony Hawk" carries weight in my world. Lean into it instead of whispering it.
