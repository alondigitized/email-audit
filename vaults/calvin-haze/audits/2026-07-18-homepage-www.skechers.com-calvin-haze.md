# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-07-18
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Skechers is leaning into a retro-athletic energy right now — "GAME ON!" with bold colorway court shoes front and center — and it actually doesn't look bad for a quick scroll. But as someone who came here to find a slip-in I can throw on before grabbing coffee, the hero is already off-mission: those are laced-up retro runners, not hands-free anything. Savings are stacked at the top, which I respect, but the conversion path feels like it needs one more tap than it should.

## What's working

- **Dual promo strip at the top** — "20% OFF with code SUMMER" *and* "Up to 40% OFF Select Styles" means savings are impossible to miss before I even see the hero. That's the right call on mobile.
- **"Slip-ins" as the first category tab** — it's not buried. One tap from the homepage to the category I actually care about. That nav placement is smart.
- **"GAME ON!" copy + retro court colorways** — the hero energy is punchy and streetwear-coded. Red, green, and blue bold sneakers on turf read like a limited-drop moment, and that language lands with me.
- **Checkered flag + "EST. 1992" badge** — tiny brand flex that signals heritage without being dusty about it. As someone who follows sneaker culture, that kind of legacy stamp gets a nod.

## What's weak

- **Hero features laced retro runners, not Slip-ins** — my actual use case (hands-free slip-in for gym, errands, weekend runs) is promoted in the nav tab but totally absent from the hero. That's a disconnect.
- **No CTA button visible in the hero** — "GAME ON!" is a vibe, not a direction. Where do I go? The "Shop | Details" text links in the strip above don't count — they're tiny and buried above the fold chaos.
- **No human model in the hero** — just shoes on turf. As someone who buys based on how a shoe *looks on a person* in a real lifestyle context, this misses. I want to see someone built like me wearing these at a weekend market, not a product-shot dump.
- **Two banners stacked before the hero** — the blue bar and the white strip eat vertical real estate and create hierarchy confusion. My eyes pinball before landing on the actual hero.

## Recommendations

- **Swap or alternate the hero to feature a Slip-in lifestyle shot** — show someone in their 30s slipping on a pair hands-free at the gym or grabbing coffee. One lifestyle hero that speaks directly to the top nav category would do more work than "GAME ON!" for my cohort.
- **Add a single bold CTA button inside the hero** — `Shop Slip-ins` or `Shop the Drop` directly under "GAME ON!" so there's a clear next tap. Right now there's no action path from the hero without hunting the nav.
- **Collapse the double-banner to a single strip** — merge the SUMMER code and the Summer Favorites offer into one slim bar. This opens up hero real estate and removes the hierarchy fight at the top.
- **Bring a reviews count or star rating onto featured product imagery** — even a small "★4.8 (12K)" stamp on one of those court shoe images would give me a reason to tap rather than continue scrolling.
