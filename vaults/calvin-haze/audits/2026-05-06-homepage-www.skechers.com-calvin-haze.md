# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- Alright, so I land on Skechers and the whole screen is Mother's Day — pink florals, mom and daughter, script font. The BOGO 50% banner at the top got my attention, and the page itself is clean, no chaos. But as a 36-year-old dude hunting slip-ins for the gym and weekend runs? I feel like I accidentally opened my wife's phone.

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% OFF top banner** — that blue bar is the first thing I see and it immediately makes me think I should at least poke around. A deal that good makes me curious even if the hero isn't for me.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA** — no ambiguity, full-width button, you know exactly what to do next. Doesn't make me hunt.
- **"25% OFF Apparel" secondary banner** — two-for-one deal communication in the first two inches of the page. That's efficient.
- **Recently Viewed rail** — just barely visible at the bottom edge, but it tells me the site knows I've been here before. That's a soft hook that could pull me back in.
- **Clean mobile layout** — zero clutter. MENU up top, no pop-up blocking the page, no cookie consent wall eating the hero. I can actually see the product.

## What's weak

- **The entire hero** — "The Mother's Day Shop" with a mom-and-daughter lifestyle shot surrounded by pink flowers. I'm a 36-year-old guy who came here for sneakers. This reads like I'm on a gifting site, not a shoe brand.
- **No Men's or Slip-ins entry point above the fold** — the only tap available is "SHOP NOW" into the Mother's Day category. If I want Men's, I have to open the hamburger menu. That's friction for my actual purchase intent.
- **No product shown** — the hero is a lifestyle/gifting campaign shot. I can see pink shoes on the mom's feet but there's no product name, no model callout, nothing to anchor me to "that's a Skechers Slip-in."
- **No trust signals** — no star ratings, no "Free Returns," no "20M+ Reviews" badge anywhere visible. For a first-session mobile shopper, that gap is real.
- **Apparel sale feels random** — 25% OFF Apparel is cool but Skechers isn't why I'm here for clothes. Feels like a secondary message fighting for attention at the wrong moment.

## Recommendations

- **Add a men's lifestyle lane to the hero carousel** — even a second hero slide showing a guy in Skechers Hands Free Slip-ins doing something relatable (gym bag in hand, grabbing coffee) would immediately signal "this is for me too."
- **Surface a Men's shortcut tile above the fold** — put a row of quick-tap tiles just below the hero: Men's / Women's / Kids / Sale. One tap to my category instead of hunting through MENU.
- **Put a product callout in the BOGO banner** — instead of just "BOGO 50% OFF Applied at Cart," add something like "BOGO 50% OFF | Hands Free Slip-ins Included." That turns a generic promo into a reason for me to tap.
- **Swap "SHOP NOW" copy to match the campaign** — "Shop Mother's Day Gifts" makes it instantly clear where I'm going. Right now "SHOP NOW" is vague enough that I might tap hoping for Men's and bounce when I land in a gifting category.
