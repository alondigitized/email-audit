# On sale & ready-to-ship!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- "On sale & ready-to-ship!" is the first subject line in Pottery Barn's June batch that leads with a logistics hook rather than a discount claim — a smart pivot after the pure-percentage drumbeat of June 18–19. The problem is the body doesn't deliver on that hook. Open the email and the hero says "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" — the exact same message Pottery Barn sent at least three times on June 19 alone. Worse, the subtext under the hero reads "DELIVERED IN 4–6 WEEKS," which actively contradicts the "ready-to-ship" promise that presumably got the open. That's a bait-and-switch, even if unintentional.
- The rest of the email is a familiar multi-section stack: category nav grid, personalized product thumbnails, Free Design Services plug, app download, Affirm financing, rewards pitch. All fine in isolation, all undercut by the length and the sheer volume of competing CTAs. There is no moment in this email where a single product earns the click.
- The logistics angle was the right idea for a furniture brand where 12–16-week lead times are a known friction point. If the "ready-to-ship" inventory actually exists and is distinct from regular sale inventory, this needed its own landing page and its own hero — not a re-skin of the standard Summer Sale template.

## What's working

- **Logistics hook in subject line is genuinely differentiated** — "ready-to-ship" has not appeared in any prior send in this batch, and it addresses a real furniture-buyer anxiety.
- **Category grid is well-organized** — eight clear shop-by-category links give different buyer intents a direct path.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section adds personalization signal** — shows product thumbnails that feel curated, not just a generic grid.
- **Rewards and Affirm blocks are visible** — both loyalty and financing options are surfaced without feeling buried.
- **Hero image renders cleanly** — the coastal bedroom lifestyle shot is on-brand, aspirational, and loads without breakage.

## What's weak

- **Subject promise vs. body delivery is a direct contradiction** — "ready-to-ship" in the subject, "DELIVERED IN 4–6 WEEKS" in the hero body is a trust-eroding mismatch visible at a glance.
- **Hero creative is identical to June 19 sends** — no differentiation from "It's ON! The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF" or "Happening now! Up to 70% off" in visual or headline treatment.
- **No ready-to-ship inventory is actually featured** — if the hook exists, there's no dedicated section or callout for it anywhere in the scroll.
- **CTA overload across seven+ distinct blocks** — SHOP ALL SALE, SHOP ALL SALE (repeated per category), BOOK NOW, Download App, earnings language, Affirm — no single action wins.
- **No time-bound deadline anywhere in the email** — "Summer Sale" without a "ends Sunday" or countdown gives no reason to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build a dedicated ready-to-ship section** — if the inventory exists, give it its own hero block with specific products, in-stock badges, and an estimated arrival date (e.g., "Order by Sunday — arrives by July 4th"). That's the email you implied you were sending.
- 2. **Kill the "DELIVERED IN 4–6 WEEKS" subhead or move it below the fold** — it directly undercuts the subject line in the first three seconds. If that's the standard lead time, it doesn't belong under a "ready-to-ship" hero.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" or a countdown tied to the ready-to-ship angle turns browsing intent into purchase urgency.
- 4. **Collapse the CTA stack** — pick one primary action per section; remove the Affirm and App blocks unless they are in a clearly marked secondary area.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Ready to ship: up to 70% off furniture`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Arrives before the 4th — up to 70% off`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `In-stock picks that ship now — no 16-week wait.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer Sale items that actually ship fast. See what's available.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links will cause dead clicks and failed image loads for affected recipients. Authentication headers were not captured by the relay, leaving SPF/DKIM status unverifiable; compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post) are also absent from the captured message.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken link (403): Credit card apply banner**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...
```
The Key CC "Apply & Buy" banner CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking that module hits a dead end. Likely a permissions/routing issue on the `clickcp.potterybarn.com` redirect handler for this specific campaign ID.

**FAIL — Broken image src (403): West Elm spacer**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
This cross-brand tracking pixel from West Elm returns 403 and is served over HTTP (not HTTPS). It will be blocked by most modern clients regardless of the 403.

**INFO — 25 tracking links skipped:** All `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs were not probed. No confirmed failures in this set, but the two confirmed 403s above are sufficient to flag the template pipeline.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (3 confirmed):** Mixed-content images will be blocked or flagged in most clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

The open-tracking pixel being HTTP means opens may go unrecorded in clients that enforce HTTPS.

**Alt text absent on 40+ images:** Virtually every content and footer image has no `alt` attribute, including primary campaign images (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`, all `slices_0*.jpg` content tiles, product images). Recipients with images disabled see blank space with no fallback text. This is a systemic template issue, not one-off.

**Malformed CSS in `<style>` block:** PHP-style HTTP headers embedded inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives that have no effect in CSS and indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic header-injection block was written into the wrong output location. Browsers/clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it, but it signals a template rendering defect.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — some clients display the title in the tab or preview; leaving it empty is a minor rendering gap.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header missing:** Not present in captured headers. RFC 2369 requires this for commercial email; major mailbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) use it for the native unsubscribe UI. Absence increases spam complaint rate and can trigger suppression at the MBP level.

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe-Post missing:** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is absent. Gmail and Yahoo have required this for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Non-compliance is an active deliverability risk.

**WARN — Authentication-Results not captured:** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay-captured headers. This is a relay visibility gap, not a confirmed authentication failure — but it means any auth misconfiguration would go undetected by this pipeline.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from the truncated source; footer was cut off. Assumed present based on Pottery Barn's standard templates but should be confirmed.
