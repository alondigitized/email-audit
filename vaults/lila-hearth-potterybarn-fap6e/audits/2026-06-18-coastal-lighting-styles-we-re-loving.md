# Coastal lighting styles we’re loving
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second editorial break on June 18 — alongside the "NEW finishes for pieces you love" send (scored 8/10 that same day) — and it follows the same editorial-pivot logic the brand has been leaning on to escape the "Up to 70% off" hammer loop that has defined every June email since the 12th. The coastal lighting angle is seasonally correct and the visual execution is clean: a lifestyle hero, a curated product grid, a "More Ways to Get the Look" cross-sell, and a personalized recommendation strip at the bottom.
- The problem is the subject. "Coastal lighting styles we're loving" is magazine-caption copy — it describes editorial curation, not a reason to open. There's no price anchor, no urgency, and no offer hook. The sale banner ("Summer Sale · Up to 70% off") is buried in a thin strip at the top of the email, not in the subject or preheader where it could do any work. By this point in June, subscribers have seen that exact banner language so many times it has become visual wallpaper.
- The "Recommended: Just for You" personalization strip at the bottom is a smart tool used wrong — it dilutes a focused editorial story with product scatter. Net read: solid visual execution, weak inbox pitch, no urgency, cadence fatigue is real.

## What's working

- **Coastal Lighting hero is clean and on-topic** — the aspirational lifestyle image of a bright, airy room with natural-material lamps reads immediately and sets the right aesthetic frame.
- **Product grid is visually coherent** — all items share the woven/neutral coastal palette, which avoids the "random clearance rack" feel common in sale emails.
- **"More Ways to Get the Look" cross-sell is editorially smart** — extending into bedding and furniture gives the email a room-story narrative rather than just a product dump.
- **Summer timing is right** — coastal lighting in late June is seasonally credible and not forced.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean across the visible render with no overlapping text, broken images, or truncated content.

## What's weak

- **Subject is all editorial, zero offer** — "Coastal lighting styles we're loving" gives no reason to open beyond curiosity; no price, no deadline, no concrete hook.
- **"Up to 70% off" sale banner is invisible from the inbox** — it's the most commercially compelling element in the email but doesn't appear in subject or preheader, where it matters for open rate.
- **No price points visible in the hero section** — the editorial section shows products without surfacing any anchoring price, so the "sale" promise feels abstract until you scroll.
- **Personalization strip at the bottom undercuts the editorial focus** — the recommendation module shifts register from curated story to generic product browse, and it hits at the weakest moment of scroll depth.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — sale end date, low-stock callout, anything — is absent; the sixth-straight "Up to 70% off" banner without a deadline is pure inbox noise at this cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the subject to a price** — the single highest-lift change. "Coastal lighting styles we're loving" tells me nothing actionable; "Coastal lamps from $X — Summer Sale" gives me two reasons to open. This is the same move that made the June 17 "Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169" the strongest subject in the batch.
- 2. **Move the sale offer into the hero copy** — the "Up to 70% off" banner is a thin strip above the fold; integrate the discount into the first visible headline so the deal and the editorial story work together.
- 3. **Add an urgency signal** — a sale-end date in the preheader or a "Sale ends Sunday" callout within the email would convert the browse impulse into action.
- 4. **Cut or move the recommendation strip** — either elevate it to a mid-email module with tighter copy ("Spotted: pieces that go with this look"), or drop it entirely to preserve editorial coherence.
- 5. **Add one price callout in the product grid** — at least one "Was $X / Now $Y" label in the coastal lighting grid would make the sale real rather than notional.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Coastal lamps & pendants from $129 — Summer Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `These woven lamps sell out every summer — up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Natural textures, airy light — coastal styles up to 70% off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Table lamps, pendants and floor lights in the coastal style we keep coming back to.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP `header()` calls embedded in `<style>` block**
The following raw PHP appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side code that was not stripped before HTML generation. It is inert to email clients but will be treated as unparseable CSS and may cause style-block parsing to abort in strict renderers.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
Email clients with HTTPS enforcement or proxy-stripping will block these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — also a **cross-brand domain leak** (West Elm asset in a Pottery Barn email)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&...` — the open-tracking pixel is also HTTP

**[WARN] 50+ images missing `alt` text**
Entire content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_24.jpg`) carry no `alt` attributes. When images are blocked (default in Outlook), the email renders as a blank white column with no readable content. Affected asset paths include:

**[INFO] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**

### 5. Compliance

Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If genuinely missing from the SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced for senders >5k/day). Needs verification at the MTA level.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence will trigger inbox suppression or placement penalties at scale.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should have DKIM alignment with `potterybarn.com`. Needs verification at relay or mail log level.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`**
Suppresses HTTP Referer on clicks from webmail clients that render the email in-browser. This will cause all webmail-sourced site sessions to appear as direct traffic in analytics, masking email-attributed sessions.
