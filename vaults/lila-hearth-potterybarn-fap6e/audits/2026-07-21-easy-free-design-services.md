# Easy & FREE design services
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-forward email, not a product-discount email — Pottery Barn Design Services is pitching its free in-home or virtual design consultations. After a July packed with 70%-off sale blasts, this email makes a deliberate gear shift toward relationship and trust: "RSVP to Free Design Help" is the hero CTA, and the body stacks several editorial-style content blocks (lighting, curtains, gallery walls) around that single axis.
- The creative is competent but muddled. Too many competing content blocks — lighting upgrade tips, curtain selector, gallery wall builder, and a new arrivals product carousel — dilute the core message. The email doesn't decide what it is: a service pitch or a product launch. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalized product strip at the bottom is fine on its own but reads as a completely different email stapled to the end.
- The free-design-help angle is a credible offer with real value, and it's the right send after a discount-fatigue month. But the execution buries the lead and loses focus.

## What's working

- **Hero is on-message.** "RSVP to Free Design Help" is specific, benefit-forward, and appears immediately above the fold — the reader knows the point of the email instantly.
- **Smart cadence pivot.** After six-plus consecutive discount hammers in July, a service email breaks pattern in a way that actually earns attention.
- **Multiple CTA pathways.** "Book Your Free Appointment," "Shop Now" on individual sections, and the product carousel give different intents something to click.
- **Personalized product strip.** "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" with actual product imagery adds relevance for a user with purchase history.
- **No render bugs.** All images load, text is readable, no overlapping elements visible.

## What's weak

- **Too many content pillars competing at once.** Lighting tips, curtain guidance, gallery wall builder, new collections, and a personalized carousel is five separate emails in one.
- **"FREE" buried.** The biggest differentiator — free consultation — is in the hero but then nearly disappears mid-email; it should recur in the footer CTA block.
- **Lifestyle imagery is generic and small.** The editorial-block images (curtains, lighting, gallery) are thumbnail-sized and don't create the aspirational pull that Pottery Barn's best emails (July 18 catalog, July 20 lifestyle send) achieve.
- **Bottom CTA after the product carousel is absent.** After "Recommended Just for You," the email just trails into legal copy — a missed opportunity for a final appointment-booking push.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Commit to either the service pitch or the product editorial — not both. Run the product carousel as a separate send. The current structure dilutes the free-design-help message.
- 2. **Repeat the appointment CTA at the bottom.** After the product strip, add one final "Book Your Free Design Appointment" button. Readers who scroll deserve a second bite.
- 3. **Make the "FREE" visually dominant in the hero.** Right now it's sentence-case body copy; it should be the largest word on the hero banner.
- 4. **Upgrade the editorial imagery.** The curtain and lighting blocks use small, catalog-style crops — replace with room-scale lifestyle photography consistent with the July 20 "effortlessly styled space" send that scored 8/10.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line.** "Easy & FREE" is fine but the "Easy &" opener is weak — lead with the scarcity/value signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free design help — book your consultation`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free interior design session is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-home or virtual — we'll help you style any room, at no cost.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lighting, curtains, gallery walls — our designers have you covered, free.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header directives in `<style>` block (invalid HTML)**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");

</style>
```
PHP server-side function calls are present verbatim inside a CSS `<style>` tag. These are not valid CSS and indicate a template rendering failure — the PHP was not executed and leaked raw into the HTML output. Email clients will ignore these as unparseable CSS, so no visual breakage, but it signals a broken template compilation step that should be investigated.

**Missing alt text — all body images (34 instances)**
Every content image in the send has `alt=""`, including:
- All 8 hero/body slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`)
- All 18 footer slices
- 6 product recommendation images (bed, chair, towel, rug, quilt, curtain rod)
- Tracking/beacon images

Recipients with images disabled (common in corporate email clients) will see a blank email. Screen reader users receive no content description. This is a systemic template-level omission, not isolated to this send.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — 2 instances**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```

Mixed-content HTTP sources. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS images. These footer assets will likely not render for a significant portion of recipients. Both assets are from 2022 — likely legacy footer templates that were never upgraded to HTTPS.

**Responsive breakpoint mismatch**
Two separate media query breakpoints are declared: `max-width: 480px` (in the first `<style>` block) and `max-width: 650px` (in a third `<style>` block). The 650px query hides/shows `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` classes, but no elements in the truncated source visibly use those classes on the main content.
