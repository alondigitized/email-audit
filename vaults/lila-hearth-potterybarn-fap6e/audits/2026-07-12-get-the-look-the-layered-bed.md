# Get the look: The layered bed
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- After five consecutive days of heavy sale emails — culminating in the July 11 outdoor furniture send and the Bellevue sofa launch — Pottery Barn pivots hard into editorial mode. "Get the Look: The Layered Bed" is a styling guide, not a promo, and that decision is felt immediately: there is no discount, no urgency, no sale hook. The hero image is lovely — a moody navy-and-white bed stacked with pillows and ruffled shams — and the four-component breakdown below (ruffle hem stripe, Stonfeld velvet coverlet, Pima organic duvet, decorative pillows) reads like a legitimate shopping checklist. Visually, this is one of Pottery Barn's cleaner sends.
- The problem is the mid-email pivot. The "Recommended Just For You" block drops a large sofa image into a bedding styling email with zero editorial transition. It reads as a raw algorithmic insertion — the kind that makes a subscriber feel like they're suddenly looking at a different email. After a week of sale-calibrated sends, this editorial break has merit as a cadence reset, but the blended content undercuts it.
- Net judgement: solid editorial execution on the bed story, self-sabotaged by an off-category personalization block and zero offer gravity.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely aspirational — the layered navy/white bed photographs well and creates the "I want that" moment Pottery Barn depends on.
- The four-product breakdown structure is smart: it makes buying the look feel achievable and naturally surfaces multiple SKUs.
- Subject line "Get the look: The layered bed" is clean, under 35 characters, and sets accurate expectations — no bait-and-switch.
- Brand voice and visual identity are fully intact: serif headline, muted palette, premium photography, no clutter.

## What's weak

- No offer. After a week of "up to 70% off" headlines, a full-price editorial with no discount hook is a significant engagement cliff.
- The "Recommended Just For You" block surfaces a living room sofa inside a bedding editorial — completely off-category and editorial-voice-breaking.
- No urgency signal anywhere — a reader who likes the look has no reason to act now versus bookmarking and forgetting.
- The individual product CTAs (if present below the breakdown) are tiny at mobile size; the email doesn't deliver a single high-contrast "Shop This Look" button that consolidates the intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the editorial to the sale.** Even "All shown starting at X" or "Sale prices on select bedding" ties the July sale momentum into this editorial format rather than abandoning it cold. The July 11 outdoor email proved that "Up to 70% off" + aspirational hero can coexist.
- 2. **Replace "Recommended Just For You" with a curated bedding upsell.** The sofa recommendation destroys editorial coherence. A matching quilt, European sham, or throw would extend the look and keep the session in-category.
- 3. **Add a single consolidated "Shop the Full Look" CTA** after the four-product breakdown — a button that links to a pre-filtered bedding page, not the homepage.
- 4. **Introduce a soft urgency hook.** Even "These shams are moving fast" or "Limited quantities in Navy" is enough to counter the "I'll come back later" reflex on no-discount editorial sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code artifact in `<style>` block (template bug):**
The following appears as literal text inside a `<style>` tag — it is PHP code, not CSS:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
CSS parsers silently discard this, so there is no visual breakage, but it confirms a template composition error where a server-side snippet was injected into the wrong block. It also leaks internal caching implementation detail.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — some clients surface this in tab or preview contexts.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

**`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` applied globally** — overrides user/OS font size preferences, compounding the zoom issue above.

**50+ images missing `alt` text** — full list in QA findings. Key examples:
- All 16 `slices_0N.jpg` content images (main bed layout)
- `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` (hero banner)
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg`, etc.

**3 images served over HTTP** (will be blocked by image proxies in Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook):
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe not supported. Gmail bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) require this for >5k/day senders.

**Authentication-Results header absent** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown due to AgentMail relay interception. Cannot confirm authentication posture from this capture.

**Plain-text body >70% URLs** (10,397 / 14,571 chars) — may increase spam scoring on content filters that penalize URL-dense plain-text parts.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are expected to be in the footer (not visible in truncated source) — not flagged as missing, but should be verified against full render.
