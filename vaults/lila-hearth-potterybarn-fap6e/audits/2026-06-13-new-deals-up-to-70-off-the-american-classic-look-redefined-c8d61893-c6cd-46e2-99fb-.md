# NEW deals up to 70% off + The American classic look, redefined
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- Another day, another Pottery Barn "Up to 70% off" email — this is at least the third near-identical promo blast since June 12. The template is familiar: sale hero at the top, lifestyle editorial story in the middle ("The American classic look, redefined"), a recommended-products grid, then the utility footer stack. The dual-story structure is the core problem: the sale and the "American classic" editorial angle compete for the eye rather than reinforcing each other.
- The lifestyle photography is genuinely warm and well-shot — floral dining scenes, a classic sideboard vignette, a relaxed living room. But at this point in the cadence, a subscriber has already seen this email twice this week. The 70% off offer has been running so long it no longer registers as urgency; it's starting to feel like a permanent markdown.
- Clear judgement: competent execution of a tired playbook. The "American classic" editorial story is the more interesting half, but it's buried below a sale banner that's lost its punch through sheer repetition.

## What's working

- The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product grid gives the email some personalization scaffolding — it's the most compelling scroll-stopper in the lower half.
- Lifestyle photography is cohesive and aspirational — warm tones, natural light, classic American interiors.
- The sale offer (70% off) is immediately visible in the hero; no hunting required.
- Brand recognition is high; Pottery Barn's visual identity is consistent throughout.

## What's weak

- This is the fourth "Up to 70% off" email in roughly 48 hours — fatigue is real and the subject confirms it ("NEW deals" = same deal, re-labeled).
- The subject line tries to do two jobs with a literal "+" connector — neither the sale nor the editorial story lands cleanly.
- No credible deadline anywhere in the visible render; "up to 70% off" with no end date reads as a permanent price point.
- The "American classic look, redefined" editorial concept is never visually developed into a coherent story — it's lifestyle images without a hook or narrative.
- CTA copy appears generic ("Shop Now" tier) rather than tied to the American classic concept or a specific category.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop sending the same sale email.** The 70% off message is exhausted. If inventory pressure requires another send, lead with a specific category (outdoor furniture, rugs, bedding) rather than the blanket banner for the fifth time.
- 2. **Commit to the editorial angle or kill it.** "The American classic look, redefined" is actually a good concept — give it its own dedicated send (like the Monique Lhuillier email on June 13 that scored 8/10 precisely because it was singular).
- 3. **Add a real deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" in the hero copy would instantly restore urgency that five identical sends have eroded.
- 4. **Tighten the CTA** to a specific category: "Shop American Classic" or "Shop Living Room Sale" rather than a generic shop link.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code leaked into `<style>` block**
The following raw PHP appears as literal CSS text — it was never executed server-side:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is invalid CSS. Strict parsers may abort style processing at this point, breaking all subsequent rules. Indicates a template include was mis-rendered or a PHP partial was inlined without execution.

**32 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image in the email — including the hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 18 American Classics trend slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all 18 footer slices, and several product tiles — has no `alt` attribute or an empty one. Clients with images disabled (common in corporate/enterprise environments) will render a blank email.

**3 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Mixed-content; modern clients will block these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Additionally the open-tracking pixel is HTTP:
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
No impact on rendering, but shows in tab/preview surfaces and some accessibility tooling.

**External Google Fonts call**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Blocked by Outlook (all versions) and privacy-hardened clients. No fallback cascade specified that approximates Montserrat — fallback chain drops straight to `verdana, arial`.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If missing at send time, Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the native unsubscribe UI — increasing spam complaints. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; header absence alone isn't a violation if a link exists in body, but omission of `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) means one-click unsubscribe via Gmail's UI is not supported.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; if DMARC enforcement is active and alignment fails, deliverability is at risk. Cannot confirm without raw headers from a delivered copy.

**Plain-text part is 71.7% URLs**
10,757 of 14,994 characters in the plain-text alternative are URL strings. This ratio is above spam-filter thresholds (typically flagged above 60–70%). The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking URLs down to human-readable form.
