# Favorite dining table & chair combos
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's first email on June 3 — a curated "best combos" editorial for dining tables and chairs. It leads with a bright, sun-drenched hero lifestyle shot of a dining room, followed by a "Traditional or Modular style" decision section and a product grid of table-and-chair pairings. There is no promotional offer anywhere in the email — no discount percentage, no sale end date, no free shipping hook — just editorial curation and a "Recommended Just for You" personalization module at the bottom.
- The execution is clean and competent. Pottery Barn's photography is always strong, the layout hierarchy works, and the split "Traditional or Modular" framing gives the email a clear navigational decision point. Fatigue is real.
- Without an offer — not even free shipping, which Pottery Barn used effectively in the May 31 "Bedding, furniture & more ready to ship!" send — there is no purchase trigger here. This is a browsing email, not a conversion email.

- **Hero photography is strong.** Airy, natural-light dining room sets a clear aspirational context immediately.
- **"Traditional or Modular" decision point** gives subscribers a meaningful path split rather than a wall of products.
- **"Recommended Just for You" module** signals personalization and breaks the scroll fatigue slightly.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, text is legible throughout.
- **Subject line is specific** — "Favorite dining table & chair combos" tells you exactly what's inside at 38 characters.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** Not a sale, not free shipping, not a member price — nothing to motivate action over "I'll look later."
- **Cadence fatigue.** This is the fourth Pottery Barn email in roughly 30 hours. The goodwill from the strong design services email (June 2, 8/10) is evaporating fast.
- **CTA copy is generic.** The product grid links and buttons appear to use standard "Shop" or nav-style copy — nothing that creates urgency or specificity.
- **No pricing visible in the hero.** The dining combos feel premium but price anchoring is absent from the top scroll — makes it feel expensive by omission.

- 3. **Anchor a price in the hero.** Show one table+chair combo with "from $XXX" so the first scroll communicates value before the subscriber bounces.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP `header()` calls embedded in `<style>` block**

Inside the second `<style>` tag (client styling section):
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP cache directives, not CSS. They were not executed server-side (they appear literally in the rendered HTML) and are invalid CSS. This is a template/build error — a dynamic image server's PHP snippet was accidentally spliced into the email stylesheet. No rendering breakage in major clients since invalid CSS is ignored, but it indicates a broken build process.
