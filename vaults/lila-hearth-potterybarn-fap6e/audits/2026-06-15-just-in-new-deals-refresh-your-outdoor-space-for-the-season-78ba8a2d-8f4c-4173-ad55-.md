# Just in! New deals + Refresh your outdoor space for the season
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn's "Easy Outdoor Updates" send has a genuinely good editorial idea underneath it: the lifestyle hero (airy patio, teak and greenery, bright summer light) is attractive, and the six-tile product grid — pillows, planters, side tables, entertaining, coffee tables, rugs — gives a shopper a fast mental map of the outdoor assortment on sale. In isolation, this is a competent promotional email.
- The problem is the drumbeat it's marching in. This is the seventh or eighth "Up to 70% off" email since June 12. The lead banner is word-for-word the same as the prior send. The subject's "Just in! New deals" is the same recycled hook. A subscriber who opens their inbox this morning and sees Pottery Barn again has already been conditioned to skim past it. The outdoor editorial angle is this email's only real differentiator, and Pottery Barn led with the sale banner instead of it.
- The "Recommended Just for You" section mid-email is an algorithmic own-goal: in an email themed around outdoor furniture, the recommendations surface bedroom furniture. It breaks the editorial momentum and signals that the personalization engine isn't aware of the email context.

## What's working

- **Hero lifestyle image is genuinely seasonal.** The patio shot communicates "summer refresh" in one glance — no reading required.
- **Six-tile category grid is fast and scannable.** Clear category labels + product thumbnails give shoppers immediate jump-off points.
- **"Recommended Just for You" shows the brand is thinking about personalization** — even if the execution misfires, the intent is right.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, consistent typography throughout.
- **Loyalty and value-add services (Free Design, Affirm)** are visible in the footer without cluttering the primary flow.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Seven near-identical "Up to 70% off" emails in four days trains subscribers to skip, not open.
- **Subject is weak at the inbox level.** "Just in! New deals" has no outdoor hook, no percentage, nothing to differentiate from the prior six sends.
- **"Recommended for You" shows bedroom furniture in an outdoor-themed email.** The algorithm ignored the email's context; the mid-scroll topic shift is jarring.
- **No price anchor in the hero.** A specific product with a was/now price would give the "up to 70% off" claim a concrete, credible face.
- **No deadline anywhere.** After a week of daily "Up to 70% off" emails, "sale ends Sunday" would be the most impactful single addition.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" banner from the top.** At send #7, it's inoculating subscribers against the offer. Lead with the outdoor editorial — let the outdoor photos do the work, and let the sale language appear as supporting copy, not the headline.
- 2. **Surface one specific product with a was/now price in the hero.** "Faye Outdoor Side Table — was $299, now $149" gives click a concrete destination and the offer a credible face.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to foreground the outdoor angle**, not the deals angle.
- 4. **Add a hard sale deadline** — "outdoor sale ends Sunday" — in the hero and the subject/preheader.
- 5. **Align "Recommended for You" with the email's outdoor theme.** Filter the recommendation engine by category to match the email context.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your patio upgrade — up to 70% off, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New outdoor picks just landed (pillows, planters, rugs)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Teak tables, outdoor rugs, summer planters — sale ends Sunday night.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six categories, one deadline. Outdoor sale ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into `<style>` block (template error):**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
PHP `header()` calls landed inside a CSS `<style>` tag. These are silently invalid CSS in all clients — but they expose a CMS/template pipeline bug where server-side cache directives were dropped into the HTML output layer instead of being emitted as HTTP response headers.

**Three non-HTTPS image sources** — may be blocked by Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail with strict mixed-content policies:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**38 images missing `alt` text** — all `slices_NN.jpg/gif` content images, hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), product images, footer slices, and tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Renders as blank in image-off environments and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents user zoom on mobile — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Empty `<title>` tag** — assistive technologies read page title on load; a blank title degrades screen-reader experience.
