# Summer Warehouse deals are GOING FAST
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's eleventh-or-so email in four days anchored to the Summer Warehouse Sale, and the opening salvo is immediately visible: the hero is clean, the offer ("Up to 60% off") is readable, and the lifestyle dining room photography is on-brand. On the surface it looks competent. But the single biggest story here is what the subject *dropped* — every prior send in this run included the specific percentage ("Up to 60% off") directly in the subject line. This one swaps it for the vague urgency claim "GOING FAST," which is a measurable downgrade. The email also packs in five distinct value propositions below the fold (sale, design services, app, rewards, Affirm), turning what should be a focused conversion send into a cluttered upsell vehicle.
- The campaign is burning subscriber goodwill at a rapid rate. Four days in, the 60%-off warehouse sale no longer surprises — it has become furniture. This email would need to either deliver a new hook (price drop, last-chance deadline, specific low-inventory item) or ruthlessly simplify to earn a click from a fatigued list.
- Solid execution on the hero; weak execution on everything that happens after it.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately readable.** Logo → lifestyle image → "Summer Warehouse Sale Up to 60% off" → dark SHOP NOW button. Zero ambiguity on the offer.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section.** Personalized product tiles add genuine signal that the email knows something about the recipient — smart inclusion.
- **Category nav bar.** Quick shortcuts to Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Rugs, etc. give a direct path to specific departments rather than forcing a wide browse.
- **Loyalty reward is visible.** "Earn $75 Back in Rewards" surfaces mid-email, which reinforces member value without competing with the hero.

## What's weak

- **Percentage was dropped from the subject.** Every previous send in this run led with "Up to 60% off" in the subject line. "Summer Warehouse deals are GOING FAST" removes the most concrete conversion lever and replaces it with vague urgency.
- **No credible deadline anywhere in the email.** "Going fast" is a fear hook that collapses unless there's a date, a countdown, or a "selling out" inventory signal to back it up. There's none visible.
- **Five competing sections below the fold.** Design Services → App → Rewards → Affirm → Social dilutes the sale message. A fatigued subscriber scanning quickly will bounce before acting on any of them.
- **"Recommended" product thumbnails are too small.** The grid layout renders the individual product images at a size where detail is unreadable — defeating the personalization investment.
- **Cadence is unsustainable.** Eleven emails in four days for the same sale event. Even a well-executed send is working against inbox saturation at this volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the percentage to the subject.** "GOING FAST" buys nothing that a concrete "60% off" doesn't already give you — plus specific numbers anchor the value proposition. See alts below.
- 2. **Add a deadline or inventory signal in the hero.** A line like "Sale ends Sunday" or "Under 200 left in this colorway" transforms the urgency from vague to actionable. Even a placeholder date outperforms no date.
- 3. **Cut to two sections below the fold.** The sale should close with the personalized product grid and a single supporting benefit (loyalty rewards is the most conversion-aligned). Bury Design Services and Affirm in footer or a separate send.
- 4. **Enlarge recommended product tiles.** 3-column layout at current render size is too small; 2-column with product name + price would convert better.
- 5. **Slow down.** Two sends on June 8 for the same 60%-off event erodes the value of the offer. If the send volume can't be cut, differentiate by segment or by product category rather than repeating the same hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off — Summer Warehouse Sale ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks are selling out — 60% off, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on furniture, bedding, rugs + your personalized picks`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Recommended just for you — while supplies last`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) affect the credit card CTA and a cross-brand image asset. A PHP `header()` code fragment is embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block — syntactically inert but evidence of a template copy-paste error. Accessibility coverage for images is near-zero.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card CTA returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
The KeyCC banner (non-member credit card apply/buy CTA) resolves to a 403. Clicking lands on an error; conversions from this module are zero.

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm-domain asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn send. It is broken and served over HTTP (see §3). Indicates a stale shared template component that was never updated.

**WARN — 25 click-redirect links not probed**
Tracking links through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not followed. UTM parameter integrity on final destinations cannot be confirmed from this audit (see §6).

**WARN — Open pixel served over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
Open-tracking pixel uses HTTP. Gmail and Apple Mail proxy all images over HTTPS; an HTTP pixel may be blocked or silently upgraded, causing open rate discrepancies.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a CSS `<style>` tag — it is syntactically invalid CSS and serves no function in an HTML email:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a copy-paste artifact from a server-side image-serving script. It should be removed.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some email clients display the title tag in the tab or preview pane. Should be populated or omitted.

**3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail's image proxy and Apple Mail will either block or attempt HTTPS upgrades on these. The 2022 path on the third URL suggests a stale asset reference.

**40+ images missing `alt` text**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, footer images, and tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. Subscribers with images blocked see a fully blank email. Fails WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 (screen reader users receive no content).

Affected examples:
- `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg` — primary content area
- `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg` — product images
- `S26_Bottom_UpTo50OffOutdoor.jpg`, `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg` — promotional banners

### 5. Compliance

Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If absent at send time, Gmail and Yahoo will not display the one-click unsubscribe UI that both now require for bulk senders. Risk of increased spam complaints.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since Feb 2024) cannot be confirmed. If neither header is present in the raw SMTP message, the send is non-compliant with current platform requirements.

**WARN — Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be verified from the captured headers. If DKIM is unsigned or SPF is failing for `e.potterybarn.com`, inbox placement will be affected.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer (standard Pottery Barn template) — not contradicted by the available source, but cannot confirm from the truncated HTML.
