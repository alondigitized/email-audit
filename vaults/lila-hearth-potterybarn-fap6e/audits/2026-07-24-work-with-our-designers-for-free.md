# Work with our designers for FREE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's design services pitch — a service-forward break from the heavy discount cadence that's defined the brand's July sends. The premise is strong: new fall arrivals just landed, and free in-home or virtual design help is a genuinely high-value offer that differentiates from the competition. The "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA is clear, the personalized product grid below the fold hedges for non-bookers, and the rewards + Affirm modules address downstream purchase friction.
- The email has a critical dead-weight problem though: a "FREE IN-STORE DESIGN CLASS" banner prominently advertises an event dated **July 19 | 10AM** — five days ago. Today is July 24. Any subscriber who opens this on or after today sees an invitation to an event that already happened. That's not a minor inconsistency; it erodes trust and makes the brand look like it's not paying attention to its own sends.
- Beyond the stale event, the module stack is bloated. Hero → event banner → personalized products → app download → rewards → Affirm is six distinct asks. The design services message gets real but the email can't decide if it wants to sell you a service, a product, an app, or a financing plan.

## What's working

- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA is specific and unambiguous** — verb + noun, above the fold, well-differentiated from a generic "Shop Now."
- **Personalized product grid ("RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU")** smartly backstops the non-booker — if you won't schedule an appointment, you can still shop a curated shelf.
- **Rewards module (10% Back)** is well-placed to close hesitant buyers; keeps the loyalty proposition visible in a non-sale send.
- **Service framing is a genuine departure** from Pottery Barn's July blast cadence — the 2026-07-22 "New dining furniture" and 2026-07-21 "Storage that clears the clutter" sends were product-only; this one actually offers something free and experiential.

## What's weak

- **The July 19 design class event is past-dated — subscribers seeing this on July 24 are being invited to an event that already happened.** This is a content QA failure and the most damaging single element in the email.
- **Six competing modules with no clear hierarchy after the hero** — the email tries to sell a service, a class, products, an app, rewards, and Affirm financing simultaneously.
- **No urgency or soft deadline on the primary appointment CTA** — "Free" is the only hook; there's nothing anchoring why you'd book today vs. next month.
- **"FREE" all-caps in the subject line** edges toward spam-signal territory, especially alongside "Free Design Services" in the hero, "FREE IN-STORE DESIGN CLASS" in the banner, and "FREE" in the rewards copy — the word appears too often at too high a register.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pull or replace the July 19 stale event banner immediately.** Either redirect to the next scheduled design class date or swap in a "virtual anytime" version that doesn't expire. This is the highest-priority fix — it actively damages credibility.
- 2. **Add a soft deadline to the appointment CTA.** Even "Book by August 15 to get first access to fall arrivals" gives a reason to act now.
- 3. **Cut two modules.** App download and Affirm both belong in transactional / cart-abandonment flows, not in a service pitch email. Removing them tightens the message to: design services → products → rewards.
- 4. **Rebalance "FREE" usage.** Use "complimentary" once in hero body copy; keep "FREE" for the CTA only so the subject doesn't land as shouting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our designers are free to help — literally`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free 1:1 design help: fall arrivals just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New styles in stores now — book an in-home or virtual session at no cost`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Style your space with a real PB designer, no strings attached`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

```
https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB
  &marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner
  &cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This link is in a commented-out block in the HTML (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not rendered to subscribers. However, it still appears in link-checker output because the raw HTML was scanned before comment stripping. **Not a live subscriber risk**, but the dead URL should be cleaned from template source to avoid confusion.


### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (2) — may be blocked by email clients:**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail proxy/block non-HTTPS image sources. Both images will fail to render for a significant portion of the subscriber base. These are footer assets — the CC banner and brand banner. Fix: update `src` to `https://`.

**Missing alt text: 30+ images flagged**, including all body slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images (curtain rod, swivel chair, curtains, quilt, rug, towel), the footer slices, and tracking pixels. With images blocked (the default in many clients), subscribers see no fallback text on any content block. The hero and product images are the highest-impact gaps.

**PHP headers inside `<style>` tag:**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
```
This is server-side PHP code that was rendered literally into the `<style>` block rather than being executed. It is inert in the browser/email client but indicates a template rendering failure — a PHP dynamic image endpoint's output was embedded raw into the HTML. No functional breakage to the subscriber, but it's a build-pipeline defect.

**Preheader mismatch:** The hidden preheader text reads "New arrivals just landed in stores!" while the subject line is "Work with our designers for FREE." These are mismatched offers, which will hurt open-rate performance on clients that show preheader text. (Flagging as a technical continuity issue, not a copy issue.)

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found**
Not captured in the relay view. If absent from actual delivery headers, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). Needs verification against raw received headers.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo) requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Absence of the `-Post` header means Gmail's unsubscribe button may route to a landing page rather than processing instantly, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found**
SPF/DKIM status unverifiable from the AgentMail relay capture. Given the sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (an ESP subdomain), DKIM should be present — likely a relay capture gap, but must be confirmed with raw `.eml` headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated HTML, but typically present in PB footers. Assume compliant pending full-source review.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The active body links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs with opaque `qs=` parameters — UTM attribution is handled server-side by the redirect resolver, not visible in the HTML. This is standard ESP practice and not a defect, but it means UTM coverage cannot be audited from source alone.

The commented-out credit card link uses `cm_ite=` (Williams-Sonoma's legacy `cm_` parameter scheme) rather than standard UTM. Since the link is commented out, this is not a live issue.
