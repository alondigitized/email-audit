# Free design help to get you guest-ready
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-led send, not a discount blast. That makes it a welcome change from the run of promos Pottery Barn has sent this week. The hero, "Need Help Getting Guest-Ready?", uses a what/when/where block (styling tips, choose a time, home, virtual or in-store). It ends in a dark BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT button. The subject and hero match, and the Thanksgiving hosting angle fits the season.
- The middle section is where it loses focus. Seasonal decorating, Festive table styling and New furniture updates each get a "BOOK NOW" text link. Those links are small and repeat the same action three times. A "Ready to get started?" button then repeats the hero CTA. The free in-store class, "Prep for Fall Hosting, October 4 | Sunday, 10AM," is the only concrete, dated offer in the email. It sits below the fold, and it's tomorrow. After that comes a generic "Recommended Just For You" grid (platform bed, swivel chair, curtain rod, ghost collection, rug, dining table) that has nothing to do with design help. Then comes a long stack of credit card, Affirm and app promos.
- The result is a decent concept with a diluted body. Compared with the 10/03 "All tabletop now ships free!" send, which had a clearer hook, the value here is softer. "Free" appears, but there's no price or deadline. I'd call it a solid 6 to 7, held back by an undated "spots go fast" and a footer that gets more space than the offer.

## What's working

- The hero is clear and on-subject. "Need Help Getting Guest-Ready?" matches the subject, and the what/when/where block explains the offer fast.
- The primary CTA is a high-contrast dark button with specific copy: "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT."
- The Thanksgiving and overnight-guest framing fits the season and feels timely.
- The three service tiles (Seasonal decorating, Festive table styling, New furniture updates) use warm, styled photography and alternate sides, which reads as editorial and on-brand.
- The "Free in-store design class" banner, with a date, time and RSVP TODAY button, is the one credible urgency device.

## What's weak

- The "spots go fast" line gives no deadline or availability. The only real date, October 4, is buried in the lower third.
- The three "BOOK NOW" links are small text and identical. They don't say which service you're booking.
- "Recommended Just For You" is unrelated product filler. It pulls the reader from a service ask into a catalog browse, and the Lit Ghost Collection undercuts the hosting theme.
- The credit card, Affirm and app blocks add a lot of length. The 10% back in rewards pitch competes with the design-help message.
- No proof is visible, such as designer names, customer rooms or reviews. A free service from an unnamed "design crew" is easy to scroll past.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the dated class up and make it the urgency anchor. Put "Free class tomorrow: Prep for Fall Hosting, Sun 10AM" directly under the hero. Give the appointment CTA a real deadline, such as "Book by Oct 15 for pre-Thanksgiving slots."
- 2. Cut the "Recommended Just For You" grid, or replace it with hosting-specific items such as dinnerware, table linens and guest bedding. Drop one of the two financial blocks.
- 3. Make the three service tiles specific: "Book a table-styling consult" rather than "BOOK NOW." Use button styling instead of small links.
- 4. Add proof, such as a designer headshot, a before/after, or a line like "Over X rooms styled."
- 5. Show what a session includes (length, who you meet, what you leave with) so "free" feels concrete.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a fixed-width (650px), image-sliced layout. Its main defects are malformed CSS, empty alt text on every linked image, plain HTTP assets, and the recipient's email address embedded in third-party pixel URLs. Header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be confirmed, and the source was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Opaque click tracking:** 46 links go through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). The QA skipped HTTP probing on them, so destinations, redirects and UTM parameters are unverified.
- **Plain HTTP images:**
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  
  Both can be blocked or flagged as mixed content. The second path is a 2022 asset, so it looks stale.
- **Cross-brand asset host:** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is hosted on a West Elm domain. It will break if that host changes.
- **Third-party ad pixels:**
  - `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501…`
  - `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…`
  - `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true…`
  
  These add retargeting calls and external dependencies.
- **Dead code in source:** A commented-out header block ships in the HTML, including a URL with `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`. It is inert but adds bytes and exposes internal tracking names.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed CSS:** The second `<style>` block begins with PHP-style text: `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: …"); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. A CSS parser folds that text into the selector prelude of the next rule. The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset is therefore likely dropped, and iOS may auto-link and restyle addresses and dates.
- **Responsive rules don't match the markup:**
  - The `@media (max-width:480px)` rules target `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`.
  - No element in the visible source has those classes. All tables are hard-coded `width="650"` and images `width="650"`.
  - The mobile rules appear to be dead code, so the email would scale down rather than reflow. The source is truncated, so this covers only the visible portion.
- **Linked images with empty alt:** `slices_01.jpg` to `slices_07.jpg` have `alt=""` inside `<a href="click.e.potterybarn.com/…">`. Those links have no accessible name, and nothing renders with images blocked. The QA also flags the following as missing alt:
  - product images such as `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg` and `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`
  - footer slices `Email-Footer/slices_01` to `slices_18`
- **Tracking pixels flagged:** `coreemail.html`, `leadback`, `d.turn.com` and `svopn` pixels have no `alt`. These should carry `alt=""` and 1×1 dimensions.
- **Document-level gaps:**
  - No `<title>` or `lang` on `<html>`.
  - No `role="presentation"` on the layout tables.
  - The ampScript comment precedes the `<!DOCTYPE>`, which can trigger quirks mode in some clients.
  - The `<link>` to Google Fonts (Montserrat) is stripped by Gmail and Outlook, and no visible element uses it.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens or ampScript were found. The ampScript slot is just empty comments.
- **PII in URLs:** The recipient address is passed in plaintext in image URLs:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  
  The address can end up in server logs, proxy caches and the third-party pixel request.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Headers not captured:** The QA found no `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) or `Authentication-Results`. It attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so this is **unconfirmed, not a confirmed defect**. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
- **Body checks not possible:** The truncated source doesn't show the visible unsubscribe link or physical postal address. The footer is built from image slices (`Email-Footer/slices_01` to `slices_18`, no alt), so these elements may be image-only. Verify against the full HTML.
- **Sender:** `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` is a branded subdomain. Nothing in the visible data contradicts it.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters are visible in any live link, because all are wrapped in opaque `qs=` redirects. The only attribution-style parameters (`cm_ite`, `brandCode`, `marketingChannelCode`) are in the commented-out block and aren't live.
- Landing-page alignment can't be assessed without resolving the redirects. The final destinations of the `click.e.potterybarn.com` links and the design-services page are unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block so the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule parses.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to all linked slices, product images and footer slices. Use `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"` on tracking pixels.
3. Change the two `http://edm.potterybarn.com/…` images to HTTPS. Replace the 2022 banner and the `westelm.com` spacer, or host them on the sender's own domain.
4. Stop putting the raw email address in `coreemail.html` and `sv_sveme` URLs. Use an opaque subscriber key instead.
5. Align the responsive CSS with the markup, either by adding the `wrapper` and `photo` classes or by rewriting the rules. Add `<title>`, `lang` and `role="presentation"`, and put the DOCTYPE first.
6. Strip the commented-out HTML block.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML and the raw headers (outside the relay) to confirm the following. Also resolve the tracking redirects to check UTMs and landing pages.
   - `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`
   - SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment
   - a text-based unsubscribe link and postal address
