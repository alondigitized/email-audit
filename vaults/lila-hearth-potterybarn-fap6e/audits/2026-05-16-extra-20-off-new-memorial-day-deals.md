# Extra 20% off + NEW Memorial Day deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's Day 2 Memorial Day sale reminder — an "Extra 20% off" hero with a product grid, a "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization block, and the now-standard credit card / app upsell footer. The visual execution is clean: logo → sale badge → headline → lifestyle hero → category grid, exactly the hierarchy you'd want. The "NEW Memorial Day deals" copy in the subject is doing real work, implying refreshed inventory rather than the same send from yesterday.
- The problem is cadence. Pottery Barn sent three emails on May 15 (two on the same Extra 20% off sale, one on free design services), and now another on May 16. A subscriber seeing this in their inbox is already filtering. The creative is strong enough to survive one more send, but the creative isn't the bottleneck — fatigue is.
- If the "NEW" deals are genuinely fresh product picks, this works. If the product grid is identical to yesterday's "EXTRA 20% OFF 📣 The Memorial Day Sale starts today" (which also scored well), the "NEW" reads as false urgency and erodes trust.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is instant.** "Extra 20% off" lands in the first visual beat — no scrolling required to know what this email is selling.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization block.** This is a legitimate conversion pull; product-level personalization below the fold earns its space.
- **Visual hierarchy is coherent.** Sale badge → headline → lifestyle image → category grid flows without fighting itself.
- **No render bugs.** The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- **Subject length is mobile-safe at 38 chars** — well under the 50-char mobile cutoff.

## What's weak

- **"NEW Memorial Day deals" is unsubstantiated in the body.** If the product grid is unchanged from yesterday's send, this headline reads as empty hype. Subscribers will notice.
- **Cadence is burning goodwill.** Fourth Pottery Barn email in two days; that kills open rate regardless of creative quality.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "Shop Now" is a placeholder. No category specificity — no "Shop Outdoor" or "Shop Dining" to orient someone who just skimmed the hero.
- **No explicit sale-end date visible.** "Memorial Day" implies a deadline, but no date is shown. Credible urgency needs a clock.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the deadline.** Add "Ends Monday, May 26" to the hero subhead. Memorial Day is a known holiday, but spelling out the date doubles the urgency signal without requiring any design change.
- 2. **Make the CTA category-specific.** Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop Furniture & Outdoor" — or dynamically pull the subscriber's most-viewed category. "Shop Now" has nowhere to go.
- 3. **Prove the "NEW" in the subject.** If even 4–6 products in the grid are genuinely new since yesterday, call them out with a "New Arrivals Added" badge on those tiles. Otherwise, cut the word "NEW" from the subject entirely — it's doing more damage than lift.
- 4. **Suppress or compress the cadence.** The Day 2 send is defensible; a third send on Monday (the day before Memorial Day) would be the right moment. Sending every day of the sale weekend trains subscribers to wait for the next email rather than act.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to lead with scarcity.** The current subject front-loads the offer (good) but buries urgency at the end.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off ends Monday — new deals added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Memorial Day picks + Extra 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals added to the sale. Shop before Memorial Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, bedding, outdoor — all extra 20% off through Monday.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two destination links are hard-broken (403), three image assets load over HTTP (mixed-content), and a critical template rendering failure leaked raw PHP `header()` directives into a CSS `<style>` block. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are absent or unverifiable through the relay.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply link returns 403**
- URL: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember...`
- This is the primary member/Key Rewards CTA in the header banner. A 403 means the click-path is dead for all recipients who aren't already logged in or for whom the token is invalid. High-impact.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer pixel returns 403 over HTTP**
- URL: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- Cross-brand asset (West Elm domain) embedded in a Pottery Barn send. The 403 plus HTTP protocol means this image will be blocked by most modern email clients. Indicates a stale/copied template that was never cleaned up.

**[INFO] 24 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains not probed; destination integrity unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header() directives leaked into CSS `<style>` block**

The following server-side PHP code appears literally inside a `<style>` tag in the rendered HTML:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not CSS — they are PHP output-control functions that were never executed by the template engine and escaped raw into the HTML. Browsers silently ignore them inside `<style>`, but this is evidence of a broken template render pipeline. Any dynamic logic in the same template block should be audited for similar failures.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed-content)**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. These will render as broken images for a significant portion of recipients.

**[WARN] Open/tracking pixel served over HTTP**
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=<email>`
- HTTP open-tracking pixel will be blocked by clients enforcing HTTPS, causing open-rate undercounting.

**[WARN] 38 content images missing `alt` text** — the entire product grid (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`), product PDPs, footer slices, and promo banners all lack `alt` attributes. Images-off rendering (default in many enterprise mail environments) produces a completely blank email.

**[INFO] Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` — has no deliverability impact but affects accessibility tree labeling in some clients.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. If genuinely absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy (>5k/day threshold). Missing this header puts the send at risk of inbox demotion.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not available** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unverifiable through the relay. Should be confirmed at the raw header level before treating the send as authenticated.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` is set** — suppresses HTTP `Referer` header on all link clicks. This will strip referring source data from any analytics platform that relies on referrer (Google Analytics, Adobe) rather than UTM params alone.
