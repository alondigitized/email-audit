# Sale styles UP TO 70% OFF + Your guide to styling patterned rugs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn running its by-now-deeply-familiar compound subject formula — "sale hook + editorial hook" — for what feels like the seventh or eighth time in the July cycle (the July 14 evening send "Summer deals ☀️ UP TO 70% OFF + New arrivals for a coastal cottage look" being the most recent copy). The rug category focus is a genuine improvement over generic "furniture" compound subjects, and the Rug Buying Guide editorial content is actually useful — but the execution buries itself. The sale hero lands fine; then the email keeps going, and going, through the editorial guide, into a "Recommended Just for You" algorithmic block, then a "Discover New Arrivals" sofa section, then an app download promo. Four distinct email objectives in one send is not a strategy — it's a content dump.
- The rug category is specific and the guide format is a smart departure from pure-promo. If this were a clean two-part email (sale hero → rug guide, full stop), it would be a strong 7. Instead, the "Recommended Just for You" and "Discover New Arrivals" sections drag this into feature-overload territory, diluting the one thing that would make someone click: a clear path from "I want a patterned rug at 70% off" to a purchase.
- Verdict: a structurally congested email with a good concept underneath it. The rug buying guide earns its place; the algorithmic appendage at the bottom does not.

## What's working

- **Category specificity in the editorial hook.** "Patterned rugs" is more targeted than the July 14 "coastal cottage look" or the generic furniture blasts — it speaks to someone mid-decision on a specific purchase.
- **Hero photography is strong.** The layered rug imagery in the hero reads as editorial-quality, not stock, and establishes the brand voice immediately.
- **Sale offer is prominently placed.** UP TO 70% OFF appears in the hero without scrolling on mobile — the offer is front-loaded correctly.
- **Rug Buying Guide is genuinely useful editorial content.** Room-pairing imagery and styling logic give the reader a reason to engage beyond the discount — this is table-stakes loyalty-building.
- **No render bugs.** Layout appears clean throughout; no overlapping text, broken images, or truncated blocks visible.

## What's weak

- **Four competing objectives sink the email's focus.** Sale hero → rug buying guide → algorithmic recs → new arrivals → app download is not a funnel, it's a feed. A reader who was ready to buy a rug gets redirected to a sofa before they can click.
- **"Recommended Just for You" feels like filler.** The block appears at the bottom of a content-heavy email, which is the worst placement for personalization — no one who scrolled that far is still in a discovery mindset.
- **"Discover New Arrivals" sofa block has no thematic connection to rugs.** This mid-email pivot signals that the send is patched together from multiple content buckets rather than built around the rug narrative.
- **No urgency signal on the sale.** The 70% off offer has no deadline, no countdown, no "ends Sunday" — it floats without an action hook.
- **Compound "+" subject formula is fatigued.** Given brand history, this is at minimum the sixth "X + Y" subject in July. A subscriber who got the July 15 sofa send yesterday and the July 14 compound sends has already seen this template.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut everything below the Rug Buying Guide.** Remove the "Recommended Just for You" block, the "Discover New Arrivals" sofa block, and the app download promo. This email has one job: move rugs. Every non-rug pixel is friction.
- 2. **Add a sale deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" turns a passive 70% off into an action trigger. No change to offer required — just name a day.
- 3. **Make the primary CTA copy specific.** A "Shop Patterned Rugs" button outperforms a generic "Shop Now" in this context — the guide sets up exactly that intent.
- 4. **Break the compound subject formula.** After six-plus uses this month, the "+" structure is invisible to repeat openers. The rug buying guide angle is interesting enough to stand alone.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your guide to patterned rugs — up to 70% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `How to style a patterned rug (+ 70% off to try it)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Area rugs, runners, flatweaves — find the right style for every room`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bold patterns, subtle textures, sale prices — pick your pairing`
- 5. **Relocate the "Recommended Just for You" block to a separate triggered email.** Algorithmic recs belong in a post-browse or post-cart sequence, not appended to editorial content.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls rendered as raw CSS text**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are PHP server-side directives that were never executed — they landed verbatim in the `<style>` block. They parse as invalid CSS selectors and will generate CSS parse errors in strict clients. This is a template/build pipeline defect.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
- `<title></title>` — blank. Some clients (notably Gmail web) display the title in tab/window labels; this is a missed surface.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — suppresses user zoom. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility auditors.

**38 images missing `alt` text**
Notable missing alt instances:
- `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero banner (primary CTA surface)
- All 12 `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_12.jpg` in the rugs editorial section
- All 18 footer slices (`Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`)
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`, etc.
- `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`

Clients with images disabled will render blank for the entire email body and footer.

**3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources**
These may be blocked by security-conscious clients (Gmail, Outlook.com):
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`

The HTTP open-tracking pixel is particularly high-risk: if blocked, open-rate data for this send will be undercounted.
