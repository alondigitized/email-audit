# On sale & shipping free✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's least differentiated send in the recent stretch — "On sale & shipping free" is the vaguest hero claim of the bunch, with no percentage, no dollar figure, no deadline. Compare it to 2026-07-30's "Starting at $299: Handcrafted rugs" or 2026-07-29's "Starting at $69: Cloud-like duvets" — both led with a hard number that gave the reader a reason to click. Here, the number is just... missing.
- Below the hero it sprawls into the now-familiar kitchen sink: an 8-tile category grid, a "Recommended Just For You" product block with zero prices, a Halloween Shop teaser, a Design Crew pitch, an app download push, and a credit-card rewards pitch — all in one email. Nothing is broken, nothing is ugly, but nothing is prioritized either.
- This will still get opened because "Pottery Barn" in the sender name carries real brand equity, but it's a coast-on-recognition send, not a converting one. Judgement: functional but forgettable — a 6/10 email propped up by strong brand trust rather than a strong offer.

## What's working

- Hero renders cleanly — soft neutral living room styling, no overlapping text or broken images.
- "SHOP NOW" button is high-contrast and impossible to miss directly under the headline.
- Category grid (Pillows, Throws, Quilts, Duvets, etc.) gives multiple low-friction entry points into the sale.
- Halloween Shop banner is a legitimate seasonal hook and reads as fresh content, not just another discount push.
- Consistent Pottery Barn visual identity throughout — no brand-voice whiplash.

## What's weak

- No concrete discount anywhere in the hero — "on sale" with no %/$ figure is the weakest offer framing this brand has run recently.
- "Recommended Just For You" products (curtain rod, platform bed, dining table, swivel chair, duvet, rug) show zero prices — can't gauge value or decide to click.
- The email tries to do five jobs at once (sale, Halloween, Design Crew, app download, credit card) with no visual priority given to any single one.
- Subject and hero copy don't call out a specific category or item, so there's nothing to anchor a quick decision.
- This is at least the 6th similar promotional send from Pottery Barn in a week-long window — cadence fatigue is real here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number in the hero — "Up to 30% Off Everything + Free Shipping" beats "On sale & shipping free" every time; this single change would lift open and click rates the most.
- 2. Add prices to the "Recommended Just For You" grid — right now those six products are unclickable without knowing cost.
- 3. Cut the email down to one job. Move Halloween Shop and Design Crew into their own sends (both already got standalone treatment on 2026-07-31 and worked well there).
- 4. Give the CTA a specific noun instead of generic "Shop Now" — e.g., "Shop the Sale" or "Shop Pillows & Throws."

- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale: pillows, throws & rugs — all ship free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on bestselling pillows, throws & rugs, today only`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "On sale & shipping free✨"

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard SFMC-style promotional template (e.potterybarn.com / click.e.potterybarn.com tracking domains) with multiple third-party open-tracking pixels, widespread missing alt text, several HTTP (non-TLS) image sources, and no detectable List-Unsubscribe headers on this copy.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 53 tracking/click-redirect links (click.e.potterybarn.com wrapper, e.g. `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7...`) were skipped by the automated HTTP probe — link destinations are unverified.
- Four distinct open-tracking pixels fire on this single email: `image.e.potterybarn.com/lib/.../3ceb0c96-....jpg`, `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`, `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` (Amobee/Turn — third-party ad-tech DMP pixel, not an ESP-native beacon), and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/0899ca85-...?mi_mid=...`. Four separate vendors tracking open is redundant and worth flagging for privacy/vendor-sprawl review.
- The `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel is served over **plain HTTP**, not HTTPS.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 40+ `<img>` tags are missing `alt` text (full list in QA output — nav banners, product slices, footer slices, all product shots e.g. `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`). Screen readers and images-off clients (default in Outlook) will show nothing.
- 3 images load over `http://` instead of `https://`: `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`. Mixed-content blocking in modern mail clients may prevent these from loading.
- The `<style>` block contains literal, non-functional PHP code: `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...");` etc. This is invalid CSS (parsed as unrecognized declarations and ignored), but its presence indicates a build/template artifact — likely a PHP image-serving snippet accidentally pasted into the CSS block instead of being executed server-side.
- `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">` — external webfont loading via `<link>` is unsupported by most mail clients (Outlook desktop/COM, Gmail app strip `<link>` entirely), so this adds a dead external request for the majority of recipients with no rendering benefit.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{firstname}}`) observed in the visible markup.
- However, the recipient's raw email address is embedded in cleartext in multiple tracking/asset URLs: `.../coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?...&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. The second instance is over **unencrypted HTTP**, meaning the full email address is transmitted in the clear and would be exposed to any network intermediary, plus is vulnerable to referrer leakage.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found — may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not surfacing it, but cannot be confirmed present.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe): not found — if genuinely absent, this fails Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC outcome): not found — authentication status of this message is unverified from available data.
- The truncated HTML excerpt does not include the footer/unsubscribe-link markup, so in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address (both CAN-SPAM requirements) could not be verified from source alone.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- All outbound links route through the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirector; final destination URLs and any UTM parameters are hidden behind this wrapper and were not resolved (probe skipped, per QA findings). Cannot confirm UTM tagging or landing-page alignment with this data set.
- The customer-service link (`https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) points directly at potterybarn.com (bypassing the click tracker) — inconsistent with the rest of the link inventory, and carries the PII exposure noted in Section 4.

**7. Recommendations**
- Add `alt` attributes to all content/product images for accessibility and images-off legibility.
- Migrate the 3 HTTP image/pixel sources to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
- Remove the stray PHP `header()` snippet from the `<style>` block — dead code pointing to a template-assembly bug.
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually present on the wire (verify with raw headers outside the AgentMail relay) given current bulk-sender requirements.
- Stop passing the recipient's email address in plaintext URL parameters, especially over HTTP (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) — switch to a hashed/tokenized subscriber ID.
- Consider consolidating the four separate open-tracking pixels to reduce redundant third-party data sharing (notably the `d.turn.com` ad-tech pixel).
- Drop the Google Fonts `<link>` since it's inert for most mail clients, or convert to an inlined `@font-face`/fallback stack if webfont support matters for webmail opens.
