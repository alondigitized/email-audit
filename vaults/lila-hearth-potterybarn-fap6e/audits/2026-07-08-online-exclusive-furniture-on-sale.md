# Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a furniture sale email doing exactly what it says on the tin — "Summer Sale: Up to 30% off Select Furniture" — organized cleanly by room category (accent, living room, dining room, bedroom) with a clear hero CTA. The "online exclusive" framing is a genuine differentiator that Pottery Barn almost entirely wastes: it gets a small badge at the top and then disappears, never once explaining what makes this exclusive or how long it lasts.
- The bigger issue is cadence. This is Pottery Barn's second email today, landing just hours after the "New arrivals inspired by heritage design" editorial send (scored 8/10). That email was inspirational and voice-driven; this one immediately snaps back to "ON SALE" mode. Subscribers who got both will experience whiplash, and anyone still recovering from the 20+ July 4th emails (which ended as recently as July 6th) is going to mentally file this in the same tired promotional pile. Pottery Barn tried to reset the cadence on July 7th with design services and editorial content — this email partially undoes that work.
- The execution is competent. The email isn't broken. But "competent furniture sale email arriving at the worst possible cadence moment" is a ceiling, not a floor.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible:** "Summer Sale: Up to 30% off / Select Furniture Now on Sale / SHOP THE SALE" is visible without scrolling and requires zero decoding.
- **Room-organized navigation:** Accent → Living Room → Dining Room → Bedroom mirrors how furniture shoppers actually browse; this is the right IA for a furniture sale.
- **"In-stock" language:** "SHOP ALL IN-STOCK FURNITURE" addresses the single biggest friction in furniture e-commerce (availability) with one visible callout.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section:** Adds a personalization layer that breaks up the generic sale grid and signals segmentation even if it's algorithmic.
- **"Online exclusive" is a real offer hook** — it promises something unavailable in-store, which is substantively different from a generic sale.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 30% off" is a ceiling claim with no floor anchor:** No example product, no "was $X now $Y" — experienced furniture shoppers know "up to" means the best item at the biggest discount, and the rest could be 10% off.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email:** "Online exclusive" implies scarcity but the email never says when the sale ends. Without a deadline, urgency is zero.
- **Email is dramatically overlong:** After the clean furniture sections, the email adds Recommended, Discover New Arrivals, App download badges, Pottery Barn credit card pitch, and Buy More Pay Over Time. The credit card section alone undercuts the "exclusive sale" tone completely.
- **"ON SALE" in all-caps in the subject is a minor spam-filter signal** and looks like low-effort email marketing from a brand that normally writes better copy.
- **Same-day cadence clash with the heritage design editorial send:** Running a promo email and an inspirational email in the same day trains subscribers that everything is promotional — the editorial work loses credibility.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline — this is the single biggest lift.** Even "Offer ends Thursday" in the hero and subject transforms this from a browse invitation into an action trigger. Without it, subscribers bookmark and forget.
- 2. **Anchor one product with real before/after pricing in the hero.** "Sectional sofas from $1,099 (was $1,499)" is more persuasive than "up to 30% off" because it proves the discount rather than claiming it.
- 3. **Build the email around the "online exclusive" angle, not the generic sale:** Lean into the exclusivity — these prices aren't in stores, limited time, online-only access. That's actually a reason to act now; "furniture on sale" is not.
- 4. **Cut the bottom third:** App download, credit card, and BNPL sections belong in a footer digest, not in the middle of a sale send. They dilute the message and extend a scroll path that most mobile readers abandon.
- 5. **Don't send this same-day as an editorial email.** If the heritage design email runs in the morning, the sale email should be tomorrow — or the sale email should be woven into the editorial narrative ("The new arrivals inspired by heritage design — now on sale, online only").

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Online exclusive: Furniture ON SALE"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard broken experiences (timeout + 403) will silently drop clicks for affected link targets. PHP `header()` calls are embedded verbatim inside a `<style>` block, indicating a template rendering failure that never got caught in QA.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Click-redirect timeout** — The Apply & Buy banner CTA (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-...`) timed out during probing. If this reflects production latency, the link is functionally dead for a percentage of recipients. No fallback destination is present.

**[FAIL] 403 on tracking spacer** — `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns HTTP 403. This is a West Elm asset served over plain HTTP, cross-domain from a Pottery Barn send — likely a stale template carry-over. The 403 will cause a broken image request on every open.

**[WARN] 25 tracking links unverified** — All click.e.potterybarn.com redirect links were skipped by the probe. These should be spot-checked manually against the final destination URLs before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header directives inside `<style>` block** — The following raw PHP output appears literally in the CSS, never executed:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is a template preprocessing failure. The PHP was not interpreted before send; it now lives as invalid CSS text. Some parsers will ignore it; others may partially corrupt the surrounding style block.

**[FAIL] 3 images served over HTTP (mixed content)** — These will be blocked by Gmail, Yahoo, and most modern clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**[WARN] 30+ images missing alt text** — The bulk of content images (hero banners, product shots, footer slices) have no `alt` attribute. Clients with images disabled will render a blank email. Screen readers have no fallback text for any visual content.

**[WARN] `<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` provides no context for screen readers or accessibility tools.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders sending >5k/day to Gmail. Absence here is a compliance gap if the header is genuinely missing (not just relay-stripped).
