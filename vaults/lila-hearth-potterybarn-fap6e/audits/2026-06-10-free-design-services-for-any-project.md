# FREE design services for any project
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn Design Services' second send of the day (following the Bloom Shop/florals email), and it's the right strategic instinct: after a relentless discount blitz, pivoting to a service offer — free design consultation — earns differentiation. The "RSVP" framing is charming and implies scarcity without being aggressive. The hero is clean, the CTA is one of the most specific in this audit cycle, and the Design Services sub-brand adds credibility.
- The execution stumbles. The email tries to do four things at once: book a design appointment, revive the George Kittle Cozy Cabin editorial from yesterday's 7/10 send, push a "Recommended Just for You" product grid, and surface the Pottery Barn Key Card. None of these sections reinforce each other. The Kittle reprise works as standalone content but feels like filler here; the product grid actively undermines the design-services pitch by shifting from "we'll help you choose" to "here, just buy these."
- The "FREE" caps in the subject line is the one avoidable mistake at the inbox level — it trips a soft spam-signal tone that clashes with the premium editorial register Pottery Barn has been earning all week.

## What's working

- "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" is a genuinely distinctive headline — stands out immediately against the UP TO 60% OFF caps-lock cadence of the past six-plus days.
- "BOOK DESIGN APPOINTMENT" is textbook CTA copy: specific verb, specific noun, no vague "Discover" or "Shop Now."
- Botanical/floral design flourish in the hero creates a quiet visual callback to the same-day Bloom Shop send — some inter-campaign coordination showing.
- The Design Services sub-brand identity is maintained cleanly; this doesn't feel like a generic mass email.
- Key Card rewards section at the bottom adds value for members without cluttering the top-fold pitch.

## What's weak

- Kitchen-sink structure (hero → Kittle editorial → product grid → card promo) splits attention four ways — there is no single story a reader can follow.
- "RSVP" implies scarcity or a deadline, but nothing in the visible body specifies when the offer expires; it reads as always-on, which defuses the urgency.
- The "Recommended Just for You" product grid appears to be generic category content, not a curated selection tied to a design consultation context — breaks the personalization promise of the sub-brand.
- The Kittle Cozy Cabin section, strong yesterday, functions as distraction here; it dilutes the design-appointment conversion path without earning its real estate.
- "FREE" all-caps in the subject line is the one unnecessary flag — it reads promotional rather than premium.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the body to one story.** Remove the Kittle reprise and the product grid entirely. Let the design services hero breathe: hero image → headline → two-sentence value prop → single CTA. Conversion emails that do one thing outperform multi-section emails in this category every time.
- 2. **Add a credible deadline.** "RSVP by June 20 — spots are limited" converts the implied scarcity into real urgency. Without a date, "RSVP" is just branding.
- 3. **Lowercase "free" in the subject.** "Free design services for any project" reads aspirational; "FREE design services" reads promotional.
- 4. **Replace the product grid with one before/after or room transformation.** Show what actually happens when someone books — that is the conversion proof, not a grid of sofas.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free design help for your next project — book a spot`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One-on-one design, no cost — RSVP before spots fill`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A Pottery Barn designer, your space, zero charge. Spots this week are open.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every project qualifies. Tell us what you're working on — we'll take it from there.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers in CSS block (malformed source):**
The second `<style>` block contains raw PHP `header()` calls:
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they landed verbatim in the HTML output. They are invalid CSS and will generate parser errors in some clients. This is a template/rendering pipeline bug.

**Missing alt text — 34 images:**
Virtually every content and footer image has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Affected images include all body slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_07.jpg`), all 18 footer slices, product images (bed, rug, sheets), and tracking pixels. Screen readers will skip all content; images-off rendering is completely blank with no fallback text.

**HTTP image sources — 2 images:**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Mixed content will cause these images to be blocked in Gmail (enforces HTTPS), iOS Mail, and most modern clients.

**`<link>` to Google Fonts:**
`<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap">` — Google Fonts is blocked in many corporate email environments and unsupported in Outlook. No fallback stack is defined for Montserrat in the CSS; the font will silently fall back to browser default rather than a declared web-safe alternative.
