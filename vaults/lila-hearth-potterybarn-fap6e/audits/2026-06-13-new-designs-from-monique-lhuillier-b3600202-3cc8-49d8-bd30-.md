# 🌼 NEW designs from Monique Lhuillier
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the most differentiated email in Pottery Barn's June 13 batch — and arguably the week. "New designs from Monique Lhuillier" breaks cleanly from the four-day "Up to 70% off" drumbeat that's dominated recent sends (the June 12 "NEW DEALS ADDED" scored 7/10; the June 13 "Up to 70% off 100s of NEW DEALS" scored 8/10). The Lhuillier editorial — lemon-yellow florals, garden table setting, soft outdoor light — is visually distinct and story-led. Subscribers who'd been tuning out another sale notice have a genuine reason to pause here.
- The execution stumbles by fusing two messages that fight each other: a premium designer collaboration launch and a "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" banner. Pottery Barn can't decide whether this is an elevated curatorial moment or another warehouse event, and the collision undercuts both. The Lhuillier story deserved a standalone send; appending the sale banner cheapens the luxury framing her name implies.
- Bottom line: a genuinely strong collaboration email that undersells itself by attaching sale mechanics it didn't need.

## What's working

- **Subject line is a clean break.** "🌼 NEW designs from Monique Lhuillier" is the first subject in several days not screaming percentages — it reads editorial, not promotional.
- **Hero visual is sharply distinct.** Lemon-yellow, garden florals, and soft outdoor dining light stand apart from Pottery Barn's usual neutral/farmhouse palette; it earns a second look mid-scroll.
- **Monique Lhuillier × Pottery Barn co-branding is legible.** The designer lockup is prominent and immediately scannable; subscribers know exactly what they're looking at.
- **"Recommended Just For You" rail adds click surface.** Even algorithmic personalization gives the email a reason to linger below the fold.
- **Clean render throughout.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks at this viewport.

## What's weak

- **Sale banner undercuts the collaboration story.** Slotting "Summer Sale Up to 70% off" onto a designer-launch hero dilutes both signals — the collaboration reads as clearance, the sale reads as an afterthought.
- **CTA is too generic.** "SHOP NOW" doesn't anchor to the collection; "Shop the Monique Lhuillier Collection" would tie the click to the story and improve attribution.
- **No urgency signal.** A timed offer ("Through June 16," "Limited availability") would lift conversions without cheapening the collaboration framing.
- **Fourth Pottery Barn email in roughly 48 hours.** Even strong creative gets suppressed by list fatigue at this cadence; open rates on this send are likely dragged down before anyone reads the subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the sale banner from the collaboration hero entirely.** Let the Lhuillier story breathe above the fold; move sale language to a secondary band below the product grid.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to name the collection.** "Shop the Monique Lhuillier Collection" outperforms "SHOP NOW" on click-through and brand storytelling.
- 3. **Add a credible deadline to any sale copy that remains.** "Through June 16" converts better than "Summer Sale" with no horizon.
- 4. **Hold this email one day.** Sending the fourth email in 48 hours suppresses open rates regardless of creative quality; this one deserved more breathing room.

- **Subject Alt B:** `She designed your dream table. Shop the collection.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Garden-party entertaining, elevated. New designs just landed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Lemon florals, soft linen, romantic outdoor dining — see the full collection.`
