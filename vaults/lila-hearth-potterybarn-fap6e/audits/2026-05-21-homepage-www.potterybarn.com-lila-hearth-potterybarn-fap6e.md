# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn looking for inspiration for my second home and the very first thing I see is a full-screen modal about golf and dads. Before I can see a single sofa, candle, or duvet, I'm being asked to hand over my email for a U.S. Open sweepstakes. This feels like a brand that knows exactly what it is — aspirational home — but completely fumbled the entry experience for me today.

## What's working

- **Timely campaign execution:** Father's Day is next month, the sweep ends 5/24 — the calendar is right. Pottery Barn is clearly not asleep at the wheel seasonally.
- **Modal visual quality:** The product styling — golf book, crystal glassware, bar accessories — is beautifully photographed and on-brand for PB's aesthetic register. If I were shopping for Dad, this would land.
- **Specific prize value:** "$4,000 grandstand seats + $500 to spend" is concrete and compelling. No vague "win a prize." I appreciate the specificity even if this isn't for me.
- **Cross-brand data disclosure:** The WSI/Williams-Sonoma callout in fine print is actually transparent — I'm a WS rewards member, so seeing that connection acknowledged (not hidden) feels like a brand I already trust.

## What's weak

- **Full-viewport modal with no homepage behind it:** I can't see a single piece of furniture, bedding, or decor before being asked for my email. For a returning customer like me, this is friction before discovery.
- **Completely off-persona targeting:** A Father's Day golf sweepstakes is male-skewed and gift-buyer-framed. I'm shopping for my own second home. There is zero relevance for my session intent.
- **No visible submit button:** The email field is cut off at the bottom of the viewport. If I wanted to enter, I can't tell what tapping does — or if there's even a button.
- **Crushing fine print density:** Four paragraphs of legal copy before I can see what Pottery Barn is selling. That's a trust friction spike, not a trust signal.
- **No loyalty hook:** I'm a Williams-Sonoma rewards member. Pottery Barn is the same family. There is no "Key Rewards member? You're already entered" or any acknowledgment that I'm a known, returning customer.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the full-viewport modal for logged-in / known rewards members** — I already have a Key Rewards account. Intercept me with a slim banner ("Enter the #1 Dad Sweep →") and let me see the homepage.
- **Add a persona-aware secondary CTA to the modal itself** — "Not shopping for Dad? Browse new arrivals for your home →" gives me an exit that lands somewhere useful instead of forcing a hard X-close.
- **Surface the Key Rewards connection in the fine print:** Change "Williams-Sonoma may send you news" to "As a Key Rewards member, you'll be auto-entered — just confirm your email." Turns legalese into a benefit.
- **Show the submit button in-viewport:** The email field is clipped. Raise the form or shorten the body copy so the CTA button is visible without any scroll on mobile.
