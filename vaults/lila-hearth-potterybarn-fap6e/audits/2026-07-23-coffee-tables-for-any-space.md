# Coffee tables for any space ☕️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a category-merchandising email built around coffee tables — a clean, functional send that reads more like a curated shop page than a promotional blast. The hero is a wide lifestyle shot of a warm, leather-sofa living room with a central coffee table, followed by a structured 3-column product grid beneath it. The offer is lightweight: there's a "Save Up to 30% Off" banner at the top and what appears to be a "Shop Now" CTA, but the discount is applied sitewide or category-wide rather than tied to a specific hero product.
- The email's biggest strength is editorial clarity — coffee tables by shape or style, organized so you can self-select. That's useful. The weakness is that the discount feels thin (30% off at a premium furniture brand lands like a shrug after a July packed with 70% summer sale blasts from this same sender). The "Recommended Just for You" personalization module below the fold is a smart structural move but the execution at thumbnail scale makes it hard to evaluate meaningfully.
- Overall this is a serviceable, mid-tier category email. Not broken, not exciting. It will convert furniture browsers but won't move anyone who wasn't already shopping.

## What's working

- **Hero image earns its space** — warm, aspirational living room shot puts the product in context immediately; visual hierarchy lands on the table first.
- **"Save Up to 30% Off" banner is present and visible** at the top — there's a concrete offer anchoring the open.
- **Category grid is navigable** — distinct product rows let a shopper self-select by shape/style without hunting.
- **Personalization module ("Recommended Just for You")** signals behavioral targeting; this is the right structural move to add relevance below the fold.
- **No render bugs visible** — layout is clean, images load, no text overlap or broken sections.

## What's weak

- **30% off feels stale** — Pottery Barn ran 70% off sale blasts through most of July (per brand history); a 30% max-discount offer positioned as the headline benefit is a step backward in urgency.
- **Coffee table subject with a coffee emoji reads cute but thin** — no offer, no urgency, no frame in the subject. Compare to the July 21st "Storage that clears the clutter" send (8/10) which at least named the benefit.
- **No deadline visible** — the 30% off has no time constraint in the visible render, which kills urgency entirely.
- **Product names/prices at grid scale are illegible** — the 3-column product tiles are too small to read in the render; CTAs appear to be generic "Shop Now" rather than product-specific.
- **Personalization module thumbnails are very small** — the "Recommended Just for You" images at this scale don't differentiate well; the module adds structural value but not visual pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the 30% offer** — "Ends Sunday" in the banner or subject transforms a passive discount into an action trigger. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to include the offer** — the emoji is friendly but it buries the lede. The discount is the news.
- 3. **Increase product tile size or cut to 2 columns** — at current dimensions the product names and prices are unreadable, which undermines the category grid's entire purpose.
- 4. **Give the hero coffee table a name and price** — anchor the lifestyle image to a specific, shoppable product instead of a generic "Shop Now."
- 5. **Surface a single star-rated bestseller** — one social-proof callout (e.g., "4.8 stars · 2,300+ reviews") in the grid would dramatically lift click confidence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 30% on coffee tables — this weekend only ☕`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The coffee table your room is missing (30% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Round, rectangular, or storage — shop every style at up to 30% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday. Find the coffee table that fits your space.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block — template rendering bug**
The following literal PHP code appears inside a `<style>` tag in the delivered HTML:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is not valid CSS. Some email clients will render this as visible text; all clients will fail to parse the surrounding style rules cleanly. This is a template pipeline defect — PHP was not executed server-side before the HTML was assembled.

**[WARN] 51 images missing `alt` text**
Includes all content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_17.jpg` across two sections), all footer slices, promotional banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`), product images, and tracking pixels. When images are blocked (default in Outlook, Gmail with images off), the email is entirely inaccessible to screen readers and image-blocked clients.

**[WARN] 3 images served over HTTP — likely blocked by modern clients**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail and Apple Mail proxy images through HTTPS. Plain HTTP sources are blocked or fail silently in Outlook desktop.

**[WARN] Open tracking pixel served over HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`

`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — WCAG 1.4.4 violation; pinch-zoom is suppressed for low-vision users.

**[INFO] Empty `<title>` tag**

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed**
Header absent from relay capture. If genuinely missing, this violates Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day senders) and reduces deliverability scoring.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed**
One-click unsubscribe via POST is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Cannot confirm presence through AgentMail relay; needs verification at the MTA level.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail unknown from available data. Sender domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ESPdomain); DMARC alignment should be verified against the `potterybarn.com` organizational domain.

**[WARN] `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`**
This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all link clicks. Landing pages will receive no referrer signal from the email, causing attribution to fall into direct/none in analytics. This is a CAN-SPAM neutral issue but a significant measurement defect (see Section 6).

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link presence in the footer cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML — must be verified in the full source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[WARN] `no-referrer` meta kills email attribution at the landing page**
Every click-through will appear as direct traffic in site analytics. If UTM parameters are present on destination URLs, those will still fire in GA4/Adobe — but any referrer-dependent attribution (server-side, or tools not reading UTMs) will be blind to email as the source.

**[WARN] Plain-text body is 72% raw URLs (10,652 / 14,803 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is machine-unreadable — a wall of tracking URLs with no human-readable content. Spam filters score heavily on plain-text URL density; this ratio is a material deliverability risk. It also means the email is completely unusable for recipients on plain-text-only clients.

**[INFO] Click tracking architecture**
All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects before reaching `potterybarn.com` destinations. UTM parameter carriage cannot be confirmed without probing the redirect chain on a live link.
