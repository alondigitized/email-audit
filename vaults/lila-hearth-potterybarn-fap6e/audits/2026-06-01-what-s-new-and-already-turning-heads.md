# What’s new—and already turning heads
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second email hitting inboxes on June 1 — the first was "American classics that never go out of style," which already scored 7/10 for a no-offer editorial send. This one doubles down on that editorial posture: a clean coastal-natural hero, a "New Designs" headline, and a long scroll of lifestyle product grids with no discount, no deadline, and no urgency mechanism of any kind. That's a defensible strategy for a brand with strong design authority — but not twice in one day.
- The production quality is high. The hero image (console/sofa table vignette, light naturals, plants) is genuinely appealing and on-trend for early summer. The multi-grid body gives the email real category breadth — bedroom, living room, storage — and the returning "Recommended Just For You" personalization module at least signals the brand is watching behavior. But "new designs that are turning heads" is not a reason to click. It's a reason to feel vaguely interested and keep scrolling your inbox.
- The subject line is the weakest link. "What's new—and already turning heads" is soft-launch hype copy: it promises social proof ("turning heads") without delivering any, and withholds the most compelling detail (what category? what product?). After five recent sends that ranged from heavy outdoor sales to free-shipping bedding offers, this reads as padding.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** The coastal-natural console vignette is well-shot, seasonally appropriate, and creates genuine shelf appeal above the fold.
- **Category breadth.** Bedroom, living room, and storage grids give this email something for every room — a valid new-arrivals showcase format.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module persists.** Pottery Barn keeps running this personalization block; it's one of their more differentiating signals in an otherwise generic-feeling promotional send.
- **Free Design Services callout.** A genuine service differentiator that competitors can't match; its inclusion lifts perceived brand value even without a sale.
- **Clean render.** No layout breaks, no broken images, no overlapping text at any visible scroll point.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** Second editorial send of the day, zero discount, no free shipping, no threshold — there's nothing to act on, only browse.
- **Subject is vague hype copy.** "Already turning heads" implies social proof but delivers zero — no product name, no category, no specific claim.
- **Second Pottery Barn email today is a cadence problem.** A subscriber who opened "American classics that never go out of style" this morning and now gets this has no new reason to engage. The brand history shows this pattern is becoming a habit (two emails/day on May 31 as well).
- **The hero doesn't land on a single product.** The lifestyle vignette is beautiful, but the CTA likely goes to a broad new arrivals page — browsing friction, not buying urgency.
- **"Turning heads" is unsubstantiated.** No reviews, no sell-through signals, no bestseller callouts — the social proof claim sits in the subject line and disappears entirely inside the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject specific.** "New designs" and "turning heads" are placeholders. Pick the one new arrival that's genuinely breakout — a named sofa, a specific rug — and lead with it. This is the single change that would lift open rates.
- 2. **Anchor the hero to one product.** Tie the lifestyle shot to a named piece (with a price) so clicking the hero image means something. Right now it's editorial wallpaper.
- 3. **Separate the two June 1 sends or consolidate them.** Two back-to-back editorial emails with no offer in one day trains subscribers to skim. Batch this content into a single "What's New" weekly digest or add a threshold offer ("Free shipping on new arrivals today only") to justify the separate deployment.
- 4. **Bring social proof inside the email.** If "turning heads" is in the subject, back it up with a bestseller badge, a star rating, or a "selling fast" signal on at least one product.
- 5. **Add one lightweight urgency mechanism.** Even "just landed" with an implied limited-quantity window does more than an open-ended browse invitation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals: [Product Name] and more just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This season's standout pieces — now in stock`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh designs for every room, with Free Design Services to help you choose.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From the living room to the bedroom — see what just arrived.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (17 confirmed)** — The entire `S26_Seasonal_B1_NewArrivals` slice set (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) is served over plain HTTP:
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/S26_Seasonal_B1_NewArrivals/slices_*.jpg
```
Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook (with image blocking) will refuse or warn on these. The same domain exists on HTTPS — this is a template generation error, not a CDN gap.

Additional HTTP offenders:
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

**Missing alt text — 40+ images** — Nearly all content images carry no `alt` attribute. Confirmed missing on the entire new-arrivals slice set, all footer slices, all product images (beds, towels, rod brackets), and promotional banners (`S26_Bottom_UpTo50OffOutdoor.jpg`, `W26_Bottom_GeneralDCBottomBanner.jpg`). Images-off rendering is completely blank.

**PHP headers embedded in CSS `<style>` block** — The following is injected verbatim into a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were never executed server-side and leaked into the HTML output. They are inert in email but indicate a template rendering defect.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` leaves the email with no document title, which affects tab display in webmail clients.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — One-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo have required this since February 2024 for bulk senders (>5K/day).
