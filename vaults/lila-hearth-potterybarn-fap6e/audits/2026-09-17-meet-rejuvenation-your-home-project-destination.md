# Meet Rejuvenation: Your home project destination
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This isn't a discount email — it's a brand-introduction play. "Introducing REJUVENATION" positions it as Pottery Barn's new sister brand for "full renovations... lighting, solid brass hardware, handcrafted designs," and the whole send is a scroll-heavy category tour (Entryway → Lighting → Hardware → More for Your Entryway) rather than a promo. It's well-composed and moody — dark wood, brass accents, warm lighting all photograph well — but there's zero price, zero urgency, and zero reason to act today.
- Compared to the brand's last six sends, this is the least commerce-forward of the run, even less so than the Halloween or Design Services emails, both of which at least had a clear single CTA. Here there are seven separate "Shop" buttons stacked across one long email, which dilutes rather than focuses intent. It reads more like a lookbook / brand-awareness push than something built to convert a subscriber checking email on their commute.
- Judgement: gorgeous but toothless — good for brand halo, weak for near-term clicks or revenue.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent visual identity — the dark wood/brass palette is well art-directed and looks premium.
- Clear "NEW ARRIVAL" flag on the Culver Sideboard gives one concrete, specific product to anchor on.
- Section structure (Entryway → Lighting → Hardware → More) mirrors a real room-by-room shopping journey, which suits a renovation-minded persona.
- No render bugs — every image loads cleanly, text is legible, no overlap.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing time-bound; this is pure top-of-funnel content in a promo-heavy inbox.
- Too many competing CTAs (Shop Now, Shop Lighting, Shop Hardware, Get Inspired, Sign Up for Updates) — no single hero action.
- "Rejuvenation" is a new, unfamiliar brand name buried in small caps under "INTRODUCING" — easy to skim past if you don't already know it.
- Subject promises "your home project destination" but the email is really an entryway/hardware showcase, not a project-planning tool — the framing overpromises scope.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a reason to click now — even a soft one ("New at Rejuvenation" is fine, but pair it with an intro offer like "15% off your first Rejuvenation order" to convert curiosity into action).
- 2. Cut CTA count from seven to two or three — one hero CTA up top, one at the bottom; the mid-section "Shop Lighting/Hardware" buttons are redundant with the visible product links.
- 3. Make the sideboard the hero, not a mid-scroll aside — it's the only named, priced-adjacent product; move it above the fold.
- 4. Clarify the subject/body relationship — either deliver more "project destination" substance (bundles, room guides) or retitle to match what's actually here (a hardware/lighting intro).

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC-generated Pottery Barn promotional email; core structure is sound, but the QA pass surfaces gaps in unsubscribe/authentication header capture, near-universal missing alt text, and two lingering legacy HTTP image assets.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 44 tracking/click-redirect links (`click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`) were skipped by the automated HTTP probe by design — no pass/fail evidence either way, flagging for manual click-through verification.
- Two third-party tracking pixels present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=...` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1`. Both load over HTTPS; no rendering issue found, but confirm both are intentional/current partners.
- Open/view tracking pixel `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` passes the recipient email address in cleartext as a URL query parameter to a tracking endpoint — evidence for the Compliance/PII section below.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 34 of 34 content/tracking `<img>` tags in the QA output are missing `alt` text (slices_01–18 in the hero body, slices_01–18 in the footer, plus the coreemail.html and tracking-pixel images). Screen readers and images-blocked clients will render this email as entirely blank content.
- Legacy VML/Outlook conditional comments, `mso-*` properties, and `-ms-text-size-adjust`/`-webkit-text-size-adjust` resets are present and correctly scoped — no issues found there.
- The `<style>` block in `<head>` contains stray PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, `header("Cache-Control: ...")`, `header("Pragma: no-cache")`) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag as invalid, non-functional syntax — dead code left over from a copy-paste of server-side caching headers; browsers/clients will simply ignore it, but it should be stripped since it does nothing and signals template hygiene issues.
- Preheader hidden text block uses standard `display:none !important; mso-hide:all` plus zero-width-joiner padding — correctly implemented, no issues found.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not found — cannot confirm authentication status from this capture.
- The `sv_sveme` open-tracking pixel embeds the recipient's raw email address in a plaintext URL parameter sent to `svopn.potterybarn.com`; combined with the `coreemail.html?email=...` link, this is a data-handling pattern worth flagging even though it's standard SFMC behavior, since it exposes PII in server logs of any intermediary that touches these URLs.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query parameters are present on any link; all outbound links use SFMC's opaque `qs=` tracking token instead (`click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABB7...`).
