# UP TO 70% OFF new sale styles + New arrivals with classic farmhouse style
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This email is doing two jobs at once, and neither one benefits from the company. The hero leans into a "New Arrivals / classic farmhouse style" editorial story — airy room shots, natural light, soft greens and neutrals. But the subject line leads with the sale ("UP TO 70% OFF"), and the body never fully commits to either lane. The result is a reader who has to figure out what Pottery Barn actually wants them to do: browse fresh inventory or shop clearance?
- Against the brand history, this is the second same-day send on June 12 — the earlier "NEW DEALS ADDED! Up to 70% off" email (7/10) already covered the sale angle. This one adds "new arrivals" as a second message but doesn't create enough separation to feel like a distinct reason to engage. A subscriber who already deleted the first June 12 email has even less reason to open this one.
- The execution is clean — no render bugs, a solid category grid, and the "Recommended Just For You" product rail adds some personalization signal — but the strategic split between sale and editorial sinks what could have been a strong standalone new-arrivals send.

## What's working

- **Visual creative is strong.** The hero image is bright, aspirational, and seasonally appropriate — exactly the farmhouse aesthetic the subject promises.
- **Category grid is well-composed.** Bedroom, dining, living room, and outdoor scenes give quick navigational paths for different buyer intents.
- **Pottery Barn brand recognition is strong.** The sender and logo are instantly trustworthy for this persona.
- **"Recommended Just For You" rail** adds a personalization layer that most same-day sale emails skip.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout top to bottom — images load, text is legible, no overlap or truncation visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is doing two things and doing neither well.** Mixing "UP TO 70% OFF" with "New arrivals with classic farmhouse style" in 73 characters dilutes both messages and blows past mobile truncation (~40 chars) well before "farmhouse" appears.
- **This is the second Pottery Barn send on June 12.** Subscriber fatigue is real; a reader who already processed "NEW DEALS ADDED! Up to 70% off" this morning will treat this as a duplicate.
- **Discount anchoring undermines the new-arrivals story.** Leading with 70% off trains the eye to look for sale prices, which conflicts with the full-price new-arrivals framing in the hero.
- **No deadline for the sale offer.** "UP TO 70% OFF" without an end date removes urgency — it reads like a permanent sale banner.
- **CTAs are generic.** At this resolution the buttons read as "Shop Now" or similar — no category-specific verbs to route different intents from the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the send.** New arrivals and warehouse sale are two different purchase mindsets. Send them separately; don't make subscribers parse a compound message.
- 2. **If you must combine: make the new arrivals the headline, let the sale be the hook.** "New farmhouse arrivals — and yes, up to 70% off sale styles" is a better story than the reverse.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the sale callout.** Even "through Sunday" doubles urgency without adding words.
- 4. **Replace generic "Shop Now" CTAs with category-specific verbs** — "Shop Bedroom," "Shop Dining" — to route intent from the grid.
- 5. **Surface one hero product** rather than going straight to the grid; give subscribers a single thing to want before presenting the full catalog.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New farmhouse arrivals — plus up to 70% off sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just arrived: Classic farmhouse styles (+ sale ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh living room, bedroom & dining pieces with the look you love.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New in: Woven textures, warm neutrals, and farmhouse-inspired finds.`
