# EXTRA 20% OFF 📣 The Memorial Day Sale starts today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's Memorial Day sale launch email — "Extra 20% off select styles, plus new deals up to 60% off" — and the creative is clean and purposeful. The hero lands the offer instantly, the category grid gives you an immediate entry point, and the "Recommended Just for You" personalization block below the fold is genuinely well-placed. Pottery Barn executes this template reliably.
- The problem isn't what's in the email — it's that this is the **second Memorial Day sale-launch email from Pottery Barn today**. The May 15 brand history already shows "Our Memorial Day Sale STARTS NOW 🔔" (8/10), which appears to be the same creative with a swapped subject line. Sending two nearly identical full promotional emails on the same day is a cadence failure regardless of how well-designed each individual send is. A subscriber who opened the first one has no new reason to act; a subscriber who didn't open the first one is unlikely to be won over by a minor subject-line reframe.
- The offer itself is solid and seasonally right. But the duplicate-send pattern erodes trust and inbox presence in ways that compound over time.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate.** "Extra 20% off" reads at a glance; the red accent on the sale badge directs the eye correctly.
- **Category grid is genuinely useful.** Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Decor, etc. give a subscriber a direct path to their category without hunting — smart for a broad sale.
- **"Recommended Just for You" block.** Real product thumbnails with names and prices, personalized — this is above-average execution for a PB email.
- **Key Rewards callout.** The loyalty section at the bottom adds a stacking incentive for members and is cleanly presented.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, layout holds, no broken blocks or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **Duplicate same-day send.** This email and "Our Memorial Day Sale STARTS NOW 🔔" are the same creative dressed up with a different subject. Two near-identical promos in one inbox session reads as operational slippage, not a coordinated campaign.
- **"Select styles" qualifier is fine-printed.** The hero says "Extra 20% off" in large type; the exclusion is in the smallest text directly beneath. The visual hierarchy undersells the asterisk.
- **No end date visible.** "STARTS NOW" implies urgency but no deadline is shown — "starts today" without "ends Sunday" leaves the offer feeling open-ended and reduces urgency.
- **CTA goes to a sale landing page, not a curated editorial.** After seeing personalized recommendations below the fold, the primary CTA drops you into an undifferentiated sale grid — the personalization promise isn't kept at the click level.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the two sends into one.** If this was meant as a second-wave email to non-openers, suppress openers in the audience and change the subject meaningfully — don't re-blast the same list with the same creative. This is the highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Add a hard end date to the hero.** "Extra 20% off — through Monday" would dramatically lift urgency without changing the rest of the layout.
- 3. **Surface the "select styles" exclusion honestly.** Move it to slightly larger text or reframe the hero as "Extra 20% off select styles" to set expectations before the click.
- 4. **Wire the personalized CTA to the personalized products.** The "Shop Now" CTA under the recommended block should deep-link to those product pages, not back to the homepage.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off — Memorial Day Sale ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your personalized picks: extra 20% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Select furniture, outdoor, bedding + more — plus deals up to 60% off through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop your recommended styles at an extra 20% off before it ends.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Memorial Day Sale" (2026-05-15)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (HTTP 403) will silently fail for recipients clicking the credit card CTA and loading a West Elm cross-brand asset. Accessibility posture is poor: 43 images lack alt text and three asset URLs use unencrypted HTTP, which modern email clients will block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card / loyalty CTA returns 403**
The "Apply and Buy" banner link resolves to a 403:
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&...`
Any recipient clicking the credit card join CTA hits an error page. This is the highest-priority break.

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm domain asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send. The 403 is likely a cross-brand permission issue; additionally it is HTTP (see §3).

**INFO — 24 click-redirect links not probed**
All `click.e.potterybarn.com` links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination validity is unverified for those targets.

**WARN — Open-tracking pixel is HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` — unencrypted open pixel; Gmail and Outlook will proxy/block it, making open-rate data unreliable.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
The following lines appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag, not as a comment:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP server-side directives that leaked into the template. They are invalid CSS and will be emitted as parse errors by all email clients. Non-breaking, but a template hygiene failure indicating the PHP layer wrote directly into the HTML rather than stripping server headers before render.

**43 images missing `alt` text**
Every product image in the `S26_Sale_MemorialDay_GraphicOverLinkFarm/` slice set, all footer images, and several banner images have no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, corporate proxies), the email renders as a blank sequence of broken boxes with no textual fallback.

**3 image assets served over HTTP**
Email clients with forced HTTPS (Outlook.com, Gmail) will block or substitute these:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — screen readers announce "untitled document" when the email is opened in a browser or forwarded.

**`no-referrer` meta tag suppresses analytics**
`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` strips the `Referer` header on all click-throughs. Any GA4 / Adobe Analytics session that relies on referrer attribution will show as direct traffic, degrading campaign reporting.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Header not captured by the AgentMail relay. If it is genuinely missing from the SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo's February 2024 bulk-sender requirement and CAN-SPAM best practice. Must be confirmed at SMTP level, not just HTML inspection.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail for >5k/day senders) is unconfirmed. Absence increases spam complaint rate and risks Gmail throttling.

**WARN — Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via the relay. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should show DKIM alignment; this should be verified at the MTA.

**WARN — Plain-text version is URL-heavy**
9,742 of 13,866 plain-text characters (70.3%) are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO` rule) flag this pattern; it degrades deliverability on plain-text-first evaluation paths.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Primary CTA is broken (403)** — the credit card apply link never reaches the landing page; no UTM data is collected for that conversion path.

**`no-referrer` meta breaks click attribution** (also noted in §3) — GA4 will attribute click-throughs as `(direct)` rather than `email`, corrupting channel-level reporting for this campaign.

**24 tracked links unverified** — destination UTM consistency (e.g., `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) on `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect targets could not be confirmed. Manual spot-check recommended for the primary sale CTA and product deep-links.

**West Elm domain asset** — `edm.westelm.com` in a Pottery Barn send is a cross-brand infrastructure leak; landing page if any would resolve to a West Elm domain, breaking brand continuity.
