# Just in! Our new digital catalog
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's least differentiated send in the recent run — it's not selling a room, a sale, or an event, it's selling the idea of a catalog. Compare it to 2026-08-03's "Get the look: traditional dining room" (a tight single-concept walkthrough) or 2026-08-04's "Just in! 1600+ new arrivals" (broad but at least anchored to volume) — this one goes a layer more abstract, asking the recipient to get excited about a PDF/digital-flip-through rather than a product or discount.
- The execution itself is clean: hero image, clear category navigation grid, a "Recommended Just For You" product rail, and the now-standard Halloween/Design Crew/App/Rewards stack at the bottom. Nothing is broken, nothing overlaps, the brand voice is consistent. But there's no offer anywhere near the top of the funnel — you have to scroll past the entire hero and category grid before hitting anything resembling value (the 10% rewards card, buried near the footer).
- Judgement: solid production values, weak strategic hook. This reads like a "we need a send today" email rather than a "here's why you should stop scrolling" email — fine as a low-stakes touchpoint in a high-frequency cadence, but it won't move a fence-sitter to click.

## What's working

- Hero section is unambiguous — "THE FALL CATALOG" headline, supporting line, one clear "GET INSPIRED" button, no competing CTAs.
- The category grid (Pillows & Decor, Armchairs, Bedding, etc.) gives multiple low-friction entry points for different shoppers in one scroll.
- "Recommended Just For You" product rail shows real, distinct products (curtain rod, platform bed, towel, duvet cover, upholstered bed, linen curtain) with clean square imagery — no lorem ipsum or placeholder gaps.
- Loyalty messaging ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is present and specific to the credit card program, which will register with existing cardholders.
- Visual system is consistent brand-to-brand with prior sends — same typography, same warm neutral palette, same footer structure — so a regular subscriber will recognize it instantly.

## What's weak

- There is no discount, sale, or promo anywhere in the primary path — the whole premise is "look at our catalog," which is the weakest possible hook in a stretch of emails that includes actual sales and sweepstakes.
- "GET INSPIRED" is a vague CTA verb pairing — it doesn't tell you what you'll get (a lookbook? a PDF? a landing page?) the way "Get the Look" or "Shop the Look" does in other recent sends.
- The Halloween Shop banner ("Enter if you dare") competing for attention mid-email, right after a "Fall Catalog" hero, muddies the seasonal narrative — is this a fall catalog email or a Halloween email?
- Product tiles in the "Recommended" rail show name only — no price, no rating, no "shop now" per item — so there's no reason to click one over another.
- Given this brand's cadence (a near-daily email, per brand history), a content-free "catalog announcement" is the kind of send most likely to get skipped or unsubscribed from.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the abstract "catalog" framing with a concrete hook — lead with one specific offer or one specific room concept (the way 08-03's dining room send did) rather than "browse our new catalog."
- 2. Move the loyalty/rewards line up near the hero, not buried after Halloween and Design Crew — "10% back" is the single most concrete value prop in this whole email and it's currently the last thing anyone will scroll to.
- 3. Give "Recommended Just For You" actual merchandising — price or "starting at $X" under each product name, so the rail does more than caption thumbnails.
- 4. Split the Halloween Shop into its own send or move it out of a "Fall Catalog" email — the two seasonal beats are cannibalizing each other's attention.
- 5. Sharpen the CTA copy — swap "GET INSPIRED" for something that names the deliverable, e.g. "Browse the Catalog."
- **Subject Alt A:** `See what's new in our Fall catalog`
- **Subject Alt B:** `1600+ new fall arrivals, just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your first look at fall — shop the new catalog`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New pillows, bedding, and furniture inside`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "Just in! Our new digital catalog"

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) transactional template (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `image.e.potterybarn.com`) with heavy MSO/Outlook conditional markup; core structural issues are missing `alt` text across nearly all images and several images/pixels served over plain HTTP.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All visible click-through links route through the SFMC click-tracking domain `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (opaque base64/encoded payload, not human-readable UTM params) — expected behavior for this ESP, not a defect.
- Secondary click tracker present: `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/005a6371-...?mi_mid=019fd3b9-...` (Marigold/Selligent redirect ID).
- Open-tracking pixel `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is served over **HTTP, not HTTPS** — may be blocked by mail clients that enforce mixed-content/TLS policies on remote images.
- Additional third-party pixel: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Amobee/Turn ad-tracking pixel) — confirm this vendor is an approved data-sharing partner.
- QA skipped HTTP probing of 57 tracking/redirect links by design (expected for click-redirect domains); link destinations were not independently validated for 404s/dead links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` text**: QA flagged ~45 `<img>` tags with no `alt` attribute, spanning hero banners, product slices, and footer imagery (e.g., `F26_Top_NewArrivals.jpg`, `slices_01–18.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`). Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled/filename-only, and content is entirely unreadable if images are blocked.
- **Non-HTTPS image sources** (mixed content, likely to be flagged or blocked by mail clients that force TLS):
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/...` (open pixel, noted above)
- Dead/inert code found in `<style>` block: literal PHP `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...");` statements are embedded as plain text inside a CSS `<style>` tag. This has no functional effect (CSS parsers ignore it) but is leftover server-side code accidentally pasted into the template — harmless but should be cleaned up.
- `<title></title>` is empty — some mail clients/inbox previews fall back to sender name, but an empty title is best avoided.
- Layout uses table-based MSO conditional structure with standard Outlook fixes (`mso-table-lspace`, VML/office XML namespaces) — no structural rendering defects observed in the provided markup.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient identifier is resolved (not left as an unrendered merge tag): `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` appears correctly substituted in the open-tracking pixel (`sv_sveme=`) and in the customer-service link (`?email=`). No broken/unresolved `%%merge%%` or `{{token}}` artifacts found in the visible source.
- No visible on-page personalization (e.g., first-name greeting) in the truncated section reviewed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- QA could not locate a `List-Unsubscribe` header or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058) on the captured message. This may reflect the AgentMail relay not preserving these headers rather than the sender omitting them — **cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe support either way from available data**.
- `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) was not present in the captured headers — SPF/DKIM alignment status is **unknown**, not confirmed-failing.
- The footer region (which would normally contain the unsubscribe link and physical mailing address required by CAN-SPAM) is outside the truncated HTML provided and could not be evaluated.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — standard for transactional email, not a compliance concern.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Links do not carry conventional `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` query parameters; instead they rely on SFMC's opaque `qs=` encoded tracking payload. This is normal for this ESP but means UTM-based attribution in an external analytics tool (e.g., GA4) depends on SFMC injecting UTM values downstream after redirect — **not independently verifiable from the raw HTML**.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` will strip the `Referer` header on outbound clicks, which can prevent the landing site from seeing the email as a referrer if it doesn't otherwise rely on the click-tracking redirect chain for attribution.
- Customer-service link resolves to `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — domain matches sender's primary site, consistent landing domain.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text (or `alt=""` for purely decorative slices/spacers) to all ~45 flagged images — highest-impact accessibility fix.
2. Migrate the remaining HTTP-only assets (`edm.westelm.com/spacer_0.gif`, `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`, `svopn.potterybarn.com` pixel) to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
3. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` lines embedded in the `<style>` block — dead code with no function, likely a copy-paste artifact from template assembly.
4. Verify with the ESP/relay whether `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` headers are genuinely absent on the wire or simply dropped by the AgentMail capture layer — confirm before treating as a real deliverability risk.
5. If UTM-based attribution is required downstream, confirm SFMC's redirect chain appends `utm_*` params before landing, since none are visible in the initial `qs=` link.
