# RSVP for your FREE design appt.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the "Free Design Services" service-offer send Pottery Barn has now run twice — the first went out on 2026-09-01, and this is a near-identical repeat two days later. It's still a genuine change of pace from the burgundy "Up to 70% off" hammer that's dominated the brand's Labor Day cadence (five straight discount sends before this), and the light, editorial hero (soft cream background, serif "labor day sale" eyebrow, clean "RSVP for Free Design Services" headline with a what/when/where mini-structure) reads more premium than the discount banners. But it immediately undercuts itself by stacking a full discount grid ("EXPLORE LABOR DAY DEALS," up to 60-70% off four categories) right below the design-services hero, plus a second burgundy "Floor Model Sale" banner further down — so the "we're different this time" pitch collapses into the same deals email with a service CTA bolted on top.
- Two sends of the same design-services creative in three days, with the deals content essentially unchanged from the 09-01 version, means this isn't differentiating from the rest of the campaign so much as repeating itself. Subscribers who opened the 09-01 send will find little reason to open or act on this one.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered hero with a clear what/when/where structure that makes the RSVP offer easy to scan.
- Visual break from the burgundy discount banners gives the top of the email a more premium, editorial feel.
- "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a specific, unambiguous CTA button with good contrast.
- Product grid images render cleanly with no broken images or layout bugs.
- The in-store "Styling Your Living Room" design class adds a second, concrete, time-bound event (Sept 13, 10AM).

## What's weak

- The free-design hero is immediately followed by a full discount grid and a second burgundy sale banner, diluting the "this is a service email" premise.
- This is a repeat of the 09-01 "FREE design services" creative with no visible new offer or copy — a subscriber who saw that one has no reason to engage with this one.
- Subject promises a "FREE design appt." but the email spends more visual real estate on percentage-off deals than on design services.
- "Recommended Just For You" personalization block is generic furniture/bedding, not tied to design services or Labor Day urgency.
- No specific deadline for the RSVP itself — "spots go fast" is vague urgency with no date.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the discount grid and Floor Model Sale banner entirely from this send — if it's the design-services email, keep it focused on design services (hero + in-store class + maybe past project photos), and let the discount emails carry the deals messaging. Mixing both waters down the value prop that's differentiating this from the rest of the cadence.
- 2. Since this is a repeat of 09-01's creative, either skip re-sending it to the same list or add a real reason to reopen — a specific slot count ("12 spots left this week") or a deadline for the design appointment RSVP.
- 3. Move the in-store "Styling Your Living Room" class up near the hero — it's the most concrete, dated offer in the email and currently gets buried below the deals grid.
- 4. Tie "Recommended Just For You" to the design-services theme (e.g., "styles our designers are using this fall") rather than a generic product grid identical to what a discount email would show.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
This is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) email with heavy tracking/pixel usage and multiple broken artifacts left in the HTML — a stray `ampScript` slot comment, PHP `header()` calls incorrectly embedded inside a `<style>` block, and legacy/mixed-protocol image assets from older campaigns.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 51 links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` tracking redirects (SFMC `qs=` encoded tokens); these were skipped by automated HTTP probing per policy and were not individually validated.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Turn/Amobee), and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (open-tracking pixel). The `sv_sveme` param exposes the recipient's raw email address in a plaintext GET query string, which is sent to a third-party-style subdomain on every open.
- A malformed/orphaned code block appears directly inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
  ```
  <style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
  ```
  This is raw PHP `header()` code (not CSS) leaked into the stylesheet — it will be ignored by mail clients as invalid CSS but indicates a template-assembly/build error upstream (likely a PHP image-serving snippet accidentally concatenated into the CSS block instead of being stripped).
- An empty `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot --> <!-- end dynamic ampScript slot -->` comment pair precedes the `<!DOCTYPE>` — placeholder markup that should have been populated or removed before send.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 34 `<img>` tags are missing `alt` text (QA-flagged), including all primary content slices (`slices_01.jpg`–`slices_09.jpg` in the hero/body, and `slices_01`–`slices_18.jpg` in the footer), product images (curtain rod bracket, rug, chair, platform beds, towel), and tracking/spacer pixels. For a slice-based image email, blocked-image and screen-reader fallback will render with no accessible text.
- Two images use `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`. Mixed-content/non-HTTPS images are commonly blocked or flagged by mail clients (Gmail/Outlook proxy over HTTPS regardless, but the source asset itself is unencrypted in transit from origin).
- Doctype is XHTML 1.0 Strict with `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and standard Outlook/`.ExternalClass`/`.ReadMsgBody` resets present — consistent with standard ESP boilerplate, no issues there.
- Mobile media queries target both `max-width: 480px` (table/img selectors) and `max-width: 650px` (`.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show`), which is redundant/overlapping breakpoint logic but not a functional bug on its own.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge/AMPscript tokens (e.g., `%%...%%` or `[[...]]`) are visible in the truncated source.
- The empty ampScript slot comment noted above suggests a personalization block was expected but not rendered — worth confirming server-side that this wasn't meant to inject dynamic content.
- Recipient email is passed in plaintext in two places: `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and the `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` open-pixel param — standard SFMC merge-field usage for personalized links but flagged since PII is exposed in unencrypted query strings/referrer headers.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found in captured headers (QA WARN) — may be a relay-capture limitation rather than absence in the original send; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM one-click compliance from available data.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found — same caveat; one-click unsubscribe support unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture.
- No unsubscribe link or physical-address footer visible in the truncated HTML provided; full footer content wasn't included in the excerpt, so CAN-SPAM footer compliance can't be assessed either way from this source.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Primary CTA/hero links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` opaque encoded tracking tokens rather than transparent UTM parameters — destination and UTM alignment can't be verified without following the redirect chain (skipped by QA probe per policy).
- The personalized service link (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) points to a standard non-tracked PB domain, consistent with brand domain continuity.

7. Recommendations
- Remove the leaked PHP `header()` snippet from the `<head><style>` block — investigate the template pipeline step that concatenated it in.
- Remove or populate the empty `ampScript` slot comment.
- Add descriptive (or explicitly empty `alt=""` for purely decorative) `alt` attributes to all 34 flagged images, particularly the content slice images which carry the offer messaging.
- Upgrade the two `http://` image sources to `https://`.
- Confirm with the ESP/relay whether `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) are actually present on the raw send — current absence may be a capture artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than a true compliance gap.
- Consider whether exposing the recipient's raw email address in plaintext tracking-pixel and link query parameters (`sv_sveme`, `email=`) meets current data-handling expectations, since it travels over GET requests to multiple subdomains/third parties.
