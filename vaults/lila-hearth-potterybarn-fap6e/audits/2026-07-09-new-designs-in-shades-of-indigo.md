# New designs in shades of indigo 💙
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean editorial new-arrivals send organized entirely around the indigo/blue color story — "INDIGO BLUES" as the hero headline, followed by three category modules (Fabric Bedding, Relaxed Seating, Tabletop), a personalized recommendations block, and a Summer Sale banner buried at the bottom. The visual execution is tight: rich lifestyle photography, consistent color palette, no obvious render bugs.
- The problem is timing and redundancy. The July 8 send — "New arrivals inspired by heritage design" (scored 8/10 here) — already played this exact card. Same aesthetic, same indigo palette, same new-arrivals positioning. Sending a near-identical editorial the very next day dilutes both. A subscriber who didn't click yesterday has no new reason to click today: no new product revelation, no pricing shift, no seasonal hook beyond "still indigo."
- The buried Summer Sale (up to 70% off) is the most action-driving element in the email and it's in a secondary banner below the fold. That's a strategic misfire. If the discount is live, lead with it. If this is purely editorial, commit fully and don't muddy it with a discount tack-on.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is immediate and legible** — "INDIGO BLUES" lands clearly over the lifestyle image; no ambiguity about the color story.
- **Three-category module structure** is well-organized — Fabric Bedding / Relaxed Seating / Tabletop gives the reader natural entry points depending on what they need.
- **"Recommended Just For You" block** adds a personalization signal that most editorial emails skip entirely.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no broken gaps or overlapping text visible.
- **"SHOP ALL NEW ARRIVALS" button** in brand navy is well-placed as a catch-all CTA after the category grid.

## What's weak

- **Thematic repeat from yesterday** — the July 8 "New arrivals inspired by heritage design" email already told the indigo new-arrivals story; today's send has no differentiated angle to justify another open.
- **Summer Sale buried below the fold** — "Up to 70% off" is real urgency; putting it in a secondary banner after all the editorial content means most mobile readers never see it.
- **No deadline on the sale** — the Summer Sale CTA has no expiry date visible, making "up to 70% off" feel abstract rather than urgent.
- **Subhead copy under the hero is unreadable at mobile scale** — whatever the editorial tagline says below "INDIGO BLUES" gets lost; no reinforcement of the value proposition at the top.
- **Category CTAs are generic** — each module's "SHOP NOW" is interchangeable; none are specific enough to create distinct click intent per category.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the sale if it's live, or cut it entirely** — the editorial and the discount undermine each other. Either restructure this as "Indigo Blues + Summer Sale: up to 70% off these new arrivals" (hero = offer + category) or strip the sale banner and commit to pure editorial. Hybrid emails that don't commit to either purpose consistently underperform both.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the Summer Sale** — "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 13" would turn a passive browse invite into a time-sensitive click. Without a date, 70% off reads like a standing evergreen claim.
- 3. **Differentiate from yesterday's email** — the July 8 send already did indigo new arrivals. Today's angle needs to be sharper: a specific new product, a room-by-room styling story, a "complete the look" bundle, or a first-look at something not in the previous send.
- 4. **Make category CTAs specific** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop Indigo Bedding," "Shop Rattan Seating," "Shop Blue & White Tabletop" — one extra word each, meaningfully higher intent signal.
- 5. **Surface the hero product detail** — the tabletop blue-and-white dinnerware is the most visually distinctive item in this email; a price anchor ("from $XX") or a product name there would give a concrete reason to click.

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Indigo Blues" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken destination links (403) and a PHP template artifact embedded in a `<style>` block constitute the most critical issues. Accessibility coverage is severely degraded with 30+ images carrying no alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken links (HTTP 403 — confirmed failures):**

- **Credit card banner link** (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407...`): The KeyCC_Banner component's primary CTA returns 403. Affects `external_id=PB_header_nonmember` segment. Any click on the credit card apply banner is a dead end.
- **West Elm cross-brand spacer** (`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`): 403 on a tracking/spacer GIF from a sister brand. Indicates a stale cross-brand asset inclusion. Also HTTP (see below).

**Mixed-content / HTTP image sources (3 confirmed):**

| Asset | Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

Gmail and Outlook proxy/block non-HTTPS images; these assets will silently fail for a significant portion of recipients.

**Tracking links:** 25 click-redirect URLs via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped from probing — destination integrity unverified by QA tooling.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code in `<style>` block — template rendering error:**

```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```

PHP `header()` calls were not executed server-side before send. Some email clients will interpret or surface this as visible text. This is a template compilation failure — the PHP block was included verbatim instead of being processed.

**Alt text — 30 images with empty `alt` attribute:**

All content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`), all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), and all product images (`farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`, etc.) are missing alt text. With images blocked (default in Outlook desktop), the email renders as a blank white column.

**Google Fonts external load:**

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```

Outlook (desktop) blocks external CSS entirely. The `Montserrat` font silently falls back to the declared `verdana,arial` stack — this is expected behavior but worth noting if font rendering consistency was tested only in webmail.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents user zoom on mobile — an accessibility barrier flagged by WCAG 1.4.4.

### 5. Compliance

The `List-Unsubscribe-Post` absence is a material gap: Gmail's bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) mandate one-click unsubscribe for senders at this volume. If the header is genuinely missing (not just dropped by the relay), this is a deliverability risk with Google.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but the footer structure (implied by `Email-Footer` slice naming) typically carries these; not flagged as confirmed missing.
