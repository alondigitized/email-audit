# Just in! NEW DEALS UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the exact same burgundy "Up to 70% off" Labor Day banner Pottery Barn has now run four days straight — same "100s of new deals added!" line, same 8-tile category grid, same "Shop All Sale" button. The subject "Just in! NEW DEALS UP TO 70% OFF" tries to manufacture freshness with "Just in!" but the creative underneath it is identical to 08-31's "It's ON 🎉" and "100s of NEW Labor Day deals" sends — there is nothing new here beyond the subject line wording.
- Below the fold it does open up a bit: a "Recommended Just For You" grid gives real personalization signal, and the Halloween Shop teaser is a genuinely different, seasonally-relevant beat that breaks the sale monotony. But by the time a reader scrolls that far, four straight days of the same maroon banner have already trained them to skip.
- Judgement: functional, on-brand, zero-risk execution — but this is peak promo fatigue. The sale content is stale even if the subject line pretends otherwise.

## What's working

- Clean, legible hero with unambiguous CTA ("SHOP NOW") and a specific offer ("Up to 70% off").
- Personalized "Recommended Just For You" product grid with real product names, prices implied, and lifestyle photography.
- The Halloween Shop section is a distinct, well-executed seasonal hook that stands out from the sale noise.
- No render bugs — images load, text is crisp, layout holds together top to bottom.
- Footer includes app download, credit card rewards (10% back), and social links — solid ancillary CTAs.

## What's weak

- Subject line "Just in!" is misleading — the hero creative is identical to three prior sends this week, so there's nothing actually "just in."
- Sale categories offer zero differentiation (Furniture, Bedding, Outdoor, etc.) — no indication of what's actually discounted or by how much per category.
- No urgency element (end date, countdown) despite "Labor Day Sale" branding — the sale could be ending today and the reader has no way to know.
- Cadence is the real problem: this is the fourth near-identical maroon banner in four days, which trains subscribers to stop opening.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the creative repetition first** — swap the hero banner or lead with the Halloween Shop / New Arrivals content instead of the fourth consecutive maroon sale banner; recycling identical creative four days running is the single biggest drag on this send.
- 2. Add a hard deadline to the hero ("Ends tonight" / "Final hours") if this is genuinely the sale's last day — turns a stale repeat into a credible urgency play.
- 3. Replace the generic 8-tile category grid with 2-3 called-out best-sellers or biggest discounts, since category names alone don't move anyone at this point in the cadence.
- 4. Move "Recommended Just For You" higher — it's the most differentiated, personalized content in the email and it's currently buried below a category grid nobody hasn't already seen three times.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks are on sale + the Halloween Shop is open`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new arrivals and spooky season finds`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Recommended for you, now up to 70% off`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard Cheetah Digital / Cordial-style responsive HTML email with heavy use of tracking pixels and redirect links; core rendering markup is sound but compliance headers weren't captured and several legacy asset references use insecure HTTP.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All visible CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` redirect tokens (e.g. the header logo link) — standard ESP click-tracking, not inherently an issue, but 57 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe, so their final destinations are unverified.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels are present: `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Turn/Amobee), `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...&sv_emopen=true` (open tracking), and `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/a56c9fc9-...?mi_mid=...` (Cordial/marketing-cloud open tracking). These are functional but represent unverified endpoints.
- `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel is served over **http://**, not https — see Rendering section.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **39 images missing `alt` text**, spanning hero slices, footer slices, product images (e.g. `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`), and tracking pixels. Since this is an all-image, linkfarm-style layout (`GraphicHeroLinkfarm`, slices_01–13, etc.), missing alt text means screen-reader/images-off users get no content at all — this is the most significant accessibility gap.
- **2 images served over http://** instead of https:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true...` (open pixel)
  Mixed-content/non-HTTPS sources risk being blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing TLS-only image loading.
- Markup includes standard MSO conditional comments, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and mobile media queries — no structural rendering defects found in the provided source.
- Note: raw `<style>` block contains stray PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) inside a `<style>` tag — these are inert as CSS/HTML (browsers/clients will ignore them as invalid CSS), but indicate a templating artifact that leaked into the compiled output. Worth flagging to the ESP team since it suggests a template include error, even though it has no functional rendering impact here.
