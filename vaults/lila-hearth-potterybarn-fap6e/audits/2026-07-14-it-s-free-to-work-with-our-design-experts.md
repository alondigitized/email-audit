# It’s FREE to work with our design experts
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a category break from Pottery Barn's July run of heavy sale emails — a pure Design Services pitch. "We're Here to Help (For Free)" leads the hero, positioning the brand's in-store/in-home design consultation as the main event. There is no discount, no percentage-off anchor, no urgency — just a soft-sell service offer buried under a long "Shop by Style," "Fall Collection" tease, and a "Recommended For You" product carousel.
- The tonal pivot is smart in theory: after seven consecutive sale emails, leading with value-add service is a reasonable cadence move. But the execution dilutes it immediately. The hero is followed by three separate content modules before the email ends, each with its own CTA. There is no single clear call to action — the email tries to be an editorial, a services pitch, and a product carousel all at once.
- Net judgement: a structurally weak send. A subscriber who opened expecting the ongoing 70% off sale will feel disoriented. A subscriber genuinely curious about design services gets an interesting offer, but has to work too hard to find it. The "Recommended For You" personalization block is the email's strongest conversion asset, and it's buried below the fold.

## What's working

- **Hero framing is clear and differentiating** — "We're Here to Help (For Free)" immediately communicates the value prop of design services at zero cost.
- **"Book a Free Appointment" CTA** in the hero is unambiguous — a specific verb + noun, clearly visible.
- **"Shop by Style" module** provides useful editorial navigation with visual category tiles.
- **"Recommended For You" product carousel** shows real product imagery and likely reflects browsing/purchase history — the only genuine personalization signal in the email.
- **Affirm financing callout** is credible social proof that reduces friction for higher-ticket purchases.

## What's weak

- **No sale signal anywhere** — subscribers primed by seven consecutive "UP TO 70% OFF" emails will scan, find no discount, and bail. The abrupt pivot to a zero-discount services email creates expectation mismatch.
- **Too many competing modules** — Design Services → Shop by Style → Fall Collection preview → Recommended For You → Pottery Barn App → Loyalty → Affirm — the email has at least six distinct content beats with no clear priority.
- **"Fall Collection" tease is jarring in mid-July** — subscribers are in summer-sale mode, and a "Fall Collection" preview with its own "Shop Now" CTA reads as out-of-season noise.
- **Hero copy is thin** — the three-line "Why / When / How" structure under the headline is small and hard to read; the value of design services is not actually explained.
- **No social proof for Design Services** — a testimonial, a before/after photo, or a "X customers served" stat would do real work here, and none is present.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut all modules except Design Services + Recommended For You.** This email is trying to be three different emails. Drop "Shop by Style" and the "Fall Collection" tease entirely. Let Design Services breathe as the hero, then let product personalization do the conversion work below the fold.
- 2. **Add one concrete trust signal to the hero block** — a short testimonial quote ("Our designer helped me nail the living room in one visit") or a stat ("Over 1M appointments booked") would convert skeptics who've never used the service.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero sub-copy.** Replace the three-line "Why / When / How" checklist with one human sentence: "A Pottery Barn designer will come to your home, measure, and help you choose — completely free."
- 4. **Move the subject away from features, toward outcomes.** "It's FREE to work with our design experts" is accurate but functional. A/B test an outcome-led subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free in-home design consult is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We'll help you pull the room together — for free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One visit. A designer measures, advises, and helps you choose. No cost.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Book a free appointment with a Pottery Barn design expert — in-store or at home.`
- 5. **Push the "Recommended For You" block higher** — it's the most conversion-ready asset in the email and it's below the fold. Swap it above the "Shop by Style" tiles.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Design Services" Email (2026-07-14)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one template assembly defect (PHP server directives embedded in a CSS `<style>` block), two broken assets, two HTTP-only image sources, and pervasive missing alt text across ~30 images. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are unverified at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken link — commented-out block (low severity)**
The credit card CTA (`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB...`) returned 403. The link exists inside a commented-out `<!-- ... -->` HTML block and does not render to subscribers. The QA scanner extracted it through the comment. The 403 is worth noting if this block is ever uncommented.

**Broken image asset (real, rendering impact)**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returned 403. This is a cross-brand West Elm CDN asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send. It will render as a broken image in any client that does not suppress missing images silently.

**25 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`. Final destination URLs cannot be verified from HTML alone; downstream link health depends on ESP redirect tables.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP directives inside `<style>` block — template assembly defect**
The following server-side PHP code appears verbatim inside a `<style type="text/css">` block:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are HTTP response header directives from a dynamic image-serving script that were accidentally included in the email template. Email clients treat them as invalid CSS and ignore them, but their presence confirms a template stitching bug. They have no runtime effect but indicate the template pipeline is pulling in fragments from incompatible sources.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — may be blocked**
Two footer images use `http://` and will be blocked or flagged by clients enforcing HTTPS (Gmail, Outlook.com):
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Pervasive missing alt text**
30 images have no `alt` attribute or an empty one. This includes all primary content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images (curtain rod, bed, towels, quilt, etc.), the header logo, footer slices, and tracking pixels. Clients with images disabled (a significant share of B2C email opens) will render a blank email with no text fallback.

**Responsive breakpoint coverage**
Two separate `@media` breakpoints are present (`max-width: 480px` and `max-width: 650px`). The 480px block uses the XHTML attribute selector form (`table[class="wrapper"]`) while the 650px block uses standard class selectors. This is intentional for older clients but means the two blocks target different client sets — verify the 650px block renders correctly in Outlook clients that strip `<style>` entirely.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%FIRST_NAME%`) found in the visible HTML. Recipient email address is correctly resolved and embedded in tracking pixels:
- `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (open-pixel URL)
- `?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (core email tracking)

No personalization issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
The `List-Unsubscribe` mail header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. If absent at send time (not just at relay capture), this will suppress inbox placement and one-click unsubscribe UI in Gmail.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe per RFC 8058 is not confirmed. Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance.

**Authentication-Results absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable from the captured headers. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture should be confirmed via DKIM signature on the raw message, not relay metadata.

**CAN-SPAM**
Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are expected in the footer (standard for WSI/Williams-Sonoma sends); not visible in the truncated HTML but not flagged as absent by QA. Assumed present.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All body CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which should append UTM parameters server-side at redirect time. UTM values cannot be confirmed from the HTML source. The commented-out credit card link uses `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` and `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner` — these are Williams-Sonoma internal tracking params, not standard UTM. If this block is ever activated, confirm whether GA4 or the site's analytics layer reads `cm_ite` correctly or whether standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` are also needed.

No UTM parameters are hardcoded on any live href in the visible HTML — all tracking passes through the click-redirect layer.
