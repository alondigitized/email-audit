# FREE design help for the holidays
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-led holiday send that opens with a clear promise: free design help, booked in-home, virtual or in-store. The hero is the strongest part. It has a table-setting image, a short who/what/when/where block and a single "BOOK AN APPOINTMENT" button. The page is organized under that: four project tiles (guest bedroom, festive table, tree, mantel), each with its own "BOOK NOW" link, followed by decorating guides, a "Home for the Holidays" class series and a product grid.
- The weakness is focus. The email asks for an appointment, then pivots to classes, guides, the rewards card, Affirm financing and a "Recommended Just for You" grid. A subscriber who came for furniture ideas gets little reason to book a consultation. Compared with the 10/20 barstool send ("Barstools to complete your kitchen, from $99"), which led with a specific price, this one has no price anchor and no urgency beyond "spots go fast." It is a competent, on-brand holiday send that's missing a hard reason to act today.
- Overall it's a solid but unspectacular send. The service hook is good, and the follow-through is diluted by too many secondary modules and a heavy fine-print block.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: "Style the Season with Free Design Help" over a festive table image, with the what/when/where details stacked right under it.
- "BOOK AN APPOINTMENT" is a visible, unambiguous primary button, repeated after the tiles.
- The four project tiles (Guest-Ready Bedroom, Festive Table, Beautiful Tree, Merry Mantel) are visual, seasonal and each has its own CTA.
- The mood is consistently warm and holiday-appropriate, and it matches the brand's earlier editorial sends (the 10/19 Harvest Decor Shop).
- The "Home for the Holidays" class series lists specific dates and times, which gives readers a concrete reason to act.

## What's weak

- There's no price or percentage offer. "Free" is the whole hook, and the email never says what the appointment leads to.
- The back half spreads attention across the class series, decorating guides, app download, rewards card and Affirm. The 11-item layout buries the booking goal.
- The "Recommended Just for You" grid (rod set, dining tables, towel, bed, curtain) is unrelated to design help and has no prices or CTAs I can see.
- Urgency is weak. "Spots go fast" has no date, and the class dates are well after the send.
- The dense fine-print and "Promotional Exclusions" block at the bottom is long and visually heavy.
- The tiles have no products, so there's no clear next step after the appointment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name a deadline and an outcome in the hero, for example "Book by Nov 1 for holiday-ready rooms," and say what you walk away with, such as a room plan or a shoppable list. That one change lifts the click rate most.
- 2. Cut the back half. Keep the hero, the four tiles and the class series, and drop the rewards card, Affirm and the generic product grid, or move them lower and shrink them.
- 3. Add a price anchor, such as a design-fee credit, a first-purchase discount or a "design help + free shipping" tie-in.
- 4. Make the tiles shoppable by linking each to the products used in the scene (tree, mantel, table settings).
- 5. Add real social proof, such as a "Designers helped 10,000 homes last year" line or a short quote.
- 6. Shorten the legal block or collapse it behind a link.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is an image-slice build (`slices_01`–`slices_11`, plus an 18-slice footer) wrapped in opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects. The main problems are accessibility (empty alt text on nearly every linked image), invalid CSS in the `<style>` block, two `http://` images, and the recipient's email address appearing in plain text in tracking-pixel URLs. Authentication and unsubscribe headers could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Opaque tracking:** 50 links point to `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABp7Im...`. The QA run skipped probing them, so destination, status and redirect chain are unverified.
- **Plain-text email in pixel URLs:** two image requests carry the recipient address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) in the query string:
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`

  This leaks PII into server logs and any proxy logs.
- **Third-party ad pixels:** `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501...` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` load from external ad networks. They add third-party requests, and some clients may block them.
- **Commented-out block:** the credit-card banner is commented out but still in the source, with a live-looking `potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?...cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` link. This is dead markup.
- **Cross-brand asset:** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a spacer served from a different brand's host. It's a minor dependency risk.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:**
  - The QA run flagged about 40 images. Those include hero slices `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_11.jpg` under `.../1020_TUE_AM2_DesignCrewInviteHolidayHelp/`, and `Email-Footer/slices_01`–`18`.
  - Product images such as `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg` and `emery-linen-curtain-l.jpg` are also flagged.
  - The visible hero slices use `alt=""` inside `<a>`. The links therefore have no accessible name, and the email reads as blank with images off.
  - Only the header logo has alt text (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`).
- **Invalid CSS in `<style>`:** raw PHP calls sit inside the second `<style>` block:
  - The calls are `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...")`.
  - They are invalid CSS, and because they are followed by `a[x-apple-data-detectors] {...}`, the parser will likely treat them as part of that rule's selector and drop it. That would defeat the iOS auto-link reset.
- **Mobile rules don't match the markup:** the media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. In the visible source, the tables are hard-coded `width="650"` with no `class`, and the slice images have no `class="photo"`. The 480px rules therefore don't apply, and the email won't scale down. (The source is truncated, so this holds only for the visible portion.)
- **Mixed content:** two images use `http://`:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- **Document structure:** the `<html>` tag has no `lang` attribute and the head has no `<title>`. Comments (`<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->`) precede the DOCTYPE, which can trigger quirks mode in some Outlook/IE-based renderers.
- **Web font:** the Google Fonts `<link>` for Montserrat is stripped by many clients, so it adds no benefit there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible tokens resolved (the recipient address appears in the pixel URLs). The source shows no leftover `%%...%%` or `{{...}}` placeholders.

The empty `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot --> <!-- end dynamic ampScript slot -->` comment pair is harmless, but it suggests an AMPscript block that rendered empty.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are absent from the captured headers. The QA tool notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed, not a confirmed defect**.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** missing, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. Alignment looks plausible: the From domain is `e.potterybarn.com` and the click domain is `click.e.potterybarn.com`. It can't be verified.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** both fall in the truncated portion of the source, so they can't be confirmed or denied from this data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters are visible. All links go through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` with an encoded payload, so campaign parameters and landing pages can't be checked from the HTML. The QA tool skipped these links.
- The only plain destination URLs visible are the commented-out credit-card link (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) and the `coreemail.html` image.
- The campaign asset path (`1020_TUE_AM2_DesignCrewInviteHolidayHelp`) matches the send date and subject, so there's no stale-asset mismatch.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Give every linked slice descriptive alt text, such as the offer or CTA. Add alt text, or `alt=""` with `role="presentation"`, to the product images and spacers.
2. Delete the PHP `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block so the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule and the rest of the CSS parse.
3. Rewrite the mobile rules to target classes that exist on the markup, or add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"`. Switch the 650px fixed tables to `max-width` or fluid widths.
4. Change the two `http://edm.potterybarn.com/...` images to `https://`, and remove the commented-out credit-card block.
5. Stop putting the raw email address in the `svopn` and `coreemail.html` URLs. Use a hashed or opaque subscriber ID instead.
6. Add `lang="en"` and a `<title>`, and move the DOCTYPE to the first line.
7. Re-run the QA against raw `.eml` headers (or a direct send) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Also resolve a sample of the `click.e.potterybarn.com` links to verify UTMs and landing pages.
