# FREE design services ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-led email dressed up as a sale email. The hero, "RSVP for Your Free Design Services," is a strong and distinctive idea, and the "what / when / where" structure makes the offer easy to scan. The "Book a Free Appointment" button is clear. But the email then stacks a second hero (the Fall Sale, up to 70% off), a third (the Oct 25 "Design a Guest-Ready Bedroom" class), and a product grid. That is three asks in one scroll.
- For Felix Plinth, a home-furniture persona, the design-services angle is relevant, and the Fall Sale tile grid is a useful follow-through. This is the sixth Pottery Barn send since Oct 7. The Fall Sale banner was already the lead in the Oct 8 "The Fall Sale is ON 🍁" email and the Oct 9 "Layers for your coziest bed" email. Here it is demoted to a second block, so the sale repeats without adding urgency.
- Judgment: a good concept, diluted by the cadence and by competing CTAs. It scores a solid 6-7, below the Oct 8 straight sale blast (8/10) because the focus is split.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and readable. The what/when/where copy turns a vague service into three concrete answers.
- "Book a Free Appointment" is a clear, full-width button that sits above the fold.
- The Fall Sale tile grid (Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, and so on) gives easy category routing for a furniture shopper.
- The in-store class block has a specific date and time (Sunday, October 25th, 10 AM) and an "RSVP Today" button.
- No broken images or layout glitches are visible. The brand voice is consistent.

## What's weak

- The hero offers a free service and the second block pushes 70% off. Neither is clearly the star, and the eye has to choose.
- The urgency is vague: "spots go fast" and "choose a time" give no deadline or slot count.
- The recommended products ("Recommended Just For You") show no prices, no sale pricing, and no reviews. That wastes the 70% off message.
- The footer is very heavy, with long exclusions, card terms, and Affirm disclosures. It undercuts the "free" message.
- This is another Fall Sale send in a run of near-identical promos. Anyone who got the Oct 8 and Oct 9 emails sees the same green banner again.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero. If design services is the point, drop the Fall Sale tile grid below the fold and keep only a one-line sale callout. If the sale is the point, make design services a secondary strip.
- 2. Add real urgency to the hero, such as "Book by Oct 18" or "Limited weekend slots at your local store."
- 3. Add prices and sale prices to the recommended products. Use "Up to 70% off" labels on the individual tiles.
- 4. Collapse the footer disclosures to a link, and keep only the Pottery Barn credit card and Affirm modules if they earn a click.
- 5. Space out the sale sends, since this was the third Fall Sale message in two days.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free room consult is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Styling tips, room refreshes & total makeovers, in your home, virtually, or in-store.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick a time that works. Fall Sale up to 70% off ends soon.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, 650px fixed-width image-slice layout. All 53 tracked links go through the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect, so none could be probed. The main technical problems are invalid CSS in a `<style>` block, empty alt text on linked images, plain-HTTP image sources, and recipient email addresses in third-party pixel URLs.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverifiable destinations.** QA skipped 53 links, all of the form `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABp7Im...`. The destinations are opaque, so landing-page status and redirect chains are untested.
- **PII in pixel and beacon URLs.** The recipient address appears unencoded in query strings:
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
- **Third-party ad pixels.** `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?...` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` add external tracking calls. Both have no alt text.
- **Cross-brand asset dependency.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is hosted on a West Elm domain inside a Pottery Barn send. It's a likely template reuse artifact.
- **Dead code shipped.** A commented-out credit-card banner block, including a link with `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`, remains in the source. It's harmless but adds weight.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid CSS in `<style>`.** The second `<style>` block begins with PHP statements: `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store..."); header("Pragma: no-cache");`.
  - A CSS parser will likely fold that text into the prelude of the next rule. That would drop `a[x-apple-data-detectors] {...}`, so iOS auto-linking of addresses and dates would not be neutralized.
- **Responsive rules don't match.** The media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. In the visible source, the tables are `width="650"` with no class, and the images have no class. The 480px rules therefore appear to have no effect, and the layout stays fixed at 650px on mobile. Part of the source is truncated, so elements further down may differ.
- **Empty or missing alt text.**
  - QA flagged about 45 images. The slices in `/pb/2026/US/1009_FRI_AM2_DC_Sale/` (`slices_01`–`14`) and `/Email-Footer/` (`slices_01`–`18`) have `alt=""` in source. Most are the sole content of an `<a>`, so the links have no accessible name.
  - Product images such as `york-slope-arm-deep-seat-slipcovered-sofa-60-108-l.jpg`, `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg` and `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg` also lack alt text.
  - The tracking pixels `coreemail.html`, `lb`, `1` and `svopn...` should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Non-HTTPS images.** Two images use plain HTTP and may be blocked or flagged as mixed content:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- **Markup before the DOCTYPE.** The `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->` comments sit before `<!DOCTYPE>`. This can trigger quirks mode in some legacy clients.
- **External font.** The Google Fonts `<link>` for Montserrat is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. The visible source gives no evidence of a font-family fallback stack, so I can't confirm one.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved `%%...%%` or `{{...}}` tokens are visible. The empty ampScript slot comments are the only residue. The email address in the pixel URLs resolved correctly. The exposure is a privacy matter (section 2), not a merge failure.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is unconfirmed rather than a verified defect. If the headers are really absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements are not met.
- **Authentication-Results not captured.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain `e.potterybarn.com` does align with the click-tracking domain `click.e.potterybarn.com`.
- **Footer not verifiable.** The truncated source doesn't reach the footer, so I can't confirm an unsubscribe link or physical postal address in the body.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No UTM parameters visible.** Every visible click link is the opaque `?qs=` redirect, so I can't confirm campaign parameters on the final landing URLs.
- **Landing alignment untestable.** Redirects weren't followed. Nothing in the source contradicts the "FREE design services" subject or the preheader "Our experts can help you shop The Fall Sale".

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block so the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule parses.
2. Add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"` to the tables and images, or rewrite the media queries to target real selectors, so the layout is responsive.
3. Add descriptive alt text to every linked slice and product image. Set `alt=""` explicitly on tracking pixels.
4. Switch the two `http://edm.potterybarn.com/...` images to HTTPS.
5. Stop putting the raw recipient email in the `svopn` and `coreemail.html` URLs. Use a hashed or opaque ID instead.
6. Replace the West Elm `spacer_0.gif` with a Pottery Barn-hosted asset, and strip the dead commented-out block.
7. Move the ampScript comments after the DOCTYPE, or remove them.
8. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml`. That would confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`, and show whether the footer has an unsubscribe link and postal address.
