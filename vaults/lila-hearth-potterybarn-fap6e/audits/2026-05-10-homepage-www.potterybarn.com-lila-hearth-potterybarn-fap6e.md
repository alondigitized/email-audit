# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-10
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-10**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn expecting to browse for the lake house, and the very first thing I got was a full-screen SMS opt-in modal blocking everything. Behind it I can just barely make out a cream sofa with floral throw pillows — which is exactly my language — but the brand won't let me see it until I hand over my phone number. This reads like a company that trusts its list-building team more than its merchandising team.

## What's working

- **The modal lifestyle photo**: A cream upholstered sofa with a mix of floral and geometric throw pillows is the right visual for me — it says "aspirational home, not a showroom."
- **The brand mark**: "POTTERY BARN" in that clean serif caps at center of the modal reinforces the premium positioning I already trust from the WS family.
- **"New arrivals" in the headline**: Leading with what's fresh is smart — I come to Pottery Barn specifically when a new catalog hits, so that framing gets my attention.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand mention in fine print**: For someone already enrolled in WS Rewards, the explicit call-out that this covers the full family of brands is a subtle loyalty signal — I noticed it even buried in the disclaimer.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen blocking modal as the first impression**: Before I've seen a single product or price, I'm being asked to commit to SMS marketing. For a high-intent returning customer that's an instant friction spike.
- **"Exclusive savings & more" is not an offer**: No threshold, no percentage, no deadline. The modal promises savings without showing any — it's bait without a hook.
- **Mobile number field above the fold, email buried above it**: Asking for a phone number before I've even seen the homepage feels aggressive, especially with the "automatic dialing system" language right below it.
- **Navigation is almost entirely hidden**: I can see tiny nav text at the very top — "Baby Kids Teen Dorm Business to Business Willi…" — none of which are my categories (furniture, bedding, outdoor). The modal has swallowed the actual store.
- **No personalization or welcome-back signal**: I have WS Rewards. There's nothing here that says "welcome back, here's what's new for members" — the brand is treating me like a cold prospect.

## Recommendations

- **Kill the blocking modal on return visitors** — replace with a soft banner or bottom drawer for email/SMS capture so I land on the actual homepage and see the merchandise first.
- **Surface a specific member offer in the modal copy**: swap "exclusive savings & more" for something like "Members get 10% off new arrivals — yours to claim" with my rewards status already pre-filled if I'm logged in.
- **Bring the sofa image forward as the hero without the modal gate**: that cream sofa with the throw pillows is doing real work — let it breathe on the full viewport instead of being obscured by a signup form.
- **Swap the nav order at the top**: for a .com visit on mobile, "Furniture" and "Bedding" should be the first two taps, not "Baby" and "Teen."
