# IN STOCK & ON SALE 🎉 Furniture & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's most scattered send in a week that's already been trending that way — after 2026-08-08's "New deals UP TO 60% OFF" catalog dump and 2026-08-09's two "sale" emails, this one adds a fourth flavor of the same pitch, and it's the vaguest yet. The hero ("Save on Furniture & More," "Delivered in 1-4 weeks," "Shop All Sale") is clean and well-composed, but notice what's missing: no percentage, no dollar figure, nothing. Every recent PB send in this history has led with "UP TO 60% OFF" in the subject or hero; this one drops the number entirely and just says "sale," which reads as a step down in offer clarity.
- The body then sprawls across four distinct ideas — a category nav grid, a "Recommended Just for You" product rail, a "Discover New Arrivals" banner, and a "The Halloween Shop" teaser — before diving into app-download and credit-card cross-sell. Each piece is fine on its own, but stacked together it's the same "everything, all at once" problem flagged in the 2026-08-08 catalog send. A Halloween teaser in an August furniture-sale email also feels like a non-sequitur next to "in stock" language, which is otherwise about immediacy, not holiday planning.
- Net: clean production, on-brand execution, zero render bugs — but the least differentiated offer of the week, wrapped in a subject line that leans on caps-lock and an emoji instead of a real number.

## What's working

- Hero renders cleanly: sofa vignette, clear "Shop All Sale" button, no layout breakage.
- The navy category grid (Furniture, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Rugs, etc.) gives an easy self-select path for shoppers who know what they want.
- "Recommended Just for You" product rail shows six distinct, tastefully shot items (curtain rod, rug, platform bed, duvet cover) rather than a wall of generic thumbnails.
- Brand voice and visual system (navy/cream, serif logo, consistent product photography) stay consistent with prior PB sends.
- Credit card rewards and Affirm blocks are legible and not visually cluttered.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere in the render — not in the hero, not in the subject. Every recent PB send in this history led with a specific "UP TO 60% OFF"; this one just says "Save" and "Sale," which is a real step down in persuasiveness.
- Four unrelated campaign beats (furniture sale → new arrivals → Halloween shop → app/credit card) crammed into one email with no narrative thread tying them together.
- "The Halloween Shop" appearing in an August 10 furniture-sale email feels premature and disconnected from the "in stock, delivered in 1-4 weeks" urgency framing above it.
- Subject line leans on "IN STOCK & ON SALE" in caps plus an emoji instead of a specific number — a mild spam-pattern substitute for real specificity.
- "Recommended Just for You" shows no visible personalization signal (same generic mix of rug/bed/curtain categories PB pushes broadly) — the label promises more than the row delivers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number back in the subject and hero. "Save on Furniture & More" with no percentage is the single biggest miss — even "Up to 30% off" would outperform this against the brand's own recent 60%-off sends.
- 2. Cut the email to one focus. Either this is a furniture sale email or a Halloween teaser — pick one and demote the other to a single footer link, the way "Must-see dining chairs" (2026-08-07, 8/10) stayed disciplined.
- 3. Give "Recommended Just for You" actual segmentation cues (or drop the personalized label if the picks are generic) — a mismatched personalization promise erodes trust over repeated opens.
- 4. Move "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" below the fold or reframe it as a value prop, not the hero's second line — right now it reads as a shipping disclaimer competing with the sale message for attention.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on bestselling sofas, rugs, and bedroom furniture.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop what's in stock — no long lead times, just savings.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "IN STOCK & ON SALE" Email

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard MI-generated HTML email with a table-based layout; primary technical defects are missing `alt` attributes across all content/tracking images, a legacy HTTP (non-TLS) image and open-tracking pixel, and no visibility into unsubscribe/authentication headers from the current QA capture.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 57 tracking/click-redirect links (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) were skipped by the automated HTTP probe as expected for redirect domains — no resolution data available, not itself a defect.
- Open-tracking pixel served over plain HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This URL also embeds the recipient's raw email address in a query string over an unencrypted connection — a PII-over-HTTP exposure.
- A second open pixel is present via `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/244d71f3-...?mi_mid=...` (HTTPS, no issue).
- Turn.com pixel (`https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...`) present — third-party ad-tracking pixel, HTTPS, functioning as expected.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Every image in the payload is missing `alt` text (43 warnings), including product images (`reeva-handwoven-rug-3-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.) and the header nav image `nav0.jpg`. Screen-reader users and clients with images blocked by default will see no fallback text at all — this is the most significant accessibility gap in the file.
- Head contains a stray `<style>` block with raw PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, `header("Cache-Control: ...")`, `header("Pragma: no-cache")`) sitting inside CSS — these are inert as CSS but indicate a template/build artifact that leaked server-side code into the client styling block. Harmless to rendering (ignored as invalid CSS) but worth flagging as a build hygiene defect.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and appropriate for email.
- Google Fonts loaded via `<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/...">` — most email clients (Gmail, Outlook) strip external `<link>` stylesheets, so this will silently fail to apply in the majority of inboxes and fall back to system fonts. Not breaking, but dead weight.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{...}}`, `%%...%%`) found in the visible source.
- Recipient identity is passed via query-string append on multiple asset URLs (`coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`, the HTTP open pixel above, `mi_mid=` param) rather than an opaque token — the plaintext email address embedded in the HTTP pixel URL is the concerning instance (see §2).

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: not found in this capture — QA flags this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay not surfacing the header rather than the sender omitting it; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM one-click compliance from this data alone.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): not found — same caveat as above.
- `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC): not found — cannot verify sender authentication status from this capture.
- A footer unsubscribe/preference link (`coreemail.html`) is present in the truncated body, consistent with CAN-SPAM's physical footer requirement, though the full footer text (physical address, unsubscribe link text) is beyond the truncation point and not verifiable here.
- **Recommendation for QA tooling**: these three header warnings are relay-side blind spots, not confirmed sender defects — reprocess via a capture path that preserves raw headers before treating as findings.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Primary nav CTA routes through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (opaque encoded redirect) rather than a direct UTM-tagged URL — cannot verify landing page or UTM alignment without following the redirect (out of scope for static HTML review).
- Two hardcoded HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset links point to `edm.westelm.com` and `edm.potterybarn.com` — cross-brand/legacy asset paths (`westelm.com` domain inside a Pottery Barn email, `/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/` dated content in a 2026 campaign) suggest stale template includes rather than campaign-specific assets:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 20+ product/content images at minimum (nav logo, product shots); decorative spacers/pixels can use `alt=""`.
2. Migrate the three HTTP-only asset/pixel URLs to HTTPS, particularly the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel that currently exposes the recipient's email address in a plaintext query string over an unencrypted connection.
3. Remove the inert `header(...)` PHP-style declarations from the `<style>` block — build/template artifact, not functional but should be cleaned from the source template.
4. Re-run QA against a header-capture path that isn't mediated by the AgentMail relay to get a real read on `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` before treating their absence as a compliance finding.
5. Replace the dated `2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner` and cross-domain `westelm.com` asset paths with current-campaign, correct-brand-domain assets.
