# 🩵 Shop UP TO 70% OFF + Celebrate summer with SAVINGS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second email of July 12 — a hard reversion to straight sale promotion just hours after this morning's editorial "Get the Look: The Layered Bed." The structure is nearly identical to the July 10 "The Summer Sale is ON" email: a blue hero announcing "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" over a 10-category shop grid, a "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalized product row, then a "Discover New Arrivals" lifestyle block. The only meaningful differentiator from prior sends is that personalized product section.
- After six consecutive days of Summer Sale messaging, nothing here justifies another open. The subject — "🩵 Shop UP TO 70% OFF + Celebrate summer with SAVINGS" — is pure volume, no angle. The brief editorial pause this morning had more narrative pull; reverting to the grid-of-categories format the same afternoon burns whatever goodwill that pivot built.
- The email is technically clean. But "clean and unremarkable" is the ceiling here, not the floor.

## What's working

- The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product row is the only structural differentiator from prior sale sends — personalized thumbnails give actual landing targets, not just a category grid.
- The 10-category shop grid (FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, BEDDING, DECOR, RUGS, etc.) gives a fast scan path for anyone already in purchase mode.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" and the Affirm "Buy Now, Pay Later" block both address real purchase friction points in the footer.
- No render bugs — email is visually clean throughout.

## What's weak

- The subject line is the fourth near-identical "UP TO 70% OFF" formula in this campaign run — urgency erodes with every repeat.
- No deadline anywhere in the email. The sale has been "on" for six-plus days with no visible end date, which completely defangs the discount headline.
- The "Discover New Arrivals" section undercuts the sale message — new arrivals imply full price, and the mixed intent weakens both pitches.
- The category grid layout is a carbon copy of July 10's "The Summer Sale is ON" — no creative evolution even as the campaign ages.
- ALL CAPS in the subject ("UP TO 70% OFF," "SAVINGS") reads as shouting and triggers spam-signal pattern recognition.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible deadline — this is the single biggest lever.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "3 days left" anywhere — hero, subhead, CTA — converts the browse-later crowd. Without it, "70% off" feels like it'll be there next week too.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to break pattern fatigue:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale ends Sunday — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: furniture, bedding & more at up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your picks are in — personalized sale styles just for you`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `10% back in rewards + 100s of new styles just added`
- 3. **Promote "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" above the category grid.** Personalized product cards are the highest-value element in this email — they're buried mid-scroll behind the same generic hero shoppers have already seen six times.
- 4. **Cut or relocate "Discover New Arrivals."** Full-price new arrivals dilute the sale signal. Replace with a scarcity hook ("Selling fast — act now") or a category spotlight like outdoor, which drove the strongest creative in this run (see July 11's "Summer treat" email).
- 5. **Add a product count or scarcity signal to the hero** — even "200+ styles added" or "Going fast: 70% off" creates forward momentum the static category grid doesn't.

## 1. Overview

This is Pottery Barn's second email of July 12 — a hard reversion to straight sale promotion just hours after this morning's editorial "Get the Look: The Layered Bed."    - **Preheader Alt A:** `Your picks are in — personalized sale styles just for you`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `10% back in rewards + 100s of new styles just added`

3. **Promote "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" above the category grid.** Personalized product cards are the highest-value element in this email — they're buried mid-scroll behind the same generic hero shoppers have already seen six times.

4. **Cut or relocate "Discover New Arrivals."** Full-price new arrivals dilute the sale signal. Replace with a scarcity hook ("Selling fast — act now") or a category spotlight like outdoor, which drove the strongest creative in this run (see July 11's "Summer treat" email).

5. **Add a product count or scarcity signal to the hero** — even "200+ styles added" or "Going fast: 70% off" creates forward momentum the static category grid doesn't.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two CTA/asset links return 403, three image assets are served over HTTP (no HTTPS), and 43 of ~45 images lack alt text. A PHP `header()` code artifact is embedded inside a `<style>` block, indicating a template injection error.

`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`

**[FAIL] West Elm cross-brand spacer — 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

**[INFO] 25 tracking links skipped**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP statements accidentally rendered into the CSS block. All browsers will ignore them silently, but they indicate a template/preprocessor pipeline failure — the email was likely rendered from a dynamic image-serving script and the PHP header calls leaked into the HTML output.

**43 images missing `alt` text**
Includes content images (12× `slices_*.jpg` product grid, product PDPs, hero banners), footer images, and tracking pixels. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, Gmail app on mobile data) produces a completely unreadable email. Tracking pixels missing alt is low-risk but adds noise.

**3 image assets served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
| File | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/...` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

Gmail and Apple Mail will flag or block mixed-content images. Note: `slices_02.jpg` references a 2022 asset path — likely stale footer template code.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`
Prevents user zoom, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Should be removed.

**Empty `<title>` element**
`<title></title>` — no impact on inbox delivery but will show blank in browser-view fallback.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If the header is missing at the MTA level (not just relay-stripped), Gmail and Apple Mail will not display the native "Unsubscribe" link in the header UI, and the email may be deprioritized by spam filters.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policies for >5k/day senders since Feb 2024) is not confirmed present. If Pottery Barn sends at bulk volume, this is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed through the relay. Sender domain `e.potterybarn.com` should have aligned DKIM signatures — unverifiable from this capture.

**`meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`** — Suppresses HTTP Referer on any non-tracked link clicks. Low compliance risk but means any fallback landing page analytics lose referrer signal.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism appear present in the footer (footer HTML is truncated but structure is visible). No issues confirmed.
