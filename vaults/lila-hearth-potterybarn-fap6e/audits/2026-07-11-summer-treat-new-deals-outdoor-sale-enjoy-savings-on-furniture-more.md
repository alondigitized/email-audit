# 🍦 Summer treat: NEW DEALS + Outdoor sale! Enjoy savings on furniture & more
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn earns its outdoor category focus here — the hero image of coastal white lounge chairs is crisp, "Outdoor on Sale" is the clearest category callout in this entire July run, and "Up to 70% off" is visible without scrolling. The visual execution is among the cleaner sends of the week.
- But the email is undermined before it's opened. The subject — "🍦 Summer treat: NEW DEALS + Outdoor sale! Enjoy savings on furniture & more" — is 75 characters of five competing messages: a seasonal metaphor, an all-caps hype phrase, the actual category, a soft CTA, and a trailing "& more." Compared to the tight July 9 "ON SALE: Expertly crafted furniture" send (9/10), which punched above its weight by committing to one message, this subject reads like it was written by a committee trying to serve every stakeholder simultaneously.
- This is also at minimum the second Pottery Barn email on July 11 — the earlier "New to sale & UP TO 70% OFF + A new sofa" send already occupied this subscriber's attention today, following three emails on July 10. At seven sends in three days, the list is almost certainly fatigued. The best-executed hero in the summer can't fix that math.

## What's working

- **Hero image lands "outdoor summer" instantly.** Coastal deck setting, white lounge chairs — no copy needed to communicate the category.
- **"Up to 70% off" lives above the fold.** Concrete discount is visible before any scrolling.
- **"Recommended Just For You" personalization block is present.** Behavioral targeting signals real audience investment beneath the blunt subject.
- **Email renders cleanly throughout.** No broken images, no layout collapse, no placeholder text at any point in the scroll.
- **"Outdoor on Sale" is the most category-specific hero Pottery Barn has used this July window,** sharper than the "Summer Sale Is ON" catch-all from July 10.

## What's weak

- **The subject is 75 characters and wants to be five emails at once.** Summer treat, NEW DEALS, Outdoor sale, savings, furniture & more — none of these reinforce each other; they cancel each other.
- **Cadence has crossed into list-fatigue territory.** Seven emails in three days, two today alone — a second same-day blast gets reflexive archive behavior from trained-up subscribers.
- **Below the hero, there's no hierarchy of intent.** Personalized recs, new arrivals, app download, and a design appointment CTA all compete at roughly equal visual weight with no clear winner.
- **No visible deadline on the sale.** "Summer Sale" is open-ended; every strong send this week that outperformed (July 9 furniture, July 10 dining) had at least implicit urgency. This one doesn't.
- **The 🍦 emoji is a tonal mismatch.** Ice cream reads "casual fun" — Pottery Barn's outdoor aesthetic in this very email reads "aspirational coastal." They're not the same brand voice.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one subject hook — the outdoor category with the discount.** "Outdoor on Sale: up to 70% off" is 35 characters, fully specific, and doesn't need an emoji chaser. The ice cream metaphor adds zero purchase intent.
- 2. **Add a closing deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday" in a banner strip or callout flips this from a passive browse to a time-sensitive decision. The July 9 send earned its 9/10 partly by forcing that urgency frame.
- 3. **Cut two of the four secondary modules.** Keep the personalized recs (highest-relevance signal) and drop either new arrivals or the app download CTA — running both plus design services creates scroll debt with no clear exit.
- 4. **Preheader should deliver the deadline or the product hook,** not repeat the subject or leak filler. It's the one free inbox impression this email has left unused.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor on Sale — up to 70% off this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio upgrade: summer sale now on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lounge chairs, dining sets & more — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the outdoor edit before the best picks go`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links (403) block live CTAs, and PHP server-response code was accidentally pasted into a `<style>` block — invalid CSS that signals a copy-paste error during template assembly. Accessibility coverage is critically low with 48 images missing `alt` text and four assets served over plain HTTP.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — Credit card apply/buy CTA (403)**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This is a primary header CTA (non-member credit card banner). The `clickcp.potterybarn.com` endpoint returned 403, meaning every recipient who clicks this link hits an error page.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer asset (403)**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
A West Elm CDN asset is referenced in a Pottery Barn email. The domain returns 403 and the asset is also HTTP-only (see §3). This is a cross-brand asset leak in the template.

**23 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. HTTP probing was skipped for redirect chains; cannot confirm destination health from QA data alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: PHP code embedded in `<style>` block**

Inside the second `<style>` tag, after valid CSS rules, the following appears:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls, not CSS. They were copy-pasted from a server-side image handler script into the HTML template. Email clients will silently discard them as invalid CSS, but this is a definite template assembly error that warrants investigation — there may be more template corruption not visible in the truncated source.

**48 images missing `alt` text** — includes all content-bearing images:
- Hero banner: `F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`
- All 16 outdoor sale slice images (`slices_01.jpg` – `slices_16.jpg`, path `0530_SAT_AM_OutdoorSale/`)
- All 18 email footer slice images
- Product images (curtain rod bracket, platform bed, towels, sheet set, curtains)
- Tracking pixels (`coreemail.html`, `turn.com/r/dd/...`, open-tracking pixel)

With images blocked (default in Outlook, Apple Mail), the email renders as a blank column for recipients relying on alt text fallback.

**4 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)** — will be blocked by many clients with mixed-content protection:

**`<title>` tag is empty** — minor, but used by some accessibility tools and screen readers when navigating the email as a document.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — suppresses user pinch-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility concern for low-vision users.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA notes this may be a relay-capture gap, but if genuinely missing from the envelope, Google and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) are violated for high-volume senders. Requires header-level verification against the raw SMTP envelope, not just the HTML body.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — one-click unsubscribe not supported or not captured. Gmail renders a prominent "Unsubscribe" button in the UI only when this header is present; its absence degrades deliverability signal.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm authentication posture from this audit.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is set in `<head>`. This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on outbound clicks, which may interfere with server-side analytics that depend on referrer data (separate from UTM params).

The images themselves are loading (no 403), but the path mismatch indicates asset reuse rather than a fresh build. If the sale terms or imagery were updated for the July send, these may be showing outdated creative.

**`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`** parameter on the CC link confirms this is a non-member targeting segment. Given the 403, this segment receives no functional CTA from the header banner.

**No UTM parameter inspection possible on product links** — all product CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect chains that were skipped by the HTTP prober. UTM parameter presence on final landing pages cannot be confirmed from this audit.
