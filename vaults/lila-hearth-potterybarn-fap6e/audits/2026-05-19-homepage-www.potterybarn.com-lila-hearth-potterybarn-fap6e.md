# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn is clearly my people — aspirational home, quality materials, the kind of brand I'd have in three browser tabs on a Saturday morning. But the very first thing I see when I open the site is a sweepstakes modal about golf and Dad, with whiskey decanters and a "Golf Courses" book front and center. I came here to think about throw pillows and accent chairs for my lake house, and instead I'm being asked for my email address to enter a Father's Day contest. The actual homepage is completely hidden.

## What's working

- **Seasonal accuracy:** It's mid-May and the Father's Day push is real — Pottery Barn is at least in the right campaign window. The U.S. Open tie-in ($4,000 grandstand seats) gives the sweepstakes a credible anchor.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand family mention:** Even buried in the fine print, seeing "Williams-Sonoma brands" is a small loyalty signal for me — I have their rewards card and I notice the family name.
- **Clean modal design:** The modal itself is uncluttered. The headline hierarchy (small cap tag → large headline → supporting copy) is easy to read even on a phone screen.
- **Chat button visible:** The live chat widget in the bottom-right corner is accessible even through the modal — useful if I want help without dismissing and hunting through nav.

## What's weak

- **Entire homepage is blocked:** I cannot see a single piece of Pottery Barn's actual home assortment. There are no rugs, no sofas, no bedding, no room scenes — nothing that would make me want to shop for my second home.
- **Modal is the wrong audience:** I'm not buying a Father's Day golf gift. This modal is wasted interruption real estate on me. It creates immediate friction before I've seen one product.
- **No loyalty recognition:** I'm a Williams-Sonoma rewards member — Pottery Barn is part of that same family. There's zero personalization signaling that they know who I am or that I have points.
- **Email capture with fine-print marketing consent:** The fine print is three dense paragraphs of legal. As a high-tech-comfort millennial, I read those. It's a lot to accept before I've even seen the homepage.
- **No visible close affordance until I look hard:** The X button is in the top-right corner and fairly small on a phone-sized viewport — and the modal takes up the full screen, so it takes a moment to orient.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the sweepstakes modal for known loyalty/rewards members** — if Pottery Barn/Williams-Sonoma can recognize me via cookie or account session, skip the lead-gen modal entirely and let me land on the homepage I came for.
- **Time-gate the modal:** Show the Father's Day sweepstakes to users who arrived via a Father's Day email or search, not to everyone cold. Segment by referral source so the interruption is relevant.
- **Replace the above-the-fold experience with a second-home lifestyle hero** — a sun-drenched great room, a porch scene, layered bedding on a platform bed — something that signals "this is your decorating destination."
- **Surface the Williams-Sonoma rewards connection above the fold** — a small "Key Rewards member? Your points work here too" badge would stop me in my tracks and immediately establish relevance.
