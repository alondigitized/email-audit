# Sale on sale 🎉 EXTRA 20% OFF select styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is at minimum the seventh Pottery Barn Memorial Day email in four days — and the second send on 5/18 alone, following "So many NEW Memorial Day deals" from earlier today. The subject's "sale on sale" framing is the lone differentiator: it signals a meaningful escalation, implying that items already reduced are getting stacked with an additional 20% off. That's a real offer worth leading with. The email doesn't lead with it. The hero still says "Extra 20% off select styles" — identical in substance and structure to the 5/15 launch email and every send since.
- The render is clean. Pottery Barn's visual language is consistent, the CTA hierarchy works, and the outdoor lifestyle photo continues the Memorial Day = backyard season thread from the 5/17 outdoor send. None of that is in question. What's in question is whether a subscriber who has already received six emails with the same template, the same category grid, and the same "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" four-product carousel is opening a seventh. The subject earns a look; the body doesn't reward it.
- Net judgment: the offer has genuine teeth — "sale on sale" is an escalation, not a repetition — but the creative execution doesn't show them. This send deserved a different hero.

## What's working

- **"Sale on sale" positioning is differentiated.** Stacking discounts on already-reduced items is more compelling than a flat "Extra 20% off" — it's an escalation that gives deal-seekers a real reason to act now rather than wait.
- **Hero is clean and immediately scannable.** "Extra 20% off" + the red "SHOP EXTRA 20% OFF" button is high-contrast and unambiguous; no hunting required.
- **Category grid covers the full range.** Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Rugs, Bath — multiple entry points with zero friction.
- **Outdoor lifestyle image is strong.** The large patio photo fits the Memorial Day = summer entertaining frame and gives the email visual depth beyond a pure promotional grid.
- **Loyalty kicker is present.** "Earn 50% Back in Rewards" is visible for members who are on the fence.

## What's weak

- **"Sale on sale" is the subject hook but absent from the hero.** The hero reads identically to the 5/15 launch email. If this is a new offer tier, the headline should say so.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" carousel appears stale.** Farmhouse Platform Bed, Standard Curtain Rod & Rod Bracket, Wicker Chaise Lounge — the same four products have appeared across multiple sends this week. It reads as an algorithm repeating itself, not a curated recommendation.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email.** Seven emails in four days with zero "ends Monday" copy. The urgency is manufactured by volume, not by a credible close date.
- **Curtain Rod & Rod Bracket undermines curation.** A $20 hardware accessory sitting next to a platform bed and a chaise lounge kills the aspirational signal of the personalization block.
- **Two Pottery Barn Sale emails in a single day.** The second send of the day is almost guaranteed to suppress open rate and accelerate unsubscribes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put "sale on sale" in the hero headline, not just the subject.** Replace "Extra 20% off select styles" with something like "Sale on sale — prices just dropped again" to make the escalation tangible. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even one line of "Ends Monday, May 26" in the hero subtext converts intent to action and justifies the email volume.
- 3. **Refresh the RECOMMENDED block with "sale on sale" examples.** Surface two or three items that were already marked down and are now further discounted — make the concept concrete with prices before and after.
- 4. **Drop the Curtain Rod from recommendations.** Replace it with a second statement piece (a rug, a sofa) to preserve the aspirational framing.
- 5. **Consolidate same-day sends.** Merge the 5/18 AM and PM sends into one daily email with multiple angles, or establish a clear hierarchy with an opt-out path for subscribers who've already clicked.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale prices just dropped again — extra 20% off ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Already on sale. Now extra 20% off. Shop before Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Items already up to 60% off are now stacked with an extra 20% — deepest deals of the season end Memorial Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our sale got a sale. Extra 20% off styles already marked down, ending Monday.`

`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (mixed content — 3 instances):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail and Outlook.com strip HTTP image sources by default. These assets will be silently blocked for a significant portion of recipients.

**Alt text absent on 38 images**, including all body content slices (`slices_01a.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`), all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), and product images. With images disabled, the email renders as a blank white block — zero fallback text conveys the sale offer, discount amount, or any CTAs.

**Injected PHP headers in `<style>` block:**
```html
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
Raw PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in HTML email but indicate a template assembly bug — a server-side script fragment was pasted into the wrong context. Browsers/clients will parse them as garbage CSS and may log errors.

**`<title>` is empty.** Minor, but some clients display the title as a tab label or preview string.
