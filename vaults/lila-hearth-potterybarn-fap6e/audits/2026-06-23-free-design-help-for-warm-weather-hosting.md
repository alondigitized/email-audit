# FREE design help for warm-weather hosting
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's "Design Services" pivot send, arriving in the middle of their Summer Sale blitz — and it earns its place in the inbox precisely because it's the first email in at least nine sends that isn't screaming "UP TO 70% OFF." The offer is structurally sound: free design consultation, no purchase required, warm-weather hosting as the thematic hook. The hero communicates the value proposition in a single scannable line ("RSVP for Free Design Services"), and the body walks through three use cases — lounge layering, fresh greenery, alfresco tablescaping — each with its own "BOOK NOW" CTA.
- The problem is those three mid-body CTAs. Every section has an identical "BOOK NOW" button with no differentiation, which fragments attention and makes the email feel like it can't decide what it's actually selling. By the time the user hits the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom, the email has split into two campaigns in one send: a services pitch and a personalized product push. That's a creative compromise that dilutes both.
- On balance, this is the strongest Pottery Barn send in the recent audit history because it actually offers something different — but it's held back by a structural reflex to include everything.

## What's working

- **Subject breaks the blitz pattern.** After nine sends of "UP TO 70% OFF," a subject that doesn't mention a discount is immediately more interesting. "FREE design help for warm-weather hosting" is the most differentiated subject in the recent window.
- **Hero value prop is clear and fast.** "RSVP for Free Design Services" with bullet benefits ("100% free, no purchase required") does the heavy lifting above the fold.
- **Season-specific framing holds.** Lounge comforts, greenery planters, alfresco tablescape — all three content pods are coherent and Summer 2026-appropriate.
- **Sender sub-brand "Design Services" adds context.** Distinguishing this from the main promotional sender helps set expectations before the open.

## What's weak

- **Three identical "BOOK NOW" CTAs dilute focus.** Each content pod has the same button copy — no specificity about what you're booking or why that category warrants a separate appointment.
- **The product recommendation grid undercuts the service pitch.** Dropping a "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product carousel at the bottom signals that the email doesn't fully believe in its own concept.
- **No urgency or scarcity.** "Free appointment" is compelling, but there's no deadline, no capacity signal ("spots filling fast"), and no seasonal close-date — nothing that makes you book today versus later (never).
- **Preview text not legible.** Whatever runs above the logo is too small to read in the render — a wasted second impression.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the three mid-body CTAs to one.** Pick the most relevant use case for this persona (alfresco tablescape or lounge — whichever tests better) and make it the single CTA. The others become supporting imagery without buttons. One destination, one action.
- 2. **Add urgency to the primary CTA.** "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is passive. Replace with "Book your summer consult — free through July 4" or similar. Even a soft deadline lifts conversion on service bookings.
- 3. **Remove or reposition the product grid.** If it must stay, put it in a separate send or move it above the footer as "What our designers are styling right now" — give it a thematic label that ties back to the service offer rather than reading as a separate promotional module.
- 4. **Write a real preheader.** The render shows nothing legible above the logo. Use that slot: "Our in-house designers will plan your whole outdoor setup — at zero cost."

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn Design Services (0623_TUE_AM2)

### 1. Technical Summary
The email contains a critical template corruption (PHP server code injected into a `<style>` block), a live cross-brand image asset returning 403, two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources, and 32 images with no alt text. Compliance headers are absent or unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[CRITICAL] PHP header() calls inside `<style>` block**
The second `<style>` tag in `<head>` begins with raw PHP:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP code that was not executed — it was emitted literally into the CSS. It is likely a dynamic image-serving snippet accidentally merged into the email template. It is syntactically invalid CSS and will be ignored by mail clients, but it indicates a broken template pipeline that could mask other substitution failures.

**[FAIL] 403 on West Elm spacer**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns 403. This is an active `<img>` reference in the rendered HTML (not commented out), used as a cross-brand spacer. It will render as a broken image.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] 32 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image, all footer slice images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), product thumbnails, and tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. With images blocked (common in B2B clients, Outlook by default), the email renders as a blank page with no informational fallback.

Notable items with no alt text that carry functional meaning:
- `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_07.jpg` (body content)
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, etc.
- Footer slices (`slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg`)

The nav/header image at `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2016/nav/nav0.jpg` does have `alt="POTTERY BARN"` — the only image with populated alt text.

**[FAIL] Two images served over HTTP**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Mixed-content blocking in Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook will suppress these images silently. Both appear to be legacy credit card banner assets.

**[INFO] Breakpoint inconsistency**
Two separate `@media` blocks use different breakpoints: `max-width: 480px` (first block, `table[class="wrapper"]`) and `max-width: 650px` (second block, `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show`). This creates a gap window (481px–650px) where mobile hiding logic fires but the wrapper width is not constrained.

**[INFO] Attribute selector CSS pattern**
Responsive styles use `table[class="wrapper"]` (exact match) rather than `.wrapper`. This is a legacy Gmail hack; it breaks if the element ever has multiple classes.
