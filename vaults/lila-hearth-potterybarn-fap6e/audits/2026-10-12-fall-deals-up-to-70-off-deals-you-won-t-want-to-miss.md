# Fall deals UP TO 70% OFF + Deals you won’t want to miss!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Fall Sale send from Pottery Barn today, and it reads like a catch-all. The green "The Fall Sale: Up to 70% off" bar is clear, and the "Now on Sale" hero (a dining table in a fall scene) is clean. But the email then sprawls into category tiles, a "Recommended just for you" grid, New Arrivals, a Christmas Shop banner, and app, credit card and Affirm blocks. Nothing owns the email.
- It is the same creative as "Don't miss The Fall Sale (UP TO 70% OFF)" from earlier today. Compared with the focused Thanksgiving send ("Everything you need for Thanksgiving") and the outdoor and botanicals category emails, this one is weaker. The subject also stacks two promises ("Fall deals UP TO 70% OFF + Deals you won't want to miss!"), and the second half says nothing.
- Judgement: a competent but redundant blast. It would perform as a reminder, not as a distinct send. It scores in the low-to-mid range.

## What's working

- The green sale bar sits at the very top, with a SHOP NOW button, so the offer is visible immediately.
- The "Now on Sale" hero is warm, seasonal and on-brand, and it has its own SHOP NOW button.
- The category tiles (Furniture, Bedding, Bath, Outdoor, Pillows & Decor) are cleanly labeled and easy to scan.
- The "Recommended just for you" block shows named products with sizes, such as the Banks Extendable Dining Table and Toscana Extendable Dining Table.
- The images render cleanly, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- There are too many competing blocks: the Fall Sale, New Arrivals, the Christmas Shop, the app, the credit card and Affirm. The eye has no single landing point.
- The sale hero says "Now on Sale" with no specific product, price or percentage. The "70% off" only appears in the top bar.
- The "Recommended just for you" tiles show no prices, sale prices or discounts, so the 70% claim is never backed up.
- It is another near-identical Fall Sale blast on the same day, so it feels like fatigue, not a new reason to open.
- The fine print is dense and the exclusions are long, which undercuts the "up to 70% off" promise.
- The Christmas Shop banner pulls attention away from the sale and mixes seasons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one idea. Keep the sale bar, a hero with a specific product and price, one product row, and the footer. Move the app, Affirm and credit card blocks into a lower-priority module or drop them from sale sends.
- 2. Show real sale prices on the recommended products (for example "Was $1,499, now $899"). That makes the 70% concrete.
- 3. Name a hero category with a real number, such as "Dining tables up to 50% off", instead of the generic "Now on Sale".
- 4. Give the sale a deadline, such as "Ends Sunday". Nothing in the render creates urgency.
- 5. Space out the sends. Three Fall Sale emails in a day teaches subscribers to ignore them.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces we think you'll love, marked down this week only.`

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn, "Fall deals UP TO 70% OFF + Deals you won't want to miss!"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout built from sliced images, with about 39 content images that have no alt text. It has three plain-HTTP asset requests, and the recipient's address appears in the query string of two image URLs, one of them over HTTP. The compliance headers could not be verified because the relay may have stripped them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverifiable destinations:** 50 links go through the `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=…` redirector and were skipped by the HTTP probe. Landing pages, redirect chains and broken links could not be checked. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Cleartext tracking pixel:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` loads over HTTP. It can be blocked or rewritten by clients and proxies, and it sends the recipient address in cleartext.
- **Third-party pixel:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` is a Turn (Amobee) ad-network pixel. It adds a third-party dependency and a cross-domain request.
- **Other pixels:** `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/25a0facb-…` is an open pixel, and `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is an image-type request that carries the address. The `coreemail.html` URL is not an image file, so it is probably a tracking beacon.
- **Mixed-content images:** three images use `http://`, which clients may block, leaving broken slots:

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** about 39 content images have no `alt`. They include the hero `H26_TOP_FallSale.jpg`, all nine `S26_Sale_B3_MINKStudioTest_Clearance/slices_01–09.jpg`, six product images (`banks-extendable-dining-table…`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg` and others), the bottom banners, and `Email-Footer/slices_01–18.jpg`. Sale and offer copy is likely baked into these slices, so with images off or in a screen reader the offer is lost. Only the logo (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`) is labeled.
- **Tracking pixels without `alt=""`:** four 1×1 pixels are flagged. This is low severity, but `alt=""` would stop screen readers announcing URLs.
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>`.
- **No language attribute:** `<html xmlns=…>` has no `lang`.
- **Zoom restricted:** `<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom. `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` and `-ms-text-size-adjust:none` also disable text scaling.
- **Server code in the CSS:** the second `<style>` block ends with `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store…"); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. This looks like leaked PHP and is invalid CSS. It sits at the end of the block, so it probably does no harm now, but a parser may swallow any rules added after it.
- **Layout markup:** some layout tables lack `role="presentation"`. The outer wrapper and the spacer table in the visible source are examples. Others carry it.
- **Web font:** Montserrat is loaded via a Google Fonts `<link>`. Many clients ignore it, so the layout depends on fallbacks.
- **Test-named assets:** `S26_Sale_B3_MINKStudioTest_Clearance` suggests test-named assets in a production send. `Email-Footer/slices_01–18` shows the footer is 18 sliced images, which adds weight and many requests.
- **Plain-text part:** 9,900 of 14,059 characters (70%) are URLs, mostly long redirect links. This is a spam-score and readability risk for text-only clients.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. The subject and preheader ("Save on pieces you'll love coming home to") contain no tokens and no unresolved merge syntax. The recipient address is resolved correctly in the pixel URLs, though that raises the privacy concern noted in section 2. The HTML is truncated, so tokens further down could not be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** not found. The QA note says AgentMail may not capture it, so it is unconfirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** not found, with the same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect one-click support.
- **Authentication-Results:** not present, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is expected through the relay and cannot be called a failure.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** not in the truncated source, so they could not be verified. The footer is made of image slices, so confirm that a text unsubscribe link exists alongside the images.
- **Address in query strings:** the recipient's email address is in URL parameters (section 2), one over plain HTTP. This is a privacy and data-handling risk.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters:** cannot be verified because all 50 links use opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=` tokens. No visible UTM parameters exist in the source.
- **Hotlinked assets:** product images are loaded directly from `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/…`. A site-side image change or deletion would break the email.
- **Cross-brand dependency:** the spacer GIF comes from `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, a West Elm host. It has no alt text and is an unnecessary cross-brand dependency.
- **Legacy asset:** a 2022 asset path (`/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/`) is reused in a 2026 send.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images, including the sale-copy slices, and use `alt=""` on pixels and spacers.
2. Move `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, the 2022 `slices_02.jpg` and the `svopn` pixel to HTTPS. Stop putting the recipient's email in plain query strings and use an opaque ID instead.
3. Remove the leaked `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block, and add `<title>`, `lang="en"` and `role="presentation"` on all layout tables.
4. Drop `maximum-scale=1` and `text-size-adjust:none`.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message from the original sender. Verify SPF, DKIM and DMARC outside the relay.
6. Resolve a sample of the redirect links to check landing pages and UTM tagging, and fetch the full HTML to check the footer unsubscribe link and postal address.
7. Replace the cross-brand `westelm.com` spacer and the test-named asset folder with Pottery Barn-hosted production assets.
8. Add a text-based fallback for the sliced sections, which would also improve the text-part ratio.
