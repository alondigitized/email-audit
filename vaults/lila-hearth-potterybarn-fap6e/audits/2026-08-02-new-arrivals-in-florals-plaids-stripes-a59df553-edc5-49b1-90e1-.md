# NEW ARRIVALS in florals, plaids & stripes
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand "new collection" showcase — Pottery Barn leading with a print-and-pattern story (florals, plaid, ticking stripe) instead of a discount. It's visually the most cohesive send in recent memory: hero armchair shot, then pillows, toile bedding, patterned dinnerware, and floral curtains all reinforce the same "heritage prints, collected look" idea before landing on a "Recommended Just For You" product grid.
- The tradeoff is that there's no offer anywhere in the body — no %, no $ off, no free shipping banner — just "Shop the Look" and "Shop New Arrivals." For a subscriber who's price-motivated (like the ones who clicked on 2026-07-30's "Starting at $299" rugs push), this send gives nothing to act on beyond browsing. It's a lookbook, not a sales email, and that shows in the click-through math.
- Compared to 2026-08-01's indigo send (8.00/10), this has a similar strength — single coherent visual story — but slightly weaker execution because the "Recommended Just For You" grid has no visible pricing on any of the six products, and there's no proof this is personalized beyond the section header. Net: a good brand-building send, a middling conversion send.

## What's working

- The pattern story is genuinely consistent — armchair, pillows, bedding, dinnerware, curtains all echo "florals/plaid/stripe," so the email doesn't feel like a random product dump.
- Two clear, repeated CTAs ("Shop the Look," "Shop New Arrivals") give an obvious next action without cluttering the layout.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text sits where it should, no overlap or broken assets.
- The Halloween Shop banner and Design Crew block are well-separated from the core content, so cross-sell doesn't compete with the main "new arrivals" message.
- Loyalty (10% back in rewards) and Affirm financing are both visible near the footer, giving price-sensitive shoppers a secondary lever even without a direct discount.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer anywhere in the body — no %, no $ figure, no free-shipping line. Compare to the brand's own rug drop ("Starting at $299"), which converts better because it anchors a price.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid shows six products with no prices and no obvious personalization signal — it reads like a generic bestseller rail, not a tailored pick.
- No social proof (star ratings, review counts) anywhere, despite plenty of white space where a "4.8★ (1,200 reviews)" badge could sit under a hero product.
- The hero CTA "Shop the Look" links to a curated collection, not a specific product — fine for browsing, weaker for someone who already knows what they want.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete anchor offer to the hero — even something modest like "New Arrivals, up to 20% off" would convert this from a lookbook into an actionable send, and it's the single biggest lever given every other recent Pottery Barn top-performer (2026-07-30, 2026-08-01) leaned on a specific price point.
- 2. Put prices on the "Recommended Just For You" tiles — right now they're unlabeled thumbnails, which kills impulse-click potential.
- 3. Swap the toile bedding module's plain caption ("a blue toile adds instant visual interest") for a price-led one, e.g. "Blue Toile Bedding, from $89" — matches the pattern the brand already uses successfully in rug/rug-collection sends.
- 4. Add a review count or "Bestseller" badge to at least the hero armchair — no social proof anywhere in this send is a missed trust signal.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **4 image sources use `http://`** instead of `https://` (`spacer_0.gif`, `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`, the `svopn` open-tracking pixel) — likely to be blocked or flagged as mixed content by mail clients that enforce TLS.
