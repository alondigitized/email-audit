# The elements of a welcoming entryway
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's numbered-guide format again — "The Elements of a Welcoming Entryway" walks through five styling categories (vases, candles, decorative objects, botanicals, baskets), each with a named product and a direct link. It's the most actionable version of the editorial format the brand has been running lately, more concrete than the July 29 "key layers for a well-styled bed" send because each step names a specific linked SKU rather than just a mood shot.
- But the email can't resist sprawling. After the entryway guide wraps with "SHOP ALL DECOR," it keeps going: a poufs/mirrors/frames/rugs text grid, a six-item "Recommended Just For You" grid spanning bedding/dining/bath, a "Discover New Arrivals" banner, an outdoor furniture sale banner, an app download pitch, a rewards card pitch, and Affirm financing — the same kitchen-sink tail flagged in the July 28-29 sends. The "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" banner at the very top also never reconnects with the entryway content below it — none of the five featured products show a sale price, so the discount promise and the editorial content feel like two different emails stapled together.
- Net: strong top, familiar bloat below. **8/10 territory**, held back from the July 28 quilt email's 9 by the disconnected sale banner and the now-repetitive tail.

## What's working

- The 1-5 numbered format (vases → candles → decorative objects → botanicals → baskets) is scannable and each step links a specific, named product rather than a vague category.
- The hero image (moody, styled entryway console) matches the subject line's promise directly — no bait-and-switch between subject and hero.
- "Recommended Just For You" keeps the same clean product-card visual language as the entryway section, so the cross-sell doesn't feel jarring.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- The "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" banner at the top never ties back to the entryway products — none of the five featured items show a discounted price, so the banner reads as generic filler bolted onto an otherwise focused editorial email.
- The email runs long well past its stated topic: bedding, dining, bath, outdoor furniture, app download, credit card rewards, and Affirm all appear after the entryway content is done.
- No pricing shown on any of the five featured entryway products — a reader has to click through blind to know if the "vase," "candle holder," or "utility basket" is $20 or $200.
- No urgency anywhere — "Summer Sale" has no end date, so there's no reason to act today versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. End the email at "SHOP ALL DECOR." The entryway guide is the differentiated content; everything after it (recommended grid, new arrivals, outdoor sale, app, rewards, Affirm) is generic tail that's appeared near-identically in the last several sends and just dilutes the entryway focus.
- 2. Make the sale banner specific to entryway decor — "Up to 70% off vases, candles & accents" — instead of a disconnected sitewide claim with no matching pricing below it.
- 3. Add prices to the five numbered picks so the value is visible before the click.
- 4. Give the sale banner a real deadline ("Ends Sunday") to create credible urgency instead of an open-ended "Summer Sale."

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "The elements of a welcoming entryway"

## 1. Technical Summary
Email renders via a standard MSO/Outlook-conditional table layout with responsive breakpoints, but ships with no unsubscribe headers, unknown authentication status, and near-total absence of image alt text (48+ images affected).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All click-through links route through tracking/redirect infrastructure (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, `d.turn.com`, `svopn.potterybarn.com`) — 60 tracking links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe because they are redirect endpoints, so final destination health is unverified.
- Open-tracking pixel is served over plain HTTP: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. Non-HTTPS resources are commonly blocked by mail clients, which would silently break open tracking for this send.
- Header logo link (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`) uses an opaque encoded query string with no visible campaign/UTM parameters — can't confirm campaign attribution without following the redirect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 48 images are missing `alt` text, including all 18 header "slices" images, all 18 footer "slices" images, and 8+ product/banner images (e.g., `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`). With images disabled (a common default in Outlook/Gmail), the email will render as largely blank content with no accessible fallback text.
- 3 images load over insecure HTTP rather than HTTPS: `spacer_0.gif` (edm.westelm.com), `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, and `slices_02.jpg` (edm.potterybarn.com/2022 path). These may be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing mixed-content/TLS policies.
- Layout uses legacy MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso 16]>`) and table-based structure appropriate for Outlook desktop compatibility — no structural issues found there.
- A stray `<style>` block contains raw PHP-style `header()` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) embedded as literal text inside CSS — this is inert (won't execute) but indicates a copy-paste artifact from template code that should be cleaned up; it has no functional rendering impact since it's inside a `<style>` tag and ignored as invalid CSS.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient email merge token resolved correctly and consistently across multiple instances: `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` appears populated (not left as a raw placeholder) in both the customer-service link (`coreemail.html?email=...`) and the open-tracking pixel (`sv_sveme=...`).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — not captured in the relayed headers, meaning mailbox-provider-level one-click unsubscribe UI may not be offered.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — even if List-Unsubscribe is present elsewhere, without the POST variant, Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe cannot function per current bulk-sender requirements.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via the AgentMail relay; this needs to be confirmed directly against the sending MTA rather than assumed.
- An in-body unsubscribe/customer-service link exists (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`), which addresses CAN-SPAM's requirement for a working opt-out mechanism in the body, independent of header-level unsubscribe.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot verify UTM parameter alignment between email links and landing pages — all outbound links are wrapped in tracking redirects (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) with opaque encoded query strings (`qs=ABB7...`, `mi_mid=019fb089-...`), and the automated probe skipped resolving these redirects.
- Product deep-links reference `potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...` image assets directly (not page URLs), so no landing-page continuity claims can be evaluated from image sources alone.
- Recommend following the redirect chain manually to confirm final URLs carry consistent UTM/campaign parameters and land on live, matching pages.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to comply with Gmail/Yahoo's one-click unsubscribe requirements (enforced since Feb 2024).
2. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly at the MTA — the missing `Authentication-Results` header here is likely an AgentMail relay artifact, not proof of a real auth failure, but should not be assumed passing.
3. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 48 flagged images, particularly the CTA/product images (currently blank fallback = image-off users see nothing).
4. Migrate the 3 remaining `http://` image sources to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
5. Serve the open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) over HTTPS to prevent silent open-tracking loss in HTTPS-enforcing clients.
6. Remove the orphaned PHP `header()` snippet embedded in the `<style>` block — harmless today, but signals a template-generation bug worth fixing at the source.
7. Manually resolve the tracking-redirect links to confirm UTM parameters and destination pages are correct, since automated probing could not follow them.
