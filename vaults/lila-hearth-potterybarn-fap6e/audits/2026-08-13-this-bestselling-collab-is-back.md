# 🎃 This bestselling collab is back
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's Peanuts × Pottery Barn Halloween collaboration relaunch — "The Great Pumpkin" Charlie Brown-themed bedding, appetizer plates, and table linens, positioned as a "bestselling collab" returning for the season. It's a genuine departure from the plain sale-dump and styling-guide sends this week: strong licensed IP, a distinct color story (pink, orange, cream), and clear seasonal timing make it visually the most differentiated email in this brand's recent run.
- The execution has real strengths — the hero art is charming, "Ships Free" is a nice trust signal, and the cascading category blocks (Whimsical Bedding → Nostalgic Dining Essentials) build a coherent narrative. But there's no explicit discount or price anchor anywhere in the promotional hero, and the "Recommended Just for You" block right after is completely off-theme (curtain rods, sheet sets, a rug) which dilutes the Halloween moment instead of reinforcing it.
- Net: a strong creative concept let down by soft merchandising follow-through. It'll likely outperform this week's generic sends on open/click purely on novelty and IP recognition, but it's leaving conversion on the table by not giving a reason to buy now beyond "it's back."

## What's working

- The Peanuts × Pottery Barn lockup and Great Pumpkin illustration immediately signal a limited, nostalgic collaboration rather than generic inventory.
- "Ships Free" badge sits right under the hero CTA, removing a common purchase objection at the point of decision.
- Clear thematic sectioning (Whimsical Bedding, Nostalgic Dining Essentials) with consistent color-blocked CTAs (orange/pink) keeps the eye moving down a long email.
- Product photography (styled bed, tablescape with pumpkin plates) is high quality and true to the licensed characters, reinforcing brand trust.
- Halloween seasonality is credible and current for mid-August retail lead time, unlike some of this week's stale styling-guide sends.

## What's weak

- No visible price, percentage-off, or "limited edition" urgency anywhere in the hero or collection blocks — just "Shop Now," so there's no concrete reason to act today.
- The "Recommended Just for You" grid (curtain rods, percale sheets, a rug) breaks the Halloween narrative right after the themed collection, undercutting momentum built by three prior sections.
- The email is very long — hero, bedding, dining, "shop full collection," recommended products, Design Crew sweepstakes, app promo, rewards, Affirm — by the time a reader reaches "Shop All Halloween" fatigue has likely set in.
- Multiple competing CTAs of similar visual weight (Shop Now, Shop the Full Collection, Shop All Halloween, Book Now, Download Now, Learn More ×2) dilute which single action matters most.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer or scarcity cue to the hero — "back for one season only" or a specific price point on the bedding set — since collab drops sell on FOMO, and right now there's no urgency lever at all.
- 2. Cut or re-theme "Recommended Just for You" — swap in Halloween/fall adjacent items (throws, table runners) instead of generic curtain rods and sheets, so the whole email stays in one seasonal mood.
- 3. Consolidate CTAs — pick one primary action (Shop the Collection) and demote Design Crew, App, Rewards, and Affirm blocks below a clear divider so they don't compete visually with the collab.
- 4. Trim length — the collab content (hero through "Shop All Halloween") could stand alone as a tighter, higher-converting send; move evergreen blocks (app, rewards, financing) to a separate footer-only placement or a different email entirely.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Pottery Barn's Peanuts × Pottery Barn Halloween collaboration relaunch — "The Great Pumpkin" Charlie Brown-themed bedding, appetizer plates, and table linens, positioned as a "bestselling collab" returning for the season. It's a genuine departure from the plain sale-dump and styling-guide sends this week: strong licensed IP, a distinct color story (pink, orange, cream), and clear seasonal timing make it visually the most differentiated email in this brand's recent run.

The execution has real strengths — the hero art is charming, "Ships Free" is a nice trust signal, and the cascading category blocks (Whimsical Bedding → Nostalgic Dining Essentials) build a coherent narrative. But there's no explicit discount or price anchor anywhere in the promotional hero, and the "Recommended Just for You" block right after is completely off-theme (curtain rods, sheet sets, a rug) which dilutes the Halloween moment instead of reinforcing it.

Net: a strong creative concept let down by soft merchandising follow-through. It'll likely outperform this week's generic sends on open/click purely on novelty and IP recognition, but it's leaving conversion on the table by not giving a reason to buy now beyond "it's back."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard MSO/Outlook-compatible HTML email with responsive media queries; QA pass rate is 57% driven almost entirely by missing `alt` attributes and two non-HTTPS legacy asset domains, plus unverifiable auth/unsubscribe headers at the relay layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Primary hero link routes through a click-tracking redirect: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...` (Ensighten/Bronto-style tracking domain) — functional, not flagged as broken.
- 55 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected behavior for redirect domains — not a confirmed defect, just unverified).
- Open-tracking pixels present and functioning as expected: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` and `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1` — both are 1x1 tracking beacons, correctly recipient-personalized.
- **Issue:** The `sv_emopen` open-tracking pixel is served over `http://`, not `https://` — see Rendering section below for blocking risk.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **41 images missing `alt` text** across hero banners, product "slices" images, footer navigation slices, and the customer-service/open-tracking pixels. This affects screen-reader users and image-blocked inboxes (no fallback text renders). Examples: `F26_Top_NewArrivals.jpg`, `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg` (product/footer nav images), `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `emery-linen-curtain-5-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`.
- **3 images served over `http://` instead of `https://`**, which many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy) will block or flag as mixed content:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - The open-tracking beacon `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/...` is also HTTP, meaning open tracking may fail to fire in clients that block mixed HTTP content.
- Head contains a stray, non-functional `<style>` block with PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, `header("Cache-Control: ...")`, `header("Pragma: no-cache")`) embedded as plain text inside CSS — these are inert (CSS can't execute PHP) and appear to be leftover/misplaced template code with no effect on rendering, but indicate a templating artifact worth cleaning up.
- MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso 16]>`) and Apple Mail reformatting disable (`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) are correctly present.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** in the captured headers — may be a relay/capture limitation (AgentMail) rather than a true absence; cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe compliance from this data alone.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe support is unconfirmed.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified via the relay capture.
- These are flagged as **relay-visibility warnings, not confirmed violations** — the truncated HTML does not show the footer/unsubscribe block, so an in-body unsubscribe link may still be present and compliant; this needs direct verification against raw SMTP headers rather than the relayed copy.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Header/logo link and hero link both route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` tracking redirects rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs, so destination/UTM alignment cannot be verified from the truncated source (redirect targets were skipped by the probe).

- Insufficient visible link data in the truncated HTML to confirm UTM parameter presence/consistency on outbound product links — flag as unverified rather than a confirmed gap.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 41 flagged images, particularly the product "slices" images and footer navigation graphics, for accessibility and image-blocked rendering.
2. Migrate the 3 remaining `http://` asset sources (`edm.westelm.com`, `edm.potterybarn.com/thekey`, `edm.potterybarn.com/2022/...`) to HTTPS to prevent mixed-content blocking in Outlook/Gmail.
3. Migrate the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel to HTTPS — as HTTP, it risks being blocked and undercounting open rates.
4. Remove the inert PHP-style `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — dead template code with no functional effect, but likely a copy-paste leftover worth cleaning from the source template.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` / `Authentication-Results` directly against raw SMTP headers at the source (not the AgentMail-relayed copy) to confirm actual CAN-SPAM and authentication compliance, since these warnings may reflect relay capture gaps rather than sender-side issues.
