# Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the most editorially distinctive Pottery Barn subject line in the June batch. "Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169" breaks from the endless "Up to 70% off" drum that has defined every send since June 12 — it names a product, uses evocative copy, and plants a concrete price floor, all in 49 characters. That is a genuine creative upgrade.
- The body partially delivers on the promise: the hero image is a clean lifestyle bedroom shot with quilt focus, and the "Beautifully Crafted Quilts" headline holds the thread. But then the email stacks three competing bottom sections — Recommended for You (with non-quilt products), Free Design Services, App download, Affirm — and the quilts focus evaporates. The subscriber who opened for quilts is being asked to book a design consultation and download an app before they've committed to anything.
- This is Pottery Barn's 8th+ email in 13 days. Compared to the June 16 "Bedding refresh in versatile neutrals" (7/10), which made a similar editorial bet and suffered the same focus-drift problem, this email has the stronger subject but the same structural weakness: a good creative hook that the layout fails to pay off.

## What's working

- **Subject line stands apart from the June batch.** Evocative copy + a specific price floor + under 50 chars — the best subject in this series.
- **Hero section delivers on the quilt promise.** Large bedroom lifestyle image, on-topic headline, and an immediate "SHOP ALL BEDDING" CTA form a clean above-the-fold unit.
- **Product grid is focused.** The quilt tile grid directly supports the hero — no bait-and-switch in the main content well.
- **Sale banner provides value context without hijacking the email.** "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" at top ties into the active June promo without overwhelming the quilts focus.

## What's weak

- **"Recommended Just for You" breaks focus.** The section surfaces non-quilt products (a bench/seating item is visible) mid-scroll — a distraction when the subject promised quilts specifically.
- **No product-level discount shown on quilts.** The "from $169" in the subject creates a pricing expectation the grid doesn't fulfill — there's no "Was $XXX, now $169" moment anywhere visible.
- **Three competing bottom modules stack awkwardly.** Design Services + App + Affirm all ask for something after the product grid; stacked together they read as padding and dilute scroll-to-CTA momentum.
- **No deadline.** The offer is open-ended — no "Ends Sunday" line — which is a missed lever after an editorial subject that already doesn't rely on urgency.
- **Cadence is punishing open rates.** Eight-plus emails in 13 days; even a strong subject can't fully overcome subscriber list exhaustion at this pace.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show before/after pricing on at least one quilt tile.** The "from $169" in the subject plants a specific expectation — deliver a "Was $XXX, now $169" callout in the product grid to validate it.
- 2. **Purge or quilt-ify the "Recommended for You" section.** Non-quilt products mid-scroll contradict the subject's promise; make it quilt-only or move it below the fold entirely.
- 3. **Add one deadline line.** A single "Sale ends Sunday, June 22" — in the hero or above the product grid — converts editorial tone into action without resorting to screamer caps.
- 4. **Pick one bottom module.** Design Services, App, and Affirm are all fine separately; stacked together they're noise. For a quilts email, Design Services is most relevant — drop the others.
- 5. **Mirror the subject in the hero headline.** "Beautifully Crafted Quilts" is generic. "Quilts with character in every stitch" (the subject) is better — use it.

This is an actionable revenue link (header banner), not a tracking pixel. A 403 on the click-capture endpoint means the destination record may have expired or been misconfigured in the ESP.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] PHP code leaked into `<style>` block.** The following server-side PHP `header()` calls appear as literal text inside a CSS `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is unprocessed PHP that was never executed. CSS parsers silently discard invalid rules so rendering impact is low, but it signals a broken template pipeline (a `.php` partial was included without being processed). Any secrets or logic that follows the same path may also leak.

**[HIGH] 3 images served over HTTP** — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, and most modern clients with mixed-content enforcement:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` (footer Key CC banner)

- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**[MEDIUM] 34 images missing `alt` text.** Confirmed missing on all content images including primary hero (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), product images, and all footer slice images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`). Affects screen reader users and image-blocked rendering.

**[LOW] Empty `<title>` tag.** `<title></title>` — inconsequential in most email clients but visible in some browser-based preview modes.

**[LOW] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).
