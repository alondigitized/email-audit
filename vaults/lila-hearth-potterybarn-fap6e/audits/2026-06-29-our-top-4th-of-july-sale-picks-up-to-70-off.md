# Our TOP 4th of July Sale picks (up to 70% off)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Nine days into a July 4th push that has now delivered the same "Extra 20% off" creative for at least the sixth consecutive send, Pottery Barn tries something different in the subject line: it goes editorial. "Our TOP 4th of July Sale picks" implies curation — a human hand selecting the best of the sale for you. Open the email and that promise evaporates. The body is the identical two-column category tile layout — Furniture, Bedding, Sofas & Chairs, Pillows & Decor, Rugs, Open Box — that has run since at least the June 26 "ON NOW: Extra 20% off select styles" send. The "Recommended Just For You" algorithmic grid at the bottom is the only element that personalizes anything.
- The discount mechanics are the strongest of the campaign window: Extra 20% stacked on top of already-reduced sale prices, with category-specific percentages climbing as high as 79% off. That's a real offer. But a subscriber who has received six near-identical sends in three days has already made a decision about this sale. "TOP picks" framing promises differentiation; the body delivers déjà vu.
- The execution is clean. The creative is spent. This email would be genuinely effective if it showed actual curated products — it isn't, so it coasts on the offer mechanics alone.

## What's working

- "Extra 20% off" hero lands immediately above the fold before any scrolling — offer legibility is instant.
- Category-specific percentages (79% Furniture, 40% Sofas & Chairs, 60% Pillows & Decor) are more credible and actionable than a flat "up to X% off" banner.
- "Open Box Outlet Deals" section is a practical value-add that targets deal-seekers who already passed on full-price.
- "Recommended Just For You" section injects per-subscriber personalization into an otherwise static template — the right instinct, wrong placement.
- Clean render throughout: no broken images, no layout failures, consistent typography.

## What's weak

- Subject promises editorial curation ("TOP picks") but the body is the same category-tile grid — there are no actual curated product picks visible anywhere.
- This is the sixth or seventh deployment of the same "Extra 20% off" hero creative; creative fatigue across the segment is at a critical level, as noted in the June 28 audit.
- Every CTA reads "SHOP NOW" — five identical generic prompts with no category specificity.
- No visible sale deadline in the body; "4th of July" implies urgency but no "ends July 4 at midnight" callout is present.
- "Recommended Just For You" is buried below the Open Box section, far below the fold — the one differentiating element is the least visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Follow through on "TOP picks."** The subject earns curiosity; the body has to pay it off. Show 4–6 actual products with names, original prices, and sale prices in the hero. This is the single change that separates this send from every prior one.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline.** "Sale ends July 4 at midnight" placed directly under the Extra 20% hero converts fence-sitters who've been sitting on this offer for days.
- 3. **Upgrade the CTA copy.** "Shop Furniture Deals," "Shop Bedding Sale" — category-specific buttons reduce decision friction and reinforce the tile's offer at a glance.
- 4. **Promote "Recommended Just For You" to mid-email.** Place it immediately after the hero section, before the category tiles. It's the only thing in this email a subscriber hasn't already seen.
- 5. **Suppress unengaged subscribers.** Anyone who hasn't opened the last three sends in this window should be held out of the remaining July 4th sends entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `These Pottery Barn pieces are worth it at up to 79% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We pulled your top sale picks (Extra 20% off today)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture up to 79% off — our editors chose these for your home.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your personalized picks from the 4th of July sale — ends July 4.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP assets (mixed content — 5 instances):**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Spacer GIF (West Elm) | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — **also 403** |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook on secure connections will block HTTP image sources. The svopn pixel is also the sender's open-tracking beacon — if blocked, open rates will be undercounted.

**Missing alt text — 46 images** have no `alt` attribute, including all 13 main content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`). With images off (common in B2B/Outlook environments), the email renders as blank. The product images (curtain rod, sheet set, quilt, dining table, towel, rug) also lack alt text, eliminating fallback product identification.

**CSS artifact in `<style>` block:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
PHP `header()` calls were accidentally pasted into a `<style>` block. These are ignored by email clients but signal a template assembly error — the wrong code fragment was included.

**`<title>` tag is empty.** Minor, but screen readers and some clients use it.

### 5. Compliance
