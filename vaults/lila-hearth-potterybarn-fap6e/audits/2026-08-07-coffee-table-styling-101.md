# Coffee table styling 101
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- "Coffee table styling 101" is one of the tightest subject-to-content matches I've seen from this brand this week — the hero ("A Curated Table") rolls straight into five styling categories (Candles, Faux Botanicals, Vases, Decorative Objects, Bowls & Trays), each with a real editorial tip ("Layer tapers in varied heights and dimension"). That's genuine content marketing tied to a specific product, not just another sale blast.
- The problem is what sits on top of it: the exact same "The Warehouse Sale — Up to 60% off" banner from 2026-08-06's "ON NOW✨ The Warehouse Sale" send, bolted onto a completely different creative concept. It works numerically (offer is visible immediately) but thematically it undercuts the "styling 101" promise — the first thing the eye hits has nothing to do with coffee tables.
- Past the styling module, the email drifts: "Recommended Just For You" surfaces a bed, curtain rods, and rugs — none of which reinforce the coffee-table angle the subject sold. Solid, well-built email, undermined by a generic banner and a generic recommendation block. Good, not great.

## What's working

- Subject and hero content are genuinely aligned — five styling categories with specific, usable tips, not vague lifestyle copy.
- Primary CTA ("Shop Coffee Tables") is unambiguous and sits right under the hero image.
- Layout is clean end-to-end — no overlapping text, no broken images, no placeholder content.
- "Ships Free" badge and "Shop Decor" secondary CTA add practical, low-friction next steps without cluttering the hero.
- Rewards/credit-card cross-sell (10% back, Affirm financing) is placed low, after the core content, so it doesn't compete with the styling message.

## What's weak

- Top banner ("Up to 60% off") is a straight repeat of yesterday's Warehouse Sale email and has zero connection to the "styling 101" theme — it reads like a bolt-on, not a designed hierarchy.
- "Recommended Just For You" swaps to unrelated categories (bed frame, curtain rod, rug, duvet, dining table) right after a coffee-table-specific pitch, diluting the focus the subject promised.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the email — for a "styling 101" educational angle, a "customers styled it like this" note or star rating would build more trust than a generic products grid.
- No credible urgency/deadline on the 60% off claim — "Up to 60% off" with no end date reads as evergreen rather than time-sensitive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic Warehouse Sale banner with a coffee-table-specific hook (e.g., "Coffee tables from $X" or "Up to 60% off coffee table styling essentials") so the very first thing readers see reinforces the subject instead of contradicting it.
- 2. Add a real deadline to the sale banner ("Ends Sunday") — urgency is the single missing rubric signal on an otherwise strong offer.
- 3. Swap "Recommended Just For You" for coffee-table-adjacent items (side tables, trays, ottomans) instead of a bed and curtain rods — keep the whole email on-theme.
- 4. Insert one line of social proof under the hero or styling grid ("Rated 4.8★ by 200+ shoppers") to close the trust gap.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Coffee table styling, solved`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Candles, vases, trays — plus up to 60% off to get the look`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your coffee table, upgraded in 5 easy adds`

# Technical Email Audit: Pottery Barn — "Coffee table styling 101"

  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — same pattern over https.
- Third-party audience/tracking pixel present: `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Amobee/Turn — data co-op tracking).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **40+ images missing `alt` text** across hero banners, the "slices" spacer/divider set, product images (farmhouse-platform-bed, reeva-handwoven-rug, banks-extendable-dining-table, etc.), and footer assets — confirmed by QA.
- **Mixed-content images (http:// on an https page)**, which many clients will block or flag:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` sets `maximum-scale=1`, disabling pinch-zoom — an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4 reflow/zoom).
- Malformed content in `<head>`: a `<style>` block contains literal PHP function calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...");`) — invalid CSS, dead code, likely an accidental copy-paste from a server script into the template. Not functional but risks CSS-parser errors in stricter clients (e.g., Outlook's Word engine).
- Preheader hidden-text block uses a very large run of soft-hyphen/whitespace padding characters, consistent with standard preheader-truncation control but on the heavier end of typical usage.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{FirstName}}`, `%%field%%`) observed in the visible source.
- Personalization is resolved correctly into tracking URLs (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`), but as noted in §2, this exposes the full recipient email address in cleartext GET parameters, including one over unencrypted HTTP — a data-handling concern more than a rendering one.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: **not present** (QA WARN) — one-click unsubscribe via mailbox provider UI not available.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058): **not present** (QA WARN) — cannot confirm one-click POST unsubscribe support.
- `Authentication-Results` header: **not found** (QA WARN) — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be determined via the AgentMail relay.
- In-body unsubscribe link/physical mailing address could not be confirmed — the footer was outside the truncated HTML window provided; this is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- All body/nav links route through the ESP click-tracking domain (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`), which is expected behavior for SFMC-based sends, but it means UTM parameters and final landing-page URLs are opaque at this layer — the 67 skipped links prevent verification of landing-page alignment in this pass.
- Product deep-links to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...` (image CDN) resolve on-domain, consistent with the brand's primary site.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to support one-click unsubscribe (Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements).
2. Move the open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) to HTTPS and confirm whether embedding the raw recipient email in tracking URLs is intentional — consider hashed/tokenized identifiers instead of plaintext addresses.
3. Fix the two remaining `http://` image sources (`edm.westelm.com`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022`) to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
4. Add `alt` text to all decorative/product images for accessibility and to preserve context when images are blocked by default.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag to restore pinch-zoom.
6. Remove the stray PHP `header()` calls from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — dead code with no effect that should be cleaned from the template source.
7. Re-run the tracking-link probe with redirect-following enabled to verify final landing pages and UTM continuity, since 67 links were skipped in this pass.
