# Head back to school with Pottery Barn Kids 🎒📚
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a Pottery Barn Kids sub-brand send, which matters: the brand history log is Pottery Barn main, so the cadence pressure here is lighter — this isn't the fourth BTS email from the same sender. The email leads with bright, lifestyle photography of children carrying backpacks, and quickly pivots into personalization as its main hook — "make it yours," "totally personalized" — which is PBK's genuine product differentiator. That's smart positioning: personalized backpacks are a reason to choose PBK over Target, and the email leans into it.
- The critical miss is that there's no sale. The main Pottery Barn has been hammering "UP TO 70% OFF" since July 10. PBK sends a full editorial email — multiple sections, lifestyle shots, app download footer — and never offers a percentage off or a threshold deal. In mid-July, when BTS shopping peaks and competitors are running discounts, this reads like a catalog, not an event. You browse; you don't necessarily buy.
- The layout is clean and render-bug free, but the email is long and the bottom third loses focus — a kids bedroom shot and an app badge feel tacked on and undermine the tight BTS momentum the top two-thirds builds.

## What's working

- Personalization callouts ("make it yours," "totally personalized") are front-loaded and visible — this is PBK's actual moat against mass-market alternatives.
- Hero photography is bright, on-season, and immediately communicates the category with real kids in real BTS looks.
- "Lunch buddies" section smartly expands the basket beyond backpacks — lunch boxes and water bottles are natural add-ons for a BTS shopper.
- Subject line emojis (🎒📚) are category-appropriate and not spammy — two is the right number.
- No render bugs; the layout holds cleanly across the full scroll.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere visible — no discount, no free personalization promo, no shipping threshold — in a high-competition BTS window where deal emails are the norm.
- "Shop Now" CTA is generic; a personalization-forward brand should say "Personalize Yours" or "Add Your Name."
- The email goes long — a bedroom/furniture section and an app download badge at the bottom kill the BTS momentum and dilute the category focus.
- No urgency mechanism — no "ships in time for the first day" messaging, no countdown, no deadline to guarantee personalized delivery.
- The main PB brand sent a desk email the same day; cross-sub-brand coordination is fine, but a parent might receive both and feel the PBK email is thinner by comparison since the main brand at least had a price anchor ($599).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a personalization delivery guarantee** — "Order by [date] for first-day delivery" is the single highest-impact addition. Personalized items have real lead times; a credible deadline creates urgency that a generic "Shop Now" cannot.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA** — change "Shop Now" to "Personalize Yours" or "Add Your Name Free" to align with the email's actual differentiator.
- 3. **Cut the bottom third** — remove the bedroom/furniture shot and the app badge. End on the personalized backpack grid with the CTA and a single footer.
- 4. **Layer in a threshold deal** — "Free personalization on orders $75+" or "Free shipping on BTS orders" gives deal-seekers a reason to act now, not bookmark for later.
- 5. **Add a credible first-day hook** — a small banner reading "Back to school starts [local first-day dates range]. Order yours today." ties the campaign to real calendar urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Personalize their backpack before school starts 🎒`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kid's name on everything — BTS is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by [date] for guaranteed first-day delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Backpacks, lunch boxes, water bottles — all with their name.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Corrupted `<style>` block — PHP headers in CSS:**

The second `<style>` block in `<head>` contains PHP `header()` calls:

```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
</style>
```

These are PHP server-side directives that were never executed — they appear to have been copy-pasted from a server-side image script into the CSS block by mistake. Mail clients will parse them as invalid CSS and discard them, but their presence indicates a template assembly error.

**Missing alt text — 30 images:**

The QA scan flagged 30 images with empty or absent `alt` attributes, including:

- `TopBanner.jpg` (hero-level content image, no alt)
- `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, and 6 other product images — no alt
- `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` (footer image slices, 18 total) — no alt
- Tracking pixels (`coreemail.html`, `lb`, `1`, `svopn`) — blank alt is acceptable for tracking pixels but should be `alt=""`  explicitly

The product image alts are accessibility failures. Screen reader users receive no product information.

**Mixed content — 2 images served over HTTP:**

- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Both reference `edm.potterybarn.com` over plain HTTP. Gmail and other clients that enforce HTTPS will block these images, leaving blank spaces in the footer credit card banner area.

**Responsive breakpoints inconsistent:**

Two separate `@media` queries are defined with different breakpoints: `max-width: 480px` (in the first `<style>` block using `element[class]` attribute selectors) and `max-width: 650px` (in the third `<style>` block using class selectors). The 480px block uses attribute selector syntax specifically to work around Gmail's class-stripping behavior — the 650px block does not, meaning the `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` classes may not function in Gmail.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — unconfirmed:**

The QA relay did not capture `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers. These are likely present at the SMTP envelope level but not surfaced through the AgentMail capture path. Gmail and Apple Mail require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click unsubscribe compliance (Google Bulk Sender policy, February 2024). This should be verified directly against raw SMTP headers, not inferred from relay output.

**Authentication-Results — unconfirmed:**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown; the header was not captured by the relay. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain (consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud), so authentication is likely configured — but cannot be confirmed from this data.

**CAN-SPAM:** An unsubscribe mechanism and physical address are present in the footer (visible in the HTML structure). No issues confirmed.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters on any click URLs.** All 23 CTA links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect URLs with opaque `qs=` query strings. The redirected destination URLs are not inspectable without following the redirect chain, so it cannot be confirmed whether UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended at redirect time.

If UTM parameters are not injected at redirect, GA4/analytics will attribute all email traffic as direct — a significant measurement gap for a promotional send.

**Cross-brand asset contamination:** The broken spacer `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm asset appearing in a Pottery Barn Kids email. This suggests shared template components are not being correctly swapped at send time, which may indicate downstream CTA or footer links also point to the wrong brand properties.
