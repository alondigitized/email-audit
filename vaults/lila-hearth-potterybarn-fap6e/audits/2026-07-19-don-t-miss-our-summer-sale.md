# Don't miss our SUMMER SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's nth summer-sale blast in a July that has already been saturated with "70% off" messaging. After scoring yesterday's "Up to 70% off ON NOW + Your first look at the NEW catalog" at 9/10, a flat "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE" subject line is a regression. The subscriber who opened yesterday's send has zero new information from this one, and the body never gives them a reason to change their mind.
- The email works structurally — the hero is legible, the category grid is functional, and a "Recommended Just for You" module adds a personalization gesture — but it fails at the only job that matters this late in the cycle: saying something new. Below the hero, the template stacks a category grid, personalized products, two lifestyle hero sections, an app download pitch, a rewards mention, and an Affirm module. That is at least three emails compressed into one. No module gets enough visual weight to compel a click; the subscriber's eye wanders and exits.
- Honest read: this is a blast-list-maintenance send. It keeps Pottery Barn top-of-mind at the cost of subject-line credibility. The brand has burned "70% off" so many times this month that a generic "SUMMER SALE" in ALL CAPS now reads as noise.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and immediately legible**: "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" with "1000s of new styles added!" communicates the core offer in under two seconds.
- **Category grid is well-organized**: Ten categories (Furniture, Outdoor, Bedding, Rugs, etc.) give a motivated shopper a one-tap path to their section.
- **"Recommended Just for You" module is present**: The personalization gesture is the highest-potential click driver in the email if the product selection is actually tailored.
- **Lifestyle photography holds the brand standard**: The "Discover New Arrivals" full-bleed section is aspirational and consistent with Pottery Barn's editorial voice.
- **Rewards mention surfaces loyalty value**: "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" at the bottom gives members a secondary reason to shop.

## What's weak

- **Subject is the weakest possible formulation**: "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE" is FOMO + generic category — zero specificity, no hook, indistinguishable from the last four sends.
- **ALL CAPS "SUMMER SALE" is a spam signal**: This alone suppresses open rates before any content question arises.
- **Too many competing modules**: App download + Affirm + two lifestyle heroes + category grid + personalized recommendations = no single CTA wins.
- **No deadline anywhere**: "Don't miss" implies scarcity but the body never delivers an end date, a countdown, or a "sale ends Sunday" — the urgency is pure bluff.
- **Cadence fatigue is terminal**: This arrives the morning after "Up to 70% off ON NOW" — a subscriber who opened yesterday gets nothing new from this send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the "1000s of new styles added" claim a number and a category**: "1,200 new outdoor styles added — up to 70% off" differentiates this send from every prior 70%-off blast and validates the hero copy.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero**: Even "Offer ends Sunday" doubles urgency without manufacturing fake scarcity. Nothing in this email signals when the sale ends.
- 3. **Kill the app download and Affirm modules**: Neither belongs in a sale conversion email. App promos warrant their own dedicated send; Affirm belongs on the PDP, not in a promotional blast.
- 4. **Collapse the two lifestyle sections into one targeted CTA**: Pick the strongest hero image and attach a specific button ("Shop Outdoor" / "Shop Rugs") rather than a fourth generic "SHOP NOW."
- 5. **Resize and label the Recommended products**: The thumbnails are too small to evaluate; showing a product name and price directly in the module would double the click signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `1,200 new styles added to the sale — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your picks are waiting — Summer Sale, up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, bedding & more — sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals just hit the sale. Shop before they sell out.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Don't miss our SUMMER SALE"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two broken CTAs returning 403, a critical build-pipeline error (raw PHP code embedded in a CSS block), three HTTP image sources that will be blocked or mixed-content-flagged by modern clients, and pervasive missing alt text across 38 images. Authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers are unconfirmed at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Two links return 403:**

| URL (truncated) | Context |
|---|---|
| `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407…?cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` | Credit card "Apply & Buy" banner CTA |
| `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` | Cross-brand West Elm spacer image (also HTTP) |

The `PB_header_nonmember` link is a named CTA (not a passive tracker), so users who click the credit card banner hit a dead page. The West Elm spacer is a cross-brand asset that should not appear in this send at all — its presence suggests a template fragment was reused without updating the asset domain.

25 additional links were not probed (click.e.potterybarn.com redirects) — no issues can be confirmed or ruled out for those.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code embedded in `<style>` block:**

The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains unexecuted server-side PHP directives:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are not CSS. They are PHP `header()` calls that were supposed to run server-side (likely for a dynamic image endpoint) but leaked into the rendered HTML as literal text. Email clients will either display this as visible text or silently discard it — either way it is a build pipeline failure, not intentional CSS.

**Three HTTP image sources (will be blocked or flagged):**

- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&…` (open-tracking pixel)

Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients proxy or block HTTP image loads. The open-tracking pixel on HTTP means open events will be missed or unreliable on those clients.

**38 images missing `alt` text** — affects screen readers and image-blocked rendering (Outlook default, Gmail images-off). Product images in the link-farm section (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) and all footer slices have empty or absent `alt` attributes.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — minor; some clients surface this in tab/window labels.

| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) | **WARN** — not found; Gmail/Apple Mail one-click unsubscribe button will not appear |

The absence of `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is a concrete issue: Gmail and Apple Mail display a one-click unsubscribe button only when this header is present. Without it, high-volume promotional mail is more likely to receive "Report spam" rather than a clean unsubscribe.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters on the 25 click.e.potterybarn.com redirect links could not be inspected (redirects skipped by probe). No verification of `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` values is possible from this data.

The `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` directive strips the referrer header on all outbound clicks. Any landing page that depends on `document.referrer` for attribution or personalization will receive an empty value — this conflicts with standard email analytics practice.

The West Elm cross-brand spacer (`edm.westelm.com`) indicates stale template fragments are in use; no further cross-brand link contamination was detectable in the truncated source, but the full send should be audited.
