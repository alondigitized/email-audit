# Introducing the new Dormify✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand partnership announcement — Pottery Barn introducing a collaboration with Dormify, a college dorm styling brand. The email leads with aspirational lifestyle imagery across four "pillars" (Sleep better, Study smarter, Feel at home, Stay fresh), each with a category shop CTA. The visual execution is clean and on-brand for Pottery Barn: muted neutrals, editorial photography, generous white space.
- The fundamental problem is that there's no offer. This is a pure awareness send — no promotional discount, no introductory deal, no urgency trigger — which means it's asking for a click on brand affinity alone. That's a hard ask from a home-goods retailer competing in a crowded inbox. The Dormify partnership is genuinely interesting positioning, but the email never tells you *why you should care today*.
- The rewards callout and Afterpay mention buried at the bottom are the closest things to a hook, and they're afterthoughts. For a launch email announcing something "new," this needed a sharper opening hand.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy per section** — each lifestyle block (sleep, study, home, fresh) has a clear headline and CTA that scan quickly on mobile.
- **Pottery Barn brand aesthetic is fully intact** — neutral tones, editorial bedroom photography, premium feel throughout.
- **Seasonal timing is smart** — May is right in the back-to-college planning window; the dormify hook is timely.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text isn't overlapping, layout holds together top to bottom.
- **Afterpay callout is practical** — college shoppers are price-sensitive; Buy Now, Pay Over Time is a relevant friction-reducer for this audience.

## What's weak

- **No promotional offer whatsoever** — an announcement email with zero price incentive is asking for clicks on curiosity alone; most recipients won't convert.
- **Four parallel sections dilute focus** — sleep/study/home/fresh compete equally for attention; there's no single hero product or hero story to rally around.
- **Dormify is not explained** — the email assumes recipients already know what Dormify is; the intro value of the partnership is never articulated.
- **The 10% Rewards callout is buried** in the footer boilerplate rather than treated as a feature above the fold.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — no limited-time framing, no "shop before move-in" deadline, nothing to prompt action now versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete offer above the fold.** Even "Free shipping on dorm orders over $75" or "10% off your first Dormify bundle" transforms this from an awareness send into a conversion send.
- 2. **Explain Dormify in one sentence in the hero.** "Dormify's college designers, Pottery Barn's quality — now one shop" gives the collab immediate context and value.
- 3. **Collapse four sections into one hero story.** Pick sleep (highest AOV) as the anchor and link the other three as secondary. One strong CTA beats four weak ones.
- 4. **Surface the Rewards benefit above the fold** — especially for a college audience who are rewards-program joiners.
- 5. **Add a deadline.** "Move-in season is 10 weeks away" or a countdown creates the urgency this send completely lacks.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains two critical code-level defects: PHP `header()` calls rendered verbatim inside a `<style>` block, and a second nested `<!DOCTYPE html>` / `<html>` / `<body>` declaration mid-document. These are structural errors that degrade rendering across clients. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, requiring direct sender-side verification.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The link is wrapped in an HTML comment (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not currently live. However, the destination returns 403. If the comment block is ever re-enabled, every click on the credit card banner will hard-fail. The URL should be verified and fixed before any reactivation.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
</style>
```
These are PHP server-side directives that were not executed — they were written literally into the CSS block. Any email client that strictly parses CSS will treat these as invalid declarations; permissive parsers will silently ignore them. This indicates a template rendering failure where PHP source code was included unexecuted. No functional impact on sent mail, but this is a build/rendering pipeline defect that needs a root-cause fix.

**[CRITICAL] Nested `<!DOCTYPE>` and `<html>` declaration inside `<td>`**
A second `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC ...>`, `<html xmlns=...>`, and `<body>` tag appear mid-document, nested inside a table cell (the body content injection point). Spec-compliant parsers will ignore or error on the second declaration; some email clients may enter quirks mode or drop subsequent content. The outer and inner document structures should be consolidated into a single valid HTML document.

**[WARN] 18 footer image slices missing alt text**
`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` (all from `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/Email-Footer/`) have no `alt` attribute. When images are blocked — default in Outlook, Gmail, and Apple Mail with remote image loading off — the footer renders as a blank region with no fallback text.

**[WARN] 2 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail will proxy-fetch HTTP images; Outlook desktop may block them entirely. Both should be upgraded to `https://`.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel images missing alt text**
`coreemail.html`, `lb` (leadback.advertising.com), `1` (d.turn.com), and the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel all lack `alt=""`. These should have `alt=""` (empty) to be explicit that they are decorative/invisible.

### 5. Compliance
