# Expert design services, for FREE
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a rerun of the May 27 "FREE design services" send that scored 8/10 — same hero concept, same sub-module layout (Layer a Pottery Barn Bed, Create an Outdoor Oasis, Our Storage Tips & Tricks, Ready to get started?), same "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA. The creative is identical or near-identical, and a subscriber who opened the May 27 version will immediately recognize it as a repeat. That's the cardinal sin here: a service-pitch email with no urgency hook gets a second airing two days later with no new angle, no deadline, and no differentiated reason to engage now versus then.
- The email itself is well-structured — the hero communicates the free offer clearly, the sub-modules are scannable, the "Recommended Just for You" product rail adds some personalization depth, and the Pottery Barn app / Affirm / Key Rewards footer is tidy. But the creative work is wasted when the calendar cadence makes it feel like filler between the May 28 outdoor sale and whatever comes next.
- The verdict: a competent email punished by poor scheduling. This should have been held at least a week, or differentiated with a new angle (a designer profile, a real before/after, a deadline on the free consultation).

## What's working

- **Hero offer is unambiguous** — "Free Design Help, Tailored to You" reads immediately and the "BOOK A FREE DESIGN APPOINTMENT" CTA is prominent below it.
- **Sub-module variety** — three distinct use-case tiles (bed layering, outdoor oasis, storage) help different subscriber segments find relevance.
- **"Recommended Just for You" rail** — product cards with prices visible add a transactional hook that the May 27 version appeared to have as well; it grounds the editorial in shoppable items.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero → sub-modules → recommendations → footer flows without clutter or render bugs.
- **No broken images or layout bugs** visible in the render.

## What's weak

- **Exact repeat of the May 27 send** — same subject concept ("FREE design services"), same hero, same sub-modules; a subscriber who opened two days ago gets zero new information.
- **Zero urgency** — "free" is the only hook; there's no deadline, no limited slots, no seasonal peg to force action now.
- **Subject is redundant in the inbox** — "Expert design services, for FREE" next to a May 27 send about "FREE design services" is indistinguishable from the subscriber's perspective.
- **App download and Affirm blocks dilute the message** — at the bottom of a service-pitch email, pushing the app and BNPL financing feels misaligned with the "we'll help you design your space" positioning.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this email or differentiate it hard** — if it must go now, replace the hero with a real designer story or a before/after transformation photo. "See how Sarah turned her dining room into a showpiece in one appointment" beats a stock lifestyle shot repeated from two days ago.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — "Book your free consultation before June 15 — slots fill fast for summer." Without it there is no reason to act today versus never.
- 3. **Pull one of the "Recommended for You" products into the hero narrative** — tie the design service offer to a specific item the subscriber has been browsing; that's what makes "tailored to you" feel real.
- 4. **Drop the Affirm block from this email** — it contradicts the "we're here to help, no strings attached" service positioning.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains a PHP `header()` code fragment leaked into a `<style>` block, two broken images (including a cross-brand asset from `edm.westelm.com`), 34+ images without alt text, and two images served over plain HTTP. Compliance headers were not captured through the relay, leaving SPF/DKIM and one-click unsubscribe status unverified.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
West Elm CDN asset used in a Pottery Barn email is broken. Will cause a missing-image gap or broken layout depending on client rendering of failed image requests.

**FAIL — Commented-out link returns 403 (low priority)**
`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
This URL is inside an HTML comment block (`<!-- -->`), so it is not rendered. However, QA flagged it as a live broken URL. If the comment block is ever removed to activate the credit card banner, this destination will return 403.

**INFO — 25 click-tracking links skipped**
All body CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`. Final destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified without following the redirect chain. No probe was attempted (by design).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code fragment inside `<style>` block**
The following text appears literally inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP that was not executed — it was included as literal text in the CSS block. CSS parsers will ignore it, so it does not break visual rendering, but it exposes a template assembly error, inflates HTML payload, and will trigger lint/validation failures. It should be removed.

**FAIL — Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

HTTPS email clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. These images will likely not render for most recipients. Both are in the footer credit card banner section.

**WARN — 34 images missing alt text**
Affects all content slices (`slices_01` through `slices_07` in body, `slices_01` through `slices_18` in footer), all product images (bed, sheets, curtain rod, mattress), the `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` CC banner, and three tracking pixels. Since the email is image-sliced with no HTML text for core content, recipients with images disabled or using screen readers receive no meaningful information. The only alt text present in the truncated source is on the header logo (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`).

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. If the header is present but stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a monitoring gap; if it is genuinely absent, it is a compliance violation.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
One-click unsubscribe header absent or not captured. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown. The sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is an ESP subdomain (Salesforce Marketing Cloud pattern) — authentication is almost certainly configured, but the relay is not surfacing the results header for verification.

**CAN-SPAM — Physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source. Footer is image-sliced, so the mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)) may be embedded in a slice image rather than HTML text — making it unverifiable by automated checks and unreadable for screen readers.

UTM parameters on body links cannot be confirmed without resolving the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects. The open-tracking pixel domain is `svopn.potterybarn.com`; the click-tracking domain is `click.e.potterybarn.com` — two separate subdomains for open vs. click, which is expected for this ESP setup.
