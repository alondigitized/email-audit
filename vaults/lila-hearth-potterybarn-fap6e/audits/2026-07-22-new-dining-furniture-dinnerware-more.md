# NEW dining furniture, dinnerware & more
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals editorial send for Pottery Barn's dining category — furniture, accent pieces, and what appears to be an "Indigo Blues" dinnerware/decor collection. The email works hard aesthetically: the lifestyle hero is warm and inviting, the multi-section layout covers hardwood furniture, curated accent pieces, and a color-story editorial block, and there's a personalized "Recommended Just For You" module below the fold. It's polished Pottery Barn in expected form.
- The core problem is the complete absence of a conversion hook. There's no price, no percentage off, no free-shipping threshold, no deadline — nothing that pushes a browser to act now. "NEW dining furniture" is a destination announcement, not an offer. Given that yesterday's sends included a $1,500 sweepstakes and a 70%+ sale email, the lack of any offer here makes this feel like a catalog drop rather than a reason to click.
- This is also the second Pottery Barn email today (the Chris Loves Julia collab landed earlier on 7/22), which compounds the fatigue from an already heavy week. The email earns its open on brand recognition alone; it struggles to earn the click.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** Warm, sun-lit dining room with natural wood and layered tabletop styling — the eye lands on the right place immediately.
- **"Indigo Blues" editorial block.** Blue-and-white dinnerware and ceramics get a named collection treatment; that's more editorial than the generic "Shop Now" grid approach.
- **"Recommended Just For You" module.** Visible personalization signal lower in the email; this is the right place to put it.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible across the full scroll.
- **Subject line names specific categories** (dining furniture, dinnerware) rather than hiding behind lifestyle language — better than the "effortlessly styled space" subject from 7/20.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere.** No price, no % off, no free shipping — nothing that creates a reason to buy today versus in a week.
- **Subject uses "& more" as a crutch.** "NEW dining furniture, dinnerware & more" trails off; "& more" is a signal there was no strong editorial north star.
- **CTAs appear to be generic category buttons.** Nothing drills to a specific hero product or collection page — the click goes to a category, not a moment.
- **Two emails in one day.** Combined with the CLJ collab send earlier on 7/22, this is aggressive cadence for a single day; subscribers who opened the morning send may tune out by the time this one arrives.
- **"Indigo Blues" collection buried mid-email.** If that's the actual editorial hook, it should be the hero — it's the most differentiated thing in this send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Elevate "Indigo Blues" to the hero.** The named collection is the most ownable moment here. Build the subject, preheader, and hero around it — a named collection drives more curiosity than "new dining furniture."
- 2. **Add a single anchoring offer.** Even free shipping on dining orders or "starting at $X" pricing in the hero would give the email a conversion rationale.
- 3. **Merge with the CLJ collab or shift cadence.** Two sends on the same day hurts both emails. If this was planned independently, it needs stronger differentiation from the morning CLJ send.
- 4. **Make at least one CTA product-specific.** Link a hero product (the specific round table in the lifestyle photo, the Indigo Blues serving bowl) rather than a category page.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The dining table, upgraded — new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New hardwood tables, blue-and-white ceramics, and accent picks for the dining room.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code inside `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
Four PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. They are inert in HTML context and will not execute, but this is a clear signal that template assembly is pulling from a server-side image-generation script without stripping its PHP preamble. Browsers and email clients will attempt to parse this as CSS and silently discard it, but it inflates payload and indicates a fragile build process.

**`<title>` is empty**
`<title></title>` — affects tab rendering in webmail clients (Gmail browser tab, Outlook Web).

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — prevents user-initiated zoom. This is an accessibility regression on mobile; WCAG 1.4.4 requires content to be resizable up to 200%.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not present**
The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured by the relay. If missing from the actual SMTP envelope (not just a relay artifact), Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the one-click unsubscribe button in their UI — increasing spam complaints and risking deliverability throttling under Google's 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not present**
RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed. This is required for Google and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (>5k/day threshold).

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via this relay. Cannot confirm authentication chain for `e.potterybarn.com` envelope domain. Should be verified directly against raw SMTP headers.

**`no-referrer` meta tag**
`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` — suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all outbound clicks. This is intentional for privacy but means landing page analytics cannot attribute sessions back to email as a channel without relying entirely on UTM parameters.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are present in the email body (standard footer pattern visible in template structure). No issues found there.
