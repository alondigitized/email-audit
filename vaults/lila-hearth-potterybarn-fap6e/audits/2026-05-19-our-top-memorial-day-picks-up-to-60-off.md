# Our top Memorial Day picks (up to 60% off!)
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the eighth Pottery Barn Memorial Day email in roughly four days — and it shows. The template is pixel-for-pixel identical to every prior send: red hero banner, "Extra 20% off" headline, category discount grid, "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product tiles, app/rewards/Affirm footer. The one creative distinction is the subject's "our top picks" curation angle, which mildly differentiates it from the prior "sale on sale" and "NEW deals" framings.
- The email itself executes cleanly. Visual hierarchy is correct, the 60% off headline discount is the strongest number they've surfaced in the campaign, and the personalization block remains one of their more effective recurring elements. Render is bug-free.
- The problem isn't this email — it's this email arriving as the eighth in four days with no structural change. A subscriber's delete-without-opening reflex is fully trained at this point. The "up to 60% off" subject works harder than most of the week's sends, but it's landing into an exhausted inbox.

## What's working

- **"Up to 60% off" in the subject is the biggest discount number of the entire campaign** — finally a concrete escalation signal rather than just "Extra 20% off" again.
- **Red hero banner delivers the offer in under 1 second** — visual hierarchy is crisp and the CTA button is unambiguous.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block** adds real perceived personalization and gives a reason to scroll beyond the category grid.
- **Explicit loyalty callout ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards")** rewards members without burying it.
- **Category discount grid is efficient** — Sofas, Bedding, Outdoor, Furniture all get a tile with a specific % off, making the email function as a useful deal index.

## What's weak

- **Day 8, same template** — the send structure is identical to the 5/18 "sale on sale" email, and that one was already flagged as fatigued.
- **No hero product** — the email leads with categories, not a single compelling item. "Our top picks" promises curation but the body doesn't deliver a featured product.
- **No explicit deadline date visible in the body** — Memorial Day implies Sunday, but a subscriber who has deleted 7 prior emails may not do the calendar math.
- **"SHOP EXTRA 20% OFF" CTA is generic** — it doesn't connect to the "top picks" curation premise established in the subject.
- **Affirm and app promo are recycled footer filler** — present in every send this week; zero new signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Introduce a single hero product** — pick one iconic item (a sofa, an outdoor sectional, a rug) that anchors "our top picks" to something real and link directly to its PDP. The subject earns the curation premise; the body should pay it off.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline** — "Sale ends Monday" anywhere in the body converts the implied Memorial Day urgency into a concrete trigger.
- 3. **Change the hero CTA to match the subject frame** — "Shop Our Memorial Day Picks" is more specific than "SHOP EXTRA 20% OFF" and continues the curation narrative.
- 4. **Surface the 60% off number as a category highlight in the hero** — it's in the subject but the hero banner still leads with "Extra 20% off." Let the 60% figure own the hero.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Furniture up to 60% off — our Memorial Day picks`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off: the pieces worth buying this weekend`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, outdoor, bedding — sale ends Monday.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Our editors' actual favorites, now at Memorial Day prices.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into a CSS `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` block in `<head>` contains raw PHP header-emission calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is server-side PHP accidentally pasted into client-side CSS. It is syntactically invalid CSS. Most clients silently discard it, but it is a template assembly defect that should be removed.

**38 images missing `alt` text**
Every content slice (`slices_01a.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, the footer credit card banner, and both open-pixel beacons carry no `alt` attribute. With images blocked (common in corporate/Outlook environments), the email renders as empty white space. Affected examples:
- `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/S26_Sale_MemorialDay_ZigZag2/slices_01a.jpg` — no `alt`
- `https://www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/ab/images/dp/wcm/202606/0011/farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg` — no `alt`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — no `alt`

**Three non-HTTPS image sources**
Will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook with default security settings:
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Open-pixel beacon is plain HTTP**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
Open-tracking pixel uses HTTP, meaning open-rate data will be under-counted in clients that block mixed content.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on iOS, a WCAG 1.4.4 failure for low-vision users.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied universally; removes OS-level font scaling for all elements.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no impact on rendering but degrades screen-reader context.
