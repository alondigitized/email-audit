# FREE design help in stores
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This breaks the run of near-identical "Early Black Friday, up to 70% off" sends. The subject and top of the email pivot to a different offer: free in-store design appointments, with a $3,000 gift card sweepstakes ("Design Crew Sweeps"). A tartan "Free Design Services" hero with a **Book a free appointment** button carries the pitch, and that is a real change of angle from the last week of sale-only blasts.
- The execution undercuts it. Below the hero the email falls back to the standard Black Friday sale grid (bedding, sofas, furniture, decor, pillows), a **Shop all sale** button, and a "Recommended just for you" block. That makes two competing messages. The sweepstakes banner, the appointment hero and the sale grid each want attention, and the long fine print at the bottom adds clutter.
- Verdict: a smart angle to break the monotony, but the email dilutes it by running the full sale template underneath.

## What's working

- The service-led hero is a real break from the 11/16–11/24 sale blasts, including yesterday's "Don't miss EARLY BLACK FRIDAY".
- The **Book a free appointment** button is dark burgundy, high-contrast and repeated, so the primary action is clear.
- The "what / where" copy ("In stores, virtually, or in your home") makes the service concrete and low-commitment.
- The plaid hero looks seasonal and on-brand without looking like a generic sale banner.
- The sale grid shows specific discounts per category (70%, 50%, 60%), so there is price motivation for anyone who scrolls.

## What's weak

- Three messages are stacked: sweepstakes, design appointment and sale. No single one wins.
- "Design Crew Sweeps" at the top competes with the hero for first attention, and the $3,000 gift card prize is not what the subject sells.
- "Choose a time—but spots go fast!" is vague urgency, with no date or deadline.
- The bottom third is heavy: an app promo, a credit card promo, an Affirm promo, a brand logo strip and dense legal fine print. The sweepstakes disclosure is hard to read.
- The "Recommended just for you" products (stockings, throw, dining table, robe, quilt) look generic and don't tie back to the design-help theme.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the sale grid and recommendations for this send, or shrink them to one slim "Shop the sale" strip. Let design help be the whole email, with one hero, one button and a short list of what you get.
- 2. Add a deadline and a stake: a concrete date for appointment availability, or a line like "Book by Friday and we'll hold your spot."
- 3. Show the service. Add one real room-makeover photo or a designer portrait to make "free design help" tangible.
- 4. Move the sweepstakes below the CTA, or make it a separate send. Right now it competes with the main offer.
- 5. Trim the footer promos (app, credit card, Affirm) to one.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud send (click.e.potterybarn.com, `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->`). It is a sliced-image layout with 53 opaque tracked links. The source has some real code defects: PHP text inside a `<style>` block, content before the DOCTYPE, and no accessible names on linked images. Several compliance checks can't be confirmed from the data given.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Opaque tracking only.** All 53 links use `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABp7Im...`, and the probe skipped them. Destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unverified.
- **Insecure image sources.** Two images load over `http://`:
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  
  These can be blocked or rewritten by clients, and they can trigger mixed-content warnings in webmail.
- **Third-party tracking pixels.** There are three:
  - `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?...`
  - `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/.../cat/1`
  - `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`
  
  These are ad-network retargeting beacons, not just open tracking, and none has alt text.
- **Stale asset reference.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a cross-brand dependency, and the 2022-dated banner is reused.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **PHP in CSS.** The second `<style>` block opens with `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store..."); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. This is leaked server-side code, not CSS. Parsers treat it as part of the next rule's selector, so the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule that follows is likely dropped.
- **Content before DOCTYPE.** `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot --> <!-- end ... -->` comes before `<!DOCTYPE>`. This can push some clients into quirks mode. The XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype is also used with deprecated attributes (`<center>`, `bgcolor`, `align`, `border`), so it doesn't validate.
- **Mobile CSS likely inert.** The media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`. In the visible source, no element carries `class="wrapper"` or `class="photo"`. The tables are fixed `width="650"`, and the slice images are `width="650"` with no `max-width`. This would cause horizontal scroll on mobile. The source is truncated, so check the rest.
- **Missing alt text.** QA flags about 45 images. Most are `slices_NN.jpg` in the hero, product and footer bands. The HTML shows `alt=""` on `slices_01`–`03`, and each of these sits alone inside an `<a>`. The links therefore have no accessible name, and with images blocked the email is blank. The product images (e.g. `classic-velvet-stockings-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-125-l.jpg`) have the same problem.
- **Tracking pixels without alt or dimensions.** The pixels (`lb`, `1`, `svopn…`, `coreemail.html`) have no alt text. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`.
- **Preheader.** It is present (`Book an appt for a chance to win $3,000`), padded correctly, and hidden with `mso-hide:all`. No issues.
- **Web font.** The Montserrat link is a `<link>` to Google Fonts. Many clients strip it, so a fallback stack is needed. The visible body text uses Verdana, so this is low impact.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens or AMPscript (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the truncated source.
- The recipient address is resolved into two URLs: `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This puts plaintext PII in query strings, and any proxy, cache or log along the way will record it. The `coreemail.html` request is also an `<img>` with no alt.
- The empty `<!-- begin/end dynamic ampScript slot -->` comments are left in the delivered HTML. This is cosmetic and exposes the platform internals.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058).** Both are not found in the captured headers. The QA tool says the relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed, not a confirmed defect**. Verify against the raw message.
- **Authentication-Results.** It is absent, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The cause is likely the relay.
- **Body unsubscribe link and physical address.** Neither is visible in the truncated source, so CAN-SPAM compliance can't be assessed from this data. The footer slices (`Email-Footer/slices_01–18.jpg`) are images with no alt text. If the unsubscribe link and address are image-only, they are unreadable with images off.
- **Sender.** The From address `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` is on the sending subdomain, and the display name matches the content. No issues.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters.** None can be verified, because all links are wrapped in `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…`. The only readable destination parameters are in a commented-out block (`brandCode`, `marketingChannelCode`, `external_id`, `cm_ite`), which is not live.
- **Campaign and landing alignment.** Campaign folder `1124_TUE_AM2_EBF_DC_TIHT` does not map to a visible destination. Landing pages for the "free design help in stores" appointment CTA couldn't be checked, and the probe skipped these links.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block, and move the AMPscript comments after the DOCTYPE (or remove them).
2. Add meaningful `alt` text to every linked slice and product image. Footer slices that carry the unsubscribe link or address need alt text or a text-based fallback.
3. Add `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` to tracking pixels. Stop putting the raw email address in `coreemail.html` and `sv_sveme`, and use a hashed or subscriber-key token.
4. Switch the two `http://edm.potterybarn.com` images to `https://`. Replace the cross-brand `edm.westelm.com` spacer and the 2022 banner with current assets.
5. Add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"` to the tables and images, or rewrite the media query. Also set `max-width:100%; height:auto` on the slice images.
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the DKIM/SPF/DMARC results from raw headers (not through the relay). Confirm a text unsubscribe link and a postal address are in the footer.
7. Resolve several `click.e` links by following the redirects, to confirm the destinations return 200 and carry the expected UTM parameters.
