# New deals (with EXTRA 20% off) + The right bath vanity for your space (& routine)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 5 of Pottery Barn's Memorial Day campaign, and the creative team has pivoted to a dedicated "Bathroom Vanities" send — the first category-specific deep-dive of the run. The structure is clean: Memorial Day offer banner → vanity sub-categories (Double, Single, Powder Room) → "Recommended Just for You" block → app footer. For the subscriber actively shopping a vanity, this is the most useful email Pottery Barn has sent all week. For everyone else, it's a hard category miss.
- The subject line is the single largest liability here. At 81 characters, it combines two distinct value propositions ("New deals with EXTRA 20% off" + "The right bath vanity for your space & routine") with a parenthetical tagline that will truncate on every major mobile client. "& routine" is the kind of editorial language that sounds good in a strategy deck and reads as noise in an inbox. The hero banner inside the email actually says "Save 20% off" — not "Extra 20% off" — introducing a subtle inconsistency that erodes confidence for attentive subscribers.
- The "Recommended Just for You" personalization block returns but appears to surface outdoor furniture, which breaks the bathroom narrative the rest of the email carefully builds. Compared to yesterday's outdoor-living send (which nailed thematic coherence), this one has a seam showing.

## What's working

- **Category-specific structure** — the three-way split (Double / Single / Powder Room Vanity) gives clear shopping intent and helps segment browsers by real decision criteria.
- **Memorial Day sale banner in the hero** — the promotion is anchored to the season immediately; no hunting for the offer.
- **SHOP NOW CTA** is visible above the fold without any friction.
- **Product photography** is high quality and consistent with Pottery Barn's aspirational aesthetic throughout.
- **"Recommended Just for You" block** adds personalization depth even if the product category alignment is off.

## What's weak

- **81-character subject line will truncate on mobile** — the second half ("The right bath vanity for your space (& routine)") is invisible to most recipients.
- **"& routine" is dead copy** — adds no information, no urgency, no reason to click.
- **Hero body says "Save 20% off" while subject says "EXTRA 20% off"** — the wording mismatch is small but noticeable to anyone paying attention across 5 days of this campaign.
- **No explicit deadline in the body** — Memorial Day implies urgency but "Ends Monday" or a countdown would convert browsers who aren't committed yet.
- **"Recommended Just for You" block appears to show outdoor/patio products**, breaking the bathroom-focused editorial thread the rest of the email builds.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject in half — pick one hook.** The dual-proposition structure is killing open rate on mobile. "Bathroom vanities with extra 20% off" is the entire subject you need.
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline line to the hero.** "Sale ends Monday, May 26" below the CTA button would lift urgency materially for the subscriber who's been on the fence for 3 days.
- 3. **Change CTA copy to category-specific verbs** — "Shop Double Vanities" / "Shop Single Vanities" / "Shop Powder Room Vanities" instead of generic "Shop Now" under each grid tile.
- 4. **Align the "Recommended Just for You" block to bath/bath accessories**, not outdoor furniture — the tonal whiplash undercuts the editorial investment in the bathroom theme.
- 5. **Remove "& routine"** from any future subject line test; replace with a concrete detail ("from $699," "free shipping," a specific material/finish).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bathroom vanities — extra 20% off this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day vanity upgrade (extra 20% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Double, single, and powder-room sizes — sale ends Monday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New deals added: shop all vanity styles now`

header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");

PHP `header()` calls embedded inside a `<style>` tag — these are inert in email but indicate a template/copy-paste error from a server-side asset. No rendering impact but signals a broken build process.
