# Summer deals ☀️ UP TO 70% OFF + New arrivals for a coastal cottage look
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn running its by-now-familiar compound subject formula: sale hook on the left of the "+", editorial hook on the right. The July 13 evening send did exactly this ("Up to 70% off is happening now + Decor details that make all the difference"), and the July 12 double-send repeated it twice in one day. This email is structurally indistinguishable from those — "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" in the hero, then a large "NEW ARRIVAL" lifestyle block underneath for the coastal cottage pitch, then a personalized product grid, then another lifestyle scene in the lower body.
- The new-arrivals section actually earns its keep here — the hero image is warm and editorial, the body copy gestures at an aspirational heritage aesthetic, and the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" bed/furniture grid is the most click-worthy module in the email. But the dual-story structure means neither story dominates. You scroll down expecting sale prices and get editorial mood; you scroll further expecting curated picks and get another SHOP NOW dead-end.
- The cadence is the real problem. This is somewhere around the seventh Pottery Barn email in four days. The "Summer Sale" anchor is now wallpaper.

## What's working

- **"Recommended Just for You" module is the strongest element** — named products (Seadrift Clyde Bed, Pearson Bed) with thumbnail images create actual click intent that the generic sale hero cannot.
- **Heritage/coastal interior hero image is on-brand and visually clean** — warm wood tones, layered textiles, and the "turn a house into a heritage home" copy all reinforce the coastal cottage angle from the subject.
- **Loyalty footer is present** — "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" and Affirm financing give budget-conscious shoppers an additional hook without cluttering the main body.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, layout holds, no overlapping text or broken gaps detected.

## What's weak

- **Two competing heroes, neither wins** — the sale banner and the new-arrivals lifestyle block split the email's purpose; a reader can't tell what action they're being asked to take first.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the CTA everywhere** — every section resolves to the same generic verb, including the new arrivals, the personalized recommendations, and the outdoor lifestyle image. No specificity.
- **Cadence fatigue is severe** — this is effectively the same email as the July 13 evening send; after seven sends in four days with the same sale depth and the same compound formula, the open is earned on brand recognition alone, not creative.
- **No deadline on the Summer Sale** — "Up to 70% off" without a close date removes urgency and trains subscribers to assume the sale never ends.
- **New arrivals and sale items are never cross-referenced** — is the coastal cottage collection on sale? Are the "Recommended" beds part of the 70% off event? The email doesn't say, which undercuts both stories.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and commit.** Either run a sale email (with specific price-drop examples in the grid) or run the new-arrivals editorial. The compound formula has been used on July 10, 11, 12 (twice), and 13 — it's exhausted. A clean single-focus send would stand out in the inbox right now.
- 2. **Add a close date to the sale.** Even "through Sunday" or "ends July 16" would create the urgency that "Summer Sale" alone cannot.
- 3. **Make the personalized product grid the hero.** Lead with the Seadrift/Pearson beds and their actual discounted prices. That's the most concrete, click-driving asset in this email and it's buried below the fold.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTAs to match the section.** "Shop the Coastal Cottage Edit," "See Beds on Sale," "Explore New Arrivals" — anything but four consecutive "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Rest the sale-email cadence for a day.** The July 13 new-arrivals-only send (the "FLORALS & PATTERNS" email) scored 8/10 precisely because it broke the pattern. One more day off from the sale anchor would reset subscriber attention.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: coastal cottage arrivals — and yes, up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Heritage home vibes just landed (+ summer sale ends Sunday)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Warm wood, layered textiles, and the beds that tie it all together`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your recommended picks — now at summer sale pricing`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Summer Deals / Coastal Cottage" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links (403), three HTTP-only image sources that will be blocked or downgraded by modern email clients, a PHP server-side code fragment leaked into a CSS `<style>` block, and pervasive missing alt text across 38+ images. Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, DKIM/SPF) were not observable via the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Credit card apply/buy CTA returns 403**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY...`
The Key Rewards non-member header banner link is dead. This is a revenue-bearing CTA; 403 means the Certona/Kibo personalization endpoint rejected the request at probe time. Needs resolution before send or the entire header CTA goes nowhere.

**FAIL — Cross-brand West Elm spacer image returns 403**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset appears in a Pottery Barn email. Both are Williams-Sonoma brands sharing ESP infrastructure, but the asset is on the wrong brand's domain, is HTTP-only, and is 403ing. This is a stale template fragment that was never updated for Pottery Barn.

**INFO — 24 click-tracking links skipped**
All route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` (Oracle Responsys), standard. Not probed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP server-side code leaked into `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls inside the final `<style>` tag. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it — no rendering breakage — but it exposes internal server-side templating details and indicates a broken build pipeline where a PHP snippet was pasted or included without rendering.

**38+ images missing `alt` text**
Affects nearly every content image: hero banners (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), product grid slices (`slices_02.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), footer slices, and several product PDPs. Email clients with images off (common in B2B environments) will render blank blocks. Accessibility for screen readers is completely broken across the body.

**3 image sources over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
All will be blocked or flagged by clients enforcing HTTPS:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (wrong brand, also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` (footer Key CC banner)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` (2022-era asset path — likely stale)

**Open pixel exposes recipient email address in plain-text URL**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
The Selligent open-tracking pixel passes the recipient's email address as a URL query param over HTTP. This is both an HTTP issue (blocked by clients) and a privacy concern — the address is visible in server logs, proxies, and referrer chains.

**Campaign/season naming mismatch in asset filename**
`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg` — the `F26` prefix (Fall 2026) in a Summer sale email suggests a recycled or mis-named asset. Not a functional break, but a production hygiene signal.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no impact on email rendering, but some clients and preview tools surface this.
