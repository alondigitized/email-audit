# On sale & ready to ship!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's inventory-clearance move — "in stock, delivered in 1-4 weeks" is the tell, aimed at shoppers who've been burned by furniture backorders. It's structurally similar to the July 24 "70% OFF + NEW designs" send that tried to do two things at once, except this one is worse about it: the hero banner promises "Up to 70% off," but the very next section pivots to a delivery-speed pitch ("Save on Furniture & More / Delivered in 1-4 weeks") with no percentage or dollar figure anywhere near it, then a wall of eight product tiles with no prices, then "Discover New Arrivals" (a non-sale message), then a second sale banner for outdoor at a different discount (60%). Three separate offers stacked in one email dilutes all three.
- The subject "On sale & ready to ship!" is honest but generic — it's the kind of subject that blends into a week of Pottery Barn sends rather than standing out. Visually the email is clean and on-brand, but there's no single, clear moment where the eye lands on one offer and one CTA. Judgment: competent execution, muddled strategy — a 5.

## What's working

- Hero banner is legible and immediate: "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" with a single "SHOP NOW" CTA above the fold.
- "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" is a genuinely differentiated, trust-building claim for furniture shoppers tired of backorders — a real product-detail signal.
- The category grid (Bedding, Outdoor, Living Room, Rugs, etc.) is clean and scannable, giving multiple entry points into the sale.
- "Recommended Just for You" product tiles look personalized and the images are clean, well-lit product shots with no render bugs.
- Consistent brand footer with app download, credit card rewards (10% back), and social links — standard but well-executed.

## What's weak

- No price or percentage is shown anywhere in the "Recommended Just for You" product grid — eight products, zero prices, which kills purchase intent right at the point of highest specificity.
- Three competing offers in one email (70% sale, new arrivals, 60% off outdoor) split attention instead of committing to one, echoing the exact critique from the July 24 "UP TO 70% OFF + NEW designs" send that also tried to straddle two messages.
- "Discover New Arrivals" sits directly between two sale pushes with no discount attached — it reads like a non-sequitur in a discount-heavy email rather than a deliberate palate-cleanser.
- Subject line "On sale & ready to ship!" doesn't mention the 70% figure that's the actual hero headline — a mismatch between what gets you to open and what you see first.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this down to one offer. Either lead fully with "Up to 70% off + ships in 1-4 weeks" and drop the New Arrivals and Outdoor sections into a separate send, or split into two focused emails.
- 2. Add prices/discount badges to every tile in "Recommended Just for You" — an unpriced product grid this deep into a sale email is a wasted click.
- 3. Match the subject to the hero: put the 70%-off number in the subject so open and hero content align.
- 4. Move "Delivered in 1-4 weeks" higher and make it a badge on individual product tiles, not just the hero — it's the most differentiated claim in the email and it's underused.
- 5. If outdoor at 60% off has to stay, give it its own module treatment (separate from the furniture 70% number) so shoppers don't have to reconcile two different discount tiers in one scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off furniture — ships in 1-4 weeks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `In stock now: up to 70% off, no backorders`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, beds & rugs on sale — most ship within a month`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Skip the wait: sale furniture that's actually in stock`

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "On sale & ready to ship!"

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on a standard ESP/SFMC template (click.e.potterybarn.com redirects, AMPscript-free HTML) with pervasive missing `alt` attributes, several plaintext-HTTP image/tracking calls, and one clear code defect — leaked server-side PHP `header()` calls sitting inside a `<style>` block.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All primary CTAs route through the SFMC click-tracking domain, e.g. `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5NDl9AAsAAAAAA0SGBm8MI6ihpudLrQpVLO9qImkFn9nNTlei1WWjUFyuJKRPO6vmwOqHFxhhJ7vRlkrZI-lonOPBXy5_2jUHeeZod48lYL-yZdw9yBDV1Ex9RUa1`. QA skipped HTTP probing on 52 such links (opaque `qs=` payload), so final landing destinations are **unverified**.
- Three additional tracking/telemetry calls present:
  - `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` — third-party (Turn/Amobee) behavioral pixel.
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — open-tracking pixel served over **plaintext HTTP** and carrying the recipient's full email address as a raw query parameter (see §5).
  - `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/d86e84d7-6da2-4ab9-b002-18d9504dd2a3?mi_mid=019fa003-5200-7000-b0a5-38382c508131&cp_tp...` — additional open-tracking beacon.
- No broken/malformed `href` values found in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **41 images missing `alt` text** across hero banners, product photos, and footer slices (full list in QA output) — screen readers announce these as filename-only or blank; also degrades the fallback experience when images are blocked.
- **Mixed content**: 4 images load over `http://` instead of `https://`, which many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy) will block or flag:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...` (tracking pixel, also see §2/§5)
- **Zoom disabled**: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` sets `maximum-scale=1`, preventing pinch-to-zoom — fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) on clients that honor this meta tag.
- **Empty `<title>` tag** (`<title></title>`) — no fallback label for inbox previews/tab titles in webmail rendering.
- **Leaked server-side code in `<style>` block**: the CSS contains literal PHP output that was never meant to render client-side:
  ```
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ```
  This is inert as CSS (no selector/braces), but indicates a template-generation bug where a PHP image-serving snippet was concatenated into the stylesheet include.
- Dead/commented-out code present (two unused `viewport` meta variants, a commented `<table>` block) — not render-affecting, but template hygiene issue.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header: **not present** in the captured headers — one-click unsubscribe cannot be confirmed at the header level (QA WARN).
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): **not present** — one-click unsubscribe (POST) not confirmed (QA WARN).
- `Authentication-Results` header: **not present**, so SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this capture (QA WARN). Note both header warnings may reflect the AgentMail relay not surfacing/forwarding these headers rather than the sender omitting them — cannot fully distinguish from this data.
- In-body unsubscribe link/footer not visible in the truncated HTML provided, so CAN-SPAM footer compliance (physical address, functional unsubscribe link) could not be verified from this excerpt.
- **PII-in-cleartext**: the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel transmits the recipient's full email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) over unencrypted HTTP — exposed to any on-path observer (network intermediary, proxy log). This is a data-handling issue independent of CAN-SPAM.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- All product/CTA links are wrapped in the opaque SFMC redirect (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`); no plain `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` parameters are visible on any link in this source, since tracking is handled via the `qs=` token rather than standard UTM query params.
- Because the redirect destinations were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (52 links), landing-page alignment (i.e., does each CTA land on the correct PDP/category page) **could not be verified** in this pass.
- Two `potterybarn.com` product image links resolve to direct CDN paths (e.g. `.../wcm/202625/0012/cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`) rather than tracked redirects — consistent with standard PDP image hosting, no anomaly.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive (or explicitly empty `alt=""` for purely decorative) attributes to all 41 flagged images to satisfy accessibility requirements and improve image-blocked fallback rendering.
2. Migrate the 4 `http://` image/pixel sources to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking in Outlook/Gmail.
3. Serve the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel over HTTPS to stop transmitting recipient email addresses in cleartext.
4. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag to restore pinch-zoom for accessibility.
5. Investigate and strip the leaked PHP `header()` snippet from the `<style>` block — indicates a broken include in the email template pipeline.
6. Confirm with the ESP/relay why `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results` headers weren't captured — verify directly against raw SMTP headers rather than the AgentMail-relayed copy before concluding they're absent.
7. Re-run link verification with redirect-following enabled on `click.e.potterybarn.com` to confirm the 52 skipped links land on matching, live landing pages.
