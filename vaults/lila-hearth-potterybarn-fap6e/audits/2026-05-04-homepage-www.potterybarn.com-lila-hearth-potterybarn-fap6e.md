## What's working

- **The lifestyle photo behind the modal** — a bright, classically styled sofa vignette with floral and neutral pillows tells me immediately this brand aligns with my taste
- **"Find out first: New arrivals, exclusive savings & more."** — the headline copy is aspirational and matches how I think about shopping here (getting the new drop before it sells out)
- **Brand recognition** — the POTTERY BARN wordmark centered over the image is confident and clean, no clutter
- **Williams-Sonoma brand mention in fine print** — even buried, it signals this is the same family as my rewards account, which increases trust

## What's weak

- **The modal as a full homepage takeover** — I came here to look at furniture for my lake house and I'm immediately hit with a data-capture wall; this is the definition of friction before value
- **"Exclusive savings" with zero specifics** — if you want my phone number, show me what I'm actually getting. "10% off your next order" would have me typing immediately
- **No loyalty/rewards CTA** — I have a Williams-Sonoma rewards account. This modal doesn't acknowledge me at all; it treats me like a cold lead
- **Dense SMS legal text** — four lines of gray fine print about prerecorded voice messages, wireless carrier liability, and STOP commands before I've seen a single product feels adversarial
- **Navigation is almost completely hidden** — the top nav (Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm...) is barely visible as a thin strip above the modal; I can't see Furniture, Bedding, or anything relevant to my visit

## Recommendations

- **Surface a member-specific hook** — if I'm cookie'd as a Williams-Sonoma rewards user, swap the generic signup modal for a "Welcome back — here's 10% off for Key Rewards members" overlay with a Shop Now CTA
- **Add a specific offer to the modal headline** — change "exclusive savings & more" to "15% off your first order + early access to new arrivals" so the value exchange is concrete and worth my phone number
- **Reduce the SMS fine print** — collapse the legal text under a "Terms apply" link; the current wall of legalese is the main visual reason to hit the X button immediately
- **Show the homepage in 3 seconds** — implement a 3-second delay or scroll-triggered modal so I can at least see the hero campaign before being asked to sign up
