# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-07-11
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- I land on Pottery Barn and the very first thing I get is a modal blocking the entire page — before I've even seen a single product, a single offer, a single reason to stay. The brand behind it is clearly high-end lifestyle home (that dreamy sectional barely visible behind the overlay absolutely speaks to me), but the page is leading with email capture, not with inspiration. That's backwards for a discovery-driven shopper like me.

## What's working

- **The lifestyle image behind the modal** — even partially obscured, that warm sectional with layered rugs and soft lamps is exactly the second-home vibe I'm shopping for. That image, front and center, would stop my scroll.
- **Headline copy** — "Find out first: New arrivals, exclusive savings & more" is clean and millennial-appropriate. Not too salesy, appeals to the FOMO I definitely have around catalog drops.
- **Close button is visible** — the X is clearly placed; I don't feel trapped.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand mention in fine print** — as a WS rewards member, that name registers as a trust signal even buried in legalese.

## What's weak

- **The modal fires before I've seen anything** — I haven't had a single reason to give Pottery Barn my email yet. I need to fall in love with the room first.
- **Zero loyalty hook** — I'm already in the Williams-Sonoma ecosystem. Show me a "Rewards members get early access" callout and I'd fill this out immediately. Instead it treats me like a stranger.
- **No offer specificity** — "Exclusive savings" is meaningless without a number. "15% off your first order" would convert me. This doesn't.
- **The navigation bar shows Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm** — those aren't my categories, and that's the only nav visible. My department (furniture, bedding, outdoor) is invisible from where I'm standing.
- **Dense legal fine print** — three paragraphs of SMS consent legalese before the "Sign Up Now" button creates friction and feels aggressive for a luxury home brand.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the modal by 10–15 seconds** (or trigger on exit intent) so I can engage with the actual homepage first — let the product earn the email ask.
- **Add a rewards-member recognition line** to the modal headline: "Already a Key Rewards member? Sign up and link your account for early access." One sentence that makes me feel seen.
- **Swap vague "exclusive savings" for a concrete hook** — even "Be first to shop new arrivals + get 15% off" gives me a real reason to hand over my email.
- **Surface the adult furniture/decor nav** above or below the modal so I can see where I'm going even while the popup is open — right now the only nav I see is Kids/Teen/Dorm.
