# ☀️ Sunny savings UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's June 22 send — their sixth or seventh touchpoint in a rolling Summer Sale blitz that started on June 18. The email opens with the exact same creative frame they've leaned on all week: "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" in large hero type, a category nav grid, and a "SHOP ALL SALE" CTA. Nothing has changed structurally from the June 21 launch email. The only meaningful addition is a "Recommended Just For You" personalized product grid, which is actually the most interesting thing in the send — but it gets buried below-the-fold.
- The subject emoji (☀️) is a cosmetic variation on a template that has now been sent near-identically for five consecutive days. "Sunny savings" is weaker than "On sale & ready-to-ship!" (June 20) or the nightstand-specific subject from June 21 — it adds nothing concrete. The brand is clearly in saturation mode, repeating the 70% hook with diminishing originality.
- The personalized product tiles ("Farmhouse Platform Bed," "Standard Curtain Rod & Wall Bracket") are a real lift versus prior sends, but the rendering at thumbnail size makes them nearly unreadable. Overall: a forgettable volume send that cannibalizes the goodwill built by stronger executions earlier in the week.

## What's working

- **Personalization grid is present** — "Recommended Just For You" product tiles represent the first explicit behavioral signal in this batch; a clear upgrade over purely category-level promotion.
- **Category nav is complete** — Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Décor, Lighting, Pillows & Throws, Rugs, Tabletop, Bath — covers the full store and aids browsing.
- **"Free Design Services" callout** is prominent mid-scroll, consistent with the June 19 service-push send and adds a non-discount reason to engage.
- **Rewards / Affirm / App callouts** in the footer create a clear benefits stack for loyalty members scanning below the fold.

## What's weak

- **Identical hero to June 21** — "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" is word-for-word the same creative used in the launch email five days ago; repeat openers are fatiguing at this cadence.
- **Subject is the weakest in the batch** — "☀️ Sunny savings UP TO 70% OFF" is vaguer than June 20's "On sale & ready-to-ship!" and June 21's nightstand-focused subject; the emoji decoration doesn't compensate for the lack of specificity.
- **Product tiles are too small to be compelling** — the recommended products section renders at a scale where product names, prices, and details are barely legible in the screenshot; a real mobile user would see thumbnails without context.
- **No time-bound urgency anywhere visible** — no "Ends Sunday," "48 hours left," or countdown; the offer feels open-ended and therefore non-urgent given how long this sale has run.
- **Dense legal footer competes visually** — the Affirm disclosure and unsubscribe block consume a significant chunk of scroll real estate at this render size.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to a specific product or category** — instead of repeating "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" for the sixth time, lead with one hero product from the personalized grid (e.g., "Your saved Farmhouse Bed is on sale now") to make the email feel earned.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the subject and hero** — "Ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" would immediately differentiate this from the identical sends earlier in the week and trigger genuine urgency.
- 3. **Surface price on the product tiles** — even if small, showing "$699 → $419" on the recommended products would dramatically lift click intent; the current render gives no price signal.
- 4. **Consolidate the footer** — Rewards, App, and Affirm blocks each deserve more visual weight or should be collapsed; they read as clutter at this cadence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your picks are up to 70% off — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Farmhouse Bed, outdoor rugs + more: Summer Sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Items in your style — now marked down. Sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday: shop the styles picked for you at up to 70% off.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Sunny Savings UP TO 70% OFF"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one broken cross-brand asset (403), three HTTP image sources that will be blocked by modern clients, and PHP template code leaked verbatim into a `<style>` block. Authentication headers are absent from the relay capture, and pervasive missing alt text affects every product image.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Cross-brand broken asset**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns 403. This is a West Elm CDN domain referenced inside a Pottery Barn email — likely a copy-paste template artifact. Will render as a broken image.

**[WARN] 25 tracking links unverified**
All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects; HTTP probing was skipped. Destination URL health and UTM integrity cannot be confirmed from this audit pass alone.

**[WARN] Open-pixel URL is HTTP and exposes recipient address in plaintext**
`http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
— unencrypted transport for a URL containing the recipient email. Most clients will block the HTTP pixel anyway; the exposure is a secondary concern.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP header code leaked into `<style>` block**
The following appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template/CMS artifact — PHP server-side code that was never executed. Some email clients will render it as visible text.

**[FAIL] 43 images missing `alt` text**
Every product slice image and all footer images lack `alt` attributes. Affected examples:
- `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg` (main content)
- All 18 `Email-Footer/slices_*.jpg`
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.

Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, Gmail app with data saver) will produce a blank email with no scannable content.

**[WARN] Three image sources are HTTP — will be blocked**
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content HTTP images.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta blocks accessibility zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from pinching to zoom on iOS/Android.

**[WARN] Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — no content. Screen readers announce this as the document title in some clients.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens visible in the provided source. The open-pixel confirms email-level personalization is being injected at send time (`sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`), so the merge pipeline is functioning. No issues found beyond the HTTP exposure noted above.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
Required by CAN-SPAM and enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5k/day). May be set at the ESP level and stripped by the AgentMail relay — needs verification at the raw MTA layer, not just relay headers.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe is now mandatory for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 requirements. If the ESP is injecting it, confirm it survives the relay.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` absent**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication chain should be verified at the MTA hop before AgentMail.

**[WARN] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`**
Suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound link clicks. If landing pages rely on `document.referrer` for attribution or A/B test targeting, this will break it. Likely intentional for privacy, but worth confirming it's deliberate.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 25 CTAs go through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects — UTM parameters on final destination URLs are not visible in source and were not probed. The `no-referrer` meta (noted above) means server-side Referer-based attribution on potterybarn.com will not receive the email signal. If PB's analytics rely solely on UTM params in the redirect chain this is acceptable; if any Referer-based logic exists it will see no referrer from these clicks.
