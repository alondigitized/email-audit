# Must-see dining chairs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn doing what it does best: a clean, single-category send that actually delivers on its subject line. "Must-see dining chairs" opens straight into a hero — "Made for Gathering" — styled with three chair types in one shot, then cascades into upholstered, wood, and bench sub-categories each with their own "SHOP NOW." No bait-and-switch, no unrelated detours until the recommendation modules kick in lower down.
- Where it loses points: the one concrete offer ("Up to 60% off," Warehouse Sale) sits in a thin top banner disconnected from the chair narrative below it — it reads like a leftover promo bolted onto a product-launch email rather than a chair-specific deal. And every single CTA in the email, all seven of them, says "SHOP NOW" — zero differentiation between "shop chairs," "shop benches," or "shop new arrivals."
- Compared to 2026-08-06's "ON NOW✨ The Warehouse Sale" (7/10, single clean offer, one CTA) and 2026-08-05's "Just in! Our new digital catalog" (8/10, weakest differentiation), this one lands in between — it's product-focused and well organized, but this is now the third dining/home-category send in four days, and a subscriber will start feeling the repetition.

## What's working

- Subject-to-hero match is tight: "Must-see dining chairs" → "new dining chairs / Made for Gathering," no bait-and-switch.
- Clear taxonomy: Upholstered, Wood, and Bench dining chairs are visually segmented with their own room shot and CTA, easy to scan.
- Individual product cards (Montclair, Abbey, Cline, Windsor) give a real click target beyond just "shop the collection."
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is consistent Pottery Barn styling top to bottom.
- Loyalty/financing stack (10% rewards card, Affirm) is present for shoppers who care about that.

## What's weak

- The only stated offer, "Up to 60% off," lives in a generic top banner unrelated to dining chairs — it's not clear if chairs are even included, which undercuts the "must-see" urgency.
- Every CTA button reads "SHOP NOW" — seven identical, generic labels with zero product-specific copy.
- No fabric, dimension, or material detail on any chair — a "must-see" chair email skips exactly the details someone comparing chairs would want.
- Cadence fatigue: this is the third furniture/dining-adjacent Pottery Barn send this week per the brand history (warehouse sale 08-06, catalog 08-05, cabinets 08-06) — nothing here signals it's meaningfully different from those.
- Long, unfocused tail: after the dining content, it pivots to bedding, rugs, a duvet cover, then Halloween decor, then a credit card pitch — the email sprawls well past its stated subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. Tie the offer directly to the hero — either state a chair-specific discount ("Dining chairs, up to 30% off") or drop the disconnected Warehouse Sale banner so the email isn't sending two competing offers.
- 2. Differentiate the CTA copy per section: "Shop Upholstered Chairs," "Shop Wood Chairs," "Shop Benches" instead of seven identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 3. Add one material/detail callout per product card (e.g., "performance velvet," "solid mango wood") — this is the missing piece for a "must-see" comparison email.
- 4. Trim the bottom half — move bedding/rugs/Halloween into a separate send; a dining-chair email shouldn't end in Halloween decor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dining chairs that make every seat count`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: upholstered, wood & bench dining chairs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ladder-backed, slipcovered, or tufted — find your match.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three chair styles, one collection worth a seat.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Must-see dining chairs"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional MSO/Outlook-hardened template with heavy tracking instrumentation; it passes basic rendering checks but has notable accessibility gaps, mixed-content (non-HTTPS) assets, leaked server-side code in a `<style>` block, and missing authentication/list-unsubscribe headers at the relay level.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Primary CTA/logo link routes through a click-tracking redirect: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...` (opaque encoded payload, not independently verifiable).
- Multiple tracking/open pixels present:
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — **served over plain HTTP**, and carries the recipient's full email address as a plaintext query parameter.
  - `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (third-party ad-tracking pixel, Turn/Amobee).
  - `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/73c8a294-180e-4b0d-8c25-172084d93c44?mi_mid=019fd9f2-dd00-7000-823a-6bb1813a67cd&cp_tp` (open-tracking beacon).
- 61 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (expected for redirect domains — not a defect, just unverified reachability).
- `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` is set, which mitigates (but does not eliminate) leakage of the email-address-bearing URLs above via the `Referer` header on outbound clicks.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **43 images missing `alt` text**, including all product/lifestyle banners (`F26_TOP_WarehouseSale.jpg`, `slices_01–18.jpg` in two different modules, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, etc.) and functional images (footer, nav). This fails WCAG image-alternative requirements and degrades the experience for screen-reader users and image-blocked inboxes.
- **4 assets served over plain HTTP** (mixed content, may be blocked/flagged by mail clients and browsers):
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/...` (tracking pixel, noted above)
- Cross-brand asset reference: a West Elm domain (`edm.westelm.com`) spacer image is loaded inside a Pottery Barn email — likely a stale/shared template artifact from a common ESP account, not a functional bug but worth flagging to the template owner.
- **Leaked server-side code in the `<style>` block**: literal PHP statements — `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...")` — appear as inert text inside `<style type="text/css">` in the `<head>`. This is invalid CSS (browsers will ignore it, so no rendering impact), but it indicates a template-generation error where raw PHP from an image-proxy script leaked into the compiled HTML. Recommend flagging to the ESP/template build pipeline since it exposes backend implementation details.
- Standard MSO/Outlook conditional comments, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and responsive `@media` breakpoints (480px/650px/700px) are correctly present.

### 7. Recommendations

2. Add `alt` attributes to all 43 flagged images, prioritizing the nav/logo and CTA banners for screen-reader and image-blocked rendering.
3. Migrate the 4 HTTP-only asset URLs to HTTPS, especially the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel, which also currently exposes the recipient's email address unencrypted.
4. Remove the leaked PHP `header()` statements from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — report to the template build pipeline as a code-generation defect.
5. Investigate the stray `edm.westelm.com` cross-brand asset reference for template hygiene.
6. Consider adding standard UTM parameters alongside the proprietary tracking scheme if GA/GA4-based attribution is needed for this campaign.
