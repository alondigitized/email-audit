# Our Holiday Headquarters is OPEN
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn shifting from single-category pushes into a full seasonal hub, and it's the strongest structural idea I've seen from them in this run — one email, three shops (Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas), each with its own hero and CTA. It solves the cadence problem the brand history has been flagging: instead of another quilts crawl, this reads as an actual event ("Holiday Headquarters is OPEN").
- Execution is clean but conservative — three near-identical hero-image-plus-"SHOP NOW" blocks stacked vertically, followed by a generic "Recommended Just For You" grid that dilutes the headline idea. The Christmas stocking shot is genuinely charming and belongs higher up, not buried third.
- Judgment: a smart concept, competently built, that doesn't fully cash in its own premise — worth a 6.

## What's working

- Clear three-way segmentation (Halloween / Thanksgiving / Christmas) gives readers an obvious reason to keep scrolling regardless of which holiday they care about most.
- The Thanksgiving tablescape hero is well-styled and photographs the product in full context (plates, linens, florals together).
- The Christmas stockings-on-mantel shot is the most emotionally warm image in the send — cozy, specific, on-season.
- Consistent "SHOP NOW" CTA styling under each hero keeps the scanning pattern predictable.
- Top nav bar ("Discover 1400+ New Arrivals" / "Shop In-Stock Furniture") gives an escape hatch for browsers not looking for holiday decor.

## What's weak

- All three hero sections are visually identical in weight and treatment — nothing tells the eye which holiday is most urgent or current (it's late September, Halloween should visually lead, not just be first).
- No concrete offer anywhere in the top fold — no %, no $, no free shipping — just "shop early... before pieces sell out," which is vague urgency, not real urgency.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid feels bolted on and generic (curtain rods, a rug, a dining table) — it breaks the holiday-hub narrative right after building it.
- Subject promises a "headquarters" but the layout is just three stacked standard promo blocks, not a hub/menu feel — no jump links, no at-a-glance navigation.
- Preview text is pure boilerplate ("view in browser") — wasted real estate right next to a genuinely good subject line.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real offer to at least one section — even a modest "Free Shipping on Holiday Décor" would convert the vague urgency into something clickable, since price/shipping is the single missing rubric signal across the whole email.
- 2. Turn the three shops into an actual navigation moment: add a 3-icon or 3-thumbnail jump menu right under the "Holiday Headquarters" header so it reads as a hub, not three sequential emails glued together.
- 3. Reorder by seasonal proximity — Halloween first is correct for late September, but visually upweight it (bigger hero, tighter copy) versus Thanksgiving/Christmas, which are further out.
- 4. Cut or relocate "Recommended Just For You" — it undercuts the "headquarters" concept; either drop it from this send or retitle it "More Ways to Get Ready" to keep it inside the holiday frame.
- 5. Fix the preview text — this is the easiest, cheapest win available.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Halloween, Thanksgiving & Christmas — all in one place`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your holiday shop list starts here 🎃🦃🎄`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cast a spell, set the table, deck the halls — all under one roof.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three seasons, one stop: shop before the best pieces sell out.`
