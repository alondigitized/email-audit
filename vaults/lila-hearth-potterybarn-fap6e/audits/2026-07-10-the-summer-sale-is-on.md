# The Summer Sale is ON
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's broadest, least focused email in the current July run. The hero delivers a strong discount signal — "Up to 70% off" — but the email immediately retreats into a 10-category shop-the-sale grid that asks the reader to do all the work. Compared to the July 8 furniture sale email (9/10), which had a tight room-by-room category story, today's send is a browse-reset dressed up as a campaign.
- The personalized "Recommended Just For You" module is the most interesting element — six specific named products, which signals genuine ML recommendation plumbing. The problem is the six products span curtain rods, a platform bed, towels, and two linen curtain SKUs. That's not a cohesive story; it's a feed dump. A shopper looking at that block has no clear signal about what Pottery Barn thinks they want.
- The subject — "The Summer Sale is ON" — punches below its weight. The 70% discount is buried in the hero. This is a send that should have led with the number. On cadence: this is the fifth Pottery Barn email in three days that leans on a sale frame, and the third to use broadly the same "sale is ON / shop all" structure. The discount keeps escalating (30% → 70%), which is interesting, but escalation without a deadline is just noise.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is prominent and credible** — "Up to 70% off" in large type above the fold, with "100s of new styles added!" reinforcing breadth.
- **Category grid is complete and scannable** — 10 categories + SHOP ALL SALE in a clean 2-column layout, good for subscribers who know what they want.
- **"Recommended Just For You" shows real personalization infrastructure** — specific named SKUs, not just category tiles.
- **No render bugs** — email is clean, images load, text isn't overlapping, layout holds.
- **Discover New Arrivals module works as a secondary story** — gives non-sale browsers a reason to engage.

## What's weak

- **Subject buries the lead** — "The Summer Sale is ON" leaves 70% off in the email body; a 9-point discount signal is wasted in the inbox.
- **The "Recommended" block is category-incoherent** — curtains, a bed frame, and towels in one block suggests the algorithm is firing but the editorial curation layer is absent.
- **No deadline, no urgency** — "Summer Sale" is open-ended; the July 8 furniture send had the same problem but at least had a single category pull. Here there's nothing to act on *now*.
- **Three bottom modules (app, rewards, Affirm) compete for attention** — in a sale email, this is clutter. Affirm belongs in cart abandonment, not a sale blast.
- **Cadence erosion** — this is the third Pottery Barn "sale is on / shop all" frame in three days; repeat openers are likely fatiguing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the number in the subject** — "The Summer Sale is ON" wastes the 70% discount entirely on subscribers who don't open. Lead with the hook.
- 2. **Make the hero category-specific** — pick one hero room (furniture, bedding) and make the SHOP NOW land there. The July 8 send did this well and scored 9/10; this email abandoned the playbook.
- 3. **Curate the personalized block by category** — if curtain rods are in someone's browse history, show curtains only. A mixed-category block reads as un-edited.
- 4. **Add a credible deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends July 13" turns a browse email into an action email.
- 5. **Cut the Affirm module** — in a sale email with 70% off, Affirm is the wrong signal. Reserve it for big-ticket cart abandonment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off: Summer Sale starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off sale — 100s of styles, shop by room`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, bedding, outdoor and more — sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picks are in the sale. Shop the edit.`
