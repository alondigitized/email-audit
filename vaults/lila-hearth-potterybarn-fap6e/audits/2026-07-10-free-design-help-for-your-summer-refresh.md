# FREE design help for your summer refresh
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's "Design Services" funnel email — the same positioning as the July 7th design services send in the brand history. The pitch is a free consultation: free design advice, free furniture layout planning, free 3D room design, all framed as a summer refresh hook. The hero is clear and low-friction — "it's free" is the whole value prop, and the email executes it cleanly with three benefit bullets up top.
- The problem is that this email is purely a service-funnel play dropped into the middle of a heavy sale cycle. The brand has been running "Up to 70% off" banners since at least July 8, and this email contains no sale mention whatsoever. A subscriber who opened "The Summer Sale is ON" this morning and now gets "Free Design Help" will feel like they're being handed a brochure while a clearance event is happening in the next room.
- The creative is fine — lifestyle photography, consistent brand voice, a clear secondary CTA around the free app. But it's the wrong email at the wrong moment in the send calendar. Service funnels work better in quieter weeks, not sandwiched between 70%-off banners.

## What's working

- **Free is the hero and it's prominent immediately** — three benefit bullets (free advice, free layout, free 3D design) are above the fold, no ambiguity about the value prop.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — lifestyle photography leads, then bullets, then "Get Started" CTA; the eye flows naturally top to bottom.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, text is legible, layout is intact throughout.
- **App module is a smart secondary CTA** — "Free App Pottery Barn" with a store badge gives a low-commitment alternative action for non-ready browsers.
- **Affirm mention at the bottom** — "Buy Now, Pay Over Time" adds a purchase-enablement layer even in a service email.

## What's weak

- **Zero sale tie-in** — the brand is mid-July summer sale (up to 70% off as of this morning's email) and this send ignores it entirely; feels like a scheduling misfire.
- **"Get Started" CTA is generic** — no verb specificity, no urgency, could belong to any service email any month of the year.
- **No social proof** — a free design service is a low-trust ask; one testimonial or "X rooms designed this summer" would do real work here.
- **The 3D room design module is small and visually underweighted** — it's the most differentiating feature but gets the least real estate.
- **No deadline or urgency signal anywhere** — "summer refresh" implies season but there's no "book by July 31" or similar credible fence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the service to the active sale** — lead with "70% off furniture + free design help to pull it all together" — make the design service feel like the answer to the sale, not a non-sequitur. This single change resolves the calendar conflict.
- 2. **Replace "Get Started" with a specific CTA** — "Book Your Free Room Consult" or "Start My Summer Layout" gives a clear job-to-be-done.
- 3. **Add a credibility line near the hero** — "Join 500,000+ homeowners who've worked with a PB designer" (or whatever the real number is) kills the trust gap immediately above the CTA.
- 4. **Add a booking deadline** — "Book by July 31 for summer delivery" creates a credible season fence without feeling manufactured.
- 5. **Surface the 3D room design feature more prominently** — it's genuinely differentiated; make it a full-width module, not a small tile.

## 1. Overview

This is Pottery Barn's "Design Services" funnel email — the same positioning as the July 7th design services send in the brand history.
