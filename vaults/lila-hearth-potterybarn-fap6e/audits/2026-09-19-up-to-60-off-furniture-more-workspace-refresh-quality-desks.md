# Up to 60% off furniture & more + Workspace refresh: Quality desks
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely well-targeted send for a home-office shopper: "Workspace Refresh" leads with a hero desk-and-chair vignette, then walks through three distinct desk collections (Pacific, Aubrey, Marina) each with its own lifestyle shot and named SKUs. Unlike the 2026-09-17 "Warehouse deals up to 60% off + Inside our NEW catalog" send flagged as a bait-and-switch, this one's subject ("Workspace refresh: Quality desks") and body are actually aligned — the desk category dominates the render top to bottom before it pivots into the usual "Recommended for you" grab-bag.
- Where it loses points is the back half: after three solid desk collections, it dilutes focus with curtain rods, rugs, dining tables, and a Halloween shop pivot that has nothing to do with the workspace hook. The 60% off banner at top is generic sitewide language, not desk-specific pricing, so the "up to 60%" promise in the subject doesn't visibly attach to any product actually shown.
- Solid, on-category creative with real product depth, undercut by a sprawling lower half and a discount claim that never gets tied to a number on an actual desk.

## What's working

- Clear category focus up top: hero image, headline, and three consecutive desk collections (Pacific, Aubrey, Marina) all reinforce "Workspace Refresh."
- Each collection shows two real named products (e.g., "Pacific Writing Desk," "Aubrey Corner Desk") with lifestyle photography, not just a homepage link.
- "Design Tip" module (style a desk as an entryway console) is a nice bit of editorial value-add, differentiating from a flat product dump.
- "More Workspace Updates" secondary nav (Modular Furniture, Bookcases, Office Chairs, Office Organization) extends the theme without cluttering the hero section.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- The "up to 60% off" banner is disconnected from any visible price — no product in the desk section shows a discounted price or percentage.
- After the three desk collections, the email sprawls into unrelated categories (curtains, rugs, dining table, Halloween Shop) that dilute the workspace focus promised in the subject.
- The Halloween Shop insert feels like a non-sequitur dropped into a productivity/desk email with no transition.
- Repeated "SHOP NOW" CTA copy across every module is generic and doesn't reinforce urgency or the discount.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual price or % off directly on at least one hero desk (e.g., "Pacific Writing Desk, Now $XXX") so the subject's discount claim has a visible anchor — this is the single biggest credibility gap.
- 2. Trim the back half — cut or move the curtain/rug/dining-table "Recommended" block and the Halloween Shop teaser to a separate send; a workspace email should stay a workspace email.
- 3. Vary CTA copy per section ("Shop Desks," "Shop Chairs," "See the Collection") instead of repeating "SHOP NOW" five times.
- 4. Add urgency/deadline copy near the top banner if the warehouse sale is time-bound, to justify the "up to 60%" framing.

- 0 of the ~50+ `<img>` elements carry `alt` text (QA: 50+ "missing alt text" warnings) — includes hero banners, product slices, and footer social/legal icons. This breaks screen-reader accessibility and leaves the layout content-less when images are blocked.
- Two images load over `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS.

- `<style>` block contains stray PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); ...`) embedded as literal CSS text inside a `<style>` tag — these are inert (not executable, just dead text in the CSS), but indicate a template artifact/leak from a PHP asset script that should not be in production email markup.
