# The key layers for a well-styled bed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn running an editorial "shop the look" bedding email — sheets, quilts, duvets, and pillows are stitched together under one theme ("The Layered Bed") rather than dumped as a grab-bag. Each product block gets its own styled photo, a short descriptive caption ("Ruffle hems in crisp white," "Stitched velvet reverses to florals"), and a specific "SHOP ALL X" CTA — that's a tighter narrative than most PB sends, and closer in spirit to the July 27 "New quilts for all-season comfort" send than the more scattered table-setting email from July 28.
- Where it loses points is scope creep and staleness. The top banner still runs the same "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" language this brand has led with across nearly every recent send, and the email doesn't stop at bedding — it tacks on a "Recommended Just For You" grid (curtain rods, rugs, towels), a "Discover New Arrivals" banner, and an unrelated "Up to 40% off Outdoor" push at the bottom. A subscriber who opens for "the key layers for a well-styled bed" gets a clean bedding story for the first two-thirds of the email, then the message dilutes into a generic cross-sell blast.
- Net: a well-crafted category story bolted onto the same recycled sale banner and a lengthy tail of unrelated categories. Solid, not a standout.

## What's working

- The hero section ("get the look — The Layered Bed") ties directly to the subject line, giving the email a coherent throughline.
- Each product block pairs a styled lifestyle photo with a specific caption and a specific CTA ("SHOP ALL SHEETS," "SHOP ALL QUILTS," "SHOP ALL DUVETS," "SHOP ALL PILLOWS") instead of one generic "Shop Now."
- Rewards/Affirm/app-download modules are present and clearly labeled, reinforcing loyalty value for existing customers.
- No visual bugs — images render cleanly, no overlapping text, no broken placeholders.

## What's weak

- The top banner ("Summer Sale: Up to 70% off") is the same broad, un-anchored sale claim this brand has led with across multiple prior sends (7/27, 7/28) — it reads as boilerplate rather than tied to the bedding shown below.
- This is the third bedding/quilt-themed Pottery Barn email in three days (7/27 quilts, 7/28 quilts, 7/29 this one) — a subscriber who opened the last two will feel the repetition.
- No price is shown anywhere for the actual bedding products featured — just a category-wide "up to 70%" at the top with nothing connecting it to the sheets/quilts/duvets pictured.
- The email sprawls past its own theme: "Recommended Just For You," "Discover New Arrivals," and a 40%-off Outdoor banner all show up after the bedding story wraps, undercutting the tight editorial framing established up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Anchor the sale claim to the category — replace the generic "Up to 70% off" banner with a bedding-specific number ("Duvets from $X" or "Quilts up to 40% off") so the offer and the hero story reinforce each other instead of feeling bolted together.
- 2. Cut the email at "SHOP ALL BEDDING" — drop the Recommended/New Arrivals/Outdoor tail, or split it into a separate send, to keep the single-category promise intact.
- 3. Vary the cadence — with three bedding-focused sends in three days, rotate in a different room/category next to avoid fatigue for engaged subscribers.
- 4. Add at least one visible price or "starting at $X" anchor on the hero duvet/quilt shown, since none of the featured products currently show a price.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Layer your bed like a stylist would`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sheets, quilts, duvets: the full bed story`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New ruffle sheets, velvet quilts, and toile duvets — plus free shipping on duvets.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build the layered bed look, piece by piece.`

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "The key layers for a well-styled bed"

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard ESP-templated HTML email (VML/MSO conditionals, table-based layout) sent via e.potterybarn.com/click.e.potterybarn.com infrastructure; core structure is sound, but automated QA shows a 50% pass rate driven by widespread missing alt attributes, mixed-content (http://) image sources, and absent unsubscribe/authentication headers at the transport layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All CTAs route through the click-redirect wrapper `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7...` (opaque base64/encoded payload) rather than plain query strings — 58 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe, so final destination and status codes are unverified.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels present: `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Amobee/Turn data-management pixel) and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` (open-tracking pixel) — both loaded over **http://**, not https.
- A second open-tracking beacon at `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/8f9c8470-...?mi_mid=...` uses https correctly.
- Plain-text alternative part is 71.7% URLs (10,470/14,609 chars) — indicates the plain-text version is likely just a flattened link dump rather than genuine text content, which can hurt spam-filter scoring.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **50+ images missing `alt` text**, including all content images in the `slices_*.jpg` layout grids, product shots (bed, curtain rod, towel, rug, duvet), and footer banners. Screen readers and image-blocked clients will render this email as empty space with no fallback text.
- **3 images served over plain http://** (mixed content, may be blocked by mail clients enforcing https):
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- Malformed embedded code: the client `<style>` block contains literal PHP-style calls — `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...");` — sitting inside CSS. This is invalid CSS and a build artifact (looks like a leaked server-side snippet from an image-serving script that was pasted into the template); browsers will silently ignore it, but it's a template hygiene bug worth flagging to the ESP.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and appropriate for a transactional/marketing email.
