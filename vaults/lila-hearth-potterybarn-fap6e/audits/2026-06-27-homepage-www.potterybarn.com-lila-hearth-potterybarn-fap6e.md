# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-06-27
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- I landed on PotteryBarn.com and immediately got hit with a full-screen email and SMS capture modal — a lifestyle shot of a linen sofa with gorgeous throw pillows, the Pottery Barn wordmark, and a pitch to sign up for "new arrivals, exclusive savings & more." The actual homepage is completely buried underneath it. As someone who already shops this brand and has a Williams-Sonoma rewards account, being treated like a cold stranger on arrival feels like a step backward — and the wall of fine print about automatic dialing and wireless carriers is genuinely off-putting.

## What's working

- **Lifestyle hero image** — the linen sofa with the floral and neutral throw pillows in that airy, windowed room is exactly the aesthetic I'm chasing for the second home. It stopped my scroll reflex for a second.
- **Brand recognition** — the clean Pottery Barn wordmark reassures me I'm in the right place. No confusion about where I landed.
- **Williams-Sonoma parent mention** — buried in the legal text but it registers; as a WS rewards member I know these brands are connected, and that's a tiny warmth signal.
- **Close button is visible** — the X is prominent in the top-right corner, so I'm not trapped. That matters.

## What's weak

- **Full-page interstitial on arrival** — blocking the entire homepage for a cold email grab is a dark pattern, and for a returning shopper it's especially alienating. I already get the catalog. I don't need to re-subscribe.
- **Zero concrete offer** — "exclusive savings" is not a deal. Tell me 20% off, free shipping over $75, something. Vague promises don't make me type in my email.
- **No loyalty recognition** — if you know I have a Williams-Sonoma rewards account, say something. "Member? Sign in to access exclusive pricing" would land so much better than treating me like a stranger.
- **Fine print wall** — two dense paragraphs about automated dialing, wireless carrier liability, and unsubscribe instructions before I've even seen the homepage. It feels like the brand's legal team won and the customer experience lost.
- **Submit button is off-screen** — I have to scroll inside the modal to see how to actually complete the signup. On mobile this is a friction cliff.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the cold acquisition modal with a returning-member recognition layer** — if the browser has a cookie or known email, skip the modal entirely or serve a "Welcome back — here's what's new for you" strip instead.
- **Put a specific offer in the modal headline** — swap "exclusive savings & more" for something like "Unlock 15% off your first order + early access to new arrivals." Give me a reason to hand over my number.
- **Collapse the legal fine print into a single-line disclosure with a "Learn more" expand** — the current two-paragraph wall reads as predatory, not transparent.
- **Make the CTA button visible without scrolling** — on a mobile viewport, condense the form so the "Submit" or "Sign Me Up" button is above the fold inside the modal.
