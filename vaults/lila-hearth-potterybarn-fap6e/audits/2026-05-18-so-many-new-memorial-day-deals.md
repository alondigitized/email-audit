# So many NEW Memorial Day deals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 6 of Pottery Barn's Memorial Day campaign, and the creative is showing fatigue. The email follows the same structural template as the 5/16 send ("Extra 20% off + NEW Memorial Day deals") almost exactly — hero with the Extra 20% off lockup, a multi-category product grid, the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization block, and the credit card / app footer. Nothing is broken, but nothing is new either.
- The most consequential misstep is the subject line. After five days of subjects that led with the explicit "Extra 20% off" hook — which worked — this send retreats to the vague "So many NEW Memorial Day deals," stripping the primary conversion lever right off the envelope. Subscribers who've been trained to respond to the discount % now get a softer tease.
- The email body itself remains competent: clean visual hierarchy, a broad product grid that gives shoppers multiple entry points, and the personalized section still earns its place. But by this point in the campaign, Pottery Barn needed to either close with urgency ("ends tonight") or open a genuinely new angle. "So many NEW" does neither. Net result: a solid mid-campaign mailer with a weak cover.

## What's working

- **Extra 20% off is front and center in the hero** — the offer loads above the fold and the eye goes straight to it regardless of what the subject promised.
- **Multi-category product grid** covers living room, outdoor, and bedroom, giving every shopper a path to click without forcing a category choice.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block** breaks the editorial monotony and adds credible personalization signal; the mattress + outdoor furniture pairing matches the broader sale context.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping elements.
- **Footer loyalty/card block** reinforces membership value without overloading the main body.

## What's weak

- **Subject drops the discount %** — every prior send in this campaign led with "Extra 20% off" in the subject; this one buries the offer behind "So many NEW," making it easier to skip.
- **No urgency signal in body or subject** — Memorial Day is the implied deadline but no "ends Sunday," "last 3 days," or countdown is visible anywhere.
- **"So many NEW" feels like campaign inflation** — this is the same sale at Day 6; calling it "new deals" after six sends strains credibility.
- **Hero CTA copy reads "SHOP GIFTS NOW"** — incongruous with a furniture/home decor send; "gifts" signals a different shopping intent than sofas and outdoor chairs.
- **No single hero product** — the grid-of-everything approach gives no clear focal point for a shopper with specific intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the discount, not a vague quantity claim.** Restore "Extra 20% off" to the subject envelope — that's what's been converting this campaign. Add urgency now that Memorial Day weekend is closing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off ends soon — shop Memorial Day deals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last days: Extra 20% off the Memorial Day sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals added — plus up to 60% off select styles through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Memorial Day. Sofas, outdoor, bedding and more are in.`
- 2. **Add a countdown or explicit end-date** to the hero section — "Sale ends May 26" is enough. Without it, "Memorial Day" is abstract for anyone not checking a calendar.
- 3. **Fix the hero CTA copy** from "SHOP GIFTS NOW" to "SHOP THE SALE" or the category-appropriate "SHOP NEW DEALS" — the word "gifts" creates a mismatch with the home furnishings content.
- 4. **Feature one anchor product in the hero** — a single in-lifestyle sofa, outdoor set, or hero item gives the eye a place to land and increases link-click depth beyond the first CTA.
- 5. **Thin the product grid or segment it** — by Day 6, a shopper who hasn't bought yet probably needs a reason (category, price, trend) more than a wider assortment.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP header() calls leaked into `<style>` block**
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are literal PHP function calls inside a `<style>` tag. They are inert in email clients but confirm a template/build pipeline failure — the ESP renderer did not execute or strip this server-side code before output. This is the highest-priority code defect in the send.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — renders as blank in tab/window previews and degrades screen reader context.

**40+ images missing `alt` text** — affects the entire content grid (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg`), all footer slices (14 images), product thumbnails, and tracking pixels. Notable misses:
