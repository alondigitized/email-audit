# July 4th deals in stores now! Up to 70% off
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh or eighth consecutive Pottery Barn send in this campaign window, and the creative fatigue is now critical. The sender is "Pottery Barn Design Services" — a sub-brand pivot from the main Pottery Barn list — but the body is essentially the same email subscribers have been receiving since June 26: Extra 20% off, 4th of July Sale, up to 70% off, same tiered category tiles, same Open Box teaser, same Recommended For You rail. The one meaningful delta is the in-store angle called out in the hero ("in stores now!") and a Floor Model Sale insert, which is a genuine new beat.
- The store pickup framing is the most defensible differentiator here, but it's buried. The hero reads "Summer Sale — in stores now!" in modest type beneath a small "4TH OF JULY SALE" badge. Someone scanning their inbox after six identical sends is not going to register "oh, this one has in-store inventory" — they'll pattern-match to last Tuesday's email and archive it.
- Judgment: this send had a real hook — in-store availability + Floor Model Sale is a distinct value prop — but the creative recycling has eroded the subscriber's willingness to look. It's a missed opportunity to break the doom loop with a genuinely differentiated in-store-only message.

## What's working

- The "Floor Model Sale" insert is a fresh creative beat not seen in prior sends — up to 50% off furniture, lighting, and more is a specific, credible offer.
- The in-store angle ("in stores now!") is a real segmentation signal that no prior send in this campaign deployed.
- "Find a Free Appointment" CTA adds a design-services angle that differentiates this sender sub-brand from the main list.
- Category tiles (Outdoor, Furniture) maintain visual hierarchy with percentage-off callouts (up to 70%, up to 79%, up to 68% off) that are easy to scan.

## What's weak

- Sender name "Pottery Barn Design Services" signals a sub-brand, but the body content is identical to the main Pottery Barn blast — the design-services angle is not reinforced past the hero CTA.
- The hero copy is small and dense — "Summer Sale" in the banner competes with "4th of July Sale" badge, creating double-message confusion.
- Six prior sends with nearly identical creative mean the "up to 70% off" claim has lost its urgency signal — this is the baseline, not a new offer.
- The "Recommended Just For You" personalization rail appears at the bottom, after the Floor Model Sale insert — the strongest personalization anchor is placed last instead of first.
- No deadline is visible in the hero — "in stores now!" implies urgency but gives no end date, weakening the time-bound push.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the in-store-only angle as the primary differentiator.** Every subscriber has seen the digital sale. The hook that breaks through is "Floor Model Sale — in stores only, this weekend." Flip the hero to make that the headline, not Summer Sale.
- 2. **Add a visible sale-end date.** "Ends July 4th" or "This weekend only" would make the urgency credible. Right now there's none in the hero.
- 3. **Make the Design Services sender earn its sub-brand.** If this is going to a design-services segment, the copy should speak to that customer: "Book your in-store appointment before July 4th and get an extra 20% off your floor model pick." Right now it's just the standard blast with a different From name.
- 4. **Suppress this send from subscribers who opened any of the last 3 identical sends.** At this cadence, this email is doing active brand damage to engaged subscribers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Floor models 50% off — in stores this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `In-store picks: up to 70% off before July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Sale now in your local store — book a free design appointment`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Floor Model Sale ends July 4th. Find your store.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP `header()` calls injected inside `<style>` block**

```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ...
</style>
```

This is server-side PHP code that was not executed — it was emitted literally into the CSS context. It is invalid CSS and indicates a template rendering defect (a PHP snippet intended for HTTP response headers was included in the wrong template partial). No known email client will interpret it as CSS, so visual rendering is likely unaffected, but it signals a broken build pipeline.

**FAIL — Pervasive missing alt text (38+ images)**

Every image slice in the body (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_10.jpg`) and footer (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`) has `alt=""`. Product images (beds, curtains, bedding) and the logo also lack descriptive alt text. With images blocked, the email renders as a blank white column for screen readers and image-off clients.

**WARN — Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**

```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook, and most modern clients block HTTP-sourced images. These will not render.

**WARN — Cross-brand asset dependency**

`spacer_0.gif` is served from `edm.westelm.com`, a sibling brand domain. It is currently 403ing. Any West Elm infrastructure change breaks this silently.
