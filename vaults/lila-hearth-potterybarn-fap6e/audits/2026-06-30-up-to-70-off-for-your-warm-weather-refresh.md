# UP TO 70% OFF for your warm-weather refresh ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a mid-campaign "kitchen sink" send from Pottery Barn — the tenth-plus consecutive email in a 4th of July push that has been running for over a week. Compared to yesterday's two sends (including the Halloween creative bet that scored 7), this one at least stays on-brand for the season with a "warm-weather refresh" frame. But that framing is skin-deep: the body delivers the same Extra 20% off hero it has delivered for a dozen emails, followed by a category nav strip, an Open Box Outlet module, a "Recommended Just For You" product grid, a New Arrivals lifestyle block, an app download prompt, and an Affirm financing pitch — six distinct sections fighting for the same viewport.
- The subject line makes a smart pivot from the usual "EXTRA 20% OFF" opener to "UP TO 70% OFF" which is a stronger lead. The "warm-weather refresh" editorial angle has potential but is never delivered: there's no curated room shot, no seasonal story, no "here's your July 4th outdoor setup" through-line. The email promises an editorial frame and then delivers a sale grid.
- Judgment: competent execution of a fatigued creative, improved by the subject line pivot. A subscriber who has been getting these for ten days is running on autopilot and the Open Box module is the only genuinely new hook.

## What's working

- Subject line pivot from "EXTRA 20% OFF" to "UP TO 70% OFF" is a meaningful upgrade in perceived value signal over the last several sends.
- Open Box Outlet Deals module is a distinct, additive offer — not a repeat of the main hero — and gives deal-seekers a real reason to click.
- "Recommended Just For You" section signals personalization and breaks the pure-discount monotony.
- Category navigation strip (Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, etc.) is clean and gives a fast-scrolling reader a shortcut to their category.
- No render bugs: layout is clean, images load, no broken elements visible.

## What's weak

- "Warm-weather refresh" promises an editorial theme that the body never delivers — no room story, no outdoor entertaining hook, just the standard sale grid.
- Six competing sections (hero → nav → Open Box → Recommended → New Arrivals → App → Affirm) make the scroll feel like a home page, not a targeted send.
- No deadline visible anywhere — "UP TO 70% OFF" with no expiration date feels like a permanent sale, not an event.
- "Recommended Just For You" product cards are rendered very small; the savings amounts are nearly unreadable.
- Cadence: this is the 10th-plus nearly identical campaign send. Even a solid email suffers from open-rate suppression at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** A specific "Ends July 4th at midnight" line in the hero block would transform the offer from background noise into an event. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Pick one editorial category and own it.** "Warm-weather refresh" should have meant a curated Outdoor or Patio story — one lifestyle image, two-three hero SKUs, one CTA. Cut the category nav strip from this send and let the theme breathe.
- 3. **Give the Open Box section a standalone tease in the subject** — it's the most novel element and subscribers haven't seen it every day.
- 4. **Increase "Recommended" card size** so prices and product names are legible without pinch-zoom.
- 5. **Drop the Affirm and App modules** to a footer link — they add cognitive load without driving the conversion this email is optimized for.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor refresh: extra 20% off — ends July 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Open Box deals from 70% off — warm-weather picks inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop furniture, rugs & outdoor for your best summer yet.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Open Box Outlet + 20% extra off. Sale ends soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` calls injected into `<style>` block**

The following raw PHP server directives appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are server-side PHP output directives that should never reach the rendered HTML — this is a template rendering failure where a dynamic image server snippet was accidentally inlined. They have no effect in email but indicate a broken template pipeline and will appear as junk text in CSS-visible clients.

**[FAIL] 50+ images missing `alt` text**

Every image in the email has `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute, including:
- Banner grid: `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_14.jpg`
- Product images: curtain rod, quilt, percale sheets, dining table, towel, mattress
- Footer slices: `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`
- Credit card banner: `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- Tracking pixels: open pixel, Turn pixel, coreemail pixel

Outlook blocks images by default. With no alt text, images-off rendering presents a blank email with no readable content.

**[FAIL] 3 content images + 1 tracking pixel served over HTTP**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| West Elm spacer | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| Key CC banner | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |

Gmail proxies HTTP images through its own CDN; other clients may block or flag them. The open pixel over HTTP will produce unreliable open-rate data.
