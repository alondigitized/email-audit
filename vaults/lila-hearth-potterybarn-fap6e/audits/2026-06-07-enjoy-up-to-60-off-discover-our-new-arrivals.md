# Enjoy UP TO 60% OFF + Discover our NEW ARRIVALS
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's ninth email in three days anchored to the Summer Warehouse Sale — and the subject line reveals the creative exhaustion. Pairing "UP TO 60% OFF" with "Discover our NEW ARRIVALS" in the same breath is a signal the team knows the sale angle alone is no longer punchy enough to earn opens, so they've bolted on a second hook. The problem: warehouse sale and new arrivals are categorically opposing propositions. The email body doesn't resolve that tension — it's another competently executed, familiar sale layout with a hero banner, multi-column product grid, and a "Recommended Just for You" personalization strip.
- The visual execution holds up. The hero is clean, the coastal/neutral palette is on-brand, the product photography is sharp, and the layout renders without visible bugs. The personalization section is the most defensible addition to this send.
- But the cadence is doing serious damage. A subscriber who opened "☀️ SUMMER WAREHOUSE SALE ☀️" yesterday and the three emails before it has already absorbed this offer. Another variant of the same sale email — with the same headline and discount — just adds to the pile. The compound subject and the fabricated new-arrivals hook don't salvage that fatigue problem; they deepen it.

## What's working

- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalization strip signals that Pottery Barn is segmenting, which is the right instinct at this stage of the campaign.
- Hero visual is clean and seasonally appropriate — the light coastal palette reads summer without being forced.
- Product grid is multi-category (bedroom, outdoor, storage), giving varied entry points for different buyer intentions.
- Pottery Barn Key / credit card section anchors loyalty positioning near the bottom without cluttering the hero.
- No visible render bugs; layout is consistent top-to-bottom.

## What's weak

- The compound subject telegraphs creative fatigue — this is the same compound-subject pattern used on June 6 ("Discover deals UP TO 60% OFF + Top your bed...") and it signals the team is grabbing for hooks rather than landing one strong one.
- "NEW ARRIVALS" in the subject conflicts with the warehouse sale positioning in the body — these are opposite messages and the body doesn't visibly resolve which is the lead.
- No credible deadline is visible anywhere in the email — the sale urgency is entirely asserted by the label, not by a date or countdown.
- Eighth-plus consecutive warehouse sale send within 72 hours; diminishing returns on every open and click metric for anyone who's been in this list since June 5.
- "Enjoy" as the lead verb in the subject is soft and aimless — contributes zero urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop the compound subjects immediately.** One send = one message. The team is borrowing credibility from two angles and landing neither. Pick sale OR new arrivals for each send, not both.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" immediately converts vague sale awareness into urgency. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 3. **Let the personalization section lead a standalone send.** "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU — up to 60% off" is a stronger email than this warehouse sale blast. Segment the list, send the personalized picks to engaged subscribers, drop the blanket sale email for the others.
- 4. **Reduce cadence to once per day maximum** for the remainder of the warehouse sale, with clearly differentiated hooks (price drop alert, last chance, category-specific).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale ends Sunday — your picks are up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off, picked for you — sale ends this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals + warehouse prices. Two reasons to shop now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your recommended pieces are on sale — don't miss the window.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "UP TO 60% OFF + New Arrivals" (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links and a server-side code injection into a `<style>` block are the most severe issues; the email is otherwise structurally sound but has pervasive HTTP mixed-content and missing alt text across the New Arrivals section that will degrade the experience in major clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card banner CTA returns 403**
URL: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
Affects the "Apply & Buy" / Key Rewards Credit Card header module. Click lands on an error, not the card application.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer GIF returns 403**
URL: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand asset referenced in the Pottery Barn send. Domain is wrong (West Elm, not Pottery Barn), the asset is HTTP-only, and it 403s. Likely a copy-paste artifact from a sibling-brand template.

**[INFO] 24 tracking/click-redirect links not probed** — ESP wraps prevent direct HTTP verification; no pass/fail can be assigned to these.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP server-side code injected into `<style>` block**
The following literal PHP statements appear inside a `<style>` tag instead of being executed server-side:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is a template rendering failure — the PHP was not processed before send. CSS parsers will skip the invalid declarations, but it is evidence of a broken build pipeline for this module.

**[FAIL] 13 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
Entire New Arrivals sliced image set (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg`) is served from `http://edm.potterybarn.com/...`. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com all upgrade or block HTTP image loads. These images will likely show as broken for the majority of recipients. Additional HTTP assets:
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597...` (open-tracking pixel)
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

**[WARN] ~47 images missing `alt` text**
All New Arrivals slices, most footer slices, all product images (`cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.), and tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. In image-blocking environments this renders the entire New Arrivals section invisible with no fallback text.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — disabling user zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and will trigger accessibility flags in some ESPs.

**[WARN] Empty `<title>` element** — minor; no client-visible impact but some accessibility scanners flag it.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA relay did not capture it. If absent in production delivery, this violates Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements (mandatory for >5K/day senders) and CAN-SPAM best practice.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Required by Gmail for bulk senders since February 2024.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown via this relay path. Cannot confirm authentication posture.

**[OK] `meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"`** — correctly prevents email HTML from being indexed if mirrored.

**[OK] `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`** — intentional for privacy; noted below for UTM impact.
