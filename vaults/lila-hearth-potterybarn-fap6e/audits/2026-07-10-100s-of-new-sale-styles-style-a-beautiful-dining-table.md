# 100s of NEW sale styles + Style a beautiful dining table
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic Pottery Barn hybrid that can't decide what it is: a "Style Your Dining Table" editorial guide (five numbered categories) tacked to a sale-volume announcement ("100s of NEW sale styles"). The "+" compound subject line is the email's original sin — it commits to neither concept, and neither half benefits from sharing the headline. After five Pottery Barn emails in three days — including yesterday's two sharply focused furniture sale sends that each scored 9/10 — this one needs a tighter hook to earn the open. It doesn't have one.
- The dining table editorial body is the email's real strength. The lifestyle hero is beautiful, the numbered breakdown (Dinnerware → Glassware → Table Linen → Placemats → Candles) is genuinely scannable and useful, and the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module below adds a personalization signal the pure-sale emails lack. If this email had been sent as a standalone editorial — or as a sale with prices visible in the body — it would be a strong send.
- Instead it reads as two emails forced to share a container. The furniture/sofa block grafted onto the bottom breaks the dining editorial entirely. Net judgement: a promising concept undermined by split-personality structure and a cadence that's burning subscriber goodwill.

## What's working

- **Dining table editorial is the freshest creative in Pottery Barn's recent run** — a genuine break from the pure-sale and color-story formats of the last 72 hours.
- **Hero lifestyle photography is strong** — the aspirational table setting is visually dominant and immediately category-correct.
- **Five numbered categories are instantly scannable** — a subscriber can find their entry point (Glassware, Candles) without reading a word of copy.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module adds personalization signal** even as a lower-scroll element — rare in Pottery Barn's recent sends.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is two emails fighting for space** — "100s of NEW sale styles + Style a beautiful dining table" at 55 characters is over mobile length and neither half lands.
- **No visible discount in the body** — "100s of NEW sale styles" is a quantity claim, not a price claim; there's no "up to X% off" in the hero or categories to motivate a click.
- **No dominant hero CTA button** — five dispersed category links replace a single call to action; click energy is diffused on mobile.
- **Furniture block grafted onto the bottom** — the sofa/living-room module breaks the dining editorial and reads like a second email taped on.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is at least the fifth Pottery Barn email in three days; without a compelling new angle in the subject, the skip reflex is strong.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one concept.** If dining editorial is the hero, remove the sale-volume banner and let the table-setting guide breathe with a single "Shop the dining edit" button. If sale is the hero, rename the five categories "Top 5 sale picks for your table" and show prices.
- 2. **Add at least one price signal to the body.** Even "Dinnerware, up to 30% off" within the numbered breakdown converts this from browse to buy.
- 3. **Replace the compound subject with a single focused line** — the "+" construction is doing neither job well.
- 4. **Cut the furniture/sofa block** or hold it for its own send. It kills the editorial coherence.
- 5. **Add a deadline.** "Shop the sale" with no end date is an open invitation to forget it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The dining table edit: new sale styles just in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dinnerware, glassware, linens and more — all on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 things every beautiful table needs (and every one is on sale)`
