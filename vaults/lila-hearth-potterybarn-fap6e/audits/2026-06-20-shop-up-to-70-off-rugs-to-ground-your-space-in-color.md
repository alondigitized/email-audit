# 🩵 Shop UP TO 70% OFF + Rugs to ground your space in color
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second June 20 send — arriving alongside "On sale & ready-to-ship!" — and it's the more interesting of the two because the subject actually narrows to a category (rugs) rather than just restating the blanket summer sale. That's a real improvement over the generic "UP TO 70% OFF" blasts that dominated June 18–19. The email delivers on the subject: three styled rug collections (Clean Blues, Shades of Cream, Warm Neutrals), a Custom Sisal section, and an editorial "Light & Airy Design Resources" segment.
- The problem is structural and cadence-driven. After six or more emails this month hammering the same "up to 70% off" headline, the discount anchor no longer reads as urgency — it reads as the permanent state of the Pottery Barn inbox. The "Recommended Just For You" module at the bottom surfaces outdoor furniture in a rug-focused email, which breaks the single-category thesis and makes personalization feel cosmetic rather than real.
- The rug-as-hero edit is the right instinct. The execution is halfway there.

## What's working

- **Category discipline in the body:** The three styled rug collections (Clean Blues / Shades of Cream / Warm Neutrals) are visually distinct with good product photography and clear collection labels — easy to scan.
- **Subject line structure:** Pairing the discount hook with a product noun ("Rugs to ground your space in color") is smarter than a pure percentage claim — it gives a subscriber a reason to open beyond "sale."
- **Custom Sisal call-out:** Breaking out Custom Sisal as its own section signals product depth and differentiates from competitors who only stock ready-made options.
- **Clean render:** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout collapse visible in the screenshot.
- **Editorial bridge:** The "Light & Airy Design Resources" block ties the rugs to a broader summer room concept rather than just a price table.

## What's weak

- **"Recommended Just For You" is off-topic:** The personalization module shows what appears to be outdoor/patio furniture in an email themed entirely around rugs — undercuts the category focus and makes the algorithm look inaccurate.
- **No visible deadline:** The Summer Sale has no end date shown anywhere in the render; "up to 70% off" has appeared in every Pottery Barn email since at least June 18, so without a countdown or close date it lands as ambient noise.
- **Emoji + ALL CAPS combo in subject:** The 🩵 paired with "UP TO 70% OFF" pushes toward spam-signal territory — either the emoji OR the caps would be fine, but together they look promotional-aggressive in crowded inboxes.
- **Subject line is too long:** At 57+ characters it truncates on most mobile clients before reaching the "Rugs to ground your space" half — the strongest editorial hook gets clipped.
- **Cadence fatigue:** This is the second send today and among the heaviest weekly volumes this brand has pushed all month; the open rate for any individual email in this batch will be suppressed by subscriber exhaustion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the subject line so the category leads:** "Rugs to ground your summer space" is shorter, more editorial, and forces the offer into the preheader where it complements rather than crowds. The discount is still the hook — it just doesn't have to win the race in the first 35 characters.
- 2. **Add an end date or countdown:** Even "Sale ends Sunday" in the hero copy converts "up to 70% off" from background radiation to a real call to act.
- 3. **Remove or replace the off-topic Recommended module:** In a single-category email, a personalization module that surfaces a different category does more damage than good — replace it with a "Top-rated rugs" carousel or hide it entirely.
- 4. **Merge or drop the second June 20 send:** "On sale & ready-to-ship!" + this email in the same inbox session is redundant. If both must go, only one should carry the discount hook; the other should be purely editorial.
- 5. **Drop either the emoji or the ALL CAPS** in the subject — not both simultaneously.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rugs to ground your space — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Color underfoot: summer rugs up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clean blues, warm neutrals, and custom sisal — sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three rug stories, one summer price. Shop before the sale closes.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block — build artifact**
The following PHP server-side functions appear verbatim inside a CSS `<style>` tag:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not valid CSS. Clients that surface unrecognized style content as text (some webmail clients) will render these lines visibly in the email body.

**47 images missing alt text**
Every content image in the email has no `alt` attribute, including the hero banner (`S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 18 rug story slices (`slices_01.gif`–`slices_18.jpg`), all 18 Email-Footer slices, and all product images (curtains, bed, quilt, etc.). Screen readers will skip these entirely; images-off rendering will be blank with no fallback text.

**4 assets served over HTTP — will be blocked**
Gmail, Apple Mail, and Yahoo block mixed-content HTTP images:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` (open-tracking pixel)

The HTTP open pixel means **open tracking will silently fail** in any client enforcing HTTPS.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied universally; disables iOS automatic text resizing for low-vision users.

**Empty `<title>` tag and two commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags**
`<title></title>` — unpopulated. Two commented-out viewport overrides remain in `<head>` — debugging artifacts not cleaned from the template.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers not found**
Both headers are absent from the captured relay output. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) for bulk senders; absence risks inbox placement penalties and forces users to rely on in-body unsubscribe only. Must be confirmed at the ESP level — may be a relay capture gap.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. `From` domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ESP subdomain); alignment and DMARC posture must be validated directly with the ESP.

**Plain-text body is 71.9% URLs**
10,757 of 14,955 plain-text characters are URLs. Machine-generated plain-text with no meaningful human-readable content reduction is a known spam-filter signal.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
Suppresses HTTP referrer on all outbound clicks. Standard for privacy, but means destination analytics will attribute all traffic as `(direct)` unless UTM parameters in click URLs are intact. No issue if UTMs are consistently applied; flag as a dependency.

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Block send or fix before resend:**

1. **Repair the credit card apply CTA** — `clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407...` returns 403. Fix the destination or suppress the module; this is an active broken experience.
2. **Remove PHP `header()` calls from the `<style>` block** — These will render as visible text in some clients. Strip the four `header(...)` lines from the CSS.

**P1 — Fix in template before next deployment:**

3. **Upgrade all HTTP image sources to HTTPS** — 3 content images and the open-tracking pixel use `http://`. The open pixel failure means open-rate data is unreliable for any client enforcing HTTPS.
4. **Remove or replace the West Elm cross-brand spacer** — Replace `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` with a Pottery Barn-hosted HTTPS equivalent or a CSS spacer.
5. **Add alt text to all content images** — At minimum: descriptive alt on hero, product, and CTA images; `alt=""` on decorative spacers and tracking pixels.
6. **Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta** — WCAG violation; users must not be prevented from zooming.
7. **Change global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` to `100%`** — `none` disables accessibility scaling; `100%` preserves layout without blocking zoom.

**P2 — Confirm with ESP:**

8. **Confirm `List-Unsubscribe-Post` injection at send time** — One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) is required for Yahoo/Gmail bulk sender compliance. Validate headers are present in the actual delivered message, not just the relay capture.
9. **Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass** for `e.potterybarn.com` sending domain.
10. **Provide a human-readable plain-text alternative** — Reduce URL density below 50% to lower spam classifier risk.
