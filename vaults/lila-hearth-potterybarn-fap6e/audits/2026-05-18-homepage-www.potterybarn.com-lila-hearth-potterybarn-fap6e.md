# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- What I see the moment I land on Pottery Barn is not Pottery Barn — it's a full-screen modal blocking everything. The "#1 Dad Sweepstakes" is the entire above-the-fold experience: a sweepstakes for a golf lover targeting Father's Day, asking me to hand over my email before I've seen a single piece of furniture. I came here thinking about my second home's living room, and I'm being recruited for a marketing list instead. The brand beneath that modal is almost entirely invisible.

## What's working

- **Headline is bold and readable.** "#1 Dad Sweepstakes" in large type is immediately legible on mobile — whoever designed this modal knows how to write a scannable headline.
- **Concrete prize value.** "$4,000" and "$500 to spend" are specific numbers, not "a chance to win a prize." That specificity earns attention even from someone not shopping for Dad.
- **Campaign timing is on.** Father's Day falling in June, expiring 5/24/26 — this is live and relevant to the calendar.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand family callout.** The fine print mentions "Williams-Sonoma brands" — as a WS Rewards member, seeing that ecosystem named is a small comfort signal for me.
- **Clean, non-broken render.** The modal loads crisply; the image (golf book, decanter, accessories styled on a bar cart) is well-photographed and aspirationally Pottery Barn.

## What's weak

- **The modal IS the homepage.** I cannot see a single furniture product, category, or banner behind it. Every metric about what Pottery Barn is selling right now is hidden.
- **Wrong audience lead.** "For the Golf Lover" is not me. I am on this site thinking about a throw for the guest room or maybe a console table. Pottery Barn knows I'm a returning shopper — they have my data — and they led with Dad's sweepstakes.
- **No dismiss affordance in the natural eye path.** The X is in the top right corner; if I miss it, I'm staring at a sweepstakes entry form with no visible "no thanks" text link. That friction will make me bounce, not enter.
- **Email ask with heavy consent copy.** Four paragraphs of legal fine print below the email field — SMS consent, wireless carrier disclaimers, state exclusions — before I've even seen a product. This is the opposite of a frictionless first impression.
- **No loyalty hook.** I have a Williams-Sonoma Key Rewards account. Pottery Barn should know that. There's no "Welcome back, Lila" moment, no rewards balance, nothing to make me feel like a valued returning customer.
- **Zero decor content.** My entire reason for visiting — decorating a second home — is completely invisible above the fold.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the blocking modal with a slim top banner or soft slide-in.** Keep the sweepstakes, but let me see the homepage first. A sticky banner saying "Enter to Win $4,000 in U.S. Open Tickets — Shop for Dad →" converts without hijacking my experience.
- **Personalize the entry.** If I'm a logged-in Williams-Sonoma Key Rewards member, skip the sweepstakes email capture entirely — you already have me — and swap in a rewards-balance callout or a "Shop our second-home edit" hero.
- **Add a visible "No thanks, take me shopping" link** directly beneath the email input. One small anchor tag reduces bounce rate significantly and signals the brand isn't trying to trap me.
- **Lead the hero with a home-decorating story.** Even within the Father's Day moment, Pottery Barn could show a beautifully styled home office gift set or a bar cart setup with "Shop the look" CTA — that's both gift-relevant and architecturally on-brand for someone like me who shops for the home.
