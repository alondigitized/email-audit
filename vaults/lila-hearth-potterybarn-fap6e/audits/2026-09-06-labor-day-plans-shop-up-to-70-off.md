# Labor Day plans? Shop UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Same burgundy Labor Day template Pottery Barn has run all week, but this one adds real substance under the fold: a category grid, a "family of brands" cross-sell module, a "Recommended Just For You" product carousel with six named SKUs, a Halloween teaser, and a new-arrivals module. It's the most content-dense send in the run, which cuts both ways — more to click on, but also more scrolling before anything new happens after the identical maroon hero.
- The hero itself is now stale by repetition: "Extra 20% off / Select styles / Plus new deals up to 70% off" is verbatim or near-verbatim to at least four prior sends this week (2026-09-05 "ON NOW!", 2026-09-05 Labor Day deals, 2026-09-04 bedding). A subscriber who's opened two of these already has seen this exact banner and CTA stack before.
- Net: solid render, no bugs, genuinely useful personalized product grid — but it's buried under a hero that's now background noise. Judgment: 6/10, same tier as the rest of this week's Pottery Barn cadence, saved by the "Recommended Just For You" module actually looking personalized.

## What's working

- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks anywhere in a long scroll.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" carousel shows six distinct named products (Farmhouse Platform Bed, Toscana Extendable Dining Table, Balboa Swivel Chair, etc.) with visible prices context via sweepstakes disclaimer nearby — feels like real personalization, not filler.
- Category grid (Furniture, Bedding, Rugs, Lighting, etc.) gives a clear secondary navigation path if the hero offer doesn't land.
- Family-of-brands cross-sell (West Elm, Williams Sonoma, Mark & Graham) is a smart low-cost re-engagement lever bolted onto an otherwise repetitive template.

## What's weak

- Hero copy and CTA stack ("Shop Extra 20% Off" / "Shop All Sale") are identical to multiple prior sends this week — zero novelty for a subscriber checking their inbox daily.
- Subject promises "UP TO 70% OFF" but the hero visually leads with "Extra 20% off" — the biggest number is subordinated in the actual design, not the story people see first.
- No urgency element in the visible hero (no countdown, no "ends tonight") despite Labor Day literally being the news hook — feels like a template running out the clock on a holiday that's already passed for most calendars.
- Email is very long (six-plus modules) before Halloween/New Arrivals content, which have nothing to do with Labor Day at all — dilutes the subject's premise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire the burgundy Labor Day hero — it's run essentially unchanged since 2026-08-27 per the brand history; swap in fresh seasonal art (fall/early Halloween, matching the module already in this email) to break visual fatigue.
- 2. Lead hero copy with "up to 70% off" since that's the subject's hook and the bigger number — right now the hero undersells its own subject line.
- 3. Move "Recommended Just For You" higher, right under the hero — it's the most differentiated, non-repetitive content in the email and currently requires several scrolls to reach.
- 4. Add a hard deadline ("Ends tonight" / "Final hours") to the hero since Labor Day urgency is the entire premise of the subject line but is absent from the visible design.

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Labor Day" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard table-based HTML email (XHTML 1.0 Transitional, MSO conditional comments) with heavy tracking-pixel/redirect infrastructure; the main technical issues are missing unsubscribe/authentication headers, an unencrypted tracking pixel that leaks the recipient's email address in plaintext, and near-total absence of image alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All clickable links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (opaque base64/encoded redirect payload) rather than direct URLs — 68 tracking/redirect links were skipped by automated HTTP probing, so live-link validity is unconfirmed.
- Open-tracking pixel served over **plaintext HTTP** with the recipient's email address exposed in the query string: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This both fails to encrypt PII in transit and will likely be blocked/flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only remote content.
- Secondary tracking beacon `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` present — third-party ad-tech pixel, no functional issue found but worth noting for a privacy/compliance pass.
- Click-tracking redirect `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/87baa12e-...?mi_mid=01a07584-4d80-7000-8f15-d228b2ae1adb&cp_tp...` appears well-formed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **51 images missing `alt` text** across hero banners, product grid slices, and footer modules (full list in QA output) — screen readers and blocked-image fallback states will show nothing.
- **2 images served over `http://`** (non-HTTPS): `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — likely to be blocked or trigger mixed-content warnings in modern clients.
- Dead/invalid code embedded in the `<style>` block: literal PHP `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ..."); header("Pragma: no-cache");` statements sit inside a CSS `<style>` tag. This is inert (browsers/mail clients ignore it as invalid CSS) but indicates a copy-paste artifact from a server-side script that should not ship in the template.
- Layout otherwise follows conventional responsive-table patterns (`@media` breakpoints at 480/650/700px, MSO conditional fixes) — no structural rendering issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) found in the visible/truncated source.
- Recipient identifier `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` is correctly interpolated into tracking/service URLs (`coreemail.html?email=...`, `sv_sveme=...`), but see §2/§5 — one of these instances transmits the address unencrypted.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — no RFC 2369 header captured, so one-click unsubscribe support cannot be confirmed at the header level.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (RFC 8058) — combined with the above, this email likely does not meet Gmail/Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe requirement, independent of whether an in-body unsubscribe link exists.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture; cannot confirm authentication compliance.
- PII-in-URL over plaintext HTTP (§2) is a privacy-handling concern worth flagging alongside compliance review.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No direct/plain destination URLs are present to inspect — all links are wrapped in the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect service with opaque `qs=` payloads, so UTM parameters and final landing-page targets cannot be verified from the HTML alone.
- Product deep-links to `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/...` are image assets only, not click-throughs, so no landing-page mapping to confirm there.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to meet one-click unsubscribe requirements for bulk senders.
2. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC are passing and that `Authentication-Results` is present at the relay — currently unverifiable.
3. Move the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel to HTTPS; it currently transmits the recipient's email address in cleartext.
4. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 51 flagged images, particularly the product grid (`slices_*.jpg`) and hero banners, for accessibility and blocked-image fallback.
5. Migrate the two remaining `http://` image sources to HTTPS.
6. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` statements from the `<style>` block — dead code from a copy-paste error.
7. Since all links route through an opaque redirect wrapper, request unwrapped/sample destination URLs (or resolve the `qs=` payloads) to verify UTM parameters and landing-page alignment.
