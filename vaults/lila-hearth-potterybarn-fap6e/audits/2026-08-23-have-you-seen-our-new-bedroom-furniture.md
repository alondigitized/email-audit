# Have you seen our new bedroom furniture?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's new-arrivals bedroom furniture catalog send — "Beautiful Foundations," anchored by an airy sunroom bedroom hero, then a straight scroll through four collections (Scarlett, Barley, Sausalito, plus a "Complete Your Retreat" category grid). It's a clean, well-lit, on-brand execution — every product shot is consistent, styled, and shoppable — but it's pure catalog browsing with zero urgency, no price, and no discount anywhere in the visible fold. Compare this to the brand's recent run: it reads more like the 2026-08-21 "Layering With Light" education send than a sale email, and given Pottery Barn has hit sale/free-shipping beats three of the last five sends, this is a deliberate change of pace toward inspiration over conversion.
- The subject "Have you seen our new bedroom furniture?" is soft and low-urgency — a question, not an offer — which tracks with the body: this is a discovery/awareness play, not a promo. It's beautifully produced but forgettable in an inbox that's otherwise trained subscribers to expect a deal.
- Judgement: strong production values, weak commercial hook. Fine as a periodic brand/discovery send, but it won't move the open or click needle the way PB's sale emails do.

## What's working

- The hero image (sunroom canopy bed) is genuinely beautiful and immediately signals "new furniture" without needing copy to explain it.
- Consistent shot styling across all four collections (Scarlett, Barley, Sausalito, and the mixed grid) makes the whole email feel cohesive and premium.
- Clear per-collection CTAs ("Shop Now," "Shop the Look") sit right under each product block, so there's no ambiguity about what to click.
- "NEW" and "BESTSELLER" badges on Barley and Sausalito add light social proof/differentiation between otherwise similar wood-furniture blocks.
- No render bugs — every image loads cleanly, text is legible, spacing is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No price or discount anywhere in the visible email — for a furniture purchase (a considered, high-ticket category), buyers expect at least a "starting at $X" anchor, and it's just not here.
- Four full collections back-to-back (Scarlett → Barley → Sausalito → grid) is a lot of scroll before anything differentiates itself; by the third dresser-and-nightstand pairing it blurs together.
- Subject line is weak — "Have you seen our new bedroom furniture?" is a rhetorical question with no specificity, no urgency, no reason to open now versus next week.
- No loyalty/rewards mention until deep in the footer (10% Rewards, Affirm) — buried well past where most recipients will have stopped scrolling.
- Given this brand's recent cadence of sale/free-shipping sends, a value-free browsing email risks reading as a lower priority to a subscriber conditioned to expect a discount.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price anchor to the hero or at least the first collection — "Starting at $999" or similar — furniture buyers won't click through four screens of product with zero pricing context.
- 2. Cut from four full collections to two, and move "Complete Your Retreat" (the categories grid) up higher as a secondary path for browsers who don't want a full bedroom set.
- 3. Sharpen the subject line to name the actual news, not ask a question.
- 4. Pull the 10% Rewards / Affirm financing line up near the top of the email — for a high-ticket category, "pay over time" is a real purchase-decision unlock, not a footer afterthought.
- 5. If there's any current promo (even free shipping), surface it in the hero — right now this reads as the one Pottery Barn send with nothing for the wallet.

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "Have you seen our new bedroom furniture?"

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard MSO/Outlook-compatible HTML email built on a legacy table-based template with third-party open/click tracking pixels; core rendering markup is sound, but compliance headers are unverifiable and accessibility/protocol hygiene has multiple gaps.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Primary nav CTA routes through a branded click-tracking redirector: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEs...` (Cheetah/CM Digital-style token). Not independently resolvable without following the redirect.
- 69 tracking/click-redirect links total were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design — redirect domains aren't followed), so destination validity for the bulk of links is **unconfirmed**, not verified-good.
- Open-tracking pixels present: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` and `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEv.../cat/1` — the `svopn` pixel is served over **plain HTTP**, which many mail clients/proxies will block or strip, silently degrading open-rate data.
- Recipient PII is embedded directly in tracking pixel URLs in cleartext: `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This exposes the recipient's email address to any intermediary logging these plain-HTTP requests.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **43 images with no `alt` text** flagged by QA (banners, product slices, footer slices, tracking pixels). Functional/content images (e.g., `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`, `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`) losing alt text is the more significant a11y issue vs. decorative slice/spacer images.
- **3 image assets served over HTTP instead of HTTPS**, likely to be blocked or generate mixed-content warnings in modern mail clients:

  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`

These will render as literal invalid CSS text rather than execute — dead/leftover code that should be removed but is otherwise inert.

- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` present — intentional for hosted-email-as-webpage scenarios, not a defect.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — no RFC 2369 header captured. One-click unsubscribe via mailbox provider UI (Gmail/Yahoo "Unsubscribe" button) will not be available unless this header exists on the raw message.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (RFC 8058) — one-click POST unsubscribe unsupported/unconfirmed. As of Feb 2024, Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements mandate both headers for senders at scale; their absence here is either a relay-capture gap (AgentMail) or a genuine sender-side omission — cannot distinguish from this data alone.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this data; cannot confirm authentication compliance.
- No CAN-SPAM footer content (physical address, unsubscribe link) is visible in the truncated HTML provided — cannot confirm compliance; likely present in the untruncated footer given the footer image assets (`Email-Footer/slices_*.jpg`) referenced.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Primary CTA and body links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirectors — final landing URLs and their UTM parameters are not visible in this source, so continuity to potterybarn.com landing pages cannot be verified.

- Customer service link resolves directly (non-redirected): `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — consistent domain, no continuity issue.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (or confirm with the sending platform/relay why they aren't being captured) to meet Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements and enable one-click unsubscribe.
2. Migrate the two remaining HTTP-only asset URLs (`edm.westelm.com`, `edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey`, `.../0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner`) to HTTPS to prevent blocked images and mixed-content flags.
3. Add `alt` text to all product images at minimum (bed, dresser, table, sheet set) for screen-reader accessibility; decorative slice/spacer images can use `alt=""`.
4. Route the open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) over HTTPS to stop transmitting recipient email addresses in plaintext over an unencrypted connection.
5. Remove the stray literal `header(...)` PHP-style statements from the `<style>` block in `<head>` — dead code with no functional effect but adds noise/confusion for future template edits.
6. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment directly with the sending ESP, since Authentication-Results couldn't be captured via this relay.
