# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn's homepage and was immediately stopped cold by a full-screen email/SMS signup modal before I could see a single product or promo. The brand clearly knows its audience — the glimpse of a cream sofa with a bold floral pillow behind the modal looks exactly like my second home aesthetic — but burying the experience behind a legal-heavy consent wall is a rough first impression. Right now the homepage is basically a newsletter gate with a brand logo.

## What's working

- **The lifestyle image behind the modal** — even half-obscured, that cream sofa with a floral throw pillow is exactly the kind of aspirational-but-livable image that stops me mid-scroll. Pottery Barn still knows how to shoot a room.
- **The modal headline** — "Find out first: New arrivals, exclusive savings & more." is a reasonably compelling value prop. For a catalog-driven shopper like me, "new arrivals" is genuinely interesting.
- **Email + SMS dual capture** — smart that they're collecting both channels in one form, since I actually do shop from their emails.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand family context** — the fine print ties this to the broader WSI ecosystem I'm already loyal to, which adds implicit trust.

## What's weak

- **The modal fires immediately on page load** — I haven't seen a single product, price, or room before being asked to hand over my phone number. That's backwards.
- **Two paragraphs of TCPA legalese dominate the modal** — it's the biggest text block I can read, and it's legal boilerplate. For a premium brand, this kills the mood instantly.
- **No rewards/loyalty hook** — I'm a Williams-Sonoma Key Rewards member. Nothing in this modal (or what I can see of the page behind it) acknowledges that.
- **The navigation glimpse is cut off** — I can barely read "Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm, Business to Business, Willi..." — nothing about living room, bedroom, or the categories I actually buy.
- **No concrete offer** — "exclusive savings" with no number attached is marketing language, not an offer. Just tell me it's 20% off.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the modal to 15–20 seconds or second pageview** — let me see the homepage, fall in love with the sofa, then pop the signup. I'll convert at much higher rates when I'm already engaged.
- **Add a member-recognition banner above the modal or in the nav** — "Welcome back, Lila — check your Key Rewards balance" turns a cold visit into a warm one.
- **Replace "exclusive savings" with a specific, time-bounded offer** — "Sign up for 15% off your next order, valid through Sunday" is a hook; a vague promise is not.
- **Trim the legalese or collapse it into a disclosure link** — lead with the benefit, footnote the compliance copy; the current ratio is inverted.
