# Warehouse deals up to 60% off + Inside our NEW catalog
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a subject/body bait-and-switch. The subject promises "Warehouse deals up to 60% off + Inside our NEW catalog," but the render is almost entirely catalog content — "THE FALL CATALOG," "Discover 1600+ new arrivals," in-store design classes, curated product picks, new arrivals, and a Halloween shop teaser. The only nod to the warehouse sale is a thin top banner strip ("The Warehouse Sale: Up to 60% off") that's easy to miss and never repeated or reinforced with a dedicated CTA.
- Compare this to the 2026-09-16 "Save up to 60% across the home" send, which led immediately with the discount and scored 7.00 for doing exactly that. This email takes the same headline number and buries it under a catalog announcement, diluting both messages — the discount doesn't get its moment, and the catalog story doesn't get to breathe as its own send.
- Net: a well-produced, clean-looking email that undersells its best asset. Subject over-promises, body under-delivers on that promise.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent Pottery Barn visual identity — serif logo, warm brown palette, cohesive photography style throughout.
- Clear top-level navigation via the category grid (Pillows & Decor, Armchairs, Bedding, etc.) gives multiple entry points.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section shows real personalization/segmentation with six distinct, price-relevant products (rugs, chairs, curtains, tables).
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken layout.
- Halloween Shop section is timely and seasonally on-point for mid-September.

## What's weak

- The 60%-off warehouse sale — the entire premise of the subject line — is reduced to a one-line banner at the very top and never appears again in the body.
- No visible dollar or percentage figure anywhere in the main body; the offer is "honest" but effectively invisible.
- Hero section CTA is "GET INSPIRED," a vague, non-transactional verb that doesn't drive a purchase decision.
- Two competing narratives (new catalog vs. warehouse sale) fight for attention with no clear hierarchy — the eye lands on "THE FALL CATALOG," not the deal.
- Cadence risk: this follows directly on the heels of 2026-09-16's dedicated warehouse-sale email, so subscribers who opened that one may feel this is a re-tread with a diluted offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send or reorder the body — if the subject leads with the sale, the sale needs its own hero block with an actual price/percentage and a "SHOP THE SALE" button above the catalog content, not a thin banner.
- 2. Replace "GET INSPIRED" with a concrete CTA tied to the sale, e.g. "Shop Up to 60% Off."
- 3. Add a second, mid-body sale reminder (banner + CTA) near the new-arrivals block so scrollers don't lose the offer.
- 4. If the goal is truly catalog-first, drop the "60% off" framing from the subject — don't set an expectation the body can't pay off.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Fall Catalog + Sale: Up to 60% Off Inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `1600+ New Arrivals Just Dropped (Plus a Warehouse Sale)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh looks for fall, and markdowns up to 60% while they last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals, design inspiration, and our biggest warehouse discounts.`
- 5. Consider running the Halloween Shop and Design Classes content in a separate send — this email already carries three distinct offers (sale, catalog, Halloween) competing for one click.
