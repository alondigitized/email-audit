# Up to 60% off & selling FAST + Dining room furniture from Chris Loves Julia
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's tenth email in three days anchored to the Summer Warehouse Sale, and the subject line captures the creative tension perfectly: a fire-sale urgency hook ("selling FAST") jammed into the same breath as a considered designer announcement ("Dining room furniture from Chris Loves Julia"). Those two messages want opposite tones — scarcity vs. aspiration — and forcing them into one 75-character subject line weakens both.
- Inside, the email actually earns more respect than the subject suggests. The Chris Loves Julia feature is a genuine creative pivot — the first named-designer collab in this campaign run — and it gives the dining room section real credibility. The visual execution is clean: a dark warehouse-sale hero followed by a warm, lifestyle-forward collab feature, then a recommended-for-you grid. The hierarchy works.
- The structural problem remains cadence. A real Pottery Barn subscriber has now received this brand in their inbox every single day since June 5, always with "up to 60% off." The collab angle is strong enough to stand alone; burying it behind a ninth repetition of the same sale headline squanders it.

## What's working

- **Chris Loves Julia collab section is a genuine hook** — a named-designer feature differentiates this email from the warehouse-sale drumbeat and gives a reason to click beyond discount hunting.
- **Two distinct sections with separate CTAs** — the warehouse-sale hero and the collab module each have their own "Shop Now," reducing the feeling that everything funnels into one generic sale page.
- **Recommended Just For You grid** — a personalized product grid anchors the lower half and adds perceived relevance without requiring another hero.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage visible in the screenshot.
- **"Selling FAST" urgency** is mild and honest-feeling, not scream-y — no !!! or ALL CAPS stacking.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is 75 characters and tries to do two jobs at once** — the warehouse sale urgency and the collab announcement fight each other; neither lands clean on a mobile lock screen.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the only CTA copy across both modules** — no specificity; "Shop the Collab" or "Shop Dining Tables" would each convert better.
- **Tenth email in three days** — even with new creative, the cadence is the dominant subscriber experience right now; suppression fatigue is almost certainly dragging open rates.
- **Warehouse sale hero leads again** — the Chris Loves Julia story is the actually new thing here; putting the generic sale banner first buries the lead.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hierarchy — lead with the collab.** The Chris Loves Julia dining story is the freshest thing in this email. Make it the hero. Push the warehouse-sale module to secondary. The sale is already well-established in subscribers' minds after nine prior sends; the new angle needs the prime real estate.
- 2. **Split this into two separate emails, or drop the warehouse-sale module entirely.** The Chris Loves Julia angle is strong enough to carry a standalone send. Forcing it to share the subject with "selling FAST" dilutes the designer credibility.
- 3. **Specify the CTA copy.** Replace both "SHOP NOW" instances with something like "Shop the Collab" and "Shop Dining Tables" to create click intent tied to the content module.
- 4. **Cap or skip an in-between warehouse-sale send.** If the June 7 plan already included a collab send, the additional generic warehouse-sale sends from earlier today are cannibalizing it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code artifact in `<style>` block**
The client stylesheet block contains literal PHP `header()` function calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are inert in a rendered email but confirm the template was not properly compiled before send — server-side PHP leaked into HTML output. This is a build/QA pipeline defect.

**33 images missing `alt` text**
Affected assets include:
- `S26_TOP_SummerWarehouseSale_NoUrgency.jpg` (hero banner — no fallback text if images blocked)
- All 18 `slices_0N.jpg/gif` in the Chris Loves Julia Dining block
- All 18 `slices_0N.jpg` in the Email-Footer block
- Product images: `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-l.jpg`, and others
- Tracking pixels: open-pixel at `svopn.potterybarn.com`, turn.com pixel, Sailthru open beacon

Missing alt on content images breaks the email entirely in image-off environments (common in corporate Outlook, Gmail web default). Missing alt on tracking pixels is a minor standards violation but not operationally harmful.

**4 images served over HTTP (mixed content)**

| File | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` (CC banner) | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open pixel | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...` |

Gmail and Apple Mail proxy or block HTTP image sources. The open-pixel being HTTP means open tracking will fail silently in those clients.

**Zoom disabled via viewport + CSS**
`maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta tag combined with `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;}` prevents users from zooming on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 failure and an accessibility risk.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — some clients (Samsung Mail, some Outlook builds) surface the title in notifications or the message list. An empty title degrades discoverability.
