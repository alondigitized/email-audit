# ⏳ HOURS LEFT for extra 20% off + Labor Day Sale favorites for every room
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the same burgundy "Labor Day Sale" banner Pottery Barn has run essentially every day for two weeks — "Extra 20% off / Select styles. Plus new deals up to 70% off," the same dining-table hero, the same category grid. It's a placeholder header propping up a subject line that's now stale copy ("Labor Day Sale favorites") sitting on top of a campaign nobody would still call Labor Day this deep into September.
- Below the fold it recovers some ground: a genuinely strong "Enter If You Dare" Halloween Shop module (haunted-house porch with ghost decor) breaks the monotony and reads like real seasonal merchandising, and the "Recommended Just For You" product grid with named items (Toscana Dining Table, Cayman Platform Bed, Westin Mattress) gives the email actual personalization signal that most of the recent sends lacked. But leading with the fourteenth identical burgundy banner, now stacked with an "Hours Left" urgency subject that reads as recycled, undercuts whatever goodwill the Halloween creative earns further down.
- Judgement: a mid-pack send — functional, on-brand, zero render bugs, but creatively exhausted at the top and reliant on scroll to find anything new.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, no broken images, category grid and product tiles all display correctly.
- The Halloween Shop module ("Enter If You Dare") is a genuine creative departure with strong seasonal styling — porch lit with ghosts, pumpkins, string lights.
- Product recommendations are specific and named (Toscana Extendable Dining Table, Remmy Swivel Chair, Westin Heavenly Bed Mattress) rather than generic tiles.
- Clear dual CTA structure up top ("Shop Extra 20% Off" / "Shop Furniture Sale") gives an unambiguous next step.
- Loyalty/financing content present (Affirm, Pottery Barn credit card 10% back) for repeat shoppers.

## What's weak

- Hero banner is the identical burgundy "Labor Day Sale / Extra 20% off" creative Pottery Barn has run daily since at least 2026-09-07 — zero visual differentiation from the last five sends I've reviewed.
- Subject promises "Labor Day Sale favorites for every room" but the campaign itself is stale — Labor Day language this far past the holiday reads as a leftover, not urgency.
- No countdown clock or visible deadline treatment despite "Hours Left" urgency in the subject — the banner itself doesn't reinforce the time pressure at all.
- The best creative (Halloween Shop) is buried four scrolls down, well past the point most recipients disengage.
- "Free Design Services" and app-download modules add length without adding conversion value — filler before the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the Halloween Shop hero at the top, not the burgundy Labor Day banner — it's the strongest asset in the email and currently it's rewarding only the most patient scrollers.
- 2. Add a visible countdown or explicit end-time ("Ends tonight at 11:59pm ET") to the hero if the subject is going to lead with "Hours Left" — right now the urgency is a subject-line claim with no visual backup.
- 3. Retire the "Labor Day Sale" banner language entirely — rename the evergreen 20%-off promo something season-neutral so it can run without looking recycled.
- 4. Move the "Recommended Just For You" grid up, directly under the primary CTA — it's the most personalized, differentiated content in the email and it's currently competing with six banner modules before it.
- 5. Trim the Design Crew / App / credit-card stack to one module — three low-intent modules back-to-back dilutes the email before the footer.

## Technical Audit

**Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Hours Left" Email**

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard PB/Williams-Sonoma email platform template with a functional tracking pixel and personalized unsubscribe path; primary issues are missing List-Unsubscribe headers, blanket alt-text omission, and several legacy HTTP (non-TLS) image assets.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 55 tracking/redirect links (click.e.potterybarn.com, clickcp.potterybarn.com) were skipped by the automated HTTP prober as expected redirect-domain behavior — not a defect, but unverified for destination validity.
- Secondary tracking pixel `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` fires over plain HTTP and embeds the recipient email address in a clear-text query string — both a mixed-content risk (may be blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS images) and a PII-in-URL exposure (logged in plaintext by any intermediate proxy/CDN).
- Third-party pixel `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee) present — confirms ad-network retargeting tag alongside standard ESP tracking.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 40+ `<img>` elements across hero, product, and footer slices are missing `alt` attributes (QA-confirmed) — screen readers and image-blocked clients (default in Outlook) will render nothing meaningful in place of these images.
- Two images load over `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients that enforce TLS for remote images.
- Malformed inline `<style>` block in `<head>` contains raw PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, `header("Cache-Control: ...")`) — these are not valid CSS and are dead code left in from a template/copy-paste error; harmless (parsers ignore invalid declarations) but indicates unclean markup.
- Uses legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype with MSO/VML conditional comments — appropriate defensive coding for Outlook desktop rendering, no issue.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) found in the visible HTML sample.
- Personalization is present and correctly resolved in tracking URLs (e.g. `lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` embedded in `coreemail.html?email=...` and the `svopn.potterybarn.com` pixel), confirming the ESP successfully merged recipient identity server-side.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — CAN-SPAM requires a clear opt-out mechanism, which may exist via in-body footer link (not visible in truncated source), but the missing header means one-click unsubscribe support in the inbox UI (Gmail/Outlook "Unsubscribe" button) is unconfirmed.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected — one-click unsubscribe via POST is not supported, a growing requirement for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo sender guidelines.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture; flagged as a relay-visibility gap (AgentMail) rather than confirmed spoofing/auth failure.
- Both header gaps may be artifacts of the AgentMail relay stripping/not forwarding headers rather than the sender omitting them — cannot confirm true absence at origin from this data alone.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- Primary CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect tracking rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs — final landing page and UTM parameter alignment cannot be verified without following the 302 chain (all 55 were skipped by the prober).
- Footer customer-service link is direct (not tracked): `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — resolves to the brand's own domain, no redirect-chain concern here.
- No issues found regarding domain consistency — all tracked/product links resolve to `potterybarn.com` or `e.potterybarn.com` subdomains, no off-brand or suspicious domains detected.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all content-bearing images (product shots, hero banners); decorative spacers/pixels can use `alt=""` to satisfy accessibility checkers without adding noise.
2. Migrate the two remaining `http://` image sources to `https://` to prevent mixed-content blocking in modern mail clients.
3. Move the `sv_emopen` open-tracking pixel off cleartext HTTP and stop passing raw email addresses in the query string — hash or tokenize the recipient identifier instead.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at origin (check directly against the ESP's raw send, not just this relay capture) — required for compliance with 2024+ Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules.
5. Remove the dead PHP `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block — non-functional and confusing to anyone maintaining the template.
6. Verify the 55 skipped tracking redirects resolve to the intended UTM-tagged landing pages (spot-check a sample manually), since automated probing was skipped.
