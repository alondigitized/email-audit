# Free design services for your outdoor refresh
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn delivers a polished, on-brand service promotion pitching free design consultations for an outdoor refresh. The email is well-structured — a clear hero headline, a step-by-step process demystifying the service, and a product recommendation module anchoring the session with shoppable items. The offer is genuinely valuable (complimentary human design advice), the seasonal timing is right for spring, and the brand renders cleanly. The main gap is urgency: there's no deadline visible anywhere, so the RSVP framing feels soft. The email is long, and the secondary modules (app, credit card, financing) dilute the single-action goal.

## What's working

- **Headline clarity.** "RSVP for Free Design Services" is direct and value-forward — the word "Free" above the fold does real work.
- **Step-by-step process module.** Showing 3 concrete steps removes the ambiguity that often kills service conversions ("What will actually happen?"). This is the strongest structural element.
- **Seasonal alignment.** Outdoor refresh in May is timely; the lush garden imagery reinforces the moment.
- **Product recommendations.** The "Recommended Just for You" grid keeps the email shoppable — someone not ready to book a consultation can still browse and convert on product.
- **Brand consistency.** The aesthetic is unmistakably Pottery Barn: muted tones, aspirational lifestyle photography, premium feel throughout.

## What's weak

- **No urgency.** "RSVP" implies scarcity but there's no deadline, no "offer ends," no "limited appointments." The CTA lands soft.
- **Email length.** The secondary modules — app download, credit card, financing — stack well below the primary goal and compete for attention. A design consultation email doesn't need all three.
- **Service specificity.** The copy describes the service in broad strokes but doesn't answer the immediate objection: *How long will this take? Is it virtual or in-store?* A one-line answer would reduce friction.
- **No social proof.** No testimonials, star ratings, or "X customers served" visible. For a service ask (not a product purchase), a trust signal would meaningfully lift conversion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** "Book by May 25 — limited slots available" is all it takes. RSVP framing already implies limited supply; follow through on it.
- 2. **Trim secondary modules.** Cut the app and financing blocks from this send. They're conversion exits that compete with the Book Now CTA.
- 3. **Add one customer quote.** A single line — *"Our designer helped me find exactly what I needed in 20 minutes"* — would address the implicit objection that design services are time-consuming or intimidating.
- 4. **Answer the logistics question above the fold.** A single line like "Virtual or in-store — your choice, 100% free" reduces the friction of clicking without knowing what you're committing to.
- 5. **Test "Free" in the subject with a deadline variant** (see Alt A below).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — 2 images**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content images. These will silently fail in secure clients.

**Missing alt text — 30+ images**
Every slice image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, and all tracking pixels lack `alt` attributes. With images disabled (common in corporate/Outlook environments), the email renders as blank. Critical slices (hero, CTA buttons) need descriptive alt text; decorative spacers should use `alt=""`.

**PHP headers in `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
```
Server-side PHP `header()` calls are embedded literally inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in email (not executed), but they are noise that will confuse validators and could indicate a template generation error — the dynamic image-serving code may have been incorrectly pasted into the HTML body.

**Viewport meta — minor**
`<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1,width=device-width" />` — uses non-standard `width=` instead of `width=device-width`. Should be `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
`List-Unsubscribe` was not found by the relay. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`) is additionally required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). Verify the header is present at the MTA level — it may be stripped by the AgentMail relay before capture.

**WARN — SPF/DKIM status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud subdomain). Deliverability depends on correct SPF/DKIM alignment for that subdomain — cannot confirm from relay data alone.

No CAN-SPAM physical address issues detected (footer content was truncated; assumed present based on standard PB templates).

If the click-redirect does not inject `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`, GA4/analytics attribution for this campaign will be dark (attributed to direct or organic). This should be confirmed by inspecting a redirect destination.
