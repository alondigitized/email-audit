# Looking for FREE design advice?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- "Looking for FREE design advice?" is Pottery Barn's service-push email — a genre shift from the discount hammer that has defined this June batch. The hero pitches a free design appointment with three access modalities (at home, virtual, or in-store), anchored by a June 21 in-store Mix & Match Press event with a specific day and time. This is structurally smart: a no-cost consultation is a meaningful value prop that doesn't cheapen the brand the way a 70% off clearance blast does.
- The problem is the email can't stop adding modules. Below the hero you get the event banner, a six-SKU "Recommended Just For You" product grid, an app download pitch, the rewards program, and an Affirm financing block. That's six competing asks in one send. By the time you've scrolled through curtains and quilts, the consultation offer has evaporated. A clean service email became a kitchen-sink blast.
- The timing makes it worse. This is the second Pottery Barn hit in the inbox on June 19 — the other being "It's ON! The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF" (scored 7/10). Selling a fire-sale clearance and premium personalized design services on the same afternoon muddles positioning. The service email would punch harder on a non-sale day, when it isn't competing with its own brand.

## What's working

- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is the best CTA in this June batch** — specific verb, zero ambiguity, real perceived value.
- **Three access modes** (at home, virtual, in-store) lower the barrier and widen the audience without adding copy clutter.
- **June 21 event with "Sunday 11AM"** is the most credible urgency signal in any Pottery Barn email this month — a specific day and time beats "limited time" every time.
- **Loyalty module earns its keep here** — the 10% back reminder is a genuine nudge for members weighing whether to bother booking.

## What's weak

- **Six competing asks kill the consultation CTA** — the product grid, app download, and Affirm block each demand separate attention and collectively bury the lead.
- **Product recommendations actively contradict the message** — a "Recommended Just For You" grid signals transactional intent and undercuts the consultative, high-touch frame the hero is trying to build.
- **No trust signals on the design service itself** — asking someone to book a one-on-one appointment is a higher-commitment ask than clicking "shop now." There's no social proof, no designer credentials, no volume signal ("10,000 appointments booked").
- **"FREE" in all-caps in the subject** is a mild spam signal and feels tonally inconsistent with the editorial voice Pottery Barn was successfully building in the June 18 sends (both scored 7-8/10).
- **Same-day as the 70% off email** creates a brand identity collision — premium service and fire-sale clearance do not coexist gracefully in a single inbox day.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill everything below the event banner.** Strip the product grid, app download, and Affirm block. Let the appointment CTA and June 21 event carry the email. Recommendations belong in the post-booking confirmation, not competing with the primary ask.
- 2. **Add one trust signal above the CTA button** — even a single line like "Our in-house designers have helped X,000 customers find their look" raises click confidence for a high-commitment action.
- 3. **Explicitly connect the event to the booking CTA** — reframe the banner as "Book your appointment before the June 21 event" so the two offers reinforce each other instead of running in parallel.
- 4. **Lowercase "free" or rephrase the subject entirely** — the all-caps reads promotional rather than editorial.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free design consultation starts here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet a Pottery Barn designer — on us`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book before the June 21 in-store event — at home, virtual, or in store`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A Pottery Barn designer is ready for you. It's free.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Design Crew Invite" (0619_FRI_AM2)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two broken asset/link requests (one 403 cross-brand image, one 403 destination URL in a commented block), PHP server-side code erroneously embedded inside a `<style>` tag, two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources, and pervasive missing `alt` attributes across 30+ images. Compliance headers are unconfirmed at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer returns 403**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand asset from a sister domain is returning 403. In email clients that block broken images, this can shift layout if the spacer is load-bearing.

**[FAIL] Credit card page returns 403 (commented out — latent)**
`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` — the link is inside a commented-out `<!-- -->` block so it is not live in this send. However, the asset destination itself is broken; reactivating the block without fixing the URL would send subscribers to a 403.

**[INFO] 25 click-tracking links not probed**
All CTA links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=<opaque-base64>`. Destination URLs and UTM params cannot be verified from the HTML alone; requires click-redirect resolution.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP `header()` calls embedded in `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
a[x-apple-data-detectors] { ...
```
Server-side PHP code was pasted directly into a CSS `<style>` block. It does not execute as PHP in this context, but email clients will attempt to parse it as CSS and fail silently or emit parse errors. The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule that follows is valid CSS and will likely still apply in most clients, but this is a template defect indicating a copy-paste error from an image-generation script.

**[FAIL] Two images served over HTTP (mixed content)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. These images will be suppressed or replaced by broken-image icons in those clients.

**[WARN] 30+ images missing `alt` text**
Includes all body content slices (`slices_01.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`), all 18 Email-Footer slices, product images (sheet set, quilt, bed, curtains, curtain rod bracket), and all third-party tracking pixels. When images are blocked — the default state in many clients before user interaction — the email renders as a blank white field with no text fallback.

Notable subset: the two main hero images (`slices_01.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg`) carry the primary Design Crew messaging and have empty `alt=""`. Subscribers with images off see nothing above the fold.

**[INFO] Mobile breakpoint mismatch**
`@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` targets `table[class="wrapper"]` at 320px, but the responsive `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` breakpoint fires at 650px. The two media queries are not coordinated; devices between 480–650px width will show the mobile show/hide state but the wrapper will not collapse to 320px.

**[INFO] XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype**
`<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Strict//EN" ...>` — outdated; modern clients ignore it and render in standards mode anyway. Not a rendering risk but signals legacy template age.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header absent at relay**
QA did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Per CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (effective Feb 2024), commercial emails must support one-click unsubscribe. If the header is stripped by the AgentMail relay rather than absent at the ESP layer, verify at the raw SMTP envelope — but if genuinely missing, this is a deliverability and compliance risk.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`POST` method) is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders. Not confirmed present.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown. `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` with sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` should be covered by a DKIM selector on that subdomain — cannot confirm from the truncated headers available.

**[INFO] CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link**
Not visible in the truncated source but expected to be present in the footer slices rendered as images (`slices_01.jpg` – `slices_18.jpg`). Since those are image-only, the address and opt-out are inaccessible to text-mode readers and screen readers. CAN-SPAM requires the opt-out mechanism to be operable for 30 days post-send regardless of image state.
