# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-30
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn and the first thing that hit me was a full-screen email/SMS signup modal — before I could see a single product category or even the real homepage. The imagery inside it is genuinely lovely (a cream sofa with colorful florals, very second-home-in-the-Hamptons energy), and the brand clearly knows its aesthetic. But right now they're asking me to hand over my phone number before they've shown me a single reason to stay.

## What's working

- **The lifestyle photo in the modal** — cream upholstered sofa, stacked books, colorful floral accent pillows — is exactly on-brand and hits my decorating sensibility instantly
- **The headline register** — "Find out first: New arrivals, exclusive savings & more." — is clean Millennial copy: direct, not cutesy, not corporate
- **The X close button** is clearly visible in the top right, which reduces the feeling of being trapped
- **Chat bubble** is present — reassuring if I have a quick question about a fabric or lead time

## What's weak

- **Immediate full-screen modal with no value exchange** — I didn't browse one product, and they want my mobile number. That's the wrong sequence for someone who is in a considered-purchase headspace
- **Vague offer copy** — "exclusive savings" with no number attached is not a reason to subscribe; it reads like every other retailer's popup
- **The legal fine print is the second-largest text block on screen** — the TCPA disclaimer eats more visual real estate than the offer itself, which signals risk rather than reward
- **The real homepage is completely obscured** — I can't see any featured categories, current campaign, or seasonal hero, so I have zero context for what Pottery Barn is actually promoting today
- **The nav strip visible at top shows Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm** — none of my categories — and it's the only navigational signal I have right now

## Recommendations

- **Show one concrete number in the modal headline** — "Get 15% off your first order" converts dramatically better than "exclusive savings & more"; even "early access + free shipping on your next order" gives me a reason to enter my email
- **Delay the modal by 8-10 seconds or one scroll** — let me see the hero product and a category or two before asking for my contact info; I need to want something before I'll subscribe
- **Compress or link-out the TCPA block** — move the full legal text to a "Terms" link and show just the one-line summary; the wall of fine print is the visual antithesis of the aspirational sofa image right above it
- **Add a rewards/Key Rewards hook inside the modal** — something like "Key Rewards members earn 10× this week" would give returning shoppers a reason to stay engaged and would resonate immediately with someone already in the Williams-Sonoma ecosystem
