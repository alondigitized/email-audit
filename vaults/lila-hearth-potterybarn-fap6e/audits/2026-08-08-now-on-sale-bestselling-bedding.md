# Now on sale: Bestselling bedding
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn running its now-familiar playbook — subject-matched hero, three named bedding products, then a long tail of cross-sell — and it mostly works the way the 2026-08-07 "Coffee table styling 101" and "Must-see dining chairs" sends did: the subject says bedding, the hero says bedding, the first three product blocks are bedding. That discipline is the email's biggest strength.
- Where it slips is value communication and cadence fatigue. Not one product (Cloud Quilt, Belgian Flax Linen Duvet, Pick-Stitch Quilt) shows a price or a specific discount — the "Up to 60% off" claim lives only in a generic top banner, and that exact banner is a repeat of the 2026-08-06 "ON NOW✨ The Warehouse Sale" send. Then, after "Discover New Arrivals," the email pivots hard into "The Halloween Shop — Enter If You Dare," which is a jarring seasonal detour inside what was otherwise a tight, single-category bedding email.
- Solid execution, recycled offer framing, and one genuinely odd module placement. Middle-of-the-pack for this brand — worse than the best category sends this week, better than the sweepstakes-disguised-as-service send from 2026-08-07.

## What's working

- Subject-to-hero match is exact: "bestselling bedding" copy leads straight into three named, photographed bedding products.
- Three distinct hero products (Cloud Quilt, Belgian Flax Linen Duvet, Pick-Stitch Quilt) each get a full lifestyle photo, avoiding a cluttered single collage.
- One clear, large offer statement ("Up to 60% off") at the very top with an unambiguous "SHOP NOW" button.
- Rewards (10% back) and Affirm financing are both surfaced for shoppers close to converting.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, no broken images, the 6-tile "Recommended Just for You" grid displays evenly.

## What's weak

- No product shows a price or a specific markdown — the 60% off claim is disconnected from every item pictured.
- The top banner and offer framing are identical to the 2026-08-06 Warehouse Sale send, two days prior — recycled creative, not a fresh angle.
- "The Halloween Shop — Enter If You Dare" appears inside a bedding-sale email in early August, an abrupt seasonal and category swap right after the bedding and new-arrivals content.
- "More Deals You'll Love" (Furniture, Lighting, Pillows & Decor, Bath) is plain text links with no imagery, breaking the otherwise photo-forward rhythm of the email.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Attach the discount to the actual products — show "Cloud Quilt, now $XX" style pricing on all three hero items instead of leaving 60% off as a disembodied banner claim. This is the single biggest lift for click-through.
- 2. Cut or relocate the Halloween Shop module — it breaks category focus and reads as premature seasonal cross-promotion inside a bedding email; save it for its own send.
- 3. Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA like "Shop Bedding Sale" — the current copy is generic and doesn't reinforce what's actually on offer.
- 4. Add thumbnail images to the "More Deals You'll Love" grid so it doesn't read as a dead zone between two photo-heavy sections.
- 5. Refresh the top banner creative — repeating "Up to 60% off" verbatim from the 2026-08-06 Warehouse Sale send within 48 hours flattens urgency for a subscriber getting daily sends.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cloud Quilt, Belgian Flax Linen Duvet & more — now up to 60% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Soft layers, big markdowns. Shop bestselling bedding now.`

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) template with functional tracking/personalization, but ships with widespread missing `alt` attributes, several non-HTTPS image sources, and no confirmable unsubscribe/authentication headers in the captured data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All primary CTAs route through the ExactTarget click-tracking domain `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (e.g., header logo link), and a secondary tracker `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/2d5808bf-2a4d-4376-980f-b95e47853554?mi_mid=019fe251-3280-7000-a847-d77433759c97&cp_tp` is also present. 56 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe, so final destination URLs and their HTTP status could not be verified — actual landing pages are unconfirmed.

- No issues found with tracking domain structure itself; the concern is limited to unverified destinations and the one open-tracking pixel using plaintext HTTP.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 40 images are missing `alt` text, spanning hero banners, product slices, and footer assets (e.g., `F26_TOP_WarehouseSale.jpg`, `slices_01–18.jpg`, `reeva-handwoven-rug-3-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`). This degrades experience for image-blocked/screen-reader clients since no fallback text renders.
- 4 image sources use plain `http://` instead of `https://`, which mail clients with mixed-content or HTTPS-only image proxying may block or flag:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`

- Notable oddity: `spacer_0.gif` and `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` are served from `edm.westelm.com` and a "thekey"/legacy 2022 path respectively inside a Pottery Barn send — cross-brand/stale template asset reuse, worth flagging to the ESP template owner even though it's not breaking rendering.

This is inert/broken leftover code — it won't execute, but it's invalid CSS content that should be removed from the template source.
- CSS uses broad `img { max-width: ... }` media-query rules and standard Outlook/MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if mso ...]-->`) — no issues found in the client-compatibility scaffolding itself.
