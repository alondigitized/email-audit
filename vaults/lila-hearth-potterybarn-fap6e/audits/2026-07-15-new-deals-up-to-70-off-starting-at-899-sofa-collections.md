# New deals up to 70% off + Starting at $899: Sofa collections
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn makes the right call narrowing this to a single category — sofas and sectionals — after a week of compound-subject scattershot emails. The hero photography is genuinely beautiful: a cream sofa in a light-flooded room with warm wood floors, the kind of aspirational image that makes you re-evaluate your own living room. The product grid below (Camille, Chaddock, Buchanan, Pacifica, each with a named starting price) is clean and scannable.
- The problem is cadence and déjà vu. This is the fourth "UP TO 70% OFF" compound-subject email in three days from Pottery Barn. The July 14 "Summer deals ☀️ UP TO 70% OFF + New arrivals" (8/10) had nearly identical bones, and the July 13 evening send ran the same formula. By now, regular subscribers have learned to treat that subject pattern as background noise — the discount offer is so persistent it's lost urgency. The colon-separated sofa hook in today's subject is genuinely differentiated, but the "New deals up to 70% off +" opener conditions readers to expect another generic sale.
- Tacked-on modules hurt what could be a clean email. The "Recommended Just for You" section shows dark accessory items — looks like throw pillows — that have nothing to do with sofas. The "Discover New Arrivals" coastal section at the bottom feels like a completely different email. Cut both, and this becomes a tight, category-focused send worth opening.

## What's working

- Hero lifestyle photography is excellent — the cream sofa in a bright, natural room is aspirational and immediately communicates the category.
- Product grid is well-structured: four named sofa lines, each with a distinct image and "Starting at" price, easy to compare at a glance.
- "Starting at $899" in the subject is a price anchor Pottery Barn rarely uses — it signals approachability without needing a click.
- Render is completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no visible layout breaks.
- The SHOP NOW CTA in the hero is clearly visible and positioned before the fold.

## What's weak

- Cadence fatigue: fourth "UP TO 70% OFF" compound-format email in three days from the same sender; the formula has lost its signal.
- "Recommended Just for You" module shows what appear to be dark throw pillows — generic, off-topic, and visibly disconnected from the sofa campaign.
- No deadline anywhere visible — "New deals" implies freshness but offers no urgency to act today vs. next week.
- "New Arrivals" coastal section at the bottom fragments the sofa focus without adding value.
- Subject line at 62 characters will truncate on most mobile clients, losing "Sofa collections" — the most specific part.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline** — even "Ends Sunday" in the hero subhead would convert browsers to buyers. The persistent 70% off message has trained subscribers to assume the sale never ends.
- 2. **Break the compound subject formula** — drop the "X + Y" structure that's run four times this week and lead directly with the category and price: something sharper (see alts below).
- 3. **Kill the Recommended module or retrain it to show sofa alternatives** — dark accessories in a sofa email signal broken personalization, which erodes trust.
- 4. **Cut the New Arrivals section** — this email's single job is to sell sofas. The bottom-of-email editorial is extra scroll for no conversion lift.
- 5. **Specifify the hero CTA** — "Shop Sofas" beats "SHOP NOW" because it confirms where the button goes and reinforces the category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sofas from $899 — up to 70% off, ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sofa sale is on: 4 styles, starting at $899`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Camille, Buchanan, Pacifica — pick your fit and save up to 70%`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on Pottery Barn's most-loved sofa collections`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn: "New deals up to 70% off + Starting at $899: Sofa collections"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403s) will cause visible failures for credit-card banner recipients. The email also carries PHP `header()` calls embedded in a CSS `<style>` block — invalid syntax that is dead code but signals a template assembly defect.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken links (FAIL — 403 responses):**

- Credit card apply/buy CTA: `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
- West Elm spacer beacon: `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`

The first is a customer-facing link; a 403 means any click lands on an error page. The second is a tracking/spacer pixel cross-referenced from the wrong brand's CDN.

**25 tracking links skipped** by the QA probe (click-redirect domains) — these are expected but mean click-path integrity is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers injected into CSS `<style>` block:**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they leaked into the HTML output verbatim. Email clients will treat them as invalid CSS and ignore them, but this indicates a template preprocessing failure.

**Mixed content (HTTP images):** Three image sources use `http://`, which will be blocked by secure email clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**Missing alt text:** 43 images have no `alt` attribute. Notable functional images affected include the hero banner (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency.jpg`), all 11 sofa collection slices, and all 18 footer slices. With images off (default in many clients), the email renders as a blank white column.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — no fallback for tab/preview display.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header:** Not detected by QA relay. This is required for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements. May be a relay capture gap, but should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Not detected. One-click unsubscribe will not function in Gmail/Apple Mail without this header.

**Authentication-Results:** SPF/DKIM status not captured. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; authentication posture unverified.

**`robots` meta tag** is set to `noindex, nofollow` and `referrer` to `no-referrer` — both correct for email HTML.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are presumably present in the footer (not visible in truncated source) — cannot confirm from provided HTML.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 25 click-redirect links pass through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which is the standard ESP click-tracking wrapper. UTM parameters are not visible at the redirect layer from the source alone (they would be appended post-redirect). Cannot confirm UTM presence without resolving the tracking redirects.

The open-pixel at `svopn.potterybarn.com` uses `http://` — this will be blocked by HTTPS-enforcing clients, silently undercounting opens.

Cross-brand asset reference (`westelm.com` spacer) is a build hygiene issue — wrong brand CDN in a Pottery Barn send.
