# Dining tables with a seat for everyone
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn doing exactly what a dining-table shopper needs: "Gather Together" leads into a size-segmented walkthrough — Seats up to Six, Eight, then Twelve — each with its own hero shot and clear "Shop Now" CTA. It's a smart organizing principle because table size is the single biggest filter for anyone actually shopping this category, more useful than color or material would be.
- Where it loses points is sameness of execution — this is the same warehouse-sale banner ("Up to 60% off") and the same collection-by-collection scroll structure the brand has run in the 2026-09-19 furniture send and the 2026-09-17 "in-stock" send. The "60% off" banner at top is generic and not tied to dining specifically, so the concrete-offer signal is weak even though a discount is technically present. The "Recommended Just for You" grid crams six unrelated categories (curtain rods, rugs, towels) into a dining-table email, diluting the seat-count narrative right when it was working.
- Net: solid category email with a genuinely useful organizing idea, held back by a generic banner and a cluttered lower half. It'll perform fine with anyone actively furniture-shopping, but it's not going to convert a browser.

## What's working

- Segmenting by seating capacity (Six / Eight / Twelve) is a real shopping filter, not just a styling gimmick.
- Each size tier has a clean hero image, a one-line benefit ("Just right for breakfast nooks," "Ready for years of hosting the holiday crowd"), and a visible Shop Now button.
- "Extendable" badges on the 8- and 12-seat tables surface a genuinely useful spec without needing to click through.
- "A Chair for Every Style" is a smart complementary upsell that stays in-category.
- No render bugs — images loaded cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The top banner ("The Warehouse Sale: Up to 60% off") is generic storewide language, not a dining-specific offer — weak targeting signal despite being a real discount.
- "Recommended Just for You" mixes in curtain rods, rugs, and towels — unrelated to the dining-table narrative and breaks hierarchy right after the strong seat-count sequence.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email — "60% off" with no end date reads as evergreen, not a reason to act now.
- Repeats the same warehouse-sale banner and multi-collection-scroll format the brand has already run this week (2026-09-19 furniture send, 2026-09-17 in-stock send) — cadence fatigue risk for an engaged subscriber.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the generic "Warehouse Sale: Up to 60% off" banner for a dining-specific offer or stat ("Dining sets up to 60% off — this week only"), tying the discount to the subject line's promise instead of a storewide banner.
- 2. Cut "Recommended Just for You" down to dining-adjacent items only (table linens, dinnerware) — drop curtain rods/rugs/towels, which break the narrative.
- 3. Add a credible deadline (e.g., "Sale ends Sunday") near the top banner to convert browsers into same-session clickers.
- 4. Vary the creative structure from the last two sends — the collection-by-collection scroll is getting repetitive across three emails in four days.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Which size dining table fits your space?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Seats 6, 8, or 12 — up to 60% off dining`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extendable tables that grow with your guest list.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From breakfast nooks to holiday crowds — shop by size.`
