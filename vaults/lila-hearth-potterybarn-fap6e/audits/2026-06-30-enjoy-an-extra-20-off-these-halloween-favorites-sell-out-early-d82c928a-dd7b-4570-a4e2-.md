# Enjoy an EXTRA 20% OFF + These Halloween favorites sell out EARLY
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's continuation of a July 4th sale campaign that has now run more than ten consecutive sends — and this email makes a bold, questionable creative bet: it grafts a Halloween editorial block onto a July 4th sale email on June 30th. The result is a two-headed animal that does justice to neither story. The subject line has to carry "EXTRA 20% OFF" and "Halloween favorites sell out EARLY" simultaneously, and it buckles under the weight — 64 characters, two unrelated seasonal messages, one inbox slot.
- The July 4th hero banner at the top is the email's strongest moment: clean layout, clear offer, unambiguous CTA. But the moment the eye drops to the Halloween section — dark moody photography, gothic decor, skulls and pumpkins on June 30th — the seasonal logic collapses. Below that, "Recommended Just For You," "Discover New Arrivals," an app download, an Affirm callout, and a social follow ask pile on. Four distinct narratives compete below the fold.
- The Halloween editorial photography is genuinely good — it just belongs in a different email, sent in mid-July when "Early Halloween Preview" would feel intentionally early rather than bizarre.

## What's working

- The July 4th sale hero is immediately legible — Extra 20% off and up to 70% off are both visible without scrolling, and "SHOP ALL SALE" is an unambiguous primary CTA.
- Halloween editorial photography is polished and mood-appropriate — it would anchor a strong standalone send closer to July or August.
- "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" loyalty callout and Affirm financing options are visible, adding purchase pathway depth for hesitant buyers.
- "Recommended Just For You" personalization module is present, which signals segmentation effort even if the module itself is generic-looking bedding.

## What's weak

- The subject line tries to do two jobs (July 4th sale + Halloween tease) and lands neither — at 64 characters, it's also too long for comfortable mobile rendering.
- Halloween content on June 30th feels forced; "sell out EARLY" urgency for October product three months away is transparently manufactured.
- The sale offer has no visible deadline — "Extra 20% off" with no expiry date reads as permanent, not urgent. Per brand history, this is now the tenth-plus consecutive "Extra 20% off" send, which has eroded the signal to near-zero.
- Visual hierarchy collapses below the hero: July 4th sale → Halloween editorial → Recommended → New Arrivals → App → Affirm → Social = six competing asks.
- No preview text visible in the render; the inbox likely shows filler or leaked utility text, wasting that real estate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split into two sends.** The July 4th sale email and the "Early Halloween Favorites" preview are two separate briefs. The Halloween editorial is strong enough to anchor its own send in mid-July. Combining them in one body dilutes both.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Ends July 4" should appear in the banner and the subject. Without it, the sale reads as wallpaper, not an event.
- 3. **Cut the subject to one job.** Choose the sale urgency *or* the Halloween tease, not both:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off ends July 4 — don't miss it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last days: up to 70% off for July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bestsellers are moving — grab your picks before the long weekend`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New sale drops added: furniture, bedding, rugs and more`
- 4. **Defer the Halloween block entirely.** Surfacing it in a June 30 sale email damages both the sale urgency and the Halloween novelty. Save it for a standalone mid-July send labeled "First look: Halloween 2026."
- 5. **Cut below-fold modules from six to two.** Recommended For You + one secondary CTA is enough. Remove App, Affirm, New Arrivals, and Social from this send — or save them for a lower-priority email slot.

Two hard-broken links (403) will cause click failures for the loyalty banner CTA and a cross-brand image dependency. Three images load over plain HTTP, which modern clients will block or flag as mixed-content.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — CTA link returns 403:**
- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...` — the loyalty/Apply-and-Buy banner CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) is dead. Clicking it yields a 403, which means the campaign token is expired, the short-link record was never provisioned, or the `cp_tp` JWT is invalid/expired.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer image returns 403 (also HTTP):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — a West Elm CDN asset used in a Pottery Barn send. Request returns 403. This is a stale cross-brand asset reference; replace with a hosted Pottery Barn equivalent or remove the spacer entirely.

**25 tracking links skipped** — click-redirect domains (`click.e.potterybarn.com`) were not probed. Confirm all destination URLs resolve correctly in staging before send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers embedded in CSS block (source corruption):**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
Server-side PHP `header()` calls appear literally inside a `<style>` tag. This is a template rendering failure — the PHP was not executed and the raw source leaked into the HTML. These lines are invalid CSS and will cause parse errors in strict renderers.

**Alt text missing on 32+ images** — including all primary content images (hero banners, product tiles, footer slices, pixel trackers). Images-off rendering (common in Outlook, Gmail with blocked images) produces a fully blank email. High-priority items:
- `S26_Top_July4Sale_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero/top banner
- `slices_01.jpg` / `slices_02.jpg` — Halloween sneak peek content
- All 18 `Email-Footer/slices_*.jpg` images

**Three images served over HTTP (will be blocked):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/...` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/...` |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365 block mixed-content images by default. The open-tracking pixel (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) is also HTTP — open-rate data for this send will be undercounted.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` breaks accessibility in screen readers and appears as a blank tab label in browser-rendered previews.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected** — the relay may have stripped it, but this must be confirmed. Missing `List-Unsubscribe` blocks Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI and can trigger spam folder placement for bulk sends.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not detected** — required for Gmail and Yahoo one-click compliance since February 2024. If absent at the MTA level (not just relay capture), this is a deliverability defect.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by the relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com`; confirm DKIM is signed for this subdomain and DMARC policy is enforced. Cannot verify from available data.

**Open pixel over HTTP** (`http://svopn.potterybarn.com`) — some privacy-focused clients (Apple MPP, Fastmail) will either block or proxy this. No direct compliance violation, but it corrupts open metrics.
