# Shop the Summer Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the most anonymous Pottery Barn email in the entire June batch. "Shop the Summer Sale" is a four-word subject line with zero hook, zero specificity, and zero urgency — it reads like a placeholder someone forgot to rewrite. It arrives after at least seven consecutive sale-themed sends since June 12, which means the bar to stand out is already high, and this email limbo-dances under it.
- The creative execution inside is competent: the floral-blue hero bedroom image is on-brand, the category tile grid is well-organized, and the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" personalized product module is a genuine asset. But the scaffolding on the outside — subject, implied preheader, every top-of-fold element — does nothing to earn the open.
- The honest verdict: this is a housekeeping blast dressed as a campaign. The "100s of new deals added!" freshness signal in the hero sub-headline is the only differentiating phrase in the entire email, and it's buried below the fold of a subject line that guarantees most subscribers won't get there.

## What's working

- **Personalization module is real.** "Recommended Just For You" product grid with 6 tiles signals actual behavioral targeting, not just a generic bestsellers dump.
- **Category tile navigation is clean.** FURNITURE / OUTDOOR / BEDDING / PILLOWS & THROWS / DECOR / BATH / LIGHTING / RUGS — eight distinct entry points reduce the "where do I even start?" friction of a broad sale.
- **"100s of new deals added!"** is the sharpest copy in the email — it implies freshness and combats the staleness fatigue from the prior seven sale emails.
- **Loyalty and financing are both surfaced.** "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" and Affirm together address two distinct purchase-hesitation modes without clutter.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is the worst of the June batch.** "Shop the Summer Sale" is 4 words of instruction with no payoff — weaker than even "Up to 70% off" because at least that had a number.
- **No urgency anywhere visible.** No deadline, no "ends Sunday," no "today only" — for a sale email, this is a meaningful conversion lever left unpulled.
- **Too many sections compete for attention.** Hero → category tiles → personalized grid → Free Design Services → app promo → rewards → Affirm → footer. The Free Design Services and App sections belong in a different email; they dilute the sale momentum here.
- **Hero offer is not linked to a specific category.** "SHOP ALL SALE" is a homepage dump link — there's no lead product or spotlight item to anchor the creative.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email 8+ in a 5-day blitz. Even subscribers who love Pottery Barn are now conditioned to ignore these.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a number or a category in the subject.** The subject is the whole game after this many sends. "Up to 70% off" appeared so often it became wallpaper — but it's still better than nothing. Better: lead with a fresh angle like "New arrivals, already 40% off" or anchor to a specific category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `100s of new Summer Sale deals — just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Outdoor furniture: up to 60% off this week`
- 2. **Write a preheader that doesn't echo the subject.** If the preheader is "Shop the Summer Sale" or "100s of new deals added!" echoing the hero, that's a wasted impression. Use it for the detail the subject can't fit.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, furniture, outdoor — biggest drops of the season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns added — ends soon`
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even a soft "sale ends Sunday" in the hero sub-headline would sharply increase click urgency without any design change.
- 4. **Cut the App and Free Design Services sections.** They belong in non-sale editorial emails (like the June 16 Free Design Services send). Here they bleed momentum from the conversion goal.
- 5. **Lead the personalized product grid higher.** Swap positions with the category tile row — behavioral data is your sharpest tool; put it to work before the reader loses interest.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked in many clients)**
Three images are served over `http://`, not `https://`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content resources:
1. `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — also broken (403 above)
2. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` — footer credit card banner image
3. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — brand banner slice (2022 asset path)
4. `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` — open-tracking pixel

**Alt text absent on 40+ images**
Every content image slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, both footer banner images, and all tracking pixels are missing `alt` attributes. When images are blocked (common in Outlook, corporate clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no readable content. Minimum fix: add descriptive alt text to content images; use `alt=""` on purely decorative spacers and tracking pixels.

**PHP header directives in `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, ...");
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
These are PHP `header()` calls placed inside a CSS `<style>` block — they are no-ops in HTML and indicate a template rendering error where server-side code was not stripped before send. They do not break rendering but signal a broken template pipeline.

**`<title>` element is empty**
`<title></title>` — some email clients display the title in tab or preview UI. Low impact but indicates incomplete template population.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders). If the header is present but stripped by the AgentMail relay, the raw SMTP headers should be checked directly. If genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` mechanism Gmail uses for the native unsubscribe button) appears absent. Gmail will show a less prominent unsubscribe path or may suppress the email for some users.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture. `From:` domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (ESP subdomain); DKIM alignment with `potterybarn.com` should be confirmed independently.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source — should be confirmed present in the footer.
