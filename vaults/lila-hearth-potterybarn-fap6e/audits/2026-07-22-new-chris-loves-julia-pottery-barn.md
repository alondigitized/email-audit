# New Chris Loves Julia + Pottery Barn ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a collaboration-reveal email for the Chris Loves Julia x Pottery Barn collection — a departure from the heavy discount cadence the brand ran through most of July. The hero positioning is sharp: the CLJ co-branding comes before any product, leaning on the influencer names as the primary hook. For a subscriber who knows who Chris Loves Julia are, this is compelling. For anyone else, the email offers no secondary hook — no price, no specific featured piece, no urgency — just an editorial browse invitation.
- The structure below the hero is well-executed: three category-scoped editorial tiles ("Pick a wood tone," "Explore new pieces," "Handsome outdoor + fireplace") break the collection into approachable entry points. The addition of a "Recommended Just for You" personalized module below shows Pottery Barn running its standard personalization playbook, which works here as a conversion floor even when the editorial hero doesn't close the sale.
- Bottom line: a clean, brand-forward launch email that converts CLJ fans readily but leaves casual subscribers with nothing to act on. Good execution on a brief that needed more specificity to earn a click from anyone who doesn't already know the collab.

## What's working

- Chris Loves Julia co-branding is given top-of-email priority — fans of the pair will open and scroll immediately.
- Three editorial tiles organize the collection by decision-type ("pick a wood tone"), which is a smarter browse prompt than a raw product dump.
- "Recommended Just for You" personalization module acts as a catch-all for subscribers unmoved by the CLJ angle — a smart conversion safety net.
- Layout is clean, no visible render bugs, and hierarchy reads naturally top-to-bottom on mobile.
- The warm, moody lifestyle photography is on-brand and consistent with the aesthetic Pottery Barn established in the July 21 new-arrivals email ("classic, comfortable, collected" — similar visual language).

## What's weak

- No price anchor anywhere in the email — not even a "starting at" — so there's no reason to act now vs. later.
- The subject line sparkle emoji ✨ is low-signal decoration; combined with no offer and no urgency, it reads as filler.
- "Handsome outdoor + fireplace" as a category label is vague and oddly informal — unclear what specifically the tile links to.
- After six sends in two days (July 20–21), this email arrives with no fresh cadence signal, and its subject line doesn't stand out from the string of lifestyle-forward sends immediately preceding it.
- The app download module takes up real estate near the bottom without any contextual reason to download — it's boilerplate, not earn.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name a signature piece.** The subject and hero should tease one iconic item from the CLJ collection (a specific sofa, a lighting piece, a cabinet) — something a follower would recognize and click to see in their home.
- 2. **Add soft urgency.** Even "now available" or "just dropped" gives a reason to act today rather than on the next Pottery Barn email.
- 3. **Sharpen the tile labels.** "Handsome outdoor + fireplace" → "Shop outdoor + fire pits" — concrete nouns, verb-led.
- 4. **Drop the app module or earn it.** If you're going to push the app, tie it to a reason: "Visualize CLJ pieces in your room with AR — download the app."

```css
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they were pasted raw into the stylesheet. They are inert in email clients but indicate a template assembly error.

**`<title>` is empty** — minor; some clients display the title as a fallback subject line.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — AgentMail relay did not surface it; cannot confirm RFC 2369 compliance. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaints.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** — One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) not confirmed. Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy for >5k/day senders.

**Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm deliverability posture. Given volume, DMARC `p=reject` with aligned DKIM signing on `e.potterybarn.com` is expected but unverifiable from this capture.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken credit card CTA (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407...`) carries UTM-equivalent parameters (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`, `marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY`) — those attribution signals are lost on every click since the link 403s before any redirect.

The 25 untested tracking links are all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com` — standard ESP click-wrapping. UTM parameter presence on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving those redirects.
