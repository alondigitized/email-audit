# 🎉 Celebrate with an EXTRA 20% off + Beautiful table linens to elevate your next meal
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's eighteenth-plus send in the July 4th campaign, and it follows the exact structural playbook of every prior email in this sequence — Extra 20% off banner, SHOP NOW button, product grid — with a single editorial pivot: table linens as the hero category. That's the right instinct. Picking a specific sub-category gives subscribers a new reason to open instead of the nth "Extra 20% off everything" blast. The problem is execution: a subject line running 85+ characters buries the category hook past where most inboxes truncate, and the "Celebrate with an EXTRA 20% off +" construction is now so familiar from prior sends that it reads as template noise before the tablecloth angle ever lands.
- The visual render is clean. The table linen hero — blue-and-white patterned tablecloth, lifestyle setting — is well-produced and clearly above-fold. The split product rail (Table Runners / Placemats / Runners) does give structure. The "Recommended Just for You" module signals personalization. None of that is broken.
- But by send eighteen of the same campaign, the discount has lost credibility as urgency. "Extra 20% off" was also live on July 2nd, July 3rd (twice), and July 4th (twice so far). A subscriber who has been ignoring this for two weeks has calibrated it as evergreen. This email doesn't do anything to break that pattern — it just adds a table linen header to a format they've learned to scroll past.

## What's working

- The table linen editorial focus is the strongest creative move in this campaign: it gives the email a specific reason to exist beyond the blanket sale.
- The patriotic red/white/blue styling of the hero tablecloth ties the product directly to the 4th of July occasion — moment-appropriate product selection.
- Product rail (Table Runners / Placemats / Runners) is well-structured and scannable; subcategory navigation reduces friction for someone already in shopping mode.
- "Recommended Just for You" module adds a personalization signal that most sends in this sequence lack.
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breakage, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Subject line runs ~85 characters — truncated on virtually every mobile inbox before "table linens" even appears.
- "EXTRA" in all caps combined with the celebratory opener echoes every prior subject in the campaign; trained-ignore is a real risk by send eighteen.
- "SHOP NOW" buttons are the most generic possible CTAs — no noun, no category specificity; "Shop Table Linens" would pull harder.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof visible anywhere in the email body.
- No loyalty/member pricing visible — Pottery Barn Key members get a better deal but there's no callout to surface that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Front-load the category in the subject.** The table linen angle is the only differentiator in this send — it needs to appear in the first ~40 characters before inbox truncation kills it. Restructure entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Table linens, 20% off — set a gorgeous 4th table`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 4th table: linen refresh + extra 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tablecloths, runners & placemats — ships in time for the holiday weekend`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on linens today. Pick up before the holiday.`
- 2. **Make the CTA category-specific.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" on table-linen CTAs with "Shop Table Linens" or "Shop Runners & Placemats." This costs nothing and lifts click relevance signal.
- 3. **Add one social-proof element near the hero.** Even "4.8 stars · 1,200+ reviews" inline beneath the hero SKU stops the scroll. This email has none.
- 4. **Surface Pottery Barn Key pricing if applicable.** If members get an additional benefit (early access, stacked discount), one line above the SHOP NOW button earns the open for your highest-LTV segment.
- 5. **Drop the "Discover New Arrivals" module for this send.** It dilutes the table linen editorial and gives the eye somewhere to wander away from the conversion moment.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — blocked by modern clients**
Three non-HTTPS image `src` values will be suppressed by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook when image loading is restricted:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-tracking pixel)

**PHP directives injected into `<style>` block — invalid CSS**
The final `<style>` tag in `<head>` contains literal PHP `header()` calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that should never appear in rendered HTML. They are syntactically invalid CSS, have no effect on the email client, and indicate a template generation defect where a PHP snippet was pasted into the wrong block.

**46 images missing `alt` text**
All product imagery slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), hero banners (`S26_Top_July4Sale_FinalWeekend.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`), product thumbnails (beds, towels, quilt), and footer slices have empty or absent `alt` attributes. Recipients with images disabled see no fallback text for any content block.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is blank. Some clients (notably older Outlook) surface this in UI; it should at minimum match the subject line.

**Zoom disabled on mobile**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents user zoom on iOS, which is an accessibility regression for low-vision users.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` disables OS-level font scaling globally, compounding the zoom issue above.

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{FIRSTNAME}}`, `[%first_name%]`) are visible in the truncated source — personalization tokens appear to have fired.

**Privacy note — recipient email exposed in tracking URLs**
The open-tracking pixel and a service URL both include the recipient address as a plaintext URL parameter:
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?...&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`

Email addresses in GET parameters are logged in server access logs, intermediary proxies, and referrer headers. Depending on jurisdiction, this may conflict with data minimization requirements.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If the header is genuinely missing from delivery (not just a relay artifact), Gmail and Yahoo will not surface the native unsubscribe button, and this send violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since 2024).

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click machine unsubscribe is not supported. Required by Google/Yahoo for senders exceeding 5,000/day to Gmail.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. Cannot confirm the send authenticated as `e.potterybarn.com`.

**WARN — Plain-text body is 70.6% URLs**
9,857 of 13,959 plain-text characters are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Barracuda) penalize this ratio; it suggests the plain-text part was auto-generated from click-tracked HTML without cleanup.

**Unsubscribe link in body** — present. No issues found.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
