# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn hoping to browse for the second home — maybe bedding, a side table, something seasonal — and instead I'm staring at a full-screen modal asking me to enter a sweepstakes for U.S. Open golf tickets and a Dad gift. The brand clearly knows how to do a sweepstakes, but I genuinely can't see a single inch of the homepage behind this overlay. It's Father's Day season and Pottery Barn went all-in on the golf dad — fine, but I'm not him.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing**: The Father's Day sweepstakes launching in late May is on-schedule. Pottery Barn is clearly running a coordinated campaign.
- **Prize framing**: "$4,000 value + $500 to spend at Pottery Barn" is concrete and sounds generous — if you're shopping for a dad who golfs.
- **Clean modal design**: The imagery (whiskey glasses, Golf Courses book, bar accessories on a wood surface) is styled in that warm, elevated Pottery Barn way. The photography is on-brand.
- **Chat widget**: The persistent chat bubble in the bottom right is a nice trust signal for anyone who has questions — it's subtle and doesn't compound the intrusion.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen modal as the first experience**: I came to browse home decor, not enter a sweepstakes. Blocking 100% of the homepage content on load — especially with this much fine print — is hostile to a shopper like me.
- **Completely wrong persona targeting**: "For the Golf Lover / #1 Dad" speaks to exactly zero of my purchase triggers. I'm here for throws, bedding, and accent furniture for a second home.
- **No homepage is visible at all**: The navigation strip at the very top (Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm, Business to Business) is all I can see of the actual site. There's no hero, no seasonal home collection, no featured products.
- **SMS consent buried in fine print**: Four paragraphs of legal text below the email field — including disclosure that entering means I'll receive automated marketing texts — feels like a dark-pattern data grab rather than an honest sweepstakes.
- **No escape without noticing the X**: The close button is small, top-right, and easy to miss on mobile. A shopper who just wants to browse might feel trapped.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "No thanks, take me to the site" text link** below the email input — explicit, respectful, and reduces bounce for shoppers who aren't interested in the sweepstakes.
- **Trigger the modal on exit-intent or after 10 seconds**, not on page load. Let me see the spring home collection first; if I'm about to leave, then surface the sweepstakes.
- **Segment the modal by visitor type**: returning loyalty members or shoppers who've browsed home decor should see a home-relevant offer (spring bedding promo, loyalty bonus), not a golf dad sweepstakes.
- **Condense the legal copy**: Move the SMS/terms fine print to a collapsible "Terms & Conditions" link instead of pasting four paragraphs in-line — it makes the modal feel sketchy when it doesn't need to.
