# Sale on sale 🎉 EXTRA 20% OFF select styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's latest in a campaign that has now run at least ten consecutive "Extra 20% off" sends across roughly five days — and the brand clearly knows it. The subject line "Sale on sale" is a clever self-aware wink at the stacking discounts, but opening the email reveals the same structural template subscribers have seen all week. The one genuinely new element is the Open Box Outlet Deals secondary section, which at least adds a new category angle rather than just re-serving the same hero creative. The personalized product grid ("Recommended Just For You") offers a small redemptive signal — it creates actual product-level differentiation that prior sends in this run lacked.
- The overall judgment: this is a structurally solid email executing a tired idea. The hero is clear, the offer is unambiguous, and the render is clean — but campaign fatigue is now severe enough that the marginal subscriber has seen this exact offer so many times that the "sale on sale" framing reads more like a cope than a genuine value escalation. The Open Box angle was the right call to add, but it's buried below the fold in a template that still leads with the same 20% hero that's appeared in every send since June 26.

## What's working

- **Open Box Outlet Deals section is genuinely new.** "Save big on returned items at unbeatable prices" adds a different value proposition — clearance/off-price — that prior sends in this campaign window haven't touched.
- **"Recommended Just For You" personalized product grid** gives individual subscribers a reason this email is different from the last one; actual product thumbnails anchor the CTA to something specific.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** The eye moves: logo → 4th of July badge → Extra 20% off hero → category nav → secondary offer → products. No confusion about what the email is selling.
- **Subject line "Sale on sale" is self-aware and cheeky.** It's a reasonable hook for an audience that's been getting 20%-off emails all week — it at least acknowledges the moment.
- **Key Rewards / Affirm loyalty blocks visible in footer.** Member benefits and BNPL are surfaced for subscribers who care about them.

## What's weak

- **Creative fatigue is now critical.** This is the tenth-plus "Extra 20% off Select Styles" send in roughly five days. The brand history confirms June 26, 27, 28 (multiple), 29 (multiple), and now June 30. The novelty budget is spent.
- **"Select styles" qualifier is doing a lot of work for "sale on sale."** A true escalation would be a new percentage, an additional category, or a flash sale structure — not the same 20% dressed in a different subject line.
- **The Open Box section is too far down.** If that's the genuine new angle, it should compete for hero real estate — not live beneath the same hero that's been running all week.
- **Category nav links (Furniture | Outdoor | Décor | Bedding…) are generic breadcrumbs** rather than editorial choices. They don't reinforce either the July 4th theme or the Open Box angle.
- **No visible deadline.** "4th of July Sale" implies an end date but no explicit "ends July 4" or countdown is visible, which weakens urgency that could otherwise be credible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Open Box as the hero, demote Extra 20% to secondary.** This is the only genuinely new offer in ten sends. Make it the reason to open. Swap the two sections entirely — the Outlet angle lets you write an editorially distinct email instead of another stacked-discount template.
- 2. **Add an explicit end date.** "Ends July 4 at midnight" or even "Last chance before the 4th" creates real, honest urgency without bait-and-switch risk. The 4th of July anchor is right there — use it.
- 3. **Surface one product from the "Recommended" grid into the hero.** The personalized picks are the most differentiated asset in the email. Pull the top recommended item above the fold and make the hero personal rather than generic.
- 4. **Give the category nav an editorial spine.** Instead of nine generic links, pick two or three categories that are strongest in the sale and weight the nav accordingly (e.g., bold "Outdoor" and "Bedding" if those have deepest inventory).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open Box prices just dropped — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance before July 4: your picks are still waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Returned items, unbeatable prices — grab them before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Extra 20% off ends at midnight July 4 — shop your recommendations`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Sale on Sale / Extra 20% Off" (2026-07-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two links return 403 at send time (one a credit card CTA, one a cross-brand asset leak from West Elm), and the HTML template contains non-functional PHP `header()` calls embedded inside a `<style>` block — evidence of a broken template pipeline. Forty-plus images carry no alt text and three assets are served over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**BROKEN — Credit Card CTA (403)**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY…`
The Apply & Buy / Key Rewards banner link is returning 403. If this is the credit card acquisition CTA it is completely non-functional at send time.

**BROKEN — Cross-brand spacer asset (403 + HTTP)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm CDN asset is embedded in a Pottery Barn email. It is both broken (403) and served over plain HTTP. This indicates a shared template was deployed without substituting brand-specific asset paths.

**Tracking coverage**
24 click links on `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped by the probe (expected for redirect-based tracking). No issues confirmed or ruled out on those links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code inside `<style>` block (template defect)**
The following lines appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP function calls. They are syntactically invalid CSS and will be ignored by all clients, but they are a hard signal that a PHP image-serving snippet was copy-pasted directly into the HTML template without sanitization. The template pipeline is broken at the source.

**Three HTTP image sources (mixed-content)**
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail block or proxy HTTP resources. All three will degrade or silently drop these images:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` (also 403)
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=…` (open pixel — tracking loss on HTTPS-enforcing clients)

**Missing alt text — 44 images**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_13.jpg` in body, full footer slice set, all product images, both tracking pixels) has no `alt` attribute. Subscribers with images off see a wall of broken image icons with no context. Also a WCAG 2.1 failure for decorative-vs-informative image distinction.

**Empty `<title>`**
`<title></title>` — some clients render this in the tab or preview; empty is a minor but unnecessary gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Low real-world impact but worth noting for accessibility posture.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
This suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all clicks originating from the email. If any destination URLs rely on referrer for attribution (rather than UTM params), they will attribute as direct. UTM params in the click links should override this for GA4, but the meta tag creates unnecessary ambiguity.

**Plain-text body is 70% URLs (9,857 of 14,033 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is essentially a raw URL dump. Spam filters penalize this ratio. Subscribers on plain-text clients (some corporate mail systems) receive an unusable experience. No readable content, offer summary, or prose is present.

**Cross-brand CDN contamination**
The West Elm spacer asset (`edm.westelm.com`) appearing in a Pottery Barn email suggests shared template ancestry. If destination links similarly inherited West Elm parameters, UTM attribution would be misrouted — not confirmed from available source but worth auditing the full link set.
