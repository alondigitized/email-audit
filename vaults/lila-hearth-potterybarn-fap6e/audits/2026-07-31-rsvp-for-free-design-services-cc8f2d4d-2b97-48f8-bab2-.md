# RSVP for FREE design services
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine change of pace from Pottery Barn's recent stretch of quilt, duvet, and rug drops — instead of a discount, it's an RSVP push for the "Design Crew" free styling service, laid out with a clean WHAT/WHEN/WHERE structure and a bolded "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" button right in the hero. That's a smart move against promo fatigue: no subscriber wants a sixth near-identical bedding sale in two weeks, and free 1:1 design help is a stickier hook for anyone mid-renovation.
- Where it falls down is exactly where Pottery Barn's emails always fall down — after the strong, focused hero, it sprawls into three "Bring This Look to Life" room galleries, a second CTA, an in-store class plug, a six-item "Recommended For You" grid, the app pitch, and the credit-card rewards block. None of that is broken or ugly, it's just the same kitchen-sink tail flagged in the July 28–30 sends, now bolted onto a service RSVP instead of a product sale.
- Net: strong differentiated hero, weak follow-through, no real urgency mechanic to force a decision. Solid mid-pack send — better subject/offer differentiation than most of the last week's emails, but it squanders that with an unfocused body.

## What's working

- Hero uses a clear WHAT/WHEN/WHERE format that answers the obvious subscriber questions before they even scroll.
- Primary CTA ("BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT") is bold, high-contrast, and repeated twice in the body for redundancy.
- Offering a free service rather than another % off is a genuine break from the brand's recent promo cadence, which should read as less "salesy" in-inbox.
- Room photography is cohesive and on-brand — no render bugs, no overlapping text, no broken image gaps.
- The in-store "Mixing Wood Finishes" class card gives a concrete, dated secondary conversion point (Aug 9, Sunday, 10AM).

## What's weak

- No actual deadline on the RSVP itself — "spots go fast" is vague urgency with no date, unlike the specific Aug 9 date given to the in-store class right below it.
- The email dilutes its own differentiated hook by tailing into the same "Recommended Just For You" product grid and rewards-card pitch every other Pottery Barn send uses.
- No social proof, testimonial, or "X families booked this week" type signal to make the free-service offer feel in-demand.
- Three near-identical "Bring This Look to Life" room galleries in a row add scroll depth without adding new information — one well-chosen look would do the job.

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the RSVP a hard deadline or capacity cue ("Only 12 virtual slots left this week") — right now the only urgency word is "fast," which isn't concrete enough to drive action.
- 2. Cut two of the three "Bring This Look to Life" galleries — keep the strongest room, move straight to the second CTA and the design class.
- 3. Add a proof point near the hero — a number of appointments booked, a designer name/photo, or a before/after — free consultations sell better with a face attached.
- 4. Move the in-store "Mixing Wood Finishes" class above the "Recommended Just For You" grid so the two service-oriented CTAs sit together, then let the product catalog trail off at the very end where it belongs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free 1:1 design help — spots fill fast`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your room, styled by our Design Crew (free)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free session — in-home, virtual, or in-store`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One-on-one styling tips, no cost, your schedule`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on a legacy XHTML 1.0 Strict / table-based ESP template (Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style, `click.e.potterybarn.com` / `image.e.potterybarn.com`) with a raw PHP code leak embedded in the markup and no unsubscribe/authentication headers captured by the relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **PHP code leaked into rendered HTML.** Inside the second `<style>` block, literal PHP `header()` calls appear as plain text in the output:
  `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0"); header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false); header("Pragma: no-cache");`
  This is not valid CSS — it indicates a template include (likely a tracking-pixel PHP script) was inlined/rendered as text instead of executed server-side. Cosmetically harmless in most clients (parsed as invalid CSS and ignored) but signals a broken build step upstream.
- All content links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` opaque redirect tokens (e.g. `ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5NTR9AAsAAAAAA3PNdrlUU7GH0Af...`) — 46 such tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe per QA (expected for redirect-obfuscated links; not itself a defect).
- Third-party tracking beacons present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501&srvc=1&betr=30802=711754[8760]` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` — both rendered as `<img>` pixels, both flagged for missing `alt` (expected/appropriate for tracking pixels, but confirms they are pixel-based, not JS-based, trackers).

## 7. Recommendations
1. Investigate the leaked `header()` PHP calls inside the `<style>` block — this indicates a broken include/build step and should be traced to the sending template source (likely an unrendered PHP snippet mistakenly concatenated into the CSS block).
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 12 content/product images (hero slices, product shots); empty `alt=""` is acceptable for the pure tracking pixels but should be explicit rather than absent.
3. Migrate the two `http://` image sources to `https://` to avoid mixed-content blocking.
4. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent at the MTA level — even if AgentMail's relay isn't capturing them, verify directly against ESP send logs, since RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe affects Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender compliance.
5. Verify `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) independently of this relay capture, given Google/Yahoo's bulk sender authentication requirements.
6. Consider replacing the plaintext email address in `sv_sveme=` and `?email=` tracking URLs with a hashed/opaque identifier to reduce PII exposure in logs.
