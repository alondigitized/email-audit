# Free design help!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the best-looking send of the week, mostly because it isn't the oxblood "Extra 20% off" banner. After the Oct 25–27 run, including this morning's "Extra 20% off is ON (but not for long) + Furniture in stock & on its way FAST", a service-led email with a green damask hero is a real change of pace. The hero says "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" and lists what, when and where in three short lines. It ends in a solid green "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" button. The eye lands on the offer and the next step right away.
- The weakness is focus. Below the hero the email turns into a general catalog: a "Discover New Arrivals" banner, a holiday class series, six "Recommended Just for You" products, then app, card and Affirm blocks. That makes at least four more CTAs competing with the appointment booking. The subject promises one thing, free design help. The email then spends about two-thirds of its length on something else.
- Overall it's a solid, honest service email that gives a fatigued subscriber something new. It's undermined by a subject that feels like a generic come-on and a body that doesn't commit to the idea.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and scannable. The what/when/where structure gives the reader the whole offer in about five seconds.
- "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a clear verb-plus-noun button, high-contrast on a white card.
- It breaks the repetitive "Extra 20% off" pattern from the last week of sends.
- The "Home for the Holidays" class series lists concrete dates (Nov 8, 15, 22, Dec 6, 13) with 10 AM start times. That is real, credible time-boundedness.
- The render is clean: no broken images, overlaps or layout gaps.

## What's weak

- The hero's "when" line is "Choose a time—but spots go fast!" with no date, so the urgency is vague.
- There are two competing CTAs back to back: "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" and then "SHOP NOW" over a New Arrivals banner. The second one reads as a generic homepage push.
- The "Discover New Arrivals" overlay text sits over a busy room photo and is harder to read than it should be.
- The "Recommended Just for You" grid has no prices, no ratings and no offer. It is six products with no reason to click.
- The footer stacks the app, credit card and Affirm blocks, plus long legalese. It dilutes a send that should be about design help.
- The email has no sale or member-price signal for a subscriber trained to expect "Extra 20% off".

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one job. Keep the hero and the class series. Drop "Discover New Arrivals" and compress the product grid to three items tied to design projects (a room refresh, a mantel, a tree). Put the booking CTA at the top and bottom.
- 2. Make the hero urgency concrete: "Appointments open through Dec 13" or "Only 12 spots left this week" instead of "spots go fast".
- 3. Replace the "SHOP NOW" overlay with copy tied to the service, such as "Shop what your designer would pick".
- 4. Add a proof point near the hero, such as a before/after image or a designer quote, so the free service feels tangible.
- 5. Move the app, credit card and Affirm blocks into a single compact footer strip.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Home, virtual or in-store. Pick a time before spots fill.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is table-based, 650px fixed-width, and sent through Salesforce Marketing Cloud. It has real code-level defects: stray PHP in a `<style>` block, empty alt text on linked hero images, and mixed-content `http://` images. The recipient's address also appears in plaintext in tracking-pixel URLs. The automated QA reports a 57% pass rate with 3 warnings. The header warnings are probably relay-capture artifacts, not confirmed failures.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Opaque click tracking.** All 42 tracked links use `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=…`, an encrypted SFMC payload. The HTTP probe skipped them, so destinations and any UTM parameters can't be verified from the source.
- **Recipient email in pixel URLs.** The address appears in plaintext in two places:
  - `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
  - `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`
- **Third-party beacons.** `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501…` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…/cat/1` are ad-network pixels. They add third-party requests and slow loads.
- **Cross-brand asset dependency.** `https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is hosted on a West Elm domain. If that asset is removed, the layout spacing will break.
- **Dead markup.** The commented-out credit-card banner block (with `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` links) is still shipped in the HTML. It's harmless but adds weight.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Stray PHP in CSS.** The second `<style>` block begins with `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store…"); header("Pragma: no-cache");` before `a[x-apple-data-detectors] {…}`. That text is not valid CSS. A CSS parser will likely treat it as part of the selector prelude and drop the `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule. Apple data-detector link styling would then not be neutralized.
- **Empty alt text on linked images.** The hero slices `slices_01.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` and `slices_03.jpg` have `alt=""` and are each the only content of an `<a href="click.e.potterybarn.com/…">`. Those links have no accessible name, and with images off the content is blank.
- **Missing alt attribute entirely.** The QA flags these product images:
  - `standard-curtain-rod-wall-bracket-l.jpg`
  - `toscana-extendable-dining-table-60-1245-1-l.jpg`
  - `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`
  - `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`
  - `belgian-flax-linen-curtain-1-l.jpg`
  - `reeva-handwoven-rug-9-l.jpg`
  - `3ceb0c96-…jpg`
- **Footer slices.** `Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` have no alt. If legal or link text is image-only, it is inaccessible. The source is truncated, so I can't confirm this.
- **Mixed content.** Two images load over `http://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`. Some clients block or warn on them.
- **Responsive rules likely inert.** The media query targets `table[class="wrapper"]` and `img[class="photo"]`, but none of the visible tables or images carry those classes. Visible tables are fixed `width="650"` and images are `width="650"` with no `max-width`. In the visible source this means no mobile scaling. The source is truncated, so later sections may differ.
- **Minor.**
  - The XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype is used with deprecated attributes (`bgcolor`, `<center>`, `border`, `align`), so it won't validate.
  - `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` disables text-size scaling.
  - The `<link>` to Google Fonts (Montserrat) is stripped by Gmail and others. I saw no fallback stack in the visible portion.
- **QA noise.** The alt warnings on `coreemail.html`, `leadback`, `turn.com` and `svopn` are tracking pixels, not content images. They are low priority.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or AMPscript tokens are visible. The `begin/end dynamic ampScript slot` comments are empty and don't leak logic. The recipient address is populated in the pixel URLs (see §2).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Headers unverified.** The QA found no `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) or `Authentication-Results` header. The QA itself attributes this to the AgentMail relay. This is a capture limitation, not a confirmed sender defect. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
- **Footer unverified.** The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated HTML. The footer is built from 18 image slices without alt text, so I can't confirm those elements exist as text. This needs a check against the full source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable from the data. Every visible link is an encrypted SFMC redirect, and the probe skipped all 42. The only plaintext destination URL is the commented-out `credit-card` link, which isn't live.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the `header(…)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block, and confirm the data-detector rule then applies.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the linked hero slices (`slices_01` to `slices_03`) and to the product images. Add `alt=""` explicitly to decorative spacers and pixels.
3. Switch the two `http://` images to `https://`.
4. Stop putting the recipient email in plaintext query strings. Use a hashed or opaque subscriber ID.
5. Add `class="wrapper"` and `class="photo"` to the main tables and images, or switch to `max-width:100%` images, so the mobile rules apply.
6. Re-run the QA against the raw `.eml`, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results.
7. Check the full HTML for a text-based unsubscribe link and a postal address in the footer.
8. Resolve a sample of the `click.e.potterybarn.com` links to confirm landing pages and UTM parameters.
