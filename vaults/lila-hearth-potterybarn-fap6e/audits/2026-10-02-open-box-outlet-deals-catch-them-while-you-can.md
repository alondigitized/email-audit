# Open Box Outlet Deals—catch them while you can!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is an outlet-clearance send for Pottery Barn's Open Box Outlet, and the subject and hero match. The red hero is clean and unmissable: "Open Box Outlet Deals," a one-line explanation, and a SHOP NOW button. The idea is easy to grasp. It is also the weakest kind of promo, because "save big" has no number behind it. Open-box stock is a bargain pitch, and the email never shows a bargain.
- The body doesn't back the hero up. Below it sits a Free Design Services banner, then a "Recommended Just For You" grid of a bed, chair, rugs, curtain rods and a ghost decoration. These are standard catalog items with no outlet prices, no "was/now," and no sign they are open-box. The Christmas Shop block and the app, credit card and Affirm modules turn it into a generic brand newsletter. A reader who clicks for outlet deals has no reason to think the grid is what the hero promised. Compared with today's earlier sends ("Your next great find is ON SALE," "Ships FREE: Your new favorite throw"), this is another sale blast with no stated discount, and the third Pottery Barn send of the day.
- Judgement: a decent frame with no payload. It's a mid-pack send that depends on brand goodwill and curiosity about the outlet, not on any concrete offer.

## What's working

- The red hero is high-contrast, and the eye lands on "Open Box Outlet Deals" and SHOP NOW first.
- The subject and hero say the same thing, so the click-through isn't a surprise.
- "Open box" makes the offer's nature clear: opened and returned items at a discount.
- The layout is clean, with no broken images or overlapping text.
- The Christmas Shop and ghost collection keep the send seasonally current.

## What's weak

- No discount is stated anywhere. "Save big" is the only price language, and there's no percentage, dollar amount or example price.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid shows no prices and no outlet or open-box labels, so it doesn't show outlet products.
- The Free Design Services banner is the second-largest block and competes with the outlet message.
- The Christmas Shop, app download, credit card and Affirm sections dilute the focus on a long scroll.
- The fine print is long and dense, which undercuts the "honest offer" feel. This is the third Pottery Barn email today, so cadence fatigue is a real risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number in the hero, such as "Up to 40% off open-box furniture," plus a sample item with its was/now price. This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Replace the generic recommended grid with 4–6 actual outlet items, each showing outlet price, original price and an "Open Box" tag.
- 3. Add a credible deadline or scarcity line, such as "Limited quantities, once they're gone they're gone," or a specific end date.
- 4. Cut the Design Services banner and the card, app and Affirm modules from this send, or move them far down. Keep the focus on the outlet.
- 5. Make the button copy specific, for example "Shop Outlet Furniture" instead of "Shop Now."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Open-box furniture, up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Outlet finds: sofas, rugs & beds marked down`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Returned, inspected and priced to go. Quantities are limited.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save on sofas, beds and rugs, plus new outlet arrivals.`

  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=…`
- **Third-party and legacy pixels:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee ad-tech), `svopn.potterybarn.com` and `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/f5bed2dc-…` are all in the body. Three separate open or tracking pixels is a deliverability and privacy overhead.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- **Direct product image links:** product images come straight from `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/…`. Those paths are tied to the website's CDN and could break if the site's image paths change.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the stray `header(...)` PHP lines from the `<style>` block.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero, product, and banner images, and `alt=""` to spacers and tracking pixels. Give the footer slices real alt text, or convert the footer to live text.
3. Switch the three `http://` image URLs, including the `svopn` pixel, to HTTPS.
4. Stop passing the plaintext email in `coreemail.html?email=` and `sv_sveme=`. Use a hashed or opaque ID instead.
5. Fill in `<title>`, add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and drop `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
6. Add `role="presentation"` to the remaining layout tables.
7. Verify the raw headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, `Authentication-Results`) from the original message rather than the relay copy.
8. Resolve the redirect chains for a sample of the 44 tracking links to confirm UTM tagging and landing pages.
9. Host the spacer GIF on the Pottery Barn domain instead of `edm.westelm.com`.
