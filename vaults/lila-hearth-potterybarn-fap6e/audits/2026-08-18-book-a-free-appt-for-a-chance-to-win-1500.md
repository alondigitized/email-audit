# Book a FREE appt. for a chance to win $1500!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Design Crew Sweepstakes" send dressed up as a $1,500 giveaway, but the actual mechanic is a lead-gen trap: book a free design appointment and you're automatically entered. It's structurally identical to the last week of Pottery Barn sends — big hero, three-room "inspo" strip (living room, bedroom, dining room), a class RSVP block, then the usual recommended-products grid and footer furniture. The hero itself is clean and legible ("WIN $1500 TOWARD YOUR HOME REFRESH" with a clear What/When/Where breakdown), which is more structured than most of their recent lookbook or category-launch sends.
- The problem is intent mismatch: the subject promises a sweepstakes, but the email's entire design is built to get you to book a 1:1 sales appointment, not to enter a giveaway. That's a bait-and-switch pattern, softened only by "NO PURCHASE NECESSARY" fine print buried at the very bottom. Compared to 2026-08-17's "Halloween Headquarters" send (6/10, genuinely styled hero) or 2026-08-16's "free shipping on towels" (6/10, honest transactional offer), this one trades honesty for a bigger subject-line hook.

## What's working

- Hero has real structure — a What/When/Where micro-layout makes the mechanic scannable at a glance, unusual for this brand's recent sends.
- Single, unambiguous primary CTA ("BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT") repeated twice in the body, so there's no confusion about the next step.
- Three-room "Inspo For Your Next Project" strip (living room, bedroom, dining room) gives visual variety and reinforces the design-consultation angle with real product photography.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, spacing is even, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The subject sells a sweepstakes ("chance to win $1500"), but the whole email is a funnel to book a paid design consultation — the offer feels bait-and-switch.
- Sweepstakes terms and "no purchase necessary" disclosure are relegated to dense gray legal text at the very bottom, invisible on first scroll.
- The email is very long and repeats itself — CTA appears twice before you even get to the unrelated Halloween class RSVP and product grid, diluting the sweepstakes message.
- No specific detail on odds, entry deadline mechanics, or what the actual design service costs if you don't win — leaves real questions unanswered.
- Mixing an unrelated Halloween design-class RSVP block into a sweepstakes email muddies the single-offer focus.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with total honesty in the subject: either sell the sweepstakes AND clearly flag it requires booking an appointment, or drop the "FREE appt." framing since nothing about the sweepstakes entry is inherently tied to a purchase.
- 2. Move the "No purchase necessary" / alternate entry method disclosure up near the hero CTA, not buried in 6-point footer text — this is both an honesty and a compliance-optics issue.
- 3. Cut the Halloween class RSVP block from this send entirely; it dilutes the single sweepstakes CTA and reads as filler.
- 4. Shorten the email — trim the three-room inspo strip to one or two rooms so the second "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA isn't so far down the scroll.
- 5. Add a concrete deadline countdown near the hero (e.g., "Ends August 30") to give real urgency instead of only mentioning it in body copy.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$1500 home refresh sweepstakes ends Aug 30`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet with a designer, no purchase necessary, and you're entered.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free 1:1 design session + a shot at $1500 toward your refresh.`

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "Book a FREE appt." Campaign

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) table-based HTML email with tracked click redirects and a mix of HTTPS/HTTP image assets; automated QA returned a 57% pass rate with 3 warning categories and no hard failures.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All CTA/hero links route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` SFMC tracking redirects (49 total tracking links) — QA skipped HTTP probing on these per policy, so live destination resolution is unconfirmed.
- Secondary/third-party tracking pixels present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...`, and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — note the recipient's raw email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) is passed in plaintext as a URL query parameter on the open-tracking pixel, which is a PII-in-URL exposure (logged in browser history, proxy logs, referrer headers).
- Malformed `<style>` block: raw PHP-style `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');` and `header("Cache-Control: ...")` statements are embedded directly inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>` (not inside a comment or script) — this is invalid CSS and will be ignored by clients, but indicates a broken/leftover code fragment from a different asset (likely a tracking pixel handler) that was pasted into the template.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 33 of 34 images have no `alt` attribute (including hero slices, product shots, and footer/legal images) — screen reader users and clients with images blocked get no fallback content.
- Two images load over plain `http://`, not `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — these will be blocked or flagged as mixed/insecure content in many clients and browsers.

- Preheader uses standard hidden-div + zero-width-space padding technique; correctly implemented (`display:none!important; mso-hide:all` plus `‌` filler).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
