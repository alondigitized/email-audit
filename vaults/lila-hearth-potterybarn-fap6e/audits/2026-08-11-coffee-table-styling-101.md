# Coffee table styling 101
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is an editorial "styling guide" send — "A Curated Table," decor tips broken into candles, faux botanicals, vases, decorative objects, bowls & trays — and it's a genuine departure from the sale-dump pattern this brand has run all week (2026-08-08 through 2026-08-10 were all "UP TO 60% OFF" catalog blasts). There's no discount anywhere in this email, which is unusual for Pottery Barn but reads as intentional: it's an inspiration/how-to piece meant to drive browsing, not a markdown push.
- The execution is clean and on-brand — moody styled vignettes, consistent "Shop [Category]" links, a tidy "More Ways to Accent the Room" grid, and a "Recommended Just for You" personalization block. But it also inherits this week's core problem: too much crammed into one send. After five decor-tip modules it pivots hard into a Halloween teaser, a sweepstakes, app download, and Affirm financing — a coffee table article that becomes a grab-bag by the bottom third.
- For a shopper who opened for "coffee table styling," the top of the email delivers on that promise; the rest doesn't need to be there.

## What's working

- Hero directly matches the subject — "A Curated Table" with a styled coffee table vignette front and center.
- Genuinely useful editorial angle (styling tips vs. another sale blast) breaks the week's monotonous discount cadence.
- Each tip section is well-structured: one line of styling advice + a clear category link (Candles, Faux Botanicals, Vases, Decorative Objects, Bowls & Trays).
- "SHIPS FREE" badge on the candles module is a concrete, relevant trust signal.
- "Recommended Just for You" section shows real personalization with six distinct products across categories (rugs, curtains, bedding, dining).

## What's weak

- No offer or discount anywhere — fine thematically, but a letdown after a week of 60%-off subject lines; recipients conditioned by recent sends may feel bait-and-switched on value.
- The email sprawls well past the coffee-table theme into Halloween Shop, Design Crew Sweepstakes, app download, and Affirm financing — four unrelated CTAs after the actual content ends.
- "MORE WAYS TO ACCENT THE ROOM" text-link grid (Pillows, Throws, Floor Lamps, Curtains, Armchairs, Rugs) is visually flat and adds no imagery, breaking the otherwise strong visual rhythm.
- Primary CTA is diffuse — six equal-weight "Shop [Category]" links plus a "SHOP ALL DECOR" button means no single dominant action.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "SHOP ALL DECOR" — drop Halloween Shop, Design Crew Sweepstakes, app download, and Affirm blocks into their own dedicated sends; this alone would turn a scattered email into the sharpest one this week.
- 2. Replace the plain-text "More Ways to Accent" grid with small thumbnail images to match the visual quality of the rest of the email.
- 3. Add one soft incentive line (e.g., free shipping threshold or loyalty points) near the hero so the email doesn't read as a total value drop-off after a week of 60%-off subjects.
- 4. Pick one hero CTA ("Shop Coffee Tables") and demote the category links to secondary text under each module.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Coffee table styling, solved`
