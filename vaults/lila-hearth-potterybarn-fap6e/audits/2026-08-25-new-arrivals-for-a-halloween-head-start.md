# New arrivals for a Halloween head start 🎃
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine category break from Pottery Barn's recent run — after five straight bedding/color-sort/catalog sends, "The Halloween Shop" is a real seasonal moment with a real hero: string lights, "TRICK OR TREAT" pillows, black cats, ghosts, jack-o'-lanterns, all staged into one atmospheric flat-lay. "Enter if You Dare" is a fun, on-brand CTA button and the mood is set immediately.
- The problem is what happens after the hero: the email pivots hard into a nine-tile Halloween decor grid with zero prices or product names, then drops Halloween entirely for a "Recommended Just for You" bedroom-furniture block that has nothing to do with the subject line. By the bottom third, this reads like two unrelated emails stapled together — Halloween teaser up top, generic house-file bedding cross-sell below.
- It's a stronger open than anything in the recent brand history (finally a fresh visual idea instead of the fourth bedding-color sort), but the body doesn't deliver a clear single action, and there's no offer or price anywhere in the email. Call it a solid subject/hero paired with a weak, unfocused body.

## What's working

- The Halloween hero is genuinely differentiated from the last five sends — string lights, textured knit pillows, pumpkins, a black cat — it looks like a real lifestyle shoot, not a product grid.
- "Enter If You Dare" is a distinctive, on-theme CTA that fits the seasonal moment (contrast with recent generic "Shop Now" clicks).
- The 9-tile "Shop All Things Halloween" grid gives clear visual variety (spiderweb dishware, ghost pillows, pumpkin candles, garlands) without feeling repetitive.
- Loyalty/rewards, app download, credit card, and sweepstakes modules are all present and consistently styled at the bottom, matching Pottery Barn's usual footer architecture.

## What's weak

- No price or discount anywhere in the email — every module is teaser/browse-only, which continues the "vague offer" pattern flagged in 2026-08-22's "On sale AND shipping free" (5.00/10).
- None of the 9 Halloween grid tiles are labeled — no product names, no prices — so there's no way to know what you're clicking into before you land on the site.
- "Recommended Just for You" bedroom furniture (curtain rods, a swivel chair, platform beds, duvet covers) has zero connection to Halloween and dilutes the hero's momentum — it reads like a bolted-on second email.
- The Design Crew Sweepstakes module competes for attention with the Halloween CTA and the bedding cross-sell, adding a third unrelated ask in one send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Label the Halloween grid tiles with product name + price (or at least price) — right now the strongest visual asset in the email has no path to purchase intent.
- 2. Cut or relocate "Recommended Just for You" — a Halloween-themed email shouldn't cross-sell platform beds and curtain rods; if cross-sell is required, make it Halloween-adjacent (throw pillows, seasonal throws) instead.
- 3. Add one concrete offer to the hero or grid — "20% off Halloween decor" or similar — this is the first fresh creative in the recent run and it's wasted without a reason to act now.
- 4. Trim the bottom-of-funnel asks (sweepstakes + texts signup + app + credit card + Affirm) to one or two — five competing CTAs after the primary shop button dilutes urgency.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cobwebs, candles, and black cats — shop the new Halloween collection`
