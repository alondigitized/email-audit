# Last chance to enter to win $1500!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's "Design Crew Sweeps" send — a $1,500 gift card sweepstakes tied to booking a free design consultation, dressed up with a "last chance" subject line. It's a genuinely different mechanic from the bedding/catalog run this brand has been on all week, which is a point in its favor for cadence variety. But the subject line oversells urgency the body doesn't back up: "Last chance to enter to win $1500!" implies a closing deadline, while the hero copy just says "Meet with a designer before August 30th" — five days out, not exactly last-chance.
- The real issue is the ask: this isn't a one-click discount, it's "book a free appointment" to be entered into a sweepstakes, with fine print about "no purchase necessary" and official rules. That's a much higher-friction, lower-immediate-value ask than a %-off sale, and it's buried under a subject line that reads like a straightforward giveaway. The product carousel below is disconnected from the sweepstakes entirely and just looks like a bolted-on "recommended for you" grid.
- Net: creative variety earns credit, but the mismatch between subject urgency and actual offer, plus a CTA that requires booking an appointment rather than a purchase, caps this in the middle of the pack.

## What's working

- Clear hero visual — a styled console table with lamp, clock, and framed photos in gold tones directly supports "Home Refresh."
- Clean visual hierarchy: badge → headline → image → CTA button, easy to scan top to bottom.
- Secondary block (free in-store design class RSVP) is well-composed and thematically consistent with "design services."
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken layout.
- Distinct from the last five Pottery Barn sends (bedding/catalog), a real cadence break worth noting.

## What's weak

- Subject says "last chance" but the body gives a deadline five days out (Aug 30) — urgency mismatch, feels manufactured.
- The offer requires booking a designer appointment, not making a purchase — the CTA "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" doesn't read as "enter to win," so the subject's promise isn't reinforced by the CTA copy.
- "Recommended Just For You" product grid (curtain rods, chairs, beds, duvet covers) has zero connection to the sweepstakes and dilutes the single-offer focus.
- Fine print ("No purchase necessary," "Official Rules") sits right under the CTA, which undercuts the "you're basically getting $1500" framing at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body urgency mismatch — either tighten the entry deadline messaging to genuinely feel closing-soon, or drop "last chance" language if 5 days remain.
- 2. Make the CTA copy reflect the actual mechanic — "Book & Enter to Win" instead of "Book a Free Appointment," so subject and CTA reinforce each other.
- 3. Cut or move the generic product carousel — it's disconnected from the sweepstakes and better suited to a separate send.
- 4. Move "No purchase necessary" closer to the headline instead of only in fine print, to build trust rather than bury it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Enter now: win $1500 for your home refresh`
- **Subject Alt B:** `5 days left to enter our $1500 giveaway`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free design session — no purchase necessary`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet a designer by Aug 30 for your shot at $1500`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Last Chance to Enter to Win $1500" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) sliced-image email using `click.e.potterybarn.com` link tracking and `image.e.potterybarn.com`/`edm.potterybarn.com` asset hosting. It has a broken CSS block containing literal PHP `header()` calls, no detectable unsubscribe/authentication headers, and near-total absence of `alt` attributes.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All primary CTAs route through SFMC's encrypted redirect pattern: `https://click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5Nzl9...` — opaque payload, cannot be statically validated for destination correctness.
- 45 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design, since redirect endpoints require live session state) — destination integrity cannot be confirmed by this tool and would need manual click-through.
- Multiple third-party ad/analytics pixels and click trackers are embedded as `<img>` beacons: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?...`, `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=...&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`. These are standard open/conversion tracking beacons but represent third-party data sharing at open time.
- The recipient's raw email address is passed as a plaintext query parameter in multiple URLs: `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This leaks PII into referrer headers/logs of any intermediary or third-party endpoint (e.g., `svopn.potterybarn.com`, `advertising.com`) — a privacy exposure pattern worth flagging even though it's common ESP practice.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Critical bug:** the second `<style>` block contains literal, unescaped PHP code — `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store...");` — mixed directly into CSS. This is invalid CSS syntax injected into a `<style>` tag; it will either be silently ignored by mail clients (harmless) or, if this template is ever served through a PHP-processed endpoint without proper escaping, indicates a template file was pasted in raw instead of rendered. At minimum this is dead/broken code that should not ship in production HTML.
- 33 of 34 flagged images are missing `alt` text (per QA), including all hero/offer slices (`slices_01.gif`, `slices_02.jpg`, `slices_03.jpg`) and product shots (`remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.). Since this is an image-heavy sliced layout, a blocked-images/screen-reader view renders essentially blank with no fallback text — significant accessibility and deliverability-fallback gap.
- Two images use `http://` instead of `https://`: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — both in credit-card banner modules, likely stale/legacy include. Non-HTTPS assets risk being blocked by mail clients enforcing mixed-content policies.
- Standard MSO/Outlook and Apple Mail resets are present and correct (`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `.ExternalClass`, `a[x-apple-data-detectors]`), and mobile breakpoints are defined at both 480px and 650px.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- `<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot --> <!-- end dynamic ampScript slot -->` at the very top renders empty — either this send has no dynamic content configured, or the ampScript failed to resolve/was stripped before delivery. No merge-tag artifacts (e.g. unresolved `%%FirstName%%`) were found in the visible source, so no broken tokens are confirmed, but the empty dynamic slot is worth verifying against the intended campaign spec.
- Recipient identity is otherwise only present via the plaintext email address embedded in tracking URLs (see §2), not as a rendered on-page personalization token.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** CAN-SPAM requires a visible unsubscribe mechanism (which may exist in the untruncated footer HTML) but the missing header means no one-click unsubscribe is offered at the mail-client level (Gmail/Yahoo "Unsubscribe" button).
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058).** Combined with the above, this sender does not support one-click unsubscribe — a requirement under Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender guidelines for senders at this volume.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found**, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from this data — flagged as unknown rather than failing, since the QA tool notes this may be a relay-capture limitation rather than a true absence.
- Physical-address/footer unsubscribe link content was not visible in the truncated HTML provided, so CAN-SPAM footer compliance itself cannot be confirmed or denied from available evidence.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present on any link; Pottery Barn uses SFMC's proprietary encrypted `qs=` token instead. This is consistent with their platform but means landing-page attribution depends entirely on the redirect resolving correctly server-side — not verifiable from static HTML.
- One direct (non-redirected) link was visible: `https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` — however this entire block is HTML-commented out (`<!-- ... -->`) and will not render, so it's dead code, not a live link.
- No issues found regarding domain consistency — all tracking/image domains resolve under Pottery Barn/Williams-Sonoma-owned subdomains (`e.potterybarn.com`, `edm.potterybarn.com`, `image.e.potterybarn.com`) except the one stray `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`, a cross-brand spacer asset reused from West Elm's evergreen library — functionally harmless but worth noting as a shared-asset dependency across brand ESP instances.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Remove the literal PHP `header()` statements from the second `<style>` block — this is either dead code or a leaked server-side template fragment and should not ship in production HTML.
2. Add `alt` attributes to all content-bearing images, especially the three hero/offer slices, so the message remains legible with images blocked.
3. Upgrade the two `http://` image sources (`Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, legacy `slices_02.jpg`) to `https://`.
4. Implement `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to meet RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe requirements for bulk senders.
5. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC status independently (e.g., via mail-server logs) since `Authentication-Results` wasn't captured here.
6. Review whether passing the raw recipient email address as a URL query parameter (`coreemail.html?email=...`, `sv_sveme=...`) to first- and third-party endpoints is intentional and consistent with privacy policy.
7. Verify the empty `ampScript` slot is expected for this send rather than a failed dynamic-content resolution.
8. Remove the dead, commented-out credit-card banner block if it's no longer used, to reduce payload size.
