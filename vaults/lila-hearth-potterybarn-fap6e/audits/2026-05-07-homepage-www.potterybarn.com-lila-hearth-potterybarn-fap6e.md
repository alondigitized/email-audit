# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn and immediately got a face full of modal. Before I can see a single product, a sign-up overlay is demanding my email *and* my phone number, complete with a wall of legal fine print about "automatic telephone dialing systems." The brand looks aspirational and expensive — that sofa image in the modal is genuinely lovely — but the aggressive intercept feels more like a timeshare pitch than a premium home brand.

## What's working

- **Modal lifestyle image:** The white sofa with the bold floral pillows is exactly the layered-texture look I'm going for in my second home — it made me pause before I hit X.
- **"Find out first" framing:** That phrase is well-pitched for a Millennial who wants to feel ahead of the drop, not behind it. It's a smarter hook than "Subscribe."
- **Brand family nav bar:** I can see Williams-Sonoma brands across the top — as a Williams-Sonoma loyalist, that cross-brand context is quietly reassuring.
- **Chat widget:** Visible immediately, bottom right. I actually use in-store consults, so a live chat option matters to me.

## What's weak

- **Full-viewport modal as the first experience:** I came to shop, not to sign a data-sharing agreement. The intercept creates immediate friction before I've seen one product.
- **Asking for mobile number upfront:** The fine print says "automatic telephone dialing systems" and "prerecorded voice." That is not the vibe for a premium home brand. It makes me feel like I'm opting into spam.
- **Legal fine print volume:** There are two dense asterisked paragraphs before the fields. On a phone screen this reads as a red flag, not a trust signal.
- **No offer specificity:** "Exclusive savings & more" is the vaguest possible pitch. What savings? A percentage? Early access? If I'm handing over my number, I need to know what I'm getting.
- **Homepage content invisible:** I cannot evaluate navigation, categories, hero offers, or new arrivals — they're all behind the modal. For someone ready to browse sofa updates for a second home, this is a dead stop.

## Recommendations

- **Remove the phone field from the modal** (or make it optional and clearly labeled "for SMS deals only — never auto-dialed"). The legal wall is a conversion killer; simplify to email only with one clean opt-in line.
- **Add a specific offer to the modal:** Replace "exclusive savings & more" with something concrete — "15% off your first order" or "Early access to new arrivals." Give me a reason to type my email right now.
- **Trigger the modal after 15 seconds or first scroll**, not on page load. Let me see the homepage first. I'm far more likely to sign up once the product has done its job.
- **Swap the generic sofa for a seasonally-relevant vignette with a named product** — e.g., "The Comfort Roll-Arm Sofa, now in Textured Linen" — so the modal image doubles as a shop entry point, not just decoration.
