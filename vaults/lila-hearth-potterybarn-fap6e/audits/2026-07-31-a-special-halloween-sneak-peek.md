# A special Halloween sneak peek 👻
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "A special Halloween sneak peek 👻," but the email opens with a "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" ribbon before it ever gets to Halloween — a small disconnect between what the inbox promised and what greets you first. Once you scroll past that ribbon, the Halloween section itself is genuinely well done: a moody six-photo grid (ghost decor, jack-o'-lanterns, a skeleton dog bed, a haunted dollhouse) under "Halloween Favorites Are Back" reads as a real seasonal teaser, not a token mention.
- The problem is what happens after the Halloween CTA. This follows the exact kitchen-sink pattern flagged in prior Pottery Barn sends (07/29 duvet email, 07/30 rug email) — six unrelated "Recommended Just For You" products (curtain rods, a dining table, bath towels), then a New Arrivals banner, then an Outdoor sale banner, then app/credit-card/Affirm blocks. Halloween is maybe 15% of the total email length; the rest is generic cross-sell that has nothing to do with the subject line that got you to open it.
- Net: strong seasonal creative undercut by a subject-to-content mismatch and the brand's now-familiar sprawl. Call it a solid mid-tier send — better than the generic promos, not as tight as the 07/30 rug or 07/28 quilt emails.

## What's working

- The Halloween grid is genuinely on-theme and well-styled — ghosts, pumpkins, a skeleton-print dog bed, and a haunted dollhouse all read as cohesive seasonal merchandising, not clip art.
- "Halloween Favorites Are Back" sits on a high-contrast dark band that visually separates it from the rest of the email, making it easy to spot even in a fast scroll.
- The CTA button "THE (EARLY) HALLOWEEN SHOP" plays up exclusivity/early-access, which matches the "sneak peek" subject promise.
- No render bugs — every image loads cleanly, text doesn't overlap, and the layout holds together end to end.
- The Toscana Dining Table listing includes a size range (60"-124.5"), a nice bit of product detail most of the other grid items lack.

## What's weak

- The email leads with a "Summer Sale: Up to 70% off" ribbon, not Halloween — a reader who opened specifically for the "sneak peek" has to scroll past unrelated summer-sale messaging first.
- The "Recommended Just For You" grid (curtain rods, platform bed, towels, chair, duvet) shows no prices at all, just product names — a real friction point for click-through.
- After the Halloween CTA, the email sprawls into New Arrivals, Outdoor sale, app download, and credit card blocks with zero connection to Halloween or to each other — the same kitchen-sink tail called out in the 07/29 and 07/30 sends.
- No credible urgency: "before they vanish again" is vague filler, not a real deadline or limited-quantity cue.
- CTA copy across the email is uniformly generic ("Shop Now," "Learn More," "Discover") rather than specific to what's being sold.

## Recommendations

- 1. Reorder the top of the email so the Halloween grid is the very first thing seen — move or drop the Summer Sale ribbon entirely for this send. A subject that says "sneak peek" needs the peek to be the hero, not buried under an unrelated promo banner.
- 2. Add prices to the "Recommended Just For You" grid — six named products with no price is a dead click for anyone price-checking before tapping through.
- 3. Cut the email after the Halloween shop CTA, or at minimum move New Arrivals/Outdoor into a separate send — a "sneak peek" email should stay a sneak peek, not become a general catalog.
- 4. Give the Halloween CTA a real deadline or scarcity cue ("Sneak peek ends Sunday" / "Only available to email subscribers through 8/10") instead of the vague "before they vanish again."
- 5. Swap generic CTA verbs ("Shop Now," "Discover") for category-specific copy ("Shop Halloween Decor," "See the Full Collection").

- **Subject Alt B:** `👻 Early access: our Halloween collection is live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ghosts, pumpkins & more — before it sells out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New for fall: skeleton dog beds, haunted houses & more`
