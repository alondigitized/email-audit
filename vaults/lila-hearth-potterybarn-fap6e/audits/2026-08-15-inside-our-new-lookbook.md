# Inside our NEW lookbook
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's seasonal lookbook send — "The Fall Lookbook" positioned as editorial inspiration ("inspired by heritage design") rather than a transactional offer. It's a big, moody, well-shot piece of content: three named vignettes (Heritage Autumn, Indigo Blues, Rustic Lodge), each with a full-bleed lifestyle photo and a "Shop the Look" CTA, followed by a "Recommended Just For You" personalized product grid.
- Compared to the rest of this week's cascade (Fairfax nightstands, dining tables, Kravet collection), this one leans hardest into brand storytelling and lightest into hard-sell — there's no discount, no urgency, nothing time-bound. It's pure inspiration/discovery, banking on beautiful photography and "1600+ New Arrivals" to pull a click.
- That's a legitimate top-of-funnel play, but as a promotional email judged for immediate action it's weak: no offer, no price signal, no reason to act today. It reads like a catalog flip-through, not a reason to open your wallet — solid brand equity, low conversion urgency.

## What's working

- Clear, singular hero message — "The Fall Lookbook" — with a real content hook ("inspired by heritage design"), not a generic banner.
- Three distinct, well-differentiated room stories (plaid/heritage, indigo/blue collected pieces, rustic lodge/leather) each get their own photo + CTA, giving the eye clear stopping points.
- "Recommended Just For You" section adds personalization signal and ties back to specific SKUs (curtain rod, platform bed, sheet set, wool rug, mattress) rather than just more lifestyle shots.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent typography and spacing throughout.
- Loyalty/rewards content present lower in the email (10% Back in Rewards, Affirm financing) for engaged subscribers.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer anywhere in the primary content — no %, no $, no free shipping threshold called out near the hero or looks.
- No urgency of any kind — nothing time-bound, so there's no reason to click today versus next week.
- "1600+ New Arrivals" is the only quantified hook and it's generic/overwhelming rather than a reason to click a specific thing.
- The Halloween Shop and Design Crew Sweepstakes banners further down dilute the "lookbook" focus — three different asks (browse looks, shop Halloween, enter sweepstakes) compete for attention in one email.
- All three "Shop the Look" CTAs are visually identical and low-contrast (thin outlined button on photo) — nothing distinguishes a primary action from secondary ones.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete incentive near the hero — even a modest "extra 10% off new arrivals this week" — since a pure lookbook send with zero offer will underperform against this brand's discount-driven sends.
- 2. Make "Shop the Look" buttons higher-contrast (solid fill, not thin outline on photo) so the CTA reads clearly on mobile thumbnails.
- 3. Cut or move the Halloween Shop / Design Crew Sweepstakes blocks to their own dedicated sends — they're already running separately this week and dilute this email's single "lookbook" story.
- 4. Lead the "Recommended Just For You" section higher, right after the three looks, since personalized SKUs are the most clickable content and are currently buried below three photo blocks.
- 5. Tighten "Discover 1600+ New Arrivals" into something more specific — a category count or a "new this week" framing reads more actionable than a large flat number.
