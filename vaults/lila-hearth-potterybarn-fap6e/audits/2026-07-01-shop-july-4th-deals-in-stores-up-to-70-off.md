# Shop July 4th deals in stores: Up to 70% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's clearest channel pivot in the entire July 4th run: a deliberate in-store traffic driver. Every prior send in this now 12-email-plus campaign has been online-friendly; this one puts "Summer savings, in stores now!" in the hero and gives the email's second act to a Floor Model Sale flagged explicitly as "In Stores Only—Limited Quantities." That's a real strategic choice — but it narrows the relevant audience to a fraction of the email list. People who have been clicking through to shop potterybarn.com for two weeks will feel the friction the moment they open.
- The production is clean. Category-level discount tiles, a loyalty rewards callout, an Affirm pay-over-time block, and an in-store design event (Find the Right Rug, Sunday 12PM) create a reasonably full experience. But the CTA sprawl is out of control — at least six distinct action buttons compete for the click with no single one dominant. The email can't decide whether it wants you to book an appointment, find a store, shop all deals, download the app, or learn about financing.
- Verdict: competent execution of a channel-constraining strategy. It will work for the subset of recipients who shop in stores; it will underperform with the online majority who make up the bulk of this list.

## What's working

- Category tiles with specific discount percentages ("Up to 40% off Pillows & Decor," "Up to 70% off Bedding") give the reader a fast mental map of where the value lives.
- "Earn 50% Bonus in Rewards" is the most differentiated offer this campaign has surfaced in 12 sends — it makes the in-store trip feel like it has a loyalty upside that online can't match.
- The Floor Model Sale section earns its place: "limited quantities" signals genuine scarcity without feeling manufactured.
- The in-store Design Class (Find the Right Rug, Sunday 12PM) is the first event-driven content this campaign has offered — fresh after 12 straight discount-only sends.
- Clean render throughout: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks.

## What's weak

- The in-store pivot cuts off most of the email list. Promotional email recipients overwhelmingly want to shop online; routing them to a store finder or appointment booking creates immediate friction.
- Six-plus competing CTAs (Book a Free Appointment, Find Your Store, Shop All Deals, Find a Store, Shop Today, Download Now, Learn More) make the email feel directionless — there is no primary action.
- After 12 consecutive "up to 70% off" sends, the discount language has been fully trained out of its urgency — subscribers have seen this number so many times it no longer registers as a signal.
- The subject line telegraphs "in stores" upfront, which will suppress opens among the online-only segment before a single pixel loads.
- No explicit sale end date is visible anywhere in the email. "July 4th" is implied but never stated as a deadline, which is the one thing that could have rescued the urgency narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** One for online shoppers (sale at potterybarn.com, clearly stated), one for in-store traffic (floor model sale + design class event). This single email is diluted trying to serve both.
- 2. **Cut to three CTAs maximum.** One for the floor model sale, one for the design class, one "Shop All Deals" for everything else. Six-plus buttons is not a hierarchy, it's a menu nobody ordered.
- 3. **Put an explicit end date in the hero.** "Ends July 4th" in the headline would do more for urgency than every percentage in this email combined.
- 4. **Promote the 50% Bonus Rewards to the subject line.** It's the most differentiated offer Pottery Barn has shown in weeks and it's buried mid-email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% Bonus Rewards ends July 4th — shop in stores`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Floor models from [price] — in-store only, while they last`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 70% off furniture & bedding. Limited floor model stock at your nearest store.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your rewards multiplier ends this weekend. Redeem in store or online.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "July 4th Sale / Design Crew" (2026-07-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends correctly but contains a confirmed PHP code-injection into a `<style>` block and two mixed-content (HTTP) image sources. Compliance header detection was incomplete at the relay layer, leaving SPF/DKIM status and one-click unsubscribe unverifiable.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No live broken links.** The 403 flagged by QA (`/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB…`) is inside an HTML comment block and is not rendered or clickable by recipients. The QA probe hit the commented-out URL, which is a false positive for end-user impact — but the dead URL is evidence the credit card banner was removed mid-production without cleanup.

**23 click-redirect links** via `click.e.potterybarn.com` were skipped by the probe (expected; this is the ESP's redirect domain). Final destinations are unverifiable from source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code in `<style>` block — template contamination:**
```html
<style>
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
...
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were not executed server-side and leaked verbatim into the email's CSS. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and discard the block. The `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset rules and Outlook line-height fix that follow in the same block may be silently dropped as a result.

**[HIGH] 2 images served over HTTP (mixed content):**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Clients with enforced HTTPS (iOS Mail, Gmail) will block these images silently. Both are on the same CDN host — HTTPS works there; this is an asset-path error.

**[MEDIUM] 30+ images missing `alt` text** — all body slices (`slices_01` through `slices_10`), all footer slices (`slices_01` through `slices_18`), and multiple tracking pixels. The sole image with alt text is the header logo (`alt="POTTERY BARN"`). Sliced-image emails with no alt text render as a blank white rectangle in image-off environments.

**[LOW] Inconsistent responsive breakpoints:** The outer media query triggers at `max-width: 480px` and snaps the wrapper to 320px, but the second `@media screen and (max-width: 650px)` triggers `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show` toggles at 650px. The 480px→320px snap is aggressive for modern phones; 600px is the standard trigger for a 650px desktop layout.

---

No unfired merge tokens or broken substitution syntax visible in the delivered HTML. The subscriber address visible in tracking pixels (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) resolved correctly — personalization fired as expected.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** by the AgentMail relay. This is required for CAN-SPAM and is enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (>5k/day senders). May be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence — cannot confirm either way from current data.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.** One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo since 2024) is unverifiable. Same caveat as above.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent** from captured headers — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown. `PotteryBarn@e.potterybarn.com` sends from `e.potterybarn.com` (likely ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud); DKIM alignment to the `potterybarn.com` parent domain should be confirmed independently.

Unsubscribe link is present in the email body (visible in footer slice structure); physical mailing address presence is not verifiable from truncated HTML but is standard for this sender.

No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters are visible in any href before hitting the click-redirect domain. UTM attribution may be injected by `click.e.potterybarn.com` on redirect — this is common for ESP-managed sends — but it cannot be confirmed without following a redirect chain. No direct landing-page links are present in the visible source — all CTAs route through the click tracker, so landing-page alignment (sale page actually showing July 4th pricing) is not auditable from source.
