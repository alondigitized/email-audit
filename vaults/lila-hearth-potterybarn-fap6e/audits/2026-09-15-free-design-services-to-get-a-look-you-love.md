# FREE design services to get a look you love
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's Design Services pitch — "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" — a service offer rather than a product discount, structured as what/when/where with a clear "Book a Free Appointment" CTA up top. It's a genuine departure from the recent run of category and seasonal-decor sends (Halloween, outdoor, Kravet, dining, holiday preview, seating) and stands out for that reason, but the offer itself is soft: "free design services" isn't a purchase incentive, it's a lead-gen funnel, and the email repeats the same "Bring This Look to Life" CTA three times against three different room vignettes without differentiating what a consultation actually gets you.
- The layout is clean and on-brand — warm, well-lit lifestyle photography, consistent typography, no render bugs — but it's a long scroll before you hit anything resembling a hard incentive (the 10% credit card rewards block near the footer). For a persona shopping on discounts or urgency, this email has neither; it's a service upsell dressed in decor eye-candy.
- Judgement: solid execution, weak incentive. This will read fine to a design-curious existing customer but won't convert an inbox skimmer looking for a deal.

## What's working

- Clear what/when/where structure in the hero makes the service offer easy to parse at a glance.
- "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" button is visible immediately and repeated later in the body, reducing scroll friction to act.
- Three distinct room vignettes (living room, bedroom, entry/bath) give the eye variety while staying visually consistent.
- "Recommended Just for You" product grid at the bottom adds a personalization touch beyond the pure service pitch.
- No layout bugs, broken images, or overlapping text — a clean render top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The hero says "FREE" but there's no discount, dollar amount, or purchase incentive — it's a soft lead-gen offer, weaker than the rubric's "concrete offer" signal.
- "BRING THIS LOOK TO LIVE" repeats three times with no differentiation — same CTA, same copy, three rooms — feels templated rather than deliberate.
- The 10% credit card rewards and free in-store design class (Sept 27) are buried near the bottom, past a very long scroll, where most recipients will never see them.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email — "choose a time that works for you" is the closest thing to a call to act now, and it's soft.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with a reason to book *now* — a deadline, a seasonal tie-in (e.g., "book before the holidays"), or a stat ("save an average of $X with a free design consult") — the current version has nothing time-bound or quantified.
- 2. Vary the three "Bring This Look to Life" CTAs — swap in room-specific copy ("Style This Living Room," "Refresh This Bedroom") so each section feels like its own pitch instead of a repeated stamp.
- 3. Move the September 27 in-store design class and the 10% credit card rewards block higher — both are concrete, dated hooks currently wasted at the bottom of a long scroll.
- 4. Add a testimonial or before/after pair near the top — design services live or die on social proof, and none is visible here.
