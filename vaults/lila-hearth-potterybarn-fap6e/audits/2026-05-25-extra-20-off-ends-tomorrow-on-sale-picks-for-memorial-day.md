# Extra 20% off 🕰️ ENDS TOMORROW + On-sale picks for Memorial Day
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's nineteenth-or-so Memorial Day email in roughly a week — but it has two things going for it that most of its predecessors didn't. First, "ENDS TOMORROW" is now actually true: with Memorial Day on 5/26, the urgency is no longer manufactured. Second, the "Recommended Just for You" module in the lower body delivers a genuine personalization signal absent from most prior sends. That combination gives this email a real hook it can lean on.
- The hero, however, is the same red "MEMORIAL DAY SALE — Extra 20% off" banner I've seen on every send since at least May 18. The category grid below it (furniture, bedding, rugs, sofas) is identical to the grids in prior sends. The subject promises "on-sale picks for Memorial Day" — a phrase that implies curated, specific products — but the hero delivers a generic broad-category layout. The personalization module is buried below the fold rather than leading.
- Bottom line: a stronger execution than the mid-week wallpaper sends, but Pottery Barn left points on the table by not wiring the "on-sale picks" promise in the subject directly to the personalized module hero. One structural swap away from being notably good.

## What's working

- **"ENDS TOMORROW" is now credible.** After days of "ENDS SOON" on every email, the final-day signal is legitimate and should convert fence-sitters.
- **"Recommended Just for You" section is visible in the body.** This breaks the pure-template monotony and gives subscribers a reason to scroll.
- **Clean render, no layout breakage.** All images loaded, buttons are present, no overlapping text or broken blocks visible.
- **Loyalty section (Pottery Barn Key Rewards) is visible.** Members get a relevant reminder near the close.
- **Hero offer is above the fold.** The Extra 20% off headline and CTA load first — no scroll required to see the deal.

## What's weak

- **The hero template is visually identical to every prior send.** The same red banner, same headline, same category grid. At send nineteen, this reads as automated, not curated.
- **Subject promises "on-sale picks" but hero delivers a generic category grid.** The specific/personal framing in the subject line sets an expectation the opening section doesn't meet.
- **Personalization is below the fold.** The "Recommended Just for You" section — the email's strongest differentiator — is buried after a category grid and a second CTA button. It should lead.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "SHOP THE SALE" and "VIEW ALL SALE" are category-agnostic labels with no connection to the "on-sale picks" framing in the subject.
- **Category grid is identical to prior sends.** Furniture / Bedding / Rugs & Pillows / Sofas is the same grid I've seen in at least four recent emails.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the personalized module.** The "Recommended Just for You" product grid is the email's best differentiator. Move it above the category grid — or make it the hero entirely. The subject line already promises "on-sale picks," so the body should deliver that immediately.
- 2. **Swap the hero banner copy to match the final-day urgency.** "Extra 20% off" has been the headline for a week. "Last chance — sale ends tonight" with the same 20% callout would feel fresher and truer to the moment.
- 3. **Specifify the CTA.** Change "SHOP THE SALE" → "See My Picks" or "Shop Your Picks" to mirror the personalization promise.
- 4. **Trim the subject to under 55 chars.** The current subject is ~63 chars; it will truncate on most mobile inboxes before "On-sale picks for Memorial Day" is visible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: Extra 20% off ends tonight 🕰️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day picks — 20% off ends tomorrow`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hand-picked sale finds based on what you love — final hours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, bedding, rugs: your curated picks before the sale closes.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn Memorial Day Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will silently fail for affected recipients. The email also carries 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset URLs across image sources and a tracking pixel, creating mixed-content blocking risk in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — 403 on credit card banner CTA**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...`
The "Apply & Buy" / Key Rewards credit card banner link returns HTTP 403. Recipients clicking this CTA land on an error. The `external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner` parameter confirms this is the header non-member credit card module.

**FAIL — 403 on West Elm spacer (cross-brand asset)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A tracking/layout spacer GIF from the West Elm CDN domain returns 403. This is a stale cross-brand asset reference — the file has either been deleted or the domain no longer serves it. It also uses HTTP (see §3).

**INFO — 24 tracking links not probed**
All `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect links were skipped by the checker. No broken-link confirmation, but no clean bill of health either.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP mixed-content — 3 image sources**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| West Elm spacer | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| Key CC footer banner | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| Brand banner slice | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-pixel tracker | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

Gmail and Outlook.com proxy all images through HTTPS; plain HTTP sources will be re-fetched through Google's/Microsoft's image proxy and may break tracking signal on the open pixel specifically.

**Missing alt text — 40+ images**
Every product slice image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` in both the Grid1 and Email-Footer blocks) and all product tile images (`cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `toscana-extendable-dining-table…`, etc.) have empty or absent `alt` attributes. When images are blocked, the email renders as a blank canvas with no legible content.

**PHP-style `header()` calls injected into a `<style>` block**
```html
<style>
  ...
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
</style>
```
Server-side PHP header directives are present verbatim inside a `<style>` tag. These are inert in email clients but indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic image-serving snippet was pasted into the wrong template block. Strict CSS parsers may flag or discard the entire style block.

**`<title>` is empty**
`<title></title>` — some clients (notably iOS Mail) surface the title in notifications or tab labels.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). Pottery Barn's send volume almost certainly exceeds that threshold.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**
One-click unsubscribe is required by the same Gmail/Yahoo policy. Without this header, Gmail will not render the native one-click unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**WARN — SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC/SPF/DKIM pass for `e.potterybarn.com`. No finding is raised — but this should be verified against live SMTP headers before the send.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** — correctly present; prevents search indexing of the email web version.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 24 tracked click URLs all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirects. UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed without following the redirects, but the redirect domain pattern is consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud link wrapping, which typically injects `cm_ite`, `cm_sp`, or custom params at redirect time.

The 403 on the credit card CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) means that specific conversion path is dead — any attribution tied to that module will not resolve to a landing page.
