# FREE design services to get a look you love
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the most strategically interesting Pottery Barn email in the June batch — not because it's the most polished, but because it breaks the mold. After seven consecutive "Up to 70% off" hammers since June 12, switching to a service-marketing angle ("Free Design Services") is the right call. The inbox is fatigued; a completely different value proposition at least earns a second glance.
- The execution is competent. The hero is clean, the CTAs are legible, and the lifestyle imagery is consistent with the Pottery Barn aesthetic. The "Recommended Just for You" product grid at the bottom is a smart hedge — it converts this from a pure service pitch into something that can still drive product clicks.
- The miss is strategic, not executional: this email asks a lot of a subscriber who has spent a week being conditioned to expect a discount. There's no bridge from "here's a free service" to "and here's what you save by using it." Without that connective tissue, the email reads like a brand refresh rather than an offer a subscriber acts on today.

## What's working

- **Clean creative break.** First email since June 12 without "70% off" in the hero — the audience needed a pattern interrupt.
- **Dual CTA strategy is smart.** "Book an Appointment" captures intent buyers; "Shop the Style" captures browsers who aren't ready to commit to a consultation.
- **"Recommended Just for You" grid.** Putting personalized product tiles below the service pitch creates a second-chance click surface — the email can convert on product even if the service offer doesn't land.
- **No render bugs.** Imagery loads cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds across the full render.
- **Sender name is differentiated.** "Pottery Barn Design Services" signals this isn't another promo blast, which may lift open rate among the segment that's started ignoring the sale emails.

## What's weak

- **No financial hook for the service.** "Free" is in the subject but the email never quantifies what you get for free — no "save on design fees," no "see how a $0 consultation turns into a room you love."
- **Missing urgency.** Every prior email this week had an implicit deadline (the sale); this one floats. Nothing signals why to book now vs. next month.
- **The "Mix & Match Prints" midpage section is visually muddy.** The secondary offer (appears to have a pricing/timing callout) gets lost between the lifestyle imagery and the product grid.
- **No social proof.** A service pitch is the one place where a customer quote or "10,000 consultations completed" stat would earn real trust — it's absent here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete anchor to the free service.** Even a line like "Design fees typically run $200–$500 — yours is on us" makes "free" feel like a real dollar save, not a marketing word.
- 2. **Add soft urgency.** "Schedule before July 4th and your designer will have summer delivery windows available" is credible and timely without being manipulative.
- 3. **Surface a social-proof element in the hero.** A single short testimonial ("I had no idea what to do with my living room — one session and I had a full plan") directly below the hero CTA would raise click confidence significantly.
- 4. **Sharpen the "Recommended Just for You" section.** Tie it back to the service: "Items your designer might suggest" — it makes the product grid feel curated rather than algorithmic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free interior designer is waiting — book now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Skip the guesswork. Free design help, anytime.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One session, one look you'll actually love — at no cost.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Design fees usually run hundreds. Yours: $0.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] PHP `header()` calls injected into `<style>` block**
The second `<style>` block in `<head>` opens with:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP server-side directives that were never executed — they leaked as literal text into the CSS block. No browser or email client interprets them as CSS; they are silently ignored. The presence indicates a template preprocessing failure where a PHP file's header logic was copied raw into the HTML template. This is a code hygiene issue; it poses no rendering risk but signals a broken build/template pipeline.

**[FAIL] Two images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block mixed-content HTTP images. Both are footer assets and will render as broken images in most modern clients. These should be `https://`.

**[WARN] 38 images missing alt text**
Every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_14.jpg`), all footer slices (14 images), product images (bed, quilt, towel, etc.), and tracking pixels have `alt=""` or no `alt` attribute. Since the email is entirely image-based (sliced JPGs carry all copy and CTAs), subscribers rendering with images off receive a blank email with no actionable text fallback. Slices carrying primary CTAs should have descriptive alt text at minimum.

**[WARN] Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
Two media queries target different widths:
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` → sets `table[class="wrapper"]` to 320px
- `@media screen and (max-width: 650px)` → toggles `.mobile-hide`/`.mobile-show`

The 480px breakpoint collapses to 320px, which is below the viewport of virtually all modern smartphones (375px minimum). The effective mobile layout will be 320px wide with visible side margins on most phones. This appears to be a stale legacy breakpoint from a 2016-era template.

### 5. Compliance

These headers are required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (>5K/day threshold). If they are present at the ESP level but stripped by the relay, this is a relay config issue. If they are genuinely absent, Gmail will suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may route to spam.

**[WARN] SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain (ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud) — DKIM alignment should be in place, but cannot be confirmed from available data.

If so, this is inaccessible to plain-text rendering and screen readers — physical address must also appear as live text per CAN-SPAM requirements.
