# The edit: Molly Sims’ favorites
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's celebrity-influencer edit — "The Edit: Molly Sims' Favorites" — a beauty founder/podcast host/mom-of-three curating fall picks, shot in a warm, lived-in library-and-armchair setting. It's a well-composed lifestyle send: Molly's portrait, quote callouts ("the shape makes it fun and a little unexpected"), and a genuine curated product grid make this feel more editorial than the brand's typical sale blast. But there's zero price, zero discount, and zero urgency anywhere in the visible render — this is a pure brand/content play, not a conversion play.
- Compared to recent sends in this brand's run — the outdoor sale ("UP TO 60% OFF," 6.00/10) and the free-shipping pillow send (6.00/10) — this one trades the quantified offer for celebrity credibility. That's a fine strategic bet for brand-building, but it will underperform on click-through relative to the sale sends unless the recipient specifically cares about Molly Sims or the exact furniture shown. The "Recommended Just For You" and Design Crew sweepstakes modules below salvage some conversion potential, but they're generic personalization/lead-gen, not tied to the influencer hook.
- Judgement: strong creative, weak commercial hook — a good brand-affinity email, a mediocre performance email.

## What's working

- Clear, legible hero: "the edit / MOLLY SIMS' FAVORITES" with a well-lit portrait and one-line bio establishing why she's credible.
- Genuine curated product grid (chair, side table, garden stool, floor lamp, bowl) with two authentic-sounding pull quotes, which reads as editorial rather than templated.
- Clean visual hierarchy top to bottom: hero → picks grid → CTA button → recommended-for-you rail.
- No render bugs — images, text, and spacing all appear intact.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing to click for besides "browse products."
- "SHOP MOLLY'S PICKS" and "SHOP ALL OF MOLLY'S PICKS" CTAs are generic — neither names a product or category.
- The "Recommended Just For You" section (curtain rods, dining table, sheets) has no visible connection to Molly Sims or the fall edit — it dilutes the influencer narrative.
- The Design Crew Sweepstakes and Halloween Shop modules crowd the bottom of the email, competing with the primary story instead of reinforcing it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete incentive tied to the edit — e.g., "Molly's picks, 20% off this week" — a lifestyle hook without a reason to buy now caps click-through.
- 2. Make CTAs specific: swap "Shop Molly's Picks" for something like "Shop the Swivel Chair" on the hero, driving to the single highest-intent hero product.
- 3. Cut or move "Recommended Just For You" — it breaks the influencer narrative; if kept, retitle it "More From Molly's Home" to stay in-story.
- 4. Trim the bottom stack (Design Crew sweepstakes + Halloween Shop) to one module max — two unrelated CTAs after the main content dilutes focus.
