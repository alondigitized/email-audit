# A bestseller, now in new finishes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-finish launch email for Pottery Barn's Sausalito Collection — a furniture line being shown off in at least three new colorways: Walnut Butter, Harvest White, and what appears to be an English Country Natural Wood finish. The hero is a rich, warm dining room lifestyle shot that leads cleanly into a grid of product-specific close-ups. Execution is solid and on-brand, but there is no promotional offer, no price visible, and no urgency mechanism — making this the third consecutive editorial-only send in less than 48 hours (following both June 1 sends that also scored 7/10).
- The Sausalito Collection story is told well visually, but the email is doing all the lifting at the top with the hero, then hands off to a "Recommended for You" personalization block that dilutes the single-collection narrative. As a standalone send, this is a clean product launch. As the third no-offer send in a row from Pottery Barn, it lands with noticeably diminishing urgency — there's no reason to act today.
- Clear verdict: well-crafted editorial, but the cadence is starting to erode open motivation. A subscriber who opened "What's new—and already turning heads" yesterday is being asked to care about another new-finish story with nothing to trigger a purchase decision.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong** — the warm walnut dining scene reads immediately as premium furniture and creates genuine desire.
- **Finish names are labeled directly on the products** — "Walnut Butter," "Harvest White," and the natural wood finish are called out clearly, making the new-colorway story self-explanatory.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — Sausalito Collection headline, lifestyle shot, product grid, then recommendations. The eye flows without confusion.
- **"Recommended for You" block adds personalization signal** — suggests some segmentation is happening, even if the anchor email is not personalized.
- **No render bugs** — all images load, layout is tight, no broken elements visible.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — no discount, no free shipping, no member pricing. Three straight no-offer sends is a pattern that trains subscribers not to act.
- **Subject line doesn't name the product** — "A bestseller" is vague; a subscriber who doesn't know the Sausalito line has no hook.
- **CTA copy is too small to read at any realistic mobile scale** — the buttons are lost in the layout at this rendering.
- **"Recommended for You" undercuts the launch story** — switching from a focused Sausalito collection pitch to a multi-product recommendation grid sends mixed intent; this is a launch email or a browse-driver, not both.
- **No urgency signal of any kind** — no "new" badge with a clear launch date, no limited-colorway framing, no deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the product in the subject** — "Sausalito, now in three new finishes" tells subscribers exactly what this is and rewards loyal customers who know the collection. The current "A bestseller" is generic and wastes the brand equity in the collection name.
- 2. **Anchor to a launch-day hook** — add a line like "Introducing today — Walnut Butter, Harvest White, and English Country Natural" to create a temporal hook without inventing a sale.
- 3. **Drop or move the "Recommended for You" block** — it belongs in a browse-abandonment or digest email, not a product-launch send. Here it fragments the story.
- 4. **Add a subtle scarcity or newness signal** — "New finishes, available now" with a shop button that links directly to the Sausalito Collection page (not the homepage).

- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the new Sausalito finishes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Walnut Butter, Harvest White, and English Country Natural — shop the collection.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Sausalito Collection just got three new looks. See them now.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "A bestseller, now in new finishes"

### 1. Technical Summary

Two links are confirmed broken (HTTP 403), 48 images lack alt text, 3 image sources use plain HTTP, and a block of PHP `header()` calls was accidentally embedded inside a `<style>` element. List-Unsubscribe headers are absent, and authentication results are unavailable via the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Credit card apply/buy CTA — HTTP 403**
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
The Key CC banner CTA returns 403. Any click on this module results in a dead destination.

**[FAIL] Cross-brand West Elm spacer — HTTP 403 + plain HTTP**
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
A West Elm asset (sibling brand) is used as a layout spacer. It is both returning 403 and served over HTTP. The asset should be replaced with a self-hosted HTTPS equivalent.

**23 tracking links** through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed and cannot be confirmed working.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP code injected into `<style>` block (template bug)**
The following server-side PHP directives appear literally inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
This is invalid CSS. The PHP was never executed — it was included as literal text, likely from a copy-paste from a server-side image script. Email clients will treat it as malformed CSS; it will not execute but will produce parse warnings in strict validators.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Affects tab labels in webmail clients and screen reader announcements.

**48 images missing `alt` text**
All 15 `slices_*.jpg` content images, all product images in the recommendations module, both footer slices sets, and the open-tracking pixel have no `alt` attribute. Affects screen readers and images-off rendering in Outlook/Gmail.

**3 image sources on plain HTTP** (may be blocked by clients enforcing HTTPS):
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif         [also 403]
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true  [open pixel]
```
The open-tracking pixel on HTTP will be blocked by Apple Mail Privacy Protection and other clients that enforce mixed-content policies, causing open rate undercounting.
