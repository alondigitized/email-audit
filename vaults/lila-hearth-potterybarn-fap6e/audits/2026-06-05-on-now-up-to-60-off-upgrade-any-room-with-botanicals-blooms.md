# ON NOW: Up to 60% off + Upgrade any room with botanicals & blooms
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's third email in the inbox today — arriving after "Our Summer Warehouse Sale is ON" and "A dedicated design expert, for FREE" — and the triple-send fatigue is the biggest structural problem before a word of copy is read. Subscribers who opened both earlier sends have already seen the sale announced and a service pitch; this third hit lands as noise.
- The deeper problem is that the email is running two campaigns simultaneously: the warehouse sale banner at the top and a seasonal botanicals editorial feature immediately below. Neither gets full real estate or a clear argument. The botanical hero image is genuinely beautiful — lush, warm, seasonally right — and the six-item product grid (faux hydrangeas, butterfly bush bundles, dahlia bouquets) is a focused assortment. But the sale drum is beating in the banner overhead while the content section tries to be editorial, and the tonal split never resolves.
- The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module mid-scroll — showing what appears to be bedroom furniture and linens — is the most visible failure: it has nothing to do with botanicals and signals the personalization engine wasn't tuned to this campaign. This email would have worked far better sent tomorrow, alone, as a pure botanicals feature at warehouse prices. As the third send today, the message it actually delivers is "we're not done selling."

## What's working

- Botanical hero lifestyle image is warm, lush, and credibly summer — the right editorial register for the category
- "Beautiful Botanicals" is a stronger content header than a plain "Shop Now" — it gives the assortment an identity
- Product grid is specific and coherent: six named faux botanical SKUs, not a catch-all "shop the collection" blob
- "SHOP ALL BOTANICALS" is tighter CTA copy than the generic "SHOP NOW" buttons above it
- "Earn 10% in Rewards" loyalty badge appears near the footer — a quiet nudge without crowding the hero

## What's weak

- Third Pottery Barn email in one inbox session today — cadence this aggressive trains subscribers to skip, filter, or unsubscribe
- "Summer Warehouse Sale" banner and "Beautiful Botanicals" hero compete for the same real estate, splitting visual focus and message
- Subject line is 65 characters — mobile inboxes cut it well before "botanicals & blooms," which is the differentiating hook
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" surfaces bedroom furniture in a botanical-themed email — the personalization module is firing off-theme
- "SHOP NOW" is duplicated as the primary CTA in two separate hero sections; identical button copy with no differentiation creates choice paralysis

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this email for tomorrow and send it alone.** The warehouse sale was already announced twice today. A standalone botanicals send at warehouse prices — without the competing sale banner up top — would have gotten full attention and a clean story. The content here is good enough to carry a send; it doesn't need to share the subject line with a general sale.
- 2. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters.** The botanicals hook is the differentiator; the sale percentage is the motivator. "Up to 60% off botanicals" does both in 27 chars.
- 3. **Add a specific sale deadline.** "ON NOW" is the weakest urgency signal that exists. "Sale ends Sunday" or "3 days left" costs nothing to write and materially converts.
- 4. **Rename the CTA buttons.** "Shop Botanicals" on the lifestyle hero and "Shop The Sale" on the banner — two actions with two different destinations need two different labels.
- 5. **Suppress the "RECOMMENDED" module in campaign-specific sends**, or tune it to botanical/floral SKUs. Bedroom linens mid-scroll in a botanicals email breaks the editorial thread visibly.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off botanicals — shop the sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Beautiful botanicals at warehouse prices`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Faux hydrangeas, dahlia bundles & more — sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bring color home for less. Up to 60% off this week only.`

**Missing alt text — 38 images total**, including all primary content images:
- `S26_TOP_SummerWarehouseSale_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero image, no alt
- All 9 `slices_0x.jpg` botanicals images — no alt
- All 18 footer `slices_xx.jpg` — no alt
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `sausalito-8-drawer-dresser-66-l.jpg`, `hydrocotton-quick-dry-towel-l.jpg`, etc.

Recipients with images disabled (a significant share of B2B inboxes) see a blank email. Screen reader experience is non-functional.

**CSS artifact in `<style>` block:** PHP `header()` calls are embedded inside a `<style>` tag — they have no effect in HTML email and indicate a copy-paste error from a server-side template:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
```
These should be removed.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — minor, but some clients display this in tab/preview UI.

**7. Recommendations**

1. Replace or remove the broken West Elm spacer GIF — it 403s and is served over HTTP from a foreign brand domain.
2. Fix the 403 credit card apply/buy CTA link (`clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407…`) — this is likely the highest-value conversion action in the email.

**P1 — Fix this cycle:**
3. Upgrade all three HTTP image sources to HTTPS to prevent mixed-content blocking.
4. Remove the PHP `header()` lines from the `<style>` block — dead code, signals template hygiene issues.
5. Add descriptive `alt` text to the hero, product, and botanicals images — minimum viable: describe the product or offer shown.

**P2 — Audit separately:**
6. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in delivered mail headers (check via Gmail "Show original") — required for bulk sender compliance.
7. Confirm UTM parameters survive the click-redirect chain for attribution accuracy.
8. Probe the 25 skipped tracking links in an isolated environment to validate destination URL health.
