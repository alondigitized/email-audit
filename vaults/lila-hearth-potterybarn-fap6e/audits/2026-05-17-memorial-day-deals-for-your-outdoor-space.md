# MEMORIAL DAY DEALS for your outdoor space ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Day 4 of Pottery Barn's Memorial Day campaign, and the creative team made the smartest pivot of the run: a dedicated outdoor-living send, themed to season and aligned to the Memorial Day = backyard season mental model. The "Extra 20% off" offer hasn't changed since May 15, but anchoring it to "your outdoor space" gives it a reason to exist rather than being a fourth identical reminder blast.
- The execution is clean — hero image lands the offer, CTA is category-specific ("SHOP OUTDOOR DEALS"), and the product grid is coherent. Where it stumbles: the "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block pulls in what appear to be indoor furniture pieces, and one of the promo tiles below the hero pitches "Up to 60% off Bedding," which is jarring mid-outdoor email. The footer bloat (design services, app, credit card, Affirm) is the same stack across every recent send and blurs focus in the back half.
- Overall, this is the best-justified send in the campaign run — but the personalization block actively undermines the outdoor narrative, and the offer fatigue from four consecutive days of the same discount is real.

## What's working

- **Outdoor hero image earns the subject line** — lifestyle shot of a fully staged patio delivers instant category recognition.
- **CTA copy is category-specific** — "SHOP OUTDOOR DEALS" is better than the generic "Shop Now" used in prior sends.
- **Offer is clearly stated** — "Extra 20% off Select Styles" is in the hero without hunting.
- **Thematic focus is sharp above the fold** — the first scroll of content is coherent and outdoor-only.
- **Memorial Day timing is contextually earned** — outdoor space + holiday weekend is a natural link that justifies the send.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 60% off Bedding" tile appears mid-outdoor email** — a visible category mismatch that fractures the outdoor narrative.
- **"RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" block pulls indoor furniture** — visible disconnect from the email's stated focus; personalization should reinforce, not contradict, the theme.
- **Fourth consecutive send with the same base offer** — "Extra 20% off" has run since May 15; no escalation, no urgency signal that the sale is ending soon.
- **No specific outdoor product highlighted** — the hero is a lifestyle scene, not a hero SKU with a name and price, so there's nothing concrete to click toward.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the "Recommended Just For You" products for outdoor-specific recs** — the algorithm likely pulls top performers globally, not by email theme. Force the outdoor category filter for this send. That single change tightens the narrative through the full scroll.
- 2. **Kill the bedding tile or replace with outdoor-specific category** — "Up to 60% off Outdoor Rugs & Pillows" would earn its spot; bedding is a non-sequitur.
- 3. **Add a sale-end date to the hero** — "Sale ends Monday, May 26" turns "Extra 20% off" from a passive discount into a credible deadline, which Day 4 needs more than Day 1 did.
- 4. **Name and price one hero SKU** — a specific sectional or lounge chair with a before/after price gives the click a destination; the current lifestyle hero gives nowhere to land except a broad category page.
- 5. **Prune the footer services stack** — design services, app download, credit card, and Affirm all appear in the same footer as the prior three sends. Move at least two to a dedicated cadence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends Monday — 20% off all outdoor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio moment: Extra 20% off outdoor furniture`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, lounge chairs, rugs — sale ends Memorial Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the outdoor edit before the long weekend hits.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (3) — will be blocked by most email clients and iOS Mail:**

- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`

**Missing alt text: 38 images** — includes all `slices_01` through `slices_18` content images, all product images, footer images, and tracking pixels. In image-blocked environments (common in B2B, government, or privacy-mode clients), the email renders as a blank table.

**PHP code inside `<style>` block — template processing failure:**

```css
/* Inside <style> tag: */
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

This is server-side PHP that was never executed — it was emitted literally into the rendered HTML. It is inert but signals a broken template pipeline or a copy/paste error from a dynamic image script. Browsers/email clients will treat it as invalid CSS text.

**Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` — minor but affects threading/tab display in webmail.

**Conflicting breakpoints:** Two `@media` rules target overlapping widths — `min-width: 700px` sets `img { max-width: 650px }` and `min-width: 650px` sets `img { max-width: 100% !important }`. The 650px rule always wins over the 700px rule, making the first rule dead code.

**Cross-brand asset:** `edm.westelm.com` (West Elm) is serving a spacer gif inside a Pottery Barn email. Functionally harmless but indicates a shared template that was not sanitized for brand.
