# Save up to 60% across the home
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's whole-house warehouse sale, and it's the most direct discount play in the recent run — "the warehouse sale / Savings Across the Home / SHOP UP TO 60% OFF" leads immediately with a hard number instead of a lifestyle story. It's also the most sprawling: bedroom, living room, dining room, a "Recommended Just For You" grid, a "Discover New Arrivals" banner, and a Halloween Shop teaser all stacked into one very long scroll, which dilutes the 60%-off hook the subject line promised.
- The hero shot (bed with layered blue/cream bedding, rattan pendants) is warm and on-brand, and the category structure — Bedroom → Living Room → Dining Room, each with two shoppable sub-tiles ("Ships Free" badges included) — mirrors the well-executed dining email from 2026-09-13. But after the third category block the email loses discipline: it tacks on a 9-tile recommendation grid, a New Arrivals banner, and a Halloween teaser, none of which reinforce the 60%-off message, so the send reads like three emails stitched together.
- Net: strong open hook (concrete %, clear brand), mediocre execution once you're in the body — the offer gets buried under scroll length and competing CTAs.

## What's working

- Hero band leads with a specific, high number — "SHOP UP TO 60% OFF" — as a standalone button right under the headline, unmissable at the top.
- Consistent three-part structure per category (lifestyle photo → headline → two product tiles with "Ships Free" callouts) makes bedroom/living/dining easy to scan.
- Clean, uncluttered product photography throughout — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- "Shop All Sale" button appears mid-email after the dining section, giving a second clear path to the full sale.

## What's weak

- The 60%-off promise isn't repeated or reinforced anywhere after the hero — each category section just says "Save on..." with no visible percentage or price, so the headline offer feels disconnected from the actual products shown.
- Email is extremely long and loses focus: after three sale categories it pivots to a "Recommended Just For You" grid, then "Discover New Arrivals," then "The Halloween Shop" — three different campaigns competing with the original sale message.
- No urgency or end-date anywhere in the visible copy — "the warehouse sale" reads open-ended, which undercuts the classic clearance-urgency play.
- Rewards/Affirm/app blocks at the bottom are fine as footer content, but combined with the New Arrivals and Halloween teasers, they push the actual sale CTA further from the fold than it needs to be.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the dining/"Shop All Sale" block — drop the Recommended, New Arrivals, and Halloween sections into separate sends so the 60%-off message doesn't get diluted by three unrelated campaigns.
- 2. Put a visible discount or price callout on at least one tile per category (e.g., "Now 40% Off" badge on the bedding tile) so the hero's 60%-off claim is substantiated as you scroll, not just asserted once.
- 3. Add a credible deadline to the hero ("Ends Sunday" / "This Week Only") — warehouse sales without a clock don't create urgency.
- 4. Repeat "up to 60% off" as a section eyebrow at least once mid-email (e.g., above the living room block) to re-anchor the offer for anyone who scrolls past the hero.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Markdowns just added across furniture, bedding & decor`
