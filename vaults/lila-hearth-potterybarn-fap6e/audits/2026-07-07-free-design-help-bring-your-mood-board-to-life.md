# Free design help: Bring your mood board to life
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- After a 20-plus-send July 4th marathon that ended with "HOURS LEFT" finales on July 6th, Pottery Barn is using a sub-brand wrapper — "Pottery Barn Design Services" — to launch a service funnel rather than another discount promo. That's a strategically sound reset. The email leads with "RSVP NOW / Free Design Services," flows through three editorial room vignettes (living room → bedroom → outdoors), and closes with a dated secondary hook: a free in-store "Find the Right Rug" design class on July 10. Structurally, it's clean and intentional.
- The problem is context and cadence. This arrives the same day as "Ready-to-ship furniture & more for a quick new look" (already reviewed, 5/10) — making it the third Pottery Barn hit in a single day. A sub-brand sender name doesn't reset the inbox counter. And the primary offer — "book a free appointment" — is a higher-commitment ask than clicking to shop, particularly for subscribers just conditioned by three weeks of discount urgency. The email doesn't explain what a design appointment involves, how long it takes, whether it's in-store or virtual, or why now.
- The visual execution is solid. Images load, no render bugs, CTA button is unambiguous. But "free" is doing all the persuasion work without enough supporting proof — and that's a thin ask after a sale that already wore out its welcome.

## What's working

- **Hero CTA is above the fold and clear** — "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" renders as a visible button with no preamble; no "view in browser" friction at entry.
- **Sub-brand sender sets correct expectations** — "Pottery Barn Design Services" signals this is a service touchpoint, not a blast promo, which calibrates inbox framing.
- **Room-by-room structure is coherent** — living room → bedroom → outdoors follows a natural home-tour logic that keeps the scroll moving.
- **July 10 rug class adds a credible date anchor** — the secondary "Find the Right Rug / Book Today" block is the only time-bounded hook in the email and it's specific.
- **No render bugs** — all images loaded, layout is intact, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Third Pottery Barn email today** — following "Ready-to-ship furniture" earlier, the sub-brand label doesn't disguise that this is the same `@e.potterybarn.com` domain hitting the inbox again.
- **No urgency on the main offer** — "Book a free appointment" floats without a deadline, slots-filling message, or any time signal; there's no reason to act today versus next week.
- **"Mood board" framing is aspirational but hollow** — the email never explains the service: in-store or virtual? How long? What do you walk away with? A vague promise to "bring your mood board to life" needs a mechanism.
- **Room vignette CTAs mislead on destination** — "BRING THIS LOOK TO LIFE →" reads like a shoppable room link but appears to route to service booking rather than the product collection, risking click disappointment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send two Pottery Barn emails on the same day** — delay this send to July 8th or fold the design services angle into the ready-to-ship email as a footer module; a third inbox hit the same day hurts open rates on both.
- 2. **Add scarcity to the appointment CTA** — "Limited spots available this week" or "Book before Sunday for next-week appointments" converts the open-ended offer into an action signal.
- 3. **Two-line "how it works" under the hero** — subscribers need to know: in-store only? Virtual option? 30 minutes? A short explainer under the hero button reduces the commitment anxiety that stops people from booking.
- 4. **Separate shoppable CTAs from booking CTAs** — "Shop the living room look" should point to the product collection; the booking button stays in the hero. Mixing both jobs into one mid-scroll CTA dilutes both conversion paths.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Book your free design session — spots filling this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `A Pottery Barn designer will style your home for free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tell us your style. We'll pull the whole room together.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Living room, bedroom, outdoors — one free appointment covers it all.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email contains a critical template assembly defect — raw PHP `header()` calls embedded inside a CSS `<style>` block — indicating a server-side code fragment escaped into the rendered output. Separately, bulk missing alt text, two HTTP image sources, and absent `List-Unsubscribe` headers round out the issues.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Mitigating factor:** This URL exists only inside an HTML comment block (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not reachable by live recipients. The QA probe still flags it because the URL is present in the source. It is dead code — confirm it can be deleted from the template to avoid confusion.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP code in `<style>` block**
The second `<style>` tag opens with raw PHP function calls:

```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are not valid CSS. They indicate a server-side template fragment that was not executed (or not stripped) before the HTML was assembled. Most email clients will silently discard invalid CSS declarations, but parsing behavior varies — some clients may surface this as visible rendered text or cause the style block to be dropped entirely (breaking the mobile media query that follows). This is a template generation defect and should be escalated to the build pipeline.

**[WARN] 26 images missing alt text**
All body content images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_07.jpg`), all footer images (`Email-Footer/slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), the credit card banner images, and all tracking pixels carry empty or absent `alt` attributes. With images-off rendering (common in Outlook, corporate environments), the email presents as a blank white strip. Functional CTAs are entirely image-based with no text fallback.

**[WARN] 2 images served over HTTP**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Mixed content (HTTP images in an otherwise HTTPS context) will be blocked by Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS, and other security-strict clients, producing broken image slots in the footer credit card banner.

**[INFO] Google Fonts `<link>` tag present**
`<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat&display=swap">` will be stripped by Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS), and most webmail clients. Montserrat will silently not load. The declared fallback chain in body text should be confirmed; the `<style>` blocks reference `verdana,arial` but body copy may not have an explicit fallback.
