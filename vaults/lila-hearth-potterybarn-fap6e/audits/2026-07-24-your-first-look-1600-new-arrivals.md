# Your first look: 1600+ NEW arrivals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's quarterly "mega-launch" move — the 1600+ number is deliberate noise designed to reset subscriber attention after a July that has been dominated by 70%-off sale blasts and focused category emails (storage, coffee tables, vases). The hero is exceptional: a moody, wood-paneled library-style living room with a tufted leather sofa that reads like a Williams-Sonoma Home ad, not a promotional email. It earns attention on aesthetics alone.
- The problem is that "1600+ new arrivals" is a volume claim masquerading as an offer. It promises everything and merchandises nothing. There's no price anchor, no hero product, no department callout — just a lifestyle image and a CTA that essentially says "go to the website." The personalized "Recommended Just for You" module below the fold is doing real work here, but it's buried. Anyone not patient enough to scroll past the moody hero never sees the thing most likely to convert them.
- Compared to the July 23 artisanal vases send (the brand's strongest recent email at 9/10), which won precisely because it was ruthlessly narrow, this email swings the other way — 1600 items is a number that paralyzes, not motivates. Strong creative, weak editorial discipline.

## What's working

- Hero image is visually arresting — the tufted leather sofa, warm library setting, and rich tones command scroll-stop attention.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module is a genuine personalization signal; this is the highest-intent area of the email.
- Layout is clean and renders without visible bugs — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- "Your first look" framing in the subject carries a mild exclusivity signal that distinguishes this from a generic promo blast.
- Subject line length (35 chars) is mobile-friendly and unlikely to truncate on any inbox client.

## What's weak

- "1600+" is a paralysis number — too many options, no editorial POV on what to actually look at first.
- Hero has no price anchor or starting-from callout — the lifestyle image is aspirational but gives no commercial hook.
- CTA copy ("SHOP ALL NEW DESIGNS") is broad and unspecific — "All designs" is the entire store, not a destination.
- Hero headline text is visually buried in the lifestyle image and nearly illegible — any ambient lighting on a phone screen makes it unreadable.
- Cadence tension: this is the sixth Pottery Barn email in four days (after vases, coffee tables, dining furniture, Chris Loves Julia, design services). "First look" exclusivity rings hollow at that frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and price it.** Pull the single strongest new arrival — a sofa, a chair, a statement piece — and print a "starting at $X" beneath the image. The July 23 vases email won because it committed to one category. This email needs the same editorial courage.
- 2. **Move "Recommended Just for You" above the fold.** The personalized module is buried below a second lifestyle image. Swap the order: personal grid first, editorial hero second. Your highest-intent content should be the first thing that loads on mobile.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA.** Replace "SHOP ALL NEW DESIGNS" with something tied to the hero moment: "Shop the New Collection" or "See What's New in [Category]."
- 4. **Add a department grid with entry prices.** "1600 arrivals" means nothing without a map — Furniture from $299, Lighting from $49, Bedding from $89. Give the subscriber a door to walk through.
- 5. **Dial back the send cadence signal.** Consider merging the "new arrivals" story with one other narrative (e.g., the CLJ collab) instead of launching a standalone email into an already fatigued July list.
- **Subject Alt A:** `1600 new pieces — see what just dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The new collection is here. Your picks are inside.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, bedding and more — all brand new this season.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We handpicked what to look at first.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "1600+ NEW arrivals" (2026-07-24)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) block user navigation and a cross-brand asset leak (West Elm spacer GIF) indicates an asset management error. Accessibility posture is poor with 40+ images missing alt text, and three mixed-content HTTP image loads will be silently blocked in modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — Broken Links (403)**

- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?...` — The Pottery Barn Key credit card apply-and-buy CTA returns HTTP 403. This is the primary non-member conversion link (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`). Any click lands on an error.
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — Returns 403 and is also HTTP (mixed content). This is a West Elm asset incorrectly referenced in a Pottery Barn send — likely a copy-paste error from a sibling brand template.

**Tracking domains skipped** — 24 click-redirect URLs through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed. These should be validated in a pre-send environment that resolves redirects end-to-end.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP image loads (will be blocked)**

Three `src` attributes use `http://` — all will be silently dropped in Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients:

| Filename | HTTP src |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |
| Open-tracker | `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` |

**Alt text** — 40 images have no `alt` attribute set. Critical omissions include all `slices_0*.jpg` content images and both promotional banner images (`F26_TOP_SummerSalePartC_NoUrgency_thintopbanner.jpg`, `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg`). When images are blocked, the email renders as a blank white rectangle with no text fallback.

**Embedded PHP headers in CSS block** — The `<style>` block near the end of `<head>` contains raw PHP `header()` calls:
```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives embedded in client-side CSS. They have no effect in email but signal a template assembly error — a PHP image-serving snippet was pasted into the wrong context. Harmless to render but indicates broken template tooling.

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` leaves the tab/preview label blank; some clients use this as a fallback label.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe / RFC 8058** — Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers were captured. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay not forwarding these headers, but the absence should be confirmed against raw SMTP headers before clearing. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5k/day senders); Pottery Barn at this volume almost certainly falls under that threshold.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay; actual pass/fail status unknown. Cannot confirm deliverability authentication from this data.

**CAN-SPAM** — Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism appear present in the footer (standard for WSI brands), but cannot be fully verified from the truncated source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The credit card CTA link (`clickcp.potterybarn.com`) is broken (403), so continuity for that funnel is fully broken.

For content CTAs routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, UTM parameter structure could not be verified since those links were skipped by the HTTP probe. A manual spot-check on one decoded click URL should confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are present and match the send.

The open-tracker (`svopn.potterybarn.com`) uses HTTP, meaning opens tracked via this pixel will be undercounted in clients that block mixed content — inflate opt-out rates or suppress send-time optimization signals.
