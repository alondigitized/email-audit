# Ends tomorrow: Extra 20% off + Online-only deals on select furniture
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is send number 20+ in Pottery Barn's July 4th campaign, arriving on July 5th — and notably, it's the **second Pottery Barn email today**, following "ONLY 48 HOURS LEFT TO SAVE ⏳" (reviewed earlier, 7/10). The key differentiation this time is the furniture-specific framing: "Select Furniture Now on Sale" in the hero, and "Online-only deals on select furniture" in the subject. That's the sharpest category targeting this campaign has done in weeks — furniture is high-consideration, high-ticket, and worth a dedicated send.
- The execution is clean. Multiple named collections (Barrett, and others) give the email some substance beyond a generic discount banner, and the "Recommended Just for You" section adds meaningful personalization signal. The Key Rewards loyalty block and the "Explore New Arrivals" section round out a full-funnel layout.
- The damage is self-inflicted: this is email two from Pottery Barn today, the hero still reads "4th of July Sale" on July 5th (the day after the holiday), and "Online-only deals" — the freshest hook in the subject — doesn't appear to get its own dedicated module in the body. The promise-to-body gap will create friction for anyone who clicked expecting a distinct online-only furniture page.

## What's working

- **Furniture-specific hero** — "Select Furniture Now on Sale" is the most category-targeted send in this entire campaign sequence; high-consideration shoppers finally have a reason to click beyond general clearance.
- **Named collection grid** — Barrett and other collections visible mid-email give shoppers something to anchor on, not just a generic "SHOP NOW" into the full catalog.
- **"Recommended Just for You" module** — personalization section appears mid-email and features specific furniture pieces, which adds relevance signal even late in the scroll.
- **"Explore New Arrivals" section** — non-promotional content gives the email a reason to exist beyond pure discount mechanics.
- **Clean, bug-free render** — high-quality lifestyle photography, no broken images, no text overlap, layout holds throughout.

## What's weak

- **Two Pottery Barn emails in one day** — following "48 Hours Left" with "Ends tomorrow" on the same calendar date is a cadence mistake; both emails dilute each other and accelerate unsubscribe risk.
- **"Online-only deals" doesn't land in the body** — the subject's freshest hook appears to have no dedicated section in the email; subscribers who click expecting a curated online-only furniture page will land confused.
- **"4th of July Sale" language on July 5th** — the holiday is over; the hero reads stale, which undermines the "Ends tomorrow" urgency.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is too generic** — after a furniture-specific subject line and hero, the CTA should say something like "Shop Furniture Deals" to maintain category momentum.
- **No visible deadline in the body hero** — "Ends tomorrow" lives only in the subject line; a subscriber who skims the email body gets no reminder of the countdown.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add "Ends [date] at midnight" to the hero banner** — the countdown urgency that drove the subject line needs to appear above the fold in the body. Right now a subscriber who opens but doesn't remember the subject line sees "4th of July Sale" with no deadline visible.
- 2. **Build a dedicated "Online-Only Furniture Deals" module** — the subject makes a specific promise; the body needs to keep it. A named section with 3–4 online-exclusive pieces would close the gap and give people a reason to click vs. waiting for the store.
- 3. **Update "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Furniture Deals"** — specific CTAs outperform generic ones, especially for a high-consideration category.
- 4. **Retire the "4th of July" language** — replace with "End-of-Summer Sale" or "Limited-Time Furniture Event" to stop the send from feeling like yesterday's leftovers.
- 5. **Drop to one email per day for the campaign's final 48 hours** — the second daily send is burning goodwill; consolidate the urgency into a single, better-crafted send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: Online-only furniture deals + Extra 20% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your furniture shortlist — 20% off, online only, ends tomorrow`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Select pieces you can't get in-store. Sale ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Barrett Collection, new arrivals, and more — final hours on 20% off.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two links return 403 errors (one a critical CTA), PHP `header()` calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` block indicating a template rendering failure, three image assets are served over HTTP (not HTTPS), and a bulk of content images reference a stale October 2025 campaign path unrelated to this send.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[BROKEN — Critical]** Key Rewards "Apply & Buy" banner CTA returns HTTP 403:
```
https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682
  ?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
Any subscriber clicking the credit card banner hits a dead end.

**[BROKEN]** Cross-brand spacer asset returns 403 over HTTP:
```
http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
West Elm asset loaded in a Pottery Barn send; also an HTTP URL (see §3).

**[INFO]** 25 click-tracking links through `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed — UTM integrity unverifiable from QA data alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[Critical — Template Rendering Failure]** PHP `header()` calls appear as raw text inside a `<style>` block, not processed server-side:
```css
/* inside <style> tag */
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are invalid CSS and will be ignored by mail clients, but their presence confirms the template was not fully rendered before send. This is a build pipeline defect.

**[High]** Three image assets served over HTTP — likely blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients when images are loaded in a secure context:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open pixel — also a PII risk; see §5)

**[High — Stale Asset Paths]** The body content images (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_07.jpg`) are pulled from an October 2025 campaign directory:
```
https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2025/US/1025_SAT_AM_OctClearanceB_DTCOnlyFurnitureSale/
```
Subject line promotes a July 4th furniture sale; these images are from "Oct Clearance B DTC Only Furniture Sale." Either wrong images are rendering, or the CDN is serving recycled content behind a mismatched URL.

**[Medium]** 38 product and layout images are missing `alt` text, including the primary hero banner (`S26_Top_July4Sale_EndsTomorrow.jpg`) and all footer slice images. Images-off rendering will show a blank email body.

**[Low]** `<title>` element is empty — not a rendering issue but degrades preview in some clients that surface it.

### 5. Compliance

**[Warn]** `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers not detected via the AgentMail relay. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules >5k/day) will not function. Needs verification against raw message headers.

**[Warn]** `Authentication-Results` header not captured — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status cannot be confirmed from this audit. Should be verified at the MTA level.

**[High — PII Over HTTP]** The open-tracking pixel transmits the subscriber email address in plaintext over HTTP:
```
http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597
  &sv_emopen=true
  &sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app
```
Email address is sent unencrypted. HTTPS should be used for all tracking beacons containing subscriber identifiers.

The `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` tag is present and will suppress the `Referer` header on all outbound clicks — this is intentional for privacy but means server-side referrer-based attribution on potterybarn.com will be blind to email as a source channel.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters could not be verified directly because all 25 CTAs use opaque encoded redirect URLs through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. The encoding format (base64-encoded query string payload) is consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracking — UTMs would be embedded in the decoded destination URL.

The `no-referrer` meta tag (§5) means any UTM-less links will also have no referrer fallback — sessions arriving without UTMs will appear as direct traffic in analytics rather than as email.

The stale October 2025 image paths (§3) raise a question about whether the corresponding landing page URLs inside the encoded CTAs also point to an October 2025 sale rather than the July 4th event. This cannot be confirmed without decoding the click URLs.
