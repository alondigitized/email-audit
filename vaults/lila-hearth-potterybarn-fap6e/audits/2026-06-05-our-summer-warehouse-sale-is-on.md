# Our Summer Warehouse Sale is ON
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- After six straight editorial and lifestyle emails — leather seating, Chris Loves Julia collab, rug features, dorm launch — Pottery Barn finally drops a hard promotional email, and the timing is right. "Summer Warehouse Sale, Up to 60% off" lands cleanly in the hero: big type, clear discount, visible CTA. This is the email that was missing from the last three days of content sends.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. The email pivots from a sale into a loyalty pitch, an app download push, an Affirm plug, and a social follow ask — all stacked in the same send. Each module is individually defensible, but together they fragment the message and reduce the chance anyone clicks anything. Warehouse sale emails succeed with single-minded urgency; this one hedges.
- Net judgement: strong premise, diluted execution. A real subscriber who has been waiting for an actual discount (not another collab story) would open this — but might not click through because nothing specific is calling their name out of the sale.

## What's working

- **Hero is immaculate.** "Summer Warehouse Sale / Up to 60% off" with a dark overlay on a lifestyle image is visually clean and hierarchy is obvious — eyes land there first.
- **Category navigation is useful.** The FURNITURE / OUTDOOR / SOFAS & CHAIRS / BEDDING row lets a subscriber immediately jump to their category, which is rare in warehouse sale emails.
- **"Recommended Just for You" is a smart personalization layer.** A curated product grid below the hero acknowledges that "60% off the whole store" is too broad; the picks create a reason to scroll.
- **Loyalty reward call-out ("Earn 10% Back in Rewards") is appropriately placed** — not in the hero where it would muddy the sale message, but visible for members who care.
- **Sender name "Pottery Barn Sale"** is a nice inbox-level signal that this is a deal email, not editorial content.

## What's weak

- **No deadline anywhere visible.** "Warehouse Sale is ON" implies it will also go OFF, but there is no end date, no "ends Sunday," no "3 days only" — which kills urgency and gives subscribers permission to defer.
- **Module overload below the fold.** Design services booking, app download, rewards card, Affirm financing, social follow — five distinct asks after the sale hero. Each one is a conversion leak.
- **"Recommended Just for You" products are too small to read.** The grid thumbnails are thumbnail-sized; no price, no product name, no "% off" badge is legible in the render. The personalization signal is there but the product detail that would actually drive a click is invisible.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the primary CTA copy — twice.** Both the hero CTA and the recommendations section use the same generic label. Specificity ("Shop Summer Sale" / "See Your Picks") would reduce friction.
- **Dense fine print block at the bottom** is visually heavy and the first thing to signal "conditions apply" to a skeptical subscriber.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a sale end date to the hero — this is the single biggest lift.** "Up to 60% off — Ends Sunday" or "3 days only" turns a passive browse into an urgent action. Every day without a deadline is a day subscribers can wait.
- 2. **Cut two of the five below-fold modules.** Drop the app download and the Affirm block from this send. Warehouse sale emails should have one message. Save Affirm for a high-ticket product email where financing is a real objection-handler.
- 3. **Add price badges to the recommended products.** "Was $399 / Now $199" on even two or three thumbnails would do more for click-through than any copy change.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP NOW" hero CTA with "Shop the Sale"** and recommendations CTA with "See All Picks" — tiny delta, meaningfully more specific.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to include the discount** — burying "60% off" inside the body instead of the subject line is a missed inbox open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Warehouse Sale — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, bedding and more — sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picks are on sale — shop before it's gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] PHP header() calls embedded in `<style>` block**

The following raw server-side code appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag near the end of `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP function calls, not CSS. They indicate a template rendering failure — a dynamic image-caching snippet was not executed server-side and its output was written into the HTML as literal text instead. Email clients parse this as invalid CSS and ignore it, but it confirms the template did not compile cleanly.

**[FAIL] 3 HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources** — will be blocked by secure-context email clients:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true…` (open-tracking pixel)

**[WARN] 42 images missing `alt` text** — the entire slices layout (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_12.jpg` in two modules), all product images, the footer slices set (`slices_01`–`slices_18`), and all tracking pixels. Subscribers with images disabled see a blank email with no text fallback.

**[WARN] `<title>` tag is empty** — some clients surface this as the window/tab title in preview panes.

**[WARN] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-zoom on mobile, a WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**[WARN] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` in `<head>`** — suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all link clicks originating from the email. Downstream analytics will not receive email as the referrer source, undermining attribution reporting outside the ESP's own click tracking.

The absence may be an AgentMail relay capture gap rather than a true omission, but it cannot be confirmed from available data.
