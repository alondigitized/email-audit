# The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's fourth or fifth "UP TO 70% OFF" subject line in roughly four days — following the June 21 send ("Enjoy UP TO 70% OFF + Nightstands…"), the June 22 "☀️ Sunny savings UP TO 70% OFF," and the June 23 "Big news! Up to 70% off…" The brand briefly found its footing on June 23 with the Design Services pivot (9/10) and the arrivals catalog send (6/10), but this June 24 send retreats straight back to the worn-out discount hammer. There is no new hook, no category focus, no specific product. The email body is structurally solid — the "Recommended Just For You" module is a genuine personalization asset — but it's buried under a hero that says nothing the last several sends haven't already said.
- The net effect is subscriber fatigue compounding itself. By the time this hits an inbox already saturated with Pottery Barn sale messages, the subject line reads as background noise. A subscriber has been trained all week that opening any of these leads to the same 70%-off grid, so the marginal value of opening this one is near zero. The Affirm and rewards sections add real value but arrive too late in the scroll for most readers.
- Competent execution, exhausted creative. The brand is burning list equity by repeating the same offer without any new editorial angle.

## What's working

- **"Recommended Just For You" module** — a genuine personalization signal that shows algorithmically selected products, giving the email something to offer beyond the category nav.
- **Hero legibility** — the "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off / SHOP ALL SALE" hierarchy lands cleanly; a first-time recipient would immediately know what this is and what to do.
- **Category navigation grid** — FURNITURE, OUTDOOR, SOFAS & CHAIRS, BEDDING, RUGS, BATH etc. lets deep-intent shoppers route themselves instantly without hunting.
- **Loyalty + Affirm combo** — the 10% Back in Rewards and Buy Now Pay Over Time blocks address both aspirational and budget-sensitive shoppers in one scroll.

## What's weak

- **Subject is a near-verbatim repeat** of at least three prior sends this week — subscribers have already been conditioned to skip it.
- **"SHOP ALL SALE" CTA is the least useful button possible** — it sends everyone to the same firehose; a June sale this deep should be linking to a curated sale-within-the-sale (clearance vs. markdown vs. new drops).
- **Hero adds no new angle** — "100s of new items added!" appears in the sub-copy but isn't surfaced in the subject line, which is the one place it would actually differentiate this send.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism visible** — the sale has been "on" all week; there's nothing here to signal that this window is closing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the "new items added" angle in the subject** — that's the only genuinely new fact this email has. "100s of new items just dropped — up to 70% off" is a meaningfully different send than anything from the past week.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP ALL SALE" with a category-specific CTA** based on the user's browse/purchase history — even a simple "Shop Your Saved Items" or "Shop Outdoor Sale" makes the click feel earned.
- 3. **Add a countdown or end-date** to the hero — even "Sale ends Sunday" arrests fatigue-scroll.
- 4. **Elevate the "Recommended" module above the category nav** — it's the strongest asset in this email and it's buried below the fold.
- **Subject Alt A:** `100s of new items just added — up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drops in the Summer Sale — 70% off starts here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh finds in furniture, bedding, outdoor, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picks from last week are now on sale.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken links will cause 403 errors for recipients clicking a credit card banner and loading a cross-brand spacer image. Forty-four images across the email carry no alt text, and three image assets are served over plain HTTP — all of which will be blocked by modern email clients enforcing HTTPS.

The loyalty/credit card CTA returns HTTP 403. Any recipient clicking the "Apply & Buy" or non-member Key Rewards banner hits a dead end. This is the highest-severity broken experience in the send.

**[FAIL] West Elm spacer image — 403**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image assets (3 instances) — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail:**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**Missing alt text — 44 images**, including all content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` in both body and footer sections), all product images (bed, curtains, towels, sheets, quilt), and tracking pixels. Recipients with images disabled see a blank email. Screen readers get no information.

**CSS in `<style>` block contains invalid PHP header() calls:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are server-side PHP directives copied verbatim into a `<style>` block. They are invalid CSS, ignored by all clients, but indicate a template error that could corrupt rendering in strict parsers.

**`<title>` element is empty** — assistive technologies and some email clients use this for tab/window labeling.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — suppresses pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility regression.
