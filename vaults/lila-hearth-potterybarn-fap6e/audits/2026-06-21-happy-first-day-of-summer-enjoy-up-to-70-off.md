# Happy first day of SUMMER! Enjoy UP TO 70% OFF
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's Summer Sale launch send — timed to the first day of summer on June 21. The email leads with a clean, no-frills hero: "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" in large type, a single "SHOP ALL SALE" CTA, and a horizontal nav bar listing sale categories. That's textbook — fast, readable, unambiguous. The personalized "Recommended Just for You" product grid below is a legitimate lift; it shows actual SKUs (beds, pillows, rugs) with images and names.
- What undercuts it is that this is now the third "Up to 70% off" email in three days from Pottery Barn. After the June 19 "It's ON! The Summer Sale is UP TO 70% OFF" (7/10) and the June 20 double-send, the subject line is pure repeat. "Happy first day of SUMMER!" as a peg is a thin creative excuse to resend the same offer.
- The bottom third — design services cross-sell, app download pitch, Pottery Barn card reward teaser, Affirm buy-now-pay-later — is cluttered and competes for attention. The email is doing too many jobs at once. Good hero, punchy product grid, forgettable close.

## What's working

- **Hero is fast and clear** — "Summer Sale / Up to 70% off" with "SHOP ALL SALE" button is readable in under two seconds.
- **Category nav bar** — Furniture, Outdoor, Sofas & Chairs, Bedding, Decor, Pillows & Throws, Lighting, Rugs, Tabletop, Bath — gives the reader a direct route to their category without hunting.
- **"Recommended Just for You" grid** — actual product images with names (Farmhouse Platform Bed, Standard Cotton Bed, Hyde Round Honey Table, Hydra/Organic Queen Duvet) signal real personalization.
- **Design Services cross-sell** ("Free Design Services — Any project big or small") is a legitimate value-add nested mid-email rather than interrupting the sale pitch.

## What's weak

- **Subject is a retread** — "Up to 70% OFF" has appeared verbatim in at least three consecutive sends; the "Happy first day of SUMMER!" wrapper doesn't change the perceived offer.
- **Bottom third is cluttered** — App download, Pottery Barn card, Affirm, and social icons all stacked in quick succession dilutes the sale focus.
- **"Recommended Just for You" grid is mostly bedroom/bedding** — the category nav teases Outdoor, Rugs, Lighting, etc., but the personalized picks are narrow and don't mirror that breadth.
- **No visible urgency** — "Up to 70% off" with no end date or "ends Sunday" type signal; nothing makes this email feel time-bounded versus the identical ones sent three days prior.
- **Design Services section breaks the sale momentum** — placed awkwardly between the product grid and the app/card stack; it reads as a stray module dropped in without editorial purpose here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" in the hero would immediately differentiate this from the prior sends and push toward click. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to earn the seasonal peg** — the summer solstice is a specific moment; the current subject squanders it with a generic discount claim.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer officially starts — and so do savings up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `First day of summer. 70% off through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop outdoor, rugs, and bedding — all on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your picks are waiting. Sale ends soon.`
- 3. **Expand the "Recommended" grid horizontally** — show one outdoor piece and one rug/lighting pick alongside the bedding SKUs to match the category nav promise.
- 4. **Consolidate the bottom modules** — App + Card + Affirm is three separate asks in six inches of email. Pick one or merge into a single utility row.
- 5. **Drop or reposition Design Services** — either lead with it before the product grid (as a service benefit) or move it below the footer. Sandwiched between products and utilities it reads as an afterthought.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content / HTTP image sources (3 instances)** — Will be blocked by default in Gmail, Outlook.com, and most modern clients:

| Image | HTTP Source |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

The `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `slices_02.jpg` are served from `edm.potterybarn.com` itself — the HTTPS versions of these paths very likely exist; this is a hardcoded `http://` reference that should be a simple find-and-replace fix.

**Alt text missing on 43 images** — Nearly every content image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`, all product images, footer slices) has no `alt` attribute. With images blocked (the default in many clients on first open), the email renders as a wall of blank boxes with no text fallback.

**Stray PHP header() calls inside a `<style>` block** — The following appears verbatim inside a `<style>` tag in `<head>`:

```
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP server-side directives accidentally left in the CSS. They are inert in HTML email but indicate a template assembly error — a dynamic image-serving script's PHP headers were pasted into the wrong context.
