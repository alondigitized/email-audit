# NEW finishes for pieces you love
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This email makes the right call editorially — breaking from the pure-discount drumbeat that has defined Pottery Barn's June batch and centering on a product story: new finishes for the Sausalito Collection. The summer sale banner ($up to 60% off) is kept in the header where it provides discount context without overriding the new-finish narrative. The result is a rare Pottery Barn send that offers a genuine reason to open beyond "sale ends soon."
- The execution is mostly solid. Lifestyle imagery is rich, the finish names (White Resin, Almond White) appear as labeled product callouts, and the layout stays clean throughout a long scroll. The weak spots are the footer section ("Recommended just for you") which reads as an afterthought, and the subject line itself — "NEW finishes for pieces you love" leans on the vague "pieces you love" tail rather than anchoring to the Sausalito name, which would have been more specific and more searchable.
- Compared to the June 17 "Quilts with character in every stitch, from $169" send (8/10) — which is the gold standard in this batch for editorial specificity — this one is slightly softer. But it's still the right instinct, and it will stand out in a subscriber's inbox after weeks of indistinguishable sale blasts.

## What's working

- **Summer sale banner used as context, not the headline** — the "up to 60% off" sits at the top without crowding out the Sausalito story, keeping both signals alive.
- **Finish-level product detail visible in the scroll** — "White Resin" and "Almond White" labeled callouts give shoppers something specific to evaluate, not just a generic "shop now" push.
- **Lifestyle imagery is strong and on-brand** — airy bedroom and dining room scenes render cleanly, no broken images, and match Pottery Barn's elevated neutral aesthetic.
- **"Shop New Finishes" CTA is repeated per section** — per-product CTAs reduce the cognitive load of navigating a long scroll and let someone jump directly to the finish they want.
- **"Recommended just for you" section below the fold** — adds a personalization signal (however shallow) that most competitors in this batch skip.

## What's weak

- **"Pieces you love" is dead weight** — the subject line would be sharper as "New Sausalito finishes" or similar; the Sausalito name has equity and is never used in the subject.
- **No deadline visible** — the summer sale has been running since at least June 12; without a visible end date, there's no urgency to act now vs. tomorrow.
- **The email is very long** — by the time you reach the "Recommended just for you" cards, visual fatigue has set in; those products are unlikely to get meaningful clicks.
- **No social proof or reviews** — for a furniture finish story (a high-consideration choice), one line of review count or a "bestseller" label would meaningfully lift conversion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the collection name in the subject.** "Sausalito" has brand recognition and specificity that "pieces you love" cannot match. This is the single change with the highest lift.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Sausalito finishes — White Resin, Almond + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Sausalito Collection just got new finishes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 60% off during the Summer Sale — now in your favorite silhouettes`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh finishes on the pieces our customers keep coming back to`
- 2. **Add a sale end date.** Even "Sale ends Sunday" injected beneath the banner turns a passive browse into a timed decision.
- 3. **Cut or collapse the "Recommended just for you" section.** It dilutes the Sausalito story and competes for the final CTA slot. If it must stay, move it above the lifestyle imagery footer so it gets seen.
- 4. **Add a review count badge to at least one hero product.** Something like "4.8 ★ · 1,240 reviews" on the most popular Sausalito piece would significantly boost click confidence on a furniture page.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail):**

| Image | src |
|---|---|
| `spacer_0.gif` | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |
| `slices_02.jpg` | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` |

All three are plain `http://` — major clients enforce HTTPS-only image loading; these will render as broken images.

**Missing alt text — 40+ images:** Every content image in the Sausalito product grid (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_15.jpg`), all footer slices, both bottom banner images, all product recommendation images, and both tracking pixels have empty or absent `alt` attributes. With images off (the default in many corporate email clients), the email renders as a blank page with no text fallback. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1 non-conformant.

**PHP `header()` calls in `<style>` block:**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache...");
```
Server-side PHP code was pasted raw into a CSS `<style>` block. This is a template authoring error — it is inert in the rendered email but signals a broken build/QA process where template fragments are assembled without validation.

**`<title>` is empty:** `<title></title>` — causes poor rendering in some preview panes and screen readers.
