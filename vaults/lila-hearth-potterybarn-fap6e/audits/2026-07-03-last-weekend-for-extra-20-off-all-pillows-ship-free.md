# Last weekend for EXTRA 20% OFF + All pillows ship free!
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is send number sixteen-plus in Pottery Barn's 4th of July campaign, arriving on the same day as "Final weekend 🔔 Extra 20% off select styles" — which means subscribers got two Pottery Barn emails on July 3rd, the second of which undercuts the urgency framing of the first. The key structural addition here is the pillow free-shipping hook, which is genuinely the freshest creative element in the entire campaign sequence: a specific category benefit layered on top of the Extra 20% Off scaffold the brand has run uninterrupted for two-plus weeks.
- The execution is clean. Visual hierarchy works — offer in the hero, focused "SHOP PILLOWS" CTA, product grid below, a "Recommended Just For You" personalization section, and a loyalty/rewards callout in the footer. That's a well-structured email. The problem is entirely contextual: "Last weekend" the day after "Final weekend" is a self-contradiction that any engaged subscriber immediately notices, and at sixteen-plus sends, the urgency language has burned to ash.
- Verdict: The pillow free-shipping angle is the strongest single idea in this entire July 4th sequence — and it was wasted as a secondary clause in the subject line of the second daily send, rather than the headline of a standalone send with room to breathe.

## What's working

- Free pillow shipping is the first genuine category-specific benefit in the campaign sequence — it gives subscribers a concrete new reason to click.
- "SHOP PILLOWS" is the most focused CTA in the entire July 4th run; far better than the generic "Shop Sale" or "Shop Now" of prior sends.
- "Recommended Just For You" personalization section adds subscriber-specific product context and is placed well within the scroll.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero offer → product grid with free-shipping items → personalized picks → loyalty footer.
- Extra 5% in Rewards is visible in the footer, reminding members their benefit stacks with the sale.

## What's weak

- "Last weekend" the day after "Final weekend" is a credibility-destroying self-contradiction — a subscriber who opened yesterday's send immediately clocks this.
- Two sends on the same day from the same brand compound fatigue; this send had no room to establish any urgency before the inbox was already occupied.
- Subject at 55 characters runs past the ~50-char mobile-friendly threshold, and the free-shipping hook — the most differentiated element — is buried after the offer that's been in every subject line for two weeks.
- No social proof visible anywhere in the render: no star ratings, no review counts, no "bestseller" or "back in stock" signals on hero products.
- "EXTRA" in all caps is a mild spam signal that, compounded across 16+ sends with the same treatment, accumulates inbox-filter pressure.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the pillow free-shipping benefit in the subject.** It's the only genuinely new offer element in this send — bury it and you've sent another generic "20% off" email. Make it the hook.
- 2. **Eliminate the second daily send or consolidate.** Two Pottery Barn emails on July 3rd is the fastest path to bulk-unsubscribes in an already-fatigued list. One send with both messages would have been stronger.
- 3. **Replace vague urgency with a real timestamp.** "Ends Sunday 11:59 PM" is both more credible and more actionable than "Last weekend" — especially after yesterday's "Final weekend."
- 4. **Add star ratings or review counts under at least the hero pillow product.** A single "4.8 ★ · 1,240 reviews" callout on the featured item materially increases click confidence at no design cost.
- **Subject Alt A:** `All pillows ship free this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pillows ship free + extra 20% off — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your faves in bedding and décor finally ship free. Extra 20% off too — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on every pillow. Plus extra 20% off select styles through the weekend.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (403) will produce dead clicks for affected recipients, and a template rendering bug leaked raw PHP `header()` calls into a `<style>` block. Accessibility is severely degraded: 38 images carry no alt text and 4 image assets load over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken links (HTTP 403):**

| Asset | URL (truncated) |
|---|---|
| KeyCC credit card banner CTA | `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-...&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` |
| West Elm cross-brand spacer GIF | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |

The first is a member acquisition CTA (Apply & Buy). A 403 means the click-redirect record is expired or misconfigured — users who click will land on an error page. The second is a cross-brand asset (West Elm CDN) embedded in a Pottery Barn send; it is both broken and a brand hygiene issue.

**25 tracking links** on `click.e.potterybarn.com` were not probed — standard redirect domain, but UTM pass-through cannot be confirmed without resolution (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers leaked into CSS (template rendering bug):**
```css
/* Inside <style> tag: */
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are PHP `header()` calls that were not executed server-side and passed through raw into the HTML. They are invalid CSS; strict parsers may discard the surrounding rule block.

**38 images missing `alt` text** — essentially every content image in the email, including:
- Hero banner: `S26_Top_July4Sale_FinalWeekend.jpg`
- All pillow product slices: `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_11.jpg` (W26_FreeShip_B1_Pillows path)
- All footer slices: `slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg` (Email-Footer path)
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `oakleigh-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.
- Tracking pixels and open-tracking images

Renders as a blank email for screen-reader users and when images are disabled by default (Outlook, Gmail image-off mode).

**4 images served over HTTP (will be blocked by most email clients):**

| Image | HTTP src |
|---|---|
| West Elm spacer | `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` |
| Key CC footer banner | `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` |

The HTTP open-tracking pixel will suppress open-rate data in clients that block mixed-content (Apple Mail, most modern clients).

**Additional rendering concerns:**
- `<title>` tag is empty — affects tab/preview labeling in webmail.
- `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta suppresses user zoom on mobile (WCAG 1.4.4 failure).
- `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` applied globally overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling.
