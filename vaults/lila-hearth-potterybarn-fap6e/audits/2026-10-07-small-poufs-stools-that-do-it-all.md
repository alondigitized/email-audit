# Small poufs & stools that do it all
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-category product-discovery email: "Small Seats That Do It All" over a living-room shot with a cube pouf. It's on-topic and easy to scan, but it has no offer, no deadline and no price. It reads as a catalog page. The 3x3 grid of poufs and stools is attractive, though none of the products is named or priced.
- Below the pouf grid, the email goes generic. "More Seating," "Recommended Just For You" (curtain rod, dining tables, ghost lights, a bed, a swivel chair), then Christmas Shop, Free Design Services, the app, the credit card and Affirm. That is about seven competing messages after the hero. The ghost lights and Christmas tree sit next to each other, so the seasonal story is muddled. Compared with the 10-06 "RSVP: 100% free design services" send (7/10), which had a single clear reason to act, this one has no urgency. It's about the same as the 10-05 entryway furniture email (6/10).
- Verdict: well made but low-stakes. It works for someone already shopping for a seat, and it gives everyone else little reason to click today.

## What's working

- The hero headline "Small Seats That Do It All" is benefit-led and plain, with a visible "SHOP NOW" button.
- The pouf and stool grid shows a good range of textures and shapes: boucle, leather, woven, tufted.
- The "SHOP ALL POUFS & STOOLS" button gives a second clear category CTA.
- The body copy ("a coffee table, a footrest, an extra seat for guests") explains the use cases well.
- The top nav pills ("Discover 1400+ New Arrivals," "Shop In-Stock Furniture") are useful shortcuts.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, or deadline anywhere in the hero or grid.
- The nine product tiles have no names, prices or "from $" labels, so you can't tell what any of them costs.
- Everything after the grid dilutes the message: Recommended Just For You, Christmas Shop, Design Services, app, card and Affirm all compete for the click.
- The recommended block mixes unrelated items (curtain rod, ghost lights, dining tables), which undercuts the "small seats" theme.
- The email is very long, and the legal and rewards fine print at the bottom is dense.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete hook to the hero, such as a "from $X" price or a limited-time offer, and put it in the subject.
- 2. Label each grid tile with a product name and price, and make each one link to its own product page.
- 3. Cut the footer promos down to one. Keep either Design Services or the Christmas Shop, not both.
- 4. Make the recommendations relevant: poufs, ottomans and accent chairs, not curtain hardware.
- 5. Add a social-proof line, such as a star rating or "best seller" tags on two or three poufs.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based, sliced-image layout. Its click links go through an opaque ESP redirector (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=…`). The main problems are widespread missing alt text (about 60 images), three `http://` image sources including an open-tracking pixel, the recipient's email address in image URLs, and PHP code leaked into a `<style>` block. The missing `List-Unsubscribe` and `Authentication-Results` headers are most likely relay artifacts, so they aren't confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Links not verifiable:** 61 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe (click-redirect domains), so destinations and status codes are unconfirmed. The visible links use the opaque `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=ABp7Im…` format, so destination alignment can't be checked from source.
- **Mixed-content tracking pixel:** `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=…` loads over plain HTTP. Clients that block or upgrade non-HTTPS images will drop this open pixel, which undercounts opens.
- **Other tracking pixels:** `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/…/cat/1` (third-party ad-network pixel) and `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/07e738dd-…?mi_cid=…` also load as images. Several tracking systems fire in one send (SFMC, svopn, Turn, clickcp).
- **Stale and cross-brand assets:** `edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` is a West Elm host serving a Pottery Barn email. `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` is a 2022 path. Both are fragile if those hosts or paths are retired.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, about 60 images:** this includes every slice in `F26_Furn_B11_PoufsStoolsOttomans/slices_01–18.jpg` (the hero and main content), `F26_Top_NewArrivals+InStock/slices_01–02.jpg`, `Email-Footer/slices_01–18.jpg`, the six product images under `/pbimgs/ab/images/dp/wcm/…` (e.g. `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`), `H26_Bottom_ChristmasShop.jpg` and `DesignCrewPrimaryBottomBanner.jpg`. With images off, the content is blank and screen readers get only filenames. The logo `nav0.jpg` does have `alt="POTTERY BARN"`.
- **Non-HTTPS images (3):** `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/…/slices_02.jpg` and the svopn pixel. These may be blocked or show mixed-content warnings.
- **Empty title:** `<title></title>`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns=…>` has no `lang`.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Leaked PHP in CSS:** the last `<style>` block ends with `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: no-store…"); header("Pragma: no-cache");`. This is server code in the stylesheet. It is harmless today because no rules follow it, but it would invalidate any CSS added after it.
- **Layout markup:** `role="presentation"` on the layout tables is good. The preheader is hidden correctly (`display:none; mso-hide:all`).

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens** are visible in the truncated source.
- **Recipient email in URLs:** `https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` (an image src) and `…svopn…&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` expose the address in plaintext query strings. The svopn one is over HTTP, so the address goes out unencrypted, and both can end up in proxy and server logs.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both not found. The QA note says AgentMail may not relay these headers, so this is **unconfirmed** and needs a check against the raw message.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, with the same relay caveat, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown.
- **Plain-text part:** 11,744 of 16,009 characters (73%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. That is a mild spam-filter risk.
- **Body footer:** the unsubscribe link and physical address are in the truncated section, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **UTM parameters:** no UTMs are visible, because links are wrapped in encoded `qs=` tokens, so continuity can't be verified from source. Any attribution parameters are applied server-side by the redirect.
- **Landing pages:** none were probed (all 61 links skipped).
- **Hotlinked product images:** the six product images point at `www.potterybarn.com/pbimgs/…` (the production site CDN), not the `edm.` email CDN. They will break if the site images are rotated.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive alt text to every content slice and product image. Use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Move the three `http://` assets to HTTPS. Fix the svopn pixel first, since it affects open metrics.
3. Remove the email address from image query strings, or hash or token-ize it.
4. Delete the PHP `header(...)` lines from the `<style>` block.
5. Add a `<title>` and `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and drop `maximum-scale=1`.
6. Check the raw message for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` before treating them as failures.
7. Resolve a sample of the redirect links manually to confirm the destinations and UTMs.
8. Check the plain-text alternative: shorten it, or drop the repeated long tracking URLs.
