# Handcrafted rugs starting at $299
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is one of the better sends in Pottery Barn's Summer Sale blitz — and the reason is the subject line. "Handcrafted rugs starting at $299" breaks from the "UP TO 70% OFF" drum that has beaten every other subject for a week straight. It anchors price, names a category, and communicates craftsmanship in four words. That's a real editorial choice.
- The body earns it. The email is category-pure — rugs only — with a clean visual hierarchy: hero lifestyle shot, then two named products (Dash Jute Rug, Gallego), then an editorial Rug Guide tile, then personalized recommendations. This is the template the whole blitz should have been using: one category, multiple entry points, a guide for shoppers who aren't yet in buy-mode.
- The main weakness is cadence. This is the ninth or tenth touch from Pottery Barn in roughly a week. Even a well-executed email has to fight through subscriber fatigue at that volume. The June 22 rug send ("🍦Treat yourself to up to 70% off + Rugs for a colorful foundation") already covered this category; a reader who clicked that and didn't buy may not come back for a second rug pitch six days later.

## What's working

- **Subject is the sharpest in the blitz** — price anchor + category + implied quality ("Handcrafted") is a formula; this one executes it cleanly.
- **Category focus** — the entire email is rugs, top to bottom. No furniture filler, no mattress cross-sell. Easier to act on.
- **Named products with distinct character** — Dash Jute Rug and Gallego are called out with their own tiles; "Bestselling" label on both provides quick social proof.
- **The Rug Guide tile** is a smart consideration-stage insertion — buyers who aren't ready to purchase have somewhere to go that keeps them in the funnel.
- **"Recommended Just For You" row** — personalization section appears in body; signals the brand is doing behavioral targeting, not just blasting the full list.

## What's weak

- **No explicit deadline** — "Summer Sale" implies a deadline that isn't visible in the email. With no "ends Sunday" or countdown, urgency evaporates.
- **Rug category already covered June 22** — the "🍦Treat yourself to up to 70% off + Rugs for a colorful foundation" send from two days prior means the segment-overlap problem is real; the email is good but the timing is tired.
- **CTA copy is likely generic** — the buttons appear to say "Shop Now" or similar; given how specific the products are, "Shop Dash Jute Rug" or "Find My Size" would convert better.
- **No price visible on product tiles** — the subject anchors at $299 but the body tiles don't appear to carry a price; the reader has to click to confirm the entry price applies to the product they want.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the hero** — "Sale ends July 4" (or whatever the real date is) would transform this from a browse email to a conversion email. The Summer Sale hook without a deadline is inert.
- 2. **Put the $299 anchor in the hero**, not just the subject. Readers who scroll past the hero or come in from a forward miss the pricing context entirely.
- 3. **Sharpen CTA copy to match the product** — "Shop Dash Jute Rug" instead of "Shop Now" is four characters longer and three times more likely to get clicked by someone who just saw the product name.
- 4. **Hold this email 48 hours** — the June 22 rug send needed more breathing room. Sending a second rug-focused email two days later with almost no creative variation is a list-burn risk.
- 5. **Surface a price in the product tiles** — even "From $299" under the Gallego tile keeps the value signal alive past the subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your rug, starting at $299 — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Dash Jute Rug is in the Summer Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Handcrafted styles marked down — find your fit in the Rug Guide.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 70% off rugs built to last. Sale ends July 4.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "Handcrafted Rugs" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken links (HTTP 403) will cause click failures for the credit card banner and a cross-brand asset reference. The email also has pervasive missing alt text across ~40 images and three HTTP-sourced assets that will be blocked by modern mail clients enforcing HTTPS.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken credit card apply link (403)**
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&...`
The Key Rewards / credit card CTA returns a 403. Any subscriber clicking the "Apply & Buy" banner gets a dead end. Likely a misconfigured or expired campaign token on the `clickcp.potterybarn.com` redirect handler.

**FAIL — Broken cross-brand spacer asset (403)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
A West Elm spacer GIF is referenced in a Pottery Barn email and returns 403. This is a dead cross-brand CDN reference — likely a stale copy-paste from a shared template. Additionally served over HTTP (see §3).

**25 tracking links skipped** — not probed; no action required, but confirm click-redirect domain `click.e.potterybarn.com` resolves correctly in a pre-send test.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail in strict mode):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open-pixel tracker)

All four use `http://` — clients with mixed-content blocking will silently drop these, breaking layout slices and open tracking.

**Missing alt text — 40 images total.** Key offenders affecting content comprehension:
- `S26_TOP_SummerSale_NoUrgency.jpg` — hero banner, no alt
- `slices_01` through `slices_11` (rug product grid) — no alt on any
- `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg`, `S26_Bottom_UpTo60OffOutdoor.jpg` — promo banners, no alt
- Footer slices `slices_01` through `slices_18` — no alt on any

When images are disabled (default in many corporate mail clients), the email renders as a blank white column with no descriptive text fallback.

**Malformed CSS in `<style>` block:**
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
PHP `header()` calls were pasted directly into a `<style>` tag. These are invalid CSS and will generate parse errors. They have no effect on email delivery but indicate a template hygiene issue and may confuse some CSS parsers in rendering engines.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — no subject line fallback in the document head.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured.** The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM and Gmail/Yahoo sender requirements (2024+) mandate this header. Unconfirmed whether it was present at the MTA level and stripped by the relay, or genuinely absent. Must be verified against raw SMTP headers.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). If this header is missing at the MTA, the sender risks deliverability penalties from both providers.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not captured** (SPF/DKIM/DMARC status unknown via relay). Confirm DKIM passes for `e.potterybarn.com` and that DMARC policy on `potterybarn.com` covers the `e.` subdomain.

Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are present in the footer (confirmed by open-pixel and unsubscribe URL patterns in source) — CAN-SPAM baseline satisfied on those points.
