# Get the look with FREE design services
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-conversion email, not a product email — and that distinction matters. "Pottery Barn Design Services" is operating as a sub-brand sender here, pitching free in-home or virtual design consultations. The hero is strong and on-brand, the free offer is clear, and the seasonal summer event hook gives a reason to act now. The structure, however, is the problem: the email tries to close three separate asks simultaneously — book a design appointment, RSVP for a summer event, and shop product recommendations — and none of the three land with full force because of the competition.
- The email sits in a context of very heavy send volume from Pottery Barn. This is the second email today alone (following "A bestseller, now in new finishes"), and the seventh in four days across the main and sub-brand sender addresses. Subscribers who've already been pitched the Sausalito Collection this morning may be fatiguing. The design services angle is differentiated enough to justify a separate send, but the timing stacks badly.
- This is a competent mid-funnel email for a high-intent subscriber. It will convert the subset of people actively thinking about redecorating. For everyone else, it's an appointment-booking ask dropped into a commercial inbox at high cadence — which is a tough sell.

## What's working

- **The free offer is prominent and credible.** "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" in the hero headline is unambiguous — no fine print visible, no discount exclusions cluttering the frame.
- **"BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA is clear and repeated.** The button appears at least twice before the fold-equivalent break, which is the right call for a service email where the conversion bar is high.
- **Loyalty rewards surfaced at the bottom.** "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible near the footer — smart for an existing subscriber audience.
- **Room vignettes support the design service pitch.** The styled photography gives concrete evidence of what the design service can deliver, not just what it is.
- **Summer Event hook adds a calendar peg.** "Here's a Summer Event" with a June date creates an in-store reason to act before a soft deadline.

## What's weak

- **Three competing CTAs fracture the ask.** Book appointment → RSVP Today → Shop Recommended Products. Subscribers are being asked to make three different decisions; most will make zero.
- **The "Recommended Just for You" product block undercuts the service pitch.** Dropping a product grid into a design services email signals low confidence in the primary offer and invites the subscriber to scroll past the service and just browse the products instead.
- **High send cadence makes this land harder.** A seventh email in four days from Pottery Barn/sub-brands means the "free design services" pitch is competing with subscriber fatigue, not just inbox competition.
- **No visible social proof for the design service itself.** Client testimonials, transformation before/after, or a number like "over 5,000 designs completed" would dramatically lift confidence in clicking to book. None visible.
- **The sub-brand sender ("Pottery Barn Design Services") is appropriate but reduces recognition.** Subscribers pattern-match to "Pottery Barn" — the modified display name slightly obscures that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the product recommendation block.** Every product tile in this email is a reason NOT to book an appointment — "I'll just buy this thing directly." Strip it out. If you must include it, move it to a post-booking confirmation email.
- 2. **Consolidate to a single CTA: book the appointment.** Remove the "RSVP Today" for the summer event as a separate ask; absorb it into the appointment booking flow ("Book your free design appointment — we're hosting a Summer Design Event in June").
- 3. **Add one testimonial or stat.** "Customers who worked with a Pottery Barn designer spend X% more time enjoying their space" or a single 5-star pull quote humanizes the service offer.
- 4. **Lead subject with the time-sensitive summer event hook** rather than the evergreen service offer — that's what creates urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free design session — Summer Event, June [date]`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free designer is waiting (summer slots going fast)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free 1-on-1 with a Pottery Barn designer before the summer rush fills up.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In-store summer design events + free virtual sessions — your room, done right.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard-broken resources (403 response + cross-brand dead image) and pervasive missing alt text across 35+ images are the primary issues. Authentication/compliance headers are unverifiable at the relay layer but warrant attention.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken link (403) — credit card banner**
`https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`
Returns 403. However, the link is inside a commented-out HTML block (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not live in the current send. The dead URL is residual dead code that should be removed to prevent accidental reactivation.

**Broken image (403) — West Elm spacer**
`https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
Cross-brand tracking pixel returns 403. The image has no alt text and renders as a broken image in image-blocking clients. Should be replaced with a same-domain 1×1 spacer or removed.

**25 tracking links skipped** — all routed through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Not an error, but confirms no direct-URL probing was possible on destination pages.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**35 images missing `alt` text** — every content slice (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all product images, the footer, and the credit-card banner image carry `alt=""`. In images-off environments (Outlook default, many mobile clients) the email renders as a blank layout. This also fails WCAG 2.1 AA for screen readers.

Affected examples:
- `slices_01.jpg` – `slices_08.jpg`: `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/0603_TUE_AM2_DesignCrewCoastalBlue/`
- Product images: `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `marina-cane-bed-1-l.jpg`, etc.
- Footer slices: `slices_01.jpg` – `slices_18.jpg`: `https://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2026/US/Email-Footer/`

**Two images served over `http://`** (mixed content — will be blocked in Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook web):
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

**PHP `header()` calls inside a `<style>` block** — the `<head>` contains raw PHP output-buffering directives (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, cache-control headers) embedded in a `<style>` tag. These are inert in a rendered email but indicate a template-generation defect — likely a copy-paste from an image-serving script into the email template. Cosmetically harmless but a code hygiene issue.

**`display:none !important` on preheader** — preheader text is correctly hidden visually, but the zero-width Unicode spacers (U+034F `͏`, U+00AD `­`) used for preheader padding are present and correct.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058)** — not found. One-click unsubscribe will not function in Gmail's native unsubscribe UI without this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`.
