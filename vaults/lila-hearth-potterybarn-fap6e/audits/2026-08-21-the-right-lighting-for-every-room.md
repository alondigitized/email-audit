# The right lighting for every room
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's lighting-category education send — "Layering With Light" over a moody pendant-light hero, then a category-by-category walk (chandeliers, table lamps, sconces) each with its own room shot and copy line. It's the same structural move as 2026-08-19's "Bright ideas: Lighting upgrades," just reorganized around fixture type instead of room, which means a subscriber who opened that one four days ago is looking at a near-repeat.
- The creative itself is well-shot and on-brand — warm, moody interiors that sell the "atmosphere" pitch — but there's no offer anywhere in the hero or body copy, just "Shop Now" repeated four times. That's a soft, top-of-funnel send, and paired with the immediate cadence repeat from Monday's lighting email, it reads as filler rather than a reason to click today.

## What's working

- Clear thematic organization — chandeliers, table lamps, sconces each get a labeled section with a one-line rationale ("Chandeliers create a focal point...").
- Hero image (layered pendant lights) is well-composed and matches the "layering with light" headline.
- "SHOP ALL LIGHTING" closing CTA gives a clear category rollup after the individual sections.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly.
- "Recommended Just For You" grid adds a personalization signal beneath the core content.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere — no %, $, or free-shipping call-out, just repeated "Shop Now" buttons.
- This is the second lighting-themed send in three days (2026-08-19's "Bright ideas: Lighting upgrades" covered nearly the same ground room-by-room); back-to-back category emails without a new offer feels like filler.
- Subject "The right lighting for every room" is generic and undifferentiated from the prior lighting subject — no urgency, no specificity, low reason to open over other unread promos.
- The "Design Crew Sweeps" module below the fold is the same sweepstakes-as-lead-gen mechanic flagged in 2026-08-18's audit, and it's oddly wedged between lighting content and the Halloween Shop promo with no visual transition.
- Product detail (price, dimensions, materials) is absent from the lighting sections themselves — only the "Recommended" grid at the bottom shows any pricing/sizing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete, time-bound offer to the hero (e.g., "20% off lighting through Sunday") — right now there's nothing pulling a browsing subscriber into an actual transaction, and four straight days without a hard offer risks list fatigue.
- 2. Differentiate this from the 2026-08-19 lighting send — either fold the two into one comprehensive lighting campaign or space them further apart; running near-identical lighting content twice in one week reads as repetitive to anyone who opened both.
- 3. Pull one or two prices into the fixture sections (e.g., "Pendants from $99") so the click has a concrete reason beyond the mood shot.
- 4. Move "Design Crew Sweeps" to its own send or place it further from the lighting content with a clear section break — the abrupt tonal shift from lighting inspiration to gift-card sweepstakes reads as bolted-on.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `From moody pendants to bedside lamps — layer your light this week.`

# Technical Email Audit — Pottery Barn "The right lighting for every room"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO/Outlook-compatible HTML table layout with ESP tracking (mi_mid/cp_tp redirects) and a Sailthru-style open beacon; it passes basic rendering checks but has systemic missing `alt` text, several plaintext-HTTP image sources, and no verifiable unsubscribe/authentication headers in the captured data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Header/logo link routes through `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud style tracking redirect) — consistent with expected ESP click-tracking pattern.
- 55 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design — redirect domains aren't followed), so live-link validity for `click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`, and `d.turn.com` destinations is unconfirmed and should be manually verified before send.
- Open-tracking pixel present: `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — served over **HTTP**, not HTTPS, which some mail clients (notably Outlook/Office 365 ATP) will block or flag, silencing open tracking for affected recipients.
- Secondary beacon `https://d.turn.com/r/dd/id/L21rdC84NjEvY2lkLzE3NDc3ODk3MzMvdC8y/cat/1` (Turn/Amobee ad-tracking pixel) loads over HTTPS — no issue.
- Recipient email address is embedded in plaintext query params on multiple beacon/tracking URLs (`svopn.potterybarn.com`, `coreemail.html?email=...`). This is common ESP practice but worth flagging as a PII-in-URL exposure point (referrer leakage risk if any downstream resource logs full referrers).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **34 images missing `alt` text** (see QA findings) — screen readers and image-blocked clients (common default in Outlook/Gmail) will render this email as a wall of unlabeled images with no fallback content, including all primary content banners (`F26_Top_NewArrivals.jpg`, `slices_01–09.jpg` lighting-by-type modules, product shots, footer legal slices).
- Extensive MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `<!--[if mso 16]>`) and Outlook-specific fixes are present — good coverage for Outlook desktop rendering.
- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and Apple-links CSS (`.appleLinks a`) present — mitigates iOS auto-styling of links/phone numbers.
- Malformed `<style>` block: raw PHP-style `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg'); header("Cache-Control: ...");` statements are embedded as literal text inside a `<style>` tag in the `<head>` (CLIENT styling section). This is inert (harmless as CSS, since it won't parse as valid CSS/JS) but indicates leftover/misplaced server-side code accidentally pasted into the template — should be removed as dead weight.
- Preheader text ("Lamps, chandeliers, pendants & more") is properly hidden via `display:none !important; mso-hide:all;` with zero-width-space padding — correctly implemented, avoids preheader/body-leak issue seen in poorly built templates.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{first_name}}`, `[[token]]`) found in the truncated source.
- Recipient identifier is hard-resolved into tracking URLs as a literal email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) rather than a token placeholder — confirms personalization merge executed correctly for this send, no broken-token risk.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` header detected** — QA flags this as possibly an artifact of the AgentMail relay not capturing/passing the header rather than the sender omitting it; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM one-click compliance from this data alone. Recommend checking raw headers at the SMTP/relay layer.
- **No `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header detected** (RFC 8058) — same caveat; if genuinely absent, this email would not qualify for Gmail/Yahoo's one-click unsubscribe requirement (mandatory since Feb 2024 bulk-sender rules).
- **No `Authentication-Results` header captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this data; cannot confirm authentication compliance. This is also flagged as likely a relay-capture gap rather than a sender-side failure.
- Footer contains a customer-service/unsubscribe-adjacent link (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) suggesting an unsubscribe/preference-center mechanism exists in-body, but this doesn't substitute for the missing `List-Unsubscribe` mail header.
- **Action needed:** verify raw SMTP headers directly from the sending ESP (not via the AgentMail relay) to close out unsubscribe-header and authentication findings with certainty.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

4. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` statements pasted into the `<style>` block in `<head>` — dead code with no functional effect but signals template hygiene issues.
