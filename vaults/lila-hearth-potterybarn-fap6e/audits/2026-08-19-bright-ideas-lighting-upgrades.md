# Bright ideas: Lighting upgrades
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's lighting-category launch, structured room-by-room: bedroom lamps, living-room floor lamps (Payton, Chelsea), dining-room pendants (Elizabeth, Cooper), bathroom sconces (Palmer, Hayden), then a "Shop All Lighting" button and a "Recommended for You" grid. It's essentially the same architecture as the 2026-08-18 "New lamps for a little extra light" send — same trendspotting-into-category-grid formula, just wearing a different room-by-room skin this time. The hero image (styled bedroom with rattan lamps and layered bedding) is genuinely pretty and on-brand, and the room-by-room organization is a smart way to let a shopper self-select their category without scrolling through a flat product grid.
- Where it falls down is the same place recent Pottery Barn sends have: there's no price, no discount, no urgency anywhere in this email. It's a pure product-discovery send, competing for inbox attention against the brand's own Design Crew Sweepstakes and Halloween Headquarters sends from the same week. For someone like me — furnishing a first home and price-sensitive — this reads as "browse our catalog" rather than "here's a reason to buy today." Solid craft, weak commercial hook. It'll drive some lookie-loo clicks from people already mid-lighting-project, but it won't convert casual openers.

## What's working

- Clean room-by-room structure (Bedroom → Living Room → Dining Room → Bathroom) makes category navigation intuitive and mirrors how a shopper actually thinks about a home.
- Each product row shows two named options with prices-adjacent styling cues (Payton/Chelsea, Elizabeth/Cooper, Palmer/Hayden) — good for quick comparison shopping.
- Hero image is well-styled and seasonally neutral (works year-round, no stale promo language to go stale).
- "Recommended Just For You" section at the bottom adds personalization signal beyond the static lighting catalog.
- No visible render bugs — images loaded cleanly, text is legible, layout is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- Zero price or offer anywhere in the primary lighting content — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing that creates urgency to act now versus browsing later.
- Subject "Bright ideas: Lighting upgrades" is generic and nearly interchangeable with last week's "New lamps for a little extra light" — brand is repeating the lighting beat without escalating the offer.
- The one financial hook in the whole email — Pottery Barn credit card's 10% back — is buried near the bottom under Affirm/rewards copy, disconnected from the lighting products above it.
- "Design Crew Sweepstakes" banner sits mid-email as a visual non-sequitur, breaking the lighting narrative with an unrelated $1,500 giveaway pitch (same lead-gen mechanic flagged in the 2026-08-18 sweepstakes-only send).
- Very long, low-density email (bedroom → living → dining → bath → recommended → sweeps → Halloween → app → rewards) risks losing scroll-through attention well before "Shop All Lighting."

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer to the hero — even "15% off lighting this week" would turn this from a catalog browse into an actionable send; right now there's no reason to act today versus bookmarking for later.
- 2. Cut or relocate the Design Crew Sweepstakes block — it interrupts the lighting narrative and dilutes focus; move it to its own send (as the brand already does) rather than double-dipping here.
- 3. Move the 10%-back credit card callout up near the hero or repeat it as a badge on the "Shop All Lighting" CTA — right now it's disconnected from the purchase moment.
- 4. Shorten the email — pick the 2-3 strongest rooms (bedroom + living room + dining) and cut bathroom sconces or fold it into "Shop All Lighting," since click-through drops off well before row 4 of a room-by-room scroll.
- 5. Sharpen the subject to name the differentiator, not just the category:
- **Subject Alt A:** `New lighting, room by room — plus 15% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Lamps, pendants & sconces for every room`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From bedside lamps to bathroom sconces — shop the full lineup.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Payton, Elizabeth, Palmer & more — new lighting arrivals inside.`

  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`

Harmless to rendering but signals a template hygiene defect worth flagging to the ESP/dev team.
- Responsive breakpoints are defined for both 480px and 650px widths with `mobile-hide`/`mobile-show`/`showmobile` classes — no structural issues found in the truncated source.

- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules) cannot be confirmed as supported.

- **Caveat**: the three header-related warnings may be artifacts of the AgentMail capture/relay stripping headers rather than true sender-side omissions — recommend verifying against raw SMTP headers at the ESP before treating as a compliance failure.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot confirm UTM parameter presence/consistency — all outbound content links route through opaque click-tracking redirects (`click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...`) with base64/encoded query strings rather than transparent UTM params, so destination and UTM alignment cannot be verified without following redirects.
- Plain-text alternative is **>70% URL characters** (12,238 of 17,209 chars), which is a spam-filter risk signal (low text-to-link ratio) independent of landing-page alignment.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 50 flagged images, particularly the product-slice and banner images that carry the primary CTA content.
2. Migrate the 3 HTTP image sources to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking in modern mail clients.
3. Replace the plaintext-HTTP open-tracking pixel with an HTTPS endpoint, and stop passing raw recipient email addresses in tracking-pixel query strings — use a hashed/opaque subscriber ID instead.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` headers directly against ESP send logs or raw SMTP headers, since Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements mandate one-click unsubscribe as of 2024.
5. Remove the stray PHP `header(...)` fragment from the `<style>` block — likely a template-merge artifact from the ESP build pipeline.
6. Reduce plain-text URL density (currently 70%+) to improve spam-filter scoring.
