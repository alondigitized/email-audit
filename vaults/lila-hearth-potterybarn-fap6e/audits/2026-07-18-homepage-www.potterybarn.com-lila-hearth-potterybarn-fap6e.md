# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-07-18
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- What greeted me the moment I landed was a full-screen email signup modal — not the homepage. Pottery Barn is clearly positioning itself as a premium home brand (the lifestyle imagery is gorgeous, very second-home-in-the-Hudson-Valley), but right now it's leading with a data-capture ask before I've even seen a single product. Classic Williams-Sonoma family playbook, but it's a friction-first experience when I just want to browse new arrivals for the living room.

## What's working

- **The lifestyle photography in the modal** is doing real work — that sectional, the layered rug, the lamps and shiplap wall reads exactly like the second-home living room I'm actively pulling together. It stops the scroll.
- **The Pottery Barn wordmark centered below the image** reinforces brand trust instantly; this doesn't feel like a third-party popup.
- **"Find out first: New arrivals, exclusive savings & more."** — the framing of insider access resonates with me as someone who waits for the holiday catalog. Early access to new arrivals is genuinely motivating.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand mention in the fine print** — I'm in the rewards ecosystem, so seeing that family name adds credibility.
- **The X close button** is prominent and accessible; this isn't a hostile trap-modal.

## What's weak

- **No actual offer above the fold** — "exclusive savings" with zero specificity is a promise without teeth. I need a number (20% off, free shipping over $149) to feel the trade is worth my email.
- **Loyalty status is completely ignored** — I'm already in the Williams-Sonoma system. If they know me, why ask me to sign up from scratch? This modal should swap for a "you're already a member — here's what's new" state.
- **The fine print is oppressive** — two paragraphs of SMS legalese dominate the bottom half of the modal. It's legally required but visually it tanks trust; the ratio of scary copy to enticing copy is backwards.
- **The homepage behind the modal is invisible** — I cannot see a single category, hero, or navigation item. For a high-intent shopper who came to browse, this is a hard stop before any discovery.
- **No mobile number pre-fill or Apple/Google sign-in** — for a tech-comfortable millennial, friction in the form itself is a reason to just close it.

## Recommendations

- **Show the offer number on the modal** — "Get 20% off your first order" or "Unlock free shipping on your next order" turns the vague promise into a tangible incentive worth a trade.
- **Add a "Skip — just let me browse" CTA styled as a secondary link** under the SIGN UP button, so browsers who know the brand don't feel forced; it respects intent and reduces rage-closes.
- **For recognized loyalty members, suppress or swap the modal** — pull their rewards balance or first name if they're cookied into the WS ecosystem and serve a personalized "Welcome back, Lila" module instead.
- **Trim the SMS fine print into a collapsible "Terms" link** — keep the legalese accessible but don't let it eat the visual real estate above the primary CTA.
