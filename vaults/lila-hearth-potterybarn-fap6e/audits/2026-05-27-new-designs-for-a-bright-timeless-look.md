# NEW designs for a bright, timeless look
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the third distinct Pottery Barn creative on May 27 alone — and it reads like the quietest of the three. Where the earlier pool-storage and free-design-services sends had clear editorial angles, this one is a broad "new arrivals" sweep with no offer, no anchor product, and no urgency of any kind. The subject line ("NEW designs for a bright, timeless look") is aspirational filler — it could describe almost any Pottery Barn email from the past decade.
- The render itself is clean and on-brand: a large, sunlit hero featuring light-wood, rattan, and linen tones, followed by a dense product grid and a personalized "Recommended Just for You" strip. The visual execution is solid. The strategic problem is that there's nothing here to act on — no discount, no deadline, no hero SKU — and it's landing the same day as two more purposeful siblings, competing for the same inbox slot.
- Coming off the Memorial Day blitz, a restrained editorial send can work. But it needs a stronger hook than a vague aesthetic promise. Right now this reads like a browse, not a buy.

## What's working

- Hero image is large, bright, and immediately communicates the "timeless" aesthetic the subject promises — visual consistency is strong.
- "Recommended Just for You" strip adds genuine personalization signal inside the body and breaks up the product wall.
- Product grid is well-photographed and covers multiple room categories, giving subscribers multiple entry points.
- No broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues — clean render throughout.
- "Free Design Services" badge near the bottom reinforces the value-add message that appeared in the earlier May 27 send.

## What's weak

- No offer whatsoever — no discount, no threshold for free shipping, no bundle — leaving subscribers nothing to act on beyond casual browsing.
- Subject line is vague and interchangeable; it could apply to any lifestyle brand and will not cut through a busy post-holiday inbox.
- Product grid is too dense with no clear hero SKU — the eye doesn't know where to land after the hero banner.
- Three Pottery Barn emails on May 27 is too many; this one, being the weakest, dilutes the stronger free-design-services send.
- No visible urgency or time signal anywhere in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a hook, not a vibe.** Pick one hero product from the new arrivals and make it the star — name, image, price. "New arrival" as a concept is weak; "The Cayman Chair just dropped — here's why it sold out last summer" is not.
- 2. **Add a low-friction offer.** Even free shipping on new arrivals orders over a threshold would give subscribers a reason to click today vs. tomorrow vs. never.
- 3. **Cut one of the three May 27 sends** — or fold this new-arrivals content into the pool/summer send as a secondary module. Three sends in one day cannibalizes each other.
- 4. **Subject: make it concrete.** Name a category or a specific piece.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New pieces across bedroom, dining, and living — see what just landed.`

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected.** The QA relay did not capture this header. If absent from the actual SMTP delivery, one-click unsubscribe in Gmail and Apple Mail will not function, and the send may fall short of Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since 2024).

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected.** Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI. Without it, subscribers must navigate to an external page — increasing friction and spam complaints.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not captured.** SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm authentication posture; if DMARC is failing, deliverability to major ISPs is at risk.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

The 24 click-redirect links all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`, which is the ESP's click-tracking wrapper. UTM parameter injection typically happens at the ESP layer here, not in the raw HTML, so the absence of visible UTMs in the source is expected for this platform.

The broken credit card CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`) does carry an `external_id` and `cm_ite` parameter, confirming campaign attribution was intended — but since the link 403s, no attribution is captured.

The `coreemail.html` tracking beacon includes the subscriber address as a query parameter:
```
https://www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html
  ?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app
```
This passes PII (email address) as a GET parameter in a URL, which is logged in server access logs, browser history, and potentially CDN/proxy logs.
