# Book FREE design help
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second "free design services" push in two days — the 5/19 send already pulled this exact move (same sender, same "RSVP for free design" concept, same "Pottery Barn Design Services" from-name), and it scored well precisely because it stood apart from the discount blasts. Sending the same creative differentiator two days later collapses the strategy: the tactic only works once per campaign cycle, and burning it again dilutes both the design-services brand and the inbox goodwill that made the 5/19 version land.
- The email itself is well-structured. Hero leads with the free appointment CTA, flows into the Memorial Day sale category grid, then closes with a personalized product carousel. The "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" module is a genuine value-add and the kind of thing that holds attention after the hero.
- The problem isn't the email — it's that subscribers who opened the 5/19 send are now looking at the same pitch with a nearly identical subject and the same button. For those who didn't open 5/19, this is a fine re-engagement; for those who did, it reads as a copy-paste mistake. The brand should have held this send until 5/23 or rotated to a different design-services angle entirely.

## What's working

- **From-name differentiation holds up**: "Pottery Barn Design Services" still creates a meaningful inbox break from the main PB handle — subscribers conditioned to skip discount blasts may still open this.
- **Hero CTA is clean and unambiguous**: "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is a concrete verb + noun with no friction language.
- **"FREE" is the right lead**: Zero-cost framing gets more lift than percentage-off in a discount-fatigued campaign window.
- **Personalized product carousel**: "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" section adds editorial value and gives a second reason to scroll — smart structural choice.
- **Rewards callout at footer**: "Earn 10% Back in Rewards" is visible and reinforces loyalty for members.

## What's weak

- **Repeat of the 5/19 design-services send**: Same sender, same concept, same creative skeleton two days later — any subscriber who opened the prior email immediately clocks this as a re-run.
- **No urgency in the hero**: The free design offer has no expiration or Memorial Day tie-in visible in the hero copy, making it feel like a standing offer rather than a campaign moment.
- **Category sale grid dilutes the design-services lead**: Dropping immediately into "UP TO 50% OFF" categories undercuts the "this is different from a discount email" positioning.
- **Subject repeats the same brand and concept as 5/19**: "Book FREE design help" vs. "RSVP for FREE design appointments" — functionally identical signals to a fatigued subscriber list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold the send or change the angle** — The design-services differentiation tactic has a one-use-per-campaign shelf life. If you're going to re-engage non-openers from 5/19, send a plain-text one-to-one message from a designer's name, not a broadcast template. That actually feels different.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the free appointment offer** — "Appointments through Memorial Day weekend" is credible, visible urgency. Right now the hero offers zero time pressure, killing the open→click motivation.
- 3. **Suppress the category sale grid from this version** — Let the design services email own its identity. The 50%-off grid belongs in the discount blasts. Here it muddies the value prop.
- 4. **Tighten the subject to a benefit + deadline**: anchor it to the holiday weekend rather than just "FREE."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free design help — book before Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free design consult expires Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Memorial Day weekend is the right moment to finally pull the room together.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our designers are booked through the weekend — grab a spot now.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed broken resources (403 on a credit card promo link, 403 on a West Elm spacer GIF), pervasive missing alt text across ~30+ images, two HTTP-sourced images that will be blocked in modern clients, and PHP header calls embedded raw in a CSS `<style>` block — likely a template artifact that will appear as visible text in some parsers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — 403 on credit card banner link:**

```
https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY&external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner&cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This link is inside a commented-out `<!-- -->` block in the current source, so it does not render. However, if the comment is ever re-enabled (it appears to be a toggle), it will 403 immediately.

**FAIL — 403 on cross-brand tracker/spacer:**
```
https://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif
```
Used as a 1×1 tracking pixel or layout spacer. Returns 403, meaning the image loads broken and any associated open-tracking tied to this asset will fail silently.

**25 click-tracking links skipped** by QA probe — all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Cannot confirm destination integrity without redirect resolution; no structural issue found in the redirect wrapper itself.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**PHP headers in `<style>` block (critical template bug):**
```html
<style>
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
...
```
These are raw PHP `header()` calls pasted into a CSS `<style>` tag. They are not valid CSS. Depending on the mail client, this text may render visibly or cause the stylesheet to fail parsing entirely. This looks like a copy-paste from a PHP image-serving script that was never stripped before template assembly.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — 2 confirmed:**
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook all block mixed-content HTTP images by default. These footer images will render as broken.

**Missing alt text — 30+ images including all body slices:**
Every content image (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg` in the body, all 18 footer slices, all product images) has `alt=""`. Since the email is almost entirely image-based, recipients with images disabled (a common corporate mail policy) see a blank email with no recoverable text. Screen reader users get nothing actionable.

**Spacer/filler characters in preheader:**
The preheader padding div uses both Unicode Ogham space marks (`͏`) and soft hyphens (`­`) — standard technique, no issue.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — QA could not confirm presence via AgentMail relay. If truly absent, this violates RFC 2369 and Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day senders).

**List-Unsubscribe-Post not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is absent or not captured. Required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy; failure results in deliverability penalties.

**Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass status could not be verified through the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. If SPF/DKIM are failing, this email is at risk of spam folder placement or rejection.

Sending domain `e.potterybarn.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — DKIM alignment likely configured at the ESP level, but cannot confirm from available data.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The credit card promo URL uses `cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember` as a campaign parameter — non-standard (not UTM). The body CTA click links all route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirect tracking; UTM parameters on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirects.

The open-tracker pixel (`coreemail.html`) loads a page from `www.potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...` — this is an unusual pattern (an HTML page loaded as an `<img>` src). It will return whatever status code that page returns; if the page is ever taken down or redirects, open tracking breaks.
