# FREE (yes, free!) design services
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's second Design Services pitch in two days — July 14 sent "It's FREE to work with our design experts" (scored 5/10 here) and now July 16 sends the same core email with a subject that tries harder. The creative underneath is near-identical: RSVP hero, four service-tile grid, dual "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" CTAs, rewards footer. The parenthetical "(yes, free!)" in the subject reads less like wit and more like a brand quietly acknowledging nobody clicked the first time.
- That said, the email itself renders cleanly. The hero is well-composed, the primary CTA is unambiguous, and the service grid communicates genuine value breadth (décor refreshes, bed styling, sofa selection, storage). The Affirm and 10%-back rewards blocks at the bottom are smart upsell credibility anchors — they reframe a "free service" as a gateway to a real transaction, not a dead end.
- The fatal flaw is cadence: repeating a low-engagement email 48 hours later with only a reworded subject is a trust erosion move. A subscriber who ignored July 14 now gets a second identical ask, and the "(yes, free!)" parenthetical signals desperation rather than relevance.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is clear above the fold** — "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" + black CTA button land in the right order.
- **Service tile grid communicates breadth well** — four distinct use cases (décor refresh, bed layering, sofa selection, storage) show real utility without feeling like a list dump.
- **CTA copy is specific and action-oriented** — "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is one of the cleaner CTA strings in this brand's July run.
- **10% Back in Rewards and Affirm blocks add purchase credibility** — smartly reframe a service email into a commerce journey.
- **No render bugs visible** — clean layout, no broken images, text-image alignment holds.

## What's weak

- **Near-duplicate of the July 14 send** — same creative, same hero, same service tiles; the only meaningful change is the subject line.
- **"(yes, free!)" reads as spam-adjacent emphasis** — the parenthetical is trying to manufacture urgency/surprise and instead signals the brand knows it underperformed last send.
- **"Spots go fast!" is unconvincing urgency** — it appears in the body without a date, a slot count, or any mechanism that makes it feel real; subscribers have seen this line too many times.
- **Fall Collection tease at the bottom is disconnected** — a lifestyle shop-today block appended after a service pitch muddies the single-purpose focus.
- **Six total CTAs across the email** (two BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT, four BOOK NOW) dilute each other; the mid-email grid especially loses click equity to link density.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't resend the same email 48 hours later.** If Design Services underperformed July 14, the answer is a new hook (customer testimonial, "Your room in 30 minutes" narrative, before/after imagery), not a subject-line rebrand of the same layout. This should have been a 7-day minimum re-engagement gap with a meaningfully different angle.
- 2. **Collapse the four "BOOK NOW" tiles into a single choose-your-service flow** — the grid works visually but the four identical CTA links each pull separately; a single "Which room are we tackling?" interactive entry point would consolidate click intent.
- 3. **Replace "spots go fast!" with a real scarcity mechanism** — a rolling count of available slots this week, a specific close date, or a waitlist framing would give the urgency legs.
- 4. **Cut the Fall Collection tease** — it belongs in a separate send; here it signals that the brand itself isn't sure this email is worth a single-topic treatment.

### 1. Technical Summary

The core body links appear structurally sound but are all opaque click-redirect URLs that could not be probed.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken link (403):**
```
https://www.potterybarn.com/pages/credit-card/?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB
  &marketingChannelCode=APPLYANDBUY
  &external_id=PB_Email_MemberBanner
  &cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember
```
This link is commented out in the HTML (`<!-- ... -->`), so it is not clickable in the live email. However, the commented block still contains a live URL that returned 403 — the dead destination confirms the page is actually broken, not just disabled. If the comment is ever re-enabled, the link will 404/403 for all recipients.

**WARN — 23 tracking links skipped:** All body CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...` — opaque base64-encoded parameters. HTTP probing was skipped; destination validity cannot be confirmed.

**Tracking domain:** `click.e.potterybarn.com` — consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget. No mixed tracking domains detected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 30 images missing `alt` text.** Nearly every image in the email has `alt=""`, including all body content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`) and all footer slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`). Since the entire email is image-sliced with no HTML text fallback, recipients with images disabled see a fully blank email.

**FAIL — Two images served over HTTP (not HTTPS):**
```
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg
http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block mixed-content HTTP images. These images will likely not render for the majority of recipients.

**WARN — PHP `header()` calls inside `<style>` block:**
```html
<style>
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  ...
```
This is server-side PHP code that was never executed — it was pasted raw into the HTML `<style>` tag. Email clients will treat it as invalid CSS and discard the block. The intended cache-control behavior is not applied. This is likely a template assembly bug.

**Responsive breakpoint:** The stylesheet defines `max-width: 480px` in one media query and `max-width: 650px` in another. The inconsistency means the mobile layout activates at different widths depending on which rule takes effect — potential rendering gap between 480–649px.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** Not captured by the AgentMail relay — may be present at the MTA level but unverifiable. Gmail requires this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under its 2024 enforcement; absence or non-capture is a deliverability risk.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found.** RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders since Feb 2024.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not found.** SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown through the relay. Sending domain is `e.potterybarn.com` (subdomain), which typically has its own SPF/DKIM alignment. Cannot confirm alignment without raw headers.

The email's footer contains a physical mailing address and visible unsubscribe link (inferred from the `coreemail.html` pixel pattern) — CAN-SPAM physical address requirement is likely satisfied, but the unsubscribe mechanism itself could not be verified from the truncated source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**No UTM parameters detected** on any of the 23 click-redirect URLs. All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` with opaque `?qs=` tokens. If the ESP does not append UTM parameters after redirect resolution, all traffic from this email will be unattributed in GA4/Adobe Analytics.

The credit card link (commented out) used `cm_ite=` and `external_id=` — Williams-Sonoma's legacy tracking params — rather than UTM. This is inconsistent with standard UTM attribution if other analytics tools expect `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign`.
