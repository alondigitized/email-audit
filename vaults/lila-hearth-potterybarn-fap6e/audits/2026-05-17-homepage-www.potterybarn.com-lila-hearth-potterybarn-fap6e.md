# Homepage snapshot · www.potterybarn.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I opened Pottery Barn and the first thing I get is a sweepstakes modal for a golf-loving dad — not exactly my vibe when I'm trying to pull together the living room in my lake house. The brand reads premium and aspirational, which I appreciate, but right now it's pushing a Father's Day campaign hard enough to completely block the homepage. I can't even tell what's on sale for *me*.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing** — Father's Day is June 15, so running this campaign in mid-May is smart calendar marketing.
- **Williams-Sonoma brand mention in fine print** — as a WS Rewards member I noticed the cross-brand language; there's latent loyalty equity here even if it isn't being activated.
- **Clean modal design** — the photography is warm and the typography is legible; Pottery Barn's visual identity still reads premium through the popup.
- **Chat button** — the persistent CHAT bubble in the bottom right is actually useful; if I need design help for the second home, I'd consider tapping it.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen interstitial before I see any product** — this is the single biggest problem. I opened the app to shop for myself and I'm immediately being asked to enter a sweepstakes for someone else. My first instinct is to close it.
- **Zero relevance to my use case** — golf courses book, whiskey decanter, U.S. Open tickets. Nothing on this screen maps to "second home, monogrammed throws, bedroom refresh."
- **No loyalty recognition** — I'm a Williams-Sonoma / PB Key Holder and there's no "welcome back" hook, no rewards balance, nothing to make me feel like a known customer.
- **Sweepstakes fine print is dense and off-putting** — three full paragraphs of legal copy (wireless carrier disclaimers, Rhode Island/Arizona exclusions, 5/24/26 end date) feel like a compliance wall. It reads like a chore, not an invitation.
- **Navigation is completely hidden** — the top nav bar is partially visible (I can see Baby, Kids, Teen, Dorm) but home categories like Furniture, Bedding, or Outdoor are buried behind the modal.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the sweepstakes interstitial for known loyalty members** — if I'm logged in or cookie-recognized, skip the email-capture modal entirely and serve me a targeted re-engagement banner instead ("Your Key Holder offer: 15% off outdoor this weekend").
- **Swap the golf lifestyle image for a second-home / lake house scene** — even if the sweepstakes stays, a sun-drenched deck with PB entertaining pieces would pull me in rather than making me feel like I've landed on the wrong page.
- **Add a dismissible sticky banner below the hero** — one line: "Shop Memorial Day outdoor + bedding | Free shipping on $149+" lets me get to my actual use case within one tap without nuking the Dad campaign.
- **Surface the rewards balance in the modal header** — "You have 240 Key Holder points — plus enter to win $500 for Dad" transforms a cold acquisition push into a personalized loyalty moment.
