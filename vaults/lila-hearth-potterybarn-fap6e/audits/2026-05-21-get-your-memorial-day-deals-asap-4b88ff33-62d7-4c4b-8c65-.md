# Get your Memorial Day deals ASAP!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's eleventh Memorial Day email in roughly six days, and it shows. The template is identical to prior sends — red hero banner, "Extra 20% off Select Styles," category discount grid — with the only distinguishing creative element being a "Recommended Just For You" personalization block. The subject line, "Get your Memorial Day deals ASAP!," attempts to manufacture urgency without delivering a specific deadline or offer, which is a miss when Memorial Day (May 25) is four days away and a concrete date would actually justify the urgency.
- The personalization section is the one genuine differentiator here. Unlike the spray-and-pray category grids from prior sends, behavioral product recommendations give the recipient something personal to click toward. It's buried below the fold, though, and the hero above it is pure rerun.
- The bottom line: subscribers who have survived eleven Pottery Barn sends this week are either already customers or definitionally immune. This email will convert the fence-sitters who need a deadline nudge, but the send-fatigue cost is real and the creative has nothing new to offer.

## What's working

- **"Recommended Just For You" product grid** is the strongest element — actual behavioral personalization vs. the generic category grid that's appeared in every prior send.
- **8-category navigation grid** (Outdoor, Bedding, Sofas & Chairs, etc.) makes it easy to self-select into a relevant category without hunting.
- **Earn 15% Back in Rewards** loyalty callout in the footer adds an incentive layer beyond the sale price — the only secondary benefit in this send.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — red hero to offer to categories to personalized picks flows logically with no layout breaks visible.
- **Affirm "Buy Now, Pay Over Time"** lowers the barrier for large-ticket purchases without burying the option.

## What's weak

- **Eleventh send in six days on the same template** — subscribers are either fatigued, converted, or aggressively filtering Pottery Barn into a folder.
- **"ASAP" urgency without a specific deadline** — Memorial Day is May 25, four days away; "Ends Monday, May 25" would outperform "ASAP" in any A/B test.
- **No countdown timer or visual deadline cue** in the hero — the urgency framing in the subject line is unsupported by anything in the body.
- **"Recommended Just For You" is below the fold** — the highest-value, most differentiated section requires scrolling past the same hero the subscriber has seen ten times.
- **Subject carries no offer specificity** — unlike the stronger sends in this campaign ("EXTRA 20% OFF," "up to 60% off!"), "Get your Memorial Day deals ASAP!" gives no reason to click before opening.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the layout: lead with personalized picks, put the category grid second.** The behavioral recommendations are the only thing this email has that the prior ten don't. Lead with that.
- 2. **Add a specific deadline to the hero.** "Sale ends Monday, May 25" > "ASAP." Give the subscriber a date to anchor on.
- 3. **Put the % in the subject line.** The May 19 send ("EXTRA 20% OFF") and May 19 catalog email outperformed the vague urgency frames. Keep the specificity.
- 4. **Add a countdown module.** Even a simple "3 days left" banner above the hero would activate the loss-aversion the subject is trying to trigger.
- 5. **Stop after this one.** Sending a twelfth email before Memorial Day will actively degrade the list.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 20% off ends Monday — your picks are waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: Memorial Day Sale ends May 25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your personalized picks are still on sale — through Monday only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 60% off + Extra 20% expires Monday. Don't leave it behind.`

```css

header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, ...");
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```

These are PHP statements copy-pasted into a CSS `<style>` block. They do nothing in an email client but add byte weight and could confuse CSS parsers in strict clients.
