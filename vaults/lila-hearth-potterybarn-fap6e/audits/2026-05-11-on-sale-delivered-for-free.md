# On sale & delivered for free
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- Pottery Barn's "Save on Easy Updates" send is a competent seasonal sale email that leans on brand authority and a free-shipping hook rather than doing real targeting work. The hero is clean, the lifestyle photography is aspirational without being cold, and the category nav gives shoppers a fast self-selection path. But the offer architecture is soft: "ON SALE & SHIPPING FREE" never tells you *how much* you save, and the email makes no attempt to create urgency or sharpen the pitch beyond a generic "Recommended Just for You" label.
- The preheader "In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" is genuinely differentiated messaging — it signals Amazon-tier fulfillment speed from a premium brand — but it's buried where most subscribers won't register it. The recommendation grid then undermines itself by mixing platform beds, curtain rods, towels, and rugs in the same "curated" row, which reads as algorithm noise rather than editorial intent.
- Verdict: a well-produced maintenance email that keeps the brand warm without doing serious revenue work. It won't move the needle the way a tighter, percentage-anchored sale send would.

## What's working

- Lifestyle hero photography (navy pillows, neutral sofa) is on-brand and aspirational — the visual registers Pottery Barn's aesthetic instantly
- Free shipping is called out in both the subject line and the hero; no ambiguity about the offer
- "In-Stock Furniture Delivered in 1-4 Weeks" is a genuine competitive differentiator worth surfacing prominently
- Category navigation grid (PILLOWS, THROWS, QUILTS, DUVETS, TABLE LINENS, SHEETS…) gives different shopper intents a fast path without scrolling
- Named product thumbnails in the recommendation grid (Farmhouse Platform Bed, Chunky Wool Jute Rug, Layman Platform Bed) surface specific SKUs and give the email something to actually click on

## What's weak

- "ON SALE" without a number is weak — no percentage off, no dollar-off anchor, nothing that makes the savings feel concrete or competitive
- The "Recommended Just for You" grid mixes wildly different categories (bedroom furniture, curtain hardware, bath towels, rugs) — it reads like a warehouse clearance, not thoughtful curation
- Zero urgency — no sale end date, no "while supplies last," no countdown; a cautious shopper will bookmark and never return
- Subject "On sale & delivered for free" applies to almost any retailer's weekly promo; no category signal, no style hook, nothing that says *Pottery Barn*
- Affirm and app-download modules dilute focus in a sale email; both belong in a separate send or post-purchase flow

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the sale with a real number.** "Up to 40% off" or "From $X" in the hero is the single highest-lift change — it makes the offer scannable and credible. Everything else is secondary.
- 2. **Add a sale end date.** Even "Ends Sunday" creates enough urgency to move fence-sitters.
- 3. **Tighten the subject around a category.** Pick the top-performing department (bedding, outdoor seating, rugs) and lead with it — "on sale & free delivery" is forgettable; "Rugs, throws & bedding — now on sale, ships free" is not.
- 4. **Curate the recommendation grid to one theme.** "Bed & Bath sale picks" or "Outdoor sale" reads as intentional; mixed platform beds + towels reads as random.
- 5. **Cut or move the Affirm and app modules** — they introduce friction and compete with the sale message in a send that should be singularly focused on converting browsers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off + free delivery — Easy Updates`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rugs, beds & bedding on sale, delivered free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `In-stock pieces ship in 1-4 weeks — sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Easy Updates edit — on sale now, no minimum to ship free`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "On sale & delivered for free"

**1. Technical Summary**

Two hard broken links (403) will cause click failures for users landing on the credit card banner and a cross-brand spacer asset. Mixed-content HTTP image references and 38 images without alt text are the dominant rendering/accessibility issues.

---

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**

**BROKEN (403) — user-impacting:**
- `https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618...` — credit card apply/buy CTA (`cm_ite=PB_header_nonmember`). Users clicking the Key Rewards banner hit a dead end.
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` — cross-brand West Elm asset embedded in a Pottery Barn send; returns 403 and is HTTP.

**Tracking links (25 skipped by probe):** All route through `click.e.potterybarn.com` — structurally correct, not probed. No issues confirmed.

---

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**

**Mixed-content HTTP image sources (3 confirmed, will be blocked in most clients):**
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`

All three also appear as tracking/open-pixel assets:
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true` — open-tracking pixel served over HTTP; will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients, silently under-counting opens.

**Alt text gaps:** 38 images are missing `alt` attributes, including all product slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_18.jpg`), all in-stock furniture product images, and footer slices. In image-blocking environments (Outlook default, corporate proxies), the email renders as a blank column of broken icons with no readable fallback.

**CSS artifact in `<style>` block:** PHP-style `header()` calls appear verbatim inside a `<style>` tag:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
```
These are server-side PHP statements that were never executed — they leaked into the template as literal text. They are invalid CSS, will cause parse errors in strict renderers, and expose an unrendered code artifact to any recipient who views source.

**`<title>` is empty** — assistive technologies read the title on email open; a blank title gives screen readers nothing to announce.
