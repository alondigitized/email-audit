# Nostalgic holiday collabs are back!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's first genuine movie-nostalgia licensing play I've logged, and it's a real creative swing after two weeks of quilts, throws, and design-service pitches — "Holiday Movie Magic" leads with three named collabs (National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation, Elf, Peanuts/"A Charlie Brown Christmas"), each with its own hero image, licensed logo lockup, and themed tablescape or pillow set. The licensing angle is the whole story here — Griswold-plaid dinnerware, "OMG Santa! I know him!" Elf pillows, and Peanuts holiday china are specific, ownable, and give the send a personality none of the recent textile crawls had.
- Where it falls apart is length and focus: after the three collabs there's a "Recommended Just For You" product grid, a Halloween Shop banner, a Design Crew pitch, an app download push, and a credit-card rewards pitch — five different asks stacked below the actual news. By the time a reader scrolls past the Halloween banner, the holiday-collab hook is long forgotten. It's a strong hero buried under a kitchen-sink email.
- Verdict: best creative concept from this brand in weeks, undercut by scope creep — trim it to the three collabs plus one CTA and this is an 8; as sent, it's a 6.

## What's working

- Three distinct, recognizable film/TV licenses (Christmas Vacation, Elf, Peanuts) each get their own hero shot and logo lockup — instant nostalgia recognition.
- Copy is specific and character-driven ("Griswold's), not generic ("OMG Santa! I know him!" nods to the actual movie line).
- Deep merlot/holiday-red header band with white serif type reads premium and seasonal, distinct from PB's usual neutral palette.
- Clear repeated "SHOP NOW" CTA under each collab plus a bolder "EXPLORE THE CHRISTMAS SHOP" close-out button for the section.
- No render bugs — images, logos, and text all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- The email doesn't stop after the holiday story — it tacks on a full product grid, a Halloween Shop banner, a Design Crew ad, and an app/credit-card pitch, diluting the one genuinely strong idea.
- No price or discount anywhere in the collab section — three collabs, zero visible offer, so there's no urgency to act now vs. browse later.
- Subject says "collabs are back," implying a recurring or returning promo, but nothing in the body says these are back (vs. new) or for a limited time.
- The Halloween Shop banner sitting directly under a Christmas-movie push creates seasonal whiplash — Halloween and "Holiday Movie Magic" compete for the same scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut everything after "EXPLORE THE CHRISTMAS SHOP" for this send — the recommended-products grid, Halloween banner, Design Crew, app, and credit card pitches should be separate emails. One story, one CTA, much higher completion rate.
- 2. Add a concrete offer or urgency line to the header band (e.g., "Shop before they sell out" or a ship-by-Christmas date) — three licensed collabs with zero price/urgency signal reads like a lookbook, not a promo.
- 3. Clarify "back" in the subject with a body callout ("Your favorite holiday collabs just returned") so the promise in the subject line pays off visually, not just implicitly.
- 4. Move "EXPLORE THE CHRISTMAS SHOP" up as a sticky/repeated CTA right under the hero band too, so mobile users who don't scroll the full three collabs still get one clear action.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Griswolds, Elf & Peanuts are back for the holidays`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your favorite holiday movies, now on your table`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Christmas Vacation, Elf & Peanuts collections just landed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the movie-inspired collabs before they're gone`

## 1. Overview

This is Pottery Barn's first genuine movie-nostalgia licensing play I've logged, and it's a real creative swing after two weeks of quilts, throws, and design-service pitches — "Holiday Movie Magic" leads with three named collabs (National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation, Elf, Peanuts/"A Charlie Brown Christmas"), each with its own hero image, licensed logo lockup, and themed tablescape or pillow set. - 48 tracking/click-redirect links present (`click.e.potterybarn.com`, `clickcp.potterybarn.com`) — not resolvable via HTTP probe per QA tooling, so destination validity is unconfirmed.
- Open/tracking pixels present and functioning as expected: `d.turn.com/r/dd/...`, `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` — note the persona/recipient email is embedded in plaintext in the pixel URL query string.
- The `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel loads over `http://`, not `https://` — non-HTTPS may be blocked by mail clients and exposes the query string (including recipient email) in cleartext.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 40+ `<img>` elements missing `alt` text across header, hero, product slices, and footer blocks (e.g. `slices_01.jpg`–`slices_18.jpg`, product images like `remmy-swivel-chair-1-l.jpg`, `banks-extendable-dining-table-76-128-1-l.jpg`, `cayman-platform-bed-1-l.jpg`). This affects screen-reader accessibility and degrades the fallback experience when images are blocked.
- Two additional non-HTTPS image sources: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — may be blocked by clients enforcing mixed-content/HTTPS-only image loading.
- Head contains a `<link>` to Google Fonts (`fonts.googleapis.com`) — most email clients strip external stylesheet links and ignore `@font-face`, so this has no effect in rendering and is dead weight.
- Stray, non-functional CSS in the `<style>` block: literal PHP-style `header(...)` calls (`header('Content-Type: image/jpeg')`, cache-control headers) sitting inside a CSS `<style>` tag — inert artifact from template reuse, has no effect but indicates unclean HTML assembly.
- Responsive breakpoints rely on `@media` queries only (700px/650px/480px) with no MSO/Outlook-specific fallback beyond basic `mso` OfficeDocumentSettings — legacy Outlook desktop (non-MSO-aware clients) will render at fixed table widths.
