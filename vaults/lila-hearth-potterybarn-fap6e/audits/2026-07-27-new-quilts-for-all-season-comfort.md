# New quilts for all-season comfort
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn back on the discount lever again, but with a cleaner single-category wrapper than most of its July sends — everything here is bedding/quilts, not a grab-bag. The top banner ("Summer Sale: Up to 70% off, 100s of new deals added!") is the same generic sale creative the brand has run repeatedly this month (see the 7/24 and 7/26 "Up to 70% off" sends), and it's slightly at odds with the subject line's specific "new quilts" promise — the first thing you see isn't quilts, it's a blanket sale banner.
- Once you scroll past that, the execution is genuinely strong: "Heirloom Layers" hero with a "Quilts Ship Free" badge, a well-shot lifestyle image, and a tight cascade of named products (Cordelia Toile, Belgian Flax Linen Floral Stitch, Lydia Palampore Embroidered, Cross Stitch Velvet) each with a distinct callout badge (Bestseller, New) and a "Shop the Look" CTA. That's good merchandising discipline.
- The one thing that nags: quilts are a layered, cold-weather comfort item, and this is landing in a "Summer Sale" wrapper in late July. "All-season comfort" is doing a lot of work to paper over that mismatch. Net: solid execution, generic top banner, seasonally odd timing. Score it a competent mid-to-high performer, not a standout.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product cascade — each quilt gets its own hero shot, name, one-line descriptor, and CTA, with no visual clutter.
- "Bestseller" and "New" badges add lightweight social proof and differentiation between products.
- "Quilts Ship Free" is a concrete, low-friction incentive placed right in the hero.
- Render is bug-free — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum.
- Loyalty tie-in (Rewards Visa, 10% back) and Affirm financing are both visible in the footer, giving repeat shoppers a reason to click beyond the discount.

## What's weak

- Top banner is a generic "Up to 70% off" sale block, not a quilts-specific hero — it fights the subject line for attention instead of reinforcing it.
- "Summer Sale" positioning for heavy layered quilts in late July is a seasonal mismatch; "all-season" reads like a workaround rather than a real hook.
- Primary CTA copy ("Shop Now") is generic — the rubric's own bad example — even though downstream CTAs ("Shop the Look," "Shop All Quilts") are better.
- No pricing, sizing, or specific discount percentage on any individual quilt — just vague descriptors like "In 40+ beautiful hues" and "Fine embroidered details."
- No deadline or end date anywhere for the sale, so there's no credible urgency to act now versus next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the quilts hero, not the generic sale banner — put "Heirloom Layers" / Cordelia Toile Quilt at the very top so the first thing a subscriber sees matches what got them to open. Move the "Up to 70% off" banner below it or fold the discount into the quilt hero copy directly ("Quilts up to 70% off, ships free").
- 2. Add a real deadline ("Sale ends Sunday") somewhere near the top offer — right now nothing in the render suggests this ever ends.
- 3. Put an actual price or discount range on at least the hero quilt (e.g., "$89–$249, was up to $349") — specificity converts better than "40+ beautiful hues."
- 4. Swap "Shop Now" for a category-specific CTA on the hero, matching the specificity already used lower in the email ("Shop New Quilts").
- 5. Reconsider the seasonal frame — if this is genuinely a summer send, lean into "lightweight quilts for warm nights" rather than "all-season," which undercuts the pitch.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Quilts up to 70% off, ships free`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New this week: layered quilts for cooler nights`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bestselling and new quilt styles, now up to 70% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping on every quilt in this drop — ends Sunday.`

# Technical Audit: Pottery Barn — "New quilts for all-season comfort"

## 1. Technical Summary
The email renders via a standard MSO/Outlook-compatible table layout but ships with broken/leaked server-side code in its `<style>` block and multiple insecure (HTTP) asset and tracking-pixel URLs, one of which transmits the recipient's email address in cleartext.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- All primary CTAs route through the click-tracking redirector `click.e.potterybarn.com/u/?qs=...` (opaque base64-style payload) — 59 tracking/redirect links total were skipped by automated HTTP probing since they are redirect-domain wrapped, so final destinations could not be verified in this pass.
- Multiple open-tracking pixels are present: `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (1x1 tracking beacon), `clickcp.potterybarn.com/o/febdd897-...?mi_mid=019fa0df-0c00-7000-82d5-db4201bf58de` (open-tracking with ESP message ID), and `svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=...`.
- **Security issue**: the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel is loaded over plain `http://`, not `https://`, and its query string carries the recipient's email address in cleartext (`sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`). This transmits PII unencrypted over the network.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid/leaked server code in `<style>` block**: the client-styling `<style>` tag contains literal PHP output, not CSS:
  ```
  header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
  header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
  header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
  header("Pragma: no-cache");
  ```
  This is invalid CSS (browsers/email clients will ignore it as malformed declarations), but its presence indicates PHP template output leaked into the compiled HTML — a build/templating defect on the sending side, not a rendering risk to the recipient.
- 50+ images across the template are missing `alt` attributes (all `slices_*.jpg` divider images, product images such as `farmhouse-platform-bed-l.jpg`, `belgian-flax-linen-duvet-cover-2-l.jpg`, banner images, and tracking pixels) — screen readers and images-blocked clients will show nothing in place of these.
- Four images load over insecure `http://` instead of `https://`, which many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy) will block or flag as mixed content:
  - `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
  - `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
  - `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/...` (tracking pixel, see §2)
- Document uses the legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype with MSO/VML conditional comments — expected for a cross-client-compatible ESP template, not a defect on its own.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- Recipient email address (`lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`) is correctly interpolated into the customer-service link (`potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html?email=...`) and the open-tracking beacons — no unresolved merge tags (e.g., `{{FIRST_NAME}}`, `%%EMAIL%%`) were found in the visible HTML.
- As noted in §2/§3, one merge-token instance (`sv_sveme=`) is exposed over unencrypted HTTP, which is a data-handling concern rather than a broken-token issue.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — one-click unsubscribe via mail client UI is not available (or was stripped by the relay); cannot confirm RFC 2369 compliance from header data alone.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe is not confirmed present.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified from available headers.
- Note: all three warnings may reflect the AgentMail relay not surfacing/forwarding these headers rather than the sender omitting them — recommend verifying directly against Pottery Barn's raw ESP send headers before treating as a sender-side compliance gap.
- Body-level unsubscribe link was not visible in the truncated HTML excerpt provided; full footer HTML would need review to confirm CAN-SPAM physical-address and unsubscribe-link requirements are met.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- All click-through links use the `click.e.potterybarn.com` redirector with an opaque `qs=` payload rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs, so campaign/UTM parameter alignment with landing pages cannot be verified from the HTML alone (parameters are presumably injected server-side on redirect).
- The customer-service link resolves directly to `potterybarn.com/customer-service/coreemail.html` (bypassing the tracking redirector) — inconsistent tracking treatment between transactional/support links and marketing CTAs, though this is a common and acceptable pattern.
- `meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"` is set, which will strip referrer data on any outbound click that doesn't go through the tracking redirector — for links that do bypass the redirector (like the coreemail.html link above), landing-page analytics will not see email as the traffic source.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the leaked PHP `header()` calls from the `<style>` block in the ESP template source — indicates a broken template compile step that should be fixed at the source, not just suppressed by browser CSS-parsing tolerance.
2. Move the `svopn.potterybarn.com` open-tracking pixel to HTTPS and confirm the email-address query parameter is not logged/stored in plaintext access logs.
3. Migrate the three remaining `http://` image assets (`spacer_0.gif`, `Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`, `slices_02.jpg` on edm.potterybarn.com/2022 path) to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking in strict mail clients.
4. Add `alt` text to all content-bearing images (product shots, banners); decorative divider/spacer images can use `alt=""` to satisfy accessibility checkers without adding noise for screen readers.
5. Confirm List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results directly against the raw ESP send (not just the AgentMail-relayed copy) to rule out a relay-side header-stripping issue versus an actual compliance gap.
6. Reduce plain-text-part URL density (currently 70%+ of the plain-text body is URLs) to lower spam-filter risk scoring.
