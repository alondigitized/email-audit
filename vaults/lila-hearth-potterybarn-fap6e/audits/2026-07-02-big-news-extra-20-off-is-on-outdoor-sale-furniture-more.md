# Big news! EXTRA 20% off is ON + Outdoor sale ⛱️ Furniture & more
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifteenth-plus send in Pottery Barn's July 4th campaign — and the second one on July 2nd alone. The first July 2nd send ("July 4th deals, going fast⚡EXTRA 20% OFF") at least tried the angle of fleeting inventory. This email opens with "Big news!" — which is not a claim that survives five seconds of scrutiny after two weeks of near-identical promo emails. There is no news here. The extra 20% off has been running continuously. The outdoor living hero is the only genuinely fresh element in the entire send, and it gets undercut immediately by a "Recommended Just For You" section surfacing bedroom furniture.
- The render itself is clean and the discount hierarchy is legible. But the email has a category identity crisis: the subject and hero push outdoor living, then the personalized recs below the fold go somewhere else entirely. A subscriber who was actually inspired by the patio lifestyle photo will click "Shop the Sale" and land in a general sale page, not an outdoor-curated destination. The creative is doing two jobs and accomplishing neither fully.
- Judgment: this is a competent, well-designed email released into a fatigue context so severe that "Big news!" reads as parody. The outdoor editorial focus was the right pivot; it just needed to carry all the way through.

## What's working

- **Outdoor living hero is the strongest creative in the entire July 4th run** — lifestyle patio photography is visually aspirational and seasonally coherent.
- **Dual-offer structure is scannable** — the "Extra 20% off" banner and the "Up to 70% Off" callout are visually distinct and easy to parse at a glance.
- **"Recommended Just For You" section adds personalization signal** — individual products with prices give a subscriber something specific to evaluate.
- **Clean render, no bugs** — no overlapping elements, broken images, or layout breaks visible anywhere in the email.

## What's weak

- **"Big news!" is empty hype** — this opener is earned exactly once; deploying it on send fifteen of a two-week campaign destroys credibility.
- **Recommended products are indoor/bedroom when the hero is outdoor** — the mismatch breaks the narrative and wastes the best creative in the send.
- **Subject is 63 characters and uses EXTRA in all-caps** — over mobile-optimal length and trips mild spam heuristics.
- **No time-bounded deadline visible in subject or hero** — after two weeks of identical urgency framing, no subscriber believes the sale ends soon unless a specific date is prominent.
- **Second email of the day compounds fatigue** — even a well-executed send would suffer arriving alongside the earlier July 2nd blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the outdoor focus total** — replace the bedroom "Recommended Just For You" block with curated outdoor furniture recs. The hero earns a click; the below-fold recs should convert it, not redirect it.
- 2. **Kill "Big news!"** — this subject opener costs credibility with every subscriber who's been in the funnel for two weeks. Lead with the specific offer and category instead.
- 3. **Pin a hard expiry date** — "Extra 20% off ends July 4th, 11:59 PM" is what restores urgency after saturation. Vague urgency is no urgency at this cadence.
- 4. **Collapse the two discount claims into one clear message** — "Up to 70% off + Extra 20%" requires mental math. "Up to 70% off outdoor furniture, ends July 4th" is one scannable promise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor sale ends July 4th — extra 20% off patio furniture`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off outdoor living — sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Seating, dining, fire pits and more — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your patio refresh at up to 70% off, extra 20% on select styles.`

## Technical Audit — Pottery Barn "EXTRA 20% off + Outdoor Sale" (2026-07-02)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard 403 failures (one on the primary credit card CTA, one on a cross-brand asset) directly break user journeys. A PHP server-side header block was accidentally embedded inside a `<style>` tag, confirming a templating pipeline error. 38 images lack alt text, and three image sources are served over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Primary CTA broken (403)**
The "Apply & Buy" credit card banner link returns HTTP 403:
`https://clickcp.potterybarn.com/c/da01b407-b21c-4618-a71b-f83bef0dd682?brandCode=POTTERYBARNCB&...`
This is a revenue-bearing CTA. 403 suggests an expired or misconfigured campaign token (`cp_cid=4fcb68173ced840b`).

**FAIL — Cross-brand spacer image broken (403)**
`http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif` returns 403 and is also served over HTTP. This is a West Elm template artifact carried into a Pottery Barn send. If used as a layout spacer it may cause rendering gaps.

**INFO — 25 click-redirect links unverified**
All CTAs route through `click.e.potterybarn.com`. Destination URL parameters (including UTM chains) were not probed. See Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CRITICAL — PHP code injected into `<style>` block**
The final `<style>` block in `<head>` contains raw PHP `header()` calls that were never processed by the template engine:
```css
header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');
header("Cache-Control: no-store, no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0");
header("Cache-Control: post-check=0, pre-check=0", false);
header("Pragma: no-cache");
```
These are not valid CSS. They will be silently ignored by email clients but confirm a broken template preprocessing step — this code was intended for a dynamic image server, not the email `<head>`.

**HIGH — 38 images missing `alt` text**
Every content image and most footer images have empty or absent `alt` attributes, including primary hero images (`S26_Top_July4Sale_NoUrgency.jpg`, all `slices_01–09.jpg` outdoor sale images, all footer `slices_01–18.jpg`). Email clients that block images by default will render a blank email with no text fallback.

**MEDIUM — 3 image sources served over HTTP**
Will be blocked or flagged by Gmail, Outlook 2016+, and iOS Mail when loaded under HTTPS contexts:
- `http://edm.westelm.com/we/evergreen/spacer_0.gif`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg`
- `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg`
- `http://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&...` (open tracking pixel — also HTTP)

**LOW — Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` renders as blank in browser previews and some accessibility tools.

**LOW — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility regression.

**LOW — Mixed seasonal asset paths**
Content images reference `S26_Sale_MemorialDay_Outdoor/` (a May campaign path) inside a July 4 email. A footer banner references `F26_Bottom_NewArrivals.jpg` (a Fall 2026 asset). These may be intentional asset reuse but are worth flagging for QA.
