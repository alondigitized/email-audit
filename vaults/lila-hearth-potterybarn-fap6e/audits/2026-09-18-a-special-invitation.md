# A special invitation 💌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a service email, not a discount email — "RSVP for Your Free Design Services" leads with a free perk (styling sessions, room refreshes, makeovers) instead of a percentage-off. That's a genuinely different lane from the warehouse-sale and catalog sends that have dominated this brand's recent cadence (2026-09-18 warehouse+outdoor, 2026-09-17 warehouse+catalog, 2026-09-16 60% off). Structurally it's clean and on-brand: a floral hero framing "what/when/where," a shop-by-style band, two free in-store class RSVPs, then the usual recommended-products grid and loyalty/app footer modules.
- The problem is the offer itself is vague where it needs to be concrete. "Choose a time—but spots go fast!" injects urgency with zero specificity (how many spots, what markets, what does virtual actually look like), and the single CTA "BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" is honest but generic. Given this brand's recent history of leaning on hard numbers (60% off), this send stands out for being soft-sell — which is refreshing for cadence variety, but it needed one more concrete hook (a specific incentive for booking, a named designer, a deadline) to convert curiosity into action.
- Judgement: solid execution, mediocre payoff — a well-designed "free consultation" email that doesn't close.

## What's working

- Hero framing ("what / when / where") is clear and scannable — answers the three questions a reader has before booking a service.
- Single, unambiguous primary CTA button ("BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT") sits directly under the offer with no scroll needed.
- Floral illustrated header differentiates this from the photo-heavy warehouse-sale sends, giving it a "special occasion" feel that matches "A special invitation."
- Two dated, RSVP-able in-store classes (Sept 27, Oct 4) add a second, lower-commitment conversion path beyond the main design-service booking.
- Clean, no render bugs — grid images load, category tiles align, footer modules (app, credit card, Affirm) are intact.

## What's weak

- "Spots go fast" urgency is unsupported — no count, no deadline, no scarcity signal a reader can act on.
- The "Recommended Just For You" product grid (curtain rods, dining table, bed, towel, rug, chair) has no visible link back to the design-service offer — it dilutes the single-CTA focus the hero set up.
- No visible incentive tied to booking (no "$X off your first design project," no gift card, no named designer) — it's a plain ask with no reason to act *now* versus *later*.
- Subject emoji "💌" plus "A special invitation" is generic warmth without telling the reader what's inside — undersells the free-service hook that's actually the strongest asset here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete incentive or scarcity number to the hero (e.g., "Book by Sept 30 for a $50 credit toward your project" or "Only 200 virtual sessions available this month") — this single change would do the most to convert curiosity into bookings.
- 2. Pull the subject line and preview toward the free-service hook instead of vague "invitation" language, since that's the differentiated asset versus this brand's recent discount-heavy run.
- 3. Either link the "Recommended Just For You" grid items into the design-service flow (e.g., "see this styled in your free session") or move it below the in-store classes so it doesn't compete with the primary CTA.
- 4. Name a designer or show a before/after room photo to make "free design services" feel tangible rather than abstract.

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC-generated Pottery Barn promotional email; core rendering markup is sound, but automated QA flags gaps in unsubscribe/authentication header capture and widespread missing alt text.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 46 tracking/click-redirect links (click.e.potterybarn.com, svopn.potterybarn.com, secure.leadback.advertising.com, d.turn.com) were skipped by HTTP probe per QA — link validity unconfirmed, not evidence of a problem.
- Pixel/beacon URLs embed the recipient's email in plaintext query params, e.g. `https://svopn.potterybarn.com/?sv_cid=0053_00597&sv_emopen=true&sv_sveme=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app` and `coreemail.html?email=lila-hearth-potterybarn-fap6e@etell.app`. This exposes the recipient address to third-party ad/tracking domains (turn.com, advertising.com) via referrer/URL leakage.
- One third-party tracking pixel served over plain HTTP: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content, may be blocked by mail clients enforcing HTTPS.
- Header CTA link uses a tracking-wrapped URL (`click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=ABB7...`) consistent with SFMC/Salesforce click tracking — no issue found.

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- All content images (slices_01–18.jpg across two campaigns, product shots, footer banner, spacer.gif) are missing `alt` attributes — confirmed in HTML source (`alt=""` or absent) and QA output. Image-only body layout with no alt text means screen-reader users and blocked-image inboxes get no content.
- Legacy/dead code present in `<style>` block: PHP-style `header('Content-Type: image/jpeg');` and cache-control header() calls are embedded inside a CSS `<style>` tag as literal text — these are inert (not executed, not valid CSS) but indicate a copy-paste artifact from a template that should be cleaned up.
- Standard Outlook/Apple Mail reset rules present (`mso-*`, `.ExternalClass`, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`) — no issues found.
- Preheader uses proper hidden-div + zero-width-space padding pattern — no issues found.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No AMPscript/merge tokens visible in the truncated source (`<!-- begin dynamic ampScript slot -->` is empty in this render) — cannot confirm whether personalization fields resolved correctly upstream; flag as unverified rather than a defect.
- Recipient email is hardcoded into tracking pixel URLs (see Section 2) rather than passed via a secure token — worth noting under both tracking and compliance.

5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- List-Unsubscribe header not found (QA WARN) — may be a relay-side capture gap (AgentMail) rather than absence in the original send; cannot confirm from HTML alone since unsubscribe is typically header-based/footer-link based, not in the truncated body shown.
- List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not found — same caveat; if genuinely absent, this is a Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirement gap.
- Authentication-Results header not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified; likely a relay capture limitation rather than a deliverability defect, but should be confirmed against raw headers at the source MTA if possible.
- Physical mailing address / footer unsubscribe link not visible in truncated HTML — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer compliance from available source.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Outbound links use SFMC click-tracking wrappers (`click.e.potterybarn.com/?qs=...`) rather than direct UTM-tagged URLs — standard for this ESP; final landing page attribution isn't verifiable without following the redirect chain (links were skipped by HTTP probe, so destination/landing-page alignment cannot be confirmed either way).

7. Recommendations
- Add descriptive `alt` text to all content images (slices_*.jpg, product shots) at minimum for accessibility and image-blocked rendering; decorative spacers/pixels can use `alt=""` intentionally (currently they have no attribute at all).
- Upgrade the two HTTP-only image sources to HTTPS to avoid mixed-content blocking.
- Remove the stray PHP `header()` call artifacts from the `<style>` block — dead code, harmless but should be cleaned from the template source.
- Confirm at the source MTA (not just via relay) whether List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results headers are actually present, since the relay may simply not be forwarding them.
- Consider whether embedding the recipient's raw email address in third-party tracking pixel URLs (turn.com, advertising.com, leadback.advertising.com) is intended — this pattern exposes PII to third parties via URL/referrer.
