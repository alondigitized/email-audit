# FREE design services✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine break from the burgundy "Up to 70% off" hammer Pottery Barn has run four sends in a row — the hero here is "RSVP for Free Design Services," a service offer, not another discount banner. But it doesn't fully commit: within one scroll it pivots straight into "EXPLORE LABOR DAY DEALS" with the same 4-tile percentage-off grid and burgundy "SHOP ALL SALE" button seen in every recent send, so the differentiation is front-loaded and then abandoned.
- The subject "FREE design services✨" is honest and on-brand for anyone furnishing a home, and the soft "what/when/where" layout on the hero card is a nice, editorial touch that's more helpful than another sale banner. The in-store design class ("Styling Your Living Room," Sept 13) adds a real, dated, local event — good specificity for anyone near a store.
- Net: a well-intentioned change of pace that gets undercut by defaulting back into the same sale furniture the brand has already sent repeatedly this week. Judgment: solid subject, mediocre follow-through — 6/10 overall execution.

## What's working

- Hero clearly breaks pattern from the last four "up to 70% off" sends — a free design-consultation offer with a clean what/when/where breakdown.
- Single unambiguous CTA button ("BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT") sits right under the offer, no scrolling needed.
- The in-store "Styling Your Living Room" class has a specific date/time (Sept 13, Sunday, 10AM) and its own RSVP button — concrete, not vague.
- "RECOMMENDED JUST FOR YOU" product grid shows real products with names and no broken images or placeholder text.
- No render bugs — text renders cleanly, images load, layout is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The email pivots from a design-services pitch straight into the same Labor Day sale grid and burgundy "SHOP ALL SALE" button used in the 08-31/09-01 sends — it doesn't stay differentiated.
- "up to 60-70% off" tiles repeat without any new numbers or products versus prior sends this week, so a subscriber who opened the last four sees the same thing again.
- Two competing CTAs stacked close together ("BOOK A FREE APPOINTMENT" and "SHOP ALL SALE") dilute what this email is actually asking the reader to do.
- No visible loyalty/rewards tie-in to the design-services offer itself (rewards card promo appears later, generic and unconnected).
- "Recommended Just For You" personalization claim isn't visibly substantiated — items look like a standard best-seller grid, not tailored picks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Commit to the design-services angle for the whole email — cut or shrink the Labor Day sale grid, since Pottery Barn already sent that grid four times this week; use the freed space for before/after room photos or client testimonials from real design consults.
- 2. Drop to one primary CTA. If design services is the hero, don't also push "SHOP ALL SALE" with equal visual weight — make it a smaller secondary link.
- 3. Make "Recommended Just For You" feel earned — add a one-line reason (e.g., "because you viewed X") or swap for design-service-adjacent items (fabric swatches, room-planning tools).
- 4. Tie the rewards card / Affirm blocks to the design-services offer (e.g., "Financing available for your design project") instead of leaving them generic filler at the bottom.

- Third-party ad/retargeting pixels present: `secure.leadback.advertising.com/adcedge/lb?site=695501...` and `d.turn.com/r/dd/id/...` (Turn/Verizon Media conversion pixel) — third-party tracking beacons embedded in the email body.
- One legacy insecure image reference: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` (also flagged in Rendering section).

3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 27 `<img>` elements missing `alt` text, including all primary content slices (`slices_01.jpg` through `slices_08.jpg`) and product images (curtain rod bracket, dining tables, platform beds, swivel chair) — screen readers will announce these as unlabeled or fall back to filenames.
- Tracking/pixel images also lack `alt=""` — while non-critical visually, unlabeled 1x1 pixels are lower priority than content-image alt gaps above.
- Two images served over plain HTTP instead of HTTPS: `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/thekey/CC_Banner/PB/Foot_NonMem_PB.jpg` and `http://edm.potterybarn.com/pb/2022/0311_Key_CC_Email_BrandBanner/slices_02.jpg` — mixed content may be blocked or flagged by mail clients enforcing HTTPS.
