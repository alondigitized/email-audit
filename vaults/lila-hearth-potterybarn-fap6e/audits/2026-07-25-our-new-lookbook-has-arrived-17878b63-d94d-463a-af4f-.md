# Our new lookbook has arrived
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Pottery Barn's seasonal editorial move — the July "Fall Lookbook" preview that retailers run to reset attention after a stretch of discount-heavy sends. Structurally it's clean and well-executed: a strong hero, three cleanly separated style stories (Rustic Lodge, Heritage Autumn, Indigo Blues), a genuinely useful "Recommended Just For You" product grid, and a loyalty/financing block before the footer. Visually this is one of the stronger builds in the recent run — no render bugs, clear section breaks, consistent photography style.
- The problem is the premise, not the execution. Running "THE FALL LOOKBOOK" directly under a "Summer Sale Up to 70% Off" banner on July 25 sends a mixed seasonal signal — it's simultaneously trying to clear summer inventory and get subscribers excited about fall, and neither message fully lands because of the other. The subject line also undersells what's actually a fairly rich, multi-collection email — "Our new lookbook has arrived" reads as filler when the body has three named collections and a real discount.
- Judgement: a well-built but conceptually unfocused send. It'll perform fine on click-through for anyone who opens it (lots of specific "Shop the Look" and "Recommended for You" CTAs), but the subject line is the weak link — it won't pull opens the way "1600+ NEW arrivals" or "NEW artisanal vases" did in the same week.

## What's working

- Hero delivers a clear, single focal point — "THE FALL LOOKBOOK" over a warm, styled lodge interior, with "GET INSPIRED" as an unmissable button.
- Three collection stories (Rustic Lodge, Heritage Autumn, Indigo Blues) are cleanly segmented with consistent typography and each has its own "Shop the Look" CTA — easy to scan and pick a lane.
- "Recommended Just For You" is a real personalized product grid (curtain rod, towel, chair, rug, curtain, quilt) — this is the strongest click-driver in the email since it's specific inventory, not just a category link.
- Loyalty and financing are surfaced together near the bottom (10% Back in Rewards, Affirm buy-now-pay-later) — good sequencing for a subscriber close to converting.
- No layout breakage, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- "Summer Sale Up to 70% Off" at the very top directly contradicts "THE FALL LOOKBOOK" hero immediately below it — the email can't decide if it's clearing summer stock or launching fall.
- Subject line ("Our new lookbook has arrived") is generic and undersells the content — no mention of fall, no mention of the sale, no named collection.
- A second, unrelated "Up to 60% off Outdoor" banner appears near the bottom, adding a third competing offer thread (summer sale / fall lookbook / outdoor sale) in one email.
- No models or lifestyle human presence anywhere — every shot is an empty styled room, so there's nothing here to signal who this is "for" beyond a generic home-decor buyer.
- Given the brand's cadence this week (new arrivals sends on 7/22, 7/23 x2, 7/24 x3), this is another "new stuff to look at" email in a very short window — fatigue risk is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one seasonal story per send.** Pull the "Summer Sale" and "Outdoor 60% off" banners out of this email entirely — they're undercutting the fall launch. Let the lookbook stand alone, and run the summer clearance as its own send.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to name the actual content** — right now it's the vaguest line in a week of subject lines that have been doing real work (e.g., "1600+ NEW arrivals," "NEW artisanal vases").
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Fall Lookbook: 3 new looks for your home`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rustic Lodge, Heritage Autumn & Indigo Blues — new for fall`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Layered color, heritage leather, and crisp plaid — get the full look.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three new fall collections, plus picks made for your home.`
- 3. **Move "Recommended Just For You" higher** — it's the most concrete, highest-intent content in the email and it's currently buried after three full collection sections.
- 4. **Add a credible deadline** to whichever single offer survives ("Summer Sale ends Sunday") — right now nothing in the email has real urgency.
- 5. **Put a person in at least one hero shot.** Every image is an empty room; one lifestyle shot with a person would help this read as "for someone" rather than a pure catalog spread.
