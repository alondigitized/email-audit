# FREE Limited-Edition Stickers When You Shop Unreal Icons! 🧡
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a hybrid promotional email — it's doubling as both a GWP ("free limited-edition stickers") and an app-download push for the newly launched UNREAL Highlighter. The sticker offer is the hook, but it's undercut immediately by an ambiguous "Shop Any Unreal Icon" mechanic that requires decoding (which SKUs qualify? what's the spend threshold?). The hero section feels like two emails stitched together: the sticker promo up top, then a reprised "Content is TOO HOT to load" creative module from the June 11 send, then an app feature strip, then social proof, then a footer. There's no clean narrative thread.
- Charlotte Tilbury continues to lean on the UNREAL Highlighter launch (now four emails deep since June 11), but the creative rotation is showing strain. Recycling the phone/overheating creative from June 11 inside this email makes the brand feel like it's running out of fresh angles. The sticker offer is genuinely charming and on-brand, but it's buried in visual complexity rather than given the room it deserves.
- Net judgment: a 5/10 email. The offer is real and the brand is trusted, but the execution is cluttered, the mechanic is unclear, and the creative fatigue is visible.

## What's working

- **Sticker GWP is a playful, distinctive hook** — limited-edition physical merch feels premium and different from the standard "free sample" offers Charlotte Tilbury usually runs.
- **Subject line is specific and concrete** — "FREE Limited-Edition Stickers When You Shop Unreal Icons" tells you exactly what the deal is before you open.
- **Strong brand consistency** — rose-gold palette, Charlotte Tilbury signature font, and "Look Good, Feel Good, Be Beautiful" footer all land cleanly with no render bugs.
- **App CTA block is clean** — the phone mockup + "Shop Now On The App" button is visually clear and well-executed.

## What's weak

- **The sticker offer mechanics are invisible** — no spend threshold, no qualifying product list, no redemption instructions visible in the hero. "Shop Any Unreal Icon" is vague.
- **Creative fatigue is on display** — the "Content is too hot to load / phone overheating" creative panel is a near-copy of the June 11 email. A subscriber who opened that one will recognize the reuse.
- **Dual-CTA confusion** — "GET YOUR FREE GIFT" (website) and "Shop Now On The App" (app) compete directly. The email can't decide which channel it's optimizing for.
- **Visual hierarchy is diffuse** — the eye has no clear landing point. The sticker pod, the app phone, and the "DISCOVER THE MAGIC" feature strip all fight for attention at the same visual weight.
- **Fine print at the bottom is dense** — the Terms block is small and long, which undercuts the "offer feels honest" signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Clarify the sticker mechanic in the hero** — Add a single line: "Spend $X+ on any Unreal Icon to unlock your free stickers" directly under the headline. Remove the ambiguity and the reader's hesitation disappears.
- 2. **Pick one channel CTA and commit** — If this is an app-push email, drop the "GET YOUR FREE GIFT" web button. If it's a product/GWP email, drop or demote the app module to a secondary strip.
- 3. **Retire the phone-overheating creative** — It ran on June 11. Replace with a close-up lifestyle shot of the actual stickers or the Unreal Highlighter swatched on skin to keep the visual fresh.
- 4. **Tighten the layout to two sections max** — Hero (sticker GWP) → secondary (app download or product grid). The current four-section layout dilutes focus.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $X+, unlock limited-edition stickers — Unreal Icons`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free stickers are waiting — shop Unreal Icons today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop any Unreal Icon and claim your limited-edition sticker gift, while supplies last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free limited-edition stickers with your Unreal Icon order — shop before they're gone.`

**Click tracking:** All links route through `clicks.charlottetilbury.com` (Braze redirect). This is expected, but the final destination URLs and whether UTM parameters survive the redirect cannot be confirmed from the source alone — see §6.

**No issues found** with tracking domain configuration itself.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading will silently fail in Gmail (web):**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<!--<![endif]-->
```
Gmail and many webmail clients strip `<link>` tags from `<head>`. The MSO conditional correctly excludes Outlook, but Gmail/Yahoo will not load this font. Without seeing the `font-family` fallback stack in the CSS, it's unknown whether a system-font fallback is specified. If not, the browser default (typically Times New Roman or serif) will render instead.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (notably some mobile clients) surface the `<title>` value in thread lists or notification previews. Leaving it blank is a minor regression — it should match the subject line.

**Accessibility positives confirmed:**
- `role="presentation"` on all layout tables ✓
- `alt` text present on visible hero image: `alt="NEW! UNREAL HIGHLIGHTER FRESH, HEALTHY GLOW STICK"` ✓
