# 20% OFF Charlotte's Best-Selling Beauty Icons! 💫
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Charlotte Tilbury's second consecutive 20% off email — yesterday's "20% Off Is HERE! ✨" (scored 9/10) launched the sale; today's send reframes the same discount around "Best-Selling Beauty Icons." The pivot to product-centric framing is the right instinct for a follow-up, but execution falls short: no deadline appears anywhere in the email, and without it, there's no reason for a subscriber who already opened yesterday to click today. The offer doesn't escalate; it just repeats.
- The visual execution is clean — polished product grid, Charlotte's trademark rose-gold aesthetic, loyalty-member callout, and a bonus app discovery section. The email packs a lot in without breaking. But "packing a lot in" is also the problem: a dense multi-SKU catalog with no urgency anchor is a browser, not a buyer driver.
- Verdict: a competent catalog-style sale reminder that gets undercut by cadence fatigue (email six in seven days) and the absence of any credible deadline. The sale won't last forever — show me when it ends.

## What's working

- **20% off is prominent immediately** — the hero states the offer in large type with "SHOP WITH 20% OFF" as the first CTA. No hunting required.
- **"Icons" framing is smarter than a generic blast** — naming the bestsellers (Hollywood Flawless Filter, Pillow Talk, etc.) ties the offer to products subscribers already recognize and want.
- **Loyalty-member exclusive section adds segmentation value** — visible differentiation for loyalty members gives this an incremental reason to exist beyond yesterday's blast.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, the layout holds cleanly at this render width.
- **App discovery section** — the charlottetilbury.com + App section and "Unlock & Free" row give non-purchasers a secondary conversion path.

## What's weak

- **No deadline visible anywhere** — without "ends Sunday" or even "while it lasts," the only urgency engine from yesterday evaporates entirely.
- **Product grid is too dense** — six-plus SKUs in a tight grid dilutes focus; the eye has nowhere to land and commit.
- **"20% Off The Icons You've Been Dreaming About…" repeats the hero** — two sections making the same offer in the same email without adding new information is filler.
- **Sixth email in seven days** — a loyal subscriber likely opened yesterday's 9/10 send; this one arrives with identical offer energy and less novelty, making skip/unsubscribe the rational move.
- **Preview text not visible in the render** — if the preheader is leaking technical junk or repeating the subject, that's a missed re-engagement signal for the subscriber who passed on yesterday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline** — this is the single change that would lift response most. "Ends midnight Sunday" justifies the re-send, creates urgency that yesterday's email didn't have, and gives a reason to click now rather than "later" (which means never).
- 2. **Cut the product grid to 3 hero SKUs** — pick the three highest-revenue icons, show them large, link each individually. More focus, more clicks.
- 3. **Drop the redundant mid-email "Icons You've Been Dreaming About" section** — replace it with the deadline callout prominently.
- 4. **Use the loyalty section as the primary CTA** — if loyalty members get a better deal (stacked or extended), lead with that differentiation; it rewards the engaged base and re-activates lapsed members.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Icons Are 20% Off — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pillow Talk, Hollywood Filter: 20% Off Ends Soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final days on the bestsellers in your saved items.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The icons you keep coming back to — now 20% off until Sunday.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Partially verifiable** — links are in the truncated section. From what is visible:

- No link `href` values or UTM strings are present in the visible head/CSS block, so click-tracking coverage cannot be confirmed.
- External font dependency at `https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css` is a live HTTP request from recipient clients. If that asset 404s or is slow, fallback fonts silently activate — not a tracking issue per se, but a deliverability-adjacent infrastructure dependency.

**Action required:** Verify all CTA buttons use tracked redirect URLs (ESP click-wrap or UTM-annotated), not bare `charlottetilbury.com` hrefs.

---

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** (`<title></title>`): Screen readers in some clients (VoiceOver + Apple Mail) fall back to reading the document title. An empty title leaves a blank announcement. Should be set to match the preheader or subject.
- **`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }`**: This Gmail-targeting rule strips underline and forces link color to match surrounding text on all anchors within Gmail's wrapper. Links become visually indistinguishable from body copy unless the template uses explicit button styling on every CTA. Inline color/underline on anchor elements needs to be confirmed for every text link.
- **Font loaded only for non-MSO clients** (`<!--[if !mso]><!-->…<!--<![endif]-->`): Outlook users receive system fallback fonts. This is expected and handled, but the fallback stack is not visible in the truncated source — confirm a named web-safe fallback is declared in the `font-family` property, not just the Google/CT font name.
- **MSO VML declarations present and correct** — `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `<o:AllowPNG/>` are both set; Outlook rendering baseline is properly configured.
- **`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — correct.
- **Image `alt` text** — cannot be verified from the truncated source; must be audited in the full HTML. Product images without `alt` are a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.
