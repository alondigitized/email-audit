# NEW! Exagger-Eyes, Free Pillow Talk + More! 💌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is another "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" digest — the same format and 💌 emoji as the May 30 send (scored 7/10). The email is doing too much at once: a Pillow Talk GWP unlock, a new Exagger-Eyes push (the brand's been running Exagger-Eyes hard since May 27 — nine days straight), Magic Mascara, a mini set, Magic Cream, and at least two lip modules. Nothing breathes. The GWP teased in the subject is the email's strongest asset, but it's buried behind a banner header before it gets to the hero section, and the offer itself doesn't clearly distinguish from the June 3 "FOUR Free Shades" email in the reader's inbox memory.
- The format is recycled, the creative is familiar, and the cadence (sixth email in eleven days) is working against engagement. Individually, several modules are well-shot and on-brand. Stacked nine-deep in a digest, they cancel each other out.
- This send feels like a roundup born from indecision — when you can't choose which product to lead with, you run everything. That's a positioning problem, not a layout problem.

## What's working

- The free Pillow Talk GWP headline is placed early and is the strongest click incentive in the email — "unlock 4 free" is a compelling value hook.
- Product photography is consistently high-quality: the Exagger-Eyes eye close-up, the Magic Cream jar, and the lip close-up all look polished and on-brand.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs are clearly visible, well-contrasted buttons under each module — no ambiguity about what to click.
- Subject line is specific enough to communicate value (names actual products, promises a free item) and comes in under 50 characters.

## What's weak

- Nine-plus modules in a single digest is too many — every product added after the first two dilutes the ones above it.
- "AWARD-WINNING" appears as a headline modifier at least once, which is a placeholder signal, not a differentiating claim — it's been on every CT email for years.
- "+ More!" in the subject is lazy and signals a kitchen-sink send; it erodes trust in the offer precision.
- The Exagger-Eyes product has been the subject of four separate emails since May 27 — featuring it as a "NEW!" hero here strains credibility.
- The digest banner ("Charlotte's Magic Highlights") immediately telegraphs this is a roundup, reducing the urgency of any individual offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one lead offer.** The Pillow Talk GWP is the strongest hook — build the entire email around it. Cut everything except one supporting product (Magic Cream or mascara). Nine modules is a scroll tax most readers won't pay.
- 2. **Retire "Charlotte's Magic Highlights" as the banner.** It's been used twice now and signals "digest" before the reader reaches the offer. Lead with the offer visually.
- 3. **Give the GWP a deadline.** "Unlock 4 free goodies" without any time signal removes urgency entirely. Add "Today only" or "This weekend" to create a credible close.
- 4. **Refresh the Exagger-Eyes framing.** After nine days of continuous coverage, "NEW!" rings hollow. Either omit it from this digest or reframe it as "customer favourite" / "as seen at the Met Gala" to give it a fresh angle.
- 5. Replace "+ More!" in the subject with the third specific thing that matters most to the reader.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Unlock 4 Free Gifts With Your Next Order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `4 luxury Pillow Talk pieces, on us — here's how to claim them`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your free Pillow Talk haul is waiting. Plus the new Exagger-Eyes drop.`
