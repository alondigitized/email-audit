# Summer SALE Has Landed On Site! ☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury sends its fifth or sixth email in six days announcing the same summer sale — this time framed as the sale "landing on site." The creative is clean and familiar: warm hero, the 45% headline, a GWP tier ladder, the app perks strip. Technically competent. Strategically tired.
- The core problem is narrative drift. June 22 declared "45% OFF Starts NOW!" June 23 pushed app early access, and earlier on June 24 CT already sent "Early Access To SALE Ends Soon!" — and now this email claims the sale has freshly "landed on site" as if it's new information. A subscriber who's been reading all week will clock this as a fourth repeat, not a new reason to act. The "arrival" framing falls flat when the sale is two days old.
- The execution holds up — no bugs, the GWP ladder is well-structured, the CTA is clear. The damage is self-inflicted: poor inter-email continuity and a cadence that's now running hot enough to generate unsubscribes.

## What's working

- **45% OFF is the visual anchor**: Largest type in the email, lands immediately after the header — the discount is impossible to miss.
- **GWP tier ladder is genuinely persuasive**: The 1/2/3 gift progression with spend thresholds is clearly visualized and adds a stacking incentive that pure discount emails don't have.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA is unambiguous**: Single button, high contrast, no hunting required.
- **App perks strip adds utility without distraction**: Free shipping, shade finder, savings, and exclusives are compact and reinforce the channel push without burying the lead.
- **Brand visual consistency**: The warm golden palette and product photography are tight and on-brand throughout.

## What's weak

- **"Has Landed On Site" is stale news**: The sale started June 22. This framing positions a two-day-old offer as a debut — it reads as either a duplicate or a channel-confusion error.
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere in the body**: Nothing says when the sale ends. "Landed" implies beginning, not scarcity — so there's zero pressure to click now rather than tomorrow.
- **Two CT emails in one day, fifth in six days**: The cadence is overcooked. Subscribers who opened the June 22 launch have now seen the same 45% sale repositioned three more times.
- **Asterisk on "Iconic Kits\*" with dense fine print**: The extensive small-print block at the bottom is a legibility problem — the asterisk signals exclusions that a subscriber shouldn't have to scroll to discover.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline — this is the single biggest lift available**: "Sale ends Sunday" turns a passive announcement into an action trigger. Without it, every subscriber rationally defers.
- 2. **Distinguish this email from June 22**: If the site launch genuinely expands something that was app-only before, say it: "Now available to everyone — not just the app." That makes the send feel like news rather than a repeat.
- 3. **Lead with the GWP as the hero, not the discount**: In a mailbox already saturated with "X% off" emails, "Get up to 3 free full-size gifts" is a more differentiated hook. The GWP ladder is buried below the fold.
- 4. **Make the CTA more specific**: "Shop Charlotte's Pillow Talk Kit — 45% Off" converts better than "Shop the Sale" because it eliminates the choice paralysis of a generic sale page.
- 5. **Hold the second daily send**: Two emails in one day on the same offer is the fastest way to accelerate list churn. Reserve this slot for a genuine sale-end countdown.
- **Subject Alt A:** `45% Off + 3 Free Gifts — Sale Ends [Day]`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Last Chance: 45% Off Iconic Kits 🌞`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Now live for everyone — plus unlock up to 3 free full-size gifts with your order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The summer sale just opened to all. Grab your Golden Goddess gifts before they're gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font CSS (blocking issue in most clients):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
Gmail (both web and app), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all strip or block external `<link>` stylesheets. This means custom fonts will silently fall through to system fonts in the majority of opens. The conditional `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` correctly skips this for Outlook/Word, but the failure mode for Gmail/Yahoo is silent — verify that fallback `font-family` stacks are defined inline on text elements.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Shows as a blank tab label in webmail browser tabs (Gmail, Outlook.com). Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

**Image alt text — all empty:**
Both the desktop logo (`alt=""`) and mobile logo (`alt=""`) carry no alt text. When images are blocked (common in B2B inboxes, Outlook default), the logo area renders blank with no fallback label. Decorative spacers warrant `alt=""`, but the brand logo should have `alt="Charlotte Tilbury"` at minimum.

**`role="presentation"` on all tables:** Correct — suppresses table semantics for screen readers.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present and correct.

**Viewport meta:** Present (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1`). Correct.
