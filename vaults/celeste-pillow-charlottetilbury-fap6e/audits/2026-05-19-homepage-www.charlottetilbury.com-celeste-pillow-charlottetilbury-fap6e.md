# Homepage snapshot · www.charlottetilbury.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Oh, I know this product — and the "IT'S BACK!" moment is genuinely exciting if you've been watching Charlotte Tilbury's drops. This is a brand that knows how to make a comeback feel like an event, and the Airbrush Flawless Blur Concealer hero is clean, confident, and very on-brand gold. What's frustrating is that the whole above-the-fold experience is essentially one product, one CTA, and a gift-with-purchase strip — it's a solid launch push but feels light on the "welcome home" energy I'd expect from a brand I spend this much with.

## What's working

- **The "IT'S BACK!" relaunch hook** — for anyone who already knows this concealer, that two-word line does real work. It's earned familiarity, not hype.
- **The product lineup shot** — six or seven concealer shades fanned out in the brand's signature gold packaging reads as "there's a shade for you" without needing a model.
- **Mini Magic Gifts spend tier** — $95 is honestly just one item for me, so this feels genuinely achievable rather than a stretch goal.
- **Search bar placement** — "Search product, shade, colour" is exactly the right prompt for a CT shopper who already knows what she wants.

## What's weak

- **No model, no aspiration** — I'm used to Charlotte's editorial face and that flawless-skin feeling. A wall of concealer tubes, however pretty, doesn't give me the "I want to look like that" pull.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing a lot of heavy lifting** — There's one CTA for the entire above-fold experience. If I'm not buying concealer today, there's nowhere else to go without opening the hamburger menu.
- **No Pillow Talk / lip product visibility** — My actual shopping focus isn't even represented in the hero. I'd have to dig.
- **The top banner is small** — "Unlock Mini Magic Gifts When You Spend $95! T&Cs Apply." is easy to miss on a phone if you're scrolling fast.

## Recommendations

- **Add a model shot alongside the product lineup** — a close-up of flawless skin wearing the concealer in one of those fanned shades would close the gap between "I see a product" and "I want that product on my face."
- **Surface a second CTA or category shortcut** — a quick-tap strip below the hero ("Lips · Eyes · Skin · Gifting") would capture shoppers like me who aren't here for concealer today.
- **Make the GWP banner stickier** — elevate the Mini Magic Gifts message into the hero itself (even a small badge or lockup) rather than burying it in a one-line strip at the very top.
- **Add shade count or "60 shades" social proof** — a single line under the hero copy would immediately signal inclusivity and reduce the "will they have my shade?" hesitation.
