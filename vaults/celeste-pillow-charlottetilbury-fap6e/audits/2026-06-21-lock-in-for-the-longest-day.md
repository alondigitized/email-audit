# Lock In For The Longest Day 💕
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Charlotte Tilbury's Summer Solstice send — June 21, the actual longest day of the year — and the creative team clearly planned around it. "Lock In For The Longest Day" is one of the better subject lines in this recent run: the wordplay earns its keep (setting spray *locks in* makeup; the *longest day* is a real date), and the 16-hours durability claim runs straight through the entire email as a coherent through-line. That creative discipline is the email's biggest asset.
- The problem is context. This is at least the seventh CT email in eight days, and the previous six were all deep into the UNREAL franchise — highlighters, glazes, stickers, the app. Switching to setting sprays without a clear narrative bridge makes the send feel like a product-marketing insert dropped mid-campaign. The GWP is back again ("2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts") for at least the third time in the same stretch, so the gift hook has lost most of its urgency.
- Net: strong concept, well-executed hero, punished by subscriber fatigue from CT's own breakneck send cadence. A real subscriber has been hearing from Charlotte Tilbury nearly every day — at some point the inbox math just doesn't work regardless of copy quality.

## What's working

- **Subject-to-hero coherence**: "Lock In For The Longest Day" → "Lasting Makeup For The Longest Day" → "Up To 16 Hours" is a clean through-line. The concept doesn't drift.
- **Seasonal timing**: Tied to the actual Summer Solstice (June 21). The date-specific hook gives the send a genuine reason to exist on that day.
- **Two-product structure is well-differentiated**: Original (radiant finish) vs. Matte (oil control) are clearly separated with distinct benefit bullets, making the choice legible.
- **No render bugs**: Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text visible.
- **Warm lifestyle hero**: The model and color palette are consistent with Charlotte Tilbury's established visual identity — cohesive with the broader summer campaign.

## What's weak

- **GWP fatigue**: "2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts" is the third or fourth gift-with-purchase tease in this 8-day window. The threshold for unlocking it still isn't visible above the fold, which undermines the hook.
- **Abrupt campaign pivot**: No narrative bridge from the UNREAL franchise (six straight sends) to setting sprays. A subscriber following along hits this email cold.
- **CTA copy is vague at product level**: The buttons on the individual product sections appear to read as generic "Shop All" — not product-specific enough given two distinct SKUs are featured.
- **Cadence damage**: 7+ emails in 8 days going into this send. Even a great email loses open rate when the inbox is this saturated from one brand.
- **The GWP minimum spend is not stated**: Gifting a free product without showing the threshold creates a click-then-disappoint dynamic that erodes trust over repeated sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the GWP threshold in the hero** — even "with any £50 purchase" moves the needle on click intent. Buried thresholds are a trust tax, and CT keeps burying them.
- 2. **Add a narrative bridge from UNREAL** — one line of connective tissue ("Your UNREAL glow deserves backup. Lock it in.") would keep the campaign coherent instead of feeling like a brand switch.
- 3. **Make the product CTAs specific** — "Shop Setting Spray Original" and "Shop Matte Spray" out-convert "Shop All" because they confirm the user's intent.
- 4. **Reduce CTA count in the footer** — the bottom third loses the through-line. The app grid, app download badges, and social links all compete with the product CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Makeup That Lasts 16 Hours — Even Today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Longest Day Needs the Longest-Lasting Makeup`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Unlock 2 free minis when you shop setting spray — offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `16 hours. No fading, no melting. Lock in your summer look.`
