# Unlock Mini Magic Gifts ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a gift-with-purchase (GWP) email: spend $55+, receive a mini Pillow Talk Matte Resolution lipstick and mini Airbrush Flawless Setting Spray automatically added to your bag. Charlotte Tilbury executes the GWP format well — the hero image is polished, product names are specific, and the "GET YOUR GIFTS" CTA is unmistakable. The brand's signature "Darlings" voice lands consistently.
- The email is structurally crowded, though. Three separate value propositions compete below the fold: a product waitlist for the new Exagger-Eyes sticks, an app discovery block, and a benefits grid. Each is independently reasonable; stacked together they dilute the primary GWP offer and give the eye nowhere decisive to rest after the hero.
- The bigger concern is cadence. Yesterday's email (2026-05-16) was the welcome send with 15% off using DARLING15. Sending a GWP push 24 hours later — before that subscriber has had any opportunity to redeem the welcome offer — makes both feel less valuable and risks burning the relationship early.

## What's working

- **Hero is direct**: "Unlock Free Mini Gifts!" headline with named products (Pillow Talk + Airbrush Spray) in the subhead — no guesswork about what's on offer.
- **CTA copy is action-oriented**: "GET YOUR GIFTS" is concrete and benefit-framed, not the typical lazy "Shop Now."
- **Luxury aesthetic is on-brand**: Model photography, rose-gold palette, and clean typography all read premium Charlotte Tilbury.
- **New product tease is smart placement**: Exagger-Eyes waitlist is a low-friction ask (just join, no purchase required) that could build a pre-launch list.

## What's weak

- **"Free" is missing from the subject line**: The hero says "Unlock **Free** Mini Gifts!" but the subject reads "Unlock Mini Magic Gifts ✨" — the single most motivating word in the offer was dropped at the inbox level.
- **No urgency signal anywhere**: No end date, no "while supplies last," no countdown. GWP offers with no expiry feel low-stakes to skip.
- **Three competing CTAs below the fold**: "GET YOUR GIFTS" → "JOIN THE WAITLIST" → app/website discovery block creates a messy hierarchy. The first CTA wins or the email loses.
- **Cadence collision with yesterday's send**: A 15% off welcome code from the day before hasn't had time to convert; piling a GWP offer on top makes the program feel spray-and-pray, not curated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put "Free" back in the subject line** — "Unlock Free Mini Gifts When You Spend $55" is both more specific and more motivating than the current subject. The threshold also sets expectations before the click.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the GWP block** — Even "offer ends Sunday" would create enough urgency to prompt action over deferral.
- 3. **Move the Exagger-Eyes waitlist to a dedicated send** — New product launches deserve their own email where they can be the hero. Here they eat attention from the GWP.
- 4. **Rethink the 24-hour re-send timing** — Let the welcome/15%-off offer breathe for at least 5–7 days before launching into the next campaign.
