# This Content Is TOO HOT To Load 🔥
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury has built a tech-humor creative concept around an "overheating phone" — the phone screen reads "SYSTEM OVERHEATING: Content is too hot to load" as a metaphor for the new UNREAL Highlighter being too hot for the internet to handle. It's a clever, unexpected riff for a beauty brand, and it extends the UNREAL franchise from the June 9 Unreal Skin email cleanly. The summer badge ties it to season, and the warm golden skin-tone imagery suits the product well.
- The execution stumbles because the email is trying to do two separate jobs: tease a product launch AND drive app downloads. The "app first" launch mechanic means the primary value — shopping the new UNREAL Highlighter — is gated behind downloading the app, which is a meaningful friction barrier. The GWP ($115 spend, free mini) is an appealing incentive, but it also requires the app. For subscribers who already have the app, this is fine; for everyone else, there are now two conversion steps before purchase.
- The subject line is the biggest liability. "This Content Is TOO HOT To Load 🔥" is creative but tells you nothing: no product, no offer, no benefit. Coming six sends into a heavy June cadence (June 3, 6, 7, 9, 11), this is the email most likely to get scrolled past.

## What's working

- The phone "overheating" creative is genuinely novel for a beauty brand — it earns a second look.
- The UNREAL Highlighter product and its "Like Skincare In A Stick" proposition are both legible above the fold.
- Summer badge in the header correctly signals seasonal relevance without being heavy-handed.
- The app benefits module (free shipping, quiz, 30% savings edits, exclusive content) is well-organized and makes a real case for the download.
- Continuing the UNREAL franchise from June 9's Unreal Skin send feels like intentional brand architecture, not a random product drop.

## What's weak

- Two competing primary CTAs ("Snap me up!" vs. "Download the App") split the email's intent and dilute both.
- The phone mock overlay partially obscures the product in the hero — the highlighter is background art, not the focal point.
- Subject gives away nothing: no product name, no offer, no benefit — pure curiosity bait from a brand sending at heavy cadence.
- The $115 app-only GWP threshold is high and the app requirement adds a real conversion step; this is buried beneath the fold.
- No price point for the UNREAL Highlighter is visible anywhere in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one CTA job.** Either this email sells the Highlighter or it drives app downloads — not both. Given the app-first launch positioning, lean fully into the app: "Get It First On The App" as the sole CTA, with the GWP as the incentive. Cut the "Snap me up!" confusion.
- 2. **Surface the product in the hero.** Swap the phone mock to a side-by-side or frame it as the phone screen *showing* a close-up of the highlighter on skin — the product should be the star, not the device.
- 3. **Add a price anchor.** Even "From £XX" gives subscribers a reason to click; without it, the email has no transactional hook.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject** to carry actual signal. The tech-humor angle can stay in the preheader or hero, but the subject should name the product or the offer.
- 5. **Acknowledge cadence.** This is the fifth send in nine days. A callout like "Launching exclusively for app users" signals something genuinely new rather than another blast.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your phone's about to overheat. New UNREAL Glow Stick 🔥`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Glow-boosting skincare in a stick. Be the first — shop before everyone else.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Content too hot to load. Spend £115 on the app, get a free mini.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Apple Mail, webmail) surface the `<title>` value. An empty title is a missed fallback and may display placeholder text in certain environments.

**External font stylesheet (blocked by major clients):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (both web and Android) and all Outlook versions strip `<link>` tags. If the CSS is not also inlined or duplicated in the `<style>` block, custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts — or worse, break layout if any sizing/spacing rules are font-dependent. Conditional comment correctly gates this from MSO, but Gmail is the primary concern.

**Image alt text:** The logo `<img>` inside the first `<a>` tag is visible but the `alt` attribute value is not shown in the truncated source. All product images in `image_block` rows should be verified to carry descriptive `alt` text — critical for screen readers and image-blocked render environments (Outlook default blocks images).
