# Homepage snapshot · www.charlottetilbury.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Charlotte is not playing around — a 20% off everything banner is practically a love letter to me. The warm rose-gold palette, the Magic Cream front and center, the Pillow Talk loyalty gift dangled right at the top? This is the brand speaking my language fluently. Right now it's pushing a big sitewide sale with a loyalty-exclusive gift layered on top, and honestly, my thumb is already hovering over "Shop Now."

## What's working

- **The loyalty banner at the very top.** "Unlock A Free Collagen Lip Bath In Pillow Talk Fair When You Spend $115" — that is a product I already own in three formats. Seeing it called out as a loyalty-exclusive gift makes me feel seen as a repeat customer, not just a new visitor being recruited.
- **The 20% OFF EVERYTHING hero.** The sizing is bold and confident. It doesn't bury the offer in fine print — it leads with it, big and pink-cream gold. Charlotte knows her customer doesn't want to decode a coupon code.
- **Product selection in the hero.** The Magic Cream jar, the quad palette, the complexion base, the lip liner — that is a complete routine laid out visually. It's not a random product dump; it feels curated for someone who actually uses the full franchise.
- **The rose-gold aesthetic.** Still unmistakably CT. Even on a sale day the brand doesn't cheapen out with red slashes and neon prices. It stays luxurious while the number does the heavy lifting.
- **Search bar prominence.** One tap to find a specific shade or product. For someone who already knows exactly which Pillow Talk lip liner she needs, this is a gift.

## What's weak

- **"Shop Now" as the sole CTA.** When you're a loyal CT customer, "Shop Now" doesn't tell me where to go. Am I shopping skincare? Lips? The whole 20% off catalog? A split CTA — "Shop Skincare" and "Shop Lips" — would convert me faster.
- **No model or lifestyle context.** The hero is pure product flatlay. I love seeing how someone my age actually wears the quad, or how the Magic Cream sits under foundation on real skin, not just a jar floating on a pink gradient.
- **No social proof above the fold.** No star ratings, no "10 million Magic Creams sold," no press quote. For new-to-CT visitors landing from an ad, there's nothing to anchor trust beyond the brand name itself.
- **The asterisk anxiety.** The * on "EVERYTHING" without immediately clarifying exclusions creates friction right at the moment I should be most excited. I've been burned by "everything" meaning "everything except the thing I came to buy."
- **No urgency signal.** There's no "ends Sunday" or countdown timer. The 20% deal is compelling, but I have no reason not to close the tab and come back later — which means I might not come back at all.

## Recommendations

- **Add a deadline line under "Shop Now."** Even "Ends Monday 11:59 PM" would make me stop scrolling Instagram and start adding to cart right now. One line of copy, huge conversion lift.
- **Split the CTA into two tappable tiles below the hero.** "Shop Skincare 20% Off" and "Shop Lips 20% Off" — let me get to my category in one tap instead of landing on an undifferentiated sale page.
- **Surface the exclusions inline.** Change the asterisk footnote to something like "Excludes bundles & gift sets" right under the main headline. Transparency here removes the hesitation and actually increases trust.
- **Drop a lifestyle frame into the hero.** Keep the product flatlay but overlay a small editorial crop — a 35-year-old with her Pillow Talk liner in hand, Magic Cream on her nightstand. It contextualizes the products and reflects who's actually buying them.
