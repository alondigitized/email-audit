# Shop NEW! Unreal Highlighter On The App! 🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Charlotte Tilbury has threaded two goals into one email: launch the new UNREAL Highlighter and drive app downloads — with every single CTA pointing to "SHOP ON THE APP" rather than the website. That's an intentional channel strategy, and the "EXCLUSIVE EARLY ACCESS ON THE APP" banner at the top gives it a logic. The problem is that if you haven't downloaded the CT app, this email is effectively a dead end. No web fallback, no "also available at charlottetilbury.com," nothing. For desktop openers or casual subscribers, every CTA is a wall.
- The UNREAL Highlighter has now appeared in back-to-back sends since June 9 — this is the fourth or fifth push in eight days. The differentiated angle (app exclusivity, early access) partially rescues the fatigue, but it's wearing thin. The June 11 "This Content Is TOO HOT To Load" send (scored 8/10) was far more memorable as a creative concept. This one is a straightforward product + app grid that leans on Charlotte's rose-gold aesthetic without a fresh hook.
- The email is also long — six distinct layout sections before the footer — with the GWP mechanic ("2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts when you spend $10") buried near the bottom. That offer should be the first thing a subscriber sees, not the last.

## What's working

- **App-exclusivity frame is strategically coherent** — the magenta "EXCLUSIVE EARLY ACCESS ON THE APP" banner sets an actual reason to engage beyond "buy now."
- **Product detail copy is substantive** — "92% Skincare Ingredients, 100% UNREAL Glow" and the blurring-effect bullet breakdown give the product texture rather than empty claims.
- **Charlotte Tilbury's brand aesthetic is immaculate** — the rose-gold palette, the summer yellow accent section, and the model imagery are all on-brand and render cleanly with no layout bugs.
- **FREE GIFT offer is present** — "Unlock 2 FREE Mini Glowing Skin Gifts when you spend $10" is a concrete, scannable deal even if it sits too far down.

## What's weak

- **Every CTA goes to the app — there's no web escape hatch.** Subscribers on desktop or those without the app hit a dead end every time they click. This is the single biggest deliverable killer.
- **UNREAL fatigue is real.** This is the fifth UNREAL send in eight days. Without the creative hook that made June 11's send memorable, this one blends into the cadence noise.
- **The GWP is buried.** "2 FREE Mini Gifts when you spend $10" is below four separate content sections — most subscribers will never reach it before they bounce.
- **"GET YOUR GLOW" in the mid-section is vague.** The surrounding CTAs say "SHOP ON THE APP" which is at least specific. "GET YOUR GLOW" breaks the pattern and provides no directional clarity.
- **The QR code section at the bottom adds visual clutter.** It's a reasonable nudge for app downloads but arrives after the user has already been asked three times to use the app.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one "Shop on the Web" secondary CTA.** The app-first strategy is fine; the no-web-fallback is not. Even a small text link below the primary button ("Prefer to shop online? charlottetilbury.com →") would rescue conversions from the majority of desktop readers.
- 2. **Surface the GWP in the hero.** Move "2 FREE Mini Gifts when you spend $10" into the first fold — above the product benefit grid. That's the conversion trigger; hide it and you've buried your best argument.
- 3. **Retire "NEW! UNREAL" as the headline unless the creative is differentiated.** At send five in eight days, the word "UNREAL" is background noise. Lead with the exclusive: *"First to the Highlighter. App Only."*
- 4. **Replace "GET YOUR GLOW" with "Shop Early Access."** Makes the mid-email CTA consistent and reminds the reader why this email exists.
- 5. **Cut one layout section.** Six blocks is two too many for an app-push email. Merge the "blurring effect" bullets into the product hero and remove the standalone QR section (app store badge links are sufficient).
- **Subject Alt A:** `App-Only: New Unreal Highlighter Early Access`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Early access is yours — new UNREAL Highlighter drops first on the app`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the new highlighter before anyone else — plus 2 free mini gifts with $10`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Get exclusive early access + unlock 2 free glowing skin gifts today`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External font stylesheet via `<link>` tag (confirmed issue):**
```html
<link href="https://www.charlottetilbury.com/static/email/fonts.css" rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```
This is wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` to skip Outlook, but `<link>` stylesheets are stripped or ignored by Gmail (webmail and Android app), Yahoo Mail, and Samsung Mail. The custom typeface will silently fall back in all of those clients with no error — undetectable to the sender unless tested. Fonts should be inlined via `@font-face` inside a `<style>` block, or the fallback stack must be acceptable as the primary rendering.

**Link inventory and UTM coverage:** Cannot fully assess — source is truncated before CTA `<a>` tags appear.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) surface the `<title>` as a window/tab label or preview. An empty string results in a blank or client-default label. Should be populated to match the subject line or a descriptive internal label.

**Link underline suppression:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This blanket rule removes underline decoration from all links in the message body. Combined with `color:inherit`, links become visually indistinguishable from body text in any client that applies this selector, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.1 — use of color alone to convey information). A minimum fix is to remove `text-decoration:none` from this rule and scope text-decoration overrides only to styled CTA buttons.

**Preheader implementation:** Correctly hidden via `display:none !important; visibility:hidden; mso-hide:all` — no issue.

**MSO/VML namespaces and `<o:PixelsPerInch>` / `<o:AllowPNG>` declarations:** Present and correctly structured for Outlook compatibility — no issue.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present — no issue.
