# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Dell went all-in on an AI-first homepage and honestly, as someone who spends half her week staring at a dual-monitor XPS rig, I get what they're going for — it's slick and tech-forward. But what I'm actually seeing is a company that led with a chatbot instead of a product, and on mobile that bet pays off in novelty points but loses me on the deal-hunting I came to do. Right now Dell looks like a company confident enough in its brand to skip the hard sell — which is both its strength and its problem.

## What's working

- **The AI prompt interface** — "Ask me about Dell products and services" is genuinely useful for a configurator-heavy shopper like me who often starts with a question anyway. This is a real shortcut.
- **"Find the best laptop for me" button** — smart quick-action that maps directly to how I actually browse when buying for personal use vs. work.
- **"Track my order" button** — practical above-the-fold placement; I actually use this more than I'd like to admit during refresh cycles.
- **Clean mobile render** — no nav elements colliding, text is legible, buttons have good tap targets. Dell Days can get messy on mobile and this doesn't.
- **Transparency Statement link** — shows maturity around the AI feature; I trust brands that acknowledge their tools' limitations.

## What's weak

- **No Premier / business account hook** — I have a Premier account. Dell knows this. There's nothing here that acknowledges returning business buyers, no "Welcome back, here's your account pricing" — it's treating me like a first-visit consumer.
- **No concrete deal visible** — "Show current deals" is a call-to-action, not a deal. A tech-savvy comparison shopper clicks through expecting a list, not a promise. Just tell me "Up to 20% off XPS this week" above the fold.
- **The monitor image is barely visible** — the bottom of the screen shows two monitors with a gorgeous colorful display, which is exactly what would pull me in, but it's mostly cropped out. That's the product, show it.
- **No urgency or time anchor** — Dell Days is a known event. If there's a sale window, it's not here. I'm not compelled to act now vs. tomorrow.
- **AI-only above fold leaves discovery to me** — if I'm a first-time visitor (or shopping for a family member on my account), the AI prompt with no categories visible means I have to know what to ask. That's friction.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a concrete Premier or deal offer as a banner strip** above the AI prompt — even "XPS 15 from $X | Premier pricing available" sets the price anchor I need to stay engaged.
- **Replace or supplement the AI prompt section with a product visual** — move the dual-monitor image up and behind the prompt; the colorful display is gorgeous and should be doing more work.
- **Add a "My Account / Premier" chip in the nav bar** — one tap to my saved configs, order history, and account pricing turns this from a cold homepage into a personalized one.
- **Put a Dell Days or active campaign badge somewhere above the fold** — even a small "Dell Days — Shop Now" pill on the hero communicates timeliness and gives comparison shoppers a reason to act today.
