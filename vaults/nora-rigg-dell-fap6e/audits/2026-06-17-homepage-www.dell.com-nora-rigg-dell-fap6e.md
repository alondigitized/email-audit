# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-06-17
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- On mobile, Dell is opening with a full-bleed XPS 13 launch — clean wordmark, tight copy, two mirrored laptops sitting like a magazine spread. It reads premium and confident, which tracks with the brand I know. But as someone who logs into my Premier account and heads straight to the configurator, this screen is all brand theater and zero action — I can see the product, read the starting price, and do absolutely nothing about it from here.

## What's working

- **XPS hero choice**: Opening a laptop refresh cycle on the XPS 13 is exactly the right call for anyone in the Dell ecosystem. I immediately know what's new.
- **Price anchoring at $699**: Dropping a hard number above the fold gives me something to hold. Comparison-driven shoppers need a stake in the ground — this does that.
- **Minimal, confident copy**: "Razor-thin. Featherlight." is two words that do real work. No marketing fluff, no feature list soup. I can read this on the train.
- **Clean render on mobile**: The page loads with no layout shift, no broken images, no overlapping text. That's a baseline that a surprising number of big brands still fumble.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button above the fold**: This is the single biggest miss. I see a beautiful product and a price and then... nothing to tap. For a configurator-heavy shopper, no "Build Yours" or "Configure" button means I'm stuck.
- **Student pricing is front and center**: "Exclusive student pricing starting at $599*" is the second piece of copy I read after the headline. I'm 41 with a Premier account — this messaging is actively wrong for me and eats valuable fold real estate.
- **No Premier/business account hook**: Dell knows I have a business account. There's no "Sign in to see your pricing" or "Premier member? Your price:" prompt anywhere. That's a personalization fail on a return visit.
- **No navigation shortcuts on mobile**: A hamburger menu and a search bar are all I get. No "Business", no "Laptops", no "New Arrivals" strip — I'd have to hunt.
- **No urgency or offer context**: Is this a launch event? A sale? A limited-time bundle? The page gives me no reason to act now vs. closing the tab and coming back from my laptop later.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Configure It" or "Build Yours" CTA button** directly below the price line — tapping into a product configurator is the whole reason a shopper like me is here; don't make me dig through the hamburger to find it.
- **Swap the student pricing line for a Premier member callout**: "Premier account holders: sign in for your price" does more for your B2B base than a student discount that doesn't apply to 95% of existing Premier customers.
- **Surface a slim personalization bar** under the search field (e.g., "Your last order shipped June 3 · View deals for your model") — even a static "Welcome back" + account link signals that Dell knows me.
- **Add a thin offer-context ribbon** ("XPS 13 Now Available · Ships in 3–5 days · Free shipping on orders over $X") to give me a reason to act from my phone rather than waiting until I'm at my desk.
