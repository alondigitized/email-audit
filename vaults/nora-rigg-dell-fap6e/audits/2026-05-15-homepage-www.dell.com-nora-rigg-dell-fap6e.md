# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I opened Dell's homepage expecting to find something relevant to the XPS refresh I've been eyeing, and instead got a full-screen Fortnite collab. This reads as a gaming brand right now — Alienware front and center, purple glow, zero acknowledgment that Dell also sells the machines I actually use for work. If I didn't already know the XPS line existed, I'd wonder if I had the right URL.

## What's working

- **"Shop Alienware 15" CTA button** — purple, high-contrast, unmistakable. I know exactly what one tap does.
- **Visual hierarchy** — the laptop render is large and well-lit; the pause button tells me there's autoplay video content, which signals production investment.
- **Clean layout** — no banner clutter, no pop-up blocking the viewport, no layout shift noise. Dell's mobile execution here is tidy.
- **Current campaign feel** — Fortnite collab with the Alienware 15 reads as a real launch moment, not a recycled promo.

## What's weak

- **Zero Premier/business acknowledgment** — I have a Premier account. The homepage doesn't know I exist. No "Sign in for business pricing," no Dell Rewards balance, nothing that recognizes I'm a repeat customer.
- **Gaming-only hero for a brand that sells business laptops** — Dell's bread and butter is enterprise and prosumer. Leading with Alienware on mobile sends me to the wrong aisle entirely.
- **No offer or pricing signal** — I check Dell specifically during Dell Days or promo windows. There's no hook here — no "Up to X% off," no financing teaser, nothing to make me think "now is a good time to configure."
- **Search bar is the only navigation visible** — there's no quick-tap path to XPS, monitors, or accessories. Hamburger menus on mobile are friction for a comparison shopper who wants to jump straight to a category.
- **No trust or credibility signals** — no review count, no rating stars, no "Free shipping" callout. For a $1,500+ purchase, these matter even at the top of funnel.

## Recommendations

- **Personalize the hero by entry point or login state** — if I'm signed into my Premier account, surface "Your next XPS upgrade" or my rewards balance instead of a Fortnite collab. Even a thin "Premier members: see business pricing" banner above the hero would convert me faster.
- **Add a Dell Days / active promo strip above the hero** — a single-line banner ("Dell Days: up to 30% off XPS + Alienware — ends May 18") would give comparison shoppers a reason to stay instead of opening a competitor tab.
- **Expose one or two category tiles below the hero** — a "Laptops," "Monitors," "Business" row takes up 40px and collapses the navigation distance for non-gaming visitors.
- **Surface a trust signal strip** — "Free shipping · 30-day returns · Premier business pricing" in a small footer-of-hero row adds purchase confidence without disrupting the gaming hero design.
