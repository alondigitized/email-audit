# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I came to Dell.com looking for something in the XPS or workstation family — maybe checking whether there's a Dell Days sale worth flagging before my refresh cycle hits — and the homepage threw a gaming laptop at me with Fortnite on the screen. Dell Technologies is a massive enterprise brand, but right now it's presenting itself as a gaming rig company targeting teenagers. I respect Alienware as a line, but this is not the page for me.

## What's working

- **"Shop Alienware 15" CTA button** — the purple pill button is impossible to miss. Good contrast, unambiguous copy. If I *were* shopping gaming, I'd know exactly where to tap.
- **Search bar prominently placed** — full-width, right below the nav. This is how I actually use Dell.com anyway (search "XPS 15" or "Precision 5690"), so at least the escape hatch is there.
- **Clean render** — no layout shift, no broken assets, no overlapping text. Mobile experience is visually solid.
- **Fortnite branding on the laptop screen** — clever product staging; makes the use case immediately legible even if that use case isn't mine.

## What's weak

- **Gaming-only above the fold** — there is zero acknowledgment that Dell sells business, productivity, or hybrid-work hardware. My XPS category is invisible.
- **No business/Premier account hook** — I have a Premier account and there's not even a hint of member pricing, exclusive offers, or "sign in to see your deals." That's a missed layup.
- **No offer or price signal whatsoever** — a Dell Days banner, a "starting at" price, a free-shipping note — anything. The page is pushing me toward a purchase funnel with zero financial anchoring.
- **Hero imagery has no human** — it's a floating laptop on a gradient. No lifestyle context, no person, nothing that makes me see myself using this machine.
- **Hamburger menu hides everything** — on mobile, all navigation is collapsed behind three lines. I can't tell if XPS, Monitors, or Deals sections exist without a tap I haven't taken yet.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the Alienware hero for a rotating carousel** — slot the gaming push as one slide, but lead with an XPS or hybrid-work lifestyle shot (coffee shop, dual-monitor home office, commuter). A 15-second autoplay rotation captures both audiences without alienating business buyers on first load.
- **Add a Premier / Business account sign-in banner strip** — a slim bar above the hero reading "Premier member? Sign in for exclusive pricing" costs nothing to ship and immediately signals that Dell knows I'm not a first-time consumer buyer.
- **Show one concrete number above the fold** — even "XPS laptops starting at $999" or "Save up to 30% during Dell Days" gives me a reason to engage rather than bounce to the configurator directly.
- **Surface the category nav chips below the hero** — after the CTA, drop a horizontal scroll row of chips: "XPS," "Alienware," "Monitors," "Workstations," "Business." One tap to my category, no hamburger required.
