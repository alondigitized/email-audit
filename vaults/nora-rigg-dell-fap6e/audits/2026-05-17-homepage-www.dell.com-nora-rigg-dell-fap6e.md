# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I opened Dell.com expecting to land somewhere near my world — XPS configurator, maybe a Dell Days banner, something that acknowledges I've been a Premier customer for years — and instead I walked straight into a Fortnite ad. The Alienware 15 hero is loud and technically clean, but it reads as a gaming rig targeting a teenager's Christmas list, not a 41-year-old hybrid worker who refreshes her dual-monitor setup on a company plan and actively compares configs before buying. As a Dell homepage, it's fine. As a homepage for *me*, it missed entirely.

## What's working

- **"Shop Alienware 15" CTA button** — purple, high contrast, unambiguous. I know exactly what happens if I tap it.
- **Product-first hero composition** — the laptop is large, well-lit, displayed at an angle that shows the build quality. As someone who evaluates hardware specs, a clean product shot beats a lifestyle blur.
- **Search bar prominence** — it's the second element on the screen, right below the nav. As a search-first shopper on mobile, I'd use it to get to XPS immediately without waiting for the hero to matter.
- **No pop-up modal or forced overlay** — the page opened cleanly. That's more than I can say for half the retail sites I hit on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Alienware / gaming hero is completely off-persona for me.** I'm here for XPS or Inspiron, not a Fortnite machine. This is a carousel presumably, but whatever I'm seeing right now is not relevant.
- **No Premier account recognition.** I'm logged into my account presumably — there's an account icon in the nav — but there's zero personalization. No "Welcome back, Nora" tier badge, no Premier pricing callout.
- **Zero promotional signal above the fold.** Dell Days, Dell Technologies World discounts, seasonal promos — nothing. I watch for these specifically, and right now the page is giving me nothing to act on.
- **No price anchor on the hero product.** The Alienware 15 has a CTA but no "Starting at $X" — I can't even decide if it's worth tapping before I already have to leave the page.
- **Hamburger-only navigation on mobile.** My category (XPS, Inspiron, business PCs) requires a tap-to-open menu. For a configurator-heavy shopper, one more step is friction I don't love.

## Recommendations

- **Rotate the hero carousel to surface an XPS or Inspiron frame for returning Premier customers.** Even a simple logged-in signal showing "Your next XPS upgrade" with a configurator CTA would make this feel like Dell knows who I am.
- **Add a Premier/Dell Days promo strip immediately below the search bar.** A single line — "Dell Days are here: up to 30% off XPS for Premier members" — captures attention for the exact use case I come here for.
- **Show a starting price on the hero product.** "$1,299.99 and up" next to the CTA removes the need to tap blind. It qualifies intent before the click.
- **Surface category shortcuts below the hero.** A 3-icon row — XPS, Inspiron, Business — lets comparison-driven shoppers like me skip the hamburger entirely and get to configs in one tap.
