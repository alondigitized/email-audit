# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I pull up dell.com looking for XPS configurator options or a Premier deal alert, and instead I'm staring at Fortnite on an Alienware. Gaming is a real Dell business, sure, but this isn't me — and it's not even close. The page reads as a consumer gaming brand, not the business-productivity powerhouse I've been buying from for the last decade.

## What's working

- **"Shop Alienware 15" CTA button** — purple, full-width-ish, impossible to miss. Good contrast, unambiguous action.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — the Alienware laptop fills the hero, copy lands below it, CTA below that. No clutter, no competing elements fighting for attention.
- **No forced modals or overlays** — I landed on the page and just saw content. That's rarer than it should be.
- **Search bar is prominent** — given that I'm a search-first shopper when I know what I want, having it front-and-center saves me from digging through nav.

## What's weak

- **Fortnite hero on a business site** — I work on an XPS rig I expense through a company plan. Alienware + a game skin communicates "this isn't for you, Nora" the moment the page loads.
- **Zero Premier account context** — I have a business account. The homepage has no idea. No "Welcome back," no business pricing badge, no Premier portal shortcut.
- **No offer whatsoever** — Dell Days, doorbuster deals, financing — nothing. I refresh hardware every three years specifically watching for these windows. This page gives me nothing to act on.
- **Hamburger-only navigation on mobile** — my category (XPS, business laptops) is completely hidden. One tap gets me to Alienware; everything else requires opening a menu.
- **No trust signals** — no review counts on the featured product, no "free shipping," no return policy callout. Even a quick "$0 shipping on orders over $X" would help.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the Fortnite/Alienware hero for an XPS lifestyle or dual-monitor hybrid-work scene** — or at minimum, run A/B personalization: show gaming to guests, show XPS or Precision to signed-in Premier users.
- **Surface a Premier/business pricing badge in the header** — even "Premier Member" next to the account icon would signal the site knows who I am.
- **Drop a promo strip above or below the hero** — "Dell Days: up to 30% off select XPS" with a clear end date. That's the hook that makes me stop and configurate instead of bounce.
- **Expand the hamburger nav into visible category tiles** — Business, Gaming, Home, Monitors — so I can tap directly to XPS laptops without a menu interaction on mobile.
