# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-06-10
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Dell is going full AI-first on its homepage — the entire above-the-fold experience is a chat interface, not a traditional hero with deals and product rails. That's a bold bet. For me, someone who already knows she wants an XPS refresh and watches Dell Days like a hawk, leading with "How can I help you today?" feels either very smart or a total detour depending on how useful that AI actually is in practice.

## What's working

- **"Find the best laptop for me" quick action** — as a configurator-heavy shopper, I'd actually tap this; it's the shortcut to a product comparison flow without digging through nav
- **"Show current deals" quick action** — I watch Dell Days; the promise of surfacing deals quickly is genuinely useful
- **"Track my order" quick action** — practical, saved me a Google
- **Clean render, no clutter** — the minimalist AI-first layout doesn't assault me with banners; as a high-comfort tech user I appreciate the clean signal-to-noise
- **Transparency Statement link** — for a chat AI on a commerce site, flagging "please verify responses" is the right move; earns a small trust point

## What's weak

- **Zero personalization for business / Premier customers** — I have a Dell Premier account, and there's no hook anywhere to sign in, check Premier pricing, or access account-specific deals. That's a miss for the segment that probably drives the highest AOV.
- **No concrete offer** — Dell Days, doorbuster SKUs, or even a headline percentage is absent. The AI promises I can find deals, but there's nothing to anchor my expectation before I type.
- **Hamburger-only nav on mobile** — every product category is hidden behind a tap. For a comparison-driven shopper, surfacing XPS, monitors, or accessories as visible chips would cut my path to conversion by at least two taps.
- **No social proof or trust signals** — no review count, no "over 10M customers," no award badges. On a big-ticket electronics homepage, those anchors matter.
- **The AI UX is a gamble** — if the chat AI is slow or gives me generic suggestions, I'll bounce. There's no fallback experience if the AI fails the test.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a Premier / business account CTA above the fold** — a single "Premier pricing available — sign in" badge near the nav would immediately signal this is the right site for me and pull me deeper.
- **Add a thin deal-strip above the AI hero** — one line: "Dell Days: up to 30% off XPS + free shipping" gives me the concrete hook before I even interact with the chat.
- **Swap hamburger nav for horizontal category chips below the search bar** — "Laptops | Monitors | Accessories | Deals" as tappable chips would get me one tap from my category instead of two.
- **Add a "Continue where you left off" / recently-viewed module below the AI hero** — for a returning XPS-owner refresher like me, showing my previously configured or viewed products is the single highest-leverage personalization play available.
