# Homepage snapshot · www.dell.com · 2026-05-05
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-05**
## Executive summary

- Dell has replaced what used to be a product-first homepage with an AI assistant experience — "How can I help you today?" is front and center with a chatbot input and quick-action chips. As someone who lives in the configurator and has a Premier account, this feels like Dell trying to be helpful in a way that mostly gets in my way. I came to browse XPS options and check Dell Days deals, not describe my needs to a chatbot. The brand is signaling AI-forward, but for a comparison-driven buyer like me, it buries the actual product discovery path.
- **4/10**
- + Primary CTA visible above the fold — "Show current deals" button is clearly rendered and one tap away
- + Visual hierarchy is clear — "How can I help you today?" headline reads immediately, AI input below it, quick-action chips below that; the eye knows the order
- + No render bugs — layout is clean, no broken elements, no overlapping text
- + The quick-action chip "Find the best laptop for me" is at least pointing at my category
- − Hero copy does not speak to my persona — no Premier account hook, no hybrid-worker framing, no XPS or business-line callout
- − No returning-shopper hook — I have a Premier account and there's no welcome back, no saved config, no rewards balance showing
- − No concrete offer above the fold — "Show current deals" is a link, not an offer; no % off, no Dell Days callout, no dollar threshold
- − No loyalty or membership visibility — Premier exists but there's zero mention of it here
- − No demographic or lifestyle signals for a 41-year-old business buyer
- − No seasonal or campaign context visible — could be any day of the year

## What's working

- **"Show current deals" chip** — one tap to deals is exactly the entry point I want when I'm checking Dell Days; it's the clearest CTA on the page
- **"Track my order" chip** — practical, obvious, saves me from hunting through the nav; I use this regularly after placing a business order
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no banner pile-up, no competing carousels fighting for attention; at least the page doesn't punish me visually
- **AI transparency disclaimer** — "Please verify responses. Transparency Statement" is responsible and shows Dell isn't trying to pass off hallucinated specs as fact
- **Search bar at the top** — my fallback when I know exactly what I want (XPS 15, specific config SKU); it's there and accessible

## What's weak

- **The entire above-fold is an AI funnel, not a product experience** — I came to compare and configure; instead I'm being asked to describe my needs conversationally, which is the opposite of how I shop
- **Zero product imagery above the fold** — the two laptops barely visible at the very bottom of the screenshot are almost entirely cut off by the AI assistant modal; I can't actually see them
- **Double AI callout** — the "New AI Assistant" modal at the bottom is a second layer of the same thing that's already dominating the page; it creates visual clutter and feels redundant
- **No Premier account recognition** — I have a business account; Dell knows who I am when I'm logged in (I'm not sure if I am here) but there's nothing acknowledging that relationship
- **No concrete Dell Days or pricing hook** — as someone who watches Dell Days actively, a homepage with zero deal content above the fold is a miss
- **"Find the best laptop for me" leads to AI, not the configurator** — I want to go straight to the XPS line and spec it out, not answer chatbot questions first

## Recommendations

- **Surface one concrete deal above the fold** — swap the full-bleed AI interface for a split layout: AI on one half, a live deal card (Dell Days promo, specific model % off) on the other; give comparison shoppers a reason to stay
- **Add a Premier account ribbon or badge** — a thin top bar ("Welcome back, Nora — your Premier pricing is active") would immediately signal that Dell knows me and convert me from anonymous browser to engaged account holder
- **Replace the "New AI Assistant" modal with a product rail** — use that screen real estate to show two or three featured XPS or business laptops with prices; the AI entry point is already above the fold, the modal is noise
- **Add a "Dell Days" or "current campaign" badge to the "Show current deals" chip** — even just changing the label to "Shop Dell Days" with a sale icon would make the offer feel real and time-bounded instead of generic
