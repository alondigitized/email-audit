# Homepage snapshot · www.amazon.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- This is my Amazon — literally, the logo is like a reflex at this point — but the homepage is working hard to sell me Prime membership I already have, while simultaneously trying to convince me I need a Cartier belt for Mother's Day. It feels like the algorithm is both over-estimating my budget and under-estimating that I'm already locked in. Right now it's pushing luxury gifting and a Prime upsell, neither of which is my Saturday-morning-pickup-line reality.
- **4/10**

## What's working

- **"Designer gifts for Mom" section** — Mother's Day must be close, and this is a legitimate impulse-browse trigger for me even if I'm not buying Gucci. I'm already scanning for ideas for my own mom or gifts to drop hints about.
- **Location context** ("Delivering to Monterey… 91754") — I love that it knows where I am. That zip-code delivery promise is the whole reason I'm here instead of at Target.
- **"Save up to 30%"** — The number is big, bold, and scannable. Even squinting at my phone in a parking lot, I clocked it immediately.

## What's weak

- **"Join Prime" CTA is dead weight for me** — I am already a Prime member. Serving me a membership recruitment hero is a wasted above-the-fold slot. It reads like Amazon forgot who it's talking to.
- **The blue hero banner is generic** — "Prime Exclusive Deals" with no specificity. What deals? For what categories? I need a reason to tap, not a billboard.
- **Cartier and Gucci in the gift rail** — I browse Amazon for convenience and value, not aspirational luxury. These SKUs feel like they wandered in from a different site. They don't make me feel seen; they make me feel like the algorithm is guessing.
- **Navigation bar is low-signal** — "Amazon Haul," "Medical Care," "Amazon Basics" at the top feels like someone grabbed random nav items. No "Your Subscribe & Save," no "Reorder," nothing sticky to my actual behavior.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the Prime recruitment hero for a personalized deals strip** — Show me "Your Price: $X" badges on products from categories I actually buy. Even "Subscribe & Save deals this week" would land harder than a generic join-Prime pitch.
- **Retitle the gift rail to "Mother's Day ideas under $75"** — Lose Cartier, bring in practical-but-thoughtful: silk pillowcases, Vitamix accessories, Kindle upgrades. That's what suburban moms actually buy each other.
- **Add a "Reorder your staples" fast-lane module** — One row of my Subscribe & Save items with a single-tap reorder. That's the 30-second win that keeps me on this app daily.
- **Give the urgency copy a deadline** — "Save up to 30%" needs a hook like "through Sunday" or "while supplies last" to make me act now instead of later.
