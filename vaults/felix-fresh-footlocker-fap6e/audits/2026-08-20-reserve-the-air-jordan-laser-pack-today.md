# Reserve the Air Jordan 'Laser' pack today!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's third reservation-launch send in the last four days, and it follows the exact template that scored well on 8/17 (Vans Old Skool) and 8/18 — dark cinematic hero, bold typographic headline, single hero product shot on a moody background, "RESERVE NOW" CTA, countdown-style urgency. Swapping in the Air Jordan 1 Low OG and Air Jordan 3 "Laser" pack keeps the formula fresh for this specific drop, and the hero-to-subject match is tight: the subject says "Air Jordan 'Laser' pack," the hero literally reads "THE AIR JORDAN 'LASER' COLLECTION."
- Where it loses points is everything below the fold. After the strong Jordan hero, the email immediately pivots into three unrelated campaigns — FLX Fridays, Week of New Balance, Triple-Black Essentials, and Kids Back to School — stacked one after another with no connective tissue. It reads less like a "Jordan reservation" email and more like a full catalog dump that happens to open with Jordan. That's the same structural habit that hurt the 8/18 "Tax free weekend" send, where the subject and hero didn't even agree.
- Net: strong hero, clear CTA, honest urgency — but diluted by bolt-on merchandising that has nothing to do with the subject line's promise.

## What's working

- Hero headline, product shot, and subject line are all in agreement — a rarity given this brand's recent mismatches (see 8/18).
- "RESERVE NOW" is a large, unambiguous, outlined CTA button placed directly under the offer copy.
- Reservation deadline ("Reservations close 8/21 at 4PM EST") gives real, checkable urgency instead of vague "limited time."
- FLX Rewards loyalty messaging (free shipping, no minimum) appears twice, reinforcing member value without being pushy.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, footer/nav render fine.

## What's weak

- Four unrelated promotions (FLX Fridays, New Balance, Triple-Black, Kids Back to School) are crammed below the Jordan hero, none of them mentioned in the subject line — the email over-promises focus and under-delivers scope.
- The secondary "FLX FRIDAYS ARE HERE" banner interrupts momentum right after the primary CTA, which is where a Jordan-specific detail (release date, price, sizes) should live instead.
- No product detail (price, available sizes, silhouette-specific imagery for both the 1 Low OG and the 3 "Laser") — only one shoe pair is actually shown despite the copy naming two silhouettes.
- No social proof or "hype" signal (limited quantity, past sellout, waitlist count) to reinforce why reservation urgency matters.
- Cadence fatigue: this is the third reservation-style hero in five days (Vans 8/17, 8/18, now Jordan 8/20), which a regular subscriber will start to register as a template rather than news.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the unrelated sections or move them to a separate send.** A subscriber who opens for "Air Jordan Laser pack" and scrolls into New Balance/Triple-Black/Kids content will bounce faster than one who gets a focused, single-offer email — trim to Jordan hero + one complementary "you might also like" strip at most.
- 2. **Show both silhouettes.** Copy promises "Air Jordan 1 Low OG and Air Jordan 3 'Laser'" but the hero image only shows one shoe — add a second product shot or a side-by-side pair callout.
- 3. **Add a price and size-availability line** near the CTA — reservation emails convert better when the shopper knows what they're committing to before clicking.
- 4. **Vary the template.** Three near-identical reservation-hero sends in five days is noticeable; alternate with a different layout style for the next drop to avoid "another one of these" fatigue.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your shot at the Jordan "Laser" pack ends 8/21`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two heritage Jordan silhouettes, one reservation window.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Air Jordan 1 Low OG + Air Jordan 3 "Laser" — reserve before 4PM ET tomorrow.`
