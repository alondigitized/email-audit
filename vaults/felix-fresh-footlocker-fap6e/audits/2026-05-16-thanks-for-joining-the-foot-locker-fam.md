# Thanks for joining the Foot Locker fam! 🙌🏽
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a textbook welcome email — clean, on-brand, and completely toothless. Foot Locker's design team delivered a polished render: strong headline hierarchy, lifestyle photography, and a tidy quad of category tiles. The problem is structural: there is no welcome offer. Every serious sneaker retailer (Nike, SNKRS, Finish Line) buries a first-purchase discount inside the welcome flow. Foot Locker gives you an emoji handshake and a generic "SHOP NOW." For a brand with the FLX Rewards program front-and-center in the header, the omission is especially jarring — you just signed someone up for a loyalty program and then declined to explain what they get for it.
- The four category tiles below the hero — Latest Arrivals, Trending Styles, Classics, Launch Calendar — are the most strategically interesting thing in the email. Launch Calendar in particular is the right hook for a sneaker-obsessed subscriber. But these tiles are buried under a full-bleed lifestyle shot and receive no copy treatment to explain the value. They function as navigation, not persuasion.
- Bottom line: a good-looking email that does the minimum. Technically competent, commercially passive.

## What's working

- **"WELCOME TO THE FAM" headline** lands immediately — bold, oversized, brand-voice-appropriate, no ambiguity about what this email is.
- **FLX Rewards logo** is present in the header, establishing loyalty context from the first pixel.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text is legible, layout holds without overflow or breakage.
- **Four category tiles** give the subscriber a clear taxonomy of what Foot Locker sells, with the Launch Calendar tile being a meaningful hook for core sneaker buyers.
- **Stay Connected / SMS signup** section intelligently pushes subscribers toward higher-intent channels before they've even spent.

## What's weak

- **No welcome offer** — joining a loyalty program with no tangible reward is a squandered conversion moment. A welcome discount or a points-bonus promise should be the hero, not an afterthought.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is maximally generic** — no product, no category, no urgency. It links to an unspecified destination that could be the homepage.
- **Lifestyle photography with no product context** — two women in street clothes look good but sell nothing; the hero image doesn't show a shoe, a product name, or a price.
- **FLX Rewards is logo-only** — it's in the header but the email never explains the program, the tier structure, or what the subscriber just unlocked by joining.
- **Multiple competing CTAs with equal visual weight** — SHOP NOW + four tiles + social icons + Download the App + SMS signup = decision paralysis.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a welcome offer to the hero** — "Here's 15% off your first order" or "You just earned 100 welcome points" transforms this from a confirmation email into a conversion email. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA to be specific** — "Shop New Arrivals" or "Claim Your Welcome Points" beats "SHOP NOW" every time. Pair it with a hard link to a relevant category, not the homepage.
- 3. **Replace or supplement the lifestyle hero with a product shot** — Show the sneaker that's driving demand this week. Give it a name and a price. Lifestyle photography belongs lower in the email hierarchy.
- 4. **Explain FLX Rewards in one sentence** — "You're now earning points on every purchase. 100 points = $5 off." That sentence would justify the logo and set the subscriber's expectation.
- 5. **Elevate Launch Calendar** — For a sneaker brand this is the strongest differentiator. Give it its own callout section with one upcoming drop highlighted by name and release date.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome — here's 15% off your first Foot Locker order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your FLX Rewards account is live. Here's what to do next.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop new arrivals, trending styles, and don't miss your first drop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `You just joined FLX Rewards. Earn points on every pair.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — External font `<link>` will be stripped by major clients:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all desktop), and Yahoo all block or ignore `<link>` tags in email `<head>`. Fonts will fall back to system defaults in the majority of opens. Font declarations must be inlined as `<style>` blocks or use `@font-face` embedded within an internal `<style>` tag.

**Bug — CSS selector typo in mobile responsive grid:**
```css
/* Line as written: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row` half should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.col-8`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets `74.25%` width on mobile instead of `66%`, and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has no mobile rule at all. Any layout section using both classes in a fluid row will break on mobile.

**Minor — Aggressive text-size override:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on such a broad selector prevents iOS Mail from scaling up small text — may cause readability failures on older iPhones. Prefer `100%`.
