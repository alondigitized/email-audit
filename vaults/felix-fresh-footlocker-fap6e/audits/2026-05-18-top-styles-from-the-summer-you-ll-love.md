# Top styles from the summer you'll love.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's third consecutive editorial email in two days — same bones as yesterday's "Top spring-ready styles of the week" (which itself scored 8/10), just with a seasonal pivot from "spring" to "summer." The rename alone doesn't earn a new open. The email actually contains two genuinely different stories — a basketball lifestyle hero ("HOOPS Live HERE") and what reads as a graduation gift guide — but it buries both under a subject line so vague you can't tell either one is inside.
- The HOOPS section is the strongest creative piece here. Dark, athletic, culturally specific. The graduation gift section pairs well with the calendar (mid-May = prime grad season) and the colorful sneakers serve that dual-audience moment well. The "Based On Your Style" personalization grid at the bottom is a nice touch.
- The core problem is the same one as last week: there is no offer. No discount, no member price, no shipping hook. Three editorial emails in a row without a reason to buy today means open rates and clicks will keep sliding on each successive send, and this subject line — structurally identical to yesterday's — accelerates that attrition.

## What's working

- **HOOPS hero is punchy.** Dark background, athlete framing, bold typography — clear visual identity and a strong seasonal cultural hook (basketball season).
- **Graduation gift section is well-timed.** Mid-May placement is perfect for graduation shopping, and the colorful sneaker product photography fits the gifting occasion.
- **"Based On Your Style" personalization grid** signals the email knows who it's talking to, even if the rest of the email doesn't reinforce that.
- **Clean, bug-free render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout drift — the design executes reliably.
- **FLX loyalty program** is present in the footer, reinforcing brand ecosystem.

## What's weak

- **No offer, again.** This is the third straight editorial email without a discount, free shipping, or member-exclusive. Engagement erodes each time.
- **Subject is a copy-paste of yesterday's concept.** "Top styles from the summer you'll love" vs "Top spring-ready styles of the week" — swapping one seasonal adjective is not a fresh send.
- **Two campaigns, zero cohesion in subject.** HOOPS and graduation gifts are both legit angles; neither appears in the subject line.
- **Graduation section CTA is undersold.** The gift guide section could drive real purchase intent in the next two weeks but it's treated like a secondary module rather than the hero.
- **No urgency anywhere.** Graduation is the one time-bounded hook available; it goes unused.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with a specific hook — either HOOPS or graduation, not neither.** "Grad gifts they'll actually wear" or "Hoops season is live at Foot Locker" are both more clickable than the current subject. Pick the bigger audience.
- 2. **Promote the graduation section to the hero** — or at minimum give it equal billing in the subject. Graduation is a natural high-intent purchase moment; the HOOPS section is cooler but doesn't have a deadline. Flip the order.
- 3. **Add a single promotional hook.** Even "Free shipping on grad gifts this week" or "FLX members: early access to summer styles" would give this email a reason-to-act-today that the last three lacked.
- 4. **Write an actual preheader** that complements the subject — the graduation angle (or an FLX member benefit) would do work here that the subject line doesn't.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grad gifts they'll actually wear.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Hoops season is live — shop the looks.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From court to ceremony — styles for every summer milestone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FLX members: your summer edit is ready.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in mobile media query:**

```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This leaves `.md-fluid-row .col-9` without a mobile width override. Any 9-column `md-fluid-row` grid section will not collapse correctly on viewports ≤600px.

**External font stylesheet will be stripped by major clients:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

Gmail (web), Yahoo Mail, and Outlook all block or strip `<link>` tags. Font fallback behavior is expected but should be validated — confirm the fallback stack in inline styles is acceptable. If the custom font is load-bearing for rendering, it will silently degrade in most of the audience.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` scope is incomplete:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

`table` is excluded. On older WebKit mobile clients, table-cell text may still be auto-sized. Low severity, but inconsistent.

**Image `alt` attributes:** Cannot verify from truncated source — confirm all `<img>` tags have non-empty `alt` values. Images-off rendering (common in Outlook) will be blank without them.
