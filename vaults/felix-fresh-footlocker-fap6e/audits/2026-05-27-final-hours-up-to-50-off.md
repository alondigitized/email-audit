# FINAL HOURS: Up to 50% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's closing bell on a summer sale sequence that's been running since May 20 — and it reads like one. "FINAL CHANCE" in oversized type, the soccer-ball-accented "50% OFF" treatment, a tightly written body line ("This is it. Today's your last day…"), and a product grid with real prices and star counts all work together cleanly. The email delivers what a last-day send should: urgency in the hero, brand navigation in the middle, product specificity at the bottom.
- The bigger problem isn't the creative — it's the context. According to brand history, Foot Locker already sent "ACT NOW! Up to 50% off top styles ends today!" earlier today and scored a 9. This email is the second urgency hammer on the same day, which taxes any subscriber's goodwill and risks both emails landing in promo or being ignored together. The first "final hours" email burned the scarcity; by the time this one arrives, the deadline has lost its teeth.
- The preheader also wastes its one job: "UP TO 50% OFF | SHOP THE SALE" in the top banner is pulling from the same offer text as the subject, so inbox preview adds zero new reason to open.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is air-tight.** "FINAL CHANCE" → oversized yellow "50% OFF" → single "SHOP THE SALE" CTA — eyes land in the right order with no distraction.
- **Soccer ball icon on the 50% badge** signals a live sports-moment tie-in, making the promo feel seasonally earned rather than generic.
- **"SHOP SALE BY BRAND" with logo tiles** (Nike, NB, Jordan, Adidas) gives scan-and-click shortcut for brand loyalists without cluttering the hero.
- **Product grid has real pricing and star ratings** — Nike Air Max DN at $90 with 4-star/41-review callout is a concrete buying signal, not a placeholder.
- **FLX Rewards teal block in footer** reinforces member value without hijacking the main message.

## What's weak

- **Second urgency email same day** — "ACT NOW! Up to 50% off top styles ends today!" already ran on May 27. Doubling up dilutes both sends and trains subscribers to ignore deadline copy.
- **Subject "FINAL HOURS" is all-caps** — mild spam-filter signal and strident tone after seven days of the same offer.
- **Preheader echoes the subject** — "UP TO 50% OFF | SHOP THE SALE" repeats the offer word-for-word; zero incremental reason to open.
- **No specific deadline time** — "final hours" is vague. "Ends at midnight tonight" or "Ends 11:59 PM ET" would make the urgency actionable.
- **Product grid pricing is uneven** — most items show a single price without visible strikethrough or original-vs-sale comparison, making it hard to confirm the 50% off claim at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the second same-day send, or replace it with a single consolidated final-hours email** — the ACT NOW + FINAL HOURS one-two punch on the same day is the single biggest conversion leak. If you must send twice, give it at least a 12-hour gap and make the second one an SMS, not email.
- 2. **Add a specific countdown deadline** ("Ends tonight at midnight ET") to the hero body copy — replaces vague urgency with a real clock.
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader** to add specificity the subject doesn't have — brand names, a specific shoe, or a lowest-price callout.
- 4. **Show sale pricing clearly** on all grid products — strikethrough original + red sale price on every item, not just one or two.
- 5. **Lowercase the subject** — "Final hours: Up to 50% off" reads less aggressively and clears the all-caps flag.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: Nike, NB & Jordan up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only — up to 50% off. Pick yours before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike Air Max, New Balance, Jordan — sale prices disappear tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your last shot at 50% off ends at midnight. Tap before it's gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `lang` attribute (confirmed):**
```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```
Screen readers cannot determine language without this attribute. Required for WCAG 3.1.1.

**Issue 2 — External `<link>` stylesheet will be stripped (confirmed):**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo all strip `<link>` tags from email `<head>`. Custom font declarations in this file will not apply. Any typography depending solely on those font-face declarations will fall through to the client's default sans-serif. Web-safe fallback fonts must be declared inline in each element's `font-family` stack.

**Issue 3 — CSS selector copy-paste bug (confirmed):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should target `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives no mobile width override, and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets a conflicting 74.25% width on mobile. Any nine-column span in an `md-fluid-row` will render at the wrong width on narrow viewports.

**No issues found:** `charset`, `viewport`, and `format-detection` meta tags are all correctly declared.
