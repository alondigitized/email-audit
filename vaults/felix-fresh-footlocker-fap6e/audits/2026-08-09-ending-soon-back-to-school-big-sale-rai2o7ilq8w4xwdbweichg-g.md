# Ending soon: Back to School Big Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's fifth back-to-school/sale email in a week, and it's the cleanest execution of the "Big Sale" creative they've run — the bold red-to-black gradient hero with oversized outlined type is genuinely eye-catching and reads instantly, even more than the 8/8 version of this same campaign. "Ending soon" urgency in the subject is backed up by "LAST CHANCE SAVINGS" in the hero, which is a rare case of subject and creative actually agreeing.
- Where it falls apart is everything below the fold: after the strong hero, the email dumps into a generic "Shop for the Family" block of four plain text-button rectangles, then a "Based on Your Style" grid that's mostly random (Vans, Saucony, a Nike Knicks t-shirt, Timberland boat shoes — no coherent story). It's a strong top-of-funnel hook wrapped around a lazy, undifferentiated back half.
- Judgement: solid subject/hero alignment lifts this above the brand's average, but the recommendation grid actively drags it down and the offer (up to 40% off, exclusions apply) is the same vague number Foot Locker has used all week.

## What's working

- Hero headline "BACK-TO-SCHOOL BIG SALE" directly matches the subject's "Back to School Big Sale," unlike several recent sends (e.g. 8/7's FLX Fridays mismatch).
- "LAST CHANCE SAVINGS" / "You don't have much time" delivers on the subject's "Ending soon" urgency — a real content match, not just a subject-line gimmick.
- Single unambiguous CTA button "SHOP THE SALE" sits directly under the offer with no scrolling required.
- No render bugs — gradient, typography, and product grid all display cleanly with real images and prices.
- FLX Rewards free shipping/returns banner is visible twice (top nav and footer band), reinforcing loyalty value.

## What's weak

- "Based on Your Style" grid is incoherent — men's running shoes, a boys' preschool sneaker, a graphic tee, and boat shoes have no shared thread, undermining the personalization claim.
- Offer is still just "up to 40% off" with "Exclusions apply" in tiny print — the same vague framing as 8/8 and 8/6, no specific product or category anchor.
- "Shop for the Family" section is four identical black rectangles with no imagery or differentiation — pure filler between the hero and the grid.
- Fifth consecutive back-to-school-adjacent send this week; fatigue risk is real even though this one's creative is stronger.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the "Based on Your Style" grid with a curated back-to-school set (kids' shoes, backpacks-adjacent apparel) so it actually supports the hero's theme instead of contradicting it.
- 2. Add one concrete number to the hero or subhead — a specific category max (e.g., "40% off sneakers") — instead of the exclusions-heavy vague "up to 40%."
- 3. Cut or visually redesign the "Shop for the Family" block — four identical text-only rectangles add scroll depth without adding information; a single image-backed row for Men's/Women's/Kids' would work better.
- 4. Add a real countdown or end-date to back up "Ending soon" — right now the urgency is only asserted in copy, not proven.
