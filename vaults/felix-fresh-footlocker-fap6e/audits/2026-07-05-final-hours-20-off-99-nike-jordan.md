# FINAL HOURS: 20% off $99 Nike & Jordan!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This "FINAL HOURS" email is the fifth consecutive send of the identical 20% off $99 Nike & Jordan promotion — and, critically, the *second* Foot Locker email on July 5 alone, following the "LAST CHANCE" version already audited today. The execution is pixel-perfect: clean dark hero, visible promo code SAVE20, category shortcuts, and a personalized product grid that's the single freshest asset in the entire campaign run. But "final" has now been declared twice on the same afternoon, which turns an urgency hook into a credibility hole.
- The "BASED ON YOUR STYLE" grid with six specific product tiles — Jordan, Nike Air Force 1s, and even an adidas option that suggests real browsing-history personalization — is the only part of this email that has evolved since July 2. Everything else is a pixel-for-pixel rerun. Subscribers who opened the debut have seen this hero five times.
- The email will convert some buyers who genuinely procrastinated. But at this cadence, the unsubscribe and spam-report risks are climbing, and the next campaign's baseline open rate will carry the bruise.

## What's working

- **Personalized product grid**: "BASED ON YOUR STYLE" with six specific product cards, prices, and star ratings is the strongest asset in the entire campaign — relevant and scroll-stopping.
- **Offer is instantly parseable**: "20% OFF $99 / ON NIKE & JORDAN / code SAVE20" is a three-second read with zero ambiguity.
- **FLX Rewards free shipping callout**: The member benefit is surfaced at the bottom and reinforces value for loyalists who might otherwise pass.
- **No render bugs**: Clean dark hero, type is legible, images load — nothing broken.
- **Category shortcuts**: Four bordered "SHOP [CATEGORY]" buttons reduce the click path for someone who wants apparel vs. footwear vs. accessories.

## What's weak

- **Two "final" emails on the same day**: "LAST CHANCE" followed by "FINAL HOURS" on July 5 teaches the list that neither deadline is real. This is the most damaging signal in the send.
- **Hero is unchanged from July 2**: Five sends, one creative. The same two male models in the same pose on the same dark background — the email is invisible to anyone who opened the original.
- **No hard deadline**: "FINAL HOURS" without a specific cutoff ("ends tonight at midnight ET") reads as marketing noise, not a real deadline.
- **Male-only hero casting**: Women's and Kids' are both in the nav, but the hero features only male streetwear models — half the subscriber base is visually unaddressed.
- **Exclusion copy buried**: "Select Nike. Exclusions Apply." sits in small type below the primary CTA; shoppers who click expecting a sitewide 20% will abandon when they hit the exclusion wall.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop the double-send immediately.** One urgency close per day is the hard rule. Sending "LAST CHANCE" and "FINAL HOURS" on the same afternoon destroys deadline credibility for every future campaign — that's a much bigger loss than whatever incremental conversion this sends delivers.
- 2. **Anchor the urgency to a real time**: Replace "FINAL HOURS" in the hero with "Ends tonight at midnight ET" — specificity converts; vague urgency no longer does.
- 3. **Refresh the hero creative**: Swap the July 2 model shot for a product flatlay or a colorway that hasn't appeared in prior sends. A single new visual element breaks subscriber pattern-recognition.
- 4. **Promote the personalized grid above the category buttons**: "BASED ON YOUR STYLE" is the best-performing asset and it lives below the fold — elevate it.
- 5. **Add exclusion context near the CTA**: "Jordan and most Nike styles included — see full exclusions" prevents click-then-abandon rage from shoppers hit by surprise exclusions.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo in mobile stylesheet:**

```css
/* Line in <style> block: */
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. As written, this overrides the earlier `.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }` rule (later rule wins), causing any 8-column cell inside a `.md-fluid-row` to render at 74.25% width on screens ≤600px. Impacts any two-thirds-width content block on mobile.

**External font via `<link>`:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

Most email clients (Outlook all versions, Samsung Mail, some iOS/Android) strip or ignore `<link>` tags. Bluecore typically handles font fallbacks, but confirm the fallback stack in that stylesheet renders acceptably without the custom font loaded.

**`<head>` stylesheet scope:**

The entire responsive CSS block is inside a `<head>` `<style>` tag. Gmail (web) strips `<head>` and all styles within it. This means all `.mobile-hide`, `.desktop-hide`, `.flex-image`, column-width overrides, and widget-specific overrides are inert in Gmail web. This is a known limitation of the Bluecore template architecture — flag only if Gmail open share is significant for this list.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` is applied globally:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```

Disabling text-size-adjust on all elements prevents iOS Mail from scaling small text to readable sizes. This is a common but poor practice — font sizes below 13px on mobile will not auto-scale.
