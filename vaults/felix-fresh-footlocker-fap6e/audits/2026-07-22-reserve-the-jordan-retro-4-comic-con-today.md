# Reserve the Jordan Retro 4 'Comic Con' today!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth consecutive "Reserve the Jordan Retro 4" send in the Foot Locker rotation, but the colorway switch to 'Comic Con' makes this a genuinely new product — not a re-skin of the Birds of Paradise campaign. The pink/white hero sneaker image is sharp and prominent, the "RESERVE NOW" CTA is unambiguous, and the Comic Con tie-in is seasonally credible (San Diego Comic-Con falls in late July). That said, subscribers who opened three straight Birds of Paradise emails in the same subject-line formula may have learned to expect these and either auto-open or auto-ignore — the template has gotten predictable fast.
- The email stacks four blocks: the Retro 4 reservation hero, a Jordan Retro 3 product block, a "Show Up In Style" editorial insert, and a brand spotlight. That "Show Up In Style" block feels like content leftover from the July 21 back-to-school editorial and creates a tonal mismatch — you're here to reserve a hyped drop, not browse lifestyle looks.
- Verdict: solid execution on a reliable formula. The new colorway earns fresh attention, but the identical subject structure and the editorial filler block leave lift on the table.

## What's working

- Hero sneaker image is large, centered, and immediately communicates the product — the pink/white Comic Con colorway pops cleanly against the light background.
- "RESERVE NOW" is the most specific, action-oriented CTA in recent Foot Locker sends — better than the generic "SHOP NOW" formula used in the secondary blocks.
- Comic Con co-branding is timely (late July aligns with SDCC), giving this reservation cultural context the Birds of Paradise sends lacked.
- Jordan Retro 3 secondary block adds a second reason to click for shoppers who passed on the Retro 4.
- Clean layout with no visible render bugs — no broken images, no overlapping text, no truncated content.

## What's weak

- "Reserve the Jordan Retro 4 … today!" is now the fourth send using this exact subject-line structure in three days — even with a new product, the formula fatigue is real.
- "SHOW UP IN STYLE" editorial block is a stylistic non-sequitur in a hype-drop email — it dilutes urgency and looks copy-pasted from yesterday's back-to-school send.
- No reservation deadline or drop time is visible in the hero — "today!" in the subject creates urgency the email body doesn't reinforce with a specific window.
- No FLX member early access callout, which is a missed hook for loyalty subscribers who are the most likely to actually reserve.
- Secondary block CTAs all read "SHOP NOW," making them feel lower-stakes than the hero reservation — the hierarchy collapses below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the subject formula.** Three Birds of Paradise + one Comic Con = four identical-structure subjects in four days. Lead with the cultural angle instead of the product name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Comic Con drops today — Jordan 4 reservation open now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Comic Con 4s are here. Reserve before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Iconic colorway. Limited reservation window. Secure your pair.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FLX members: get early access to the Retro 4 Comic Con before anyone else.`
- 2. **Add a drop clock or specific deadline to the hero.** "Reserve by [time] today" turns vague urgency into a real reason to act now rather than after lunch.
- 3. **Cut or move the "Show Up In Style" block.** It belongs in a separate editorial email (like the July 21 back-to-school send), not sandwiched between two hyped Jordan drops. Replace it with sizing/availability context for the Retro 4.
- 4. **Surface FLX early-access benefit above the fold.** If members get priority reservation, say so in the hero subhead — that single line differentiates this from a public-facing drop page.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector typo — responsive grid break:**

```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.col-8`. As written, `.col-8` gets two conflicting width rules (66% and 74.25%) on mobile and `.col-9` has no `md-fluid-row` override at all. If any 9-column fluid layout is used, it will not collapse correctly on small screens.

**Preheader encoding artifact:**

```html
<div style="display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden;">
  &amp; don't miss the Jordan Retro 3 'True Blue'.
```

The preheader text begins with `&amp;`, which renders as a literal `&` character in inbox preview snippets (e.g., Gmail, Apple Mail). Likely intended as a sentence continuation (e.g., `"… & don't miss…"`) but the leading `&` without context reads as malformed copy in preview.

**Viewport meta — missing `initial-scale`:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```

Missing `initial-scale=1`. While most modern mobile clients handle this gracefully, it can cause zoom behavior inconsistencies on some Android WebView-based clients. Low severity.

**No dark mode media query** visible in the `<style>` block. Not a hard defect, but Foot Locker images with white backgrounds may invert or clash in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) if images lack dark-mode alternates.
