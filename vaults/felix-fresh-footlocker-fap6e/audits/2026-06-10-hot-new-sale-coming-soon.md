# Hot new sale coming soon!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a teaser email — its job is to build anticipation for a sale dropping tomorrow, not to close a transaction today. The hero execution is polished: bold magenta on black, "MEMBERS ONLY" in stacked oversized type, and a clear FLX Rewards frame that positions loyalty subscribers as insiders with advance notice. That's the right instinct after the relentless May discount drumbeat.
- The problem is that "something big" without any specifics is a bet that subscribers still care enough to wait. Foot Locker burned through a lot of credibility in May — "FINAL HOURS" sent twice, a sale extension, "ACT NOW! Ends Today!" — and followed that with the June 5 Father's Day pivot. Teaser mechanics only work when the audience trusts the payoff. That trust is fragile here.
- The bottom half of the email undermines the mood entirely: a standard "Looks You'll Love" carousel featuring UGG Minis, Nike React, Crocs, and Tasman Joggers reads like filler copy-pasted from a different send. The two halves don't feel like one email — they feel like a teaser and an organic grid stapled together.

## What's working

- Magenta/black hero is visually punchy and reads as "something different" against the May sale aesthetic.
- FLX Rewards framing is well-placed — centering the tease on loyalty members makes subscribers feel rewarded, not just retargeted.
- "Something big is coming **tomorrow**" gives a specific, one-day window rather than an indefinite "soon."
- "JOIN FLX FOR FREE" CTA is clear, low-friction, and a useful secondary conversion hook for non-members.
- Star ratings and prices in the product grid provide some browsable value for the scroller.

## What's weak

- Subject line "Hot new sale coming soon!" is pure filler — no category, no magnitude, no urgency signal strong enough to move the needle in a crowded inbox.
- No visible preheader — the inbox preview is almost certainly leaking nav text ("Mens Womens Kids Sale"), wasting the second line of the open.
- The "Looks You'll Love" carousel is tonally disconnected from the Members Only teaser; it dilutes the exclusive, anticipatory frame the hero built.
- "Something big" is empty hype — after back-to-back "FINAL HOURS" and "LAST CHANCE" sends in May, vague superlatives from this sender land with less punch than intended.
- The sale category is never revealed — footwear? apparel? site-wide? Not knowing what to expect reduces the urgency to come back tomorrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tease the category or scale.** One concrete signal — "Up to 50% off sneakers drops tomorrow" or "your most-wishlisted styles just got cheaper" — converts empty hype into something actionable. Subscribers need a reason to care before they'll set a mental reminder.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject.** "Hot new sale coming soon!" is the weakest possible framing for a members-exclusive advance notice.
- **Subject Alt A:** `FLX Members: big sale drops tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tomorrow only: members get early access`
- 3. **Add a real preheader.** The inbox preview is currently thrown away.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Something big is landing for FLX members — be first.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Not a member yet? Join free before tomorrow.`
- 4. **Cut or reframe the product carousel.** Either remove "Looks You'll Love" or merchandise it explicitly as "dropping in tomorrow's sale" — that turns passive filler into a teaser amplifier.
- 5. **Add a hard timestamp.** "Tomorrow" works, but "Drops tomorrow at 9AM ET" gives subscribers a reason to set a reminder and makes the urgency feel earned rather than vague.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED BUG] CSS selector typo in mobile media query:**

```css
/* Line in <style> block: */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
/*                        ^^^^^^^^^^^^^ should be .md-fluid-row .col-9 */
```

Effect: `.md-fluid-row .col-9` never receives its 74.25% mobile width override (renders at desktop width instead). `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives two competing rules — 66% (correct) and 74.25% (erroneous) — with 74.25% winning due to cascade order.
