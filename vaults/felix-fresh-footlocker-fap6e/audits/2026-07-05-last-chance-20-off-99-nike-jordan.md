# LAST CHANCE: 20% off $99 Nike & Jordan.
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth consecutive Foot Locker send on the exact same creative and offer — July 2 debut (9/10), July 3 extension (10/10), July 4 urgency-close (7/10), and now July 5 "LAST CHANCE." At a glance the execution is fine: the hero lands hard, the offer is clear, and the product cards give a subscriber something to browse. But four sends in four days on the same creative is not confidence — it's desperation. The fireworks background earns one more day of seasonality before it becomes stale. Code SAVE20 is visible, the FLX Rewards banner is prominent, and star-rated product tiles do real work. This is the last credible firing of this particular bullet.
- One off-note in the personalized product grid: an adidas product appears alongside the Nike & Jordan offer. That's a minor but genuine confusion signal — a subscriber who opens on "20% off Nike & Jordan" and sees a competitor brand in their "Based on Your Style" row will notice the mismatch.
- The email earns its numbers on pure execution. What it cannot earn back is the attention debt accumulated from three prior sends. Anyone who was going to act has already acted or tuned out.

## What's working

- Hero hierarchy is immediate — "LAST CHANCE / 20% OFF / $99" reads in under a second with no scrolling required.
- Code SAVE20 appears three times (top banner, hero body, and by inference the preview) — zero chance a subscriber misses it.
- Fireworks background earns the July 5 date; seasonal relevance is real and not forced.
- Star ratings and prices on the Jordan Son of Me and Jordan AJ / Low SE tiles add genuine purchase signals without cluttering the layout.
- FLX Rewards section is prominent and actionable — app download and SMS opt-in both present.

## What's weak

- Fourth consecutive send on the same offer kills cadence. The July 3 extension already scored this sequence's best marks; every send since is diminishing returns on the same subscribers.
- "LAST CHANCE" in ALL CAPS in the subject line trips spam-signal heuristics and is visually aggressive after three prior screams.
- An adidas product appearing in the "Based on Your Style" grid undermines the Nike & Jordan hero offer — brand confusion in a sale-specific email.
- Preview text ("Today's your last day to save big on top styles from Nike & Jordan") echoes the subject concept rather than adding a new dimension; the code is the only new information.
- "Select Styles, Exclusions Apply" sits directly below the CTA in small text — visible but potentially friction-creating for subscribers who click and can't find their target pair on sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the send, or pivot the angle.** A fifth send tomorrow on the same offer is almost certainly list-negative. If the sale does extend, reframe entirely — lead with a specific product (the Air Force 1 at $116 → effectively $93 with the code) rather than the umbrella offer.
- 2. **Pull "LAST CHANCE" out of ALL CAPS** in the subject — "Last chance:" or "Ends tonight:" reads with the same urgency without the spam flag.
- 3. **Remove the adidas tile** from the personalized product grid on a Nike & Jordan–specific email. Either filter it out programmatically or replace with a fourth Nike or Jordan option.
- 4. **Lead the preview with the code**, not the rephrased subject — "Use SAVE20 at checkout — ends midnight tonight." That's new information.
- 5. **Add a specific product hero** above the brand-logo tiles — e.g., one clean shot of the Air Force 1 with its effective post-discount price — to give fence-sitters a concrete anchor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: use SAVE20 on Nike & Jordan ($99+)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — Air Force 1 & Jordans, 20% off with SAVE20`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use SAVE20 at checkout — offer closes midnight tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jordan Son of Me, AF1, AJ Low — all in the sale right now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `lang` attribute (WCAG 3.1.1 / A):**

```html
<html>   <!-- no lang="en" -->
```

Screen readers cannot determine language for pronunciation. Required for WCAG conformance and correct hyphenation in some clients.

**Issue 2 — CSS selector copy-paste error in responsive grid:**

```css
/* Line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` is listed twice (once here, once correctly for `col-8`) and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has no mobile width rule. Any layout using `.md-fluid-row .col-9` will not collapse correctly on narrow viewports.

**Issue 3 — Aggressive `text-size-adjust` applied globally:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

`!important` prevents per-element overrides. If footer/legal copy is rendered at a small point size, iOS Safari cannot scale it up for readability.

- Dark mode `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` handling.
