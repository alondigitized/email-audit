# FINAL HOURS: up to 50% off!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh Foot Locker blast in six days, and it recycles the exact subject line — "FINAL HOURS: up to 50% off!" — word-for-word from the June 22 send that scored 7/10 in this audit run. The creative is competent: lime-green hero, oversized "50% OFF," an On Running sneaker as the hero product, clean six-item recommendation grid with star ratings and prices, and a FLX Rewards free-shipping callout. On its own, the email is a perfectly functional promotional send.
- The problem is entirely above the fold. Subscribers who received "FINAL HOURS" on June 22, watched it get extended on June 23, extended again on June 24, and "LAST CHANCE"-d again on June 26 will recognize this immediately as a scheduling artifact, not a genuine closing window. The urgency phrase has been fully devalued. This send doesn't reset the clock — it confirms that every future urgency claim from Foot Locker can be safely ignored until further notice.
- This is where the campaign's credibility officially breaks. The email creative earns its scores mechanically; the campaign strategy does not.

## What's working

- Lime-green hero with dominant "UP TO 50% OFF" typography — the offer is impossible to miss.
- On Running shoe is a strong hero product choice: aspirational, on-trend, and clean against the green background.
- FLX Rewards purple bar ("FREE STANDARD SHIPPING") adds a loyalty hook that differentiates for members.
- Six-product grid with visible star ratings and prices provides multiple click-entry points without clutter.
- 28-character subject is perfectly mobile-safe — zero truncation risk.

## What's weak

- **Recycled subject line:** "FINAL HOURS: up to 50% off!" is identical to the June 22 send — an engaged subscriber clocks this immediately.
- **Burned urgency:** After two blasts on June 22, extensions on June 23 and June 24, and a "LAST CHANCE" on June 26, there is no credibility left in any time-pressure language from this sender.
- **Product grid contamination:** The Nike Football jersey (visible mid-grid, green pattern) is a jarring mismatch in a sneaker-focused send — looks like a recommendation algorithm leak.
- **Low-contrast CTA:** "SHOP THE SALE" appears as an outlined button on a white section background — far less tap-visible than a filled lime-green or black button would be.
- **No differentiation:** Nothing in this send — not the copy, not the creative angle, not the product selection — gives a subscriber a reason this email is distinct from the previous six.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire "FINAL HOURS" permanently for this campaign.** It has no credibility left. Any new close needs a completely different frame — or the brand needs to actually close the sale and stop sending.
- 2. **Fill the CTA button.** Outlined white on white is low contrast. A lime-green or solid black fill matches the hero palette and meaningfully improves mobile tap-target visibility.
- 3. **Filter the recommendation grid to footwear and headwear only.** One football jersey in a sneaker email is enough to break the category coherence — cap the algorithm to relevant SKU types.
- 4. **Add a verifiable hard deadline.** "Ends tonight at midnight PT" is specific, checkable, and partially restores credibility that "FINAL HOURS" has burned. Vague urgency is now worse than none.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale ends tonight — sneakers from $49`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `On Cloud, New Balance & Nike at half price — final 24 hours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FLX members get free shipping. Shop before midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector copy-paste bug (confirmed):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Result: on medium-fluid layouts, `.col-8` gets the wrong width (74.25% instead of 66%) and `.col-9` has no mobile override at all.

**External font load will silently fail in most clients:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
Gmail (web), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Apple Mail with "Protect Mail Activity" enabled all block or ignore external `<link>` stylesheets. No declared `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the truncated CSS. If the font is load-bearing for brand rendering, it needs an inline `@font-face` block with a safe fallback (e.g. `Arial, sans-serif`).

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html>
```
Should be `<html lang="en">`. Required for screen readers and WCAG 3.1.1. Also triggers some spam filters.

**`format-detection` is incomplete:**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Does not suppress address or date auto-linking (`address=no, date=no`). Minor, but can cause iOS Mail to auto-link text in unexpected ways.
