# Top styles that dad will love for Father's Day.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Foot Locker makes the right call here: after nearly two weeks of relentless "UP TO 50% OFF / LAST CHANCE / FINAL HOURS" drumbeating — the same move they pulled through May 27-31 — dropping a seasonal occasion email is exactly the creative reset this list needs. The multi-module structure is smart: hero gift guide, sweepstakes for engagement, topical NBA jersey hook, and an always-on AF1 spotlight. That's four distinct reasons to click, which is more than the discount-only sends gave subscribers.
- The execution is solid but not sharp. "Trending Kicks for Dad" is warm and clear, but the email never commits to a specific offer — no % off, no free shipping, no price anchor — so it's asking subscribers to browse rather than act. With Father's Day 10 days out, there's a real conversion opportunity being left on the table. The subject line is similarly pleasant but passive; "Top styles that dad will love" reads like a Pinterest board caption, not a shopping trigger.
- The Spurs vs. Knicks module is the creative highlight: timely (June NBA action), specific (Brunson #11), and the "rematch from 1999" nostalgia hook is the kind of copy the sale sequence never had. This is the energy the full email needed more of.

## What's working

- **The creative pivot lands.** After the exhausting sale countdown sequence, a gift-occasion frame with warm hero copy ("Trending Kicks for Dad") is a welcome change in register.
- **Four-module structure gives multiple entry points.** Hero, sweepstakes, NBA jerseys, AF1 product — a subscriber who ignores the Father's Day angle might still click on the Knicks jersey.
- **NBA module is genuinely topical.** "Rematch from 1999" with a specific Brunson jersey image gives the email a cultural hook the May sends lacked entirely.
- **Sweepstakes is an engagement play that adds value.** "$500 Foot Locker gift card" as a give-to-win mechanism is a credible incentive that doesn't require a purchase intent.
- **No render bugs** — all images loaded, text is legible, buttons visible, layout intact across the email.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer in the hero.** The "NEW MARKDOWNS ADDED" banner is a small header strip — the main email body has zero price signals. Father's Day is June 15; this email needed at least "free shipping on gifts" or a specific product price to move from browse to buy.
- **Subject line is soft.** "Top styles that dad will love" is pleasant but neither urgent nor specific — no product name, no offer hook, no time signal for a deadline 10 days out.
- **Father's Day deadline is never stated.** There's no "order by June 13 for delivery" or "10 days left" language anywhere visible. That's the strongest urgency lever this email had and it was unused.
- **"Find Your Force" module breaks the Father's Day frame.** The AF1 section reads as evergreen product content dropped in, not a curated Father's Day gift — disrupts the cohesive gifting narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a shipping deadline to the hero.** A single line — "Order by June 13 for guaranteed Father's Day delivery" — converts this from a gift inspiration email into a purchase trigger. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Sharpen the subject to include a specific product or hook.** The current line is too soft. Lead with the NBA angle or a specific shoe that's actually trending.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad-approved: Jordans, jerseys & the AF1.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day is 10 days away. Here's what he wants.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by June 13 for guaranteed delivery — shop trending kicks for Dad.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kicks, jerseys, and a $500 sweepstakes — Father's Day sorted.`
- 3. **Align the AF1 module to Father's Day gifting.** Reframe "Find Your Force" as "Get Dad His Colorway" — same product, same CTA, tighter thematic fit.
- 4. **Sequence the sweepstakes as the secondary module, not the hero.** Right now the sweepstakes sits above the NBA and AF1 content — but the product modules are stronger clickthrough candidates. Lead with purchase intent, use sweepstakes as the hook for non-buyers.

### 1. Technical Summary

Bluecore-served email (`bc.footlocker.com`) with standard responsive table layout. One confirmed CSS selector bug in the mobile grid and an external `<link>` stylesheet that will be stripped by most inbox providers.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External font stylesheet via `<link>` tag — will be stripped**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo Mail, and most iOS/Android mail clients strip `<link>` tags entirely. The custom font will silently fail across the majority of clients. If fallback font stacks are not defined in every inline `font-family` declaration, this degrades to a browser default. Verify fallbacks are present in the truncated body markup.

**Bluecore click-wrapping** — not verifiable from the truncated source, but standard for Bluecore deployments. Confirm that tracked redirect URLs include UTM params before the final destination (covered in §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector typo in mobile grid (confirmed):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row` selector targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. Any `.md-fluid-row .col-9` element will miss its 74.25% width rule and fall through to the `width: 100% !important` catch-all from the `.row .col-9` rule. This causes `.md-fluid-row` 9-column cells to collapse to full-width on mobile. The correct rule should be:
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-9 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```
Screen readers cannot reliably identify the document language. Required for WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Using `none` (rather than `100%`) completely disables iOS font scaling for all elements. This will break accessibility for users with larger system font sizes set. Should be `100%`.

### 5. Compliance

**Sending domain subdomain vs. DMARC alignment:**
`From: footlocker@bc.footlocker.com`
`bc.footlocker.com` is Bluecore's sending infrastructure subdomain. If Foot Locker's DMARC policy at `footlocker.com` uses strict identifier alignment (`adkim=s`), mail signed under `bc.footlocker.com` will fail DMARC alignment. Organizational alignment (`adkim=r`, the default) passes if DKIM is signed under `footlocker.com`. Verify DMARC record:
```
dig TXT _dmarc.footlocker.com
```

**Preheader padding technique is confirmed:**
```
&#160;&#8204; (×60+)
```
Whitespace-stuffing via NBSP + ZWNJ characters is functional but inflates message size and can trigger spam filters on aggressive scanning engines. Acceptable for major ESPs but worth noting.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — not visible in the truncated source. Must be confirmed present in the full HTML footer. These are required per CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) and §5(a)(3).
