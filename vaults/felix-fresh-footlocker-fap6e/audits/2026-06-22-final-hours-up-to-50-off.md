# FINAL HOURS: up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- "FINAL HOURS" is Foot Locker's second blast on June 22 — the brand already sent "Last Chance: up to 50% off top styles!" earlier today (scored 8/10 in this run). The creative is competently built: the "FINAL CALL" hero is clean and legible, the Shop by Brand strip gives fast navigation to Nike/NB/Jordan/Adidas, and the Curated For You grid with actual prices and star ratings is more useful than the generic blasts from earlier in the week. The FLX member pricing on individual product tiles is a genuine lift.
- The problem is entirely strategic, not executional. When a subscriber has already received a "Last Chance" email from you today, "FINAL HOURS" doesn't feel like urgency — it feels like a system re-send. The deadline credibility that made the earlier June 22 send earn its 8/10 is exactly what this one depletes. The creative can't compensate for the cadence damage.
- The email is a solid template being used one send too many. Ship this tomorrow as a post-sale wrap, or drop it entirely. Two near-identical "this ends today" emails in one inbox session read as automation failure, not genuine urgency.

## What's working

- **"FINAL CALL" hero is bold and unambiguous** — the offer reads instantly at any scroll depth.
- **Shop Sale by Brand strip** with four brand logos (Nike, NB, Jordan, Adidas) is the most useful navigation element in this campaign's recent run.
- **Curated For You product grid** shows real thumbnails, prices, and star ratings — more scannable than a generic percentage claim.
- **FLX member pricing is visible inline** on product tiles (e.g., "Special Member Price" callout), which rewards loyalty subscribers.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Second sale blast same day** — the June 22 "Last Chance" already established the deadline; this "FINAL HOURS" send undermines it rather than reinforcing it.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA dumps to a general sale page** — no specific product or category anchor after scrolling past the brand logos.
- **"Up to 50% off" hero claim is vague** — the curated products visible are $65–$110, which doesn't clearly demonstrate the maximum discount.
- **"FINAL HOURS" in all caps** in the subject is a mild spam-signal that stacks on top of an already hard-sell send pattern this week.
- **No personalization visible** — no name, no category preference, no "based on your history" framing anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate to one June 22 send.** The earlier "Last Chance" email was the right vehicle. This one should not have been sent — or should replace it, not stack on top of it. The compounding urgency destroys credibility faster than any creative can rebuild it.
- 2. **Anchor the hero to a specific markdown.** Instead of the generic umbrella, lead with the steepest concrete deal visible in the curated section (e.g., "Nike AF1 Low — now $64.99, was $110"). Price anchoring outperforms vague percentage claims every time.
- 3. **Make the CTA category-specific.** "SHOP MEN'S SALE" or "SHOP YOUR SIZE" beats "SHOP THE SALE" — it promises relevance before the click, which lifts CTR.
- 4. **Surface the FLX member deal higher.** The member pricing is buried below the fold; move a "FLX members save an extra X%" line into the hero where it adds urgency that's actually differentiated.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: Nike, NB, Adidas up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your FLX deal ends at midnight — up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The biggest markdowns of the summer are gone by midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nike, New Balance, Adidas — final picks before it's over.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS grid selector mismatch:**

```css
/* Line in mobile @media block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The selector pairs `.fluid-row .col-9` (correct) with `.md-fluid-row .col-8` (wrong — should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`). Effect:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` renders at 74.25% instead of its intended 66%
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has **no mobile width rule at all**, so it falls through to inherited/default width

This will misrender any 8- or 9-column `md-fluid-row` blocks on mobile.

**Text size adjust:**
```css
-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important;
```
`none` fully disables user zoom scaling. The non-prefixed `text-size-adjust` property is absent. Using `100%` instead of `none` is the safer standard — `none` can trigger accessibility violations in some audit tools.

**Accessibility:** No `role`, `aria-label`, or `alt` attribute content is visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm image alt text coverage for the product widgets.
