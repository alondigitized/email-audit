# Get up to 40% off the latest New Balance styles.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the third time Foot Locker has run this exact purple-gradient New Balance hero in six days — 8/17 ("Get up to 40% off top New Balance styles!"), 8/18 ("Tax free weekend is here" with zero tax-free content), and now 8/22 with a fresh subject but the identical creative. The hero itself is clean — bold "UP TO 40% OFF," legible sneaker shot, clear CTA button — but there's a glaring copy bug baked into the banner: "NEW BALANCE DEALS ENDS TOMORROW" is a grammatical error sitting in 40pt type at the very top of the email, which is hard to unsee once spotted.
- The bigger miss is the "Looks You'll Love" product grid: it's six items, and not a single one is a New Balance shoe. It's adidas Samba, Vans, Nike, adidas slides — an off-brand product rail directly under a New Balance-branded hero, which undercuts the entire premise of the email and reads like a template that never got swapped for the right merchandising feed.
- Given the subject-matter recycling flagged on 8/18 and 8/20, and now this internal mismatch between hero and grid, this send is a step down from the well-executed 8/20 Jordan reservation email (9/10). Judgment: mid-pack execution dragged down by a visible copy error and a merchandising mismatch that a subscriber would notice within two seconds of scrolling.

## What's working

- Hero offer is unambiguous: "UP TO 40% OFF" in large white type against a high-contrast purple gradient.
- Single clear CTA — "SHOP THE SALE" — bordered button with good whitespace around it.
- FLX loyalty tie-in ("FLX members get 3X points on all New Balance purchases") gives members a reason to click beyond the base discount.
- No broken images or overlapping text; the render is technically clean.
- "Exclusions apply" is present but small and unobtrusive rather than buried in a wall of fine print.

## What's weak

- Visible grammar error in the hero: "NEW BALANCE DEALS ENDS TOMORROW" (subject/verb mismatch) in the largest type on the page.
- "Looks You'll Love" grid is 100% off-brand — adidas Samba x3, Vans, Nike, adidas slides — with zero New Balance product, directly undermining the New Balance hero above it.
- This is the same purple-gradient New Balance creative Foot Locker has now sent on 8/17, 8/18, and 8/22 — heavy creative fatigue for anyone opening consecutive emails.
- No specific New Balance styles, colorways, or price points are shown anywhere in the email — just one generic hero shoe and a percentage.
- "Deal ends tomorrow" urgency isn't dated, so a recipient can't tell if this is the same deadline as the earlier sends or a new one.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the hero headline grammar immediately — "NEW BALANCE DEAL ENDS TOMORROW" or restructure entirely (e.g., "NEW BALANCE SALE ENDS TOMORROW"). This is the single highest-impact fix; it's the largest text on the page and currently reads as broken.
- 2. Replace the "Looks You'll Love" grid with actual New Balance styles — right now it's advertising a competitor-adjacent assortment under a New Balance banner, which is a merchandising/relevance failure, not just a nice-to-have.
- 3. Retire or meaningfully refresh the purple-gradient hero creative — it's run three times in six days; swap the shoe angle, background, or layout so this doesn't read as a repeat to anyone who opened 8/17 or 8/18.
- 4. Add a real date or countdown timer to "ends tomorrow" so the urgency is credible and distinguishable from the prior sends' urgency claims.
- 5. Surface 2-3 specific New Balance SKUs with prices in the hero or immediately below it, mirroring the product-card treatment already used for the grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Balance sale ends tomorrow — up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: New Balance styles up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `FLX members earn 3X points on every New Balance pair.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop 990v6, 2002R, and more before the sale ends.`
