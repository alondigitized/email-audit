# Reserve the Jordan Retro 4 'Birds of Paradise' today!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's second consecutive send for the same product — yesterday's subject was "Reserve the Jordan Retro 4 'Birds of Paradise' today." (period); today's adds only an exclamation mark. For subscribers who saw both and didn't act, the decision is already made. The creative makes the fatigue problem worse: instead of a sharper second-push angle, the email packs three separate reservation blocks — Jordan Retro 4, a Vans Souvenir Pack, and then a second Jordan Retro 4 block labeled "TOPIC.COM." That structure splits attention three ways and buries the lead product underneath a confused multi-brand layout.
- The countdown timers are the email's strongest asset: legible, prominent, and present in every block. The "RESERVE NOW" CTAs are clear. But the unexplained "TOPIC.COM" label on the third block will stop subscribers cold — most won't know it's a Foot Locker affiliate, and it reads as cross-promotion for a competitor. The Vans block adds further noise to what should be a focused single-product drop alert.
- Verdict: the first send (July 20) did the job well enough. This repeat adds nothing — no new angle, no new incentive, no tighter urgency. It's a copy-paste with a punctuation change.

## What's working

- **Countdown timers** are legible, prominent, and create real urgency — three separate timers all immediately scannable
- **"RESERVE NOW" CTA** is unambiguous in every block — consistent verb + noun format
- **Jordan Retro 4 hero image** is large, colorway detail reads clearly at a glance
- **Dark aesthetic** signals premium drop energy; on-brand for a sneaker release
- **Top loyalty banner** keeps the "GET 20% OFF WHEN YOU..." membership benefit in view above the fold

## What's weak

- **Day-2 repeat, near-identical subject** — no subscriber who ignored yesterday's email is moved by today's identical ask
- **Three reservation blocks dilute focus** — Jordan appears twice (blocks 1 and 3), making the email read like a drop-calendar catalog, not a reservation call to action
- **"TOPIC.COM" brand label is unexplained** — subscribers don't know this is a Foot Locker banner; it creates unnecessary confusion and looks like a competitor insertion
- **Vans Souvenir Pack breaks the Jordan narrative** — feels like padding, not a curated companion drop
- **No new hook for the second send** — no "X pairs left," no early-access framing, no member perk not visible in the July 20 email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the Vans block and the TOPIC.COM Jordan duplicate.** Single-product hero, one timer, one CTA. The multi-block format is killing urgency.
- 2. **Give the second push a new angle.** Add "Last 24 hours" or "X reservations remaining" — anything that makes this email meaningfully different from yesterday's instead of a rerun.
- 3. **Explain the cross-banner link or remove it.** If TOPIC.COM reservations stay, label it: "Also available via our partner store" — don't let an unfamiliar brand name create hesitation.
- 4. **Cut the TOP RECOMMENDATIONS grid or move it to a separate digest.** Scroll depth past the third reservation block is working against conversion, not for it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector typo — responsive grid breakage (confirmed):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row .col-9` selector is missing; instead `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is declared twice. On mobile, any `md-fluid-row col-9` column will receive no explicit width, falling back to browser default. The preceding `.md-fluid-row .col-8` at 66% also loses its overriding specificity in the cascade.

**Viewport meta — missing `initial-scale`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Should be `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Without `initial-scale=1`, some iOS browsers may zoom on load.

**External font dependency — no declared fallback stack:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
If this Bluecore CDN call fails or is blocked (common in Gmail/Outlook), no fallback font-family is declared in the inline styles visible here. Verify the font stack in the widget `block-content td` rules includes system fonts.
