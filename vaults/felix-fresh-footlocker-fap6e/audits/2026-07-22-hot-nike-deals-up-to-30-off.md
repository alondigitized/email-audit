# 🔥 Hot Nike Deals! Up to 30% Off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's fourth consecutive email on the same "Up to 30% off Nike & Jordan" campaign that launched July 19. The creative refresh is "More Styles Added!" — a legitimate reason to re-engage, but the structure is nearly identical to the July 20 send. Subscribers who've opened the last three emails will recognize the layout before they read a word.
- The hero is bold and legible, FLX perks are surfaced early in the header, and the Shop by Brand tiles let you self-segment to Nike or Jordan instantly. That's all solid. But the subject line stacks three spam-adjacent signals in six words (🔥, "Hot," "!"), and the "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" product grid undermines the Nike promise by surfacing adidas Samba OG and what appears to be adidas Football gear. If the subject brought you in for Nike deals, adidas showing up without framing is a trust tax.
- Four emails, same promo, no hard deadline, no meaningful structural change. Cadence is eroding equity faster than the offer is converting.

## What's working

- **"More Styles Added!"** is a specific, credible reason for an existing subscriber to re-engage — better than just re-sending the same hero
- **30% off Nike & Jordan** is immediately legible in the hero; no ambiguity about the offer
- **Shop by Brand tiles** (Nike swoosh / Jordan Jumpman) are a clean self-segmentation mechanism
- **FLX free shipping & returns** surfaced in the header — good friction-reducer for fence-sitters
- **Star ratings in the product grid** add lightweight social proof without adding visual weight

## What's weak

- **Subject line is three spam signals deep**: 🔥 emoji + "Hot" + "!" in rapid succession reads aggressive for a major national retailer
- **adidas in a "Nike Deals" email**: the product grid shows Samba OG and adidas Football alongside Nike — directly breaks the subject-line promise
- **"For a limited time only" has no deadline**: this phrase has appeared in every email since July 19; it has lost all urgency
- **Fourth email in four days on the same deal** with no meaningful structural differentiation — cadence is now a liability
- **"SHOP SALE" CTA is maximally generic** — it could belong to any sale email from any brand

## Recommendations

- 1. **Purge the adidas items from this send** (or clearly label a secondary "Also on sale" bucket) — the subject says Nike, the grid must deliver Nike
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** everywhere "limited time" appears: "Ends Sunday" or "Through July 24 only" — a specific date converts; vague copy doesn't
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to drop the spam stack and distinguish this send from prior ones:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `More styles added to the Nike & Jordan sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `30% off Nike just got bigger — new styles in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh Jordan colorways + Nike classics now included — shop before it ends`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Nike styles dropped into the sale. Ends Sunday.`
- 4. **Replace "SHOP SALE" with "Shop Nike 30% Off"** — closes the loop from subject line to click

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed CSS selector bug:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written, `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets width `74.25%` instead of its correct `66%`, while `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no mobile override at all. This will misalign 8-column and 9-column fluid-row blocks on mobile.

**External font stylesheet dependency:**
```
https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css
```
`<link rel="stylesheet">` in email `<head>` is stripped by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Font will silently fail in the majority of clients. The rendered fallback font stack is undefined in the provided source — confirm a safe web-safe fallback is declared inline on all font-bearing elements.

**Cannot assess from truncated source:**
- Image `alt` text presence and quality
- Color contrast ratios
- Button tap-target sizes on mobile
