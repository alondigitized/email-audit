# Back to school is here! Shop to get first day fresh.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- After five consecutive sends of the same 20% off $99 Nike & Jordan promotion — including two blasts on July 5 alone — Foot Locker pivots hard to a Back to School content email. The creative is clean and the BTS hook is timely, but the pivot leaves subscribers who were trained to expect a deal with nothing to buy into. This is an editorial refresh, not a revenue email, and it reads that way.
- The "STANDOUT SUMMER" module is a visible QA failure. The body copy reads like it was never proofread ("Or find a vibe. Sumba love.") and the CTA appears to say "SHOP CORAL APG" — both of which look like placeholder or corrupted template text that shipped. For a brand that just ran five polished promo blasts, this is a jarring credibility dent.
- The personalized "PICKS FOR YOU" section at the bottom is the strongest functional element — real prices, star ratings, and product variety. The rest of the email is structured around three category pushes (BTS, Slides, Standout) with no offer attached to any of them. Expect lower conversion than the promo run, but a healthy reset in tone.

## What's working

- **Hero is unmistakable.** "FIRST DAY FRESH" in bold white on dark anchors the BTS narrative in one glance — the backpack-and-sneaker imagery sells the season immediately.
- **Clean multi-section architecture.** BTS → Slides → Standout flows logically; each section has a dedicated image and CTA button with no layout confusion.
- **"Picks for You" personalization is solid.** Real prices, visible star ratings, and multiple product thumbnails give subscribers something actionable without a hard offer.
- **Timely theme shift.** BTS on July 6 is a natural relief valve after the exhausting promo run — the brand-calendar logic is sound even if the execution is uneven.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no hook.** After five consecutive discount emails, subscribers have been conditioned to expect a savings angle. A pure content/editorial email with zero % off, no threshold deal, and no urgency will land flat for the deal-tuned segment.
- **STANDOUT SUMMER section shipped broken.** "Or find a vibe. Sumba love." and "SHOP CORAL APG" look like corrupted placeholder copy — they read as an editorial QA miss, not brand voice.
- **Subject line is generic and splits its message.** "Back to school is here! Shop to get first day fresh." does two things poorly — announces and pitches — without doing either sharply. "First day fresh" is lifestyle-vague.
- **BTS arrives without a specific product or category lead.** Unlike the July 3 editorial send ("Top picks to get your summer style right"), this BTS email doesn't feature a single hero product the reader can anchor to — just broad category buckets.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a threshold offer or early-access hook.** Even a soft "BTS early access" or "free shipping on BTS orders" gives subscribers a reason to click beyond browsing. Content-only emails underperform for deal-trained lists. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Pull the STANDOUT SUMMER module or fix the copy before next send.** "Sumba love." and "SHOP CORAL APG" need an immediate QA pass — if this references the Adidas Samba, say that explicitly. Garbled CTA copy kills trust fast.
- 3. **Surface one hero product in the BTS section.** Pick the #1 BTS sneaker of the season and feature it — a photo, the name, the price, a direct link. Specificity drives clicks far more than category-level "shop all" buttons.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to be specific.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `First day kicks: shop Foot Locker's BTS picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New year, new fit — BTS styles just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh sneakers, slides, and standout looks. Get set for day one.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything you need to walk in looking like you meant it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo:**

```css
/* Line in media query block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` — it should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This is a copy-paste error. Effect: on mobile, `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no responsive width override (falls back to whatever desktop value is set), and `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is incorrectly assigned 74.25% instead of its correct 66% (set two lines earlier).

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**

```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```

Screen readers cannot infer the document language. Required for WCAG 2.1 Level A (SC 3.1.1).

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```

Should be `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`. Without `initial-scale=1`, some iOS clients can render at a zoomed-out scale on first load.
