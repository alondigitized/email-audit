# You've still got time to get up to 50% off.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the first follow-up beat of Foot Locker's Summer Savings campaign — the "reminder" to yesterday's June 14 opener. The creative pivot here is smart: where the June 14 send was brand-generic, this one narrows to Nike specifically, giving the hero a crisp "UP TO 50% OFF NIKE / SAVE ON SWOOSH" identity that the opener lacked. The lime-green palette is doing real work — it reads Nike before you process a single word.
- The execution stumbles in two places. First, there are two near-identical ghost CTAs sitting side by side in the hero — "SHOP THE NIKE SALE" and "SHOP THE SALE" — which creates choice paralysis exactly where you want momentum. Second, the product grid buries the lede: six specific shoes with star ratings and real prices are the most compelling part of this email, but they're below the fold and competing with three blocks of watered-down family-nav buttons.
- Overall this is a competent campaign send — better than the June 12–13 near-identical repeats in the FLX Members cycle — but it doesn't fully capitalize on the Nike brand equity it signals. The subject line's "you've still got time" framing correctly implies scarcity, but after six Foot Locker emails in six days, subscriber fatigue is a real headcount problem no amount of green creative can fix.

## What's working

- **Nike brand anchor is clear and immediate.** "UP TO 50% OFF NIKE / SAVE ON SWOOSH" in the hero reads brand-first without needing a logo — strong on a locked screen.
- **Lime-green hero palette is distinct.** Breaks cleanly from the previous four magenta-on-black FLX emails; subscribers won't confuse this for a repeat.
- **Product grid with real prices and star ratings.** Six specific Nike shoes with visible names, prices, and social proof is the most click-worthy real estate in the email.
- **FLX Rewards member benefit is visible.** The magenta FLX bar surfaces free-shipping for members — a loyalty hook most brands bury in fine print.
- **"SHOP SALE FOR THE FULL FAMILY" section adds navigational utility.** Men's / Women's / Kids' / All gives quick-exit lanes for shoppers who already know what they want.

## What's weak

- **Two hero CTAs create choice paralysis.** "SHOP THE NIKE SALE" and "SHOP THE SALE" sitting at the same visual weight is a split-test that wasn't meant to be one — pick the Nike-specific version and delete the other.
- **No urgency anchor in the body.** Subject says "you've still got time" but nothing in the visible email copy tells me *when* time runs out. No deadline date, no countdown, no "ends Sunday."
- **Family-nav button block slows the scroll.** Four nearly identical outlined buttons between the hero and the product grid interrupt momentum without adding context.
- **The "SHOP KIDS'*" asterisk is unresolved.** The asterisk implies exclusions but there's no visible footnote — a small trust signal going the wrong direction.
- **Cadence context is a problem the creative can't fix.** This is subscriber email six in six days from Foot Locker. Even a clean send like this pays a fatigue penalty at open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one hero CTA.** Kill "SHOP THE SALE" entirely. "SHOP THE NIKE SALE" is the send's whole premise — give it a full-width button, not a pair of ghost twins.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the body copy.** One line under the hero subhead: "Sale ends [Day, Date]." The subject implies urgency; the email should land it.
- 3. **Move the product grid up.** Cut or compress the family-nav button block; bring "HOT STYLES" directly under the hero. The shoes with prices and ratings are the conversion engine.
- 4. **Resolve or remove the Kids'* asterisk.** Either anchor a footnote at the bottom ("*excludes [X]") or drop the asterisk. Unexplained fine-print signals distrust.
- 5. **Reduce send cadence.** This is a brand-level note, but after 6 emails in 6 days the open rate on this send will be suppressed regardless of creative quality.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Save on the Swoosh: up to 50% off Nike`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Air Max, Zoom Fly, and more — full sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FLX members get free shipping. Shop Nike before it's gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS grid selector bug (confirmed):**
```css
/* Line in mobile @media block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector incorrectly targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This duplicates the col-8 mobile override (which already appears correctly at 66%) and leaves `.md-fluid-row .col-9` with no mobile width rule — any 9-column fluid block in an `md-fluid-row` will not reflow correctly on mobile.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` (confirmed):**
```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Using `none` instead of `100%` prevents iOS from auto-sizing small text, but also blocks user-initiated pinch-zoom on all those elements. The accessibility-correct value is `100%`.
