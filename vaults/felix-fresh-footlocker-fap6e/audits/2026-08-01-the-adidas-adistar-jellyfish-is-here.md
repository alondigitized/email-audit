# The adidas Adistar Jellyfish is here!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the "reservation calendar" launch format Foot Locker has now run three times in five days (2026-07-28, 2026-07-29, both 9.00/10), but the hero has shrunk from a multi-launch reservation grid down to a single sneaker, and the framing has drifted from "reserve today" to "it's here" — a weaker, less urgent hook than the prior two sends. The underwater hero shot for the Adistar Jellyfish is striking and on-theme (the name literally is a jellyfish), but it's followed by four more unrelated sections — Jordan Retro 1, Converse/Vans, kids' back-to-school apparel, and backpacks — turning a focused launch email into a full-catalog scroll with no single clear CTA.
- The subject line "The adidas Adistar Jellyfish is here!" is honest and specific, which is good, but it undersells relative to 07-29's four-launch subject and doesn't carry any urgency ("here" vs. "reserve now"). No pricing, no member benefit, no deadline anywhere in the email — just five stacked merchandising blocks.
- Judgment: this is a serviceable but unfocused send. The visual is the strongest asset in the email and everything after the first section dilutes it. Given the brand has already proven the multi-hero reservation format works better (9.00/10 twice), this feels like a step backward in message discipline.

## What's working

- The hero image (underwater sneakers, water droplets) is genuinely eye-catching and thematically tied to "Jellyfish" — strong art direction.
- Two clear CTAs on the hero ("Shop Adidas" / "Store Locator") give both an online and in-store path.
- Section headers are visually distinct with alternating color blocks (maroon, navy, charcoal, teal), making the email easy to scan section-by-section.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and type all display cleanly.
- FLX Rewards banner (free shipping, no minimum) is placed both in the nav bar and again near the footer, reinforcing loyalty value.

## What's weak

- Five distinct product pushes (Adidas, Jordan, Converse/Vans, kids' apparel, backpacks) in one email means no single dominant CTA — this dilutes the "Jellyfish is here" premise the subject promised.
- No price, discount, or member-exclusive access mentioned anywhere — pure product marketing with zero urgency.
- The back-to-school kids' and backpack sections have nothing to do with the Adistar Jellyfish launch and would be better as a separate send.
- Subject says "is here" but nothing on the offer differentiates this from any other new-arrival email — no drop time, no limited-quantity cue, no "reserve" language despite the brand using that hook twice already this week.
- Visual hierarchy after the hero flattens out — sections 2-5 are all roughly equal weight, so a reader skimming loses the thread of what this email is actually about.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to just the Adistar Jellyfish launch (hero + maybe one supporting shot) and spin the back-to-school/backpack content into its own campaign — this email is trying to be two emails at once.
- 2. Bring back the "reserve" urgency language from the 07-28/07-29 sends instead of the flatter "is here" — those sends scored 9.00/10 partly on that mechanic.
- 3. Add a concrete detail: release date/time, retail price, or "limited pairs" — right now there's nothing that signals scarcity or timing.
- 4. If the Jordan Retro 1 must stay, cut Converse/Vans and kids' sections to tighten the email to two hero products max.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Reserve the adidas Adistar Jellyfish before it's gone`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Adistar Jellyfish drops today — reserve yours`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `One of 2026's most coveted releases. Reserve online or find it in-store.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited pairs. Shop now or reserve at your nearest store.`
