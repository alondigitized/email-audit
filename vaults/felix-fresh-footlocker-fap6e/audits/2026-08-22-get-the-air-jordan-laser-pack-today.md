# Get the Air Jordan 'Laser' pack today!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's cleanest send this week from a craft standpoint — dark cinematic hero, bold "THE AIR JORDAN 'LASER' COLLECTION" headline, single clear "SHOP JORDAN" CTA above the fold. It's the same reservation-launch playbook that scored 9/10 on 8/20, and visually it holds up: no render bugs, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum.
- But the subject line oversells what's inside. This isn't a "reserve" or launch-countdown email — there's no reservation mechanic, no countdown timer, no "LAUNCH RESERVATION" badge like the 8/19 and 8/20 sends. It's actually a multi-brand catalog blast (Jordan → Hoka → Crocs → Reebok → "Looks You'll Love" grid) wearing a Jordan hero as its opener. Compared to 8/20's "Reserve the Air Jordan 'Laser' pack today!" which delivered exactly what it promised, this one uses similar Jordan imagery but pivots into a much broader, less focused email.
- Judgement: solid production quality, but it's really a general catalog email in launch-hero clothing, and a sneakerhead who clicks expecting a drop/reservation will bounce once they hit "Shop Jordan" and land on a plain PDP or category page rather than a reservation flow.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous: bold headline, clean product shot, single "SHOP JORDAN" CTA button, all visible without excessive scrolling.
- No render bugs — every section (Hoka, Crocs, Reebok, product grid) rendered fully with images, prices, and star ratings intact.
- Section-to-section color blocking (black → blue → green → burnt orange) gives strong visual rhythm and makes the email easy to skim.
- "Looks You'll Love" grid at the bottom shows real prices, ratings, and colorways — good product-level detail (Samba, Mexico, Vans, Nike Killshot, adidas Adilette all priced and rated).
- FLX Rewards membership perks (free shipping/returns, no-minimum banner) are reinforced twice — top nav and footer.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a "Laser" pack in isolation, but the pack occupies only the first ~15% of the email; four more brands (Hoka, Crocs, Reebok, plus a mixed grid) follow — subject/body mismatch, similar to the 8/18 "Tax free weekend" miss.
- No reservation/launch mechanic visible anywhere — no countdown, no "reserve" language, no release date — despite the near-identical hero template Foot Locker used for actual reservation drops on 8/19 and 8/20.
- No discount, price, or offer anywhere near the Jordan hero — a shopper can't tell if this is full-price, exclusive, or on sale until they click through.
- Karol G / Reebok section ("Born classic. Worn for life") is generic lifestyle copy disconnected from footwear specifics — no product name, price, or shoe model shown, just a portrait shot.
- Sixth consecutive week of near-identical purple/dark hero templates (8/17, 8/18, 8/19, 8/20 x2) — creative fatigue risk for a daily subscriber.

## Recommendations

- 1. Align subject to what's actually inside — either strip the "Laser pack" framing and go generic ("New drops across Jordan, Hoka, Crocs & Reebok"), or cut the multi-brand catalog content and make this a true single-product Jordan send matching the "reserve" template that scored 9/10 on 8/20.
- 2. Add a concrete offer or release-date/price callout directly under the Jordan headline — right now there's zero price or urgency signal in the hero, which is the single biggest lift opportunity.
- 3. Give the Reebok/Karol G block an actual product name and price — a portrait with no shoe visible undersells the section.
- 4. Vary the hero treatment — six near-identical dark/gradient sneaker heroes in six days is measurable fatigue; try a lighter or lifestyle-forward hero for at least one send this week.
