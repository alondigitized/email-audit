# Up to 50% off hot styles is still available! 🔥
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is day three of Foot Locker's Summer Kickoff Sale arc — and it shows. The creative is a near-copy of the May 21 launch email (same pool-and-poolside hero, same "UP TO 50% OFF" treatment, same "SHOP THE SALE" CTA), with "is still available" bolted onto the subject as the only meaningful change. The brand sent a sale teaser on May 20, a launch on May 21, and now a "still available" nudge on May 22 — three emails in three days for the same promo. Cadence fatigue is the primary risk here, not execution quality.
- The "CURATED FOR YOU" grid underneath the hero is the only genuinely differentiated element, but it undercuts itself: five of six products are Nike LaFrance colorways (wolf gray, blue, pink, blue/purple, pink again) plus one Nike WB-05. Showing five colorways of the same shoe isn't curation — it's color sorting. The FLX Rewards banner carries odd, truncated-looking copy ("FLX REWARDS MEMBERS GET FULL PROPOSALS") that reads like a render glitch or a dynamic text field that didn't resolve correctly.
- The bones of this email are solid, and if a subscriber missed the May 21 send, this lands fine. But for anyone who opened yesterday's email — which is likely, given it scored 9/10 — this is visual déjà vu with a weaker subject line. The "still available" framing positions this as a scraps-and-stragglers email rather than a reason to come back.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is clean.** "50% OFF" in oversized yellow type on the pool background is unambiguous — the offer registers in under a second.
- **Star ratings in the product grid add light social proof** without slowing the visual rhythm.
- **Store localization is a nice touch** — showing a specific Pasadena, CA address + store-pickup option is a credible local-relevance signal.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA is unambiguous** — direct verb + noun, contrasting blue button, easy tap target.

## What's weak

- **Recycled hero creative.** The pool scene and 50% OFF treatment are effectively identical to yesterday's launch send. A subscriber who opened May 21's email will feel no new reason to click.
- **"CURATED FOR YOU" is five colorways of the same shoe.** The LaFrance grid reads as inventory dumping, not personalization — it undermines trust in the "curated" label.
- **FLX Rewards banner has broken-looking copy.** "FLX REWARDS MEMBERS GET FULL PROPOSALS" is not coherent marketing copy — this looks like an unresolved dynamic field or a truncated string.
- **No deadline.** "Still available" implies urgency but the email shows no end date, no countdown, no "ends Sunday" — the urgency is asserted, not credible.
- **Third sale-arc email in three days.** Cadence alone will suppress open rates among engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Refresh the hero for day-3.** The same pool image three days running is a fatigue signal. Swap to a street/indoor shot, change the color treatment, or lead with the product grid as the hero. The visual differentiation is more important than the copy.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" converts "still available" from a shrug to a decision trigger. Without it, there's no new reason to act today vs. tomorrow.
- 3. **Fix or remove the FLX Rewards banner copy.** "FULL PROPOSALS" isn't intelligible — either resolve the dynamic tag or replace with static copy ("FLX members earn double on sale styles").
- 4. **Genuinely diversify the product grid.** If the algorithm is surfacing five LaFrance colorways, override it: cap same-model instances at two and fill remaining slots from different silhouettes or brands.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to signal something new.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends soon — 50% off before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your curated picks are 50% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer Kickoff Sale closing out — Nike, Jordan, adidas. Shop before stock goes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `6 picks waiting for you. Sale ends soon.`

The `md-fluid-row` half of this rule targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. Any `.md-fluid-row .col-9` element will not receive its mobile width override and will fall through to a default or conflicting width.
