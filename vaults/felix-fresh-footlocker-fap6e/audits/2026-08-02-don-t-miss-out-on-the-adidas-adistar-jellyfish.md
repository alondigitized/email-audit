# Don't miss out on the adidas Adistar Jellyfish!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's third pass at the adidas Adistar Jellyfish in six days, and each version has been thinner than the last. The 2026-07-29 send was a multi-launch reservation calendar (9.0/10). The 2026-07-31 send stripped that down to a single sneaker with a reservation date and specific urgency (6.0/10). This one strips it further still — the reservation calendar and the specific date are both gone, replaced with a vague "don't miss out on your chance to secure one of the most coveted sneakers of 2026." That's a regression, not an escalation.
- The execution is otherwise clean: bold, on-brand hero typography over a moody wet-pavement photo, two unambiguous CTAs (Shop Adidas / Store Locator), and a well-organized scroll into Jordan Retro 1 and a Converse/Vans "Refresh the Rotation" section. Visually it's competent Foot Locker boilerplate. But as a subscriber who has now seen this exact sneaker three times in under a week with steadily less information each time, the "don't miss out" framing reads as recycled urgency rather than real urgency — there's no launch date, no price, and no size/availability signal anywhere in the render.
- Net: solid production values riding on a stale creative concept. This email would benefit far more from a fresh angle (or an actual concrete detail — price, date, restock count) than from another coat of the same hero shot.

## What's working

- Hero section reads instantly — large "ADIDAS ADISTAR JELLYFISH" wordmark over the product shot leaves zero ambiguity about what's being sold.
- Two clear, high-contrast CTA buttons ("SHOP ADIDAS," "STORE LOCATOR") sit directly under the hero copy with no scrolling required.
- Clean section separation via color blocking (teal → maroon → navy) makes the Jordan Retro 1 and court-classics sections easy to scan without confusing them with the adidas offer.
- FLX Rewards membership benefit (free shipping/returns) is visible twice — top nav and footer band — reinforcing loyalty value without being pushy.
- No render bugs: no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content anywhere in the send.

## What's weak

- No price, launch date, or availability info anywhere — a "coveted sneaker" pitch with zero specifics is a weaker ask than the 07-31 version, which at least had a reservation date.
- Third appearance of the same Adistar Jellyfish hero image/product in six days (07-29, 07-31, 08-02) — a real subscriber will recognize the repetition and it dilutes urgency rather than building it.
- "Don't miss out on your chance" is generic hype copy with no proof behind it (no stock counter, no "limited pairs," no countdown).
- Primary CTA says "Shop Adidas" (brand-wide) rather than something tied to the specific sneaker, which undercuts the hero's specificity.
- "View in Browser" sits above the fold as the first line of content — minor friction before the reader even reaches the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real deadline or scarcity signal back in — even a soft one ("Limited pairs. While supplies last.") beats the vague "most coveted sneakers of 2026" line. This is the single biggest lever: the 07-29 and earlier 07-31 sends outperformed this one specifically because they had concrete timing.
- 2. Change the CTA from "Shop Adidas" to something sneaker-specific like "Shop Adistar Jellyfish" so the button matches the hero's specificity.
- 3. Add a price or "from $X" — right now there's no purchase-decision anchor anywhere in the email.
- 4. Vary the creative asset — a fourth send on the same product deserves a new angle (on-foot shot, close-up detail, different colorway) rather than reusing the same wet-pavement hero.
- 5. Move "View in Browser" out of primary visual weight (smaller, less prominent) so it's not the first thing read.
