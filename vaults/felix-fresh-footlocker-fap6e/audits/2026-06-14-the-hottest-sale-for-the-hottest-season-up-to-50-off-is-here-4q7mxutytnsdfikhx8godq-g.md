# The hottest sale for the hottest season: up to 50% off is here!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the opening send of Foot Locker's "Summer Savings" campaign — a full creative refresh after four near-identical "20% off $99+ FLX Members Only" emails hammered subscribers June 10 through June 13. The pivot from magenta-on-black FLX exclusivity to a bright yellow-green summer banner is smart: it reads as a new chapter rather than another re-skin of the same promo. The offer escalates too — "up to 50% off" is a bigger headline than the 20% that just closed, and "one week only!" in the hero subtext gives the urgency a credible window.
- The execution is competent but not tight. The hero is clean, the CTA is direct, and FLX Rewards gets its own section with a tangible benefit. Where this send stumbles is the product grid: pink women's Merrell sneakers, a Nike SFB 6" military boot, men's adidas runners, and what appears to be an adidas yoga top share a single unfiltered scroll — no curation for any actual buyer profile. The subject also uses "hottest" twice in the same clause, which is the kind of slip a single read-aloud pass would have caught.
- Net verdict: a welcome campaign opener that earns its place after last week's four-email fatigue, but it leaves personalization, preheader value, and product curation entirely on the table.

## What's working

- **Fresh creative break.** Yellow-green hero after four days of magenta-on-black registers as a genuinely new campaign — not another blast of the same promo.
- **Clear offer hierarchy.** "UP TO 50% OFF / SUMMER SAVINGS" reads in one glance; no ambiguity about the email's purpose.
- **Unambiguous CTA.** "SHOP THE SALE" is a direct verb + noun that doesn't hide behind "Discover" or "Explore."
- **Credible time anchor.** "One week only!" in the hero subtext gives urgency a believable scope without manufactured panic.
- **FLX section earns its space.** Free shipping + free pickups for members reinforces the loyalty program's value rather than just being decorative.
- **Star ratings in the product grid.** Social proof is visible without scrolling to a dedicated reviews section.

## What's weak

- **Product grid is a demographic jumble.** Pink women's Merrell sneakers, a Nike SFB 6" military boot, and what looks like an adidas yoga top ("adidas Lace Top") share one scroll — no signal that Foot Locker knows who it's talking to.
- **"Hottest" repeated twice in the subject.** "The hottest sale for the hottest season" reads as an unreviewed draft, not polished campaign copy.
- **Subject clocks 63 characters.** Runs long on mobile; the most valuable payload ("50% off") risks clipping before it's seen.
- **Preheader is wasted.** "View in Browser" is the first visible text in the email body — that's what most inbox clients will surface as preview text, and it communicates nothing.
- **"Up to 50% off" is unanchored.** No struck-through prices or before/after callouts in the grid; the "up to" qualifier could mean one item at 50% and everything else at 15%, and nothing here disproves that read.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader immediately.** "View in Browser" as preview text is a zero-value occupant of one of the inbox's prime real-estate slots. Even a generic line beats dead air.
- 2. **Shorten the subject to under 50 chars.** Drop the wordplay and lead with the number.
- 3. **Segment the product grid by subscriber profile.** Show the men's grid or the women's grid — not both. An unsegmented grid signals a broadcast blast, not a recommendation.
- 4. **Anchor "up to 50% off" with one before/after price.** A single hero product showing "Was $130 → $65" in the banner subtext makes the promise feel real rather than lawyerly.
- 5. **Swap the apparel item for footwear.** An adidas Lace Top in a shoe-brand sale email breaks the category message mid-scroll.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top styles from Nike, New Balance, adidas — up to half off this week only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer sale is live: one week to grab up to 50% off your favorites.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo (mobile grid):**

```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

`.md-fluid-row .col-8` appears in two rules (the explicit `.col-8` rule and again here). `.md-fluid-row .col-9` is never declared. On mobile viewports, any `md-fluid-row` element with class `col-9` will inherit the wrong width (or default desktop width), breaking the grid.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```

Applying `none` (rather than `100%`) across all interactive text elements disables user-initiated text scaling in WebKit-based clients (iOS Mail, Safari). This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text, Level AA). The correct value for suppressing *automatic* iOS scaling while preserving user scaling is `100%`, not `none`.

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```

Best practice (and required for consistent rendering in some hybrid clients) is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`.

**External font dependency — single point of failure:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

If `partner-fonts.bluecore.com` is unavailable or blocked by a corporate proxy/firewall, all custom typefaces silently fall back to system fonts. No `font-display` or inline fallback declaration is present in the visible CSS. This is standard Bluecore behavior but worth noting for deliverability in enterprise mail environments.
