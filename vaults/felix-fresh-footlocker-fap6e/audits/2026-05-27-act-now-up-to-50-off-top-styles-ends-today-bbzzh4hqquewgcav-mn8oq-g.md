# ACT NOW! Up to 50% off top styles ends today!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the sale-close email for Foot Locker's Summer Sale — the sixth or seventh touch in a sequence that kicked off with the May 20 teaser and has run nearly daily since. The creative brief is correct: send an urgency-close email on the last day of the sale. But the execution has two structural problems. First, the subject line opens with "ACT NOW!" in all caps — a textbook spam trigger that torpedoes inbox placement and marks the sender as panicked before the offer is even seen. Second, the hero says "ENDS SOON" while the subject says "ends today" — a small inconsistency that undermines trust in the deadline itself.
- Inside the email, the execution is actually solid. "ENDS SOON / UP TO 50% OFF" dominates the hero legibly, the family segmentation CTAs (SHOP MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS') are smart, and the "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" product grid adds browseable social proof with visible star ratings and prices. The FLX Rewards callout in the footer rewards loyal subscribers. But the pool-party hero with the floating Nike shoe hasn't changed since May 21 — subscribers have seen this image six times in a week.
- Bottom line: a structurally competent close email sabotaged by a spam-coded subject line and creative fatigue deep enough to blunt real urgency.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is immediate** — "ENDS SOON" in massive type followed by yellow "UP TO 50% OFF" is readable in under a second.
- **Family segmentation CTAs** (SHOP MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS' / SHOP ALL) make the email feel useful rather than just promotional.
- **Product grid with star ratings and prices** gives subscribers something to browse and adds real social proof.
- **FLX Rewards footer callout** surfaces loyalty benefits for members who are on the fence.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout issues across the visible screen width.

## What's weak

- **"ACT NOW!" opener is a spam signal** — ALL CAPS command with an exclamation mark triggers filters and reads as desperation, not urgency.
- **"ENDS SOON" vs. "ends today"** — the hero and subject give different time signals; one undermines the other.
- **No specific deadline time** — "ends today" without a timezone/hour ("tonight at midnight ET") lets subscribers defer indefinitely.
- **Same pool-party hero since May 21** — six sends, zero creative variation; fatigued subscribers have already tuned this image out.
- **No new hook or product callout** — nothing in the body rewards subscribers who have opened every email this week; there's no reason to act *now* vs. having acted on May 24.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject — kill "ACT NOW!"** The single highest-leverage change. Lead with the offer or the deadline, not a panic command.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: up to 50% off styles for the whole family`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance — Summer Sale ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Men's, women's & kids' — shop before midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale closes tonight. Don't leave savings behind.`
- 2. **Lock the deadline with a specific time** — add "tonight at midnight ET" to the hero copy. "ENDS SOON" is meaningless; "Ends Tonight — Midnight ET" is actionable.
- 3. **Introduce one featured deal** — surface a single high-demand shoe (e.g., the Air Max 95 OG already in the grid) with its before/after price in the hero. Give fatigued subscribers a new reason to click that they haven't seen in five prior emails.
- 4. **Swap the hero image** — even a different colorway or a different silhouette breaks pattern recognition and signals "new send" to subscribers who are trained to ignore the pool scene.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] CSS selector typo — broken 9-column responsive layout:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` — it should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Any `md-fluid-row col-9` cell will inherit the wrong width on mobile (falling through to a wider rule or default), while `.md-fluid-row .col-8` gets this width applied twice. This is a copy-paste regression in the media query block.

**[MEDIUM] Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Missing `initial-scale=1`. Some mobile clients (older Samsung Internet, some WebViews) will render at a zoomed-out scale without it.

**[MEDIUM] Global text-size-adjust disabled:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
Applied to every text element in the mobile breakpoint. This prevents OS-level accessibility text scaling (user font size preferences, display accessibility settings). The selector scope is too broad — this should only target elements that actually break layout when scaled.

**[LOW] No `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
The `<html>` tag carries no `lang` attribute. Screen readers cannot determine the content language, which affects pronunciation and Braille translation.
