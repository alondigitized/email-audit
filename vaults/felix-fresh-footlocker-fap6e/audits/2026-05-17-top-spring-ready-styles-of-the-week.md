# Top spring-ready styles of the week.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's "editorial product roundup" template: a "STILL FRESH" hero anchored to triple-white Air Force 1s, followed by a six-product "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" grid spanning Jordan, Converse, Nike, and Vans. The hero concept is genuinely good — white sneakers are seasonally timeless and the spring angle works in May. The execution is clean. That's where the compliments end.
- The email is three disconnected ideas duct-taped together. The hero says "white kicks, all season" with a "SHOP TRIPLE WHITE" CTA. The product grid immediately pivots to pink Polo sneakers, black Vans, and blue Jordans. There's no through-line. And this is the third Foot Locker send in two days — no escalating offer, no new concept, same browse-discovery template as yesterday's "Refresh your style" email.
- The verdict: solid production quality, coherent brand voice, but a generic product roundup that gives a subscriber zero reason to shop *today* rather than *next week*.

## What's working

- "STILL FRESH" headline is punchy and confident — one of the stronger hero lines from this brand in recent sends.
- Hero product photography is clean and seasonally appropriate for May.
- Product grid mixes sale prices (strikethrough pricing visible) with full-price items — creates the impression of value.
- Star ratings appear beneath individual product tiles, adding lightweight social proof without cluttering the layout.
- FLX free-shipping banner is visible — loyalty benefit acknowledgment is there, even if below the fold.

## What's weak

- Hero CTA "SHOP TRIPLE WHITE" is too narrow: any subscriber who came for color or a non-white silhouette is already excluded before the product grid renders.
- Product grid visually contradicts the hero — pink, blue, and black colorways immediately undercut the "white/fresh" opening.
- No discount or urgency anywhere in the hero; the email asks for a click but gives no reason this week beats next week.
- "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" implies algorithmic personalization but there's no signal this is actually tailored — no name, no browsing tie-in, no personalization hint.
- Third Foot Locker send in two days, same editorial browse format with no offer escalation from yesterday's "Refresh your style" send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a discount to the hero.** "Still Fresh. 20% off triple white kicks this week only." shifts this from browse to buy. Even a small, time-bounded offer makes the click feel urgent.
- 2. **Make the product grid theme-consistent.** If the hero is triple white, serve white/neutral colorways only. Or hard-pivot with a deliberate section break: "Want a pop of color? Shop this week's picks →" Don't let the grid silently contradict the hero.
- 3. **Surface the FLX free-shipping benefit in the hero sub-headline.** "Free shipping for FLX members — no minimum" above the fold changes the value equation immediately for logged-in subscribers.
- 4. **Add a credible deadline.** "This week's picks refresh Sunday" tied to the "of the week" framing creates pressure that matches the subject line's editorial conceit.
- 5. **Differentiate this send from yesterday's.** The "Refresh your style" email ran the same pattern. Lead with a discount this time, or a new brand story — not another browse grid.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CONFIRMED BUG — CSS selector typo in fluid grid:**

```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The `.md-fluid-row` variant incorrectly targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. Any `md-fluid-row col-9` element on tablet breakpoints receives no width rule from this declaration, falling through to the catch-all 100% width. The adjacent `.col-8` rule is also affected — it now matches twice with potentially conflicting specificity.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

This blanket rule disables iOS system text scaling for all elements, breaking accessibility for users with larger font preferences set at the OS level.

**Fixed pixel heights on product card cells (mobile):**

```css
.name { height: 24px }
.gender_age { height: 25px }
.prod_color { height: 25px }
.price { height: 25px }
```

`min-height: auto` overrides are applied on mobile, but the desktop/default fixed heights will clip multi-line product names or prices in products with longer strings.

**External font dependency:**

```
https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css
```

`<link rel="stylesheet">` in email `<head>` is stripped by several clients (Gmail, Outlook.com). Fonts won't load in those environments. Layout should degrade gracefully — verify fallback stack is defined in inline styles.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag** — omitted from source. Required for screen reader language detection.
