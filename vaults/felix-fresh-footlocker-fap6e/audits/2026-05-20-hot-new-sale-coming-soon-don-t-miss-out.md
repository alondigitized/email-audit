# Hot new sale coming soon! Don't miss out 👀
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure teaser — Foot Locker is announcing a sale that hasn't happened yet. The email delivers two conflicting messages simultaneously: "something big is coming" and "in the meantime, shop these." The execution is technically clean — pool/summer aesthetic, bold hero typography, and a tidy product grid — but the strategic payoff is hollow. Subscribers who opened expecting the "hot new sale" found an invitation to wait instead.
- This is the fifth Foot Locker email since May 16 (two on the 16th, editorial roundups on the 17th and 18th, now this on the 20th). The cadence is starting to grind against itself. Compare this to the May 17 and 18 roundups, which at least gave you specific products to buy immediately — this one substitutes anticipation for value and delivers neither urgency nor specificity.
- Clear verdict: a well-produced container with nothing concrete inside. The brand team built a good shell; the strategy left it empty.

## What's working

- Bold, oversized "NEW SALE COMING SOON" hero reads instantly at mobile size — no ambiguity about the email's premise
- Product grid is well-shot with star ratings and prices, giving subscribers something to actually click now
- FLX Rewards banner is large, high-contrast, and unmissable
- "SHOP SALE STYLES" is a clean CTA — verb + noun, no "Discover" softness
- Summer/pool imagery feels current and season-appropriate

## What's weak

- The subject's main promise ("hot new sale") is never delivered — no date, no discount percentage, no product categories teased
- Fifth Foot Locker email in four days — subscriber fatigue is accumulating fast and this send does nothing to justify its slot
- "Coming soon" with no deadline is noise, not urgency
- Product mix is unfocused — running (adidas Pureboost, HOKA), training (Nike Metcon), basketball (Knicks jersey), trail (HOKA Speedgoat), lifestyle (Converse) — no cohesive story
- "Don't miss out 👀" in the subject is generic FOMO with zero specificity

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the sale a date** — "New sale drops Friday 5/23" transforms "coming soon" from noise into genuine urgency. Every other element in this email could stay the same and this change alone would lift it.
- 2. **Name one category or anchor product in the teaser** — "Jordans, Nike, Hoka — all hitting sale this Friday" is a subject line worth opening. The current version reveals nothing, which makes it feel more like a reminder that the sale isn't here yet than a reason to engage.
- 3. **Lead the hero with one marquee product at its sale price** — show a single shoe at $89 (was $140) rather than describing the future discount in the abstract.
- 4. **Reconsider the teaser format entirely** — a "coming soon" email justifies its slot only when the sale is genuinely newsworthy (e.g., a Nike SNKRS drop with waitlist access) and the teaser has real specificity. Otherwise, skip the teaser and send a strong launch-day email instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New sale Friday — Jordans, Hoka, Nike all dropping 👟`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sale drops in 48 hrs. Here's what's in it.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Drops Friday. Up to 40% off sneakers and styles — mark your calendar.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Air Force 1s, Jordans, Hoka Cliftons making the cut. Sale starts Friday.`
