# Reserve the adidas x Bad Bunny F50 Ghost Spirit.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- Foot Locker sent this reservation alert on July 15 — the day after an essentially identical send on July 14 with subject "Reserve the adidas x Bad Bunny F50 Ghost Spirit!" The creative is unchanged: the only material difference is the punctuation shift (! to .) and the countdown timer naturally ticking down. This is the same urgency-escalation playbook that defined the five-send Nike & Jordan 20% off sequence in early July.
- That said, the email earns its place. The Adidas x Bad Bunny F50 Ghost collab is a genuine heat drop, and pairing it with the Jordan Retro 3 True Blue in the same send is smart programming — two countdowns, same close date (7/17 at 5 PM ET). The visible countdown timers with specific date/time are about as credible as urgency gets. The shoe-first visual hierarchy is exactly right for an audience that makes decisions on aesthetics.
- The "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" lifestyle block at the bottom — hoodies, tees, pants — actively hurts the email. It reads like an algorithmic cross-sell tacked on to a drop alert and dilutes the sneaker-culture signal. The email should've ended after the upcoming drops tease.

## What's working

- Dual-drop format (Bad Bunny F50 Ghost + Jordan Retro 3 True Blue) doubles the reservation hook in a single send — efficient use of subscriber attention.
- Countdown timers with an explicit deadline ("Reservations close 7/17 at 5 PM ET") are specific and credible, not the vague "limited time" filler most brands use.
- "RESERVE NOW" CTA is unambiguous — verb + action, high contrast, immediately below the hero product on both blocks.
- The FLX loyalty benefit ("Full Members get Free Shipping & Returns") in the header keeps the member value prop visible without taking up email real estate.
- Upcoming drops tease (visible shoe silhouettes below the two hero blocks) creates browse intent beyond just the two featured drops.

## What's weak

- This is a carbon copy of the July 14 send — same subject, same creative, same countdown. A subscriber who opened yesterday gets zero new information and will feel the repeat.
- "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" apparel cross-sell at the bottom is completely off-brand for a drop-alert email; it signals the CRM is auto-appending engagement modules that don't belong here.
- The subject says "Ghost Spirit" while the email body renders as "F50 GHOST SPRINT" — product name inconsistency between subject line and hero copy is a credibility hairline crack.
- No price anchoring anywhere in the email — for a reservation that drives urgency around exclusivity, even ballpark MSRP would help subscribers commit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate the subject with a tighter deadline hook** — "2 days left" or the specific close date forces subscribers who ignored July 14 to act now without repeating the identical line.
- 2. **Drop the "LOOKS YOU'LL LOVE" block entirely** — this send is a drop alert; end after the upcoming drops ticker and let the email breathe. Save the apparel cross-sell for a standalone lifestyle email.
- 3. **Reconcile the product name** — "Ghost Spirit" vs "Ghost Sprint" needs to be one thing everywhere; fix the subject to match the hero copy.
- 4. **Add MSRP** — even a single "$XXX" under the product name gives subscribers a value anchor and reduces post-click friction.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — fluid grid CSS selector mismatch:**

```css
/* In the <style> block, mobile media query */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The right-hand selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. As written, `.col-8` inside `.md-fluid-row` gets col-9's width (74.25%), and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` gets no mobile-width override at all. Any column using that combination will reflow incorrectly on mobile.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**

```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```

Screen readers default to the system language. Required for accessibility compliance (WCAG 3.1.1) and correct pronunciation by AT.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` without unprefixed property:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

The unprefixed `text-size-adjust: none` is absent. Modern browsers (Firefox, some Chromium builds) ignore the prefixed form. Low severity but technically incomplete.

**No issues found** with viewport meta, `format-detection`, or charset declaration.
