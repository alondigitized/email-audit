# Super Hot Deals on Nike & Jordan!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth Nike/Jordan sale email from Foot Locker since July 19 — and it shows. The subject line "Super Hot Deals on Nike & Jordan!" is the bluntest, most generic copy in the entire recent rotation, a clear step down from the creative momentum built by July 23's "Hoop Like Her" athlete-editorial pivot. The hero creative — "More Styles Added!" in red over a blue background with two male models — is pulled nearly verbatim from the July 22 send. From the perspective of an engaged subscriber who has seen all five emails, this reads as a copy-paste requeue, not a new send.
- That said, the structural bones are strong. The 30% off offer dominates without scrolling, the SHOP NOW CTA is high-contrast and unambiguous, FLX+ loyalty branding is visible in the header, and the "Curated for You" product tiles add a personal layer at the bottom. A first-time opener would find this a perfectly competent sale email.
- Verdict: technically solid, creatively exhausted. The offer is real and the layout works — the campaign is the problem, not the execution.

## What's working

- "UP TO 30% OFF NIKE & JORDAN" in massive white type on blue is unmissable — visual hierarchy does its job.
- Orange "SHOP NOW" button is high-contrast against the hero and fires immediately on the eye.
- FLX+ loyalty badge in the header rewards members without requiring a scroll.
- "SHOP SALE BY BRAND" section with Nike and Jordan logos gives subscribers a fast lane to their preferred brand.
- "Curated for You" product tiles at the bottom add genuine personalization and specific product pathways.

## What's weak

- "Super Hot Deals on Nike & Jordan!" is the weakest subject in the current cycle — pure discount-speak with no hook, no athlete, no product specificity.
- "More Styles Added!" hero copy is recycled verbatim from July 22; an engaged subscriber has seen this layout twice in two days.
- No visible deadline anywhere in the email — "up to 30% off" floats with no urgency anchor.
- "SHOP NOW" is the most generic CTA possible; any verb-noun pairing ("Shop Jordan Sale," "Grab 30% Off") would outperform it.
- Five Nike/Jordan sale emails in six days is aggressive cadence; the list's most valuable segment is the most at-risk of unsubscribing here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline visible in the hero.** Even "Ends Sunday" or "3 Days Left" turns a passive browse into an action. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Kill "SHOP NOW" — make the CTA specific.** "Shop Jordan Sale" or "Save on Nike Now" tests higher because they confirm the destination.
- 3. **Refresh the hero creative.** New models, new setting, or a new headline beat — anything other than the July 22 requeue. "More Styles Added!" has been used; subscribers notice.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to lead with product or deadline, not hyperbole.** "Super Hot" is the laziest word in promotional copy.
- 5. **Consider a gap day before the next send.** A 24-hour pause would do more for list health than any copy change.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS selector bug (confirmed):**

```css
/* Line in the mobile media query */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector targets `.md-fluid-row .col-8` instead of `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This means 9-column fluid rows on medium-breakpoint (`md-fluid-row`) will not receive the correct width override — layout will break or fall back unexpectedly on viewports where this class applies.

**Missing `lang` attribute:**

```html
<html>  <!-- no lang="en" -->
```

Screen readers cannot determine language; assistive tech will default to system language. Add `lang="en"` (or appropriate locale).

**External font stylesheet will fail silently:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

Gmail (webmail and Android app), Outlook 2013–2021, and Apple Mail in privacy-relay mode all strip or block external `<link>` tags. Any typography depending on this font will degrade to system fallback with no warning. Font declarations should be inlined in a `<style>` block or the design must accept fallback rendering.

**Incomplete `format-detection` meta tag:**

```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

Only suppresses phone number auto-linking. Clients (especially older iOS Mail) will still auto-link dates and email addresses. A complete suppression is: `content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important`:**

Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on the global rule `div,p,a,li,span,td` suppresses user-initiated text scaling entirely. This is an accessibility regression for visually impaired users on iOS. Use `100%` instead.
