# Refresh your style with Foot Locker whenever, whenever!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a generic new-arrivals brand blast dropped on the same calendar day as Foot Locker's welcome email ("Thanks for joining the Foot Locker fam!" — also 2026-05-16). The design team delivered a clean render: strong hero-to-product flow, a sizing section, and a well-placed FLX Rewards module at the bottom. That's the good news.
- The bad news is everything the envelope is doing. The subject line "whenever, whenever" lands like a broken A/B test or a failed Shakira reference — it's confusing rather than charming, and it tells you nothing about what's inside. Worse, this email carries no offer. A newly activated subscriber who got a welcome email mere hours ago is now receiving a second email that says, in effect, "we have new stuff." No discount, no urgency, no reason to click today rather than tomorrow.
- Foot Locker is burning its highest-value conversion window — the 24-48 hours after signup — on a content browse. A single first-purchase offer would have flipped this from a cataloguing exercise into a revenue event.

## What's working

- Visual hierarchy is tight: hero image → product grid → size picker → app promo → rewards module flows without confusion.
- "SHOP SHOES IN YOUR SIZE" (Men's / Women's / Kids') is a genuinely useful UX detail that reduces click friction.
- FLX Rewards section is visible and prominent with a clear "JOIN FOR FREE" CTA — keeps the loyalty flywheel spinning.
- Specific brand tiles (Adidas, HOKA, New Balance footwear + apparel) give the email real browsable content.
- No render bugs — clean across all sections.

## What's weak

- "Whenever, whenever" in the subject is baffling — the doubled word reads like a draft artifact, not copy.
- Zero concrete offer: no discount, no free shipping, no flash hook. It's a look-book with no reason to act.
- Same-day cadence after the welcome email is aggressive; two Foot Locker emails before a subscriber has clicked anything is a fast path to the unsubscribe link.
- The app download block mid-email competes directly with the shopping CTAs at the worst possible moment in the funnel.
- "THE LATEST AND GREATEST" is the most generic possible hero headline for a new arrivals email — no season, no story, no specificity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a new-subscriber offer.** 10-15% off first purchase on new arrivals converts this brand email into a transactional trigger. The onboarding window is the highest-leverage moment in the lifecycle.
- 2. **Drop the app download section entirely for the first two sends.** Let the subscriber buy something first, then pitch the app on the third touch.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject line.** "Whenever, whenever" is actively damaging open rate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fresh drops: Adidas, HOKA, New Balance — shop your size`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals just landed at Foot Locker`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New styles are in. FLX members get early access.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The fits everyone's wearing — now in your size.`
- 4. **Anchor the hero to a season or story.** Even "Summer styles are here" gives the browse a narrative that "new arrivals" alone doesn't.
- 5. **Collapse the two hero CTAs ("SEE WHAT'S NEW" / "SHOP CLASSICS") into one focused CTA** pointing to a curated new arrivals collection.

## 1. Overview

This is a generic new-arrivals brand blast dropped on the same calendar day as Foot Locker's welcome email ("Thanks for joining the Foot Locker fam!" — also 2026-05-16). ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing**
QA confirms `text/plain` part is 0 characters. Many corporate mail filters and screen readers depend on a populated text part. An empty text part can trigger spam scoring.

**[WARN] 7 images missing `alt` text**, including:
- Tracking pixel `ho.gif` — `src: https://trk.bc.footlocker.com/...ho.gif` (expected to be empty-string alt, not absent)
- Tracking pixel `o.gif` — `src: https://mi.footlocker.com/...o.gif`
- 5 content images (hero, welcome-series assets, app module, FLX module)

Absent `alt` on decorative tracking pixels should be `alt=""`. Absent `alt` on content images (hero, product banners) degrades image-off rendering in Outlook and Gmail app.

**CSS typo — `.fluid-row .col-9` selector:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
`.md-fluid-row .col-8` is duplicated (`.col-8` already has a rule above); `.md-fluid-row .col-9` is never defined. This will cause the 9-column fluid grid to break on mobile for the `md-fluid-row` variant.

### 5. Compliance

QA notes the header was not found, attributed to AgentMail relay interception. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders (>5k/day to Gmail). Missing this header will cause Gmail to show a manual unsubscribe prompt rather than the one-click UI, and risks deliverability penalties.
