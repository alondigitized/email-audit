# You've still got time to get up to 50% off!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a Nike-branded sale burst within Foot Locker's Summer Savings campaign — and the creative is tighter than the recent generic blasts. Narrowing the hero to Nike ("SAVE ON SWOOSH") gives the email a single, brandable hook instead of the catch-all department-store feel of the June 14 opener. The layout is clean, the hierarchy reads sale-first, and the "CURATED FOR YOU" section with visible star ratings adds a useful personalization layer at the bottom.
- The problem is the subject line. "You've still got time to get up to 50% off!" is **verbatim identical** to the June 15 send — same phrasing, same structure, same urgency — and it lands one day after June 20 told subscribers "Up to 50% off is almost over!" That sequencing destroys the credibility of both messages. A subscriber who opened yesterday expecting a last chance is now being told there's still time. The urgency signal is burned.
- The Father's Day gift card nudge is also stitched in at the top ("THE GIFT THAT ALWAYS FITS"), which was the same dual-message problem flagged on June 20. If it's a Nike sale email, commit to Nike.

## What's working

- **Nike-specific hero** is a smart narrowing move — "SAVE ON SWOOSH" gives the sale a brand story instead of generic percentage-off noise.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — the eye lands on the Nike offer immediately; the 50% off callout is large and readable.
- **"SHOP THE NIKE SALE" CTA** is specific and action-oriented — verb + brand noun, not "Discover."
- **"CURATED FOR YOU" section** with product cards and star ratings is the strongest personalization signal in the email, and it's visible without heavy scrolling.
- **FLX Rewards footer** provides member context without cluttering the main offer.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a direct repeat of June 15** — an identical open invite one week later, the day after the "almost over!" send on June 20, reads as a mistake or lazy copy, not a deliberate strategy.
- **No deadline date** — "still got time" without a specific end date (e.g., "ends Sunday") makes urgency feel hollow, especially after yesterday's near-miss language.
- **Father's Day gift card sub-header** dilutes the Nike focus and looks like an afterthought tacked onto a different campaign.
- **Category-level CTAs** ("SHOP MEN'S / WOMEN'S / KIDS'") in the family section all lead to the same generic sale — no product-level specificity to drive a click.
- **Curated product names are truncated** ("PSD Graphic Brie...", "New Balance Cas...") — on mobile these read as broken.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject line** — the June 20 send already said "almost over." Either commit to a final deadline or reframe around the Nike focus:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike's in the sale — 50% off ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Save on Swoosh: Nike up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The best Nike styles are marked down right now — shop before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dunks, Air Force 1s, and more Nike dropped into Summer Savings.`
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — if the sale ends June 22 or 23, say it. "Ends Sunday" or a countdown-style callout converts hesitant openers into same-session buyers.
- 3. **Cut the Father's Day gift card block** — it's June 21, and Father's Day is over. Move it out of this email entirely.
- 4. **Un-truncate product card names** in the curated section — full product names and prices remove friction at the bottom-of-funnel when someone is already warm.
- 5. **Hero shoe names** — identify the featured Nike models by name (e.g., "Air Force 1," "Dunk Low") directly in the hero copy. One named product anchors the "SAVE ON SWOOSH" message with something to search.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed CSS bug:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The right selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.md-fluid-row .col-8`. This orphans `.col-9` in `md-fluid-row` contexts and double-applies the 74.25% width to `.col-8` on medium viewports, breaking 9-column grid layouts.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
`none` completely suppresses user font-size scaling — an accessibility violation. The correct value is `100%`, which prevents iOS auto-inflation while preserving user overrides.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — screen readers cannot infer document language, which affects pronunciation and assistive tech behavior.

**No issues** with charset (`utf-8`), viewport meta, or format-detection declarations.
