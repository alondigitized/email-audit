# Top styles of the season at prices you'll love.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- After seven consecutive "50% off / LAST CHANCE / EXTENDED / FINAL HOURS" blasts squeezed into six days, Foot Locker finally breaks the pattern — but stumbles into the opposite ditch. Where the sale run was aggressive and repetitive, this send is aggressively vague. "Fresh Heat Now Live" as a hero and "prices you'll love" in the subject promise something warm and deal-adjacent without delivering either. It reads like a campaign reset drafted in a hurry once the Summer Savings creative was exhausted.
- The multi-module structure is solid: ASICS Summer, slides, Air Force lineup — three distinct hooks give subscribers three reasons to click. The layout is clean. The photography is competent. But there is no concrete offer anywhere in this email, and after seven sends that trained subscribers to expect "up to 50% off," showing up with "prices you'll love" is going to land with a thud.
- Bottom line: a structurally decent product showcase that arrives at exactly the wrong moment in this brand's cadence, with a subject line that is the weakest in this entire June run.

## What's working

- **Multi-module layout with distinct product stories** — ASICS, slides, and Force each get their own section with a dedicated CTA, giving three separate reasons to click rather than forcing the whole email through one hero.
- **Seasonal relevance** — "It's an ASICS Summer" is the best copy line in the email; it's specific, timely, and actually creates mild curiosity.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage across any section; the email renders crisply.
- **Subject line length** — 48 characters; hits the mobile-safe window cleanly, even if the content is weak.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer anywhere visible** — after seven emails conditioning subscribers to expect "up to 50% off," landing with "prices you'll love" in the subject and zero discount signaling in the body is a jarring bait-and-pull. The implied promise in the subject doesn't exist in the body.
- **"FRESH HEAT NOW LIVE" hero is wasted space** — this is the most prominent real estate in the email and it says nothing a subscriber couldn't infer from opening a footwear website on any Tuesday.
- **Subject line is the worst in this entire campaign run** — "Top styles of the season at prices you'll love." has no specificity, no urgency, no curiosity hook, and zero personalization. It would lose an A/B test against almost any alternative.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the eighth Foot Locker send in seven days. Even a good email would be fighting subscriber burnout; a vague one has almost no shot at an open.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic across all modules** — each section defaulting to the same verb does nothing to differentiate the ASICS pitch from the slides pitch from the Force pitch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to a real offer or a real product drop** — if this is a new-arrivals email, say "New This Week" with a specific count or brand. If there's any pricing advantage (even "under $100"), say it. "Fresh Heat Now Live" is a tagline in search of a fact.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject line around one of the three product stories** — the ASICS angle is the most defensible; it's brand-specific and seasonal. Use it.
- 3. **Give each module a distinct CTA label** — "Shop ASICS," "Shop Slides," "Shop Air Force" over three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons. Marginal lift, zero cost.
- 4. **Add a pricing anchor to at least one module** — even "starting at $X" is more compelling than nothing, especially immediately after a 50% off campaign where subscribers know what full-price feels like.
- 5. **Drop cadence or bundle** — sending this email three days after the seventh sale blast guarantees the lowest open rate in the run. Hold it a week or merge it with the next send that has an actual offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New ASICS just dropped — summer's best silhouette is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh arrivals: ASICS, slides & Force — shop before sizes go`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three new reasons to update your rotation this July.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `ASICS Summer is here. Plus slides and Force styles you haven't seen yet.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] CSS selector collision — `.md-fluid-row .col-8` duplicated instead of `.col-9`**
```css
/* Line in mobile media query: */
.fluid-row .col-8,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 66% !important; }
.fluid-row .col-9,.md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
/*                  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ should be .md-fluid-row .col-9 */
```
Result: `.md-fluid-row .col-8` is declared twice — the cascade resolves to 74.25% (wrong), and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has no mobile rule at all. Any `md-fluid-row` block using 8 or 9 columns will render at incorrect widths on mobile.

**[MEDIUM] Viewport meta missing `initial-scale=1`**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Should be `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`. Without it, some iOS clients may render at a zoomed-out default scale before snapping.

**[LOW] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` in mobile styles**
```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none !important; }
```
Using `none` disables user text scaling entirely. Best practice is `100%` to preserve accessibility scaling while preventing automatic resizing.

**[LOW] External render-blocking font dependency**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
No `font-display` fallback visible. If `partner-fonts.bluecore.com` is unavailable, no system font fallback is in scope from the available HTML. Confirm the linked CSS includes `font-display: swap` and a fallback stack.
