# Last Chance: 20% off $99+.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Foot Locker email in four days — June 10 teaser, June 11 payload (the 9/10 send), June 12 near-identical repeat, and now June 13 "Last Chance." The creative refresh here is minor: "LAST CHANCE FOR" has been appended above the hero "20% OFF" lockup, and a "SHOP FULL FAMILY SALE" category-button block has been inserted between the hero and the personalized product grid. Neither change adds new information. The offer is identical to what landed in the subscriber's inbox 48 hours ago.
- The execution itself is clean — the magenta-on-black hero reads fast, the FLX Rewards loyalty hook is persistent throughout, and the "Based on Your Style" block with visible star ratings adds genuine relevance. No render bugs. The fine print about login requirement and exclusions is legibly placed under the category buttons, which is a honesty point in its favor.
- The problem is entirely cadence: "Last Chance" has now been said three times in four days in roughly the same voice. The June 11 email already closed the loop perfectly; this send exists to wring one more open out of the list, not to deliver new value. As the history note on the late-May campaign shows, Foot Locker runs this exact playbook — multiple "LAST CHANCE" sends after already extending the sale — and it erodes the credibility of the brand's urgency claims faster than it converts.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" hero reads in under a second — size, weight, and contrast are all doing their job.
- "SHOP FULL FAMILY SALE" category buttons (Men's / Women's / Boys' / Girls') are a genuine addition over the June 12 send; they reduce the click-path for subscribers buying for multiple family members.
- "Based on Your Style" personalized product grid with star ratings and price anchors adds relevance below the fold.
- Fine print ("Must be logged in for discount to be applied in cart") is visibly placed under the offer block, not buried in the footer — honest scoping.
- The "JOIN FLX FOR FREE" secondary CTA maintains acquisition intent for non-members who somehow missed the prior three emails.

## What's weak

- "Last Chance" is doing zero work — it's the third urgent deadline in four days and the brand extended the May sale twice under the same framing. This subject line is crying wolf.
- No preheader is visible at the top of the render; the email will leak navigation or utility text as the inbox preview, which wastes the subject's momentum.
- The hero creative is pixel-for-pixel identical to June 11 and 12 — even a single new visual element (a shoe, a colorway, a lifestyle shot) would signal that this isn't a duplicate.
- No models or demographic lifestyle imagery appear anywhere in the email — the "Based on Your Style" grid is all product-on-white, which limits emotional pull and aspirational purchase intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or restructure the send cadence.** The single highest-leverage change isn't copy or creative — it's not sending this email at all, or collapsing June 12 + 13 into one genuinely last-day send with a hard countdown timer. That would make "Last Chance" mean something again.
- 2. **Add a countdown timer to the hero.** If this truly is the final day, show a live clock ending at midnight. A static "Last Chance" header is zero added urgency over the June 12 email.
- 3. **Set an explicit preheader.** The current render has no visible preheader, meaning inboxes will likely pull nav link text. Write something that builds on the subject rather than repeating it.
- 4. **Refresh the hero visual.** Swap in a single aspirational product — a shoe, a colorway, a new-in item — to signal this isn't a re-send. Even a different background hue (navy instead of black) differentiates it in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 20% off $99+ for FLX members`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends tonight — FLX members save 20%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Log in, shop, done — discount applies automatically at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FLX members: your 20% off expires at midnight. Here's what to grab.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — CSS selector typo in responsive grid:**

```css
/* Line in <style> block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8`, but it should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. Effect: on mobile, any `md-fluid-row` 9-column element loses its responsive width override (falls through to the desktop width), and `md-fluid-row .col-8` elements now receive two conflicting `width` rules (the correct 66% from the col-8 rule + this 74.25% override, with specificity determining the winner — likely 74.25% wins due to source order).

**External font loaded via `<link>` tag:**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

Gmail (web and app), Outlook.com, and most mobile clients strip `<link>` tags. The custom font silently drops to system fallback in the majority of opens. This is a standard ESP limitation, but worth noting if custom typography is load-bearing for the design. No `@import` fallback present in the `<style>` block.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` applied globally:**

```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

This prevents iOS Mail from bumping up small text for readability. Common in email templates but increases risk of unreadable text on small viewports if any copy is set below ~13px.

**Cannot confirm** alt text coverage on images — HTML truncated before `<img>` tags appear. Manual check required.
