# Keep your style fresh this summer with new AF1 colors.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's product pivot email for May 31 — the right instinct after nearly two weeks of pure-discount drumbeating, including a "LAST CHANCE" send that went out the same day as this one. The subject positions this as a fresh-color AF1 story, which is the correct creative beat, but the email body quickly contradicts itself: the second module is another "UP TO 50% OFF" sale banner for Father's Day, the third is a sweepstakes, the fourth is a $100-and-under grid. Four different stories in one scroll is too many.
- The AF1 hero ("FIND YOUR FORCE") is clean and visually strong — orange and blue colorways against a dark background with the Nike swoosh is exactly the kind of product-led creative that made the May 30 Jordan Retro 4 email land so well. That email scored high precisely because it committed to the product story. This one chickens out and staples the sale, a sweepstakes, and a deals grid below it, diffusing the payoff the subject line promises.
- Bottom line: good impulse, poor follow-through. The subject-to-body trust is broken the moment you scroll past the AF1 hero and hit the UP TO 50% OFF banner — again.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is sharp.** "FIND YOUR FORCE" with the orange and blue AF1 colorways is aspirational and visually clean — a genuine break from the discount aesthetic the brand has been running.
- **Subject line is a tonal reset.** No CAPS, no exclamation points, no "FINAL HOURS" — after this cadence, a calm product-focused line stands out.
- **Father's Day timing is on point.** The father's Day sale module and sweepstakes are timely (the holiday is ~3 weeks out), and the "$500 gift card" sweepstakes is a legitimate engagement hook.
- **Hot Picks Under $100 is a useful module.** A price-capped grid gives subscribers who aren't sale-hunting a concrete, easy entry point.

## What's weak

- **Subject and body mismatch.** The subject promises new AF1 colors; the body is a four-module omnibus (AF1 + Father's Day sale + sweepstakes + under-$100 grid). Subscribers who opened for AF1 get sale content immediately after the hero.
- **UP TO 50% OFF reappears — again.** Foot Locker sent a "LAST CHANCE: UP TO 50% OFF!" the same day this email landed. The sale banner in module two makes this feel like a stealth second sale-hammer wearing a product-story disguise.
- **Four CTAs, no clear priority.** SHOP NOW / SHOP FATHER'S DAY / ENTER NOW / SHOP STYLES compete equally; there's no visual weight telling the reader which action matters most.
- **Sweepstakes placement undercuts the product.** "FATHER'S DAY SWEEPSTAKES" is a different job than selling AF1s — it drops in the middle of the email and breaks the narrative flow rather than closing the loop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the AF1 story or commit to Father's Day — not both.** If AF1 is the hero (as the subject suggests), the entire email should be AF1: hero colorway, supporting colorway grid, one "Shop Now." The Father's Day and sweepstakes modules belong in a separate send.
- 2. **Give the Father's Day sweep its own email.** A sweepstakes is a high-engagement mechanism being wasted as module three of a four-part send. One standalone email — "Win a $500 Foot Locker gift card for dad" — would almost certainly outperform this buried placement.
- 3. **Kill the UP TO 50% OFF banner on this send.** If you're billing this as a product story, the discount ribbon is brand voice dissonance. Trust the AF1 to sell itself. Save the sale reminder for the next cadence beat.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA on the hero.** "SHOP NOW" is the weakest possible button copy for a colorway launch. "Shop New AF1 Colors" or "See the New Colors" matches what was promised in the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New AF1 colorways just dropped — find your pair`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The AF1 just got a summer refresh`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Orange, blue, and more — shop new Air Force 1 colors now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh AF1 colors + hot picks under $100 — all inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed CSS selector typo:**
```css
/* Line in @media (max-width: 600px) block */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. On mobile, any `md-fluid-row .col-9` column will not receive the correct 74.25% width override — it will fall through to a wider/default value. All other col-N pairs in the same block are correct; this is a copy-paste error specific to col-9.

**`<meta name="viewport">` is incomplete:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Missing `initial-scale=1`. Some mobile clients and webmail renderers use this value for scaling decisions. Recommended: `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"`.
