# EXTENDED: up to 50% off top styles.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth distinct blast in Foot Locker's Summer Savings campaign in roughly four days, and the second consecutive "extended" email in as many days — arriving 24 hours after the June 23 "Up to 50% off EXTENDED!" send that already scored 9/10 in this run. At the template level, the email is well-built: the 50% OFF hero is clean, the CTA is unambiguous, and the "CURATED FOR YOU" HOKA product grid with real prices and star ratings is the best personalization this campaign has shown. That grid is what saves this email.
- The problem is purely strategic. Foot Locker declared "FINAL HOURS" twice on June 22, then "EXTENDED" on June 23, and now "EXTENDED" again on June 24. Any remaining urgency signal has the credibility of a car dealership that never takes down its "BLOWOUT SALE" banner. Subscribers who opened those June 22 blasts have been trained that Foot Locker's deadlines are decorative.
- The execution is strong enough to drive clicks from motivated buyers. The brand has simply borrowed against its trust account one too many times.

## What's working

- **"CURATED FOR YOU" product grid** — six HOKA and Brooks shoes with model names, prices ($98.98, $119.98, etc.), and visible star ratings is the first genuinely personalized content this campaign has shown; it gives a real reason to scroll past the hero.
- **Hero visual hierarchy** — the oversized "50% OFF" on the green background dominates above the fold; no eye-tracking confusion.
- **FLX Rewards free shipping call-out** — the purple rewards footer ties member benefit directly to the sale, which is a meaningful loyalty hook that prior sends buried or omitted.
- **Clean render** — all six product images load, no broken layouts, no overlapping text, announcement bar is crisp.

## What's weak

- **Credibility is gone** — "EXTENDED" lands as hollow after "FINAL HOURS" ran twice on June 22 and another "EXTENDED" ran June 23; no subscriber believes this sale actually ends anymore.
- **No deadline date** — the hero says "Save while you can!" with zero specificity; at minimum this email needs "Extended through Sunday" or a concrete close date.
- **Product curation is too narrow** — all six curated items are HOKA or Brooks running shoes; a Foot Locker subscriber with any other category interest (Jordan, Nike Lifestyle, adidas) gets nothing personalized here.
- **Preview text and subject repeat the same signal** — announcement bar leading with "SUMMER SAVINGS UP TO 50% OFF | SHOP THE SALE" echoes the "50% off" already in the subject; a chance to add a second hook (free shipping, specific brand) is wasted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the extension authentically** — ditch the fake urgency and try "Good news: we extended the sale" copy that acknowledges the extension honestly; subscribers see through manufactured deadlines and respond better to brands that admit it.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline date in the hero** — "Extended through June 26, midnight" or similar makes the extension feel real and gives subscribers a reason to act now vs. waiting to see the next blast.
- 3. **Elevate the CURATED FOR YOU grid above the generic hero** — the personalized HOKA section is what differentiates this email from every other send in this campaign; putting it second throws away the best card.
- 4. **Widen the product curation beyond running** — if the subscriber data shows they buy running shoes, great; but validate against their full Foot Locker category history before locking six slots to one brand.
- 5. **Rest the cadence** — the June 22 "FINAL HOURS" framing burned the urgency budget; the only way to restore it is to stop sending for 3–5 days before the next campaign.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your HOKA picks: still 50% off (for real this time)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Extended through Sunday: up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `FLX members get free shipping — shop your curated picks before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your saved styles are still 50% off. This one actually ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — CSS selector bug (line in `<style>` block):**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This means any `md-fluid-row` container using a 9-column class never receives a mobile width override — it will inherit the desktop width and overflow or collapse incorrectly on viewports ≤600px.

**Confirmed issue — `<link rel="stylesheet">` for custom fonts:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
This tag is stripped by Gmail (web + Android app), Outlook (Windows, all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Any text styled with the Bluecore-hosted font silently falls back to system fonts in the majority of inbox environments. This is a known Bluecore limitation; confirm fallback `font-family` stacks are defined on all text elements in the inline styles.

**Cannot confirm** — alt text coverage on images, since `<img>` tags were not present in the truncated excerpt. Bluecore widgets (e.g., `widget_4aa58a2ce8c151fc33f3d7106879d394`) dynamically inject product images; verify the template enforces `alt` attributes on all injected `<img>` tags.
