# The Jordan Retro 4 'Toro Bravo' is here!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the creative reset Foot Locker subscribers needed. After six consecutive sends beating the "UP TO 50% OFF" summer sale drum — including two on May 27 alone screaming "FINAL HOURS" and "ACT NOW" — landing a product-drop email with a specific sneaker and its actual name in the subject line feels earned. The Jordan Retro 4 Toro Bravo hero is clean: bold type, the red-and-black colorway doing the work, a members banner at top, and a single primary CTA. For any Jordan collector on the list, this opens.
- The structural problem is the kitchen-sink body. Below the Jordan hero, Foot Locker stacks four more sections — a lifestyle shoe, the recurring "UP TO 50% OFF" mid-email sale block, a "Still Fresh" section, and a gifts section. The sale block in particular undermines the drop-day energy. A subscriber who just got the "Extended!" sale email this same day (May 30) will either roll their eyes at the repeat, or worse, wonder whether the Jordan Toro Bravo is on sale (it isn't — it's a new-release hero, almost certainly full price).
- The Jordan section itself earns its place. The surrounding filler does not.

## What's working

- **Hero credibility:** The red-and-black Toro Bravo colorway is instantly recognizable to Jordan buyers, and the oversized model name typography nails the product-drop tone.
- **Subject line specificity:** Naming the exact model *and* colorway in the subject is rare for Foot Locker — it rewards fans who know what they're looking for and creates genuine curiosity in the inbox.
- **Member banner placement:** "MEMBERS: UP TO 50% OFF" in the persistent header signals ongoing value without burying the lead.
- **Cadence reset:** After the sale marathon, this email reads like news rather than retargeting, which makes it stand out in a fatigued inbox.
- **CTA clarity:** "SHOP JORDAN" is unambiguous and visually prominent in the hero.

## What's weak

- **Mixed signals on sale vs. new drop:** The mid-email "UP TO 50% OFF" banner, combined with a same-day prior send on the sale extension, creates confusion about whether the Jordan is discounted or full price. These should never share a send.
- **No scarcity copy on the Jordan:** Jordan Retros sell out fast and subscribers know it. The hero has no "limited sizes" or "while it lasts" urgency — a missed conversion lever that costs nothing to add.
- **"SHOP JORDAN" CTA is generic:** On a specific product-drop, this should be "GET THE TORO BRAVO" or at minimum "SHOP NOW — LIMITED SIZES."
- **Section overload:** Five distinct content blocks dilute the Jordan's moment. The "Step Into Innovation" and "Gifts for the..." sections have no narrative connection to a new Retro drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the mid-email sale block entirely.** When Foot Locker sends a product-launch email, the sale section is a guest that crashes the party. One message, one hero. If the sale must appear, relegate it to a single line in the footer.
- 2. **Add scarcity copy to the Jordan hero.** A line like "Returning for the first time since 2011 — sizes limited" uses true product history to manufacture real urgency without being dishonest.
- 3. **Sharpen the primary CTA.** "SHOP JORDAN" → "GET THE TORO BRAVO" or "SHOP BEFORE IT'S GONE." Specificity on a Retro launch is a conversion tactic, not cosmetic.
- 4. **Cut to two sections max below the hero.** Jordan → trending pairs that complement → footer. Remove the unconnected lifestyle and gifts sections for this send; they're diluting the drop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Jordan 4 Toro Bravo — limited sizes, shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drop: Jordan Retro 4 'Toro Bravo'`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Back for the first time since 2011 — sizes going fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The red-and-black Jordan 4 is back. Don't sleep.`
