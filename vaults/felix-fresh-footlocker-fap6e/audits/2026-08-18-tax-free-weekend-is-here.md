# Tax free weekend is here.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- The subject line promises "Tax free weekend is here" — but nothing in the rendered body mentions tax-free anything. The hero is 100% "WEEK OF NEW BALANCE UP TO 40% OFF," a purple gradient sneaker banner with zero connection to the subject's premise. This is the most jarring subject/body mismatch I've seen from Foot Locker this week, and it breaks the trust this brand has otherwise built with tight hero-subject pairing on 8/17's Vans sends (8/10, 8/10).
- Setting aside the mismatch, the New Balance creative itself is competent: single hero product (1906R), clear "UP TO 40% OFF" callout, FLX 3X points as a loyalty hook, and a "SHOP THE SALE" CTA. But a recipient who opens on the promise of tax-free savings and lands on a generic New Balance percentage-off sale will feel baited, and that erodes open trust for the next subject line.
- Judgement: solid creative execution undermined by a subject line that doesn't describe the email. This needs a rewrite before it goes out again — either change the subject to match the New Balance sale, or change the hero to actually reflect tax-free weekend messaging.

## What's working

- Clean single-hero layout: one product (New Balance 1906R), one offer (up to 40% off), one CTA — no competing messages.
- FLX loyalty tie-in (3X points) gives members a reason to prefer this over a generic discount.
- "Exclusions apply" is disclosed directly under the CTA rather than buried in a footer, which reads as more honest than some past sends.
- Top Recommendations grid shows real strikethrough pricing ($31.50 was $42, $32.50 was $65) with visible product imagery — no broken images or placeholder text.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Tax free weekend is here" but the entire rendered body is a New Balance sale — there is no tax-free content anywhere in the screenshot. This is a bait-and-switch.
- No urgency element for the New Balance sale itself (no end date/countdown), despite the subject implying a time-bound weekend event.
- Star rating on "Nike Club Spring..." shows only 3.0 stars with no review count, which is weak social proof to lead with.
- Product names are truncated ("Nike Loose Love ...", "Nike NSW M90 Sho...") in the recommendations grid, making it harder to identify items at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/body mismatch first** — either swap in an actual tax-free-weekend hero (state exclusion, eligible states/categories, dates) or change the subject to describe the New Balance sale that's actually in the email. This is the single highest-impact fix; right now the email actively misleads on open.
- 2. Add a specific end date/time to the New Balance sale hero ("Ends Sunday 11:59pm") to justify urgency implied by "Week Of."
- 3. Untruncate product names in the recommendation grid so shoppers can identify items without hovering.
- 4. If tax-free weekend is a real promo, add a dedicated banner/module for it above or below the New Balance hero rather than omitting it entirely from the body.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `No sales tax + up to 40% off New Balance. Ends Sunday.`
