# Up to 50% off EXTENDED!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the post-"FINAL HOURS" resurrection email. Foot Locker sent two blasts on June 22 explicitly telling subscribers the Summer Savings sale was ending — "Last Chance" and then "FINAL HOURS: up to 50% off!" — and here on June 23 they're announcing it's been extended. That sequence is the story of this email, and not a flattering one. Regular subscribers who acted on yesterday's urgency signals will clock this immediately. The creative itself is clean and functional: the green hero lands hard, the brand grid and "Curated For You" section give it real depth beyond a generic sale blast. But trust is this email's primary cost, and it can't recoup that within the frame.
- Taken on its own merits, it's a solid mid-tier sale email. In context, it's exhibit A in why manufactured urgency backfires — the more often a brand cries "last chance," the less a real deadline means anything.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate.** "SALE EXTENDED / UP TO 50% OFF" reads in under a second on mobile; the green field and oversized numerals do exactly what they're supposed to do.
- **"SHOP SALE BY BRAND" grid** (Nike, New Balance, Jordan, Adidas) elevates the email above a generic CTA — it gives brand-loyal shoppers a direct path instead of dumping them on a sale page.
- **"Curated For You" with star ratings** is the email's strongest close — specific products with visible pricing and social proof are more persuasive than any copy.
- **FLX Rewards footer banner** acknowledges member status without cluttering the main layout.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout issues, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Credibility collapse from cadence.** Foot Locker said "FINAL HOURS" and "Last Chance" on June 22; "EXTENDED" on June 23 tells subscribers the urgency was manufactured, not real. Every future deadline is now discounted.
- **"EXTENDED" with no new end date.** The word signals a deadline exists but doesn't tell subscribers when. "Runs through Sunday" or "3 more days" would have done real work here; the vague extension removes the urgency it was meant to create.
- **Preheader repeats the subject.** The top bar text ("SUMMER SAVINGS: UP TO 50% OFF | SHOP THE SALE") leaks as the preview, echoing "up to 50% off" from the subject line word-for-word — a wasted second impression in the inbox.
- **Partial all-caps in subject ("EXTENDED!") with an exclamation** is borderline spam-signal territory, especially as the seventh email in five days on the same campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the extension to a concrete date in the subject.** "Sale extended through Sunday" or "2 more days left" is more clickable than an open-ended "EXTENDED!" and partially restores the urgency the prior blasts burned.
- 2. **Use the preheader to complement, not repeat.** Name the brands or lead with a product hook — the subject already covers the percentage.
- 3. **Add a single acknowledgment line in the hero.** One line — "We extended it. Here's what's still available." — reduces the bait-and-switch read without requiring a redesign.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence after this send.** Seven emails in five days on the same sale guarantees unsubscribe acceleration; the next send should be a new campaign beat, not another variation on 50% off.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale extended through Sunday — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We extended the sale. Here's what's left.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, Jordan, adidas & more — prices drop to half off`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your top picks are still in the sale. Grab them now.`
