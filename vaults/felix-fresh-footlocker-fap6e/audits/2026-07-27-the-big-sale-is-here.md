# ⚠️ The BIG sale is here! ⚠️ ​
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's generic "sitewide sale" beat again — the same fallback creative pattern flagged in the 2026-07-26 "LAST CHANCE: up to 30% off Nike & Jordan" send (5.00/10), just re-skinned for back-to-school. The hero is bold — huge BACK-TO-SCHOOL / BIG / SALE type treatment on red, with a metallic adidas Samba dominating the frame — but the subject line undersells it with vague "BIG sale is here" copy bracketed by warning emojis that read more like spam flags than urgency.
- The bigger problem is a visible render bug: four solid black rectangles sit below the "Exclusions apply" line in the hero, almost certainly broken trust-badge or payment-icon images that never loaded. That's a real deliverability/QA miss, not a nitpick — it's the first thing a scrolling eye hits right after the CTA.
- Compared to the brand's stronger recent sends (Air Max 95 "Solar Red," Jordan Retro 4 "Comic Con"), this lacks a single hero product with a name, price, or story. It's competent template execution dragged down by a subject line that doesn't earn the open and a broken image block that undercuts the polish once you're in.

## What's working

- Hero type treatment ("BACK-TO-SCHOOL / BIG / SALE") is huge, high-contrast, and impossible to miss even glancing quickly.
- "Up to 40% on top styles" gives a concrete discount ceiling right under the fold-in offer.
- FLX Rewards free-shipping banner up top and the pink FLX Rewards footer band reinforce loyalty value for members.
- "Picks for You" grid shows real prices and star ratings (Nike Ja 3 $135, Nike Zoom KD 6 $109.99 w/ strikethrough $145), giving concrete product signal below the hero.
- Clean category navigation (Shop Men's/Women's/Kids'/All) makes browsing frictionless.

## What's weak

- Four black rectangular blocks render broken beneath "Exclusions apply" in the hero — a clear visual bug that looks unfinished/broken in an inbox.
- The hero shoe (metallic silver adidas) is unlabeled — no name, price, or reason to want it, unlike the brand's stronger single-SKU sends.
- Subject line is generic and unspecific — no percentage, no product, no category — despite a warning-emoji wrapper trying to manufacture urgency it doesn't back up.
- No visible deadline anywhere in the hero (no "today," "ends Sunday," etc.), so the sale reads open-ended and skippable.
- Per brand history, this is at least the sixth generic markdown-style send in two weeks — cadence fatigue is real and this one doesn't differentiate itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the broken black image blocks under "Exclusions apply" before this ships again — that's a hard QA blocker, not a style note.
- 2. Rewrite the subject to carry a real number and a deadline instead of emoji bracketing — specificity is what's been driving this brand's 8-9/10 sends.
- 3. Name and price the hero shoe (the metallic Samba) so it functions as an actual product push, not just decoration.
- 4. Add a hard end date/time to the hero copy to make "BIG SALE" feel urgent rather than evergreen.
- 5. Given four back-to-back sitewide-sale sends recently, pivot the next one back to a single hero SKU (this brand's proven winning format) rather than another "shop everything" email.
