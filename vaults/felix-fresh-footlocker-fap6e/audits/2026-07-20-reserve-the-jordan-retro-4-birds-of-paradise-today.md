# Reserve the Jordan Retro 4 'Birds of Paradise' today.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Foot Locker's third drop-alert in a format subscribers have seen before: "Reserve the [product]." The July 14–15 adidas x Bad Bunny sends used the exact same subject-line formula, and this one doesn't differentiate from it. The Jordan Retro 4 Birds of Paradise is a genuine heat release and the hero shot is clean — golden silhouette on a warm background, legible colorway name — but the email sprawls across four separate product blocks (Retro 4, Retro 3, Nike Book 2, and a Back to School lifestyle block), which dilutes the urgency of the "reserve today" hook. If this is a reservation alert, it should feel like a starting gun, not a catalog page.
- The timing also works against it. Yesterday and the day before, Foot Locker sent 30% off Nike & Jordan discount emails. Subscribers who opened those are now getting a zero-discount reservation ask. The mental context switch from "sale" to "go on a waitlist" takes more persuasion than this email delivers.
- The execution is technically clean — no render bugs visible, solid visual hierarchy within each block — but the strategic brief is muddled. A reservation alert should be the only thing on the screen.

## What's working

- **Hero creative is on point.** The Jordan Retro 4 Birds of Paradise gold colorway is a genuine conversation shoe; showing it large and centered earns the subject line.
- **Product variety covers multiple buyer types.** Retro 4, Retro 3, and Nike Book 2 give segments of the subscriber list something to click, even if it blunts the urgency of the hero.
- **No render bugs.** Layout, images, and buttons all appear intact. Clean execution throughout.
- **Subject-line specificity.** Colorway name ("Birds of Paradise") is in the subject — that's the right call for Jordan fans who track specific releases.

## What's weak

- **Four product blocks kill the reservation urgency.** Stacking Jordan Retro 3 and Nike Book 2 below the hero trains the eye to scroll past the action item.
- **No deadline or inventory signal.** "Today" is vague. "Reserve opens at noon, 500 pairs" creates action; "today" does not.
- **Zero price information.** After two days of 30% off emails, jumping to a full-price reservation with no anchor feels like a mood mismatch for that segment.
- **Same "Reserve the [product]" formula as July 14–15.** Two weeks later, same structure — subscribers who got the Bad Bunny alert recognize the template and already know this format often means they won't get a pair anyway.
- **No social proof or heat signal.** A "high demand" badge, a sell-through callout, or even a release date would add believability to the "reserve today" CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip to one product.** Kill the Retro 3 and Nike Book 2 blocks. This should be a one-product emergency, not a multi-product browse.
- 2. **Add a deadline or scarcity signal in the hero.** "Reservation window closes at midnight" or "Limited release" text under the CTA turns "today" into actual urgency.
- 3. **Break the subject-line formula.** The "Reserve the [product] today." construction is now three emails old (Bad Bunny ×2, plus this). It's lost its freshness signal.
- 4. **Anchor the price or offer context.** Even "Full retail, members get early access" gives subscribers a frame vs. the discount emails they just saw.
- 5. **Add a heat-signal callout.** "One of the most-anticipated Jordan drops of the year" or a simulated demand bar does more than empty white space.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited pairs. Reservation closes tonight — don't miss the Jordan Retro 4 Birds of Paradise.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector collision:**
In the `@media screen and (max-width: 600px)` block:
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The second selector should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`, not `.col-8`. As written, `.col-8` receives two conflicting responsive rules and `.md-fluid-row .col-9` receives none. Any nine-column `md-fluid-row` block renders at its desktop width on mobile.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important`** applied broadly to `div, p, a, li, span, td` — `none` fully disables iOS auto-sizing rather than clamping at `100%`, which can cause unintended text scaling in iOS Mail.

**Viewport meta missing `initial-scale`:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">
```
Best practice is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"` for consistent zoom on first load.

**`format-detection` incomplete:**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Missing `address=no, date=no, email=no` — iOS Mail will still auto-link addresses and dates in body copy.
