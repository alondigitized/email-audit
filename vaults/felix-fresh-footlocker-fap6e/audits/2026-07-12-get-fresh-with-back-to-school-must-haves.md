# Get fresh with back to school must-haves!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Foot Locker delivers a clean, four-block Back to School content email built entirely around voice and mood — no discount, no price point, just sneaker culture energy. The "FIRST DAY" hero is punchy and the BTS framing is seasonally on-point for mid-July. Visually this renders without bugs and the hierarchy is sensible: big hero → product story → product story → brand close.
- The problem is this is the second BTS content email in six days — the July 6 send ("Back to school is here! Shop to get first day fresh.") already staked the same territory, same vibe. Without an offer to break the pattern, this send risks feeling like a rerun. The CTAs degrade as you scroll — "SHOP NOW" repeated three times below the hero is a wasted opportunity to deepen each product story.
- Solid execution for a category they know. But two content emails back-to-back with no price hook and the promo fatigue that preceded them means this is fighting for clicks on brand goodwill alone.

## What's working

- **"FIRST DAY" hero copy lands.** Short, evocative, BTS-coded without spelling out "Back to School" in the most boring way possible.
- **"REFRESH THE ROTATION"** is real sneakerhead language — the multi-colorway fan spread backs it up visually.
- **Four distinct product stories** give the email range — someone here for clean casualwear, someone here for performance, both get a reason to scroll.
- **No render issues** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text throughout.
- **Subject line wordplay ("Get fresh")** ties the product (fresh kicks) to the seasonal moment naturally.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere.** Second consecutive content email, no price incentive — this will underperform the promo sends on clicks by a wide margin.
- **Three generic "SHOP NOW" CTAs below the hero** — each section deserved its own hook ("Shop the Rotation," "Shop the Look") matching its own headline.
- **Hero image is dark.** The sneaker imagery behind "FIRST DAY" is muddy at email render size; the product nearly disappears.
- **Exact same creative lane as July 6.** Two BTS content sends in six days dilutes both rather than building on each other.
- **No social proof, no brand-specific differentiation** — this could be any footwear retailer's BTS email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price hook.** Even "BTS styles from $59.99" or a members-only offer in the sub-headline anchors the scroll and gives a reason to click now vs. later.
- 2. **Differentiate this send from July 6 with a specific product drop.** "New Jordan 1s for back to school" beats a second content wave every time. Give subscribers something they haven't seen.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" sub-CTAs with section-specific copy** — "Shop the Rotation," "Get the Look," "Find Your Fit" — one line of copy work that meaningfully lifts click differentiation.
- 4. **Lighten or reframe the hero image.** "FIRST DAY" deserves a bright, confident product shot — the dark background undercuts the energy the copy promises.
- 5. **Add one product detail per section.** Even a brand badge or a single callout ("Air Max cushioning," "sizes 4–15") gives shoppers a reason to click rather than just browse.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — CSS selector typo (mobile column grid):**

```css
/* In <style> block, mobile media query */
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```

The second selector is `.md-fluid-row .col-8` but should be `.md-fluid-row .col-9`. This means:
- `.md-fluid-row .col-9` has **no mobile width defined** (it will inherit unintended widths or break layout).
- `.md-fluid-row .col-8` receives the `74.25%` rule twice (once from its correct rule, once from this typo), which is harmless but confirms the copy-paste error.

**Confirmed issue — aggressive text-size-adjust:**

```css
div,p,a,li,span,td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```

`none` suppresses iOS auto-scaling entirely on all text elements, which can make small text unreadable on older iPhones. The body already sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%`; the media query should match `100%`, not `none`.

**External font dependency (availability risk):**

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```

External CSS is blocked by several corporate email clients and some privacy-focused filters. If this request fails, the email falls back to system fonts with no declared fallback stack in the `<head>`. Confirm a safe fallback is specified in inline styles on text elements.

**Positive findings:**
- `<meta name="viewport">` present ✓
- `charset=utf-8` declared ✓
- Preview text preheader uses correct `display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden` pattern ✓
- Zero-width space padding (`&#8204;`) correctly pushes content below preview length ✓
