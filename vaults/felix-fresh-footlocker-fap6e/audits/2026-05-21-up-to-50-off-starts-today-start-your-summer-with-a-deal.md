# Up to 50% off starts today! Start your summer with a deal.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the sale-launch payoff to yesterday's teaser ("Hot new sale coming soon! Don't miss out 👀" — May 20, 9/10). The teaser did its job; now Foot Locker is delivering: a summery blue-pool hero, oversized "50% OFF" in yellow, the Nike Air Max up front, and a clean "SHOP THE SALE" CTA. The first impression is strong — the visual hierarchy does exactly what it needs to.
- The problem lives one scroll down. The "HOT STYLES" product grid serves six consecutive HOKA shoes. Not one Nike, Jordan, or Adidas product — the three brands prominently featured in the "SHOP SALE BY BRAND" tile block directly above. That disconnect erodes the promise of the hero and makes the email feel algorithmically assembled rather than curated for a sale launch.
- The offer also floats — "starts today" but no end date anywhere in the email. Without a deadline, the urgency that "starts today" creates evaporates immediately. This is a sale launch email that earns its open but does half the job of actually driving urgency at the cart.

## What's working

- **Hero lands the punch.** Big yellow "UP TO 50% OFF" on a pool-blue background with a Nike shoe is visually arresting; the eye lands there first, every time.
- **Brand grid is correct.** Nike, New Balance, Jordan, Adidas — four credible brand tiles right below the hero tell a shopper exactly where to go.
- **"Starts today" + subject pairing is tight.** The teaser yesterday built the setup; this lands the payoff with a clean launch signal.
- **Star ratings on product tiles add credibility** without any extra copy needed.
- **FLX Rewards footer keeps loyalty awareness alive** even inside a pure-discount email.

## What's weak

- **"Hot Styles" = 100% HOKA.** Six HOKA products in a row when the hero promises a brand-spanning summer sale — the disconnect feels like a catalog pull gone wrong.
- **No sale end date anywhere in the email.** "Starts today" without "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" gives readers permission to wait.
- **"Up to 50% off" is a vague ceiling** with no floor anchored. The product tiles show $95–$165 HOKA prices without visible crossed-out original prices to prove the discount.
- **Subject line runs 58 characters** — the second clause "Start your summer with a deal" is filler that gets clipped on most mobile clients.
- **Sixth email in six days** — even the best-executed send in a fatigue window loses open rate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the product feed.** Swap at least three of the six HOKA slots for Nike, Jordan, or Adidas — brands the hero and brand grid explicitly promised. The mismatch is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. **Add an end date.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "3 days only" converts "I'll check later" into a click today. Insert it in the hero subheadline and repeat it in the product section header.
- 3. **Cut the subject's second clause.** Kill "Start your summer with a deal" — it's 29 extra characters that add nothing. Let the cut land harder.
- 4. **Show crossed-out original prices** on the product tiles so the "up to 50% off" claim lands with evidence, not just assertion.
- 5. **Elevate FLX Rewards into the hero** — a single line like "FLX Members: Early Access + Free Shipping" above the CTA converts a footer footnote into a signup driver.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — Summer Kickoff Sale is live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Sale starts NOW: up to 50% off top brands`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nike, Jordan, New Balance, Adidas — all on sale through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop up to 50% off. Sale ends Sunday — FLX members get free shipping.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — CSS selector typo:**
```css
.fluid-row .col-9, .md-fluid-row .col-8 { width: 74.25% !important; }
```
The `.md-fluid-row` half incorrectly targets `.col-8` instead of `.col-9`. Any 9-column fluid element inside an `md-fluid-row` will inherit the wrong width on mid-breakpoint viewports, causing layout misalignment. Every other column pair in the block is correct; this one was transcribed wrong.

**External font `<link>` stripped by Gmail:**
```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://partner-fonts.bluecore.com/footlocker/footlocker_fonts_1776712244.css">
```
Gmail (web and Android) strips `<link>` tags from email `<head>`. The font load silently fails; fallback stack in inline styles determines what renders. If no web-safe fallback is declared in inline `font-family`, recipients on Gmail see the client default. Confirm that inline styles on headline elements carry a complete fallback stack.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important` applied too broadly:**
```css
div, p, a, li, span, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none !important; }
```
This blanket rule prevents iOS Mail from scaling up small text for readability. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) is not formally required for email, but this will suppress the OS-level accessibility accommodation for low-vision users. Scope it to only the elements where fixed sizing is structurally necessary.
