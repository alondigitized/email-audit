# Save 20% on Points for Your Next Getaway
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Hyatt's second "buy Bonus Points at a discount" email in three weeks — following July 17's "Ends July 27: 20% More Bonus Points on Points Purchase." Same mechanic, same 20% discount, same "Buy Points" CTA, just re-skinned with a new hero image and a fresh deadline (October 5). The creative execution itself is clean: a moody cabin hero, a direct headline, a single unmissable blue CTA button, and — smart addition this time — a tiered "redeem your points" gallery showing exactly what 10K–30K points buys (specific hotels, specific point costs).
- Where it falls short is personalization and stakes. Helena is shown as "0 Current Points," which makes the entire pitch — buy points now to book a getaway later — land as generic acquisition copy rather than a targeted nudge tied to her actual account behavior. The redemption tier gallery is the best part of the email; it makes the abstract "buy points" ask concrete by showing what those points actually convert to.
- Net: well-produced, on-brand, no rendering issues, but it's the same lever pulled twice in a month, and the fine print (minimum purchase, no elite-tier credit, no refunds) undercuts the "honest offer" read a discerning loyalty member would apply.

## What's working

- Single, high-contrast CTA ("Buy Points Now") is impossible to miss and appears right after the offer headline.
- The "Turn your points into a summer sendoff" module ties the abstract points purchase to four real, priced redemption options — this is the one truly useful addition versus the July 17 send.
- Member status bar at top ("Hi Helena Globalist, Member | 0 Current Points") is a real personalization touch, even if the number undercuts urgency.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent brand typography and blue palette end to end.
- Brand/collection logo wall near the footer reinforces portfolio breadth without cluttering the primary offer.

## What's weak

- This is the second "discount to buy points" campaign in three weeks (July 17 vs. Aug 7) — a subscriber will notice the repeat.
- Zero points balance shown for the recipient makes "buy points for your next getaway" feel like a cold-acquisition pitch rather than a tailored nudge.
- The offer's real friction (minimum 2,000-point purchase, no elite-tier credit, no refunds, non-US resident fees) is only in dense footer legal text — not signaled anywhere near the CTA.
- A second, unrelated offer (75K Bonus Points credit card) is jammed into the same email, diluting the single message the subject promised.
- No urgency in the body — "Offer ends October 5, 2026" is nearly two months out, which won't create any real reason to act today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Tie the ask to her actual balance — with 0 points, lead with "Buy your first points and unlock [specific redemption]" rather than generic "boost your balance" copy; zero-balance members need a different narrative than points-rich members.
- 2. Cut the credit card cross-sell entirely from this send — it splits attention from the points-purchase CTA and this audience already got a dedicated card pitch on July 13/20.
- 3. Surface one key restriction near the CTA (e.g., "min. 2,000 points, doesn't count toward elite tier") so the offer reads as transparent rather than fine-print-gated.
- 4. Pull the redemption-tier gallery higher, above or immediately beside the CTA — it's the strongest proof point in the email and currently sits below the fold.
- 5. Space this out from July 17's near-identical send, or reframe explicitly as "we heard you missed it" if intentionally repeating.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Turn a small point purchase into 3 free nights — here's how.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what 10K–30K points unlocks this fall.`
