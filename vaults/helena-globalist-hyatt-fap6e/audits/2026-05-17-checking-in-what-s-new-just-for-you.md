# Checking In—What’s New, Just for You
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Hyatt's second email in 24 hours — the welcome landed yesterday (May 16), this newsletter drops today (May 17). That cadence is aggressive for a brand-new subscriber who hasn't even had time to book a first stay. The email is a destination-discovery newsletter, not a promotional mailer: heavy on aspirational hotel photography, a personalized "Destinations Picked for You" block, and a full Hyatt brand logo grid at the bottom. There is no visible specific discount, dollar amount, or points bonus anywhere in the scroll.
- The personalization signal is real and appreciated — "just for you" in the subject and a dedicated curated-destinations section suggest the CRM is doing something useful. But the execution doesn't deliver on that promise. Multiple property cards compete for attention, there's no single headline offer to act on, and the brand logo grid at the bottom reads as filler. As a new Hyatt member who hasn't stayed yet, this email gives me nothing actionable: no reason to book now, no urgency, no offer.
- The verdict: competent editorial content that arrives too soon and asks nothing of the reader.

## What's working

- **Personalization hook is visible** — a dedicated "Destinations Picked for You" section signals the email is member-specific, not a blast.
- **Property photography is strong** — hotel imagery is aspirational and on-brand for a premium travel loyalty program.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — text, images, and sections all appear intact with no overlapping elements or broken assets visible.
- **Subject line pun lands** — "Checking In" works as wordplay for a hotel brand checking in on a new member; under 50 characters and spam-free.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no reason to click now** — no discount, no bonus points, no limited-time rate. Pure editorial content with no conversion hook.
- **Cadence is wrong** — sending a content newsletter the day after a welcome email doesn't give the new member time to explore the program or develop a reason to re-engage.
- **Too many competing CTAs** — multiple property cards each pulling the eye in different directions; no single hero action wins.
- **Brand logo grid adds zero value** — a grid of sub-brand logos (Park Hyatt, Grand Hyatt, etc.) at the bottom is portfolio padding that a day-one member doesn't need.
- **No points balance, no progress bar** — a loyalty email to a brand-new member is the ideal moment to show their starting balance and how far they are from a first reward night. That hook is absent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the member's points balance and a clear goal** — "You have 500 points. Earn X more for a free night" is the single highest-lift addition. It personalizes the email with real account data and gives a reason to book.
- 2. **Collapse to one featured destination with one CTA** — pick the top personalized property, give it the full hero treatment, add a "Book Now — Rates from $X" button. Cut the multi-card grid.
- 3. **Hold this send by 48–72 hours** — welcome email just hit; give the new member time to explore the program before the second touch.
- 4. **Replace the brand logo grid** — swap with a brief "Your tier benefits" summary (late checkout, bonus points, etc.) to reinforce the value of membership.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your first World of Hyatt stay — here's where to go`
- **Subject Alt B:** `500 points waiting. Here's what they'll get you.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `We picked 3 destinations based on your profile — rates from $X`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New member perk: double points on your first stay through June 30`
