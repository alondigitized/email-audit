# Homepage snapshot · skechers.com · 2026-07-22
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so first impression: Skechers is leaning hard into performance running right now, which is not really where my head is at. The 25% off banner at the top catches my eye immediately — that's the kind of thing that makes me stop scrolling — but the big athletic guy on the bleachers with a basketball doesn't exactly scream "busy mom grabbing a quick pair of slip-ons." I do notice "Slip-ins" is literally the first nav tab, which gives me hope, but the hero itself feels like it's aimed at someone training for a 5K, not chasing a toddler through Target.

## What's working

- **The 25% off promo strip** is front and center at the very top — code is visible, threshold is clear ($100+), and as someone who checks for deals before anything else, this is exactly the right placement
- **"Slip-ins" as the first nav tab** — whoever made that call understood their audience; I can tap straight to my category without any hunting
- **"AVAILABLE AS SKECHERS SLIP-INS" badge** on the hero shoe — a small but smart touch that ties the running campaign back to a product line I actually care about
- **"Skechers x Care Bears" secondary banner** — honestly kind of fun, and as a mom I'd probably click that out of curiosity (or for my kid)

## What's weak

- **The hero model and imagery** is entirely the wrong demographic — a young male athlete on gym bleachers with a basketball doesn't speak to me at all; I feel like I've landed on the men's athletic page by mistake
- **No CTA button in the hero** — "COMFORT THAT PERFORMS" is the headline but there's nowhere to tap from the hero section itself; the athlete just sits there
- **No free shipping mention** — this is one of my biggest motivators and it's nowhere above the fold
- **"OG ANUNGY" / "A FORCE TO BE RECKONED WITH"** — I have no idea who this person is and the sports-celebrity framing doesn't move the needle for me at all
- **No trust signals or reviews visible** — as an online shopper I want to see stars, review counts, or at minimum a "free returns" callout before I commit to exploring

## Recommendations

- **Swap or rotate the hero to a lifestyle shot with a female shopper** — a woman walking through a farmers market, dropping kids off, or on a casual weekend outing would land so much better for the comfort-focused audience that Slip-ins actually attract
- **Add a "Shop Slip-ins" button directly in the hero** — don't make me hunt through the nav; if your first tab is Slip-ins anyway, put a tap-ready button where the eye already is
- **Surface free shipping in the top promo bar** — even "Free shipping on orders $X+" next to the 25% off code would dramatically improve conversion intent; that's a decision-driver for me
- **Move a review count or trust badge above the fold** — even one line like "4.7 stars · 200K+ reviews" near the promo strip would make me feel more confident before I start clicking around
