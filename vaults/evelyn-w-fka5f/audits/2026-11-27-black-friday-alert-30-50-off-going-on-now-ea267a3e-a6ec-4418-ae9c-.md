# BLACK FRIDAY ALERT: 30-50% off going on now!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the Black Friday day-of send, and it works as a billboard. A black hero carries "30%–50% OFF SITEWIDE" in huge red type. A red "SHOP THE SALE" button sits under it, and the deadline is "Valid online only thru 11/29." Directly below, a dashed red strip says "Skechers Plus members stack an EXTRA 15% OFF EVERYTHING, CODE: BF2026." The eye lands on the offer first.
- The send is also the same email you've been getting all week. The 11/23 "Our biggest sale of the year is now live!" and the 11/26 "Fan favorites, now for less" sends used the same banner and the same BF2026 member strip. Only the headline, "Wake Up & Save!", is new. The body is a grid of category tiles with discount badges, a $49.99 doorbuster block, and a long list of links. Nothing in it is personalized or product-specific. Every CTA is a generic "Shop Now" to a category.
- Verdict: a solid, clear sale billboard that adds nothing new for a subscriber on the fifth near-identical Black Friday send. The urgency is real, since it's the day itself, but the creative is recycled.

## What's working

- The hero offer is unmissable. Huge red "30%–50% OFF" on black, with a clear "SHOP THE SALE" button.
- The member stack is concrete: "EXTRA 15% OFF EVERYTHING, CODE: BF2026". It rewards loyalty with a specific number.
- Each tile has its own discount badge (30%, 40%, 50%, up to 50%), so shoppers can pick a category by savings.
- The $49.99 doorbuster block gives a price anchor, and the ticket-style border sets it apart from the tiles above it.
- The deadline, "thru 11/29", is stated in the hero and is credible.

## What's weak

- It's the fifth or sixth Black Friday send this week with the same banner and member strip, so the cadence is tired.
- All CTAs are generic "Shop Now" buttons to categories. No single hero product is featured, and no sizes, reviews or ratings appear.
- The discount tiers are muddled. "30–50% sitewide", per-category badges, "up to 50%" and the "extra 15%" are four stacked claims. The fine print is dense and buried at the bottom.
- The tile images carry small "AI generated model" labels, and the "Sale Styles" tile has no clear single offer.
- The second half of the email is long and flat: a link list, Klarna/Afterpay, an app download block and social icons. Nothing there is a compelling reason to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero product or "best sellers under $60" instead of four category tiles. Specific product, price and rating beat a generic "Shop Now".
- 2. Collapse the offer to one headline claim and one member claim. Move the per-category badges below the fold.
- 3. Tell the member what the stacked price actually is, for example "$70 shoe → $42 with BF2026".
- 4. Change the CTA copy to verb + noun, such as "Shop women's sneakers" or "Grab the $49.99 doorbusters".
- 5. Add a real deadline with a time, such as "Ends 11/29 11:59 PM PT", and put a countdown timer in the hero.
- 6. Cut the footer link stack and the app block, and keep one fine-print block.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Doorbusters from $49.99 — today + 15% extra`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your extra 15% (BF2026) stacks today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Members: code BF2026 takes another 15% off. Sale ends 11/29.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `30–50% off sneakers, boots and apparel, plus $49.99 doorbusters.`

## Technical Audit

# Technical Audit: "BLACK FRIDAY ALERT: 30-50% off going on now!"

The HTML source is truncated, so everything after the preheader (body links, footer, unsubscribe block, physical address) was not inspected. Findings are limited to what the source and QA data show.

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is structurally sound: a table-based layout, responsive media queries, and valid Gmail promotion annotations. It has two images served over plain `http://`, two images with no alt text, a zoom-disabling viewport meta, and no `lang` attribute. Header-level compliance and authentication could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 40 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe because they are on click-redirect domains. Their destinations and status codes are unverified, so the QA data neither confirms nor rules out broken links.
- **Insecure tracking pixel:** `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108862811&mi_ecmp=PRO_US_NA_U_BLACKFRI_FULL_EN_11272026_AM` is loaded over HTTP. Clients that enforce HTTPS or block mixed content may drop it, which would undercount opens and any dynamic-content callbacks.
- **Identifier in plaintext:** the same URL carries `mi_u=8108862811`, which looks like a per-recipient identifier. Sending it over unencrypted HTTP exposes it to network observers.
- **Favicon:** `<link rel="icon" href="http://image.emails.nl.skechers.com/...70bf5f91-...png">` is also HTTP. It is on a different subdomain (`emails.nl.`) from the other assets (`emails.`), which suggests a leftover from a template or an older ESP configuration. Favicons are ignored in email clients, so this is low severity.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content-bearing image:** `http://image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115707564047a731c78/m/1/dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png`. This image also loads over HTTP, so it may be blocked. If it is blocked and has no alt, the recipient sees nothing in its place. This is the highest-impact rendering issue.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixel:** `o.gif` has no `alt` attribute. For a 1×1 pixel, `alt=""` is the correct fix; omitting `alt` lets some screen readers announce the file name.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). The CSS rule `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }` also stops iOS text scaling.
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>` has no `lang` or `xml:lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the correct pronunciation.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Skechers&reg;</title>` doesn't match the subject line. This is minor.
- **Mixed table semantics:** the visible table uses `role="presentation"`, which is correct. I could not confirm whether the rest of the layout tables do the same, because the source is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved merge tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) are visible in the inspected portion. The only per-recipient value is `mi_u=8108862811` in the pixel URL, which appears to be resolved. The truncated remainder was not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are reported missing. The QA tool notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed, not a verified defect**. Check the raw message at the source ESP. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe from bulk senders (5,000+ messages a day).
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not found. This is expected via the relay, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The sender is `no-reply@emails.skechers.com`, and a `no-reply` address is a minor deliverability and CAN-SPAM-hygiene concern.
- **Body unsubscribe link and postal address:** not visible in the truncated source, so not assessed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
The only destination I could inspect is the JSON-LD annotation URL:
`https://www.skechers.com/black-friday-cyber-deals/?srule=top-sellers&src=2026Q4BLACKFRIDAY&utm_source=trig&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=PRO_US_NA_U_BLACKFRI_FULL_EN_11272026_AM&utm_content=annotation&utm_term=googleannotation1`

- UTMs are complete (source, medium, campaign, content, term), and `utm_campaign` matches the pixel's `mi_ecmp`, so the tracking is consistent.
- `utm_source=trig` and `utm_term=googleannotation1` are non-standard. Confirm that analytics maps them correctly, since `trig` may not match the source value used on the body links.
- The JSON-LD offer window runs from `2026-11-23T00:11:00Z` to `2026-11-29T23:11:59Z`. Those odd minute values (`00:11`, `23:11:59`) look like a typo for `00:00:00` and `23:59:59`. This only affects how the annotation is displayed.
- Body link UTMs and landing page alignment could not be assessed, because the links are on skipped tracking domains and the source is truncated.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve all images over HTTPS. This covers `dde00662-...png` and the `ink1000.com` pixel, and ideally the favicon. Check whether `https://www.ink1000.com/...` is supported.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to `dde00662-...png`, and `alt=""` to `o.gif`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta, and replace `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` with `100%`.
4. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` plus SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the original message at the ESP, not through the relay.
6. Resolve the 40 skipped tracking links with a redirect-following probe, and confirm the final URLs carry the expected UTMs.
7. Fix the JSON-LD `availabilityStarts` and `availabilityEnds` timestamps if they are not intentional.
8. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to cover the footer, unsubscribe link, physical address and body links.
