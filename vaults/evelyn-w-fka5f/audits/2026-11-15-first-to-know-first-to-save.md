# First to know, first to save
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- This is an SMS-acquisition email dressed up as a Black Friday send. The "LET'S TEXT!" hero and the big "20% OFF YOUR PURCHASE" numeral are bold, and the eye lands there first. The offer is easy to understand, and "OPT IN & SAVE" is a clear button. Compared with the 2026-11-13 "Our #1 style is calling your name" and 2026-11-14 "Our top picks, all in one place" sends, which led with BOGO 50% off, this one asks for something before it gives anything.
- The weak part is the mismatch. The 20% is gated behind a first-time text signup, and the only explanation is the fine print at the very bottom. The subject, "First to know, first to save", promises early access or savings, but the body is a permission ask. The product block is thin: two lifestyle shots, "Best Sellers" and "New Arrivals" links, then a plain category list. Someone who already gets these emails and just wants the BOGO deal has little to click.
- Overall it's a competent but low-value send. It works if the goal is growing the SMS list, and it is weak as a revenue driver this close to Black Friday.

## What's working

- The "20% OFF" numeral is huge and high-contrast, so the offer reads at a glance.
- "OPT IN & SAVE" is a clear, outlined button that says what you do and what you get.
- The text-alert pitch names Black Friday and Cyber Monday, so it fits the season.
- The two holiday-styled product photos (winter boot, brown sneaker) are warm and on-theme.
- The layout is clean and uncluttered, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The 20% is conditional on a first-time SMS signup, and the exclusions are buried in tiny footer text. That undercuts how honest the offer feels.
- The phone mockup repeats the offer, "Unlock 20% OFF" and "GET 20% OFF NOW", so the same message appears three times. Its legal text is unreadable.
- Nothing here is time-bound. There is no deadline and no Black Friday date, so there is no reason to act today.
- The product section is weak. There is no price, no named style and no reviews, and the stacked text-only category list feels like filler.
- The "Join Skechers Plus" bar at the top competes with the main ask. Loyalty points and an SMS discount send people two ways.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Black Friday product offer, such as the BOGO 50% off banner from 11/13 and 11/14, and make the SMS signup a secondary module. The hero should deliver value first.
- 2. State the condition near the offer, for example "20% off your first order when you sign up for texts", so the footer isn't the first place anyone learns it's gated.
- 3. Add a deadline or a hint of exclusivity, such as "Early access starts Thursday", to give the "first to know" idea some force.
- 4. Replace the category list with 3-4 specific products showing price and a star rating, and link to each one.
- 5. Drop the Plus bar from this send, or move it below the main CTA. Remove the repeated 20% in the phone mockup.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off when you text us: Black Friday early access`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Get Black Friday deals before everyone else`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Opt in to texts for 20% off your purchase and first look at Black Friday deals.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Text alerts unlock 20% off plus early access to Cyber Monday savings.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is structurally sound (table layout, `role="presentation"`, a responsive CSS block). It has two non-HTTPS images missing alt text, a third-party hashed-identifier tracking stack, and accessibility-hostile viewport and text-size settings. The source is truncated, so links, footer and unsubscribe markup could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **29 tracking links were not probed.** The QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations and redirect health are unverified.
- **Third-party pixels in the hidden preheader block:**
  - `https://beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif?partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_sha25&partner_uid=f03aefa3…8628` passes what appears to be a SHA-256 hash of the recipient identifier, likely the email address, to a third-party DMP (Krux).
  - `…partner=LprH63a7_sfmc_100007688_subid&partner_uid=123064971` passes the subscriber ID.
  - `…1x1_ad_impression.gif?confid=so3yexmpm&advertiserid=100007688&campaignid=MKG_US_NA_U_JOINSMS_ACQ_EN_11152026` is a third ad-impression pixel.
  - All three have no `alt` attribute. The `width=0 height=0` attributes are unquoted, which is valid but inconsistent with the first pixel.
- **Per-recipient ID in a URL.** `http://www.ink1000.com/p/up/7076d8b15a776085/o.gif?mi_u=8108862811&mi_ecmp=MKG_US_NA_U_JOINSMS_ACQ_EN_11152026` is a pixel over plain HTTP. Its `mi_u` value is a per-recipient identifier and is exposed unencrypted.
- **Inconsistent image hosts.** The favicon `<link>` uses `image.emails.nl.skechers.com`, and other images use `image.emails.skechers.com`. The first pixel uses `click.emails.skechers.com`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Mixed content.** Two images load over `http://` and may be blocked or rewritten:
  - `o.gif` from `ink1000.com`.
  - `dde00662-169f-447d-b0e2-fc65f6c2290c.png` from `image.emails.skechers.com/lib/fe3115…/m/1/`.
- **Missing alt text.** Both images above have no alt (QA-confirmed). The PNG is a content image, not a pixel, so it is the more serious of the two.
- **No `lang` attribute.** The tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" …>` with no `lang`, so screen readers can't select a pronunciation profile.
- **Zoom is restricted.** `<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch zoom (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Text resizing is disabled.** `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }` stops iOS text resizing.
- **Possible missing content in some clients.** `.desktop-content { display: none; }` is the base rule, and it is only shown inside `@media (min-width: 768px)`. Clients that strip or ignore media queries (e.g. some Gmail account types) would render only the mobile block. This is a risk inferred from the CSS, not a confirmed failure.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `${…}`) appear in the visible source. The truncated portion was not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is **unconfirmed**, not a verified sender defect. Check the raw message headers in the original mailbox. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` is absent,** so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `emails.skechers.com`. Verify alignment against the raw message.
- **Footer not inspected.** The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated section, so they are unverified.
- **Disclosure of data sharing.** The hashed-identifier and subscriber-ID pixels send recipient data to a third party. Confirm this is covered in the privacy policy.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Unverifiable from the data provided. All 29 tracked links were skipped and the `href` values are not in the truncated source. The only campaign identifier visible is `MKG_US_NA_U_JOINSMS_ACQ_EN_11152026` (in the `krxd` and `ink1000` pixels). It indicates an SMS-signup acquisition campaign, but no UTM parameters were confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Serve both flagged images over HTTPS. Add a descriptive `alt` to `dde00662-…png`, and `alt=""` to `o.gif` and the `krxd` pixels.
2. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta. Replace `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` with `100%`.
3. Inspect the raw headers (not the AgentMail copy) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass.
4. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Resolve the 29 tracked links through their redirects and check landing-page UTMs, the unsubscribe link and the postal address.
5. Decide whether the `krxd` hashed-ID pixels and the HTTP `ink1000` pixel are intended. Remove them or move them to HTTPS if not.
6. Consider a fallback for `.desktop-content` for clients that ignore media queries.
