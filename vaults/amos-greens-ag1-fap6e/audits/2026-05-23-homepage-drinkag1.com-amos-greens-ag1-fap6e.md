# Homepage snapshot · drinkag1.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is my brand — I drink this stuff every morning — so I'm not a cold visitor here, I'm a loyalist checking the homepage like I'd check a forum for updates. What I see is a clean, Hugh Jackman-anchored campaign that leans into performance credibility without overselling. It looks polished, trust-focused, and very one-product. The vibe is "serious supplement company," not "flashy wellness brand," which is exactly right for me.

## What's working

- **"Better Mornings, No Matter Hugh You Are" headline** — the wordplay is clever without being cringey. It earns attention without screaming. For a 38-year-old who reads ingredient panels twice, this feels like it was written for someone who appreciates signal over noise.
- **The Hugh Jackman framing** — he's not just a celebrity, he's a documented obsessive about his physique. That specificity makes this credible. Naming him by name in the sub-copy ("the morning ritual that keeps Hugh Jackman at the top of his game") is the right move.
- **NSF Certified for Sport badge** — as someone who cross-checks supplements against banned substance lists, this single badge does more work than a paragraph of benefits copy.
- **"Backed by industry-leading research" and "Trusted by leading scientists and athletes"** — these land as the right trust ladder for my brain: certification → research → endorsement.
- **The green CTA button** — high contrast against the dark hero, clearly the action to take.

## What's weak

- **"Get Started" CTA copy is generic** — I'm already started. I've been started for years. This copy reads like an acquisition funnel, not a page that knows I might be a returning subscriber. There's no "Manage My Subscription" or "Shop Travel Packs" or anything that acknowledges I exist.
- **The hero image is dark and hard to read** — moody aesthetic is fine, but I can barely make out what's happening in the image. If Hugh Jackman is in there, I can't tell from this viewport. A stronger visual tie between the man and the product would sharpen the pitch.
- **Zero mention of the subscription model above the fold** — this is a subscription-first product. I've been auto-shipped AG1 for years and there's no hint of what the subscription looks like, what the savings are, or that a subscription even exists. That's a miss for someone like me who thinks in annual commitments.
- **No price anchor or trial offer** — for a first-time visitor on the fence, there's nothing to reduce the activation energy. The bottom-of-page lifestyle grid is too far below the fold to do that work.
- **No navigation bar visible** — I can only see the logo and the hero. There's no visible way to jump to travel packs, bundles, or anything specific without committing to "Get Started."

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Manage Subscription" or "Member Login" entry point** in the header alongside the logo — even a small avatar icon — so returning subscribers like me feel seen immediately rather than being funneled through the same "Get Started" path as a cold prospect.
- **Change "Get Started" to something product-specific** — "Start Your Morning Ritual" or "Subscribe & Save" makes the value proposition explicit and filters intent better.
- **Show the subscription price anchor above the fold** — even a line like "From $2.63/day on a subscription" gives my brain a number to evaluate. Right now there's no economic signal at all.
- **Make the Hugh Jackman connection visually unmistakable** — if he's in the hero image, show his face. If the image is a lifestyle shot, put his name in the image itself or add a small portrait. The copy earns it; the visual should match.
