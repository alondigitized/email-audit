# Homepage snapshot · drinkag1.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Okay, AG1 knows exactly what they're doing here — Hugh Jackman as the morning-ritual anchor is a very deliberate flex for the performance-optimization crowd I live in. This is a clean, brand-confident acquisition page: one product, one CTA, trust signals front and center. But as someone who already has an annual subscription and is about to pack travel sachets for a trip to Japan, this page doesn't see me at all — it's pure top-of-funnel new-customer machinery.

## What's working

- **The Hugh pun headline** — "Better Mornings, No Matter Hugh You Are." is genuinely clever and it hooks me before I process it consciously. For a brand that lives in the self-optimization space, light wit is exactly the right register.
- **NSF Certified for Sport® badge** — I read ingredient panels twice before I buy anything. NSF certification is the one trust signal I actually care about because it's an independent third-party verification, not brand copy. Putting it immediately below the fold is smart.
- **"Backed by industry-leading research"** — as someone who has read Bryan Johnson's Blueprint stack, this lands. It's not just "made with good stuff," it's research-backed.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no noise, no 15 nav items, no pop-up modal assaulting me on load. For a premium supplement brand, restraint is brand-appropriate.
- **Green CTA button** — the brand color doing heavy lifting. High contrast, immediately actionable.

## What's weak

- **"Get Started →" is a new-customer-only CTA** — I'm already subscribed annually. This button is completely useless to me. There's no "Manage Subscription," "Order Travel Packs," or "Member Dashboard" path visible anywhere.
- **No price or offer information** — AG1 is expensive ($79–$99/month territory). For a new visitor doing due diligence, there's zero financial context above the fold. That's a real friction point.
- **Hero image is underlit/dark** — The Hugh Jackman photo in the hero reads visually muddy on mobile. I can tell it's a person, outdoors, doing something athletic, but the detail is lost. The brand is premium; the image should pop.
- **No reviews or social proof counts** — "Trusted by leading scientists and athletes" is brand copy. I want to see "400,000+ subscribers" or a star rating. The lifestyle grid at the bottom has photos but no review snippets.
- **No ingredient or formula teaser** — For someone like me who is genuinely curious about the 75-nutrient stack, there's nothing above the fold that says "here's what's in it." That's the hook for high-information buyers.

## Recommendations

- **Add a returning-member entry point** — A small "Already a member? Sign in →" link in the header nav costs nothing and stops existing subscribers from bouncing in confusion.
- **Replace "Get Started →" with "See What's Inside →" as a secondary CTA** — Keep the primary acquisition CTA, but add a research-curious path for people like me who won't click "Get Started" until they've read every ingredient.
- **Show the subscriber count or review count** — Swap "Trusted by leading scientists and athletes" for "Join 500,000+ daily subscribers" or "4.8★ from 35,000 reviews." Specific numbers > vague authority claims.
- **Brighten the Hugh hero image** — Increase contrast or exposure on the mobile crop. On a 390px-wide phone screen, a dark hero reads as a load failure, not a mood.
