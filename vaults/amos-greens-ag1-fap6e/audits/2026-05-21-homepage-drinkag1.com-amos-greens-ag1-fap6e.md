# Homepage snapshot · drinkag1.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is my brand — literally what I blend every morning — so I came in with high expectations. What I get is a clean, confident homepage built around a Hugh Jackman celebrity partnership and a tight trust-signal stack. It reads as a premium performance supplement brand that knows it's for serious people. Right now it's pushing one thing: the "Better Mornings" Hugh campaign, and it does that with focus. But as a returning annual subscriber, it has almost nothing to say to me specifically.

## What's working

- **"Better Mornings, No Matter Hugh You Are" headline** — the pun is genuinely good and the framing is dead-on for a biohacker who treats mornings as sacred. This isn't a vague wellness play; it's a performance ritual message.
- **NSF Certified for Sport® badge** — as someone who reads every ingredient panel twice, NSF certification is the first thing I check. Having it in the trust trifecta immediately above the fold is exactly right.
- **"Backed by industry-leading research" + "Trusted by leading scientists and athletes"** — these two trust bullets land together; they tell me this isn't just influencer marketing, it's substantiated.
- **Green CTA button contrast** — the lime green against the dark hero image pops hard on mobile. I see it immediately.
- **Lifestyle imagery grid at the bottom** — people in outdoor/active settings, not a sterile lab. Feels lived-in rather than pharmaceutical.

## What's weak

- **"Get Started →" CTA is cold to me as a subscriber** — I'm not getting started, I've been here for years. There's no "Manage my subscription," no "Reorder travel packs," no member-anything. This entire above-the-fold is built for acquisition, and I'm invisible.
- **Hero image is dark and underexposed** — whatever Hugh Jackman lifestyle shot is behind that headline is murky on mobile. I can barely make it out. The image isn't doing the heavy lifting it should.
- **No pricing signal anywhere** — I understand the brand doesn't lead with discounts, but even a "from $X/month" anchors expectations and reduces bounce for curious new shoppers without alienating me.
- **No product specificity** — "AG1" could be anything to a new visitor. What exactly is it — a powder? A capsule? I know, but a first-timer doesn't, and there's no product shot or format callout above the fold.
- **Sub-copy is faint and small** — the sentence referencing Hugh Jackman's routine is hard to read at mobile resolution. If that's the proof point, it needs to breathe more.

## Recommendations

- **Add a member-state CTA path** — even a secondary link like "Already a member? Log in →" below the primary CTA would signal that returning subscribers are seen. It costs almost nothing and reduces the friction of feeling like a stranger on your own brand's homepage.
- **Swap the dark hero crop for a brighter lifestyle shot** — Hugh Jackman or whoever the talent is should be clearly visible. If this is a celebrity-anchor campaign, show the celebrity. The murky image wastes the partnership.
- **Add a format/product callout in the hero** — one line under the headline: "75 vitamins, minerals & whole-food sourced nutrients. One daily scoop." That's it. Grounds what AG1 actually is without undercutting the premium feel.
- **Surface the travel-pack SKU in the lifestyle grid** — as someone who stockpiles travel packs before every trip, seeing travel-pack imagery or copy would immediately increase my session depth. It's a real use case that likely drives LTV.
