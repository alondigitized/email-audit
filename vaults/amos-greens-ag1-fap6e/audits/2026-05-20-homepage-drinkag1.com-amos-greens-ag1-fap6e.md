# Homepage snapshot · drinkag1.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is AG1 doing exactly what AG1 does — one product, clean execution, celebrity credibility front and center. The Hugh Jackman campaign is a smart play for the biohacker-adjacent crowd I run in: the guy is notorious for his Wolverine prep discipline, and seeing his name attached to my morning ritual hits different than a random influencer. That said, the homepage is clearly acquisition-mode, not retention-mode, and I'm an annual subscriber — the page doesn't know I exist.

## What's working

- **"Better Mornings, No Matter Hugh You Are" headline** — the wordplay is sharp, age-appropriate, and immediately signals this is a performance/optimization brand, not a diet brand. I read that twice.
- **Hugh Jackman credibility anchor** — for my cohort, Jackman's fitness discipline (Wolverine prep, documented caloric protocols) makes this more credible than any doctor endorsement. It's aspirational without being bro-y.
- **Three trust bullets** (NSF Certified for Sport®, industry-leading research, leading scientists and athletes) — these are the exact keywords I scan for when vetting a supplement. NSF certification is genuinely meaningful and not just marketing copy.
- **Green CTA button** — high contrast against the light background, impossible to miss, good size for a thumb tap on mobile.
- **Minimalist layout** — no clutter, no carousel wars, no pop-up assault. For a product I already take daily, this restraint feels premium.

## What's weak

- **Hero image is barely there** — the hero photo is so pale and overexposed it's almost an empty white rectangle with a ghost of a person. The Hugh Jackman visual — presumably the whole point of this campaign — is washed out to near-invisibility. If I didn't know who he was, I'd have no idea there was a person in that image.
- **Zero returning-customer surface** — no "Log In," no "My Subscription," no loyalty hook. As someone who auto-renews annually, the page is 100% acquisition-focused. I feel like a stranger at my own house.
- **No price signal anywhere** — AG1 is expensive. Not anchoring price or showing member/bundle pricing above the fold leaves new visitors guessing and existing subscribers with nothing to upgrade or upsell toward.
- **"Get Started →" CTA copy is weak for subscribers** — it implies I'm a newcomer. An existing customer sees this and feels no pull. A "Shop Travel Packs" or "Reorder" path would serve me far better.
- **Bottom lifestyle strip feels unresolved** — the row of small lifestyle images at the bottom of the viewport is thumbnailed and cramped. I can see people outdoors and active but can't parse what they're communicating at this size.

## Recommendations

- **Fix the hero image contrast immediately** — the Hugh Jackman photo is the entire creative investment of this campaign and it's invisible. Darken the overlay, boost his exposure, or crop to a close shot. This is a one-CSS-variable fix that unlocks the whole visual story.
- **Add a returning-customer CTA alongside "Get Started"** — a simple "Already a member? Log in →" secondary link or a "Reorder / Manage Subscription" ghost button costs nothing and stops alienating your highest-LTV customers.
- **Surface the price or a bundle hook above fold** — even "From $79/month · cancel anytime" or "Travel pack bundles available" gives high-intent visitors a reason to engage rather than bouncing to check if it's worth clicking through.
- **Swap the bottom lifestyle thumbnails for a single full-bleed editorial shot** — one strong image of someone mid-morning routine (blending, journaling, gym bag packed) would do more than four tiny crops at the bottom of the viewport.
