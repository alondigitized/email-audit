# Homepage snapshot · www.autozone.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I opened AutoZone's site on my phone and got a wall. Not a homepage — a bot-block. The AutoZone logo shows up at the top, and then it's just a white screen with "Access is temporarily restricted" in plain text and a diagnostic message telling me my IP got flagged. Whatever they're selling today, I have no idea — I never got past the front door.

## What's working

- The AutoZone logo renders correctly — at least I know I'm in the right place before the door slams.
- The "Submit feedback" link is functional — small mercy if I wanted to waste time reporting this.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is missing — a paying customer who just opened the app to grab a part before heading to the garage hits a dead end immediately.
- The error copy reads like a server log: "Automated (bot) activity on your network (IP 75.140.9.0)" — that's not customer-friendly language, that's IT documentation pasted on a public page.
- No retry button, no "tap here to continue," no CAPTCHA challenge — just a hard stop with a UUID reference ID that means nothing to me.
- The layout bottom-justifies the explanation text with a massive empty white void above it, which looks broken on mobile.
- Zero merchandising, zero brand personality — this experience communicates distrust to a loyal Rewards card holder who just wanted to check part availability.

## Recommendations

- Replace the hard block with a CAPTCHA or "tap to verify you're human" flow — I'm a real customer, give me a path forward instead of a door in the face.
- If a block page is unavoidable, brand it: keep the orange/red palette, add a short line like "Something looked off — let's get you back to your parts," and surface a direct link to the app or store locator.
- Surface a customer service phone number on the block page — if I can't browse, at least let me call and order the part I need today.
- Audit the bot-detection thresholds for mobile users; if normal phone browsing is tripping the IP flag, legitimate traffic is being blocked and that's lost revenue every day.
