# Homepage snapshot · www.autozone.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- I pulled up AutoZone and got a bot wall instead of a homepage — "Access is temporarily restricted" with a generic error page. That's the entire experience. The brand right now looks like it doesn't want my business, which is a rough first impression for a store I actually use regularly for parts and the Loan-A-Tool program.
- **1/10**

## What's working

- The AutoZone logo renders correctly at the top — at least I know I'm in the right place and didn't fat-finger a URL.
- The "Submit feedback" link exists, so there's technically a recovery path.

## What's weak

- The headline "Access is temporarily restricted" gives me nothing actionable — no ETA, no alternative (like "try the app"), no customer service number.
- The error message leads with bot-detection blame ("Automated bot activity from your IP 47.41.15.19") — feels accusatory toward a regular customer.
- The listed reasons (rapid taps, developer tools) read like a technical debug list that belongs in an internal log, not a customer-facing page.
- The entire viewport below the error text is dead white space — there's no fallback content, no search bar, nothing.
- The "Submit feedback" link is the only interactive element, and it does nothing useful for someone who just wants to check if my local store has rotors in stock.

## Recommendations

- Replace the bot-block page with a CAPTCHA or a simple "Tap to verify you're human" step so real customers pass through instead of hitting a dead end.
- Add a fallback strip with a phone number or "Open the app" CTA so I can still get what I came for even if the web flow is blocked.
- Drop the IP address from the customer-facing copy — showing "IP 47.41.15.19" means nothing to me and feels like surveillance theater.
- At minimum, soften the tone: "We're having trouble verifying your connection — try refreshing or visit our app" is better than "We detected unusual activity."
