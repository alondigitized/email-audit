# Homepage snapshot · www.autozone.com · 2026-05-31
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- I pulled up AutoZone on my phone and got a wall — not a homepage, not a sale, not a parts lookup. The site hit me with a bot-wall that says "Access is temporarily restricted" and lists my IP address like I'm some kind of suspect. This isn't a brand experience, it's a locked door. I've got my code reader result in hand ready to buy and I literally cannot get in.

## What's working

- **AutoZone logo** renders correctly at the top in the brand's recognizable red/orange — at least I know I'm in the right place.
- **"Submit feedback" link** is present and functional-looking — there is some path to report the issue.

## What's weak

- **"Access is temporarily restricted"** headline — this is what I see instead of a homepage. Zero commerce, zero parts, zero deals.
- **IP address callout ("IP 75.140.9.0")** — seeing my raw IP on screen feels clinical and borderline accusatory. Not a great look for a brand I've been shopping for a decade.
- **No recovery path** — there's no CAPTCHA, no "prove you're human" button, no retry option. Just a list of reasons why I might be a bot and a feedback link.
- **"JavaScript disabled or not working"** — I'm on my phone browser like a normal person. Being told my JS is broken when it isn't is confusing and friction-generating.
- **Massive dead white space** between the error heading and the footnote block — the layout is sparse and empty, which makes the failure feel even more stark.
- **No nav, no search bar, no category tiles** — I can't route around the block to find brake pads another way.

## Recommendations

- **Implement a CAPTCHA challenge instead of a hard block** — let real users solve a puzzle and continue. A dead-end with no retry is pure bounce.
- **Strip the IP address from the user-facing message** — showing "IP 75.140.9.0" to a customer is unnecessarily technical and feels aggressive. Swap it for a plain "we flagged your session" message.
- **Add a "Try again" button** — even a simple reload CTA gives the user something to do besides leave.
- **Provide a direct link to the store locator or customer service number** — if the site can't serve me, at least hand me a lifeline to get my parts another way.
