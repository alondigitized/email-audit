# Homepage snapshot · www.autozone.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- I pulled up AutoZone on my phone expecting to check if they had the brake pads I need in stock, and instead I got a white wall telling me I'm locked out. The page shows nothing but an "Access is temporarily restricted" block screen — logo, an error message, and a bot-detection explainer. There's no homepage here to review, just a security gate that treated me like a crawler instead of a customer.

## What's working

- The AutoZone logo renders cleanly at the top — I at least know I'm in the right place before the disappointment hits.
- The "Submit feedback" link is present, so there's technically a path to escalate the problem.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage failed to load — I got a bot-detection block instead of product content, navigation, or any shopping experience.
- The error copy ("Rapid taps or clicks," "JavaScript disabled," "Automated bot activity on your network") reads like I did something wrong. I just opened the URL on my phone.
- My IP address (47.41.15.19) is printed in plain text on the screen — unnecessary and slightly creepy for a regular customer.
- There are no recovery options: no CAPTCHA, no retry button, no "Continue anyway" — just a dead end with a feedback link.
- A massive empty white void fills the middle of the screen between the header message and the footer explainer, making the experience feel broken and abandoned.

## Recommendations

- Add a CAPTCHA or "I'm not a robot" flow so legitimate mobile users can immediately prove themselves and continue shopping — right now there's no self-serve recovery.
- Replace the raw IP exposure with a generic "your connection was flagged" message — printing my IP is unnecessary and feels invasive.
- If the block is going to stay, show at least a store-locator link or a phone number so I can still get what I came for without needing to reload.
- Tune the bot-detection threshold for mobile browser user agents — a customer opening the app store listing and then tapping to the website shouldn't look like a crawler.
