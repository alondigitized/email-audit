# New markdowns are already selling fast, don’t miss out!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's post-Memorial Day gear-change email — the first send after a five-day escalation sequence that peaked at two same-day blasts on May 26 topping out at 60% off and Double Rewards. The brand is making a smart pivot from "sale ending" urgency to a "new markdowns" discovery frame, essentially re-opening the clearance narrative with fresh inventory language. The 60% off hero carry-over from yesterday maintains continuity. But the Double Rewards loyalty beat — the hook hammered in every single Memorial Day email since at least May 23 — is gone without explanation, and that's the biggest miss here.
- TheDesignDesk section adds welcome brand depth, and the "we love these for you" personalized row shows real segmentation work. But the subject line is the weakest in the entire Memorial Day sequence: "New markdowns are already selling fast, don't miss out!" is nothing but soft urgency and zero specificity — a marked regression from the crisp percentage-off subjects that drove clicks all week.
- Competent cadence reset, but it drops its best tool (loyalty) and leads with the emptiest urgency language in the brand's recent playbook.

## What's working

- **Hero preserves the 60% off clearance headline** — continuity from the Memorial Day peak keeps the value visible without resetting to zero.
- **TheDesignDesk section elevates the brand** — the room-design lifestyle image repositions Crate & Barrel as a design partner, not just a clearance rack, which is useful the day after a sale hammering.
- **"We love these for you" personalized row** — individual product tiles signal segmentation and give a concrete reason to scroll beyond the hero.
- **Clean, structured render** — layout holds, images load, hierarchy flows from hero to product grid to secondary sections with no visible bugs.
- **Cadence reset is the right call** — not sending a fifth "FINAL HOURS" variant on May 27 was correct; subscribers needed a new narrative.

## What's weak

- **Double Rewards / loyalty hook completely dropped** — the brand trained subscribers to expect 2x Rewards in every send since May 23; removing it without explanation the day after is a quiet trust ding for members and kills the "act today" lever.
- **Subject line has no specificity** — "new markdowns" names no category, no percent, no product. The May 26 subjects ("Up to 60% off + earn Double Rewards") were more informative in fewer words.
- **"Don't miss out" is empty urgency** — classic filler that reads as desperation rather than a real scarcity signal; it's the weakest closer in the brand's recent toolkit.
- **No date-bound deadline anywhere** — "selling fast" is passive scarcity. A concrete anchor ("through Sunday," "this week only") would convert browse intent into same-session clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the loyalty hook** — a single line ("Keep earning Rewards on clearance") reactivates the habituated behavior and differentiates acting today vs. next week.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject with a number and a category** — subscribers have been conditioned to respond to percent-off signals all week; don't break the frame now.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even "New clearance drops through Sunday" converts "selling fast" from vague into actionable urgency.
- 4. **Give TheDesignDesk a specific CTA** — "Get a free consultation" or "Shop our designer picks" beats a generic button and makes the section worth a click.
- 5. **Name the category in the hero** — "New furniture + dining markdowns added" beats "new markdowns" for anyone scanning on mobile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off clearance — new furniture & dining just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New markdowns up to 60% off — rewards still stack`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh clearance drops through Sunday — furniture, dining & more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Just added to clearance: up to 60% off + keep earning Rewards`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `img { max-width }` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640 px rule appears after the 768 px rule. At viewport widths 640–767 px, only the 640 px rule fires (correct). At 768 px+, both rules fire and the 640 px rule wins via cascade order, overriding the intended 600 px desktop cap. Swap the declaration order so the 768 px rule is last.

**`* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none }` applied globally**
The universal selector disables font scaling across all elements, which can break user accessibility preferences. Scope this to `body` or specific containers rather than `*`.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` each appear twice (once in a combined tag, once separately). Redundant but non-breaking; clean up for hygiene.

**No dark mode support**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode is explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Gmail will force-invert, potentially breaking white text on colored backgrounds. Not a defect per se, but a known rendering risk if the design relies on white-on-white image masking.
