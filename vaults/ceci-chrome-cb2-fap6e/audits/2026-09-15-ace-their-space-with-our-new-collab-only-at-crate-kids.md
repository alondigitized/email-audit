# Ace their space with our new collab, only at Crate & Kids 🎾
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' fifth Venus Williams-adjacent send in the recent run, and it finally gives the collab a genuine angle: tennis. The green/gold court palette, the racket-bristle hero texture, an "ACE" throw pillow, and a "championship-worthy decor" section built around tennis balls as decor accents all read as a real creative concept rather than a slapped-on celebrity name — a step up from the "Early on our app" send (2026-09-14, 4.00/10) that just crammed the same collab into a phone mockup.
- But the email is enormous — bedroom, playroom, study, decor, then a full Venus Williams x Crate&Barrel cross-brand pitch, then a Design Desk pitch, then a "we love these for you" carousel, then a credit-card footer offer. It's five emails stitched into one scroll, and by the time a reader hits the actual card offer at the bottom, the tennis hook from the subject line is long forgotten.
- There's no visible price, discount, or concrete offer anywhere in the body — just repeated "Shop the New Collab" buttons across six room vignettes. For a subscriber who's now seen this collab four times this week, a fifth send needs either a genuine price hook or genuine novelty to earn the open; this has neither.

## What's working

- The tennis motif (racket texture in the hero, "ACE" pillow, tennis-ball accents in the "championship-worthy decor" section) gives the collab a coherent story instead of just a celebrity name.
- Consistent green/cream/tennis-yellow palette carries through every section, so the email reads as one visual system.
- Repeated "Shop the New Collab" CTA buttons are clearly styled and easy to spot on every scroll stop.
- Room-by-room structure (bedroom, playroom, study) gives shoppers multiple entry points based on what they're actually furnishing.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere — no %, no price, no "starting at $X" — just lifestyle copy across the entire email.
- The email is excessively long, bundling the core collab pitch with an unrelated Venus Williams x Crate&Barrel plug, a Design Desk pitch, and a credit card offer, diluting the single message the subject promised.
- This is the brand's fifth Venus Williams-themed send in under a week (following 2026-09-14's "Early on our app," "Shop it first," and 2026-09-15's Crate & Barrel Venus send) — fatigue risk is high and this email does nothing to signal why this one is different upfront.
- Product callouts ("Team Blue Twin Sheet Set," etc.) are in tiny, low-contrast text that's hard to read at this render size, undermining the product-detail signal shoppers need.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email by two-thirds — lead with the tennis collab (hero + one or two rooms + the "ACE"/tennis-ball decor beat), drop the cross-brand Venus/Crate&Barrel plug, Design Desk pitch, and card offer into separate sends.
- 2. Add a real offer line under the hero — even "New arrivals, starting at $29" gives the eye something concrete to act on.
- 3. Differentiate this subject from the prior four Venus sends by naming the tennis concept explicitly, not just "collab."
- 4. Move the "championship-worthy decor" (tennis-ball accent) section higher — it's the most distinctive, on-brand-for-a-tennis-collab visual in the email and it's buried mid-scroll.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rackets, stripes, and championship style for their room`
