# UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's classic clearance-urgency play — "ENDS TODAY" in the subject, two stacked sale banners in the body, a personalized product grid at the bottom. It's competent execution of a format CB2 uses often, and it's not a bad email — but after the genuinely editorial "THINGS OF QUALITY" and "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US" sends from the past two days, reverting to an all-caps clearance blast feels like a step backward. The brand was building momentum toward an elevated editorial voice; this undercuts it.
- The bigger structural problem is the subject-body discount mismatch: the subject says "UP TO 40% OFF" but the hero clearance block shows up to 60% off. That's actually a positive surprise for anyone who opens, but it's a missed opportunity in the inbox — "UP TO 60% OFF" would have been a stronger hook without lying.
- Two competing sale sections (Summer Edit Clearance + Outdoor Collection) dilute focus. Pick one hero offer, commit hard, let the other be secondary. As-is, the email tries to be two things and is fully neither.

## What's working

- The hero lifestyle photo is warm and on-brand — unmistakably CB2, the room composition earns a second look.
- The 60% clearance number in the body is genuinely compelling and over-delivers on the subject's 40% promise — positive surprise is good.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid with visible prices at the bottom is a smart engagement layer; it gives the browser something specific to click.
- "ENDS TODAY" urgency is clean and credible — no fake countdown timer, no asterisk-hedged language visible.
- No render bugs, clean two-column layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- The subject understates the offer by 20 percentage points — "UP TO 40% OFF" when the body shows 60% leaves open rate on the table.
- Two competing hero sale sections (Clearance + Outdoor Collection) with separate CTAs splits the eye and the click — no single priority emerges.
- "SHOP THE EDIT" and "SHOP THE SALE" are weak CTA labels — they land on browse pages, not targeted product moments.
- ALL CAPS subject triggers mild spam-filter suspicion and inbox fatigue for subscribers who've seen CB2 use this pattern heavily.
- After the editorial reset of the past two sends, landing back on a clearance-blast format creates cadence whiplash — it erases the brand elevation work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject discount figure.** The body says 60% — lead with that. "UP TO 60% OFF ENDS TODAY" is a factually stronger hook and won't feel like a bait-and-switch for scanners who notice the mismatch.
- 2. **Pick one hero section.** Drop the Outdoor Collection banner from above-the-fold. Let Clearance own the hero. Outdoor can be a mid-email module or a separate send.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTAs.** "SHOP THE EDIT" → "Shop Summer Clearance" or "See All Clearance Items." Give people a destination, not a vibe.
- 4. **Sentence-case the subject.** "Up to 60% off ends today" reads as confident editorial, not shouty mass email. CB2's editorial voice is lowercase and deliberate — match that.
- 5. **Add a specific time to the urgency.** "ENDS TODAY" is fine; "Ends Tonight at Midnight" is better — it makes the countdown feel real rather than another soft "today."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Summer Edit is on final sale — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, lighting, rugs: up to 60% off through midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary
Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with VML/MSO conditional blocks. Several infrastructure issues found: developer artifacts left in production HTML, malformed font URLs, and a cross-brand asset dependency. Compliance and UTM sections are partially assessable due to truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Source truncated** — full link inventory and UTM parameter audit cannot be completed. Audit must be re-run against the full HTML.
- **Cross-brand font CDN dependency**: Web fonts are loaded from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (4 `@font-face` declarations using `HNW05`). CB2 email rendering depends on a sibling-brand domain. If that domain has a CSP change, CDN issue, or is ever deprecated, CB2 emails silently fall back to system fonts with no alert.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Developer comment left in production** (line in `<head>`):
  ```html
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Typo ("IMPUT") and internal authoring note shipped to end recipients. Should be removed before send.

- **Version tag in production**:
  ```html
  <!--V2-->
  ```
  Internal template versioning artifact exposed in live HTML.

- **Malformed font URLs — double forward slash**:
  ```
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
  https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
  ```
  Three `@font-face` declarations use `//` after the domain. Most servers normalize this, but it is a defect and can fail on strict origin validation or certain proxies.

- **`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```
  Prevents low-vision users from zooming. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

- **Global text-size-adjust suppression**:
  ```css
  * { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
  ```
  Applied to every element, not just `body`. This overrides browser accessibility settings globally and conflicts with the `body`-level declaration also present in the stylesheet.

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint with hard-coded width**:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
    .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
  }
  ```
  418px breakpoint is non-standard. The inner `width:414px !important` hard-codes a pixel width inside a fluid media query context, which will overflow a 375px-wide viewport (iPhone SE/standard).
