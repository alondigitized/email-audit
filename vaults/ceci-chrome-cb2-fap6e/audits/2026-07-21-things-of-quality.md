# THINGS OF QUALITY
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- "Things of Quality" is CB2 in full editorial mode — a summer furniture showcase anchored by a soft curved armchair hero, followed by a cascade of lifestyle vignettes (a cognac leather sofa, a low slung coffee table with plants) and a $5,000 sweepstakes section before landing on a "Made for You" product grid. The positioning is confident and on-brand: CB2 is staking out quality craftsmanship as its summer editorial identity, not discounting its way through July.
- The problem is cadence and subject vagueness in combination. After "Add these to your art collection" on 7/20 — another editorial send with no sale — subscribers are now two consecutive days into aspirational-but-no-offer territory. "THINGS OF QUALITY" in the subject line tells me almost nothing actionable: quality of what, at what price, for how long? The hero delivers on that promise beautifully once you're inside, but the subject line doesn't earn the open.
- The sweepstakes section ($5,000 Refresh) is buried mid-scroll and feels bolted on, same as the "$1K Could Be Yours" app-download send on 7/19. CB2 keeps reaching for sweepstakes as an engagement lever without integrating it into the email's editorial story.

## What's working

- Hero image and headline pairing are excellent — the curved armchair reads as aspirational but attainable, "Summer Edit / Things of Quality" sets a confident editorial tone without overselling.
- Lifestyle photography across the scroll is cohesive: warm neutrals, cognac leather, low-slung modernism — the aesthetic voice is fully consistent.
- "Made for You" personalized product grid at the bottom is the right close for an editorial send; it converts browsers into potential buyers without disrupting the upper editorial feel.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, visual hierarchy holds at each section.
- "SHOP FURNITURE" CTA is unambiguous and verb-forward.

## What's weak

- Subject "THINGS OF QUALITY" is vague to the point of near-meaninglessness in the inbox — no product category, no offer, no urgency.
- The sweepstakes ($5,000 Refresh) is mid-scroll and disconnected from the editorial narrative — it feels copy-pasted from the 7/19 app-download send.
- Two editorial sends in two days with no purchase incentive strains the cadence; subscribers who just saw "Add these to your art collection" yesterday have no new reason to click through today.
- No pricing, no inventory signal, no "new arrivals" count — the editorial positioning needs at least one concrete hook to convert.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the subject to a specific product or category** — "THINGS OF QUALITY" could be any brand; ground it in furniture, or a material, or a room. Something a subscriber can picture.
- 2. **Integrate the sweepstakes into the editorial story** — if you're running a $5,000 Refresh giveaway, make the hero about the refresh: "Win a $5K Room Refresh. Shop the Edit." That ties the contest to a purchase action and makes both elements stronger.
- 3. **Add one concrete incentive** — free shipping on furniture, a limited-time category offer, or a "New This Week" count. Editorial emails need a hook for subscribers who won't click for atmosphere alone.
- 4. **Space the editorial sends** — two aspirational-no-offer sends in 48 hours dilutes both. Let yesterday's art collection email breathe before hitting the next editorial beat.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom — fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `line-height: 100%` on universal selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px;
   mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` globally via `*` clips descenders and causes text overlap on multi-line elements in several clients (Yahoo Mail, older Android). Should be scoped to specific elements, not the wildcard.

**[MEDIUM] `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector**
The global `none` value (vs `100%`) suppresses automatic text scaling on iOS. Combined with the `maximum-scale=1` lock above, small type becomes unreadable on mobile without any fallback.

**[LOW] Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Breakpoint fires at 418px but sets `width: 414px` — targets iPhone 6/7/8 native resolution but misses the 415–418px range it claims to cover. Common email breakpoints are 480px or 600px; this custom value risks leaving some viewports in an intermediate broken state.

**[LOW] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
The family is declared three times with overlapping `src` URLs — once without weight/style descriptors, and twice with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The unweighted declaration will override browser matching for intermediate weights. Consolidate into a single block per weight variant.
