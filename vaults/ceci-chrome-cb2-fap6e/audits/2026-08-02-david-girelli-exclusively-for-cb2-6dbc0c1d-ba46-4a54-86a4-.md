# David Girelli, exclusively for CB2
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 running its "designer spotlight" playbook — the same format that scored 8/10 on 2026-07-30 ("This home office is a study in creativity") — but stretched thinner. Where 07-30 committed to one designer's story, this send opens on David Girelli (the subject line's whole promise), gives him three products, then pivots into an entirely separate "MORE FROM OUR DESIGNERS" block featuring three other, unrelated names (Studio Anansi, Dach & Zephir, Ross Cassidy) before finally landing on the actual money offer — 30% off bedroom + bath — nearly 8,500 pixels down.
- The craft is there: clean portrait photography, consistent serif/caps typography, named products, no render bugs anywhere in a 12,000px scroll. But the email doesn't know what it's selling. It spends the top half on editorial storytelling that the subject line promised was about one designer, then buries the discount, the sweepstakes, and the loyalty perks in a long tail most subscribers won't reach. The fine print confirms the 30% off "Ends August 5" — real, near-term urgency — but that deadline never appears anywhere in the actual creative, only in 8pt legal copy at the bottom.
- Good editorial instincts, weak commercial execution. This is a 07-30/08-01 hybrid that forgot to keep the hybrid balanced — call it a 6.5-7 relative to a brand history that's shown it can do focused editorial (8/10) and focused urgency (9/10) separately.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render across the full scroll — no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent typography.
- The David Girelli hero (portrait against a sketch-collage backdrop) is a strong, distinctive visual open that differentiates from a generic product-grid email.
- Named products throughout (Aldine Sofa, Aldine Chair, Turno Sconce, Davison Console, Lecko Glass Pendant) with designer attribution reinforces CB2's "shoppable editorial" identity, consistent with the 08-01 send.
- "More For You" grid at the bottom shows real struck-through pricing ($999→$849, $399→$349), giving late scrollers a concrete reason to click.
- Footer loyalty stack (CB2 credit card 10% back, app perks, SMS 15% off) is genuinely useful and visible for members.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "David Girelli, exclusively for CB2," but he owns only the first third of the email — three more designers get equal-weight spotlights afterward, diluting the promised focus.
- The actual discount (30% off bedroom + bath) doesn't appear until roughly 70% of the way down the email — zero pricing or offer signal above the fold.
- The sale's real deadline ("Ends August 5" — today is 2026-08-04, so this is genuinely urgent) is buried only in bottom legal fine print, never stated in the banner itself ("UP TO 30% OFF" reads open-ended).
- Too many competing CTAs of equal visual weight (Shop the Collection ×2, Meet the Designers, Shop Now, Shop the Sale, Tell Us Everything) — no single primary action.
- No product detail (dimensions, materials, availability) or review/star ratings anywhere, despite an entire banner asking recipients to leave reviews.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** The Girelli editorial and the bedroom+bath 30% off are both strong ideas fighting for the same slot — CB2's own history shows pure-editorial (8/10 on 07-30, 07-31) and pure-urgency (9/10 on 07-28, 07-29) sends outperform hybrids that bury one under the other.
- 2. **Surface the real deadline.** "Ends August 5" is sitting in fine print — move it into the 30% off banner itself ("Up to 30% off — ends 8/5") to convert the fine-print urgency into a visible one.
- 3. **Cut the "More From Our Designers" block from this send**, or move it to a separate email. If the subject is about one designer, the body should stay about that one designer through the first CTA.
- 4. **Pick one primary CTA above the fold.** Let "Shop the Collection" (Girelli's pieces) be the singular action in the top half; save "Shop Now"/"Shop the Sale" for the bottom, where the offer content actually lives.
- 5. **Add a line of product substance** (dimensions or material) under at least the hero product — right now every product is a name and a designer credit, nothing tactile.

## Technical Audit: CB2 — "David Girelli, exclusively for CB2"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO/Outlook-hardened HTML template (VML namespaces, conditional comments, `mso-*` fixes) served via Bronto/Message Systems-style infrastructure (`mail.cb2.com`, `image.mail.cb2.com`, `mi.cb2.com`). Automated QA shows a 57% pass rate driven by missing anti-spam authentication headers, absent alt text on all content images, and legacy non-HTTPS ad-tracking pixels.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 72 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (opaque redirect domains) — link destinations were not independently verified and should be manually checked before send.
- Multiple third-party ad-tracking pixels load over plain `http://` instead of `https://`:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&dtm_cmagic=654788&dtm_fid=103&dtm_format=6&cli_promo_id`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php` (10 sequential beacons)
  - Impact: Many mail clients (Outlook, Gmail proxy, iOS Mail) block or strip mixed-content/non-HTTPS image requests, meaning these Dotomi retargeting pixels may silently fail to fire for a meaningful share of opens.
- First-party tracking pixels (`https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `https://dv.cb2.com/o/...`) correctly use HTTPS.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 11 content images are missing `alt` attributes, including all primary product/lifestyle imagery served from `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/...` (11 distinct asset IDs). With images blocked by default (the common default in Outlook/Gmail), recipients get no fallback text describing the offer.
- The Dotomi tracking pixels also lack `alt` text, which is expected/acceptable for 1x1 beacons but is flagged generically by the scanner.
- Template includes standard MSO conditional blocks and `-webkit-text-size-adjust` / `-ms-text-size-adjust` resets, indicating deliberate Outlook/mobile rendering hardening — no rendering-breaking issues detected in the visible source.
- Leftover build artifact in `<head>`: `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` — a placeholder comment (with a typo, "IMPUT") left in from the template scaffold; not customer-visible but indicates the font-import step may not have been consciously completed/removed.
