# STEALS OF THE WEEK
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's roll-up format: four discount tiers stacked (Furniture 40%, Bedroom & Bath 30%, Outdoor 50%, Clearance 60%) instead of one clear hero offer. It's the same architecture as the 2026-07-24 "UP TO 30% OFF BEDROOM & BATH" send and the 2026-07-24 "AVAILABLE AGAIN" email — CB2 keeps reusing this exact multi-category tile stack with new photography dropped in. The visuals are clean and on-brand (moody furniture photography, consistent typography, no render bugs), but there's no single reason to click — it's a discount menu, not a story.
- "STEALS OF THE WEEK" as a subject is generic bargain-bin language that doesn't match the aspirational, editorial photography CB2 is known for — it reads more like an outlet-store blast than a design brand. Compare to the 2026-07-25 "The most important room in the house" send, which scored well specifically because it led with a curiosity hook tied to a category story. This one skips that entirely and goes straight to percentage-off tiles.
- Judgment: competently produced, zero creative risk, cadence-fatiguing. It's the same sale-anchor playbook CB2 has run at least four times in the last week under different names.

## What's working

- Four distinct discount tiers are legible at a glance — no ambiguity about what's on sale.
- Photography is consistent, moody, and on-brand across all four category shots.
- Clearance at 60% off is a genuinely strong number that should be leading, not buried last.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid at the bottom gives a secondary, product-specific path to purchase.
- No layout bugs, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- Subject "STEALS OF THE WEEK" is generic and undercuts CB2's design-forward brand voice — it's interchangeable with any discount retailer.
- No single hero offer — four percentage tiles compete for attention with zero visual hierarchy pointing to the best deal (60% clearance is stacked last, easy to miss on a quick scroll).
- Every CTA under each tile is presumably identical/generic (visually there's no distinct button text per section, just the banner) — no differentiation in urgency or copy between "40% off furniture" and "60% off clearance."
- Near-identical structural template to the 2026-07-24 Bedroom & Bath and "AVAILABLE AGAIN" sends — a subscriber who reads a few CB2 emails a week will recognize this shell instantly and disengage.
- No deadline or urgency signal anywhere in the visible body — "steals of the week" implies a time window but nothing states when it ends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Clearance 60% off as the hero**, not the fourth tile — it's the strongest number in the email and currently gets the least visual weight (buried at the bottom of the stack).
- 2. **Add a real deadline** ("Ends Sunday," "3 days left") near the top — "steals of the week" implies urgency the design never delivers.
- 3. **Cut to two tiers max** (Clearance + one category) instead of four — a single strong offer converts better than a menu, and it breaks the sameness with recent sends.
- 4. **Differentiate CTA copy per section** (e.g., "Shop Clearance" vs. "Shop Outdoor") instead of a repeated generic button, so each tile reads as its own decision point.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off clearance — this week only`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `4 sales, one email: up to 60% off`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance just dropped to 60% off. Plus furniture, bedroom, and outdoor markdowns.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Your biggest markdowns of the month, all in one place.`
