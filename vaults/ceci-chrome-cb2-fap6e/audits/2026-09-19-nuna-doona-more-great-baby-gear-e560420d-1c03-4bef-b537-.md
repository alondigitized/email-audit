# Nuna, Doona & more great baby gear
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running its now-familiar room-by-room catalog format, but pointed at baby gear registry categories instead of furniture — strollers, car seats, high chairs, bassinets, then a brand-logo wall for Nuna, BabyBjörn, Stokke, and Doona. It's tightly organized and the subject line ("Nuna, Doona & more great baby gear") actually matches what's inside, which isn't a given for this brand based on prior sends like the 09-18 Ninja "SO fun" overreach.
- The problem is pacing, not content: this is one of the longest catalog dumps in the recent history for this brand, with five nearly-identical "category + 3 SKUs + Shop All" modules before the sale banner even shows up. The 20%-off "limited time only" offer — the single best conversion lever in the whole email — is buried past the fold, after the brand wall and a stylist/Design Desk pitch. That's a sequencing miss on a genuinely usable discount.
- Solid, on-brand execution with good product photography throughout, but this needed either a hero-level callout of the sale or a trim of one or two category modules to get the reader to the offer faster.

## What's working

- Subject-to-content match is strong — Nuna and Doona are both named and both appear in the "quality brands" module, avoiding the overselling seen in the 09-18 Ninja send.
- Clear category segmentation (strollers → car seats → high chairs → on-the-go gear) mirrors real registry-building order, which is intuitive for the target shopper.
- Lifestyle photography is consistent and warm (parent-with-stroller, nursery scenes) — no render bugs, broken images, or placeholder text visible anywhere in the scroll.
- "The quality brands you love" module is a smart trust-building beat, name-checking Nuna, BabyBjörn, Stokke, Doona in one glance.

## What's weak

- The actual offer — "up to 20% off best baby essentials, limited time only" — doesn't appear until roughly 80% down the email, well past where most readers stop scrolling.
- Five back-to-back "category + product row + Shop All" modules before any differentiation or offer creates serious scroll fatigue.
- No urgency detail on the sale (no end date, no countdown) despite "limited time only" framing — reads as generic markdown language rather than a credible deadline.
- The Design Desk / stylist consultation module interrupts the shopping flow right before the sale banner, pushing the offer even further down.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20%-off sale banner to directly under the hero, or add a hero sub-line calling it out — this is the strongest CTA in the email and it's currently the least visible.
- 2. Cut two of the five category modules (e.g., fold high chairs and on-the-go gear into a single "nursery essentials" module) to shorten the scroll before the offer.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the sale copy ("ends Sunday 9/28") instead of "limited time only," which reads as stock copy.
- 4. Move the Design Desk stylist module to the very end, after the sale — it's a nice-to-have, not a purchase driver.
