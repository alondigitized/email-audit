# STARTS NOW: Up to 20% off our Back-to-School Sale!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids launching its broader Back-to-School sale — a natural sequel to yesterday's single-category desk flash sale that "ENDS TODAY." The hero delivers exactly what the subject promises: a colorful, lifestyle-forward flat-lay of backpacks and school supplies anchored by a clear 20% off offer. The timing is pitch-perfect; late July is peak back-to-school shopping mindset, and the "STARTS NOW" framing creates a clean narrative arc from yesterday's "ENDS TODAY."
- The problem is below the fold. What should be a focused sale-launch email becomes a kitchen-sink send: the $1,000 review sweepstakes module (which already appeared in the July 21 email), a "Just Dropped" new arrivals section, an app download promo, and a designer collaboration — all stacked on top of each other. The hero gets the brief right; the body loses the thread.
- Net judgement: a good subject line and a great hero attached to an overstuffed email. The sale launch would hit harder if it had a visible end date and half the modules cut.

## What's working

- **Hero image is on-brief.** Colorful backpacks and school supplies in a flat-lay composition — seasonal, visually energetic, exactly what a back-to-school sale should lead with.
- **"STARTS NOW" is smart sequencing.** As a direct callback to yesterday's "ENDS TODAY: Up to 20% off ALL kids desks," it signals the campaign is graduating to a broader event, not repeating the same email.
- **Offer clarity.** "Up to 20% off" appears in both subject and hero copy with no ambiguity about what's discounted.
- **Back-to-school timing is correct.** Late July is when the research and purchase intent peaks; the brand isn't late to this party.
- **Product mix is visible and relevant.** Backpacks, lunch kits, and storage products are all shown in the grid — the range sells the breadth of the sale.

## What's weak

- **No sale end date anywhere in the visible email.** "STARTS NOW" tells me when it opens; nothing tells me when it closes. That's a conversion killer — urgency requires a deadline.
- **The $1,000 sweepstakes module reappears.** It was in the July 21 send. Running it again inside a sale-launch email dilutes both messages; the sweepstakes now reads as a standing fixture, not a genuine incentive.
- **Email is too long, too many sections.** Sale grid → sweepstakes → new arrivals → app promo → designer partnership is five competing asks. The sale drowns in its own email.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is generic.** Given that backpacks are the clear hero product, "Shop Backpacks" or "Shop the Sale" would pull harder than a catch-all verb.
- **Offer overlap ambiguity with 7/22.** A subscriber who bought into yesterday's "20% off ALL kids desks" sale has no signal whether desks are still included, newly excluded, or repriced.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard sale end date** — in the hero, in the CTA button label, or both. "Sale ends Sunday" outperforms "STARTS NOW" because it creates a closing window, not just an opening one. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Cut the sweepstakes module.** Move it to its own dedicated send or to the footer. Inside a sale launch it competes for the click; two weeks in a row it reads like wallpaper.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** Change "Shop Now" to "Shop the Sale →" or "Shop Backpacks & More" — anything with a noun the reader is actually shopping for.
- 4. **Add a one-line callout on the desk offer.** "Desks still included — yesterday's deal extends to the full BTS collection" resolves the overlap confusion and rewards repeat openers.
- 5. **Trim to three sections max**: hero + primary product grid + one supporting module (app or new arrivals, not both).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-School Sale starts today — 20% off everything`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your back-to-school checklist, 20% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, lunch kits, desks, and more — sale ends [day].`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before school starts. 20% off the full collection through Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A. Brand/title mismatch**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Email sender is "Crate & Kids SALE" but `<title>` reads "Crate & Barrel" — wrong brand. Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>` value.

**B. User zoom disabled**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure for mobile users.

**C. Text size adjustment globally suppressed**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied to all elements (`*` selector). Overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling.

**D. Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both rules fire above 768px; declaration order means `max-width: 640px` (the 640px query, declared last) wins on wide viewports — likely unintended since the 768px rule targets a narrower `600px` cap.

**E. No dark mode handling**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Dark mode explicitly disabled. Apple Mail and Gmail app (Android) users in dark mode will get forced light rendering. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override is present.

**F. Duplicate/redundant meta tags**
- `charset` declared twice (`http-equiv` + standalone `<meta charset>`)
- `format-detection` declared four times with overlapping attribute pairs (telephone, address, email, date, url — split across multiple tags)

These are harmless to rendering but indicate sloppy template cleanup.
