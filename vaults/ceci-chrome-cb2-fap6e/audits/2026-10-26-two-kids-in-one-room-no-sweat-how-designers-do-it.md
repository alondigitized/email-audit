# Two kids in one room? No sweat! How designers do it →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a design-advice send built around a shared kids' room guide from "The Design Desk." The subject promises "Two kids in one room? No sweat! How designers do it," and the body follows through. The hero is a bunk-bed room, and the "our shared room guide" section is split into three tips: "balance it out," "go vertical" and "keep color cohesive." Each tip has product tiles and a shop button. It's useful and on-topic, and the subject and body agree, which wasn't true of the 10/24 "Holiday decor starting at $2.95" send.
- The email is also very long, and it dilutes itself. After the guide it adds a second free-design-appointment block, a "free shipping on tops $99+" banner (already in the header), "up to 60% off clearance," a product carousel, a credit card promo ("new! the card you love just got better"), and an app download pitch with a $500 chance. That is six or seven competing asks for a reader who came for shared-room ideas. The editorial section is strong. The commerce section under it is generic and the offers contradict each other.
- Judgement: a solid, relevant content send that is undermined by the pile of promotions at the bottom. It's a 6, in line with the other editorial sends this week (the 10/24 Venus Williams tour and the 10/25 recipe email).
- Caveat: the render was only available at about 126px wide, so I could read headings and layout but not product names, prices or fine print. I haven't commented on anything I couldn't read.

## What's working

- The subject and hero match. A bunk-bed room opens a shared-room guide, so there's no bait-and-switch.
- The three-part structure ("balance it out," "go vertical," "keep color cohesive") is easy to scan, and each tip has its own product row and CTA.
- The lifestyle photography is large and consistent, with muted, cohesive rooms that suit the "designer advice" angle.
- The free design appointment CTA fits the shared-room problem well, since it's a service for people who need layout help.
- The header's "free shipping on tops $99+" gives a concrete, honest offer without taking over the hero.

## What's weak

- Too many competing modules after the guide: a second appointment block, clearance, product picks, the credit card, and the app. The focus is lost.
- "Free shipping on tops $99+" appears twice, in the header and again as a mid-email banner. That is redundant.
- "Up to 60% off clearance" sits in an unrelated autumn-leaf image and has no connection to shared rooms. It reads as a template block.
- The "we love these for you" product carousel looks like generic recommendations rather than shared-room items. It breaks the story the email built.
- The email is extremely long, and a lot of dense legal text fills the footer. On mobile the guide's payoff is far from the top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut everything after the third tip, or reduce it to one module: a "Shop shared rooms" bundle (bunk beds, trundles, dressers) with a single clear CTA. This is the biggest lift.
- 2. Put one product or price point in the hero, such as the featured bunk bed with a "shop bunk beds" button, so the click doesn't depend on scrolling.
- 3. Remove the duplicate free-shipping banner and move the clearance, credit card and app content to their own sends.
- 4. Give each tip a named product and a price or benefit line so the tiles do more than decorate.
- 5. Add social proof next to the product rows, such as ratings or "most-loved by parents of two."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Two kids, one room: 3 designer tricks that work`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sharing a room? Bunk beds, color, and layout tips`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Go vertical, balance the room, keep colors cohesive. Plus a free design appointment.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our designers' favorite bunks, beds and storage for small shared spaces.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO/VML fallbacks and a 460px mobile breakpoint. The main confirmed problems are about 50 images flagged without alt text and a viewport tag that blocks zoom. The header checks (unsubscribe, SPF/DKIM) are inconclusive because of the relay, and 91 tracking links went unprobed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 91 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none were resolved. Destination status, redirect chains and UTM handling are unknown.
- **Per-recipient identifiers in image URLs:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=56aaa2787a67…` and `…/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` carry a recipient hash. `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/7ad65760-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` is another open/view tracker.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa2787a67…&n=1..5` calls go to a LiveRamp domain. The `s=` value starts with the same hash as `mi_u`, so the recipient identifier appears to be shared with a third party. This is observable in the source. The purpose isn't stated.
- **Reliability:** the `rlcdn.com` and `mi.` pixels are external dependencies. If blocked, nothing visible breaks, but they add 7+ extra requests.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, ~50 images).** About 36 content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg`, plus `m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`, are flagged. So are:
  - `s7d5.scene7.com/.../2026%5F0926%5FCBK%5FClearance%5FSecondaryStory.gif`
  - `…/971aa10f-db11-4b79-b1d6-d95ad4507a7c.jpg`
  - The check doesn't say whether the `alt` attribute is absent or empty. If the images are linked, a screen reader or image-blocked client gets no usable link name.
- **Decorative and tracking images:** the spacer `042025%5FCBK%5FKidsBeds1%5FRule%5FSpacer%5F40px`, the `rlcdn` and `mi.` pixels and `o.gif` should carry explicit `alt=""`. This is low severity.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` stops pinch-zoom on some clients (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Leftover template placeholder:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is left in the head.
- **Redundant meta tags:** `format-detection` is declared four times (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then separate `date`, `address` and `telephone` tags).
- **Title:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, and the title doesn't match the Kids sender or subject.
- **Dead CSS:** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is overridden by the later `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`, since both apply at ≥768px with equal specificity.
- **Truncated source:** the HTML was cut off, so the body structure (headings, link text, table roles) couldn't be reviewed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the visible source. The only per-recipient values are the `mi_u`/`mi_cid`/`mi_mid` tracking parameters.

## 5. Compliance
- **Inconclusive, not confirmed defects.** `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) and `Authentication-Results` were not found. The QA output attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly stripping or not capturing them. Don't treat them as failures without checking the raw message in a direct-delivery inbox.
- **Body footer:** the visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated section and could not be verified.
- **Sender:** `crateandbarrelkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a subdomain of the brand domain, which is consistent with aligned DKIM/SPF, but that can't be proven from this data.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Unable to assess. UTM parameters and landing pages sit behind the 91 unresolved redirect links. The subject promises a "two kids in one room" design story, but the destinations weren't checked. The scene7 asset named `…ClearanceSecondaryStory.gif` suggests a secondary clearance module, but its target is unknown.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all linked content images, and `alt=""` to spacers and tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport tag.
3. Re-test headers on a direct-delivery seed inbox (Gmail/Outlook) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
4. Extend QA to follow tracking redirects with a real GET, then record the final URL, status and UTM parameters.
5. Review the `rlcdn.com` pixels, which share the recipient hash with a third party, for consent and privacy disclosure.
6. Clean up the template by removing the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder, the duplicate `format-detection` tags and the dead 600px media rule, and escaping `&` in the `<title>`.
7. Fetch the full HTML instead of the truncated version so the footer and body structure can be checked.
