# Refresh their room before school starts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids leads with a genuinely nice hero — rolled rugs, a plush bunny, "new finishing touches starting at $29," clean white CTA button — and pairs it with a back-to-school banner up top. That's a fine 15 seconds of email. Then it falls into the same trap the brand's 7/31 sofa send ("Starting at $1599") got dinged for: kitchen-sink stacking. Bedding, decor, rugs, lighting, a back-to-school gear callout sitting directly above an unrelated *nursery crib* photo, a second "up to 50% off summer sale" banner, and finally a "we love these for you" grid that includes a Halloween pumpkin basket and bat-print plates — in a "before school starts" email in early August.
- The subject promises one clean idea (refresh the room before school), but the body delivers three overlapping campaigns (back-to-school, summer sale, and what looks like early Halloween cross-sell) with no single hero offer to rally around. Compare this to the brand's 9.00/10 Cotswolds lookbook or the 8.00/10 Aspen dinnerware send — both of those won by picking one story and stopping. This one doesn't stop.
- Verdict: solid production values, muddled offer strategy. It reads like three merchandising teams' modules got concatenated rather than edited into one email.

## What's working

- Hero section is genuinely clean: one image, one price anchor ($29), one white pill CTA ("Shop New Arrivals") sitting right on the photo.
- "Ships Free*" callout on the bedding section is a real, concrete purchase-decision signal.
- Consistent Crate & Kids visual identity throughout — soft palette, consistent typography, no render bugs, no broken images or overlapping text.
- "TheDesignDesk" free design appointment CTA near the footer is a nice loyalty-adjacent touch even without a formal loyalty program shown.
- "We love these for you" product grid shows real prices with strikethrough discounts (e.g., $64.80 → $45.36), which is more concrete than most of the banners above it.

## What's weak

- Three competing offers stack on top of each other: "back-to-school sale up to 20% off," "up to 50% off summer sale," and a discount card footnote — none is positioned as *the* offer, so nothing wins.
- The "Our new & exclusive backpacks, desks & more" / "Shop New Gear" back-to-school module is immediately followed by a photo of a *nursery with cribs* — visually it reads as baby furniture, not school-age gear, a jarring mismatch right under the CTA it's supposed to support.
- "We love these for you" mixes a Halloween pumpkin candy basket and bat-motif plates into a back-to-school room refresh email — seasonally confusing this early in August.
- Subject promises "refresh their room," but the hero and most modules (rugs, decor, lighting, summer sale) aren't explicitly tied to school — only the thin top banner carries that theme.
- Seven-plus stacked promotional modules before the footer is a lot of scrolling for a subscriber who opened for one specific reason.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one campaign. Pick either back-to-school or summer sale — not both — and drop the Halloween items from "we love these for you" entirely; they undercut the "before school starts" premise.
- 2. Swap the crib/nursery photo under "Shop New Gear" for an actual desk, backpack, or school-age bedroom shot — the current pairing looks like a template error even though nothing is technically broken.
- 3. Make the hero image itself back-to-school-specific (a styled kid's room/desk) instead of a generic rug flat-lay, so the subject line's promise is visible in the first frame, not just the banner above it.
- 4. Cap the email at 3-4 modules max, ordered by offer strength, and move "we love these for you" and "TheDesignDesk" into a separate send — this email is doing the job of three emails.
- 5. Add a real deadline ("sale ends Sunday") to the back-to-school banner — right now the urgency is thematic, not time-bound, so it's easy to defer.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New desks, bedding & storage — starting at $29`
