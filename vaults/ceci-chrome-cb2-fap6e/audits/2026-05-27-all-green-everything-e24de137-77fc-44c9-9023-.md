# All green everything
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's editorial color-story send — a full "all green" palette drop featuring forest-green velvet sofas, accent lamps, and coordinating decor. The email opens with a lush hero shot of a deep green sofa (appears to be "The Salgado"), flows into a mid-section lighting/accent feature, and then broadens out into a multi-product grid in the lower half. No sale, no offer, no urgency — pure aspiration.
- That editorial ambition is the email's strength and its liability. The photography is gorgeous and the color story is cohesive, but coming off two back-to-back Memorial Day sale closers on 5/26, this immediate pivot to "buy things because green" asks a lot of the subscriber. There's nothing here to motivate a click beyond "that sofa looks nice."
- Verdict: a competent editorial send that would land better mid-month than the day after a sale. It scores on brand execution; it loses on conversion signal.

## What's working

- Hero photography is genuinely arresting — the forest-green velvet sofa has strong visual pull and lands the color story immediately.
- The "all green" theme is cohesive top-to-bottom; palette discipline is tight and the editorial throughline is clear.
- Clean layout with no render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- The product grid in the lower section gives options for shoppers who don't respond to the hero product specifically.
- Cadence is appropriate as a palette-cleanse after the Memorial Day urgency push — the editorial mode is earned.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no discount, no free shipping, no loyalty reward. After two high-intensity sale emails on 5/26, subscribers have been trained to expect a reason to buy.
- "All green everything" is a vibe, not a hook — it communicates nothing about price, newness, or exclusivity.
- No visible CTA copy that's specific enough to read at any reasonable scroll speed.
- The lower half of the email abandons the green color story and shifts to warmer terracotta/wood tones — visually jarring and thematically inconsistent with the subject.
- No social proof, no bestseller callout, no reviews — for a full-price editorial ask, you need some conversion scaffolding.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one anchor offer** — even free shipping or a "New arrivals, no promo needed" confidence line would give subscribers permission to click. Right now there's nothing to close on.
- 2. **Make the hero product explicit** — if The Salgado is a new collection, say so. "New: The Salgado Collection" in the subject gives the send a news peg.
- 3. **Cut or colorize the lower grid** — the warm-toned products in the bottom half undercut the green color story. Either extend the green palette throughout or save those products for a separate send.
- 4. **Add a loyalty nudge** — CB2's double-rewards hook from the 5/26 Memorial Day closer was excellent; even a single "Earn rewards on every green pick" line would activate the member segment.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next sofa is very, very green`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals in the shade you didn't know you needed.`
