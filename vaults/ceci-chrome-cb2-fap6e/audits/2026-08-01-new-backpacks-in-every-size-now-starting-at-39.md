# New backpacks in every size, now starting at $39!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the best-organized Crate & Kids send in the recent run: the subject promises "backpacks in every size, starting at $39," and the first three screens deliver exactly that — a hero built around a literal size guide, then small/medium/large sections each paired with a "what fits inside" panel. That's a genuinely useful buying aid, and a sharp contrast to the July 31 sofa email that opened with an unrelated bronze finish before ever showing a couch.
- The problem is everything after screen four. Once the backpack story wraps with "make it a bundle," the email keeps going for another 8+ screens — a $5,000 design sweepstakes, a 50% off nursery furniture sale, a "we love these for you" grid stacked with a $1,300 stroller and a $1,499 dresser, a new credit card pitch, an app download push, and a store locator. This is the same kitchen-sink pattern flagged in the 07-31 dinnerware send, and it dilutes an otherwise focused idea.
- Net: a strong hook let down by scope creep and a couple of avoidable execution slips (a low-contrast button, no visible prices on any actual backpack SKU). Solid, not exceptional — trim it and it's one of the better sends in this brand's recent history.

## What's working

- Hero directly answers the size question with an overlay CTA against real kids-for-scale imagery — the "small / medium" size-comparison shot mid-email reinforces it well.
- Consistent architecture across all three size tiers (product grid + "what fits inside" list + CTA button) makes the email easy to scan in sections.
- Subject's core claim ($39 starting price, back-to-school) is delivered in the first two screens, unlike the 07-31 sofa email's mismatched opener.
- Diverse kid models spanning different ages signal which size tier is for whom.

## What's weak

- The "Enter Now" sweepstakes button renders in near-white text on a pale blue-gray panel — hard to read against the background.
- None of the nine backpack thumbnails shown (small, medium, or large) display a price, so the headline "$39" claim is never substantiated against an actual product.
- Past the bundle section, the email sprawls into unrelated cross-sells — nursery furniture, a $1,300 stroller, a credit card pitch — repeating the kitchen-sink problem seen in the 07-31 dinnerware send.
- This lands one day after "Refresh their room before school starts" (07-31) and two days after the Newborn Shop send (07-30) — three back-to-school-adjacent emails in a short window risks fatigue for the same segment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "make it a bundle" (~3 screens) — the backpack story is complete there; everything after it is unrelated inventory that drags focus away from the stated subject.
- 2. Fix the "Enter Now" button contrast in the DesignDesk block — swap to dark text or a solid navy fill to match the other CTA buttons' legibility.
- 3. Add a visible price under each backpack thumbnail so the "$39" claim is verifiable at the point of decision, not just asserted in body copy.
- 4. Replace the generic "we love these for you" grid (strollers, dressers, gliders) with backpack-adjacent accessories — lunch boxes, pencil cases, name tags — to stay on-theme with the subject line.
- 5. Tighten the primary hero CTA from generic "Shop Now" to something size-specific like "Find Their Size" to reinforce the guide hook that's already doing the heavy lifting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Backpacks by size: small, medium & large from $39`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Which backpack size fits your kid? (starting at $39)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Machine-washable, free shipping, and a size for every grade.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Take the guesswork out of backpack shopping — sizes inside.`
