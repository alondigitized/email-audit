# Brand new beds, dressers, desks & bookcases →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing exactly what the main C&B brand did two days ago with the Ninja espresso machine send: using a new-product-arrival beat to break from the 4th of July warehouse-sale drumbeat that has now run for a full week across both sub-brands. After five-plus consecutive sale hammers, the pivot to "look what's new" is the strategically correct move. The email is catalog-heavy — a hero bedroom scene followed by grid after grid of individual SKUs across all four categories named in the subject. That breadth is both the email's strength and its weakness.
- The hero is well shot: a styled children's room with a canopy metal bed frame, natural wood tones, and aspirational-but-achievable staging. The visual hierarchy is clean enough — hero first, then category grids, then a mid-email sale crosspromo banner. The problem is there's no price incentive here. New arrivals on their own are a weaker hook than "new + on sale" or "new + exclusive," and a long catalog scroll without a savings hook will lose most subscribers before they get to the bookcases at the bottom.
- Bottom line: a sensible cadence reset, decently executed, but undersells itself by not tying the new arrivals to any offer. The June 22 Ninja send scored an 8 because exclusivity (Only at C&B) replaced the discount. This email doesn't find an equivalent hook.

## What's working

- **Cadence reset is well-timed.** After six warehouse-sale sends across both brands, a new-arrivals editorial is a breath of fresh air. Subscribers who almost unsubscribed from the sale hammer will re-engage.
- **Hero image is strong.** The canopy bed with natural-light styling looks expensive, aspirational, and right for the brand — immediately communicates "new, elevated kids furniture."
- **Subject line delivers.** "Brand new beds, dressers, desks & bookcases →" is specific, clean, under 50 chars, and tells you exactly what's inside. The → arrow is Crate & Kids' consistent voice signature.
- **No render bugs visible.** Product images load cleanly, grid layout holds, no broken cells or overlapping text.
- **Category breadth is signal of a real drop.** Covering all four furniture categories in one send makes this feel like a genuine collection launch, not a thin refresh.

## What's weak

- **No offer hook anywhere in the hero.** New arrivals without a price story — no introductory discount, no exclusive availability, no free-shipping sweetener — makes this feel like a catalog, not a reason to act now.
- **Mid-email sale crosspromo breaks the narrative.** There's a warehouse-sale banner mid-scroll that undercuts the "fresh new arrivals" framing. It reads as two emails stitched together.
- **Grid depth is punishing on mobile.** Showing all four categories as individual product tiles in a single scroll means the desks and bookcases are far below the fold. Subscribers who care about beds never reach them.
- **No social proof or scarcity signal.** No "new this week," no "just dropped," no reviews — nothing that creates a reason to act on this send versus just bookmarking and forgetting it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a light offer or exclusivity hook to the hero.** "Free shipping on all new arrivals this week" or "Just arrived — first to shop the new collection" costs nothing to write and closes the urgency gap. The Ninja email got to an 8 by leading with "Only at Crate & Barrel" — do the equivalent here.
- 2. **Remove or relocate the mid-email warehouse-sale crosspromo.** If it must stay, push it to the footer. Running it mid-email torpedoes the editorial intent of the send.
- 3. **Collapse the grid to a hero product per category.** One hero bed, one hero dresser, one hero desk, one hero bookcase — then a "Shop all beds →" link per section. This is scannable; the current wall of thumbnails is not.
- 4. **Add a "just arrived" temporal signal.** Even a small badge ("New this week") on the hero or section headers creates urgency without discounting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just arrived: new beds, dressers & desks for kids`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New kids furniture — beds, dressers, desks & bookcases`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on new arrivals this week — shop the full collection`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh drops for every room — shop new kids furniture before it sells out`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch (confirmed)**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
From-name is "Crate & Kids"; title tag reads "Crate & Barrel." Affects the browser tab when recipients open in webmail and may confuse screen readers.

**Global zoom suppression — accessibility failure (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user-initiated zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA) violation.

**Global text-size-adjust disabled (confirmed)**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to the `*` selector — blocks OS-level font scaling for low-vision users on all elements, not just the body fix it's intended for.

**Conflicting `img { max-width }` media queries (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Source order means the `640px` rule wins for all viewports ≥ 640px, including desktop. The `600px` cap at 768px is never applied. Likely unintentional — images will render 40px wider than intended on desktop.

**Duplicate meta declarations (minor)**
`format-detection` appears four times (once combined, three individual). Redundant; no functional harm, but indicates template cruft.

Duplicate charset:
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Harmless in practice; keep one.
