# New rugs in breezy blues & greens, right this way
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel is in editorial mode again — this is a seasonal rug collection send structured around a color story (breezy blues and greens) rather than a discount. The hero delivers on the subject line promise: a lifestyle dining-room shot with a blue-tinted rug anchors the email and gives the scroll a reason to start. The free premium door-to-door delivery callout buried in the mid-section is the only real offer, and it's undersold — it should be in the hero.
- The brand history pattern is becoming a friction point here. This is the third Crate & Barrel / Crate & Kids email landing on June 9, after "All the things to finish their dream room" and the Crate & Kids toddler send earlier today. The coffee table editorial (June 9, 8/10) was genuinely useful content. This one is thinner — the product grid below the hero mostly shows neutral/beige rugs, not the blues and greens the subject line and hero promised. The color story lands in the opener and then disappears.
- Net judgement: a competent mid-tier editorial send with good bones and a solvable mismatch between subject-line promise and product grid delivery.

## What's working

- Hero image directly matches the subject line — a lifestyle dining room with a blue/green-toned rug makes the color story tangible immediately.
- Subject line is unusually specific for a rug send; naming the color palette is a real differentiator in a crowded inbox.
- Free premium door-to-door delivery is a genuine value prop and is visible (even if undersold).
- Clean layout with no render bugs, no overlapping text, no broken images — the email reads without friction.
- Outdoor rug vignette section adds lifestyle context beyond the standard grid, giving a seasonal-use case.

## What's weak

- Product grid breaks the color story — most of the rug swatches visible below the hero are neutral/beige/natural, not blues or greens. The subject line promises a specific palette and the scroll doesn't deliver it.
- Free door-to-door delivery is buried mid-email in small type rather than amplified in the hero where it would drive urgency.
- No price signal anywhere — not a discount, not a price range, not a "starting at" — which makes the scroll feel exploratory rather than actionable.
- "Right this way" as a closing hook in the subject line is weak; it adds length without adding value.
- App promotion near the footer is brand-standard but adds no relevance to a rug-browsing session.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the delivery offer in the hero.** "Free premium door-to-door delivery on rugs" is a tangible benefit that removes the biggest friction in rug buying (bulky, hard to return). Move it above the fold.
- 2. **Curate the product grid to match the color story.** If the subject promises blues and greens, the first two rows of products should be exactly that. Neutral options can appear below as alternatives, not as the primary scroll content.
- 3. **Add a price anchor.** Even "rugs from $129" in the hero subhead gives browsers a reason to click rather than wait for a sale.
- 4. **Cut "right this way" from the subject.** The color-palette hook is strong on its own; the closer weakens it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New arrivals: rugs in breezy blues & greens`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer rugs — breezy blues, greens & free delivery`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free premium door-to-door delivery on every rug, now through summer.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New indoor + outdoor rugs in cool summer hues — free delivery included.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Conflicting image `max-width` media queries** — two `min-width` rules contradict each other:
  ```css
  @media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
  @media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
  ```
  Because the 640px rule appears last, it wins at all widths ≥ 640px. The 600px cap is effectively dead code — images on desktop will max at 640px, not 600px. Reorder or consolidate these rules.

- **Universal `text-size-adjust: none`** — the `*{}` rule applies `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` to all elements. This can suppress user-initiated browser zoom and is a WCAG concern (Success Criterion 1.4.4 — Resize Text). Scope this to `body` only.

- **Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — the attribute appears four times: once as a combined value and again three times individually (`date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`). Redundant but not harmful; trim to one declaration.

- **No dark mode support declared** — `content="light"` on both `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` explicitly opts out of dark mode rendering in Apple Mail and other clients. Estimated 30–40% of recipients use dark mode; forced-light mode can produce poor contrast inversions if a client overrides anyway.

- **Template placeholder comment left in production HTML:**
  ```html

  ```
  This is a raw build-time instruction with a typo ("IMPUT"). It is visible in source and indicates the template was not fully cleaned before send.
