# OUTDOOR IS ON SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's outdoor-category cut of the ongoing Midseason Sale, arriving on 5/30 — late May is peak outdoor buying intent, so the segmentation instinct is right. The email opens with a clean 40%-off hero shot of an outdoor lounge area, then works through four named products (Bay Sectional, Calla Dining Table, Oakley Dining Chair, Lotus Rug) each with individual CTAs, before bridging back into the broader Midseason Sale and a "More for You" catch-all tail.
- The problem is the execution is nearly indistinguishable from the 5/28 Midseason Sale send, which already led with up-to-40%-off across categories. This doesn't sharpen the outdoor story — it just re-serves a discount that's already live. And arriving as the fifth email in four days, the ALL CAPS subject "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" reads like a shout to a list that's already been shouted at.
- A category send like this should feel like a privilege — here's your curated outdoor edit — not a reminder that the same sale is still running.

## What's working

- Seasonal timing is exactly right: outdoor furniture in late May sits at maximum purchase intent before summer fully arrives.
- Four named products give real entry points — subscribers can go straight to the Bay Sectional or Oakley Chair rather than landing on a homepage.
- The 40% hero is visible immediately above any scroll, no hunting required.
- Render is clean throughout — no broken images, no layout gaps, no lorem ipsum.
- Bridging back to the Midseason Sale block reinforces the deal context without requiring the email to stand alone.

## What's weak

- ALL CAPS subject "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" is a shout, not an invitation — it reads spam-adjacent and carries none of CB2's design credibility.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs on every product are generic; "SHOP THE BAY SECTIONAL" or "SEE DINING CHAIRS" would pull harder.
- No deadline, no urgency — the Midseason Sale has no end date visible anywhere in the email, so there's no closing pressure on the 40% offer.
- Fifth email in four days: cadence fatigue is real here, and this send doesn't feel earned or differentiated enough to justify the slot.
- No price anchors on the product cards — showing "Was $X / Now $Y" on even one hero product would make 40% feel concrete rather than aspirational.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline** — even "Shop by Sunday" in the subject and hero would create closing pressure that's currently absent.
- 2. **Product-specific CTAs** — replace all four "SHOP NOW" buttons with named CTAs ("SHOP BAY SECTIONAL," "SEE DINING CHAIRS"); it's a one-line change that lifts CTR on category sends.
- 3. **Add one price anchor** — a "Was $1,299 / Now $779" callout on the Bay Sectional makes 40% feel real; without it the discount is abstract.
- 4. **Lowercase the subject** — title case or sentence case immediately drops the shout-signal and aligns with CB2's editorial voice.
- 5. **Cut the "More for You" tail** — it dilutes the outdoor focus and adds friction to a send that should be single-category tight.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4)**
