# Meet our break-resistant wine glasses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-education email built around Crate & Barrel's break-resistant wine glasses — a genuinely differentiated product story that leads with a clear functional benefit. The hero photography is sharp and the layout follows C&B's usual clean aesthetic: logo, hero image, descriptive copy, primary CTA, then a long tail of secondary product sections filling the scroll.
- The core problem: this email was sent June 26, right in the middle of C&B's aggressively promoted 4th of July Warehouse Sale cycle (four sale emails in two days, June 24-25). A product-feature email that doesn't mention the sale or attach a price hook is a wasted placement. Anyone primed by the Warehouse Sale emails is in discount mode; "Meet our break-resistant wine glasses" doesn't meet them there.
- The email would be genuinely interesting to the right buyer at the right moment — but it neither creates urgency nor captures the sale energy already in market. It's a product blog post dressed as an email.

## What's working

- Break-resistant is a specific, credible, and differentiated product claim — it hooks on a real pain point (broken glassware).
- Hero photography maintains C&B's premium aesthetic; the stemware is elegantly lit and the composition is on-brand.
- Subject line is concise (37 chars), readable on mobile, and spam-free.
- The email opens on the hero product without friction — clean render, no broken images, no competing banners.
- Long editorial tail features complementary barware and home goods, giving browsers somewhere to go after the hero.

## What's weak

- No price offer anywhere in the visible hero — this is a "look at this" email, not a "buy this" one, which kills conversion intent.
- Completely disconnected from the 4th of July Warehouse Sale running this same week; no tie-in language or price callout squanders the promotional momentum already built.
- The email is very long — multiple secondary product grids dilute the single break-resistant story and scatter click intent.
- No urgency signal anywhere: no deadline, no limited-stock note, no sale badge — nothing to create action now vs. someday.
- CTA copy is not clearly readable at mobile scale; if it defaults to "Shop Now" that's the weakest possible verb for a product with a specific story to tell.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Attach this product to the Warehouse Sale.** "Break-resistant wine glasses — in our 4th of July Sale" would be the same product email with a conversion lever. No redesign needed; add a sale badge to the hero and link to the sale landing page. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Add a price anchor or discount callout.** Even "from $X" in the hero copy gives readers a reason to click today instead of favoriting for later.
- 3. **Shorten the email.** Cut the secondary grid sections. One hero product, one CTA, one secondary "pair with" section max. The break-resistant story is strong enough to carry the email alone.
- 4. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Shop Break-Resistant Glasses" > "Shop Now."
- 5. **Add a micro-testimonial or proof point** near the hero — "dropped twice, still standing" type copy or a star rating snippet would reinforce the break-resistant claim without needing a full review module.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our wine glasses won't break. Seriously.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Break-resistant wine glasses — now in our July sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Finally, a glass that survives the dishwasher, the dog, and your toddler.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The only wine glasses we've ever felt good about owning.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**ISSUE — `maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disabling user zoom fails WCAG 2.0 Success Criterion 1.4.4. Apple Mail and most modern clients ignore this, but it remains a compliance flag and degrades experience for low-vision users in clients that respect it.

**ISSUE — Conflicting image `max-width` media queries (dead rule):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }   /* line ~68 */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }   /* line ~72 */
```
Both are `min-width` queries; for viewports ≥ 768px, both match but cascade order means the 640px rule always wins, making the 600px rule dead code. If 600px was intentional for a 768px+ desktop cap, the rule ordering needs to be reversed.

**ISSUE — Global wildcard `line-height: 100%` risks override collisions:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; ... }
```
This can suppress custom `line-height` values on headings and buttons that require non-100% values; observed pattern in this file where client CSS later attempts specific overrides.

**Minor — Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (4 entries):**
The combined declaration already covers all fields; the three trailing single-field tags are redundant and add parse noise.
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">       <!-- redundant -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">    <!-- redundant -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">  <!-- redundant -->
```

This is a raw template instruction comment — including a typo (`IMPUT` → `INPUT`) — that was not removed before send. While invisible to subscribers, it is visible in "View Source" and signals an incomplete template process. The adjacent empty comment `<!---->` is also a residual artifact.

`<!--V2-->` is a template/build version marker placed between the doctype and root element. This is not valid HTML structure position and indicates the template preprocessing pipeline did not strip build metadata before delivery.
