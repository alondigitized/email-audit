# Trying to get back to your cooking routine? Start here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a total pivot from the Labor Day marathon — Crate & Barrel drops the countdown timers entirely and goes long-form editorial with a kitchen-routine theme. The subject line ("Trying to get back to your cooking routine? Start here") is genuinely well-targeted seasonal copy — early September, back-to-routine framing, no fake urgency — a nice change of pace after six-plus "ENDS TONIGHT" sends this week.
- The problem is execution, not concept: this is an enormous kitchen-sink scroll (blenders, Caraway cookware, GreenPan, Nespresso, Ninja CREAMi, more "even more arrivals," bedding, mixers, an app pitch) with no single hero offer, no price anchor anywhere, and at least eight separate "Shop Now" CTAs competing for attention. A reader has to scroll past a dozen product blocks before hitting anything that reads like a deal.
- Verdict: smart subject, weak body — this reads like a full catalog dump rather than a curated "start your routine" story, and it'll lose people by the third scroll.

## What's working

- Subject line ties directly to a real seasonal behavior (post-summer routine reset) instead of manufactured urgency — a refreshing break from the week's countdown spam.
- Clean product photography throughout, no render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text.
- Logical narrative arc at the top (meal prep → cookware → cooking essentials → smoothies) before it sprawls into catalog mode.
- "Ships free" callout on the Ninja blender section gives at least one concrete purchase incentive.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no %/$ off, no free shipping banner, no loyalty pricing visible in the whole scroll.
- Too many competing CTAs (7-8+ "Shop Now" buttons) with no visual hierarchy telling the eye where to land first.
- The email drifts from "cooking routine" (subject promise) into bedding and an app-download pitch by the bottom, breaking its own narrative.
- Product names are tiny and hard to parse at thumbnail size (e.g. "GreenPan Reserve Pro Ceramic Nonstick Everyday Pan") — no price shown under any of them.
- Ends on an app-install pitch ("it's all on the app, including your chance at $500") instead of reinforcing the cooking-routine hook it opened with.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero product (the Vitamix-style blender at the top is the strongest candidate) and lead with a real price or % off — right now nothing in this email tells me what anything costs.
- 2. Cut the email in half — six-plus product sections is catalog behavior, not a "start here" guide; trim to 3 curated categories max.
- 3. Keep the "cooking routine" throughline all the way down — drop the bedding block and the app-install pitch, or move them to a separate send.
- 4. Add visible prices/sale prices under at least the top 2-3 product blocks so scanning shoppers see value immediately.
- 5. Consolidate CTAs — one strong primary CTA above the fold, secondary links styled smaller below.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meal-prep blenders, new cookware colors, and more — all ship free`
