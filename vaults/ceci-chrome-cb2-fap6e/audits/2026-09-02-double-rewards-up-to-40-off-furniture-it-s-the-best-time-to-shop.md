# Double Rewards. Up to 40% off furniture. It’s the best time to shop →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel stacking two offers into one hero — "Double Rewards" and "Up to 40% off furniture" — split across two side-by-side panels at the top, plus a bottom banner promising "up to 60% off" for the Labor Day Event. That's three different percentage claims (40%, 60%, and an unspecified rewards multiplier) competing for attention before the eye even settles, which is the core problem here: no single clear offer, just a wall of overlapping promo math.
- Below the fold it's the now-familiar Crate & Barrel Labor Day grid — category tiles (sofas, kitchen, decor, rugs, outdoor, kids), then a TheDesignDesk sweepstakes pitch, then a grilling guide, then outdoor furniture, then a "we love these for you" product rail, then app download and loyalty blurbs. It's comprehensive but exhausting — six distinct content modules in one email with no clear hierarchy about which one actually matters today.
- Consistent with the brand history here (2026-09-01 sends scored 6/10 for similar "seasonal hero on top of Labor Day machinery" patterns), this is another serviceable-but-crowded send. It's competent execution of a cluttered strategy, not a good email.

## What's working

- Clean product photography throughout — furniture, tabletop, and outdoor shots are well-lit and on-brand.
- Two clear CTA buttons ("Shop Furniture Sale," "Shop The Labor Day Event") right under the hero, so the reader isn't left hunting.
- The category grid with small percentage callouts (60% off bedding, 40% off kitchen, etc.) gives specific, scannable offers rather than one vague blanket claim.
- "The grilling guide" and outdoor furniture sections are seasonally relevant for early September.
- No visible render bugs — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Three competing top-line offers (Double Rewards / 40% off / 60% off) create genuine confusion about what the actual deal is.
- The hero is split into two small side-by-side tiles instead of one dominant visual — weak visual hierarchy right where it matters most.
- Email is extremely long (six+ distinct sections) with no signal of which module is the priority — feels like every merchandising team got a slot.
- "TheDesignDesk" sweepstakes ($5000 giveaway) is a scope-shift in the middle of a sale email that dilutes the furniture-sale message.
- Subject promises "Double Rewards" prominently but the body doesn't explain the mechanic (what is "Double Rewards," how do I get it) — it's just a badge, not a clear benefit.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer and lead with it at full width — either the rewards multiplier or the 40% furniture discount, not both stacked as competing tiles. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Cut the sweepstakes/TheDesignDesk module out of this send or move it to its own email — it doesn't belong wedged between the sale grid and the grilling guide.
- 3. Move or trim the "we love these for you" and app-download blocks — they're generic filler this far down and most readers won't scroll this far.
- 4. Add a real deadline ("ends Monday," "through Labor Day") near the hero CTA — right now the urgency is implied by the event name only.
- 5. Explain the Double Rewards mechanic in one line under the badge (e.g., "Earn 2X rewards points on every purchase") so it reads as a benefit, not just a stamp.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn double rewards points on every order through Labor Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on sofas, dining sets, and storage — shop before it's gone.`
