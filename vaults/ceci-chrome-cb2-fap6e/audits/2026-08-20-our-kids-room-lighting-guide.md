# Our kids' room lighting guide ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running its lighting category as an educational guide rather than a discount push — "new & bestselling designs to light up their space," starting at $99, then a clean run through pendant lights, table lamps, floor lamps, and flush mounts. It's organized and on-brand, with warm, styled room photography that actually shows the fixtures in context (a real differentiator vs. the flat product-grid emails this brand often sends).
- But it's long. Five distinct lighting subcategories, each with its own hero shot, product row, and "Shop" button, followed by a "loved by parents" testimonial banner, a $500 sweepstakes, a "we love these for" cross-sell strip, a credit card pitch, and an app download block. By the time a reader reaches the sweepstakes they've scrolled through what is effectively four separate emails stitched into one. It reads as a category catalog masquerading as a single campaign.
- Compared to 2026-08-19's "7 easy playroom updates" (5/10, also a straight listicle) this is a step up in production value — better photography, clearer category segmentation — but it shares the same core problem: no offer, no urgency, and too much to scroll through before landing anywhere.

## What's working

- Clear opening hook: "new & bestselling designs to light up their space" with a "starting at $99" anchor gives a concrete price entry point.
- Each lighting subcategory (pendants, table lamps, floor lamps, flush mounts) gets its own styled room photo, which sells the fixtures in situ rather than on white background.
- Star ratings are visible on individual products (table lamps, floor lamps), adding light social proof.
- Repeated "Shop All [Category] Lighting" CTAs make navigation by sub-interest easy for a reader who only cares about one room.
- No visible render bugs — images loaded cleanly, text is legible, layout is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No discount, promo code, or sale framing anywhere in the lighting content — it's a pure catalog send with a soft "starting at $99" price tease.
- The email is extremely long: five product sections plus testimonial, sweepstakes, cross-sell, credit card, and app blocks — a lot to ask of a mobile scroller.
- The "enter for a chance to win $500" sweepstakes is buried near the bottom after all the lighting content, where most readers will have already dropped off.
- Multiple competing CTAs ("Shop All Lighting," five separate "Shop" buttons, "Shop All Bestsellers," "Enter Now," "Learn More," "Get a Discount") dilute the primary action.
- The subject's "✨" and "guide" framing promise editorial content/tips, but the body is a standard product catalog with no actual styling advice or how-to copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email roughly in half — pick the two or three strongest lighting categories and drop the rest to a "Shop All Lighting" link; five full sections is too many for one send.
- 2. Add an actual offer or urgency line near the top (% off, free shipping threshold, or a dated sale) — right now there's nothing to click for beyond curiosity.
- 3. Move the sweepstakes higher, or cut it — a $500 giveaway competing for attention against five product categories gets lost at the bottom.
- 4. If the subject promises a "guide," deliver a short styling tip or "how to layer lighting" callout near the top to earn that framing — otherwise rename the subject to match the catalog reality.
- 5. Trim the tail: testimonial + sweepstakes + cross-sell + credit card + app promo in one email is stacking too many secondary asks after the primary content.
