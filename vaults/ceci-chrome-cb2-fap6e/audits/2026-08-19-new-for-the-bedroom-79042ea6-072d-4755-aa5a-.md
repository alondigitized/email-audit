# NEW FOR THE BEDROOM
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 finally doing the thing they should've done days ago: a straightforward category launch with a clean hero and real product news. "JUST IN — NEW FOR THE BEDROOM, ONLY AT CB2" is direct, and unlike the Goop tie-in (2026-08-19, 5/10) or the Paige Desorbo apartment send (2026-08-18, 5/10), there's no celebrity gloss layered on top — just four new bedroom pieces (Jackson Bed, Hawk Nightstand, Russell Bed, Rio Dresser) shown clearly with designer credits and individual CTAs.
- The problem isn't the concept, it's the depth. This is a long scroll with zero pricing until "MORE FOR YOU" near the bottom, and even the hero offer is really just "new arrivals" — there's no discount, no urgency, nothing that converts a browse into a click for someone not already shopping for bedroom furniture specifically. It reads like a catalog drop, not a campaign.
- Solid execution, weak hook. This will perform fine with existing furniture shoppers already mid-purchase-decision on a bed or dresser, and poorly with everyone else in the inbox.

## What's working

- Clear "JUST IN" hero with an unambiguous "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" button over an appealing bedroom lifestyle shot.
- Each new product (Jackson Bed, Hawk Nightstand, Russell Bed, Rio Dresser) gets its own image, name, designer credit, and "SHOP NOW" link — good scannability.
- Category navigation grid (Rugs, Mirrors, Lighting, Wall Art) gives multiple paths deeper into the site.
- Footer reinforces loyalty mechanics visibly: "EARN 10% BACK" and referral "GET $15 OFF" blocks.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- No pricing or discount anywhere until the "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the very bottom — a shopper has to scroll past the entire new-collection story before seeing a single dollar figure.
- Subject "NEW FOR THE BEDROOM" is generic catalog language with no offer, no urgency, nothing that differentiates it from any other new-arrivals send.
- This is an extremely long single-column scroll (4 hero products + shop-bedroom banner + 4-category grid + app download + 6-product "more for you" grid + footer) with no compression for skimmers.
- "SHOP BEDROOM" mid-email is a flat gray button with no visual weight — easy to miss against the surrounding product imagery.
- Design continues CB2's recent pattern of full-catalog cascades (see 2026-08-17's "midcentury" send, 5/10) rather than the tighter, single-offer format that scored best this month (2026-08-16 "SUMMER CLEARANCE," 6/10).

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook to the subject/hero — a price anchor ("Beds from $999") or urgency framing beats "new arrivals" alone; new-collection sends without an offer consistently underperform CB2's clearance/sale sends in this brand history.
- 2. Move at least one price point into the hero or first product block — don't make people scroll to the bottom grid to see what anything costs.
- 3. Cut the email roughly in half — the four-product bedroom showcase plus the category grid plus "more for you" is redundant; pick either the curated new-arrivals story or the broad grid, not both.
- 4. Give "SHOP BEDROOM" the same visual treatment as the hero CTA — right now it's a flat afterthought button in the middle of a strong visual flow.
- 5. Surface the "EARN 10% BACK" loyalty line higher up (near the hero) instead of burying it in the footer, since it's a real incentive that could pull hesitant clickers through.

## Technical Audit

# Technical Email Audit — CB2 "NEW FOR THE BEDROOM"

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a standard MI (Message Impact/MessageGears)-templated CB2 promotional email built on legacy MSO/VML table markup; it relies on third-party ad-redirect pixels served over plaintext HTTP and is missing standard compliance/authentication signals in the delivered headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 67 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped from HTTP probing (redirect domains not resolvable via automated check) — cannot confirm destination validity or redirect chain integrity.
- Multiple Dotomi ad-redirect endpoints are loaded over **unencrypted HTTP**, which mixed-content policies in modern mail clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will likely block or strip:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&dtm_cmagic=654788...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php` (10 sequential cookie-sync pixels, all HTTP)
- These 11 HTTP-only pixel/redirect calls are a functional deliverability risk, not just a security nit: if blocked, downstream ad-network cookie sync and any attribution tied to it silently fails with no error surfaced to the sender.
- Legitimate first-party tracking pixels (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/...`) are correctly served over HTTPS.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 10 content images (`image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/*.jpg`) are missing `alt` text — screen readers and clients with images-off will render blank space with no fallback context.
- The 11 ad-redirect/tracking pixels are also missing `alt` attributes, though this is standard/expected for 1x1 tracking pixels.
- Template uses legacy XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype with heavy MSO/VML conditional comments — appropriate for Outlook desktop compatibility, no issues found in the conditional structure itself.
- `@font-face` declarations reference multiple external font hosts (`cb2.com`, `crateandbarrel.com`) — fonts will fail to load in most clients (Gmail, Outlook) since `@font-face` support is inconsistent; this is a graceful-degradation case (fallback stack `Helvetica, sans-serif` is present on at least the `a` selector), not a hard rendering bug.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`, `[FIELD]`) found in the truncated source.
- No issues found.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe support at the header level (may exist in untruncated body via footer link, but header-level mechanism is absent or not captured).
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found** (RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe (POST) is not confirmed supported.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status cannot be verified from available data.
- Caveat: all three are flagged as WARN, not confirmed failures — the QA tooling notes these may not be captured by the relay rather than confirmed absent from the original send. Recommend verifying against raw headers from the original ESP delivery, not just the relay capture.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Cannot assess — the 67 tracking/redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing, so destination URLs, UTM parameter structure, and landing-page alignment are unverified in this pass.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Convert the 11 Dotomi HTTP endpoints (`login.dotomi.com`, `ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/.../pub1–10.php`) to HTTPS, or confirm with the ad-network partner whether HTTPS endpoints are available — plaintext HTTP resources are increasingly blocked client-side.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 10 content images at `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/*.jpg`.
3. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in the actual ESP-delivered message (not just the relay-captured copy) to confirm RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance.
4. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication results directly from the sending ESP's logs, since the relay-side `Authentication-Results` header was not captured.
5. Re-run link validation directly against the 67 skipped tracking-redirect URLs (bypassing the skip filter or resolving the true destination) to confirm landing-page and UTM continuity before send.
