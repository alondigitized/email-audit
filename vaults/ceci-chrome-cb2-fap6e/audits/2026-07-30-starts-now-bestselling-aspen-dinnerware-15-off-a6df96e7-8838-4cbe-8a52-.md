# Starts now! Bestselling Aspen dinnerware 15% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- The top third of this email is genuinely strong: one hero image, one collection (Aspen dinnerware), one offer (15% off), one button. It reads like the Cotswolds lookbook send from 2026-07-29 that scored a 9 — specific, uncluttered, and the review quote ("A classic design and offers great quality for the price") adds real social proof right where the eye needs it after the product grid.
- Then it falls into the same trap that's dragged down other Crate & Barrel sends this week: a "in season now: summer produce" recipe detour, a "we love these for you" cross-sell grid spanning cookware, KitchenAid mixers, and Le Creuset, a credit-card pitch, an app download ask, and a design-consultation CTA — none of which have anything to do with Aspen dinnerware or the 15% offer that got someone to open this email. By the time a reader scrolls past the second "Shop" button, the email has stopped being about the thing it promised.
- It's also the second Crate & Barrel promotional send today — "Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale →" went out the same day (2026-07-30, 8/10). Two sale-flavored sends in one day from the same brand is cadence fatigue, even if the creative in each is decent on its own.

## What's working

- Single-collection hero: Aspen dinnerware, one clear photo, one headline, one CTA — no kitchen-sink at the top.
- Transparent pricing throughout: every product shows Sale $X vs. Reg $Y, which reads as honest rather than a vague "% off" teaser.
- A real customer quote ("A classic design and offers great quality for the price") sits right after the first product grid, reinforcing the purchase decision at the right moment.
- Two well-placed CTAs after the grid ("Shop the Sale" / "Shop All Bestselling Tabletop") give a clear next step without forcing a scroll back up.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks anywhere in the send.

## What's weak

- The email abandons its own subject line halfway through: summer produce recipes, cookware cross-sells, a credit card pitch, an app download ask, and a design consultation offer all compete for attention after the Aspen story ends.
- Both CTA buttons say some form of "Shop" ("Shop Now," "Shop the Sale," "Shop All Bestselling Tabletop") — none tell the reader what they'll get by clicking.
- No deadline on the 15% off offer — "Starts now" implies urgency but never says when it ends, so there's no reason to act today versus next week.
- This is the second Crate & Barrel sale email today (after "Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale →"), and the two offers aren't reconciled — is Aspen 15% off, or is it part of the up-to-60%-off sale? A reader who saw both sends will be confused about which discount actually applies.
- Cross-sell grid ("we love these for you") has zero connection to tabletop/dinnerware — mixing stand mixers and dutch ovens into a dinnerware-focused email dilutes the story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut everything after the second "Shop" button (produce recipes, cross-sell grid, credit card, app, design desk) into its own send, or at minimum move it below a hard "you might also like" divider — let the Aspen story end where the story ends.
- 2. Reconcile the offer math with today's other Crate & Barrel send: if Aspen is part of the sitewide sale, say "15% off, plus up to 60% off Summer Sale" in one hero, not two separate emails on the same day.
- 3. Add a real deadline to "Starts now" — even "through Sunday" gives a reason to click today instead of bookmarking it.
- 4. Replace generic "Shop Now" CTA copy with something specific to the product, e.g. "Shop Aspen Dinnerware."
- **Subject Alt A:** `15% off Aspen dinnerware — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our bestselling dinnerware set, now 15% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plates, bowls, glassware & more from our most-loved tabletop collection.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The set our customers keep coming back for — now discounted.`
