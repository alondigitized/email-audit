# The high chair they'll use until middle school (really!) in our exclusive color→
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely well-targeted single-product launch: Crate & Kids partnering with Stokke to introduce the Tripp Trapp high chair in a new "olive green" colorway. The hero is unambiguous — brand lockup, product name, color, and a Shop Now button all visible in the first frame — and the mid-email block reinforces credibility with "over 50 years" heritage copy and "500+ reviews" social proof. This is exactly the kind of specific, single-hero send that performs better than the brand's usual sprawling catalog dumps (see 2026-08-13 kitchen and bestsellers sends, both 5.00/10).
- Where it falls apart is length and focus creep after the strong open. Once you scroll past the Tripp Trapp block, it turns into the same everything-but-the-kitchen-sink catalog the brand keeps defaulting to — "even more from Stokke," a fall arrivals teaser, a $5000 sweepstakes, a "we love these for you" grid, a credit card pitch, and app/store CTAs, all stacked in one send. The subject line promise ("until middle school, in our exclusive color") is honored visually up top, but the email doesn't know when to stop.
- Strong hero, weak discipline. This would score meaningfully higher as a tight, product-only send.

## What's working

- Hero clearly states the product, brand, and new exclusive color ("Tripp Trapp in olive green") with a matching product photo and a visible Shop Now button.
- Heritage/trust copy ("over 50 years, Stokke has challenged the ordinary") gives the premium price a rationale before you see it.
- "500+ new reviews, one of the best high chairs" is real, specific social proof placed right at the price-justification moment.
- Lifestyle photography (baby in the chair, styled nursery shots) matches a parent/baby-gear persona well.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- The email is extremely long for a single-product launch; the focus dilutes fast after the first two screens.
- No visible price anywhere in the hero or product blocks — a premium chair (Tripp Trapp typically $300+) with zero price transparency reads as evasive.
- The sweepstakes, credit card pitch, and "even more from Stokke" sections compete with and bury the actual news (the exclusive color).
- Generic secondary CTAs ("Shop Now," "Shop All Stokke," "Learn More") don't reinforce the specific product angle.
- This is the brand's third "new arrivals" style email in four days (Halloween 8/12, bedroom 8/13, kitchen 8/13) — cadence fatigue risk for anyone opening more than one of these.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the "iconic high chair" block — drop the sweepstakes, credit card, and "we love these for you" grid entirely, or move them to a separate send. A single-product exclusive-color launch should read as an event, not a catalog.
- 2. Add the price (and any strikethrough/comparison if relevant) directly under the hero product shot — high-consideration purchases need this visible before the click, not after.
- 3. Replace the generic "Shop Now" CTAs with product-specific copy, e.g. "Shop the Olive Green" — ties the button directly to the exclusivity hook in the subject.
- 4. Move the "500+ reviews" proof point higher, ideally right in or just under the hero, since it's the strongest trust signal in the email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Tripp Trapp high chair, now in exclusive olive green`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The chair that grows with them — new color, just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ 5-star reviews. One chair, birth through big kid.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stokke's iconic Tripp Trapp, reimagined in olive green.`
