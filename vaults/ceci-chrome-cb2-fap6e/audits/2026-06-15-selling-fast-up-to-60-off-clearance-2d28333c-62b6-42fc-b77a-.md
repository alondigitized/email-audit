# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' clearance follow-up, arriving two days after Crate & Barrel's own "Up to 60% off! Don't miss The Summer Clearance Event" on June 13. The hero earns its headline — "up to 60% off + new markdowns!" lands immediately above the fold with a clear Shop Clearance button — but the floral/sunflower hero image reads more like a botanical lifestyle brand than a kids-furniture retailer. The "+new markdowns" addition is the one genuinely fresh signal; everything else is a re-run of a message subscribers already received from the sibling brand.
- The email gets architecturally greedy fast. Below the clearance hero it pivots to a "gear up for summer" editorial section, then personalized product picks, then a Crate & Barrel cross-promo, then an app-download push, then a TheDesignDesk appointment offer. Five separate narratives in one send. The clearance urgency that opened strong is fully diffused by the time the reader hits product recs.
- Bottom line: a competent clearance blast with a cadence problem and a multi-message layout that undercuts the urgency it opened with.

## What's working

- **"+ new markdowns" freshener** — appending this to the standard "up to 60% off" claim creates a genuine reason to open even for subscribers who saw the June 13 C&B send.
- **Hero CTA placement** — "Shop Clearance" button sits inside the hero block, visible without scrolling on mobile.
- **"We love these for you" product grid** — four product tiles with visible pricing (including struck-through original prices) give the email tangible product substance.
- **Summer editorial section** — the "gear up for summer" room scene with natural-wood furniture and botanical bedding is well-styled and on-trend.

## What's weak

- **Hero image mismatch** — a close-up sunflower/floral photo is more Williams-Sonoma than Crate & Kids; it doesn't signal "kids clearance" at a glance.
- **Layout sprawl** — five distinct sections (clearance, summer editorial, product picks, app download, design desk) make this feel like three emails stapled together; urgency drains with each scroll.
- **No deadline** — "selling fast" is soft social proof, not a real countdown; there's no "ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" to anchor the urgency claim.
- **Cadence collision** — this is the 5th+ send from the Crate & Barrel brand family in 72 hours; open fatigue is the real enemy here, not creative quality.
- **App and TheDesignDesk blocks** — bolting on two unrelated conversion asks (app install, free design appointment) dilutes the clearance message and competes for the same tap.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero image** — use a styled kids' room or nursery with clearance-tagged products visible rather than a macro floral shot. The image should do half the selling.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" turns "selling fast" from a soft nudge into a real urgency driver.
- 3. **Cut to two sections max** — clearance hero + personalized product picks. Kill or defer the app download and design desk blocks to a separate send.
- 4. **Tighten the subject to the new-markdowns angle** — that's the only incremental news vs. June 13; lead with it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns just dropped — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh clearance cuts: 60% off kids + nursery`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New items added to clearance — furniture, bedding, gear`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends soon: biggest price drops of the summer, now on kids faves`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta prevents pinch-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting desktop `img` max-width rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both rules fire on screens ≥ 768px; the 600px rule wins due to cascade order, making the 640px rule dead code. On 640–767px viewports (some tablets), images are capped at 640px even inside a 600px container — risks horizontal scroll or overflow. These breakpoints need to be reconciled.

**[LOW] Overly aggressive global text-size-adjust reset**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The `*` selector applies `none` to all elements, including body text. The safer value is `100%`, applied to `body` or `html` only. The `html` block later correctly sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` again, duplicating the rule.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
One combined meta and three individual metas declare overlapping suppression rules. Redundant but harmless in most clients; adds unnecessary head bloat.
