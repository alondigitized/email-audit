# Heads up: These holiday bestsellers always sell out →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a scarcity-led holiday decor send. It's stronger than the recent Early Black Friday blasts because it leads with "these sell out every year" and numbered bestsellers instead of a percent-off banner. The top bar sets a credible deadline: order by 11/16 for Thanksgiving delivery. Beneath it sit two cards, "last Double Rewards this year" and "Early Black Friday is here, up to 30% off." The product tiles each carry their own short story and a specific button.
- The email then loses focus. After the bestsellers it adds category links, an Early Black Friday grid, "top kitchen deals (they ship free!)", "just in for your kitchen", a second Double Rewards block and a "we love these for you" block. It's the fourth or fifth Crate & Barrel send in about three days, after the 11-11 Early Black Friday sends and the 11-10 Double Rewards pushes. The scarcity hook is good, but the rewards and sale messaging is the same as before.
- Verdict: a good concept that's too long, and the "always sell out" framing is undercut by the generic sale content below it.
- The screenshot is heavily downscaled. I read the layout and larger headlines, but not the fine print or small body copy.

## What's working

- The hero line "these sell out every year." is a clear scarcity hook that matches the subject.
- The 11/16 order-by deadline in the top bar is specific and tied to Thanksgiving delivery.
- Numbered bestseller tiles (hemlock garland, Joan Anderson plates, monochrome candle collection, advent calendar) give the email a scannable "top picks" structure.
- Button copy is specific, for example "Shop Hemlock Garland" and "Shop Joan Anderson Plates", not a generic "Shop Now."
- Double Rewards and the up-to-30%-off Early Black Friday offer are both stated in the first screen.

## What's weak

- The email is very long. The bestseller story is diluted by an Early Black Friday grid, kitchen deals, a "just in" block and a second rewards block.
- Two offers compete at the top, Double Rewards and Early Black Friday, plus a third, free shipping, further down. Nothing says which one matters most.
- The "sell out" claim isn't backed by any visible stock cue, such as "low stock" or "X left," so it reads as marketing copy.
- The cadence is heavy. Rewards and Early Black Friday have appeared in every send since 11-10, so the urgency feels routine.
- Kitchen and cookware deals sit awkwardly in a holiday-decor email and pull attention away from the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the hero plus six bestsellers, one sale banner and one rewards line. Drop the kitchen deals, "just in" and "we love these" sections.
- 2. Add a real scarcity cue on each tile, such as "Low stock" or "Selling fast," or the date last year's version sold out.
- 3. Pick one headline offer, either Double Rewards or up to 30% off, and demote the other to a single line.
- 4. Move the 11/16 deadline from the thin top bar into the hero.
- 5. Put a "Shop all bestsellers" button right under the hero so the first click doesn't depend on a specific product.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a large, table-based XHTML Transitional build with about 97 tracked links. Its main confirmed issues are missing `alt` text on roughly 55 images, a zoom-blocking viewport tag, and a URL-heavy plain-text part. The header checks (unsubscribe, authentication) and the UTM/landing-page checks couldn't be confirmed from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverifiable links:** The QA run skipped all 97 tracking links (`97 tracking link(s) skipped`) because they are click-redirect domains. Destination status, redirect chains and 404s are untested.
- **Third-party pixels:** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `&n=5` are five identical ad-network pixels (rlcdn.com is LiveRamp's domain). They carry a per-recipient `s=` identifier. Each one adds a third-party request and shares a recipient ID outside the sender's domain.
- **Open tracking:** `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/9e3d8e7b-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` are tracking pixels. They are normal for this ESP setup, but they are flagged below for the alt-text check.
- **Plain-text bloat:** The plain-text part is 19,592 of 27,901 characters URLs (70%+, per the QA warning). It is mostly raw tracking links, which hurts readability and is a spam-filter signal.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, confirmed by QA:**
  - About 43 content images from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (e.g. `bf3053a9-…jpg`, `6e5bbd3c-…jpg`) and `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-….png`.
  - 7 dynamic recommendation images from `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`.
  - The tracking pixels `o.gif`, `448796.gif` ×5 and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…`.
  - For the content and recommendation images, a missing `alt` leaves the content unreadable with images blocked or a screen reader on. For the pixels, an empty `alt=""` is the correct fix, but there is no `alt` attribute at all.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 concern).
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` under an XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype should be `&amp;`.
- **Template leftovers:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` is an unfilled template placeholder. There are also duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (`telephone`, `address` and `date` are each declared more than once) and redundant charset declarations (`http-equiv` plus `<meta charset>`).
- The source is truncated, so I can't verify the MSO/Outlook conditional blocks beyond the head section, or the body table structure.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible HTML or QA data.
- The `mi_u=` value in the pixel and image URLs is a hashed recipient ID, so it is resolved. It also means `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images are per-recipient dynamic content.
- The only placeholder is the template comment noted in section 3, which is not a merge token.

## 5. Compliance
- **List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post:** The QA tool reports both headers as not found, with the note that the header "may not be captured by AgentMail relay". This is unconfirmed, not a verified defect. Verify against the raw message from the original sender, because RFC 8058 one-click is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found (expected via the relay). SPF, DKIM and DMARC status are unknown. The sender uses `mail.crateandbarrel.com` and image/tracking subdomains of `crateandbarrel.com`, which at least suggests aligned branding domains.
- The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm them either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be assessed. The QA probe skipped all 97 links as tracking redirects, and the truncated HTML doesn't expose the final destinations.
- The deliverable here is a gap: no destination URLs were resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to all content and product images, and `alt=""` (plus `width="1" height="1"`) to the tracking pixels `o.gif`, `448796.gif` ×5 and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/…`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Escape the title ampersand (`Crate &amp; Barrel`), and remove the `[IMPUT HERE …]` comment and the duplicate meta tags.
4. Consider consolidating the five `sr.rlcdn.com` pixels into one, or dropping them. They are redundant requests that share a recipient ID with a third party.
5. Shorten the plain-text part (target under 50% URLs), for example with shorter link text.
6. Re-run the audit with the raw original headers (or a direct-to-inbox capture) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
7. Re-run the link probe with redirect-following (or resolve the redirects) so the UTM parameters and landing pages can be checked.
