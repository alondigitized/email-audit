# Prepare to want it all
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's "new arrivals" mode — a seventh distinct creative approach this week, following heritage-editorial (8/10), sweepstakes (8/9), discount-stack (8/9), custom-upholstery (8/8), shoppable editorial (8/8), and the weak design-services pivot (8/7). "So Much New / Prepare to Want It All" is a clean, well-photographed product parade: sofa, dining table, pendant lights, vase, rugs, each with a named designer credit and a dedicated shop button. It's competent, on-brand, and completely offer-free.
- The execution is CB2's best visual craft this week — warm, consistent styling, no render bugs, clear per-category CTAs. But there's zero price incentive anywhere in the hero-to-rugs section; the only real hooks are a $1,000 app-download sweepstakes and a $1,000 review sweepstakes buried two-thirds down. For a subscriber who just saw genuine sale stacking on 8/9 ("THIS WEEK'S STEALS," 40-60% off), this reads as a step backward toward pure catalog browsing.
- Judgement: a nicely produced brand email that will underperform on clicks because it asks the reader to want things rather than giving them a reason to buy now.

## What's working

- Hero image and gold-ring product shot are elevated and on-brand, with a clear "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" CTA immediately visible.
- Consistent structure — each category (sofa, dining, lighting, decor, rugs) gets its own hero shot, product name, designer credit, and a distinct "SHOP NEW [CATEGORY]" button, making the email easy to scan and self-segment.
- Designer name-drops (David Girotti, Studio Anansi, David Aguirre) add a premium editorial touch without derailing the layout, echoing the "DESIGN LEGENDS" beat from 8/10.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, buttons are legible.
- "More For You" personalized product grid near the bottom with visible prices adds a secondary browsing path.

## What's weak

- No discount, price cut, or promo code anywhere in the entire new-arrivals section — after 8/9's "THIS WEEK'S STEALS" this feels like a step backward for anyone shopping on price.
- Six nearly identical "hero image + product name + designer + Shop button" blocks in a row is repetitive scrolling with no variation in pacing or format.
- The two real incentives in the email — $1,000 app-download sweepstakes and $1,000 review sweepstakes — are stacked as bare black bars near the bottom, easy to miss and visually disconnected from the rest of the design language.
- Subject "Prepare to want it all" is vague catalog-speak, not a concrete offer or product hook — weakest subject line signal of the week.
- Fourth+ CB2 creative mode in a matter of days (8/8, 8/8, 8/9, 8/9, 8/10, 8/11) risks fatigue; a subscriber opening daily has now seen sweepstakes, discounts, custom-upholstery, editorial, and now plain catalog — cadence is aggressive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an offer, not aspiration — pull one of the two $1,000 sweepstakes or a new-arrivals discount into the hero itself; "So Much New" alone won't beat 8/9's steals email on clicks.
- 2. Cut the six-block repetition to 3-4 categories max, or vary the layout (e.g., alternate full-width hero vs. two-up grid) to reduce scroll fatigue.
- 3. Promote the app-download and review sweepstakes higher, and redesign them to match the warm editorial palette instead of stark black bars that read as an afterthought.
- 4. Add at least one concrete price signal (e.g., "New arrivals from $299") to the hero so browsers become buyers.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The Aldine Sofa just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: download the app for a shot at $1,000`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Designed by David Girotti, Studio Anansi & more`

## Technical Audit

1. Technical Summary
CB2 promotional email built on a standard responsive MSO/VML template; QA scan passed 57% with 0 hard issues and 3 warning categories, concentrated in third-party ad pixels, missing alt text, and unverifiable auth/unsubscribe headers.

2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 69 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober (domain-based redirect links, not independently validated for 200 status).
- Multiple legacy Dotomi ad-serving pixels/redirects served over plaintext HTTP: `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?...`, and `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`. Mixed-content HTTP resources may be blocked or stripped by mail clients enforcing HTTPS-only image loading (e.g., Outlook, Gmail proxy), silently breaking that tracking.
- Primary open/click tracking pixels use HTTPS correctly: `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/...`.

- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` and `format-detection` meta tags present, correctly suppressing iOS auto-linking/reformatting.
