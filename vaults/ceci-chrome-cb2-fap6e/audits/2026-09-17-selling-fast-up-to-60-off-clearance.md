# Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- The subject line promises "up to 60% off clearance" but that offer is dead last in visual priority — the hero has it, then the email pivots to a Venus Williams collab, a baby-essentials sale, six random "we love these for you" products spanning cribs to activity chairs, a credit card pitch, an app download, and a Design Desk service ad. This is a kitchen-sink send wearing a clearance subject line as a hook. Compare to the 2026-09-16 "Spooky season starts NOW" email, which stayed disciplined around one category launch — this one can't decide what it's about.
- The hero itself is clean — daisy field, bold white "up to 60% off + new markdowns" text, clear "Shop Clearance" button — and the category nav grid right below it (Furniture, Rugs, Kids Bedding, Baby Bedding, Decor & Lighting, 40-60% Off Final Sale) is a genuinely useful click-through structure. But everything after that nav grid dilutes the clearance premise the subject line sold.
- Net: strong top, unfocused everywhere else. A subscriber who clicks in for clearance has to scroll past a collab, a baby sale, and a product grid with no discount context before finding anything else clearance-related.

## What's working

- Hero visual is legible and on-brand — clean daisy photography, high-contrast white text, offer stated plainly ("up to 60% off + new markdowns").
- The 6-tile category nav (Furniture, Rugs, Kids Bedding, Baby Bedding, Decor & Lighting, Final Sale) gives an immediate, low-friction path into the sale by category.
- Two products in the "we love these for you" grid show a visible strikethrough price ($1450→$1085, $1150→$1010), which supports the discount narrative.
- No broken images or overlapping text — every module renders cleanly.

## What's weak

- The subject says "clearance" but the second, third, and fourth modules (Venus Williams collab, baby essentials sale, unlabeled "we love these" grid) have nothing to do with clearance — it reads like three unrelated campaigns bolted together.
- Four of six "we love these for you" products show full price with no discount badge, so they visually contradict the clearance premise mid-scroll.
- No urgency mechanism beyond "selling fast" — no countdown, no date, no stock indicator to back up the claim.
- The Venus Williams collab and baby-sale banners get bigger visual real estate than the clearance offer they're supposedly secondary to, undermining hierarchy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one thesis: clearance. Drop or move the Venus Williams collab and baby-essentials sale to their own sends — they're diluting the exact offer the subject promised, and stacking three offers in one email trains subscribers to skim past all of them.
- 2. Add a real urgency signal near the hero — a specific end date or "while supplies last" tied to actual inventory, not just "selling fast."
- 3. Label every product tile in "we love these for you" with its clearance discount (or drop the non-discounted items entirely) so the grid doesn't contradict the subject line.
- 4. Move the category nav grid (Furniture/Rugs/Bedding/etc.) up to sit directly under the hero with zero modules in between — right now it's already adjacent, keep it that way if other cuts are made.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: up to 60% off kids clearance`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance markdowns just dropped — up to 60% off`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns added across furniture, bedding & decor`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop cribs, dressers & more before they're gone`
