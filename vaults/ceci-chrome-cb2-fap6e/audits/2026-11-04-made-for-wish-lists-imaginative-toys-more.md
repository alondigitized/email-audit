# Made for wish lists: imaginative toys & more
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a catalog send dressed up as a gift guide. It's about 9,450px tall and organized by age: babies, toddlers, kids. It uses the same structure as the 11-03 "Get inspired: new playroom picks" email, and the playroom email also had no offer in the hero. The top is clean. A warm, muted hero reads "screen-free, heirloom-quality gifts, made for wish lists" with a single "Shop All Gifts" button.
- The age-based split is the best idea here. Each tier gets a lifestyle image, three labeled products (Bestseller, Exclusive, Personalized) and its own button. The back half is where it loses the plot. A gift-bag promo, a "hundreds of new markdowns up to 60% off" clearance block, a "sign up, save 10%" block, a stroller and car-seat recommendation grid, a credit card promo, an app download, a Design Desk plug and a store locator all pile on after the toys. Roughly the last 40% is generic brand furniture, not wish-list toys. Judgment: a decent, on-brand holiday gift guide, but it has no offer up top and no real urgency. It also repeats the "everything everywhere" pattern this brand keeps sending.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: one headline, one button, and a warm lifestyle photo that fits the "heirloom" idea.
- The age segmentation (babies, toddlers, kids) lets a parent skip to their own child.
- Product badges like Bestseller, Exclusive and Personalized add a little social proof and scarcity at a glance.
- Each section ends with its own CTA, so there's always a button nearby.
- The tone is consistent and the seasonal timing is right for early November gifting.

## What's weak

- The hero has no offer, so there's no price or deadline reason to click now.
- The "we love these for you" strollers and car seats at the bottom don't match the toy and wish-list promise.
- The email is stacked with competing promos: clearance up to 60% off, 10% signup, credit card, app and Design Desk. The clearance block is the strongest offer and it's buried mid-email.
- The fine print and footer are dense and tiny, and the footer link row is crowded.
- The tight 3-up product tiles are small, with small text for names and prices, which is hard to read on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real offer or a deadline in the hero, such as a shipping cutoff or the markdown event. Move the "up to 60% off" block into the top third.
- 2. Cut the email after the kids' section. Drop the strollers, card, app and Design Desk blocks, or move them to a separate send.
- 3. Link products to specific pages and add review stars or "gift under $X" filters.
- 4. Make the product tiles bigger, two per row, so names and prices are readable on a phone.
- 5. Add a clear gift-by-price or ship-by-date line to give the urgency a credible basis.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Gifts for every age, plus up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heirloom-quality picks for babies, toddlers & kids, plus clearance up to 60% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery. Shop by age.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with a mobile media query at 460px. Two problems are confirmed: about 37 images have no alt text, and the head has several markup defects. The unsubscribe, authentication and link-destination checks could not be completed because headers were not captured and the HTML was truncated.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verified:** 77 links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they use tracking or click-redirect domains. Link health, redirect chains and destination status are unknown.
- **Third-party pixels:** `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1` through `n=5` are five separate LiveRamp pixels. Each is a third-party request and none has alt text.
- **First-party tracking images:** `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…` appears six times. `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/a238751a-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` also appear. The same `mi_u` identifier appears in the pixel URLs, so any forwarded copy exposes it.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, 24 content images.** Examples:
  - `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg` (about 20 files, e.g. `2be1e1ea-…jpg`, `0c255528-…jpg`)
  - `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`
  - `s7d5.scene7.com/…/2026%5F1104%5FCBK%5FQualityGifting%5FHeroVideo%5F2`
  - `s7d5.scene7.com/…/20261101%5FCBK%5FFall%5FClearance%5FPM%5Fv3%5FTert`
  
  With images blocked or read by a screen reader, the hero and product content is empty.
- **Missing alt text, 13 tracking pixels.** These are the rlcdn, `mi.` and `dv.` images above. They should carry `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"` so they are explicitly decorative.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, a WCAG 1.4.4 issue.
- **Focus outline removed:** `a { outline:0; }` removes the keyboard focus indicator on every link.
- **Invalid markup for the declared doctype:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype.
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` is not self-closed.
  - `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" …>` and `<meta charset>` are both present, so charset is declared twice.
  - `<meta name="format-detection">` appears three times, in one combined and two split forms.
- **Template leftovers:** the comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` (typo included) and an empty `<!---->` are still in the source. They add weight but are harmless to rendering.
- **Title mismatch:** `<title>` is "Crate & Barrel", not the subject line "Made for wish lists: imaginative toys & more". It does not affect inbox rendering, but it is a weak title for web-view and accessibility.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible HTML. No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRST_NAME]` appear in the truncated source. The rest of the body was not available.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe not found (WARN):** QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip it, so this is unconfirmed. If it is truly absent, it is a deliverability risk for Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (WARN):** the same relay caveat applies. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders under the Gmail and Yahoo 2024 rules.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status could not be checked. The sender is `crateandbarrelkids@mail.crateandbarrel.com`, and alignment on that subdomain is unverified.
- **Footer not assessed:** the physical postal address and the in-body unsubscribe link are outside the truncated source, so they could not be confirmed.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The tracking links were skipped and the truncated HTML does not show destination URLs or UTM parameters. No continuity defects are confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Add descriptive alt text** to the 24 content images. Start with the hero image (`…HeroVideo_2`) and the product tiles. Add `alt=""` to the 13 tracking pixels.
2. **Re-run the QA with raw `.eml` headers** so List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post and Authentication-Results can be evaluated. Until then, treat the three WARNs as unconfirmed.
3. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta and **restore a visible `:focus` style** in place of `a { outline:0 }`.
4. **Fix the head markup:** change `&` to `&amp;` in `<title>`, self-close `<meta charset>`, deduplicate the charset and format-detection metas, and delete the leftover `IMPUT HERE` comment.
5. **Resolve and follow a sample of the 77 tracking links** to confirm destinations and UTM carry-through, since the probe skipped them.
6. **Review the five `rlcdn.com` pixels.** Confirm they are intentional and covered in the privacy disclosure. One consolidated call may be enough.
