# 20% off ALL kids desks!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids shifting into promotional gear after a string of editorial-only sends (the July 16 "stripes lookbook" and "fairy tale style" sends had zero discounts). The subject is the brand's sharpest offer in at least a week — "20% off ALL kids desks" is concrete, timely (back-to-school desk season is real), and zero ambiguity. The hero delivers on that promise with a clean desk lifestyle shot and the offer headline front and center.
- The problem is what happens below the fold. Rather than staying focused on desks, the email quickly piles on "up to 30% off select storage," then "500+ new fall arrivals starting at $19," then what looks like a product grid and a stroller/baby-gear module. Each section is defensible in isolation but together they dilute the one thing the subject promised. A subscriber who opened specifically for the desk offer has to work to find it again.
- The overall send is solid — better than the past week's editorial-only cadence — but the kitchen-sink layout keeps it from being a great promotional email. A clean single-offer structure would lift click-through meaningfully.

## What's working

- **Subject-to-hero alignment is tight** — "20% off all kids desks" in the subject lands directly on a desk lifestyle image and matching headline. No bait-and-switch.
- **The offer is unambiguous** — 20% off a full category is simple and easy to act on; no "select styles," no asterisk hunt visible.
- **Timely seasonal hook** — pushing desks mid-July hits the back-to-school intent window right as parents start shopping; the "500+ new fall arrivals" callout reinforces that seasonality.
- **Secondary offer is stacked, not buried** — the storage module (up to 30% off) appears as a clear second section, not hidden in fine print.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **Too many offers, too soon** — desks → storage → fall arrivals → strollers is four distinct pitches in one email. The desk message is lost by the third scroll.
- **No visible deadline** — 20% off is compelling, but without "ends Sunday" or even "this week only," there is nothing to motivate acting today vs. next week.
- **Baby/stroller module feels off-brief** — the desk subscriber is likely shopping for school-age kids; stroller content signals the brand doesn't know which life stage this subscriber is at.
- **CTA buttons are small and numerous** — multiple low-contrast CTAs compete rather than funnel attention to a single action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to one hero offer.** Kill the stroller module entirely and trim fall arrivals to a single teaser row. The desk offer earned the open; give it the whole email.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** "20% off all kids desks — ends July 20" doubles urgency with zero additional cost. The prior week's editorial cadence means subscribers haven't seen a hard deadline from this brand in days.
- 3. **Make the primary CTA button larger and singular.** One prominent "Shop Kids Desks" button under the hero, then supporting CTAs below. Right now buttons compete at equal visual weight.
- 4. **Segment stroller content out.** Baby-gear and school-age-desk buyers are distinct cohorts; mixing them in one email signals poor list hygiene and wastes click opportunity on both segments.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kids desks are 20% off — stock up before school`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kid's dream desk is 20% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 30% off select storage. Ends July 20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The desk deal you've been waiting for — shop all sizes.`

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: MISSING**
Required for bulk senders under Gmail and Yahoo's February 2024 enforcement. Without it, inbox providers cannot surface the native unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaint risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058): MISSING**
One-click unsubscribe is mandatory for senders above 5,000/day to Gmail. The absence of this header means Gmail cannot execute machine-triggered unsubscribes, and the sender is out of compliance with current bulk sender policy.

**`Authentication-Results` header: NOT CAPTURED**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown — the AgentMail relay did not preserve this header. The sending domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` should have DKIM alignment; status cannot be confirmed from available data.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be verified from the truncated HTML, but are assumed present given this is a known commercial sender.
