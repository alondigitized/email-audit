# Have you seen these hot collabs yet?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' brand-collaboration editorial — an inspiration-first send that leads with "get inspired: our favorite collabs" rather than a price point. The email scrolls through several featured partnerships (what appear to be designer or brand collabs for kids' room furnishings), anchored by airy, well-lit lifestyle photography. There is no visible discount in the hero, and no deadline anywhere above the fold.
- Against the brand's recent history, this one stands out as a pure awareness/discovery play. The 06-07 Crate & Kids "free design consult" and "new furniture in 7 days" sends were also non-promotional, so this channel has been running an editorially-leaning week while Crate & Barrel proper does all the discounting. That's a coherent two-brand strategy — but the casual subject "Have you seen these hot collabs yet?" undersells what the email actually delivers.
- The execution is competent but not sharp. The photography is strong, the layout is clean, and there are no visible render issues. The miss is that "collabs" is deliberately vague, and without a concrete hook — a name, a product, a price — the email asks a lot of the reader before revealing its hand.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is beautiful and on-brand** — the full-bleed bedroom lifestyle image sets the aspiration clearly and matches Crate & Kids' light, airy visual identity.
- **Multiple collab sections create a sense of editorial depth** — feels like a curated lookbook, not a generic blast.
- **Consistent brand voice** — clean white space, restrained typography, no visual clutter or competing offers.
- **Layout renders cleanly** — no broken images, overlapping text, or obvious formatting failures visible in the render.

## What's weak

- **No price, discount, or offer of any kind** — the email asks for scroll and click commitment without any incentive signal; most recipients will bounce after the hero.
- **Subject is vague and non-specific** — "hot collabs" could mean designer collections, brand partnerships, or influencer rooms; the curiosity hook requires the reader to already care.
- **Hero CTA copy and button are not legible at mobile scale** — whatever the button says is too small to read, which undercuts the visual hierarchy.
- **Collab partner names are not clearly readable** — the value of a "collab" email is that the partner name carries pull; if those names don't scan at a glance, the selling point dissolves.
- **No urgency or time-bound signal anywhere visible** — pure editorial sends with no deadline require strong brand loyalty to convert.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the collab partners in the subject line** — if the partnerships are with recognizable brands or designers, lead with the name. That's the single change that lifts this most. "Our [Designer Name] x Crate & Kids collab is here" outperforms "hot collabs" every time.
- 2. **Add one concrete hook below the hero** — even a light nudge ("new arrivals, limited quantities") converts passive readers who aren't yet brand-loyal.
- 3. **Make CTA button copy specific** — "Shop the collab" with a named partner beats generic "Shop Now."
- 4. **Increase partner name typographic weight** — the collab names should be the largest, most scannable text in each section after the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Meet our newest collab: [Partner Name] × Crate & Kids`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: designer kids' rooms you haven't seen yet`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Exclusive collections from [Partner A], [Partner B], and more — shop now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our favorite collab pieces, now available in store and online`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — Prevents user zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` wildcard** — Disables OS-level text scaling on every element. Should be scoped to `body` and set to `100%`, not `none`:
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }  /* ← current, problematic */
```

**Inverted media query cascade for `img` max-width** — The wider breakpoint is declared first, so the narrower breakpoint's rule wins at all widths ≥640px, defeating the intended 600px constraint at desktop:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — overrides above */
```
At a 900px viewport both queries match; the 640px rule wins by cascade order, so images render at 640px instead of the intended 600px.

**`* { line-height: 100% }` + `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` globally** — Risks text clipping in Outlook for multi-line elements with descenders. Should not be applied to the wildcard selector.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — Combined declaration plus three individual declarations (telephone, address, date). Redundant, harmless, but indicates template hygiene issues.

**`<title>` brand mismatch** — Title reads "Crate & Barrel" but sending brand is "Crate & Kids." Matters for screen readers and browser tab display on web-view opens:
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
