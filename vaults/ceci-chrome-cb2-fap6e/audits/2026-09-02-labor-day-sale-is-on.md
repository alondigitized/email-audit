# LABOR DAY SALE IS ON
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the third near-identical "THE LABOR DAY SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" hero CB2 has sent in this stretch — same green backdrop, same velvet chair, same "SHOP THE SALE" button, just this time with a fully category-shopper-friendly nav below it (Furniture, Outdoor, Lighting, Rugs, Decor & Mirrors, Entertaining). The subject "LABOR DAY SALE IS ON" is at least honest this time — no false-specificity bait like "this best-selling lamp" or "exclusive designs" that didn't deliver, which has burned CB2 in three of the last five sends per the brand history. That's the good news.
- The bad news is cadence fatigue: this is the fourth or fifth Labor Day promo email in a week, and the render offers zero new reason to click versus yesterday's or the day before's. The category grid is a genuinely useful structural device, but it's decoration on a message this subscriber has already seen (and priced out) multiple times. The Double Rewards card push at the bottom is a stronger, more differentiated offer than the sale itself at this point.
- Judgement: competent, on-brand, honest subject — but it's redundant creative in an oversaturated send window, and does nothing to earn a click from someone who's already seen 3+ nearly identical Labor Day emails this week.

## What's working

- Subject line matches the hero exactly — no bait-and-switch this time, a real improvement over "This best-selling lamp" (2026-08-30, 5/10) and "Exclusive designs" (2026-08-31, 5/10).
- Clean category navigation grid (Furniture, Outdoor, Lighting, Rugs, Decor & Mirrors, Entertaining) gives shoppers a real way to jump straight to their interest instead of a single flat CTA.
- Two distinct CTAs are visible and unambiguous: "SHOP THE SALE" (top and bottom) and "LEARN MORE" for the credit card offer.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together top to bottom.
- Clear urgency/date stamp on the rewards card offer ("NOW THROUGH SEPT 9TH, 2026").

## What's weak

- This is essentially a repeat of 2026-09-01's "Keep-forever decor" and 2026-08-31's "Exclusive designs" hero — same green scene, same chair, same "UP TO 50% OFF" copy block, just relabeled. A subscriber who opened those gets nothing new here.
- No single hero product or must-have item is called out — it's a pure category sale, which the brand history shows scores lower than editorial or single-product sends (compare "That's so '70s" at 8/10).
- The Double Rewards credit-card offer — arguably the most concrete, differentiated value prop in the whole email (20% back through Sept 9) — is buried at the very bottom after a long scroll through category tiles.
- "UP TO 50% OFF" with no minimum spend, category-specific tiers, or promo code visible leaves the actual savings vague until click-through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Break the Labor Day creative rut — swap the hero for a single named product or curated "editor's pick" collection instead of another generic "up to 50% off" banner; this is the difference between the 8/10 "'70s" send and the 5-6/10 generic sale sends.
- 2. Move the Double Rewards card teaser up near the hero (or split it into its own send) — it's dated, specific (20% back), and distinct from the sale fatigue.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the main sale banner itself (the rewards card has "Sept 9th" but the sale hero has none) — urgency should anchor the primary offer, not just the secondary one.
- 4. Reduce cadence — this brand has sent near-identical Labor Day promos five times in three days per history; consolidate into fewer, higher-signal sends.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Labor Day picks are 50% off — through Sept 9`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, earn double rewards on every purchase this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, lighting, rugs & more — all on sale now.`

# CB2 "LABOR DAY SALE IS ON" — Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard MI/CB2 responsive template with correct MSO/Outlook conditional handling, but ships with several third-party ad-tracking pixels served over unencrypted HTTP and lacks required unsubscribe/authentication headers at the transport layer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 60 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by automated HTTP probing (redirect domains not resolvable via static probe) — cannot confirm destination validity without manual click-through testing.
- Multiple third-party ad pixels route through `dotomi.com` (Dotomi/Aki Technologies cookie-sync/retargeting):
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php` (10 sequential cookie-redirect pixels)
- All of the above `dotomi.com` pixels use **plain HTTP**, not HTTPS — flagged as "Non-HTTPS source may be blocked" by mail clients (Gmail/Outlook proxy image loading and may drop or flag insecure image requests, degrading tracking accuracy and potentially triggering mixed-content warnings in webmail preview panes).
- First-party tracking pixels (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/...`) correctly use HTTPS.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 9 content images missing `alt` text (product/lifestyle images under `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/...`) — screen readers and image-blocked clients will render blank space with no fallback text.
- All tracking/ad pixels (Dotomi UCMController, pub1–pub10.php, o.gif, dv.cb2.com/o) also missing `alt` — expected/benign for 1x1 pixels but flagged for completeness.
- Template includes proper Outlook MSO conditionals (`<!--[if mso]>`), `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and mobile media queries (`max-width: 418px`) — no structural rendering issues found in the visible source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens or personalization placeholders visible in the truncated HTML source. No issues found.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — CAN-SPAM/RFC 2369 compliance cannot be confirmed at the header level; may only exist as an in-body unsubscribe link (not visible in truncated source) or may be genuinely absent from the relay capture.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules as of Feb 2024) cannot be confirmed as supported.
- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this capture; this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay stripping headers rather than a sender-side failure, so it should not be treated as confirmed non-authentication.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot assess — click-redirect links were skipped by the automated probe (60 tracking links unresolved), so destination URLs, UTM parameters, and landing-page alignment could not be verified from this data. Manual click-through testing is needed to confirm continuity.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Migrate the 11 Dotomi ad-sync pixels (`login.dotomi.com`, `ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1–10.php`) to HTTPS endpoints to prevent silent drops under mixed-content/image-blocking policies in modern mail clients.
2. Add `alt=""` (empty, decorative) to the 9 content images at minimum, and descriptive `alt` text to any that convey offer/product information not otherwise present as text.
3. Verify List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers exist at the raw SMTP level (outside the relay capture) — required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance; if genuinely absent, add both headers.
4. Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication independently of this relay (e.g., via mail-tester.com or direct MTA log) since Authentication-Results wasn't captured here.
5. Manually click-test a sample of the 60 tracking links to confirm final landing pages carry correct UTM parameters and resolve to live CB2 Labor Day sale pages.
