# It’s back. And now it’s on sale.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "It's back. And now it's on sale." is CB2's attempt at a teaser-style subject line break from their recent blunt-offer drumbeat — but the tease misfires. You're now seven-plus sends deep into the CB2 sale cycle, and a mystery subject competes against inbox fatigue rather than cutting through it. A subscriber who saw the June 25 Floor Sample Sale stack and the June 25 July 4th relaunch already knows something is on sale; they need a reason *this* email is different, not a riddle.
- Inside the email, the creative is cleaner than recent sends. The hero finally commits to a single lead message — "THE JULY 4TH SALE / UP TO 50% OFF / & IN STOCK" — and the "in stock" angle is the genuinely smart move here: it signals to anyone burned by backordered items that these specific products are actually available now. The category tiles (Living Room, Bedroom, Accent Tables) reinforce that hook efficiently.
- The problem is the email doesn't stay focused. The Warehouse Sale block (60% off) reappears below the fold, recreating the exact two-offer confusion from the June 25 Floor Sample Sale triple-stack. One message, one offer, one CTA would make this email significantly more powerful.

## What's working

- **"IN STOCK" as hero differentiator** — pinpointing currently-available inventory is the most meaningful thing CB2 has added to this sale cycle, and the email leads with it correctly.
- **Category tiles are clean and scannable** — Living Room / Bedroom / Accent Tables give the shopper a fast path without forcing them to browse a full catalog.
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — cream/warm lifestyle hero, clear headline, dark "SHOP NOW" button; the eye moves naturally.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product grid** — personalized product recommendations at the bottom add a useful low-friction secondary entry point.
- **No obvious render bugs** — clean layout across the visible render, no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Subject line abandons specificity at exactly the wrong moment** — after six-plus sale emails, "It's back. And now it's on sale." creates curiosity but delivers no incentive to open over the competition in a crowded inbox.
- **Dual-offer stack returns** — the Warehouse Sale (60% off) block below the fold undermines the July 4th hero; same problem as the June 25 three-offer send.
- **"SHOP EVERYWHERE" CTA is meaningless** — it sits between the category tiles and the Warehouse Sale section with no clear context; "everywhere" isn't a destination.
- **No deadline in the body** — "July 4th Sale" implies urgency but no explicit end date is visible; the clock isn't running.
- **60% warehouse vs. 50% July 4th creates cognitive dissonance** — the bigger discount is lower in the email but could actually be the stronger offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to deliver the "in stock" payoff** — that's the actual news; the mystery tease is a missed opportunity. The subject should tell people *what* is back.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back in stock — and 50% off for July 4th`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sold-out stuff is back. 50% off this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Living room, bedroom + accent tables — available now, on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Missed it the first time? It's here, it's 50% off, and it won't last.`
- 2. **Kill the Warehouse Sale block or consolidate** — if both promotions must appear, frame them as one tiered offer ("up to 60% off") with a single CTA, not two separate blocks with competing headlines.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP EVERYWHERE" with a category-specific CTA** — "SHOP LIVING ROOM" or "SHOP IN-STOCK PICKS" gives the click a destination; "everywhere" tells the reader nothing.
- 4. **Add an explicit sale-end date** — even "through July 4th" in the hero increases urgency meaningfully without requiring a redesign.
- 5. **Surface the 60% figure in the hero** — if warehouse sale items qualify, lead with the maximum discount and explain the range; 60% beats 50% and the current structure buries the better offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Unremoved template artifact in `<head>`**

Typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") and placeholder was never replaced. Harmless to rendering but confirms the template was not fully sanitized before send.

**[MEDIUM] `@font-face` weight conflict — "Lineto Brown Sub" / weight 400 declared twice with different source files**
```css
/* Declaration 1 — resolves to BrownLight */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2");
  font-weight: 400; }

/* Declaration 2 — resolves to BrownRegular */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2");
  font-weight: 600; }
```
Wait — weight 600 on second declaration is fine. But cross-referencing the first block:
```css
/* Earlier block — no weight declared, catches ALL weights */
@font-face { font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub";
  src: url(".../BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.eot") ... }
```
Three `@font-face` rules share the same `font-family` name with no weight on the first, then 400 and 600 on the next two. The weightless declaration will shadow or conflict depending on the client's CSS cascade. Weight-400 text may resolve to Regular instead of Light.

**[MEDIUM] Double-slash in font URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Four font `src` URLs use `cb2.com//Styles/` (double slash). Most CDNs normalize this silently, but it is a latent 404 risk if the origin is strict about path resolution.

**[LOW] `font-display: swap` applied inconsistently**
The first two `@font-face` blocks omit `font-display`; the last four include it. In email clients that honor `@font-face` (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail), the first two fonts will block render until loaded rather than swapping to fallback.

**[LOW] Mobile breakpoint mismatch**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
```
Breakpoint fires at ≤418px but forces a fixed 414px width — functional, but non-standard. iPhone 14/15 Pro (430px logical width) will not trigger this breakpoint and will not receive the `.mobile` width reset.
