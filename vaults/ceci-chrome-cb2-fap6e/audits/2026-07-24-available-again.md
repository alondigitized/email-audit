# AVAILABLE AGAIN
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- "AVAILABLE AGAIN" is a curiosity bait — the subject line sets up an expectation of specific back-in-stock products, but the body delivers what is plainly another Furniture Event multi-category blast, structurally identical to the July 23 "THE FURNITURE EVENT IS ON" send. The "SEIZE the Moment" hero is fresh creative with a moody bedroom tableau that looks good, but it answers no question the subject raised. A subscriber who opened expecting to see a coveted piece returned to stock will find room vignettes and a $5,000 design refresh offer they've seen at least twice this week.
- The photography is CB2 at its best — warm, aspirational, editorial. The section-by-section category breakdown (SHOP BEDROOM, SHOP SECTIONALS, SHOP SIDEBOARD/BAR) is logical and shoppable. The "MADE FOR YOU" personalized grid at the bottom is useful. But none of it is connected to the back-in-stock promise in the subject.
- This is the fifth CB2 email in three days. "AVAILABLE AGAIN" reads like a subject-line trick to prop up opens on a send that would otherwise repeat the July 23 Furniture Event email at a lower rate. That works once; it trains unsubscribes over time.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is strong.** The moody, low-lit bedroom scene in "SEIZE the Moment" is the best creative in this week's CB2 batch — more editorial, less sale-rack.
- **Category CTAs are honest and specific.** "SHOP BEDROOM," "SHOP SECTIONALS," "SHOP SIDEBOARD/BAR" give the reader a clear next step without vague "DISCOVER" language.
- **MADE FOR YOU grid adds personalization signal.** The bottom product reel shows individual items and creates a secondary engagement layer if the hero section doesn't convert.
- **No render bugs.** The email renders cleanly across all visible sections — no broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body mismatch is the whole problem.** "AVAILABLE AGAIN" promises back-in-stock product discovery; the body delivers a Furniture Event blast. There is no callout — not a badge, not a hero module, not a product label — connecting to "what came back."
- **Fifth email in three days; the $5,000 refresh is back again.** The design services banner has appeared in the July 21, July 23, and now July 24 sends. Subscribers are habituating to it as wallpaper.
- **No offer in the hero.** "SEIZE the Moment" is evocative but says nothing about price, availability, or event terms — which matters when the July 22 double-send already burned the "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" urgency. What's the current state of the sale?
- **All-caps subject in a week of all-caps CB2 subjects.** "AVAILABLE AGAIN" stacked behind "THE FURNITURE EVENT IS ON" and "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" all-caps reads as shouting in an inbox where CB2 already shouts a lot.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject true in the body.** If "AVAILABLE AGAIN" is the hook, the hero must open with a specific back-in-stock callout — a grid of 3–4 best-sellers with a "BACK IN STOCK" badge, low-inventory messaging ("Only 4 left"), and a direct product link. One sentence: "The [sofa name] sold out in two days. It's back — for now." That is a different email from what was sent.
- 2. **If keeping the Furniture Event creative, change the subject.** The body earned a subject like "The Furniture Event: new arrivals + bestsellers" — not a bait-and-switch back-in-stock hook.
- 3. **Retire the $5,000 refresh module for at least five sends.** It's been in every furniture event email this week. Give it a dedicated send — which CB2 did well on July 21 with "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US." Recycling it as filler devalues both the design service and the email containing it.
- 4. **Drop to sentence case for this subject.** "Available again" reads calmer and stands out more in a CB2 inbox full of caps — paradoxically more likely to catch the eye.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back in stock: the pieces everyone waited for`
- **Subject Alt B:** `It sold out. It's back. Act fast.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three CB2 bestsellers just returned — limited quantities.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The [Hero Product Name] is available again. Here's what else came back.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before `<body>` content; no `<a href>` tags are visible in the provided source. Confirm UTM parameters and click-tracking wrapper on all links before send.

**Font URLs with double-slash paths** — three `@font-face` declarations use `//` in the path:

```
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```

Double slashes typically trigger a 301 redirect, adding latency for clients that load web fonts (Apple Mail, Gmail web). The earlier `BrownRegular` declaration uses single slashes correctly — the `//` variants appear to be copy-paste errors.

**Cross-domain font dependency** — `HNW05` font (4 variants) loads from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. This creates a hard dependency on a sibling brand's domain. If that domain's CDN or CORS policy changes, fonts silently break in CB2 emails without any CB2-side alert.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unresolved template placeholder left in production:**

```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```

This is a build-time comment ("IMPUT" is a misspelling of "INPUT") that was not removed before send. Harmless to rendering but indicates the production template was not cleaned.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on the `*` selector:**

```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```

Using `none` (rather than `100%`) on a universal selector disables the iOS/Safari text-size adjustment for every element. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) is violated — users who rely on system font scaling will get no adjustment. The `body` rule correctly uses `100%`; the `*` override undoes it.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — fails WCAG 1.4.4. This is a common legacy pattern but should be `maximum-scale=5` or removed.

**Redundant `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — declared three times with overlapping weight mappings (`400` appears twice with different `src`). Browsers resolve by last-defined rule; the earlier `.eot`-based declaration for legacy IE is effectively dead code given the later woff2-only redeclarations.

**`alt` attributes** — not visible in truncated source; must be verified. All product images require descriptive `alt` text; decorative spacers should use `alt=""`.
