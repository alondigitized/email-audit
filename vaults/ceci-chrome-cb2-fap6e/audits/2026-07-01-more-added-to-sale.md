# MORE ADDED TO SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's ninth-plus consecutive sale send — and notably the **second email arriving on July 1 alone**, following the "Up to 50% off furniture & more" send from earlier today (audited this session, scored 8/10). "MORE ADDED TO SALE" is the weakest subject in the entire streak. An inventory refresh announcement is not a hook. The email body is competent: a clean 50%-off hero, the now-familiar category grid (OUTDOOR / FURNITURE / RUGS / LIGHTING / DECOR / TABLETOP), and a "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product tile section that's the only genuinely differentiated element from the morning send.
- The structural problem is that "more" is not an offer. Subscribers who opened the first July 1 email have zero new information in the subject to justify opening a second one. The body never names what was added — a category, a collaboration, a price cut tier — so even inside the email the "more" hook resolves to nothing.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" product tiles are the email's actual workhorse. Specific items, visible prices, direct links — that section does what the headline can't. But it's buried below a category grid that's been photographed and placed identically across three-plus previous sends. This email wins on body execution, loses on subject, and gets crushed by its own brand's cadence.

## What's working

- Clear **50% off hero** occupies the top of the email without scrolling — offer is immediately legible
- **Category grid shortcuts** (OUTDOOR / FURNITURE / RUGS / LIGHTING / DECOR / TABLETOP) serve intent-driven shoppers who know what they want
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalized product tiles** with product names and prices visible — the most clickable, most differentiated element in the send
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA** is visually unambiguous — dark button, strong contrast against the light background
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem-ipsum artifacts

## What's weak

- **"MORE ADDED TO SALE" reveals nothing** — no category, no brand name, no price tier; weakest subject of the entire streak
- **Double-sending on July 1** burns goodwill fast; a subscriber who opened the morning email has been given no new reason to open this one
- The hero graphic uses **"MORE" as a decorative typographic element** — it looks like a concept piece, not a sale update
- **No explicit deadline in the hero** — "JULY 4TH SALE" names the event but doesn't say "ends July 4" anywhere visible; the urgency is implied, not stated
- The **category grid is visually identical** to the June 28 WAREHOUSE SALE, June 29 SALE OF THE SUMMER, and June 30 NEW JUST IN sends — the template is showing

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name what was added.** The subject must surface the specific category or product: "Outdoor, lighting & rugs just hit 50% off" is infinitely stronger than "MORE ADDED TO SALE." If you can't name it, don't send the refresh email.
- 2. **Don't double-send on the same day.** Merge this into the morning send as an "also dropping today" section, or hold it for July 2 as a deadline-urgency send. Two promotional emails in one day from a brand that has already sent eight consecutive sale emails is a churn accelerant.
- 3. **Promote "MORE FOR YOU" above the category grid.** The personalized product tiles are doing more work than the hero. Move them up.
- 4. **Add an explicit "Sale ends July 4" line** to the hero — even a one-line "Offer ends July 4 at midnight" below the CTA button converts the implied deadline into a reason to act today.
- 5. **Replace "SHOP THE SALE" with a CTA that reflects the new-addition framing** — "SHOP WHAT'S NEW" or "SEE NEW ARRIVALS" — so clicking feels purposeful rather than duplicative of the morning send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just added to sale: outdoor, rugs & lighting — ends July 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New to 50% off: [category] + more for the long weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `These just dropped in price — sale wraps July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh additions to the July 4th sale. See what's new.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Production template comment left in**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") and unfilled placeholder shipped to recipients. Indicates incomplete template hygiene.

**[MEDIUM] Double-slash paths in font `src` URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Double slashes appear on three separate `@font-face` declarations. While most servers normalize these, it is a defect that can fail on strict CDN configurations.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure; also flagged by Apple accessibility guidelines.

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `line-height` rule**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Applied globally via `*` selector. In email clients that do not fully sandbox CSS, this bleeds into elements where relative `line-height` collapses text — particularly buttons and multi-line headings.

**[LOW] Mobile breakpoint hardcoded to 418px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
Targets legacy iPhone 6/7/8 width exactly. Current standard phones (375–430px viewport) and Android devices above 418px receive desktop layout. Industry standard breakpoint is 600px.

**[LOW] Cross-brand CDN dependency for HNW05 font**
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-...woff2")
```
Four `HNW05` `@font-face` declarations load from `crateandbarrel.com`. CB2 email rendering silently depends on a sibling brand's CDN uptime and cache headers. Any crateandbarrel.com CDN change or CORS policy shift breaks this font with no CB2-side control.

**[LOW] "Lineto Brown Light Sub" missing woff2 source**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` declared; `.woff2` (supported by all modern clients) is absent for the Light weight. A separate rule lower in the stylesheet loads Light as a weight variant of "Lineto Brown Sub", which may cause inconsistent font resolution depending on client cascade order.
