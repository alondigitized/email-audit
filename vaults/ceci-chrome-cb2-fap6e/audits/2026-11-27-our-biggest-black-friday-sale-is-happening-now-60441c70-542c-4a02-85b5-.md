# Our biggest Black Friday sale is happening now!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel Kids' fourth or fifth Black Friday send in 48 hours. It's a full catalog dump. The hero is clear: "biggest sale of the year! up to 50% off Black Friday + free shipping", with a "Shop All Deals" button directly under it. After that it runs through about ten category tiles (furniture, toys, bedding, play furniture, rugs, baby gear, decor, holiday, Babyletto/Namesake), each with its own percent-off headline and a "Shop Now" button.
- The render is heavily downscaled (113px wide, scaled from 680×12000), so I can read the headlines and layout but not the fine print. The photography is warm and on-brand. The repetition is the problem. On the same day the brand already sent "Our biggest sale of the year is HERE!" and the kitchen send, and this one nearly repeats that subject and offer. The subject, "Our biggest Black Friday sale is happening now!", is generic and reads like a duplicate.
- Overall it's competent execution of a tired formula. The offer is honest and easy to scan, but it's a very long scroll with no single hero product and no new reason to open or click.

## What's working

- The hero states the offer plainly: "up to 50% off Black Friday + free shipping".
- Each tile has a bold percent-off headline and its own "Shop Now" button, so the layout is easy to scan.
- The "Black Friday" ribbon tag on each image keeps the campaign consistent and seasonal.
- The lifestyle photography (nursery, holiday stockings, play chairs) matches the parent audience and the season.
- The "free shipping" tile near the bottom reinforces the value close to the end.

## What's weak

- At about 12,000px tall, the email is an extremely long scroll with ten-plus near-identical tiles and no priority among them.
- It's a near-duplicate of earlier sends today and on Thanksgiving ("Our biggest sale of the year is HERE!"), so cadence fatigue is likely.
- Most tiles use the same "Select styles" qualifier, which makes the discounts feel hedged. The ranges also drop from 50% to 30% to 20% with no hint of which tile is best.
- The bottom section adds clutter. It has a Design Desk promo, a credit card promo, an app download block and a "Book a Free Appointment" button, and each one competes with the sale CTAs.
- The text on the image tiles is small and low-contrast in places, for example the "the personalization..." banner near the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the top 4–5 tiles by revenue, or lead with a single hero product and a deadline. Drop the app, credit card and Design Desk blocks from a sale send.
- 2. Add real urgency. State the end time ("Ends Monday 11:59 PM PT") instead of just "now".
- 3. Reorder the tiles so the deepest discount (50% off) comes first, and lead with the strongest category for this audience, such as nursery or play furniture.
- 4. Make the hero button specific ("Shop Kids Furniture") rather than the generic "Shop All Deals".
- 5. Space out sends. A single morning send plus one late-day "last chance" beats four near-identical blasts.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is built on a standard table-based XHTML template. The only confirmed defects are about 39 `<img>` tags with no alt attribute and a few head-level markup problems. The compliance and authentication checks are inconclusive because the relay didn't capture the headers, and the HTML source was truncated before the footer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click links not verified:** 80 links were skipped because they go through tracking or click-redirect domains. Their destinations, UTM parameters and HTTP status are unconfirmed.
- **Third-party pixel stack (confirmed in QA data):**
  - Five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa2…&n=1` through `n=5` requests.
  - `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…`
  - `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/474990b1-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`
- **Hashed identifier sent to a third party:** the `s=` value on the rlcdn.com pixels looks like a 64-character hex string, probably a SHA-256 of the recipient's address. It matches the `mi_u=56aaa2787a67…` value on the first-party pixel. If so, a stable hashed identifier goes to a third-party domain on every open.
- **Alt text on pixels:** none of these pixels has alt text. See section 3.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** QA flags about 39 images with no alt attribute.
  - 31 are content images, for example `…/m/47/bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png`, 28 more under `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|gif`, and the scene7 image `2026%5F1105%5FCBK%5FPersonalizedGiftShop%5FSeconda…`.
  - 8 are tracking pixels (`rlcdn.com` ×5, `o.gif`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com`). These should carry `alt=""` plus `width="1" height="1"`.
  - The alt text is absent rather than empty. With images blocked, which is common in Outlook, or with a screen reader, the content images carry no text.
  - The QA output was cut off, so the real count may be higher.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom in clients that honor it. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Unescaped ampersand:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` should be `&amp;` under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional doctype. The title is also generic and doesn't reflect the subject line.
- **Redundant head markup:**
  - Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` and `<meta charset>` are present.
  - `format-detection` is declared three times with overlapping values.
  - Neither is harmful, but they are template cruft.
- **Placeholder left in:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` and an empty `<!---->` remain in production HTML.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved merge tokens such as `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `[FIRST_NAME]` appear in the truncated HTML.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post:** both were not found. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed, not a confirmed sender defect. The headers need to be checked on a direct-delivery copy. Without them, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender one-click unsubscribe requirements (RFC 8058) can't be confirmed.
- **Authentication-Results:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC are unknown. Alignment is plausible, since the From domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` matches the image and tracking hosts, but it is unverified.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** these couldn't be checked because the HTML source was truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
This can't be assessed. All 80 links were skipped as tracking redirects, and no final landing URLs or UTM parameters were resolved. The only parameters visible are the tracking-pixel IDs `mi_cid` and `mi_mid`.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 31 content images, and `alt=""` with explicit 1×1 dimensions to the 8 tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Change the title to `Crate &amp; Barrel` and use the campaign name. Remove the `IMPUT HERE` placeholder comment and the duplicate meta tags.
4. Capture the raw message, not the relay copy, to verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
5. Resolve the redirect chains of a sample of the 80 tracking links to confirm UTM parameters and landing pages.
6. Confirm that the `s=` hash on the rlcdn.com pixels matches the privacy policy and consent basis.
7. Re-run the audit on the full HTML rather than the truncated copy, so the footer unsubscribe link and postal address can be checked.
