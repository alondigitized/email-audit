# Homepage snapshot · www.cb2.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- CB2 knows exactly who I am — this is literally my store — but the second I landed, a modal hijacked the entire screen and made me choose between surrendering my phone number or clicking "CONTINUE WITHOUT SAVING" in what feels like a shame spiral. Under all that, there's a Memorial Day Sale hero doing real work: clean type, sculptural editorial imagery, a genuine 50% off headline. The brand is pushing the sale hard with layered incentives, but the gating mechanism is the story right now.

## What's working

- **"MEMORIAL DAY SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" hero** — the sale headline is bold, readable, and gives me an actual number. I came here to shop the sale and the sale is right there.
- **DOUBLE REWARDS top banner** — I have a CB2 account. Seeing double points layered on top of a 50% sale makes me do math in my head immediately. That's engagement.
- **Sculptural editorial hero image** — the organic, oversized tree form against warm sandy tones is very CB2's current aesthetic direction. It's not a product shot; it's an invitation to a world, which is what I respond to.
- **"THE COOLEST PEOPLE WE KNOW" section** — even just the headline visible below fold is enough to make me want to scroll. It feels like a collab or editorial drop, which I'll tap on immediately.
- **Navigation bar** — Furniture, Outdoor, Lighting, Rugs, Bedding & Bath all visible in one row. I can find my velvet sofa category in one tap.

## What's weak

- **Required phone number in the modal** — you want my email AND my mobile number just to enter the site? That's aggressive. The "required" label on both fields reads as shakedown, not welcome.
- **The modal's fine-print block** — the legal disclaimer under the form fields is four lines of dense 8px text. I'm 33, not a contracts lawyer. If I have to squint to know what I'm agreeing to, trust erodes fast.
- **"CONTINUE WITHOUT SAVING" as the escape** — framing opting out as "without saving" is the kind of dark-pattern language that makes me feel punished for not handing over my data. It's a sour note for a brand I actually like.
- **No urgency signal on the sale** — "MEMORIAL DAY SALE" implies a deadline but I see no end date, no "ends Sunday," no countdown. The offer floats without a hook that makes me act now versus bookmark for later.
- **Free shipping threshold MIA** — CB2 ships furniture and large items; knowing the threshold upfront would push me toward adding that side table to hit free delivery. Not visible anywhere above fold.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the required-phone-number modal with an email-only gate** — or make phone optional and relabel the escape to "Skip for now." The friction is costing conversions from warm visitors who already typed in cb2.com.
- **Add an explicit sale end date to the hero** — swap "UP TO 50% OFF" for "UP TO 50% OFF — ENDS MONDAY" in a contrasting badge. Same visual real estate, 10x more urgency.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold in the top banner alongside the DOUBLE REWARDS callout** — something like "Free shipping on orders $75+ · Double Rewards through Monday." That banner earns its space.
- **Pin a "New Drops" or "Just Landed" chip on the hero CTA** — CB2 shoppers hit the sale AND the new arrivals in the same session. A secondary CTA below "SHOP THE SALE" for new arrivals would capture both intent types without a redesign.
