# Psst… something new just hit up to 60% off!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth clearance- or warehouse-sale-adjacent email Crate & Barrel (and its sister brand Crate & Kids) has sent in five days. That cadence context is everything: by June 17 the "up to 60% off" headline has been sitting in subscribers' inboxes since June 13, and the 4th of July Warehouse Sale already launched this same morning with the June 17 "Starts today!" send. What saves this email from being pure noise is the subject-line gambit — "something new just hit" reframes the send not as a reminder but as a fresh inventory drop. That's a smart editorial choice in an exhausted sale week.
- The execution inside the email is competent and consistent with the C&B brand. The hero delivers the discount clearly, the multi-product grid gives multiple category entry points, and the "We Chose These for You" personalization module is a genuine differentiator. But the buried placement of that personalization block, combined with the fourth-or-fifth "60% off" hero in a row, blunts its impact. This is a solid promotional email running in a bad cadence position.
- Clear verdict: the email itself earns its keep; the campaign schedule around it does not.

## What's working

- The "Psst… something new just hit" hook is the right creative decision for day five of a sale — it implies new inventory rather than resending the same urgency.
- "Up to 60% off" is prominently anchored in the hero with no ambiguity; the offer is visible without scrolling.
- Multi-product grid covers rugs, decor, furniture, and bedding — broad category representation gives diverse subscribers a reason to click regardless of their room focus.
- "We Chose These for You" personalization module adds subscriber-specific relevance that the broadcast sale emails lack.
- Clean visual hierarchy: logo → hero offer → product grid → personalization — no layout confusion or render issues visible.

## What's weak

- "Something new" is fatally vague — the curiosity hook collapses if the subscriber doesn't know whether it's outdoor furniture, rugs, or kitchen accessories that dropped.
- No explicit deadline in the subject or hero; "4th of July" is implied by the campaign name but a real date ("Ends July 4") would sharpen urgency substantially.
- The personalization module ("We Chose These for You") is below the fold — the single strongest tool against sale fatigue is buried where most mobile subscribers won't scroll.
- Cadence is visibly broken: this lands on the same day as the "Starts today! Up to 60% off the 4th of July Warehouse Sale" send, making two separate "up to 60% off" emails from the same brand on June 17 alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the category in the subject.** "Something new" is the problem. "Psst… new rugs + outdoor just hit 60% off" earns the open on day five of a sale blitz; vagueness does not.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** An "Ends July 4" stamp or a simple countdown module turns the implied urgency into a concrete reason to act today rather than later.
- 3. **Move personalization above the product grid.** "We Chose These for You" should be the second thing subscribers see, not the fourth. On a day when two identical-looking "60% off" emails arrive from the same sender, a personalized block is the tie-breaker.
- 4. **Deduplicate the send schedule.** Sending both the "Starts today!" warehouse sale launch and a "something new just hit" clearance email on the same day cannibalizes both. Segment the latter to recent browsers and cart-abandoners; rest the full list.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — blocks user pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` reset** — `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` applies to every element, overriding OS-level font scaling for low-vision users.

**Forced light-mode only** — both `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` are hardcoded to `light`. Dark-mode email clients (Gmail Android, Apple Mail) will invert or override colors unpredictably without a `dark` variant or explicit dark-mode overrides.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules** — two `min-width` media queries set `img {max-width}` at incompatible values. The 640px breakpoint is declared *after* the 768px one, so in cascade order the 640px rule wins at all desktop widths. The `max-width: 600px` rule is dead code:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* never applied */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* overrides above */
```

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no` and `address=no` appear in the combined tag *and* again as separate tags, producing three redundant declarations:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```

**Alt text on images** — cannot verify; `<img>` tags not present in truncated source. Must confirm all product and promotional images carry descriptive `alt` attributes.
