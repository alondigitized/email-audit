# Rugs on sale. So worth it.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 finally narrowing the Labor Day formula to a specific category — rugs — instead of the generic "up to 50% off" everything-blast that's run repeatedly this stretch (2026-09-02 "LABOR DAY SALE IS ON," 2026-09-03 "More added"). The hero is a genuinely nice close-up rug texture shot with a countdown timer, which is new for this sequence and gives it a reason to exist beyond just another sale reminder. But after that hero, it immediately sprawls into a six-category "MORE ON SALE THIS WAY" grid (furniture, outdoor, lighting, decor, entertaining) that undercuts the rug focus entirely, then piles on a Double Rewards credit-card push and a separate 60% clearance banner before finally returning to actual rug products at the very bottom.
- The subject "Rugs on sale. So worth it." is the most specific, human-sounding subject CB2 has sent in this whole logged stretch — a real improvement over "The sale you can't miss" and "LABOR DAY SALE IS ON." It's let down by an email body that doesn't commit to the rug premise it opened with.
- Verdict: best subject line of the CB2 run, decent hero, but the body is unfocused and this is the fourth-plus Labor Day countdown-banner send logged — cadence fatigue is real. 5/10 territory, saved from lower by the subject and hero quality.

## What's working

- Hero rug texture close-up with visible weave detail is more premium and tactile than the recycled beach-dusk/green-velvet-chair hero shots from prior sends.
- Countdown timer ("SALE ENDS IN 04:17:19:17") adds credible urgency directly under the hero.
- Subject line names an actual category (rugs) rather than generic sale language — a real departure from the brand's recent subjects.
- Product grid at the bottom shows real rug SKUs with prices and strikethrough discounts (e.g., "$399.00 $279.00"), giving concrete proof of the offer.
- Clean, consistent CB2 visual language throughout — no render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Subject promises rugs, but the first content block after the hero is "MORE ON SALE THIS WAY" spanning furniture, outdoor, lighting, decor, and entertaining — rugs get buried until the very bottom.
- Two competing offers (Double Rewards credit card, 60% clearance) sit between the rug hero and the rug products, diluting a single clear CTA.
- This is at least the fifth near-identical Labor Day countdown/percentage-off send in this logged stretch — recipients have seen "up to 50%/60% off" banners repeatedly this week.
- No rug-specific detail (size options, material, room-fit guidance) surfaced near the hero despite rugs being a category where fit/sizing questions drive hesitation.
- Primary hero CTA "SHOP THE SALE" is generic — doesn't say "Shop Rugs," missing a chance to reinforce the subject's specific promise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero CTA say "Shop Rugs" (not "SHOP THE SALE") and move the real rug product grid up to directly follow the hero, cutting or shrinking the six-category cross-sell block — the subject sold rugs, the email should deliver rugs first.
- 2. Cut one of the two mid-email offers (Double Rewards or Clearance) — running a credit-card pitch and a second sale inside a rug-focused email splits attention three ways.
- 3. Add one rug-specific trust signal near the hero (e.g., "free shipping on rugs," size-range callout, or a stain-resistant/material badge) since sizing/fit is the #1 hesitation for rug purchases.
- 4. Vary the countdown/hero format next send — this is the fifth-plus similar countdown banner logged; a format break (like the 2026-09-04 plain-text send) would refresh cadence.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on hand-knotted wool, jute, and more — before the sale ends.`

# CB2 "Rugs on sale" — Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Legacy MSO/XHTML-transitional template with functional tracking but multiple hard failures: unencrypted third-party ad pixels, missing unsubscribe headers, and near-total absence of alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 69 tracking/click-redirect links present but skipped by automated probe (expected behavior for redirect domains — not itself a defect, but means link-destination validity is unverified here).
- Multiple **plaintext HTTP** third-party pixels/redirects from `ads.dotomi.com` (`pub1.php` through `pub10.php`) and `login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController` — all served over `http://`, not `https://`. Modern mail clients (Gmail, Outlook) commonly block or strip mixed-content HTTP image requests, meaning these tracking calls may silently fail to fire.
- Legitimate first-party tracking (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/...`) is correctly HTTPS.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 10 content images missing `alt` text (`7762f0ff...jpg` through `c5974994...png`), all served from `image.mail.cb2.com`. Screen readers and images-blocked clients will render blank/no fallback text for these.
- All 12 tracking/ad pixels also missing `alt` (lower severity — decorative/invisible by design, but compounds accessibility scan noise).
- Heavy legacy MSO conditional styling (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`, `mso-table-lspace`) and multiple `@font-face` declarations (Lineto Brown, GT Sectra, HNW05) with no universal fallback stack beyond `Helvetica, sans-serif` on the `a` tag — acceptable but font-loading failures will fall back inconsistently across clients.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No merge tokens or dynamic personalization fields visible in the truncated source (`mi_mid`, `cp_tp` in the `dv.cb2.com` URL are tracking/session IDs, not merge tags). No issues found.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
- **List-Unsubscribe header missing** — not detected in message headers. CAN-SPAM requires a functioning opt-out mechanism; absence of the header means mailbox providers can't offer one-click unsubscribe at the client level (a body-level unsubscribe link may still exist further down the truncated HTML, but couldn't be confirmed here).
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post header missing** (RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules since Feb 2024) is not confirmed supported.
- **Authentication-Results header missing** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified via this relay; this is a QA-tooling visibility gap (AgentMail relay) rather than confirmed proof of an auth failure, but it should be verified independently given the bulk-sender header gaps above.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from the truncated source — the visible content is head/style declarations only; no `<a href>` CTA links with UTM parameters are present in the truncated excerpt. Cannot confirm UTM presence/consistency without the link-containing body markup.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Migrate the Dotomi ad pixels/redirects (`ads.dotomi.com`, `login.dotomi.com`) to HTTPS or drop them if the vendor doesn't support TLS — currently at risk of being blocked outright by major clients.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 10 content images from `image.mail.cb2.com`.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are actually being sent by the ESP (this may be a relay-capture gap rather than a true absence — verify against raw ESP send logs) and add RFC 8058 support if missing, given Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
4. Independently verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status at the sending MTA, since this QA pass couldn't confirm it via the relay.
5. Obtain full (non-truncated) HTML to audit CTA links for UTM parameter consistency and landing-page alignment.
