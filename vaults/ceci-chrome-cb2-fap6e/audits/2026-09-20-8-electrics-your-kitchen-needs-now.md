# 8 electrics your kitchen needs now →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel running a "shop the kitchen" numbered countdown — 8 electrics, ranked #01 through #08, from a Breville espresso machine down through a Fellow kettle and toaster oven. It's a coherent, single-theme send, a notch above the grab-bag catalog format the brand has leaned on recently, but it's also just a brand-recognition-driven product roundup with zero discount, zero urgency, and zero reason to click today versus any other day. Photography is clean and consistent, and the numbered format gives it more narrative structure than the typical "everything in one email" send.
- The problem is depth over width: eight numbered products, each with its own hero shot and "Shop X" button, followed by a "more recipes" module, a Design Desk pitch, a Thanksgiving hosting guide banner, a repeat "top 10" module, a "we love these for you" strip, an app-download pitch, and a footer — this is an extremely long scroll for a list email with no offer anchoring it. A subscriber opens this for "what's new in kitchen electrics," not to keep scrolling through five additional unrelated modules.
- Verdict: solid concept, bloated execution, and it undersells itself by burying "no discount" under a wall of scroll — 5/10.

## What's working

- Clear numbered structure (01–08) gives the list a browsable, skimmable hierarchy that's easy to follow on mobile.
- Hero image (stand mixer, Breville espresso, kettle) is high quality and on-brand kitchen aesthetic.
- Each product gets its own dedicated "Shop [Brand]" CTA, so intent is unambiguous per item.
- Subject line ("8 electrics your kitchen needs now") matches the body content exactly — no bait-and-switch.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, buttons are visible throughout.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the visible scroll — no %/$ off, no free shipping, nothing that would move a considered $200–$700 purchase today.
- The email is far too long for its concept — after the 8 electrics, it tacks on recipes, Design Desk, a Thanksgiving hosting guide, a repeated top-10 module, and a "we love these for you" strip, diluting the countdown premise.
- No urgency or timing hook — nothing says why "now" in the subject actually matters.
- Visual hierarchy is flat: with 8 nearly-identical product+button blocks stacked vertically, nothing signals which is the actual hero/best deal.
- No pricing or member-pricing visible on any product, which matters for planned appliance purchases.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real incentive — even a modest one (e.g., "$50 off orders $250+" or a loyalty perk) — the countdown format is compelling, but without an offer there's no reason to buy this week versus browsing later.
- 2. Cut the email to the 8 electrics plus one clear CTA at the end; drop the recipes/Design Desk/Thanksgiving/top-10/"we love these" modules — a focused list email should end when the list ends.
- 3. Promote the top 1-2 items (Breville, KitchenAid) to a larger hero treatment and shrink the rest into a lighter grid — right now every item gets equal visual weight, which flattens the "must-have" framing the subject promises.
- 4. Surface price or "starting at $X" next to at least the anchor products, since kitchen electrics are a considered purchase and price is often the deciding factor.
