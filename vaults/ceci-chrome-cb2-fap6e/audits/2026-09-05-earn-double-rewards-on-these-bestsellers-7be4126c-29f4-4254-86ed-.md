# Earn DOUBLE REWARDS on these bestsellers →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Labor Day cadence for the sixth time in two days, and it finally leans into a genuinely different angle: Double Rewards tied specifically to "bestsellers" rather than another blanket sitewide percentage-off. The structure is a long, room-by-room bestseller crawl (living room, bedroom, dining room, kitchen) each with a real customer quote ("The quality craftsmanship is amazing!") and star ratings, which is a stronger trust device than anything in the last several sends. But it's still the same template chassis — same header band, same "Shop Bestsellers" CTA styling, same closing grid — so a subscriber who's opened three of these this week will recognize it instantly.
- The star-rating + testimonial pull-quotes per category are the standout addition and the one thing worth keeping for future sends. Overall this lands mid-pack: better differentiated than the pure "up to 60% off" repeats, but still fatigued by sheer repetition.

## What's working

- Room-by-room bestseller sections each carry a specific customer testimonial and 5-star rating, adding real social proof not seen in prior sends.
- "Earn Double Rewards" is paired with concrete reward math ("Earn $25-$100 in Reward Dollars") rather than vague loyalty language.
- Clear date-bounded urgency in the header ("Now through September 9, 2026").
- Consistent, recognizable Crate & Barrel visual identity (serif logo, warm neutral photography) throughout.
- Category-specific CTAs ("Shop Living Room Bestsellers," "Shop Bedroom Bestsellers") rather than one generic button repeated.

## What's weak

- Extremely long, scroll-heavy single email — six-plus product modules stacked with no visual break makes the hero offer easy to lose.
- Same modular template and color palette as the last five sends this week — no fresh creative treatment.
- "Bestsellers made even better" and other section headers are generic filler that don't add information.
- The Design Desk $1,000 sweepstakes module at the bottom feels bolted on and dilutes the primary Double Rewards message.
- No fresh product photography — same warm, neutral lifestyle sets recognizable from earlier sends this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to 3 room sections max and move the rest to a "See more bestsellers" landing page — this send is too long for the offer it's carrying.
- 2. Move the testimonial/star-rating device to the very top, right under the hero, since it's the most differentiated asset in the email and currently doesn't appear until deep into the scroll.
- 3. Drop or relocate the Design Desk sweepstakes to its own send — it competes with the primary CTA here.
- 4. Vary the hero product photography from prior sends this week so the brand doesn't read as one continuous email to a fatigued inbox.
