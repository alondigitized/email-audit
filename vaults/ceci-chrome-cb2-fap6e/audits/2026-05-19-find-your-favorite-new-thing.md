# Find your favorite new thing
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- CB2 sends a beautiful editorial catalog email — warm earth tones, quality photography, clean grid layout — and packages it under the blandest possible subject line. "Find your favorite new thing" could come from Target, Amazon Basics, or a pop-up shop at a county fair. The disconnect between the evocative hero copy ("Bring Your Heart's Desire Home") and the throwaway subject is the central problem here. The email earns its looks but not its open.
- This is also the third CB2 email in as many days — following the welcome email on 5/18 and the "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" send on 5/17. That cadence makes a generic browse-everything email feel especially like filler. The "Future Classics" framing from 5/17 had a sharp editorial concept; this email doesn't bring one. It's a polished product grid masquerading as content.
- The standout element nobody notices: the free interior design appointment CTA is buried mid-scroll. That is a genuine service differentiator — most furniture retailers don't offer it — and it deserves top billing, not paragraph four.

## What's working

- Strong hero photography — warm, tactile, aspirational; the dining scene with ceramics and natural light earns attention.
- Clean visual hierarchy within each section; the eye moves predictably from image to headline to CTA.
- The interior design appointment is a real, differentiated offer — something CB2 can own that IKEA and Wayfair cannot.
- Everyday Free Shipping is prominently placed as its own section — concrete, useful.
- Loyalty rewards (20% back, 10% off via app) are visible even if buried, which adds genuine perceived value for members.

## What's weak

- Subject line is completely persona-agnostic — "Find your favorite new thing" applies to any brand on earth.
- No editorial through-line: the email bounces between furniture, bedding, lighting, best sellers, free shipping, design appointments, and app loyalty with no connective concept.
- "SHOP NOW" repeats as the CTA across three separate sections, which trains the eye to skip it.
- The interior design appointment — the most differentiating offer — is placed after free shipping, after best sellers, after category tiles; it's fourth-act content that deserves to open the email.
- Third email in three days to a subscriber who got a welcome email yesterday; cadence fatigue risk is real.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line using the hero copy already in the email** — "Bring your heart's desire home" is significantly better than the generic subject; use it.
- 2. **Lead with the interior design appointment** or give it its own dedicated send — it's the actual differentiator and gets lost in a nine-section scroll.
- 3. **Give the email a single editorial concept** — like 5/17's "Future Classics" angle, each send should have a reason to exist beyond "here's our categories."
- 4. **Vary CTA copy across sections** — "Book Free Design Help," "Shop Best Sellers," "Claim Free Shipping" instead of three identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Surface loyalty benefits earlier** — 20% back is a strong hook that's buried below the fold.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Free design help + free shipping inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free interior design appointment — our designers are waiting.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everyday free shipping, 20% back rewards, and rooms you'll love.`
