# It’s not done-done without NEW wallpaper!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a launch email for Crate & Barrel Kids' first-ever wallpaper collection, and it handles the launch well. The hero is a pink-striped nursery with a mirror, followed by "choose from curated styles." Below that are three room vignettes: a kid's bedroom, a bedroom with a plaid wall, and a nursery. Each vignette has a pull-quote card and a "Shop Now" button. It looks editorial and on-brand, and the subject's "NEW wallpaper" promise is met right away.
- The weakness is the second half. After the wallpaper story, the email turns into a general catch-all. "Up to 60% off clearance," a holiday-lit banner, a "we love these for you" product grid, a gift card promo and an app download all follow. The only wallpaper offer is free samples. There's no price, no deadline and no hero product. The clearance and holiday modules also look like they're competing with their background photos. The text sits on busy images and is hard to read at this size.
- My verdict is a good brand launch but a weak direct-response email. It's a 6, and it would lift if it committed to one wallpaper action, such as order free samples.

## What's working

- The subject and hero match. "New wallpaper" in the subject lands on a "first-ever wallcovering collection" hero.
- The lifestyle photography is strong. The nursery, plaid-wall bedroom and floral-wallpaper nursery sell the look better than swatches would.
- The three vignettes follow a repeated rhythm of photo, caption, pull-quote card and "Shop Now." The pattern is easy to scan.
- The "more to love" block offers up to 10 free wallcovering samples. That's a low-risk way to try a high-commitment purchase.
- The "Shop All Wallpaper" button gives a clear category-level next step.

## What's weak

- There's no wallpaper price, discount or deadline anywhere. The only price-led modules are about clearance and free shipping at $99+.
- Every vignette uses the same generic "Shop Now" button. None names the product or style it links to.
- The "up to 60% off clearance" and holiday banners seem to have text sitting directly on busy photos. Legibility looks poor at this size.
- The lower half switches to clearance, product favorites, a gift card and an app download. This dilutes the wallpaper story.
- It follows several near-identical sends from this brand over the past two days. On 10/24 it sent Holiday decor, Venus Williams and Halloween emails, and on 10/25 it sent the dining tables email. This one competes with those in the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the free samples the primary hero CTA. Use a button like "Order free wallpaper samples." Move the samples up from the middle of the email and add a deadline or a limit, such as "10 free samples."
- 2. Cut or move the clearance, holiday, product-grid, gift card and app modules below the wallpaper content, or drop them. A launch email should have one job.
- 3. Replace the repeated "Shop Now" with style-specific buttons. For example, "Shop plaid wallpaper," "Shop floral wallpaper" and "Shop nursery wallpaper."
- 4. Put a price or "starting at $X" on the hero. Add a social-proof line if there are reviews.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Third-party pixels:** five LiveRamp sync pixels, `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=…&n=1` through `n=5`.

  - They can be blocked by privacy-focused clients.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, content images (about 20):**
  - 17 on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/…` (e.g. `bbf6f9d4-….png`, `34a386c7-….jpg`, `1b3e75ad-….gif`).
  - 3 on `s7d5.scene7.com`: `2026%5F1002%5FCBK%5FGiftGuide%5FSecondaryStory.gif`, `2026%5F0926%5FCBK%5FClearance%5FSecondaryStory.gif`, and the `…Rule%5FSpacer%5F40px` spacer.
  - With images blocked, or for screen readers, these will render blank or read out filenames or URLs. Alt text is also missing on GIFs, which may carry content.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixels (13):** the `mi.`, `rlcdn.` and `dv.` images listed above. This is lower impact, but pixels should carry `alt=""`. Screen readers may otherwise announce the filename.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` restricts pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Global CSS:** `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` and `*{line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px}` apply to everything. They can override inherited alignment and line-height unexpectedly.
- **Markup hygiene:**
  - `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`.
  - `<meta name="format-detection">` is declared three separate times, with conflicting values on the first.
  - `.ReadMsgBody` is defined twice.
  - The placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` shipped in production.
  - `<meta charset>` and `http-equiv` Content-Type are both declared. This is redundant but harmless.
- **Strengths:** `lang="en"`, a color-scheme meta tag, and a mobile breakpoint at 460px.

1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the ~20 content images, especially the GIFs. Use `alt=""` on spacers and tracking pixels.

4. Strip the leftover template comment from the production build.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC against the raw message headers. A direct-to-inbox test would avoid the relay.
6. Re-run the link audit with redirect resolution to check UTMs and landing-page health.
