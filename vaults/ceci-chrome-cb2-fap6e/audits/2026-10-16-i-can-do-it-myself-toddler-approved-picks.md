# “I can do it myself” toddler-approved picks →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises a toddler-independence story ("I can do it myself"). The email opens on a sale banner instead: "The Semi-Annual Bedroom & Nursery Event, Up to 50% off*". Under it is a Montessori-inspired toddler-room hero. The creative is polished, and the body does follow the independence theme: a table for arts and crafts, an eye-level bookshelf, a low bed for easy climbing, toy bins for tidying up. But the strongest hook, up to 50% off, is not in the subject line, and a footnote asterisk qualifies it.
- The email is very long, about 12,000px, and every module gets a pale, low-contrast pill button. The page gets repetitive well before the footer. The ending drifts into the Crate Card, an app download with a $500 sweepstakes, the Design Desk and a store locator. A parent shopping for a toddler gets pulled off-topic four times.
- Verdict: a good concept with a lot of pretty pictures. It is held back by a mismatch between the subject and the banner, weak buttons and too many stacked CTAs. It is better than the brand's recent "Designed to get dinner on the table ASAP" send, because here the body does deliver on the theme. It is still a 6.

## What's working

- The Montessori toddler-space hero is on theme, and the "Shop the Toddler Shop" button is a clear dark pill.
- The banner states a concrete offer: "up to 50% off" on furniture, bedding and rugs.
- Each module pairs a short benefit line with its own category button, for example "toy bins for tidying up" and "Shop Bins & Baskets".
- The photography is calm and consistent, with muted blues, creams and wood tones.
- Near the end, the product grid shows real prices, including a strikethrough $369.00 → $295.20 on the crib mattress.

## What's weak

- The subject says "I can do it myself" but the first thing a reader sees is a sale banner. Neither the 50% off nor the event name is in the subject or in the hero copy.
- The module buttons ("Shop Bins & Baskets" and others) are pale grey-blue pills with white text, and they have poor contrast. They are far weaker than the dark hero button.
- The email is very long with about eight near-identical image-plus-button blocks. The body is two stacked blocks of repetition and then a closing run of unrelated pitches.
- The footer stack of the Crate Card, the app with a $500 chance, the Design Desk and the store locator dilutes the toddler message and adds four more CTAs.
- The hero offer is footnoted with an asterisk, so exclusions are not visible. Sale pricing appears on only one product in the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the subject and hero so the open and the first screen match. Say "up to 50% off" and tie it to the independence angle.
- 2. Cut the email to about four modules, or build one "shop by skill" grid with 6 to 8 products and visible prices.
- 3. Make all buttons the same high-contrast dark as the hero button. Use specific verbs, such as "Shop low beds" instead of "Shop Bins & Baskets".
- 4. Move the card, app and Design Desk pitches to a separate send or a single slim footer row.
- 5. Show the exclusions inline or in a short line under the banner, so the asterisk doesn't undercut the offer.
