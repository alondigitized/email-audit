# New chairs that will make you forget all the rest
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is making a full-gear shift here. After an aggressive five-send promotional drumbeat — Warehouse Sale (June 10, 13), Design Legends Sale close-out (June 13), editorial marble pivot (June 14), and the "48 HOURS LEFT" double-sale finale (June 15) — this June 16 email is the brand's reset send. No discount banner, no countdown, just new product. The subject line leans into the boast ("forget all the rest") rather than an offer, which is a gutsy call the day after hammering subscribers with up-to-60%-off messaging.
- The email itself is a clean product showcase — hero lifestyle shot of a sleek black accent chair, then a vertical stack of featured chairs with names and prices, finishing with a compact secondary grid of additional styles. Layout is tidy and on-brand. The photography does the selling. But without a hook — no price anchoring, no member exclusive, no urgency — this lives entirely on the strength of the product.
- The timing is coherent as a brand-breath send, but the subject line is doing too little work for a cold inbox. "New chairs that will make you forget all the rest" is memorable in a headline context; it's weaker as a subject line where the reader has no visual backup. This will open well for loyal CB2 furniture shoppers and underperform for the rest of the list.

## What's working

- **Hero image lands immediately.** The dark velvet accent chair is visually striking and the "CHAIRS" editorial heading cuts through fast.
- **Product stack is easy to scan.** Three featured chairs shown at consistent scale, each with visible name and price — no hunting required.
- **Brand reset timing is smart.** After five sale emails in six days, pulling back to full-price new arrivals re-establishes brand value rather than training subscribers to wait for discounts.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images loaded, no broken grid cells or overlapping elements visible.
- **Secondary product grid below the fold** gives reason to scroll — the browse-mode buyer has more options without the email feeling cluttered.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** After being trained on "UP TO 60% OFF" last week, full-price new arrivals need a stronger hook — even "complimentary design consult with any chair purchase" would bridge the gap.
- **Subject oversells and underspecifies.** "Forget all the rest" is brand confidence without any evidence — no model name, no material call-out, nothing for the inbox scanner to grab.
- **No urgency or scarcity signal.** New arrivals emails live and die on some form of "limited quantities" or "just landed" freshness cue; neither is visible in the render.
- **No social proof.** The Warehouse Sale recap and Design Legends content gave buyer confidence via heritage / archive framing. This email has nothing comparable — no reviews, no star ratings, no "bestseller" badge.
- **CTA buttons are small and low-contrast** in the product cards — the visual hierarchy favors imagery over action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a material or product name to the subject.** The boast needs an anchor. Something like "The Anza swivel just landed — and it's worth it" outperforms a generic claim in click-through consistently.
- 2. **Open with a scarcity or freshness hook.** "Just dropped" or "limited first run" in the first line of body copy turns a browse send into an act-now send.
- 3. **Increase CTA button size and contrast.** The product-level buttons are undersized relative to the photography; they should be unmissable.
- 4. **Add one bridge sentence from the sale period.** Something like "The sale wrapped. Now meet what's next." — a single line in the subhead would give loyal subscribers a context reset and make the transition feel intentional rather than abrupt.
- 5. **Surface a design consult tie-in.** You have a Design Services team (June 15 "rug that ties it all together" send). Chairs are the perfect upsell moment: "Not sure which works in your space? Book a free design consult."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta tag**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom for low-vision users. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure.

**Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to every element via the `*` selector. Overrides OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility needs.

**Unquoted `font-family` with a space — CSS parse error**
```css
@font-face { font-family: GT Sectra; ... }
```
`GT Sectra` contains a space and must be quoted: `font-family: "GT Sectra";`. Without quotes, this is a CSS syntax error; the font will silently fail to load in strict parsers.

**Double-slash paths in font URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
```
Double slashes (`//`) after the hostname are technically valid HTTP but non-canonical. Some CDN/server configurations return 301 redirects or 404s on double-slash paths. Single slash is correct.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint with hardcoded width**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width: 414px !important; }
}
```
418px is a legacy threshold (iPhone 6 Plus era). Current devices with 390px–430px logical widths (iPhone 14/15 series) may fall into or out of this breakpoint inconsistently. The hardcoded `414px` width could overflow on 390px-wide devices.

**Redundant/conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks share the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` name. The first has no `font-weight`, which means it implicitly matches `normal` — but the third block also declares `font-weight: 400` for the same family. The duplicate will cause one to shadow the other depending on parse order.
