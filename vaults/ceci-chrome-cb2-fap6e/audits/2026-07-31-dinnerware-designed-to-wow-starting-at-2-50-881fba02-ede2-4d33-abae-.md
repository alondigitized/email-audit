# Dinnerware designed to wow, starting at $2.50 →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a kitchen-sink dinnerware showcase — Marin, Jardin, Le Creuset, Loire, Toben, and Aspen all stacked one after another, plus a "TheDesignDesk" home-makeover editorial and a store credit-card pitch, before the email is done. The subject promises "starting at $2.50" but nothing in the visible render is anywhere near that price point — that's a bait-and-switch a subscriber will notice within the first couple of scrolls.
- The top hero (trend alert, "Midnight Mocha," Shop Now) is genuinely well-composed on its own. The problem is everything after it: six-plus separate collection pitches with no hierarchy telling you which one matters, followed by two entirely unrelated asks (a celebrity home-tour plug and a credit card offer). Compare this to "NEW | Our Cotswolds-inspired lookbook" (2026-07-29, 9.00/10) or "Shop up to 60% off the Summer Sale" (2026-07-30, 8.00/10) — both won by picking one story and finishing it. This send repeats the exact kitchen-sink mistake flagged in the "Starting at $1599" sofa email (2026-07-31, 7.00/10): too many good ideas fighting each other instead of one clear one winning.
- Net: competent execution per-section, no execution failure, but a strategy failure — this needed to be two or three separate emails.

## What's working

- Photography is clean and consistent across every collection — no overlapping text, no broken images, no rendering bugs anywhere in the scroll.
- The top hero delivers a real trend hook ("Midnight Mocha," "trend alert: dinnerware that pops") with a clearly visible Shop Now button.
- Aspen (15% off) and the Le Creuset credit-card offer ($175 off) are both concrete, specific figures — not vague "save big" language.
- Every collection section (Marin, Jardin, Loire, Toben) has its own dedicated "Shop [Name]" button, so within-email navigation is easy even though the volume is high.
- The "for setting the table together" grid (flatware, glassware, table linens) is a smart complete-the-look cross-sell.

## What's weak

- The subject's "$2.50" claim is never anchored to any visible product — it reads as bait once you scroll and can't find it.
- No single hero offer: six-plus collections plus an editorial plus a credit-card pitch is far too many competing asks for one send.
- Zero urgency anywhere — no deadline, no "ends Sunday," nothing that makes today the day to click.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof on any product shown.
- The Lupita Nyong'o home-makeover feature and the credit-card offer are tangents that dilute an already crowded dinnerware narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. Anchor the subject's $2.50 claim to an actual visible product at the top of the email — if it's a small accent piece, show it with a price tag in the hero, or drop the price claim from the subject entirely.
- 2. Cut collection count from six to two (e.g., Marin + Aspen sale) and push the rest (Jardin, Le Creuset, Loire, Toben) to a follow-up send — this is the same stacking problem that dragged down the 7/31 sofa email.
- 3. Remove the Design Desk and credit-card sections from this send; they belong in their own dedicated emails, not bolted onto a dinnerware pitch.
- 4. Add a real deadline to the Aspen 15% off ("ends Sunday") to create the urgency this email currently lacks.
- 5. Add customer ratings/review counts under at least the hero collection to build trust.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Midnight Mocha colorway, plus this week's table-setting favorites`
