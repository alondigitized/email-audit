# Summer gear (and toys!) are here…
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal new-arrivals announcement — summer gear, strollers, outdoor toys — sent under the display name "Crate & Kids SALE." That sender label is the most glaring problem: the last five sends from this brand were heavy Memorial Day discount emails (40%–60% off, 2x Rewards), and now the list is getting a product-launch email from an address that still screams "SALE." Subscribers who click expecting a deal are going to land on full-price new arrivals.
- The email itself is reasonably well-organized — a lifestyle hero, a product tile grid, a stroller/gear section, a toys row — and the seasonal timing is correct (late May for summer launches makes sense). But the creative leans on a broad "here's what's new" approach rather than a single compelling hero product or an anchored offer. After a weekend of escalating Memorial Day percentage-off hooks, this lands as a step backward in persuasion.
- The bottom line: the pivot from sale to launch is reasonable strategy, but the execution is sloppy — wrong sender label, no offer anchor, and no reason-to-click-today energy. This email will get opens from habit, not desire.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing is right.** Launching summer gear in the last week of May is exactly when a parent is thinking about this category.
- **Product variety signals breadth.** Strollers, outdoor chairs, a bright yellow bag, toys — the grid communicates "we refreshed the whole summer assortment," which is a legitimate hook.
- **Clean layout, no visible render breaks.** The tile grid renders without overlapping elements or broken images; easy to scan on mobile.
- **Short subject line.** At ~36 characters, "Summer gear (and toys!) are here…" clears the mobile truncation window cleanly.

## What's weak

- **Sender label "Crate & Kids SALE" is wrong for this creative.** A non-sale email under a SALE sender name sets up a bait-and-switch feeling, especially after five consecutive discount sends.
- **No offer anchor visible.** After 40–60% off Memorial Day messaging, this email offers nothing. There's no price, no discount, no member benefit visible — no reason to click now vs. in two weeks.
- **Hero doesn't land a clear single product or idea.** The top image appears to be a soft lifestyle scene rather than a specific hero item with a name, price, and CTA — the eye has nowhere to go first.
- **Ellipsis subject ("are here…") kills urgency.** The trailing ellipsis signals "take your time" rather than "act now." Combined with no offer, the subject generates curiosity but not intent.
- **Cadence whiplash.** Five sale emails in two days, then a zero-discount new-arrivals launch with no transition acknowledgment (e.g., "Sale's almost done — and new summer arrivals just hit") — the pivot is abrupt.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the sender label before next send.** "Crate & Kids SALE" needs to revert to "Crate & Kids" for non-sale creative. This is a single Klaviyo/ESP setting change with outsized trust impact.
- 2. **Bridge the Memorial Day sale to the new arrivals.** One line — "The sale ends Monday. These just arrived." — turns the pivot into a story instead of a non-sequitur.
- 3. **Anchor on one hero product with a name and CTA.** Pick the stroller or the standout toy, name it, show the price, put a single "Shop [Product Name]" button beneath it before the grid starts.
- 4. **Add a new-arrival exclusivity signal.** "Just landed — not part of any sale" is actually a stronger positioning than a discount for a new-arrivals email; it signals freshness and full-price desirability.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to create a reason-to-act.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: summer gear + toys just landed at C&K`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer checklist starts here — new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Strollers, outdoor gear, and the toys they'll actually play with.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Just in: everything you need before the season starts.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[CONFIRMED] Conflicting `img max-width` breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Both rules target desktop, but the 640px query is declared after the 768px query. On screens 640–767px wide, the 640px cap applies correctly; on screens ≥768px, specificity is equal so the last rule (640px) wins — overriding the intended 600px cap. Consolidate into a single breakpoint.

**[CONFIRMED] Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `text-size-adjust: none` to all elements via `*` can break font scaling on iOS Safari and is more aggressive than the body-scoped declaration already present. The body-level rules (`-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%`) conflict with this.

`telephone=no` is declared twice (once combined, once standalone); `address=no` appears twice. Redundant but harmless — clean up for template hygiene.


Explicitly locks to light mode. Apple Mail, Outlook (new), and Gmail dark mode will force-invert images and backgrounds. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides are present in the visible CSS.
