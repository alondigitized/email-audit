# BACK IN STOCK
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- "BACK IN STOCK" is CB2 finally matching subject to hero — "SEIZE the MOMENT / IT'S BACK IN STOCK" lands immediately on a moody, well-shot velvet chair and side-table vignette, no bait-and-switch. What follows is a genuinely structured in-stock catalog: Bedroom, Coffee Tables, Sectionals, Storage, Sofas, each with its own hero shot, category label, and "SHOP NOW" — this is the most organized, subject-honest send in the recent CB2 run.
- The catch: after six clean in-stock category blocks, the email still can't resist bolting on "THE LABOR DAY SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" as a generic banner near the bottom, plus a "MORE FOR YOU" product grid — the same sale-strip habit flagged in "The perfect sofa does exist" (2026-08-26) and "SUITE UPGRADES ON SALE" (2026-08-26). It dilutes an otherwise well-executed restock narrative into a longer, kitchen-sink scroll.
- Solid execution, undercut by scope creep. This is a 7 — the best subject-to-hero match in the recent CB2 history, docked for length and a redundant sale pivot that isn't what anyone opened for.

## What's working

- Subject "BACK IN STOCK" is delivered on immediately — hero literally says "IT'S BACK IN STOCK."
- Six category blocks (Bedroom, Coffee Tables, Sectionals, Storage, Sofas, Lighting/Rugs/Wall Art/Decor) each have a clear photo, label, and CTA — easy to scan.
- Prices are visible on individual products further down (e.g. "$579.00," "$399.00"), giving concrete anchor points.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is consistent block-to-block.
- Footer includes app download, loyalty rewards ("EARN 10% BACK"), and a referral/sign-up offer ("GET 15% OFF") — good use of real estate for existing customers.

## What's weak

- The Labor Day sale banner near the bottom is a non-sequitur after six "in stock" blocks — it's the same recycled 50%-off strip from four other recent sends, not tied to what's back in stock.
- The email is very long for a restock announcement — six full category sections plus a sale banner plus a "MORE FOR YOU" grid is a lot of scroll for one theme.
- No urgency or scarcity cue for the restock itself (no "limited quantities," no specific SKUs called out as newly back) — it reads as a general catalog refresh rather than a true restock alert.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalization label appears with no visible personalization signal (no name, no "based on your browsing") — generic product grid dressed as tailored.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Labor Day sale banner entirely from this send — it contradicts the restock narrative and this brand already has five other emails carrying that message this week; let "back in stock" be its own clean story.
- 2. Add scarcity language to the hero or first category block — "back by popular demand, limited quantities" — to justify urgency and earn the click.
- 3. Trim to 3-4 category blocks max (the highest-demand categories) and move the rest to a "shop all restocks" link — shorten the scroll significantly.
- 4. Replace the generic "MORE FOR YOU" grid with "restocked favorites" framing so it reinforces the subject line instead of introducing a new theme.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your favorites are back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Restocked: the pieces you wanted`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, sofas, and storage — finally back on the shelf.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited restock — shop before it sells out again.`
