# Our latest collab is serving this star’s unmatched style
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Venus Williams collab launch, and it leads with the strongest asset it has — a large, editorial hero shot of Venus against a styled living-room backdrop with a clean "NEW & EXCLUSIVE!" badge and a punchy copy line about her "passion for design with the precision of the game." That's a genuinely differentiated hook compared to the brand's recent run of generic Design Desk / holiday-preview sends.
- From there it turns into the standard Crate & Barrel long-scroll catalog: new Lawn Club sectional, Meadows bed, Monte Carlo dining table, Legacy sideboard, then a lighting section explicitly branded "serving unmatched style," then a Crate&Kids Venus tie-in, then an unrelated kitchen-tools module and a generic "we love these for you" grid. The collab concept is strong but gets diluted fast — by the third or fourth product block it reads like any other new-arrivals email with a celebrity sticker on top.
- Net: better hook than the September 24 Venus Williams send (7/10) thanks to a cleaner hero and Venus quote testimonials sprinkled through, but it still tails off into the same grab-bag-of-CTAs pattern this brand keeps repeating (see the 09/24 Design Desk email). Solid open, mediocre sustained engagement.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous: Venus Williams photo, "NEW & EXCLUSIVE!" badge, one clear "Shop the Collab" CTA button front and center.
- Personal quotes from Venus ("I couldn't love this line more... this is my heart.") are woven between product blocks, adding authenticity beyond a logo slap.
- Each product module (sectional, bed, dining table, sideboard, lighting) has its own green "Shop the Collab" button — consistent, easy-to-spot CTA styling throughout.
- Lifestyle photography is cohesive — warm wood tones, black-and-white checkered floors, consistent styling voice that reads as a real collection, not a stock catalog.

## What's weak

- The email is extremely long with no visible way to jump to a category — a reader has to scroll past 8+ full-width modules before reaching the kitchen tools / "we love these for you" grab-bag at the bottom.
- Those final two sections (kitchen tools, generic recommendations) have nothing to do with Venus Williams or the collab and dilute the single-story premise the hero promises.
- No visible pricing, % off, or offer anywhere in the render — this is a pure new-arrivals push with no urgency or savings lever, which caps click intent for a price-sensitive shopper.
- Repetition fatigue risk: this is essentially the same Venus Williams collab already sent 09/24 ("Winning styles from Venus Williams' new collab"), just restructured — two sends in two days on the same collab.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email by 40% — keep hero + 3 hero products (sectional, bed, dining table) + one closing CTA to "Shop the full collection," and drop the kitchen-tools/generic-recommendations modules entirely so the story stays Venus-only end to end.
- 2. Add a concrete offer or urgency line under the hero (e.g., "new arrivals — limited quantities" or early-access framing for loyalty members) since there's currently zero price/urgency signal.
- 3. Move one of the Venus quotes up near the hero — social proof that close to the top would boost credibility before asking for a click.
- 4. Since this is the second Venus send in two days, differentiate the subject/preheader explicitly as "new pieces" or "just landed" so subscribers don't feel like they're getting the same email twice.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Venus Williams collab: 5 new pieces just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New from Venus Williams — shop the collection`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sectionals, sideboards, and lighting inspired by her game.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces she designed for her own home.`
