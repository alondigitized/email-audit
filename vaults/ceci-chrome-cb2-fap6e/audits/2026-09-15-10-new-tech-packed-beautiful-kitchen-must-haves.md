# 10 new, tech-packed & beautiful kitchen must-haves
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a straight top-10 listicle email — new/exclusive kitchen appliances numbered 1 through 10, each with its own product shot, name, and "Shop Now" button, running through KitchenAid, Le Creuset, DeLonghi, Ninja, Caraway, JURA, GreenPan and more. It's the most product-dense, least gimmicky send in this brand's recent run (no Venus Williams collab tie-in, no app-gating) — it's just inventory, clearly laid out, which after five straight celebrity-collab emails is almost a relief.
- The problem is that density comes at the cost of a single clear hero. There's a "Meet Luminaire" callout near the top that tries to act as the lead story, but visually it competes with nine other products that all get equal weight — nothing tells the eye where to land first. The layout is also a very long single-column scroll with repetitive "Shop Now" buttons, so by product 6 or 7 it reads as a catalog page rather than a curated pick.
- Solid execution, no render bugs, genuine new-arrival copy — but it's an inventory dump dressed as a top-10 list, not a targeted offer.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no broken images or layout breaks.
- Numbered 1–10 format gives scannable structure even in a long scroll.
- "new + exclusive" framing repeated across several items signals genuine novelty, not stale promo copy.
- No discount/urgency gimmicks bolted on — feels like an honest new-arrivals roundup.
- Bottom sections (apartment collection, "we love these for you", app CTA) add relevant cross-sell without derailing the main list.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer or price point visible anywhere — every item is "new/exclusive" with no dollar figure, making the whole email feel like browsing rather than buying.
- Ten near-identical "Shop Now" buttons in a row dilute the CTA; nothing stands out as THE thing to click.
- The top "Meet Luminaire" section looks like it wants to be the hero but gets visually buried between the numbered grid above and the list below.
- Extremely long single-column scroll (likely 10,000+ px) — most subscribers will bail well before item 10.
- Subject promises "tech-packed" but nothing in the visuals (specs, tech callouts, comparison points) actually surfaces that angle.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero (Luminaire or the JURA espresso machine) and give it 2x the visual real estate at the top with an actual price or "starting at $X," then compress items 2–10 into a tighter grid (2-across) below the fold instead of one item per full-width block.
- 2. Add at least one concrete price/offer anchor somewhere in the email — right now there's zero price signal, which kills urgency.
- 3. Cut the list to a "top 5" or split into two sends — this send is roughly twice as long as it needs to be to get someone to click.
- 4. Make the "tech-packed" subject line true visually — add small spec callouts (e.g., "auto steam wand," "10-cup capacity") under 2-3 of the appliances.
