# The goop sofa that never goes unnoticed
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's goop collaboration drop — a branded editorial email spotlighting what appears to be a large, sculptural blush/dusty-rose rounded sofa as the hero product. The warehouse sale banner persists at the very top, which immediately undercuts the premium-collaboration framing the subject line was building toward. You open expecting an exclusive editorial event; you land on a sofa sitting inside a sale context.
- The visual storytelling is strong — the hero image is arresting, and the product grid below shows multiple angles and complementary pieces. But there is no visible price anchor, no discount percentage, and no urgency mechanism. CB2 tried this exact move five days ago with the Todd Snyder drop and scored a 5. This one has roughly the same structure: aspirational collab, beautiful photography, no conversion engine. Without a price or a "only X available" signal, the email is all mood and no pull.
- Judgement: nice-looking brand moment that will drive brand affinity but not basket adds. The goop name in the subject is the strongest asset here; the email does not spend it wisely.

## What's working

- The hero sofa image is visually striking — an oversized blush/mauve sculptural form that earns the subject line claim of "never goes unnoticed."
- Subject line is concrete and curiosity-driving; "the goop sofa" is a specific noun that stands out in a crowded inbox.
- Product grid below the hero shows range — multiple lifestyle shots and complementary pieces fill out the collection without feeling padded.
- Room-context lifestyle shot near the bottom grounds the sofa in an aspirational but livable setting.

## What's weak

- The warehouse sale banner at the top of the email immediately repositions a prestige collab as a clearance vehicle — tone whiplash.
- No price or offer is visible in the hero; "never goes unnoticed" is a brand claim, not a purchase reason.
- No CTA button copy is legible at this render size — CTAs appear to exist but are visually buried beneath the product photography.
- The goop brand is named only in the subject; there is no visible goop logo, co-brand lock-up, or attribution copy in the hero — the partnership signal disappears once you're inside the email.
- CB2 sent a near-identical collab editorial (Todd Snyder, June 19) five days ago; same beat twice in a week trains subscribers to skip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove or relocate the warehouse sale banner** — or explicitly resolve the tension: "The goop sofa is now in the Warehouse Sale" is a dramatically more compelling hook than a generic sale badge floating above a prestige hero image.
- 2. **Add a visible price or offer in the hero** — even "$X,XXX — now in the sale" or "Members save 15%" turns a mood board into a decision point.
- 3. **Make the co-brand lock-up visible in the hero** — the goop wordmark or a "CB2 × goop" treatment should appear inside the image, not just the subject line.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA** — "Shop the Goop Edit" or "See the Full Collection" beats whatever generic button is currently rendering at illegible size.
- 5. **Add scarcity or exclusivity language** — "limited run," "exclusive to CB2," or a unit count converts intrigue into urgency.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
**Partial audit only — HTML truncated before body/CTA links.**

- **Double-slash font URLs**: Four `@font-face` src values use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...` (double slash). These resolve via 301 redirect in browsers but add a round-trip; email clients may not follow redirects for font assets, causing silent fallback to system fonts.
  - Affected: `lineto-brown-light.woff2`, `lineto-brown-regular.woff2`, `GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
- **Cross-domain font dependency**: `HNW05` (Helvetica Neue Web) fonts are loaded from `www.crateandbarrel.com`, not `cb2.com`. If `crateandbarrel.com` is unreachable or changes paths, CB2 email rendering degrades silently.
- CTA link tracking (UTM parameters, click-tracking wrapper domains) cannot be verified from the provided source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Global text-size-adjust disabled (High)**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The wildcard selector overrides the `body` rule that sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` four lines earlier. This disables text scaling for all elements in WebKit/IE clients, breaking WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). The inline comment attributes this to a "font fix for small values" — if needed, scope it to the specific selectors causing the problem rather than `*`.

**Issue 2 — Viewport zoom locked (Medium)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. iOS Safari 10+ ignores it for user-initiated zoom, but Android WebView and older clients enforce it. Best practice for email accessibility is to omit `maximum-scale` entirely.

**Issue 3 — Image alt text unverifiable**
HTML truncated before `<body>` content. Alt text on product images cannot be confirmed.
