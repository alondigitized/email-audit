# The kitchen brand that makes cooking SO fun
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel doing a full Ninja-brand catalog dump dressed up as a lifestyle email, and the subject line oversells what's inside. "The kitchen brand that makes cooking SO fun" promises inspiration but delivers seven stacked shop-by-category product blocks — toaster oven, espresso machines, air fryer/multi-cooker, blenders, coolers, cutlery — with three separate "Shop Ninja" CTAs before you even hit the fold-two content. There's no price, no percentage off, no urgency anywhere in the Ninja section; it's pure catalog browsing dressed as brand storytelling.
- The email doesn't stop at Ninja either — it tacks on a Venus Williams tennis-blender collab, a SMEG exclusive, a "so scary, it's chic" Halloween teaser, a "we love these for you" cross-sell grid (which is where the only real discounts live — Le Creuset at $289.95 down from $460, utensils at $109.95 down from $113.75), and a credit-card pitch, all in one send. It reads like four separate campaigns merged into a single scroll.
- Against the brand history, this lands below the Design Desk sends (7-8/10) and closer to the "Selling fast" clearance email (5/10) that also buried its real offer under unrelated content. The hero visual (5-star review quote + toaster oven) is strong and the styling is consistent, but as a piece of direct-response marketing this is unfocused and underselling its own discounts.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and confident: 5-star rating, a punchy testimonial quote ("I'm obsessed—no regrets. Best find ever."), and a hero-image match (Ninja toaster oven) with a single visible CTA.
- Product photography throughout is consistent, well-lit, and on-brand (marble/wood staging).
- "New + Exclusive Color" and "Ships Free*" badges on the espresso machines add a real, specific reason to click.
- The bottom "we love these for you" grid actually shows strikethrough pricing and real discounts (Le Creuset $460→$289.95), which is more concrete than anything above it.

## What's weak

- Subject line has zero concrete offer, no price, no urgency — just vague brand enthusiasm ("SO fun"), which undersells the actual markdowns buried at the bottom.
- The email is a kitchen-sink send: Ninja electrics, a Venus Williams collab, a SMEG exclusive, a Halloween teaser, and a credit card pitch all compete for attention in one scroll — no single thesis (contrast with the brand's own stronger "apartment collection" or "Design Desk" sends).
- Three near-identical "Shop Ninja" / "Shop Ninja Electrics" / "Shop Ninja Espresso & Coffee" buttons in a row create CTA fatigue instead of one clear next step.
- No pricing at all is shown for any Ninja product (toaster oven, espresso machines, Crispi multi-cooker) — a shopper can't gauge value until they click through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: one focused purely on the Ninja lineup with an actual price or bundle offer up front, and a separate one for the Venus Williams/SMEG/Halloween content — the merge is diluting all three.
- 2. Move a real discount (the Le Creuset or utensils markdown) into the subject line and hero — right now the strongest offer in the email is the last thing a reader sees.
- 3. Add prices under every Ninja product shown in the hero and mid-body blocks; unpriced hero products kill click confidence.
- 4. Collapse the three "Shop Ninja" CTAs into one persistent style/copy so the eye isn't retrained each scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ninja espresso machines, now in Midnight Mocha (exclusive)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Save up to $170 on Le Creuset + Ninja essentials`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New colorway, free shipping on select espresso machines, and kitchen markdowns inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The exclusive Ninja Luxe color you can't get anywhere else.`
