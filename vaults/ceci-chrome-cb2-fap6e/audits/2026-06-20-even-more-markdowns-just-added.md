# Even more markdowns just added
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's warehouse sale — again. "Even more markdowns just added" is the fifth or sixth variation on the same template CB2 has been running since June 10, and it compounds the problem by cramming two competing events into one send: the Warehouse Sale (online, 60% off) and the Floor Sample Sale (in-store only). Neither gets the spotlight it deserves. The June 17 "NEW ITEMS ADDED: Warehouse Sale" was the same format executed better — that send had one clear message and it felt fresh. This one doesn't.
- The warehouse sale hero is technically solid — 60% off is concrete, the category pills are navigable, the render is clean. But the Floor Sample Sale block sitting immediately beneath it (with a completely different CTA mechanic: "FIND A STORE," in-person only) creates cognitive dissonance. A subscriber who clicked to shop online is now being asked to drive somewhere. The "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" strip wedged between those two sections makes it worse — three unrelated asks before you even reach the product grid.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" product recommendations below are the most clickable content in the email, but they arrive after so much noise that many recipients will be gone before they reach them. This email does the basics right and the strategy wrong.

## What's working

- **60% off is front and center** — no ambiguity on the offer magnitude; the hero communicates the deal in under two seconds.
- **Category navigation pills** (Furniture, Lighting, Decor, Rugs, Shop All) give direct entry points below the hero without forcing a "SHOP ALL" homepage dump.
- **Product grid is the strongest section** — specific named items with prices visible; this is actually shoppable content.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no text overflow, layout holds at this viewport.
- **"EARN 10% BACK" rewards call-out** is visible mid-email rather than buried in the footer.

## What's weak

- **Dual-event structure splits attention** — Warehouse Sale (online) immediately followed by Floor Sample Sale (in-store) with a different CTA type ("FIND A STORE") creates two incompatible asks.
- **"LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" strip is misplaced** — a review solicitation between the warehouse hero and the floor sample section breaks the shopping flow; belongs in the footer or a dedicated send.
- **No deadline anywhere in the email** — "UP TO 60% OFF" floats without an end date, reducing urgency that "WAREHOUSE SALE" language promises.
- **Fifth+ iteration with no creative evolution** — the June 17 send was structurally identical; this template is fatigued and subscribers who are engaged have already seen it multiple times.
- **Floor Sample Sale has no discount** — "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" with no percentage and a "FIND A STORE" CTA is hollow compared to the 60% hero above it; it punches below its weight.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one message.** Drop the Floor Sample Sale block entirely or send it as a separate, standalone email. The warehouse sale needs the full email to breathe.
- 2. **Add a deadline or item count to the hero.** "60% off — ends Sunday" or "200+ new items added, sale ends soon" converts urgency from implied to felt.
- 3. **Move the product grid to position 2.** After the hero/CTA, go straight into "MORE FOR YOU" — that's the click driver. The Floor Sample Sale and review strip can live below or disappear.
- 4. **Kill "LEAVE US A REVIEW" mid-email.** This is a footer behavior at best; it stops the shopper's mental purchase flow cold.
- 5. **Replace "SHOP ALL" with a specific verb.** "SHOP NEW MARKDOWNS" or "SHOP 60% OFF" beats a category dump page.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off just landed on 200+ new styles`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: warehouse markdowns up to 60% — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, rugs — the new ones just dropped in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh cuts in every room. Shop before they're gone.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming text on mobile. Violates WCAG SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` is overly aggressive**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applying to `*` suppresses OS-level text scaling for all elements. Should be scoped to `body` at most, and only as a targeted fix — not a blanket rule.

**Conflicting `@font-face` blocks for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` declarations share the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` name:
- First: no `font-weight` descriptor, loads `.eot`/`.woff` (IE legacy)
- Second: `font-weight: 400`, loads `.woff2` only
- Third: `font-weight: 600`, loads `.woff2` only

The unweighted first declaration conflicts with the weight-400 second. Browsers may ignore or unpredictably resolve the duplicate. Consolidate into a single weighted set and drop the legacy EOT block (IE is not a supported email client).

**Double slashes in font asset URLs**
```
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
```
`//Styles/` contains a double slash. Servers typically normalize this, but it risks cache key mismatches at CDN layer. Fix to single slash.

**Overlapping responsive image breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 100% !important} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img {max-width: 690px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules fire; `!important` on the 768px rule always wins. The 690px rule is dead code for viewports above 768px. Collapse into one rule or reorder with correct specificity.

**Mobile breakpoint targets 418px (dated)**
`.mobile { width:414px !important }` maps to iPhone 6/7/8. Current flagship CSS widths are 390px (iPhone 15 Pro) to 430px (15 Pro Max). This doesn't cause breakage but the hardcoded pixel width is fragile.
