# We found you the perfect sofa
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- CB2's design-services arm is doing the right thing with this send — "We found you the perfect sofa" is a strong, pseudo-personalized subject that earns an open, and the "THE HUNT IS OVER" hero headline is the best creative hook CB2 has put on a design-services email all month. The pivot from sender name ("CB2 Free Design Services") also creates inbox separation from the promotional CB2 flood that's been hammering this subscriber since June 3rd.
- The problem is what happens after the open. The email is structured like a brochure: three "CONSIDER" panels explaining how to think about sofa selection, a "3 WAYS TO MEET" block, an inspired products grid, and a "BEST SOLD OUT YET" section — all piled into one long scroll. There's no single dominant product. The "BOOK NOW" CTA is present but its value proposition is buried under editorial scaffolding. The $1000 mention near the footer reads as a threshold, not a reward.
- This is better than the June 6th "Fine dining. Free design advice." send on creative energy alone — but it shares the same structural flaw: the services pitch and the product browse impulse are competing, and neither wins cleanly.

## What's working

- **"THE HUNT IS OVER"** is punchy, emotion-forward, and earns the subject line promise immediately.
- **Sender differentiation** — "CB2 Free Design Services" stands out in a cluttered CB2-heavy week in the inbox.
- **Subject line** at 31 chars is clean and mobile-optimized; "We found you" feels like a warm handoff, not a blast.
- **3 WAYS TO MEET** section gives the reader clear optionality (in-person, presumably virtual, phone) — that's useful for a services pitch.
- **Visual hierarchy** from hero → explanation panels → booking CTA is logical even if the execution is long.

## What's weak

- **No specific sofa is showcased.** The hero shows a styled room, not a named, linked sofa. "We found you the perfect sofa" makes a promise the email never delivers on — there's no product hero with a name, price, or direct link.
- **The $1000 threshold near the footer** undercuts the "we're here to help you" tone — it reads like a services gatekeeper, not a benefit.
- **Three "CONSIDER" panels** before the CTA adds friction; the educational scaffolding is more appropriate for a blog than a conversion email.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is CB2's 6th+ email in six days. Even with the sub-brand sender, the subscriber is in a CB2 saturation window.
- **"BEST SOLD OUT YET"** is an odd editorial frame for a sofa-finding email — it dilutes the single conversion goal and pulls attention toward unrelated items.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the sofa.** Pick one specific sofa with a name, price, and "Shop This Sofa" button. The subject promises "the perfect sofa" — the hero should deliver it. Right now the email makes a personal promise and then hands the reader a design methodology PDF.
- 2. **Move the services value prop above the fold as a one-liner.** Something like "Free design consultation — no commitment" between the headline and the first panel, so the ask is clear before the scroll begins.
- 3. **Cut the "CONSIDER" panels to one.** Three educational panels before a booking CTA is too much runway. Collapse to a single "Here's how it works" row (3 icons, 3 phrases, 1 CTA).
- 4. **Reframe or bury the $1000 threshold.** If it's a qualifying minimum, don't lead with it. If it's a gift-card offer or reward, make that the headline benefit.
- 5. **Remove "BEST SOLD OUT YET."** It's off-brief for a sofa-discovery email; it reads like a leftover module from the Midseason Sale template.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next sofa — picked by our designers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free design consultation, no commitment. Tell us your space.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One conversation. One sofa. No guesswork.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with MSO conditional comment support. A developer placeholder comment was shipped in the production send, and several font-declaration inconsistencies and a narrow mobile breakpoint are the primary rendering risks.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess — HTML is truncated before body links appear.** The following is flagged from what is visible:

- No tracking links or UTM parameters are present in the `<head>` portion. Full link audit requires complete source.
- Font asset requests are made to two distinct origins: `https://www.cb2.com/` and `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/`. Both origins will generate separate HTTP connections; if either CDN is slow or blocked by a corporate firewall, fallback fonts silently replace brand fonts — expected behavior, but worth confirming both CDNs have consistent uptime SLAs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mobile breakpoint is anomalously narrow.**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
This triggers only below 418px. Most Android devices report logical widths of 360–414px and will not trigger this breakpoint. The industry standard for email responsive breakpoints is 600px. Any device between 418px and 600px receives no mobile layout overrides.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation (Resize Text) and may trigger accessibility complaints.

**Global `line-height: 100%` with `max-height` hack:**
```css
*{...; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
The `line-height: 100%` on the universal selector is a known cause of collapsed line-height in Gmail's rendering engine when combined with inherited `font-size`. If any text elements set font-size in px but rely on inherited line-height, they will render too tight.

**Alt text not assessable** — HTML truncated before `<img>` tags appear in the body. Requires full source to verify.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Developer placeholder comment shipped in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unstripped template artifact. "IMPUT" is also a typo (should be "INPUT"). While invisible to end users, it is visible in view-source and signals a QA gap in the pre-send checklist.

No unresolved `{{merge_tag}}` or `%%token%%` syntax visible in the truncated `<head>`. Full body requires review.
