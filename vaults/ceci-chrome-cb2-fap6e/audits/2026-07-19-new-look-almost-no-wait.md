# New look, almost no wait
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- "New look, almost no wait" is a delivery-speed promise masquerading as a promotional send — and it almost works. The hero lands correctly: big lifestyle shot of outdoor furniture, clean "a new look by next week" headline, and a four-item product grid beneath it. The concept is differentiated from the relentless 60%-off drumbeat Crate & Barrel has been running all week.
- The problem is everything below the fold. A sweepstakes module, a lifestyle "bring home this winning setup" room shot, a 30%-off sale banner, a personalized product grid, and an app download promo all compete for the same scroll. The email pivots subject three times before the footer. By the time you reach the actual sale, the delivery-speed narrative has completely dissolved.
- The cadence situation makes this worse. This is the fourth or fifth email from the Crate & Barrel family in under 48 hours — and the sweepstakes module that appears here already ran as its own standalone email on 7/18. Recycling it as a buried mid-email module reads as filler, not value.

## What's working

- **Hero is genuinely on-brief.** The outdoor lifestyle shot is strong, and "a new look by next week" is a refreshing angle after two days of pure sale messaging.
- **Product grid under the hero is tight.** Four distinct furniture pieces with clean labels — gives the browser a fast scan.
- **Up to 30% off is present.** The discount module exists and the CTA is readable.
- **Personalized "we love these for you" grid.** Feels earned — six items, clean thumbnails. Shows the brand knows the subscriber's category.
- **No visible render bugs.** Layout is intact, images loaded, no broken placeholders.

## What's weak

- **Subject-to-body mismatch.** "New look, almost no wait" sets up fast delivery; the email also sells a sweepstakes, a 30%-off clearance, and a mobile app. None of those are "almost no wait."
- **Sweepstakes module is recycled content.** This exact prize hook ("enter to win") ran as a standalone email 7/18 — inserting it mid-scroll here feels like padding.
- **Four separate CTAs, none primary.** Shop outdoor → Enter to win → Bring home → Shop 30%-off sale → download the app. No clear top-priority action.
- **The 30%-off sale is buried.** After the hero, the sweepstakes, and a room-shot module, the actual discount offer arrives below the fold in the fourth module. If the subject hinted at this, it would land harder.
- **App promotion at the bottom is dead weight.** TheDesignDesk app has no connection to the delivery-speed premise. Feels bolted on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one story and cut the other three modules.** If the bet is delivery speed + new arrivals, drop the sweepstakes module, the app promo, and move the 30%-off banner to a single supporting callout rather than its own full-width section. If the bet is the sale, rewrite the subject and lead with 30% off.
- 2. **Make the subject specific about what's new.** "New look" could be sofas, outdoor, bedding, anything — name the category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New outdoor furniture — yours by next week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fast ship: our freshest summer pieces`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Refresh your patio before the weekend. Up to 30% off select styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals that ship in days — shop the outdoor edit now.`
- 3. **Kill the recycled sweepstakes module.** It ran yesterday as its own email. Repeating it 24 hours later as a mid-scroll module tanks cadence trust.
- 4. **Give the personalized recs grid a headline that sells the speed angle.** Something like "ready to ship to you this week" turns the recs section into proof of the subject-line promise instead of a disconnected cross-sell.
- 5. **Consolidate CTAs to two max.** Primary: shop the new arrivals. Secondary: see the sale. Everything else off this email.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — viewport blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — dead/conflicting `img` max-width media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule. For any viewport ≥640px, both match but the 640px declaration wins (last in cascade), making the 768px/600px rule dead code. On screens 768px+, images will be capped at 640px, not 600px — likely not the intended behavior. Fix declaration order or consolidate.

**Issue — redundant `format-detection` meta tags:**
The combined declaration covers all directives:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
```
Then three separate tags re-declare `date=no`, `address=no`, and `telephone=no` individually. Redundant; minor bloat, no functional harm.
