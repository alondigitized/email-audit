# Our 4th of July Warehouse Sale just got BIGGER!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's "update" send for their 4th of July Warehouse Sale — the message is that new items have been added and the sale got bigger. It's competently assembled: clean hero photography, a scannable product grid, clear percentage-off framing, and enough variety (outdoor, kitchen, dining) to have something for most subscribers. The "The Bear" tie-in section is the most tactically interesting moment in the scroll.
- The problem is context. C&K sent nearly the same "new items added / up to 50% off" message on June 25 (scored 7/10), and C&B itself sent a dedicated The Bear email the day before. Including the Bear section again here, 24 hours later, as a sale upsell feels like recycled filler rather than earned relevance. "Just got BIGGER!" is also doing a lot of work without delivering — "bigger" than what, by how much, for how long? The subject promises a revelation the body doesn't quite deliver.
- On the whole this is a solid 4th of July promotional email that would have hit harder if it launched before the sibling C&K sends rather than after them.

## What's working

- The hero is above the fold and leads with the right hierarchy: brand event → discount level → CTA.
- "The Bear" section is smart category-blurring — cookware as entertainment prep. It connects a cultural moment to product intent without feeling forced.
- Product photography is consistent and high-quality throughout; no visible layout breaks or empty image slots.
- The 4th of July seasonal framing is well-timed and the patriotic/festive visual treatment in the hero reinforces the campaign moment.
- The multi-category grid (outdoor entertaining, kitchen, dining, furniture) covers enough of the catalog that almost any home subscriber finds something relevant.

## What's weak

- "Just got BIGGER!" is the hero claim, but no quantity anchors it — bigger by how many items? What new % off? The curiosity created isn't paid off anywhere above the fold.
- "BIGGER!" in all-caps is a mild spam signal that undercuts the otherwise polished sender brand.
- The Bear section ran as C&B's hero send on June 25; seeing it again here as a subsection within 24 hours dilutes both sends.
- The email is very long — outdoor, kitchen, Bear, furniture, "new items for you" — with no editorial spine tying sections together. It reads like a category dump rather than a curated sale pick.
- No urgency anchor anywhere in the body: no countdown clock, no "ends July 4th," no "limited quantities." The 4th of July deadline is real but not activated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Quantify "bigger."** Lead the hero subhead with something specific — "500+ new items added, up to 50% off" or "We just added our biggest furniture drop of the summer." The vague superlative is the single biggest weakness.
- 2. **Add an urgency anchor.** A single line — "Sale ends July 4th" — above the hero CTA costs nothing and converts deadline-motivated shoppers.
- 3. **Drop The Bear section entirely or swap it.** It's recycled creative from yesterday. Use that slot for a category that hasn't already gotten a standalone send this week.
- 4. **Editorial spine.** Pick three hero products from the new arrivals and write a two-line reason to buy each. "We just added the table we kept selling out of" beats another grid of thumbnails.
- 5. **Subject line:** The core message is "new items, still discounted." Write to that instead of "BIGGER":
- **Subject Alt A:** `500 new items just hit our July 4th Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `What's new in the warehouse (up to 50% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor entertaining, dining, and kitchen — all added this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our biggest furniture markdown of the summer just dropped.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: Conflicting desktop image max-width rules**

Two `min-width` media queries define `img { max-width }` in reverse specificity order:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) {
  img { max-width: 600px }   /* declared first */
}
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) {
  img { max-width: 640px }   /* declared second — overrides at ≥640px */
}
```

The 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule, so at any viewport ≥640px, images resolve to `max-width: 640px`. The 768px rule is effectively dead. If the design intent is `600px` at wide desktop, the declaration order must be reversed, or the 768px rule must be moved below the 640px rule.

**Issue: Unescaped `&` in XHTML title**

```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```

The document declares `XHTML 1.0 Transitional`. The `&` must be `&amp;`. Benign in practice (browsers handle it), but technically malformed and will fail strict XML parsers.
