# Their personal spaces. Our collectors' items.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's third collector-editorial email in the span of four days — following the July 12 "How a gallerist lives with objects" and a related July 13 thread — and the creative fatigue is starting to show. The subject ("Their personal spaces. Our collectors' items.") sets an editorial expectation that the email immediately undercuts: by the time you've scrolled past the moody hero portrait, you're staring at a 40% off Summer Edit living-room banner, then a $5,000 design-appointment pitch, then a review solicitation, then a product grid. Four distinct asks in one send.
- The render is clean and the individual components are each well-executed in isolation. The hero photography is on-brand — dark, artistic, collector-coded — and the Summer Edit module carries real visual weight with its lifestyle shot. But those two sections compete for top billing without a connective thread, and the "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" block drops in like an unrelated memo. The structural confusion is the defining problem here.
- The email would work better as either an editorial collectors piece (own the subject line's promise) or a Summer Edit sale email (own the 40% off). Doing both in the same send, with no editorial bridge between them, makes neither land. The July 12 gallerist send earned a 7 by committing fully to one idea; this one hedges.

## What's working

- The hero portrait photography is moody and distinctive — it earns attention and fits the "collectors' items" subject line framing.
- Summer Edit section is visually strong: the lifestyle living room shot + "UP TO 40% OFF" badge communicates clearly.
- The product-recommendation grid at the bottom ("MORE FOR YOU") is well-organized and gives the subscriber somewhere specific to go.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no broken images, proper rendering throughout.

## What's weak

- Four competing calls-to-action (editorial discover, shop the edit, book a design appointment, leave a review) with no clear primary — the email has no single job.
- The subject promises collectors editorial; the first offer visible on scroll is a 40% off sale banner — a bait-and-switch on framing, even if accidental.
- "FIND OUT MORE" on the hero CTA is the weakest possible button copy for an editorial piece — vague, low-urgency, no noun.
- The "$5,000 Refresh / Book a Free Design Appointment" section feels grafted in; it has nothing to do with the collectors thread or the Summer Edit offer.
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" breaks the editorial tone entirely — this is a loyalty/ops message that belongs in its own send, not mid-campaign.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one message.** Split this into two sends: a clean collectors editorial (own the subject) and a standalone Summer Edit sale email. The July 12 gallerist send worked because it had one idea. This one has four.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** "FIND OUT MORE" should become something like "Meet the Collectors" or "See Their Spaces" — keep the editorial register but give the CTA a clear destination noun.
- 3. **Move or drop the review-solicitation block.** A $1,000 giveaway ask has its own send. Tucking it mid-campaign dilutes both messages.
- 4. **Give the Summer Edit offer a deadline.** "UP TO 40% OFF" with no end date removes urgency from the one module that could drive a same-day click.
- 5. **Bridge the editorial and the sale editorially.** If both must live in one send, add a one-line bridge: "The collectors we love? They shop the Summer Edit too." — something that earns the transition.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unfilled template artifact in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a development instruction comment (also contains typo: "IMPUT" → "INPUT") shipped to subscribers. Not harmful, but confirms the send bypassed at least one QA gate.

**`maximum-scale=1` viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 failure for users with low vision. This is common in email but worth flagging given the subscriber base.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied via universal selector, this prevents OS-level text scaling across all elements. Compounds the `maximum-scale=1` issue for accessibility.

**Duplicate `@font-face` family name:**
`"Lineto Brown Sub"` is declared four times — once with just `.woff` src, once with `.eot`/`.woff2`/`.woff` stack, and twice more with `font-weight: 400` and `600`. The first two declarations share the same family name with no weight discriminator. Browsers process these in order; the final rule wins, making the initial two declarations dead weight that add parse time without effect.

**Image rendering** (`-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic`) — the `img` rule is partially visible. Cannot verify alt text coverage without full source.
