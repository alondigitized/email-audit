# EXPERT DESIGN, ON US
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a services pitch, not a product promo — and after CB2's recent drumbeat of "MORE ON SALE," app sweepstakes, and double-sends, the pivot is the right call. The "$5,000 REFRESH" hero hook is strong: it reframes free design services as a budget-sized gift rather than a generic "we'll help you shop" offer, and the two-CTA structure (hero + mid-body) gives the service pitch real estate before the product grid fills out the scroll.
- The problem is what the $5,000 actually means is never explained in the visible hero copy. It reads like a contest prize or store credit before context clarifies it's a design budget they work within. That ambiguity undercuts what is otherwise the sharpest opening CB2 has run in this recent stretch. ## What's working

- **"$5,000 REFRESH" is a compelling hook** — dollar-anchoring the free service makes the value feel concrete and significant, not vague.
- **Two CTAs placed well** — "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" appears in the hero and again mid-body after the services explanation; second placement catches readers who needed the copy to convert.
- **Sender name "CB2 Free Design Services" does pre-inbox work** — signals the content type before the open, reducing bounce for uninterested recipients.
- **Cadence break is earned** — after five promotional/clearance emails between July 18–20, a services email reads as a genuine editorial reset rather than fatigue.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text or broken gaps.

## What's weak

- **"$5,000 REFRESH" is ambiguous without explanation in the hero** — it's unclear whether this is a prize, a credit, or a budget ceiling until you read further down; the hook creates friction rather than pulling the click.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — free design services are presumably always available; nothing in the email suggests why to act now versus next month.
- **The product grids dilute the service message** — LIVING ROOM / DINING ROOM / NEW IN grids at the bottom make this feel like two emails stitched together; it blurs whether the primary ask is "book an appointment" or "shop now."
- **No social proof** — for a service ask (not a product buy), a before/after testimonial or "X customers served" stat would do real work here. Nothing visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Explain the $5,000 in the hero subhead** — one line beneath the headline ("Our designers help you plan a full room, from $500 to $5,000 — no charge for their time") removes the ambiguity without killing the dollar anchor. This is the single change that would most lift conversion.
- 2. **Add a limited-time hook** — "Book by [date] to get priority scheduling" or "Summer appointments filling fast" gives the always-available service a reason to act now.
- 3. **Lead with one real testimonial or project stat** — "Over 10,000 rooms designed" or a two-sentence customer quote in the services section converts skeptics better than the current descriptive copy.
- 4. **Trim or separate the product grids** — either cut them entirely to stay focused on the service CTA, or explicitly bridge them ("Your designer will pull from pieces like these") so they reinforce the appointment rather than distract from it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Viewport zoom lock — WCAG 1.4.4 failure:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting `text-size-adjust` rules:**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; }
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The `*` rule overrides `body` due to specificity. `none` suppresses OS-level text scaling entirely. Change the `*` rule to `100%` to match intent.

**Aggressive global `line-height`:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
A 100% line-height on every element compresses descenders in many email clients (especially Outlook). This is a known rendering defect pattern.

**Mobile breakpoint at 418px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
Modern Android devices (e.g., Pixel 8: 412px logical width) and some iPhones (SE 3rd gen: 375px) are within range, but devices between 419–599px wide receive neither the mobile stack nor a true desktop layout. Standard breakpoint is 600px.

**Image alt text:** Cannot confirm — HTML truncated before `<img>` body tags.
