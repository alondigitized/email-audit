# Early on our app: Venus Williams for Crate & Barrel!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's third Venus Williams send in as many days, and it's the most explicitly app-gated of the bunch — the hero literally puts the collection inside a phone mockup and the only CTA is "Download the Crate App." There's no shop-now link, no product grid for the collab itself, nothing purchasable in the email at all for anyone who doesn't want to download an app. That's a hard filter on click-through no matter how good the celebrity hook is.
- Past the hero, this is a scattershot compilation email — Halloween decor, a random six-item "we love these for you" furniture grid with no unifying theme, an app-exclusive $500 sweepstakes teaser, a credit card pitch, and a free design-consult pitch, all stacked vertically with generic section dividers. It reads like five different emails were pasted together rather than one coherent send.
- The Venus Williams hook is genuinely strong casting and the hero visual (tennis rackets, the phone frame, the palm-and-tile setting) is well produced. But burying it behind an app-only wall, then following it with four unrelated pitches, wastes the one asset that could have driven real engagement. Net: attractive production, weak conversion architecture.

## What's working

- Hero art direction is polished — model, tennis-racket prop styling, and phone-mockup framing feel premium and on-brand for a celebrity collab.
- The "so scary, it's chic" Halloween section has a clear, specific price anchor ($9.95) and a real seasonal hook.
- Clean typography and generous whitespace keep the long scroll from feeling cluttered.
- Footer navigation and social/follow block are complete and standard — no broken elements.

## What's weak

- Primary CTA is "Download the Crate App" — there is no way to shop the Venus Williams collection from the email itself, which kills click-through for anyone not on mobile or not willing to install an app cold.
- The "we love these for you" grid (sofas, a dresser, a dining table, a swivel chair) has zero visible connection to Venus Williams or any stated personalization logic — it looks like a stock recommendation block dropped in.
- Too many competing offers in one email: app-exclusive collab, Halloween sale, unrelated furniture picks, $500 sweepstakes, credit card rewards, and free design service — no single dominant CTA survives that stack.
- This is the third Venus Williams-themed send this week (following the 2026-09-14 "Shop it first" email); repeating the same celebrity hook without a new angle or product reveal risks fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the Venus Williams collab a real in-email shoppable moment — even three product tiles with prices — instead of forcing an app download as the only path forward; app-gating should be a secondary CTA, not the only one.
- 2. Cut the email down to two sections max: the collab hero and one complementary offer (Halloween or the sweepstakes, not both plus furniture plus credit card plus design service).
- 3. Drop or re-theme the "we love these for you" grid — either tie it explicitly to Venus Williams' aesthetic or remove it; unlabeled generic recommendations dilute the celebrity narrative.
- 4. Since this is the third Venus Williams email this week, differentiate the subject/hook from prior sends — lead with a specific product from the line rather than repeating "early access" language.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Venus Williams x Crate & Barrel: the collection, first look`
- **Subject Alt B:** `App-exclusive: shop Venus Williams before anyone else`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `48-hour early access to the full collection — furniture, decor, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what Venus designed for your home, only in the app.`
