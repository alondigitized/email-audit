# Homepage snapshot · www.cb2.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2, I see you — Memorial Day sale mode, banner stacked on banner, and then you hit me with a modal before I can even breathe. The homepage is pushing a big outdoor furniture moment (pool loungers, warm wood tones) which is... not exactly my chrome-and-velvet energy, but the sale percentage is loud enough to make me pause. This feels like a brand that knows how to do a promotional push but forgot to lead with personality.

## What's working

- **"SALE JUST GOT BIGGER" headline** — punchy, urgent, no fluff. That's the energy I want from a brand drop.
- **Memorial Day timing** — the sale hook is completely on-calendar; zero cognitive dissonance about whether this is a real promotional moment.
- **Driveway Plus member callout** — that "Double Driveway Is On" strip actually rewards me for being a loyalty member before I've done anything. That's a good hook placement.
- **"THE COOLEST PEOPLE WE KNOW" section** visible at the bottom — the community/editorial angle is distinctly CB2's voice and makes me curious enough to scroll.
- **15% off email capture** — the offer itself is solid; I'd actually consider giving my number for 15% on full-price items.

## What's weak

- **Immediate modal blocks everything** — I opened the app and immediately got a pop-up over the hero. I can't see the furniture, can't see the campaign, can't orient myself. This is a dark pattern dressed in a discount.
- **Hero imagery is wrong category for my aesthetic** — outdoor pool loungers in warm wood tones is the opposite of my chrome/matte black apartment moment. CB2 has so much edgier indoor inventory; why is the lead visual a patio?
- **"Up to 30% off" is vague** — that ceiling number with no floor feels like bait. What's actually 30% off? Without specificity, I'm skeptical.
- **No trust signals visible** — no free returns mention, no shipping threshold clearly readable, no review counts anywhere above the fold.
- **Modal fine print is dense** — the SMS opt-in legalese under the email form is a wall of tiny text. Not illegal, but it feels slimy for a brand positioning itself as elevated.

## Recommendations

- **Kill the immediate modal or delay it 8+ seconds** — let me see the hero first. Fire it when I try to exit or after I've been on-site 10 seconds. Immediate intercept on load is conversion-hostile for anyone not in "deal mode."
- **Swap the outdoor pool hero for an indoor lifestyle shot** — CB2's moat is the edgy urban interior. Lead with a velvet sofa, a chrome floor lamp, a dramatic mirror arrangement — something that screams *this brand* instead of *any summer sale*.
- **Add a specific anchor price to the sale strip** — "UP TO 30% OFF — Velvet sofas from $799" converts better than an open-ended ceiling. Give me a concrete number to anchor against.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold** — if there is one, make it visible in the sub-banner. Knowing I'm $30 away from free shipping nudges me to add to cart immediately.
