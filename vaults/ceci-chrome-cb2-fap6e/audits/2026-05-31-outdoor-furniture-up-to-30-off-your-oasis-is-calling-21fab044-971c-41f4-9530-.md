# Outdoor furniture. Up to 30% off. Your oasis is calling →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-timed outdoor furniture sale email from Crate & Barrel's dedicated SALE sender, landing at exactly the right moment — late May, right before outdoor entertaining season. The hero delivers "up to 30% off" clearly, the lifestyle photography is aspirational without being inaccessible, and the six-category nav strip is a genuinely useful shortcut for someone actively in-market for patio pieces.
- The email earns its keep through good timing and a legible offer structure, but it buries itself under its own weight below the fold. After the hero and the "It's time to host outside" mid-section, you get a personalized product grid, then a credit card pitch, then an app promo, then a design service upsell. By the third cross-sell, the outdoor sale that opened the email is a distant memory.
- Against recent sends: the May 30 Crate & Kids clearance email was already showing "up to 60% off" — so subscribers who saw that yesterday are now staring at a weaker ceiling. The cadence is also strained; this is the third Crate & Barrel brand email on May 31 alone. Solid execution of a familiar format, no more.

## What's working

- **Perfect seasonal timing**: "Your summer oasis" on May 31 lands exactly when backyard projects kick off — this is rare and the brand actually nailed it.
- **Offer is redundantly clear**: "up to 30% off" appears in the subject AND the hero, so the value prop survives any partial read.
- **Six-category nav strip** (Sofas & Sectionals, Dining, Lounge Chairs, Umbrellas, Shop Outdoor Sale, Shop All Outdoor) gives an in-market shopper a useful direct jump without scrolling.
- **"We love these for you" product grid** shows items with visible sale pricing — the right format for a promotional email.
- **Clean, bug-free render** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, consistent layout top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **Four competing CTAs below the hero** (outdoor entertaining, personalized products, credit card, app, design service) fragment the email and dilute conversion focus.
- **"Up to 30% off" is a weak ceiling this week** — the May 30 Crate & Kids clearance send was advertising up to 60% off. The relative framing works against this email with any subscriber who's been following their inbox.
- **No urgency mechanism** — no deadline, no "ends Sunday," no countdown. A summer sale email with no close date gives subscribers zero reason to click today vs. next week.
- **Product grid is too small to scan** at render size — sale prices and product names require zooming, eliminating the "see something you want" impulse-click moment.
- **Credit card + app + design service block** reads as brand housekeeping, not a curated subscriber experience — three back-to-back non-sale asks that follow immediately after the product section.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove or push credit card / app / design service to the footer.** The outdoor sale is the one message this email has — every non-sale pixel below the fold is a vote against clicking the furniture CTAs.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero**: "Up to 30% off — ends June 8" converts browsers into buyers. Without a date, this is a someday email.
- 3. **Feature 1-2 hero products with full names and prices above the fold** — not just a lifestyle shot. The product grid is doing the right job, but it's too small and too low.
- 4. **Cut "Your oasis is calling →" from the subject** — it adds 22 characters without adding urgency or information, and pushes past mobile-safe length.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor furniture up to 30% off — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your patio at 30% off. Shop the outdoor sale.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining sets, lounge chairs — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Complete your outdoor setup before summer hits.`
- 5. **Sharpen the mid-section CTA** — "Shop Outdoor Entertaining" is too generic; "Shop dining sets up to 30% off" tells you exactly what you're clicking into.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Wildcard selector suppresses OS-level text scaling across all elements. Should be scoped to `body` only, not `*`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewport ≥ 768px both rules apply; cascade order makes the 600px rule win, clipping images that are sized for a 640px container. The 768px block should be removed or consolidated.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone`, `address`, and `date` are declared twice each (once in the combined tag, once individually). Redundant but harmless.

**Alt text on images** — not verifiable from truncated source; must be confirmed separately.
