# Fine dining. Free design advice.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- CB2 pivots from its product-sale drumbeat with a pure services email — and the execution is mostly solid. "Fine dining. Free design advice." is the best subject line in CB2's recent run: short, parallel, and genuinely surprising in a way that earns the open. The hero does its job — a warm, aspirational dining room with a clear "WITH FREE DESIGN SERVICES" callout — and the body scaffolds the offer logically: services intro → product accessories → three ways to book → product grid.
- The core tension is the commitment gap. Every other CB2 email this week asked you to shop; this one asks you to schedule a consultation. That's a meaningfully higher-friction action, and the email doesn't do enough to pre-suade skeptics who wonder what "free" actually means. The "$1,000" figure buried near the bottom of the scroll — likely a gift card or minimum purchase threshold tied to the service — should be much higher in the email.
- This is also CB2's second send today (the editorial "*This* is midcentury" already landed earlier). The cadence is compounding and the goodwill from that strong send risks being diluted here.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the week's best** — "Fine dining. Free design advice." breaks the "MIDSEASON SALE" repetition cleanly; the two-beat parallel structure is punchy and memorable at ~32 chars.
- **Hero-to-service bridge is clear** — a styled dining room directly tied to a design service offer; no conceptual leap required.
- **"3 WAYS TO MEET" section with individual BOOK NOW buttons** — smart friction-reducer; it pre-answers the "how do I even start?" objection and gives the reader three clear paths.
- **"THE FINER TOUCHES" product module** — the dining accessories grid gives transactional readers something to click even if they're not ready to book a consult.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no layout issues, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- **$1,000 incentive buried too deep** — if that figure is a gift card or service credit for booking, it's the single most compelling line in the email and it's nowhere near the hero.
- **"Free" never gets explained** — in a brand-trust-heavy category like design services, "free" with zero qualification creates hesitation, not urgency. What's the catch? What's included? The email doesn't say.
- **Second email on the same day** — coming after "*This* is midcentury" this morning, the cadence is visibly heavy and this send will cannibalize the goodwill from the earlier, stronger send.
- **No social proof** — a design services email is exactly where a two-line client quote or "over X,000 rooms designed" social proof would close the hesitation gap; none is visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the $1,000 incentive to the hero** — if there's a meaningful financial hook (gift card, design credit), it belongs in the first scroll. A hero that reads "Fine dining. Free design advice — plus a $1,000 gift card" closes much faster.
- 2. **Add one trust signal in the services section** — a client testimonial, a "X rooms designed this year" stat, or a designer headshot + name. Free design services require trust before commitment; the email assumes it.
- 3. **Explain "free" in one line** — add a parenthetical or subhead: "No commitment. No fee. Just a conversation about your space." Kills the catch-anxiety immediately.
- 4. **Consolidate to one email per day** — swap the order if needed, but the CB2 team should pick the stronger email for any given day and sit on the other.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your dining room, designed free`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Book a free designer. Keep the $1,000.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three ways to meet — in person, virtual, or by phone. No commitment required.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us your vision. We'll handle the rest — at no charge.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Severity | Location | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | `<meta name="viewport" ...>` | `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 violation for low-vision users. Remove or raise to `maximum-scale=5`. |
| Medium | `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none }` | Global wildcard selector suppresses OS-level font scaling for all elements. Scope to `body` or `table` only. |
| Low | `* { line-height: 100% }` | Applied globally on `*` — intended to fix Gmail clipping but can cause text truncation in non-standard clients. Should be scoped to `table td` or specific containers. |
| Low | `@font-face` — "Lineto Brown Sub" | Family name declared three times with conflicting `src` stacks. The third declaration (`font-weight: 400`) silently overrides the first catch-all rule. Consolidate into a single `@font-face` block per weight. |
| Info | `@font-face { font-display: swap }` | `font-display` is ignored by all major email clients. Harmless noise but adds byte weight. |
| Info | HNW05 font loaded from `crateandbarrel.com` | Cross-domain font load from a sibling brand. Will 404 if that domain's CORS policy changes. Consider hosting on `mail.CB2.com` or the ESP's CDN. |
