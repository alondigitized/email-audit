# Welcome to CB2
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic orientation welcome email — brand positioning, category discovery, app install, rewards enrollment, and a soft intro offer all stacked into one long scroll. CB2 executes the format cleanly: the hero is atmospheric and the "WELL MADE IS ALWAYS WORTH IT" tagline delivers a real brand promise rather than a throwaway welcome. The visual standard is high and consistent with the prior send (2026-05-17 "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE").
- The problem is structural: six competing modules fight for attention below the fold. App download, rewards, registry, and a 10% off offer all appear in the same email with roughly equal visual weight. A new subscriber doesn't know which action matters, so most of them take none. The welcome email is the highest-open moment in the entire subscriber lifecycle — diluting it with a feature dump wastes that shot.
- Verdict: A well-dressed email that earns its place in the inbox but undersells the welcome moment. The 10% off hook is buried when it should be the headline.

## What's working

- **Hero executes the brand promise.** "WELL MADE IS ALWAYS WORTH IT" is a real positioning statement — it connects quality to price justification, which CB2 needs given its premium tier.
- **Category navigation tiles are scannable.** Living Room / Bedroom / Decor & Mirrors / Pillows & Throws gives a new subscriber a mental map without overwhelming them.
- **Rewards + intro offer are both present.** "EARN 10% BACK" and "GET 10% OFF" together make a credible value stack for a new subscriber deciding whether to stay subscribed.
- **No visible render bugs.** Layout is clean, images loaded, no overlapping text or empty placeholder blocks.
- **Instagram/feed section grounds the brand in real life.** "YOUR DAILY DOSE OF LUXURY" with the feed CTA adds social texture that the product shots alone don't.

## What's weak

- **10% off is buried four modules deep.** The single most motivating offer for a new subscriber is the last thing they see. It should be the hero's sub-headline.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic.** A welcome email's primary click should go to a curated new-arrivals or best-sellers collection, not the homepage. "Shop What's New" or "Start With Our Favorites" earns the tap.
- **App download prompt is premature.** Asking a brand-new subscriber to download the app before they've purchased once is a low-conversion ask that dilutes the email's conversion focus.
- **No personal greeting or personalization signal.** "Welcome to CB2" in the subject with no name anywhere reads as a batch send. Even a first-name in the hero ("Welcome, Alon") would have lifted the welcome-moment warmth.
- **Compared to the prior send:** The 2026-05-17 "FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" email had a single focused concept that made the hero image and the offer tell one story. This welcome email has six stories and tells none of them fully.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 10% off the hero sub-headline.** Pull it directly under "WELL MADE IS ALWAYS WORTH IT" — e.g., "Here's 10% off your first order." The welcome moment is the highest-intent read; lead with the conversion hook.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a curated entry point.** Link to "New Arrivals" or "Our Editors' Picks" so the first click lands on a discovery experience, not the homepage homepage.
- 3. **Cut the app module entirely from V1.** Move it to email #2 or #3 in the welcome series, after the subscriber has browsed or purchased. The welcome moment should close one action, not three.
- 4. **Add first-name personalization in the subject and hero.** Even simple token personalization signals "we see you" to a new subscriber.
- 5. **Consolidate rewards + intro offer into a single "Your benefits" block.** Right now they appear as separate disconnected modules. One clean "Here's what you get as a CB2 subscriber" summary table is more scannable.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 10% off welcome gift is inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet CB2 — and here's 10% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Discover what well made really means — plus earn rewards on every purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals, living room to bedroom. Your first order ships smarter.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Duplicate `<meta charset="utf-8">` | Appears twice in `<head>` — once inline, once as a standalone tag. Redundant; some parsers may trigger warnings. |
| Redundant `format-detection` metas | A single combined tag (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) is present **and** three separate individual tags for `date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`. The combined tag's `email=no` and `url=no` values have no corresponding individual fallback — inconsistent and noisy. |
| Light-mode only declared | `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` + `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">` — clients in forced dark mode (Apple Mail, Gmail Android) will apply their own inversion algorithm, potentially inverting background colors unexpectedly. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides present. |
| `display:none` on `.ecxhm` | `.ExternalClass .ecxhm { display:none }` hides elements with this class in Outlook.com/Hotmail. Confirm no visible content is assigned `.ecxhm` unintentionally. |

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Remove duplicate `<meta charset="utf-8">`** — keep one, in the first position within `<head>`.
2. **Consolidate `format-detection` metas** — use a single tag with all values, or use individual tags consistently. Remove the hybrid.

4. **Audit `.ecxhm` class usage** — confirm it is only applied to intentionally hidden elements (e.g., preheader spacer).
5. **Verify full footer in untruncated source** — physical address, unsubscribe link, and List-Unsubscribe headers are non-negotiable for deliverability and legal compliance.
6. **Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass** on `mail.cb2.com` via raw headers from a test send before production deployment.
