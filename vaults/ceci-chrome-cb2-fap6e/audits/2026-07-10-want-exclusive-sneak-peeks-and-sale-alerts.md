# Want exclusive sneak peeks and sale alerts?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is an SMS list recruitment email — Crate & Barrel asking existing email subscribers to also opt into text alerts for "sneak peeks and sale alerts." The hero is a clean phone mockup with "get on the list" copy and a "Sign Up for Texts" CTA. Then the email completely changes gears: outdoor dining essentials, a product recommendation rail, an app download pitch, and a TheDesignDesk section stack below. The result is a diluted dual-purpose email that commits fully to neither mission.
- The timing compounds the problem. This is Crate & Barrel's third email in roughly 24 hours — the free-shipping trigger went out earlier this morning, two Crate & Kids emails also landed today. Asking a fatigued subscriber to add yet another channel at this cadence is precisely the wrong moment to make that ask.
- The absence of any incentive to sign up for texts is the single biggest miss. No "get 15% off your next order," no "text-exclusive drops," no "you'll see the sale 24 hours early" — just a vague promise of "sneak peeks and sale alerts," which the email program is already delivering via email anyway.

## What's working

- "Sign Up for Texts" button copy is direct and verb-forward — no ambiguity about what you're clicking.
- The phone mockup in the hero gives the list-building ask a natural visual anchor.
- Outdoor dining product section is seasonally correct for July; the food photography is strong.
- Brand aesthetic is consistent throughout — typography, spacing, and color are clean Crate & Barrel.

## What's weak

- Zero incentive for signing up — no discount, no early access perk, no concrete "what's in it for me" hook.
- The email tries to be both a list-growth play and a product merchandising email, and fully fails at both.
- Cadence is broken: this is the third Crate & Barrel family email in 24 hours, making a "join more channels" ask read as tone-deaf.
- Subject line is a weak question with no specificity — "want exclusive sneak peeks?" is something you could say about any brand's text program.
- The email is excessively long; the app download section and TheDesignDesk module feel bolted on and bury any chance the SMS CTA has of converting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete incentive for signing up for texts** — "Get 15% off your next order when you join" converts list-building emails; vague promises do not. This is the single change with the most lift.
- 2. **Cut the email to one job** — strip out the product rails, app module, and TheDesignDesk. A focused list-building email that ends at the hero CTA (with one testimonial or "what you'll get" benefit row) will outperform this kitchen-sink layout.
- 3. **Send this on a lighter-cadence day** — the ideal moment for a list-growth ask is a standalone, not sandwiched between free-shipping triggers. Hold this for a day when nothing else has hit the inbox.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the incentive, not the ask** — the question format is passive; lead with what the subscriber gets.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Text subscribers get 15% off — join now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Be first to the sale: join our text list`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See new drops and exclusive offers before they hit your inbox.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Text-only deals, early sale access, and zero inbox clutter.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting `img` `max-width` media queries (confirmed bug):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
```
At viewport widths ≥ 768px, the 600px rule overrides the 640px rule, constraining images to 600px on full desktop even if the container supports more. The ordering makes the 600px cap the effective desktop value.

**Zoom prevention via viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom in webview renders (iOS Mail, Gmail app). WCAG 1.4.4 failure for users needing text magnification.

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via wildcard:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applies to all elements, suppressing iOS automatic text-size correction globally. Should be scoped to `body` only, or use `100%` rather than `none`.
