# New! The Ninja espresso machine that’ll make your summer →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel breaks six consecutive days of warehouse-sale drumbeat with this Ninja espresso machine launch — and it lands well. The hero is clean: a single product, an "Only at Crate & Barrel" exclusivity badge, and a clear product shot. After the cadence fatigue of the June 19–21 blitz ("up to 60% off!" → "up to 50% off!" → "ON NOW!"), this editorial reset is exactly what the list needed.
- The problem is the email can't quite decide what it is. The subject and hero promise a product spotlight, but scroll down and you're back in the warehouse-sale funnel with secondary product grids and discount callouts. The Ninja machine deserves a clean feature send; instead it shares real estate with a grab-bag of summer home goods.
- Overall verdict: above average execution for a new-product launch. The exclusivity angle is a real hook. But the email undersells its own most interesting idea by stuffing in the sale clutter.

## What's working

- **Exclusivity is the right lead.** "New! & Only at Crate & Barrel" is a genuine differentiator — it gives a reason to buy now that isn't a discount.
- **Clean hero visual.** The Ninja espresso machine is the immediate focal point; no clutter competes with it above the fold.
- **Cadence reset earns opens.** After six warehouse-sale sends, a product-first subject line feels fresh and is less likely to be filtered as noise.
- **CTA buttons are visible and unambiguous** throughout the scroll — green/teal buttons with enough contrast to be scannable.
- **Summer framing is on-season** and matches the "make your summer" lifestyle hook in the subject.

## What's weak

- **Hero carries no price.** A new product launch with no MSRP visible means the reader has to click just to find out what it costs — unnecessary friction on an exclusive item.
- **Secondary sections dilute the story.** The email devolves into a product grid that looks identical to the warehouse-sale sends from June 19–21. The Ninja pitch and the summer home goods clearance are fighting for the same email.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** "New" is not a deadline. An exclusive launch without scarcity or a ship-by date leaves the reader with no reason to act today vs. next week.
- **Subject runs long (~58 chars).** On mobile the "→" arrow likely clips, removing the call-to-action punctuation that was presumably intentional.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the price or an anchor.** Add the MSRP (or "starting at $X") directly under the product name in the hero. Exclusivity + a specific number is far more compelling than exclusivity alone.
- 2. **Cut the secondary product grid or move it below a hard divider.** Let the Ninja machine breathe. If the warehouse sale must appear, relegate it to a clearly labeled "While you're here" module at the bottom.
- 3. **Add a soft urgency signal.** "Available now — new arrivals sell out fast" or "Be the first — just arrived" costs nothing and nudges the fence-sitters.
- 4. **Shorten the subject** — drop the arrow and trim the lifestyle tail.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New at Crate & Barrel: The Ninja espresso machine`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Only here: Ninja's new espresso machine is in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The cold brew, lathe, and espresso machine Ninja just dropped — exclusively ours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the Ninja espresso machine before it sells out — only at Crate & Barrel.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**3a. Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules**
Two competing `@media` blocks targeting desktop viewports set different image caps:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥768px, both rules apply; the 768px rule appears later in source order and wins, capping images at **600px** on a 640px-wide template. This under-renders images by 40px at desktop widths. One of these rules should be removed.

**3b. Duplicate `@media (max-width: 460px)` blocks**
Mobile overrides are split across two separate `<style>` elements — one in the "MI styling" block and one in the "CLIENT styling" block. Duplicate properties between them (e.g., `.container`, `.contenttable`) create unnecessary specificity ambiguity. These should be consolidated into a single block.

**3c. Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied universally via the `*` selector. This disables iOS Safari's auto text scaling across all elements. Standard practice is `100%` (preserve scaling) not `none` (disable it). May cause readability issues on small viewports.

**3d. Duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags**
`telephone=no`, `date=no`, and `address=no` each appear twice in `<head>` — once combined and once standalone. Redundant but harmless; should be deduplicated for cleanliness.

**3e. `lang` attribute** — `lang="en"` present on `<html>`. No issue.

**3f. Alt text** — Cannot verify; image tags not visible in truncated source.
