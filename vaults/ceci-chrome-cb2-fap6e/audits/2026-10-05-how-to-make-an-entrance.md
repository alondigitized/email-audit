# How to make an entrance
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, editorial entryway-furniture send. It sells a mood ("First impressions are everything") rather than an offer. The hero and the three stacked lifestyle panels (Davison, Rio and Scatola consoles) are well composed and in-brand. The "Shop New in Entryway" button appears twice, so the path is obvious.
- It is also a no-discount email that landed the same day as "TIME'S RUNNING OUT" (Fall Design Sale, up to 50% off). A subscriber who just saw a 50% urgency send gets a calm, full-price console pitch with no sale tie-in. The subject, "How to make an entrance," is cute but says nothing about entryways or product.
- Judgement: strong creative and weak commercial logic. It will do brand work for a browsing audience, but it won't move anyone who is waiting on a deal.

## What's working

- The hero ("First impressions are everything / What's your entry saying?") is on-theme and gives a clear first CTA above the fold.
- The three consoles are shot in warm, cohesive lifestyle scenes, with the product name and designer in the caption.
- "Shop New in Entryway" is a specific, category-aligned button, repeated after the third panel.
- The "There's more" 2x2 grid (mirrors, table lamps, decor, wall art) widens the path without clutter.
- The fabric swatch banner and the "More for you" grid with visible prices add useful secondary routes.

## What's weak

- There is no discount, deadline or sale reference, even though the Fall Design Sale was the main push earlier the same day.
- The "Don't see your fabric of choice?" banner has low-contrast text over a busy photo, and the copy is hard to read.
- The $1,000 app giveaway sits mid-email as a big number, but it's a sweepstakes entry. It competes with the entryway story and has weak relevance.
- The three hero panels link to one generic CTA rather than to each console, so the product captions don't pay off.
- The footer carries long, dense legal and rewards text, and the loyalty benefit (10% back in Rewards Dollars) is buried there.

## Recommendations

- 1. Tie the entryway story to the live sale. Add a line such as "Up to 50% off entryway, ends tomorrow" in the hero, so the creative carries the strongest offer.
- 2. Link each console panel to its own product page, with price and a "Shop the Davison" style button.
- 3. Move the app-download/sweepstakes block below "More for you", or drop it from this send.
- 4. Fix the contrast on the fabric swatch banner with a darker overlay or a solid panel.
- 5. Pull the Rewards 10% back benefit up into a one-line strip near the top.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Entryway consoles, up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your entryway, finished: new consoles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dark oak, marble and sculpted wood consoles, plus Fall Design Sale pricing through tomorrow.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three new consoles designed to make guests pause at the door.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed rendering defect, an unresolved merge token (`%%reviews_skulist%%`), plus 11 content images with no alt text and 11 insecure third-party `http://` pixels. The QA run passed 43% of checks. Several compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **71 tracking links were not probed.** They go through click-redirect domains, so their final destinations, status codes and redirect chains are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Insecure third-party pixels.** These load over plain `http://`:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199b...=1`
  - Mail clients may block or rewrite them, and the cookie-sync calls will fail silently. They also expose requests in cleartext.
- **Many third-party calls.** The email makes 11 Dotomi (Epsilon) calls in addition to the first-party pixels `mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif` and `dv.cb2.com/o/3c45ef75-...`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 11 content images.** All are hosted on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`:
  - `e2e2388e-...jpg`, `fe88f89a-...jpg`, `972aa036-...jpg`, `3c723f49-...jpg`, `67f2e534-...jpg`
  - `8d09e9e5-...jpg`, `2cd4907d-...jpg`, `9360acdf-...jpg`, `66a75060-...jpg`, `badb0ec1-...jpg`
  - `8e957a75-...gif`

  With images blocked or read by a screen reader, there is no text fallback.
- **Tracking pixels without alt.** The Dotomi pixels, `o.gif` and `dv.cb2.com/o/...` have no `alt`. Add `alt=""` so assistive tech skips them.
- **Zoom disabled.** The viewport meta has `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. The CSS also sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none`, which blocks user text scaling in iOS Mail.
- **Template leftover.** The head contains `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. This is a placeholder comment, with a typo, that shipped to production.
- **Font URLs.** `@font-face` rules load fonts from `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...`, a different domain. Some `cb2.com//Styles/...` URLs have a double slash, which may fail on strict servers. I couldn't confirm that either one breaks. Most clients ignore web fonts anyway.

The reviews or SKU-list dynamic content failed to populate. It may be visible text, or it may sit in an attribute or URL. The truncated source doesn't show which.
