# The 3 things our pros look for in a nightstand →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-built editorial guide. "Nightstands 101 with The Design Desk" is framed around practical questions (storage, space, statement, light, functionality), and each question gets a lifestyle room shot, a named product and a shop button. Crate & Barrel's tone is consistent and trustworthy, and nothing looks broken.
- The send is muddled on three counts. The top banner sells the Thanksgiving dining room event (up to 30% off), which has nothing to do with nightstands. The subject promises "3 things," but I count five question sections. The tail end also drifts into a $5,000 sweepstakes, Affirm financing, a cardholder promo and an app download. It starts as one clear nightstand story and ends as a generic catalog.
- This matches the brand's pattern this week. The earlier 10/16 sends, such as "1 new nursery chair, customized 4 ways" and the "I can do it myself" toddler email, also opened on an unrelated sale banner and scored 6/10. This one lands at the same level.

## What's working

- The "nightstands 101" framing gives the email a useful job, not just a product dump.
- Each section opens with a plain question, so a skimmer knows where they are.
- Room photography is strong, and the dark-wood close-up and the lamp shot add variety.
- Section-level "Shop…" buttons keep product intent close to the content.
- The Design Desk branding gives the advice a credible "pros" voice that matches the sender name.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "3 things," but the body runs through five questions (storage, space, statement, light, functionality). The mismatch undercuts the hook.
- The top banner promotes the Thanksgiving dining room event, which competes with the nightstand story.
- Most sections carry two buttons, such as a product shop button plus "Shop All Nightstands," so the primary action is diluted.
- The email is very long (about 12,000px). The tail stacks the Design Desk appointment, the $5,000 giveaway, Affirm, cardholder financing and the app promo, and the nightstand story gets lost.
- The only visible discount is "up to 30% off" on dining rooms. Nothing ties a price or offer to nightstands.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject and body agree. Either cut to three sections or retitle the subject to match the five questions.
- 2. Replace the dining room banner with a nightstand-specific message, or drop the banner on editorial sends.
- 3. Use one button per module. Keep the product-specific button and move "Shop All Nightstands" to a single closer.
- 4. Cut everything after the "we love these for you" product grid. The sweepstakes, Affirm, cardholder and app blocks belong in a footer strip.
- 5. Add an offer or price point near the hero, such as "nightstands from $X," so the editorial has a reason to click now.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a table-based XHTML Transitional template, apparently built on Movable Ink (`mi.crateandbarrel.com`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com`). It passes 57% of the automated checks and has no hard failures. Its main confirmed problem is missing `alt` attributes on about 47 images. The compliance and authentication checks were inconclusive because the relay did not capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 82 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so none of their destinations or redirect chains were validated.
- **Third-party pixels:** five `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `&n=5` pixels (LiveRamp) load from a third-party domain. Each one carries the same long hashed identifier in its query string.
- **Identifier in image URLs:** the `mi_u=56aaa278…` token appears in `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` and `o.gif` URLs, and `mi_cid` / `mi_mid` appear in the `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/f377be8d-…` URL. These URLs identify the recipient.
- **Extension-less image:** `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/f377be8d-607f-44ff-8361-84a3d72a97fe?mi_cid=…` has no file extension. It is probably a dynamic or open-tracking image.
- **No broken links confirmed:** the probe didn't run on tracking links, so none are confirmed broken.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (about 47 images, confirmed by QA):**
  - 33 are content images on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/*.jpg|png|gif` (for example `bbf6f9d4-…png`, `56be7a63-…jpg`).
  - 7 are Movable Ink images on `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png`.
  - 5 are the `sr.rlcdn.com` pixels.
  - 1 is `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`.
  - 1 is `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/f377be8d-…`.
  - With images blocked, the content images render as blanks. Screen readers will read out the filenames or URLs.
  - The tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Malformed title:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`, which is invalid under the declared XHTML doctype and should be `&amp;`. The title also doesn't match the subject line.
- **Duplicate meta tags:** `format-detection` is declared four times (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, `date=no`, `address=no`, `telephone=no`). `charset` is declared twice, via `http-equiv` and `<meta charset>`.
- **Template leftovers:** the source still contains the placeholder comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->` and empty conditional blocks (`<!--[if !mso]><!--> <!--<![endif]-->`).
- **Global CSS:** `*{line-height:100%}` and `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center}` apply to every element, so the layout depends on per-element overrides.
- **Image width:** `@media (min-width:768px) { img {max-width:600px} }` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px) { img {max-width:640px} }`. Because both media queries match above 768px and the 640px rule comes later, the later rule wins in the cascade, so the 600px cap is dead code.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FNAME]`) were visible in the truncated source.
- The personalization identifiers (`mi_u`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`) are resolved, so the render is per-recipient.
- The body past the truncation point was not available, so this is a partial check.

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`:** QA found neither header. QA itself notes the relay may strip them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a violation. If they really are absent, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) won't work, and Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The `From:` domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` and the `image.mail.` / `mi.` hosts suggest aligned branding, but alignment isn't verifiable here.
- **Footer:** the visible unsubscribe link and physical address are not in the truncated HTML, so they weren't checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- All 82 links are click-tracking redirects, so final landing URLs and UTM parameters could not be verified.
- No UTM parameters are visible in the available source.
- Check landing page alignment by following the redirects from a full-source run.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to all 33 content images and the 7 `mi.*/p/rp/*.png` images. Add `alt=""` to the `rlcdn.com`, `o.gif` and `dv.*` tracking pixels.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Change the title to `Crate &amp; Barrel` and make it match the subject.
4. Remove the duplicate `format-detection` and `charset` meta tags and the leftover template comment.
5. Remove the dead `img {max-width:600px}` rule, or order the media queries so both widths apply as intended.
6. Capture raw headers (not via the relay) to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
7. Resolve the 82 redirect links in a follow-up crawl to confirm they return 200 and carry the expected UTM parameters.
8. Review the five `rlcdn.com` pixels and the identifier-bearing URLs for privacy and consent coverage.
