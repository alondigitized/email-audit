# Just for you: a holiday sneak peek, only on our app!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids asking its email subscribers to go download an app to see a "holiday sneak peek" — there's no actual holiday product shown anywhere in this email, just a bed with sheer curtains and a vague teaser headline. That's a real disconnect: the subject promises a peek, the body delivers an app-install pitch and a $5000 sweepstakes instead.
- Below the fold it recovers some ground with a legitimate "back in stock: Jellycat" call-out and a solid "we love these for you" product grid with real prices, plus a credit-card cross-sell. But the hero — the thing that has to earn the click — is a teaser with no product, no price, no urgency, just a "Download Our App" CTA. For a Crate & Kids subscriber checking email on a commute, this reads as a lead-gen play for app installs riding on holiday-season interest, not a merchandising email.
- Compared to the Labor Day marathon I've been tracking from sibling brand Crate & Barrel all week (six-plus countdown-timer sends), this is at least a different beat — no fake urgency, no timer. But swapping "final hours of a real sale" for "download our app to maybe see something holiday-related" is a downgrade in click motivation, not an upgrade.

## What's working

- Clean, on-brand hero photography — the styled bedroom looks aspirational and seasonally appropriate for a nursery/kids furniture brand.
- The Jellycat "back in stock" module is a clear, single-product, single-CTA block that a parent shopper would recognize and act on.
- The "we love these for you" grid shows six real products with visible prices — no lorem ipsum, no broken images.
- No fake countdown timer or false urgency — the sweepstakes ("through September 13") reads like real, honest urgency.

## What's weak

- The subject promises a "holiday sneak peek" but the rendered hero shows no holiday product, decor, or imagery at all — just a plain bedroom and an app-download button. This is bait-and-switch.
- Primary CTA in the hero is "Download Our App," not a shoppable action — for an email subscriber who didn't ask to be pushed to a new channel, this is a weak, low-intent ask to lead with.
- Very long, multi-offer scroll (app download, $5000 sweepstakes, Jellycat restock, six-product grid, credit card pitch, app again, design service again) dilutes any single message — nothing says "this is the one thing to do."
- No holiday product is ever actually shown in the whole email — despite the subject and hero copy both promising one — which undercuts the entire premise of the send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put an actual holiday product in the hero.** If the "sneak peek" is real, show one hero holiday SKU (a stocking, tree, ornament, festive bedding) with a price and a "Shop the Preview" CTA — not an app-store badge. This is the single highest-leverage fix since it's the core promise of the subject line.
- 2. **Demote the app-download ask.** Move "It's all on the app" further down or into a smaller module; don't let it be the hero CTA on an email people opened expecting shoppable holiday content.
- 3. **Trim the offer stack.** Pick two priorities max (e.g., holiday preview + Jellycat restock) and cut or de-emphasize the sweepstakes/credit-card/app repeats to sharpen the click decision.
- 4. **Tighten subject/preheader honesty.** Either deliver visible holiday product in-email, or change the subject to accurately reflect an app-exclusive teaser so recipients aren't misled.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Holiday favorites are here — see them first`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New for the holidays: cozy bedding, toys & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: Jellycat restock and a chance to win $5000 from TheDesignDesk`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the first holiday drops before they're gone`
