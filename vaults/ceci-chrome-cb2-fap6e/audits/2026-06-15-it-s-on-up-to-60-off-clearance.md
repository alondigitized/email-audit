# IT’S ON | Up to 60% off clearance!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's third clearance-adjacent send in three days — the June 13 Summer Clearance Event, the June 15 Crate & Kids clearance follow-up, and now this. The fatigue is real. The hero does its job: plants, warm light, and "up to 60% off Clearance" in a large, readable headline. The offer is the email's strongest asset and the layout respects it.
- The problem is everything else competes with it. Below the hero fold there's a product grid, then an editorial TheDesignDesk block, then another product row, then an app-download CTA. That's four different conversion asks in one scroll. The editorial insertion (TheDesignDesk with a lifestyle sitting-woman image) mid-email breaks the clearance momentum entirely — it reads like two different emails stapled together.
- The subject's "IT'S ON |" opener is a cliché urgency device that adds no information and flags spam filters. Given that Crate & Kids sent an identical clearance-percentage email the same day, a subscriber with both accounts received two nearly-identical headlines on June 15. Cadence is the quiet killer here.

## What's working

- **Hero delivers the promise immediately** — "up to 60% off Clearance" reads at a glance; the plant imagery gives it visual warmth without distracting from the offer.
- **No visible render bugs** — the grid, images, and text all appear intact at a clean layout; no broken image gaps or overlapping copy.
- **Product grid has genuine category breadth** — furniture, lighting, and decor are all visible in the grid, making the "clearance" claim feel substantiated rather than one lonely SKU.
- **Brand consistency is solid** — Crate & Barrel's neutral palette and clean typography are intact throughout.

## What's weak

- **TheDesignDesk editorial block mid-email kills momentum** — dropping from a clearance grid into branded lifestyle content with a seated woman is a jarring context switch; it dilutes the urgency of the sale.
- **No deadline visible** — "up to 60% off" with no end date or inventory signal leaves the offer floating; there's nothing pushing action now vs. tomorrow.
- **Subject's ALL CAPS "IT'S ON |" is a spam/hype signal** — adds noise without adding information; the pipe separator reads like a placeholder that never got cleaned up.
- **App-download CTA at the bottom competes with the sale** — a subscriber in buy mode doesn't want to be routed to the App Store; it dilutes the email's singular job.
- **Same-day cadence collision with Crate & Kids** — a subscriber to both brands got two "up to 60% off clearance" emails on June 15; even for a single-household shopper this reads as brand fatigue rather than brand love.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or relocate TheDesignDesk block** — the clearance email should be one relentless sell; move the editorial piece to its own day or a dedicated newsletter. A single focus would increase click density on the sale CTAs.
- 2. **Add a real deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "While supplies last — moving fast" in the hero sub-copy turns this from a passive browse into a timed decision.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to drop the ALL CAPS opener** — remove "IT'S ON |" and lead with the actual offer; it's a better hook on its own.
- 4. **Suppress or delay the app-download footer** — save that unit for a non-sale send where cross-channel loyalty is the whole point.
- 5. **Kill the app block and use that space for a social-proof row** — a few customer review pull-quotes or a "4.8 stars on 1,200 reviews" badge would add purchase confidence that's currently absent.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta restricts user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared after — wins on 640–767px AND 768px+ */
```
The 640px rule is declared after the 768px rule, so cascade order makes it win on all screens ≥640px, including those where the 600px rule was intended. The 600px rule is effectively dead above 640px.

**[LOW] Dark mode explicitly excluded**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light rendering in Apple Mail and modern clients. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. Not a blocker, but will produce uncontrolled inversion or forced light mode.

**[LOW] Duplicate CSS rule blocks**
`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`, and several related rules are declared in two separate `<style>` blocks. Not functionally broken but adds unnecessary payload and maintenance risk.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no` and `address=no` each appear twice — once in a combined tag and once in a dedicated tag. Redundant; the latter declarations override the former.
