# 8 steps for reducing plastic in your kitchen →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel in full editorial mode — a sustainability-framed listicle ("8 steps") that walks through kitchen upgrades with no sale, no discount, and no deadline pressure. The concept is smart: reduce plastic anxiety is a real consumer pain point, and C&B can credibly offer glass, stainless, and wood alternatives. The hero is a lifestyle shot of stainless cookware that sets the premise cleanly.
- The execution is competent but diffuse. Eight steps means eight product categories means eight competing CTAs, each diluting the one before it. The email reads more like a brand magazine spread than a targeted conversion vehicle. There's nothing wrong with editorial sends, but C&B just ran two of them in a 24-hour window (the eclectic table vibe email yesterday, the C&B Kids "favorite part of their space" editorial also yesterday) — the cadence is starting to feel content-for-content's-sake rather than purposeful storytelling.
- As a standalone, this is a solid subscriber-engagement play. As the third editorial in 48 hours during what is supposed to be the Summer Sale window, it fragments attention from the 60%-off message they're also trying to drive.

## What's working

- Subject line is one of the stronger ones in recent sends — a specific number, a real consumer behavior ("reducing plastic"), clean length, no gimmicks.
- Hero cookware image is immediately legible and on-brief for the sustainability angle.
- Layout is clean, no render bugs visible — each of the eight steps gets its own visual compartment.
- Breaking from the "ENDS TODAY / UP TO X% OFF" pattern is the right call here; it restores trust before the inbox tunes out entirely.
- Category breadth (cookware, knives, storage, textiles, cast iron) makes this useful as a reference send — high save/bookmark potential.

## What's weak

- No price incentive anywhere visible — eight CTAs with no offer means each one is an uphill ask.
- Attention budget is shredded: eight steps × one CTA each is too many exits to optimize for.
- Arriving on the heels of two consecutive editorial sends weakens the cadence argument — the third soft-sell in a row reads as avoidance, not strategy.
- The arrow emoji in the subject (`→`) is a small thing, but it's become a tic in recent C&B sends (also in the 7/23 eclectic table subject); it no longer reads as intentional design.
- No urgency hook tethering any of the steps to the active Summer Sale — easy lift, missed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie at least one step to the Summer Sale** — even a single "Step 4 is on sale through Sunday" callout converts the editorial into a hybrid that serves both the content and the revenue brief.
- 2. **Cut from 8 steps to 5** — fewer, deeper sections with larger imagery and more specific product detail beats the eight-compartment parade. Give the reader room to breathe and want.
- 3. **Add one "member price" or "loyalty exclusive" tag** on a single product — even a small one gives a reason to act now rather than save-for-later.
- 4. **Rotate the subject arrow emoji out** — try a colon or em-dash; the `→` has appeared three times this week already.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Conflicting desktop image max-width rules:**
Two `@media` blocks both target desktop viewports with different `img {max-width}` values:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```

The 640px rule appears second in the stylesheet and will win for viewports between 640–767px, but the ordering creates an unintentional cascade conflict for viewports ≥768px depending on specificity resolution. The correct pattern is a single rule at the container breakpoint.

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `<html>`:**
```css
html { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
Disables browser text scaling on iOS Safari. Combined with `maximum-scale=1`, this double-locks font size for users who need larger text. Should be `100%` not `none`.

**Issue — Dark mode not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. Will render with hardcoded light backgrounds in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS) — likely a white-on-white or harsh contrast situation depending on background declarations in the body.
