# On now: Up to 25% off your bedroom refresh!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the cleanest Crate & Barrel bedroom send I've seen from this run — the hero delivers exactly what the subject promises: "up to 25% off your bedroom refresh" over a genuinely aspirational canopy bed shot, with the actual sale price and reg price called out right under it ($1,899 vs. $2,299). Compare that to the 08/22 clearance email that buried its markdown message under a beach photo, or the 08/23 giveaway that led with sweepstakes instead of product — this one has its hierarchy right: offer, hero product, four sub-categories with specific discount tiers, then two clear CTAs.
- Where it loses points is bloat and message-splitting. After the strong bedroom block, it pivots hard into a second "Design Desk" $5,000 sweepstakes, then a "60% off clearance" banner, then a "we love these for you" cross-sell grid spanning six unrelated products (dinnerware, a stand mixer, a beverage tub), then a credit card pitch, then an app-download pitch. By the time a reader scrolls to the bottom there have been five different asks. The bedroom refresh promise from the subject line gets diluted fast.
- Net: a strong top-of-email, undercut by an email that tries to be four campaigns at once.

## What's working

- Hero image (canopy bed) is on-brand, aspirational, and directly tied to "bedroom refresh" — no genericism.
- Actual price shown ($1,899 sale / $2,299 reg) gives the offer teeth instead of just a percentage.
- Four-up category grid (beds, dressers/nightstands, bedding, decor/lighting) each with its own discount tier reads clearly and is genuinely bedroom-focused.
- Two stacked CTAs ("Shop Bedroom Sale" / "Shop All Bedroom") give a scannable primary/secondary action right after the offer.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text aligned, no overlap.

## What's weak

- The email pivots away from "bedroom refresh" almost immediately into a second campaign (Design Desk $5,000 sweepstakes), which competes with rather than reinforces the subject line.
- A third campaign (clearance banner) and a fourth (six-item "we love these for you" grid with non-bedroom items like a stand mixer and dinnerware) further dilute focus.
- Two more asks follow (Crate Card credit offer, app download) — by the bottom the email has stacked five distinct CTAs, none of which is "come back to bedroom."
- The cross-sell product grid has no discount callouts on some items (just crossed-out original price with no clear savings language), which reads weaker than the crisp bedroom section above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to two focused sections: bedroom hero + category grid, then ONE secondary CTA (pick either Design Desk or clearance, not both) — everything else (credit card, app download, cross-sell grid) should move to a separate send or the footer only.
- 2. If the cross-sell grid stays, make it bedroom-only (nightstand lamps, throw pillows) instead of dinnerware and a stand mixer — those items actively work against the "bedroom refresh" narrative.
- 3. Add urgency to the hero — "up to 25% off" with no end date invites the same complaint as recent sends (stale, open-ended promos); a specific end date would sharpen it.
- 4. Give the sweepstakes and clearance banners a single unifying color treatment closer to the top bedroom section so the email doesn't feel like four different brands stitched together.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Beds, bedding & decor — up to 25% off now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a chance to win a $5,000 Design Desk gift card`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on beds, dressers, bedding and lighting`
