# Something special is up to 60% off! Shop clearance →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel doing the opposite of its recent editorial streak — instead of one single-topic, single-CTA send like "Quality favorites at new prices" (2026-07-25, 8.00/10), this is a kitchen-sink mega-email that opens as a clean clearance sale and then sprawls into four unrelated pitches: a Lupita Nyong'o "Design Desk" home-makeover feature, a $5,000 sweepstakes, a store credit card upsell, and an app download push. The hero fold is genuinely strong — clear offer, clear button, no clutter — but by the time you've scrolled past the eighth section you've forgotten this was supposed to be a clearance email.
- The category grid (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, rugs, etc.) under the hero is well-executed and exactly what a "shop clearance" subject line promises. Everything after that is scope creep that dilutes the ask rather than reinforcing it.
- Judgment: strong top, muddled everything else. This reads like three separate campaigns were stapled together to hit a send quota, not one coherent pitch.

## What's working

- Hero banner nails the promise: beach photography, "up to 60% off clearance*," one visible "Shop Now" button — no scroll needed to see the offer.
- The clearance category grid (furniture, outdoor, kitchen, tableop & bar, lighting, decor & pillows, rugs, bedding & bath) gives shoppers a fast way to jump straight to their category.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum, consistent brand typography and color from top to bottom.
- Preview text ("Furniture, decor, rugs & more. Hurry, these deals won't last!") is real copy that adds urgency and category detail rather than repeating the subject.
- Product tiles in "we love these for you" show actual prices, giving shoppers something concrete to click toward.

## What's weak

- The email abandons its own premise halfway through — a "shop clearance" subject leads into a celebrity home-makeover editorial, a sweepstakes, a credit card ad, and an app download pitch, none of which are clearance.
- Too many competing CTAs (Shop Now, Shop All Clearance, See The Reveal, Book Your Free Appointment ×2, Learn More, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment) — a reader has no single obvious next action after the hero.
- "up to 60% off clearance*" carries an asterisk with no visible resolution in-frame, a soft honesty ding even before you factor in the unrelated upsells stacked underneath.
- No deadline anywhere — "While quantities last" is the only urgency cue, and it's vague enough to not actually motivate immediate action.
- This is a much longer, lower-focus send than the brand's own recent single-topic guides (rug guide, coffee table guide, backpack guide) — a step backward in discipline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email in half.** Split this into two sends: a pure clearance email (hero + category grid + Shop All Clearance) and a separate Design Desk/Lupita Nyong'o editorial send. Stapling them together weakens both.
- 2. **Add a real deadline to the hero.** "up to 60% off clearance — ends Sunday" converts vague urgency into something actionable.
- 3. **Resolve or drop the asterisk.** If there's an exclusion, put it inline near the offer, not buried in the footer.
- 4. **Pick one CTA per fold.** If the sweepstakes and credit card content must stay, give each its own clear single button instead of stacking two "Book Your Free Appointment" CTAs plus "Learn More" plus "Download Now" in one scroll.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off clearance — final markdowns`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got deeper: up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, rugs & decor — prices this low won't last the week`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just dropped across every category`
