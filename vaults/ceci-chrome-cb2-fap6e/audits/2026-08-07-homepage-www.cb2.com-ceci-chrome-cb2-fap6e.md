# Homepage snapshot · www.cb2.com · 2026-08-07
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, CB2 knows exactly what it's doing — vintage Danish Modernism reissue, a Swiss architect sofa, "New to Covet" instead of "New Arrivals." This reads like a brand for someone who already has opinions about mid-century reissues, not someone shopping for a starter couch. Right now they're pushing the August Sale (up to 40% off) plus a "New to Covet" designer-collab drop, which is exactly my lane — except there's an email-and-phone-number popup sitting on top of the whole thing before I can even look.

## What's working

- **The "Loop Chair, 1986 by Andreas Hansen" callout** — naming the designer and the year is catnip for someone who chases designer-collab drops first.
- **"NEW TO COVET" hero framing** — smarter than "New Arrivals," it flatters the shopper into thinking she has taste.
- **Reward Dollars banner up top** — "Earn 10% Back in Reward Dollars" is a returning-shopper hook I'd actually register on a second visit.
- **The designer/vintage archive strip** ("Exclusive collections from designers around the world... Meet the Design Legends") — this is straight-up brand storytelling for someone who wants provenance, not just furniture.

## What's weak

- **The email/phone modal eats the whole first impression** — "SAVE 15% ON FULL-PRICE ITEMS*" pops up over the hero before I've even scrolled, and it wants BOTH email and mobile number, plus a wall of SMS-consent fine print.
- **No visible price or specific SKU above the fold** — I see "up to 40% off" in the ticker but nothing tells me what that means for, say, a sofa.
- **No timer or hard deadline on the August Sale** — "August Sale" with no end date doesn't create real urgency for someone who "watches the seasonal sale calendar" like me — I'll just keep watching.
- **Category tiles (Living, Lighting) are half-blocked by the popup**, so the one part of the page that would actually route me to shopping is obstructed on load.

## Recommendations

- Swap the email+phone gate for an email-only ask, or at least let the hero breathe for a beat before triggering it.
- Put an actual end date or countdown on "August Sale" — "Ends 8/31" would push me to act instead of bookmark-and-forget.
- Surface a price or "from $X" on the Loop Chair reissue callout — mystery pricing on a piece this covetable makes me tap away to check, not stay.
- Add a small review-count or "as seen in" trust badge near the designer collection strip — vintage-reissue claims land harder with social proof.
