# Announcing our newest bundles of joy →
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a textbook post-sale gear-change: after five days of Memorial Day discounting and yesterday's double editorial send from Crate & Kids ("Design Desk" + tween bedroom refresh), the brand pivots hard to new arrivals. The nursery theme is handled well — clean lifestyle photography, a visible star-rated crib/furniture collection mid-scroll, clear category navigation pills, and a well-contrasted "Shop Nursery" CTA. For a subscriber actively in the nursery shopping funnel, this email rewards the open.
- The problem is the front door. "Announcing our newest bundles of joy →" is a pun that tells you nothing — no product named, no offer, no urgency. Coming as the third Crate & Kids email in two days, it competes for inbox attention without giving the subscriber a reason to prioritize it. The same-day Crate & Barrel "up to 60% off clearance" drop (already in the inbox) sets a high bar that a no-discount new-arrivals email can't match.
- The body earns its click. The subject does not earn its open.

## What's working

- Lifestyle photography and nursery aesthetic are cohesive — the category reads immediately on first glance
- "Shop Nursery" CTA is above the fold, well-contrasted (gold/yellow button), and unambiguous
- Category navigation pills ("new nursery furniture," "crib & nursery sets," "crib bedding," "storage") give four secondary entry points without clutter
- Star rating and social proof are visible mid-email for the featured furniture collection — a strong credibility signal for a considered purchase
- No render issues — layout is clean, images load, no broken blocks

## What's weak

- Subject is a nursery pun with zero specificity — no product name, no SKU count, no offer, no urgency
- Third email from Crate & Kids in two days; cadence is compressing subscriber patience before it's been replenished
- No price incentive anywhere — a new-arrivals email is a harder sell when yesterday's Crate & Barrel 60%-off clearance push is still visible three messages up in the same inbox
- Hero CTA links to the root nursery category ("Shop Nursery"), not the specific new-arrival collection or the star-rated crib visible below
- "We have these for you" mid-section header is generic filler — it could name the collection or the lead product

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the hero product or collection in the subject.** "Announcing our newest bundles of joy" gives no reason to open. The star-rated furniture collection should be the hook.
- 2. **Add a specific count or scarcity signal.** "12 new nursery pieces just landed" or "first look at our most-wished-for crib" beats a pun every time.
- 3. **Deep-link the hero CTA to the featured crib collection page,** not the nursery category root. The star-rating section mid-email is doing the conversion work — make it clickable from the top.
- 4. **Hold one day before the next send.** The double May 27 send plus this May 28 send is three emails in two days from one sub-brand. A single rest day protects unsubscribe rate.
- 5. **Rewrite "We have these for you"** to name the specific arrivals — "Cribs, bedding, and storage we've been saving for you" is specific and warms the grid below it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `12 new nursery arrivals you haven't seen →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New cribs + nursery furniture, just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our most-wished-for nursery styles are finally here — shop the drop before they sell out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh arrivals: the crib, the storage, the bedding — all in one place, all new.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch:** `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — this email is sent from the Crate & Kids sub-brand. The `<title>` element is read by screen readers and appears in some accessibility trees.

**28 images missing `alt` attributes**, including content images and tracking pixels. Both are problems for different reasons:

- *Content images* (product shots, hero): Subscribers viewing with images disabled receive no fallback text. Affected examples:
  - `bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png` (likely logo or header)
  - `c2ab4970-31bc-4a07-9e54-0ca0f4f9a455.jpg` and 7 additional product images from `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com`
  - Scene7-hosted images: `2025_0105_CBK_NewBornCapsuleLaunch`, `2026_0123_CBK_Clearance_Promotional`, `202504_CBK_JennyLind`

- *Tracking pixels* (`f3c0d22992a01946.png`, `448796.gif`, `o.gif`, `f2179052...`): Should have `alt=""` to suppress screen reader announcement. Currently omitted entirely, which is WCAG 2.1 non-conformant (failure on Success Criterion 1.1.1).

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:** The email declares `format-detection` three times — once as a combined value and twice as split individual declarations. This is redundant noise but not a rendering failure.

**Template artifacts in source:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
Both are stale production comments from the template system. No functional impact, but indicates incomplete template cleanup before send.

**Conflicting `max-width` media query rules:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
The `768px` rule is more specific by breakpoint but declares a narrower `max-width` (600px) than the `640px` rule (640px). On screens between 640px–767px, images cap at 640px; above 768px, they re-cap at 600px. Likely unintentional ordering.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** Prevents user zoom on mobile — a WCAG accessibility failure (SC 1.4.4).

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — unconfirmed:** One-click unsubscribe header not captured. Same caveat as above — relay artifact possible, but must be confirmed. Its absence would also violate current Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy.

**`Authentication-Results` header — unconfirmed:** SPF and DKIM pass/fail status unknown from relay capture. The sending domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`; DKIM alignment should be verifiable by inspecting raw message headers directly. Flag for ESP/postmaster confirmation.
