# Tricky entryway? We’ve got solutions
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Design Desk running an entryway-focused editorial that stitches together console tables, mirrors, rugs, wall art, and lighting into "vignettes" you could plausibly recreate at your own front door. It's a soft-sell service pitch — "book a free appointment," "3 ways to meet a design desk pro" — wrapped around a scroll-heavy product carousel, closer to the "Montessori toddler space" or "shared room guide" editorials than a hard-offer blast, and it lands in the same 5-6 range as those: genuinely nice photography, zero urgency, no real offer.
- The hero is well-composed (arched mirror, warm entry vignette) and the "3 ways to meet with a design desk pro" section is a smart mid-funnel CTA for anyone actually mid-renovation. But there's no price, no percentage off, no timeline — just "shop" and "book" buttons stacked five times over. Compare to the sofa/nursery-chair sends that scored 7 on a tight single-category hook; this one dilutes the entryway premise by drifting into rugs, wall art, and general "bestsellers" by the bottom third.
- Judgement: a competent lifestyle/service email, not a sales driver. Fine for brand warmth and Design Desk lead-gen, weak for anyone scanning for a deal.

## What's working

- Hero image (arched doorway, round mirror, console) is genuinely on-brief for "entryway" and visually the strongest asset in the email.
- "3 ways to meet with a design desk pro" (home / in-store / online) is a clear, well-differentiated CTA block with distinct copy per path.
- Product tiles include star ratings, "Exclusive" and "Bestseller" badges, and price-adjacent context (e.g., "Antique White Terracotta Vase") — decent product-detail density.
- Consistent typography and section rhythm (lowercase editorial headers, centered CTAs) keeps the long scroll from feeling chaotic.
- The reviews/social-proof section ("the reviews are in," 5-star row) is a nice trust beat before the "we love these for you" recs.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere in the main body — every CTA is "Shop," "Shop Entryway Furniture," or "Book a Free Appointment"; nothing says price off or sale.
- The email drifts off its own "entryway" premise fast — rugs, wall art, moodboards, and "bestselling furniture" by mid-scroll dilute the stated focus, echoing the same complaint as the Texas-store-opening send that abandoned its premise.
- It's an extremely long single-scroll email (12+ sections) with no anchor nav or "jump to" — a real fatigue risk on mobile.
- The $500 sweepstakes/app teaser at the bottom ("it all adds up to a home you love, get $500 off a Reward's Dollars") is buried after everything else and easy to miss — that's arguably the closest thing to a real incentive in the whole email.
- Subject promises "solutions" to a "tricky entryway" problem, but the email is generic room-styling content, not problem/solution structured (no "small entryway," "no coat closet," "narrow hallway" framing).

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the $500 Rewards/app teaser or turn it into an actual entryway-specific offer (e.g., "20% off entryway furniture this week") — right now the strongest incentive is buried at the very bottom.
- 2. Cut the email to 5-6 sections max, strictly entryway (console, mirror, rug, lighting, wall art) — drop the "bestselling furniture" and generic "we love these for you" grids that break the theme.
- 3. Make the subject's "solutions" promise literal — add copy callouts like "small entryway?" / "no coat closet?" next to relevant vignettes so the email pays off its own premise.
- 4. Move "3 ways to meet a design desk pro" higher, right after the hero — it's the most differentiated, non-generic CTA and shouldn't compete with six rounds of "Shop."
- 5. Add a preview text that names an actual entryway pain point instead of leaving it blank/generic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Small entryway, big fix: 5 furniture wins`
- **Subject Alt B:** `No coat closet? Start here.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Consoles, mirrors, rugs — plus a free design session`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your entryway's missing 3 things. We found them.`
