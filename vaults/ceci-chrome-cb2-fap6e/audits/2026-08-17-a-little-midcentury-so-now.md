# A little midcentury. So now.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 back in full-catalog mode — "INSTANT CHARACTER" hero for the Belmond sectional, then a cascading wall of "MORE NOW AND FUTURE CLASSICS" (Berkeley, Eastman, Oriole), a new-arrivals grid, a rug spotlight, a sweepstakes banner, a review-ask, a "MORE FOR YOU" product grid, then app/rewards tiles. It's the densest, longest send in the run — more a catalog scroll than a single-offer promo.
- Compared to yesterday's "SUMMER CLEARANCE" (6/10, 2026-08-16), which was praised precisely for being CB2's plainest, most direct send of the run, this one reverses course completely: it's back to the multi-section, multi-collection sprawl that characterized the designer-spotlight stretch earlier in the week. There is no price, no percentage off, no deadline anywhere in this email except a vague "Now through September 16" sweepstakes entry — everything else is pure browse-catalog.
- The hero image and copy are clean and on-brand (warm, editorial, "midcentury" positioning lands visually), but as a marketing email this is an inventory dump, not a targeted send. Judgement: mid-pack execution, weak strategy — this reads like a "we have a lot of new stuff, look at all of it" email rather than one built around a single compelling reason to click today.

## What's working

- Clean, warm hero shot of the Belmond sectional with clear "INSTANT CHARACTER" headline and legible "SHOP BELMOND" CTA button.
- Consistent product photography style throughout — same warm-toned room sets for every section, giving visual cohesion despite the length.
- The "EVERYONE WANTS IT, YOU CAN WIN IT" sweepstakes section stands out with a distinct color treatment and gives one section actual urgency ("Now through September 16").
- Clear category iconography grid (Furniture, Lighting, Mirrors + Decor, Rugs) breaks up the sofa-after-sofa monotony.
- No visible render bugs — images load, text is legible, buttons are consistent.

## What's weak

- No discount, price cut, or promo code anywhere in the main flow — everything is full-price browse content, a step back from yesterday's clearance send.
- Extreme length and repetition: three near-identical sectional/sofa sections (Berkeley, Eastman, Oriole) each with the same "product shot + name + Shop Now" formula before you even reach new arrivals.
- No visual hierarchy beyond the hero — after that it's a flat, undifferentiated scroll of sofas that all blur together.
- The sweepstakes CTA ("SIGN UP FOR TEXT") requires opting into SMS, which is a bigger ask than a simple click, and it's buried two-thirds down.
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalization section shows generic product tiles with prices but no explanation of why these are "for you" — feels like a template label, not real personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this email in half. Pick one hero collection (Belmond) and one supporting theme (new arrivals or rugs) — don't run three sequential sofa sections back to back.
- 2. Add a real, visible offer. Zero price incentive in a 9000px-tall catalog email is a missed opportunity — even a "new arrivals, extra 10% this week" line would give a reason to act now instead of browse later.
- 3. Move the sweepstakes higher and make it a straight email CTA instead of requiring SMS signup — text opt-in adds friction most inbox readers won't take mid-scroll.
- 4. Make "MORE FOR YOU" actually feel personalized (browsing history callout, "because you viewed X") or cut the label — as-is it's a false personalization signal.
- 5. Trim redundant sections (rugs, mirrors/decor grid, app download, rewards) into a single footer block rather than four stacked footer-adjacent modules.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
CB2's "midcentury" campaign email is a standard MSO/VML-conditional responsive template using image-heavy content and a third-party MI (mail.cb2.com) ESP; automated QA flags missing accessibility attributes, unencrypted third-party pixel domains, and absent compliance/authentication headers (the latter likely a relay artifact rather than a sender fault).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 74 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (opaque ESP redirect domains) — not independently verified as functional; recommend manual click-through QA before send.
- 10 sequential Dotomi pixel calls load over **plain HTTP**: `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`, plus `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?...`. Mixed-content HTTP resources are commonly blocked or stripped by mail clients (Gmail image proxy, Outlook), reducing tracking pixel fire rate and adding unnecessary redirect latency (11 sequential ad-network round trips).
- Open-tracking pixels (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/...`) are HTTPS and correctly formed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 13 content images are missing `alt` text (e.g. `51df67be-f5ef-49ab-8e03-cbb8efffa76f.jpg`, `0f2478e7-47ab-4d87-ba33-8f34502998cf.jpg`, and 11 others under `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`). With images blocked by default (Outlook, many corporate mail gateways), this leaves the email with no textual fallback for product imagery.
- Template uses standard MSO conditional comments, VML namespace declarations, and `@media` breakpoints at 418px/690px/768px — structurally sound for cross-client rendering (Outlook desktop, mobile, webmail).
- Multiple `@font-face` declarations (Lineto Brown, GT Sectra, HNW05) with woff2/woff/eot fallbacks and `font-display: swap` — reasonable fallback chain, though most mail clients strip custom `@font-face` entirely and will render the Arial/Helvetica/sans-serif fallback specified inline.
