# Make your dinner party guests jealous
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's Interior Design Services pitch dressed as a dinner-party aspiration play — and it's the most structurally coherent break from the warehouse-sale drumbeat CB2 has been running since early June. The from-name sub-sender ("CB2 Free Design Services") signals the intent before the inbox even opens, and the subject ditches the promotional promise for an emotional hook that earns curiosity rather than demanding a click. The hero delivers: a warm, candlelit dining-room lifestyle shot anchored by a "FINE DINING / WITH FREE DESIGN SERVICES" headline makes the offer legible immediately, and the "3 Ways to Meet" section (in-person, send a photo, virtual) smartly surfaces flexible formats to reduce booking anxiety.
- The problem is conversion architecture. Free design consulting is a high-consideration, high-friction offer — even with "BOOK NOW" buttons, this ask requires a calendar commitment, not a cart click. The product grid tacked on at the bottom hedges toward a standard promo email but ends up diluting the service narrative rather than reinforcing it. The email is trying to be two things at once.
- Net: a smart tonal reset after the six-send warehouse-sale run (echoing the same editorial instinct as the June 22 "saves" curation send, which also tried to buy goodwill with service content), well-targeted for the season, but fighting itself between "inspire to book" and "inspire to shop."

## What's working

- Free design services as the hero offer is a credible, differentiated value prop after weeks of "% off" hammering.
- Warm, candlelit dining-room lifestyle image is strong seasonal creative — June dinner-party entertaining is a real consumer moment.
- "3 Ways to Meet" section does the heavy lifting of reducing commitment anxiety by giving the reader control over format.
- The creative break from the warehouse-sale cadence makes this visually distinctive in the inbox — a real asset after six near-identical promotional sends.
- Subject's social-envy register ("guests jealous") gives this a distinct emotional voice CB2 hasn't used in recent sends.

## What's weak

- "BOOK NOW" demands calendar friction immediately — far harder to convert than a browse-or-buy action, and nothing in the email earns that commitment.
- The product grid at the bottom collapses the editorial focus; it makes the email feel like a service pitch that lost its nerve halfway through.
- No urgency or scarcity signal anywhere — "free, forever, anytime" doesn't prompt action today.
- Sub-sender name ("CB2 Free Design Services") may suppress open rates for subscribers who whitelist the main "CB2" address only.
- The subject doesn't telegraph the free service offer, so a reader who mentally filed this as a product promo will feel slightly mismatched on open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add scarcity to the service CTA.** "Design sessions fill up" is true — CB2 designers have finite hours. A line like "Limited spots available" or "Book before summer fills up" is honest and creates real pull.
- 2. **Cut the product grid or restructure it as proof, not shop-now.** Show two or three "rooms our designers transformed" with before/after thumbnails instead of a generic product carousel — that sells the service far harder than tableware SKUs.
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA.** "Book a Free Session" beats "BOOK NOW" — it restates the zero-cost offer at the decision moment.
- 4. **Send from the main CB2 address** to maximize open rates across the full subscriber base, not just the design-service segment.
- 5. **Add one social-proof element** in the hero section — a stat ("10,000 rooms designed"), a designer headshot with a first name, or a customer testimonial snippet.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**3a. Double-slash paths in font URLs (3 occurrences)**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Servers typically normalize `//` to `/`, but it is an unnecessary defect and will 404 on any server with strict path handling.

**3b. Cross-brand font dependency**
`HNW05` typeface is loaded from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (4 declarations). A CB2 email depending on crateandbarrel.com font assets creates a cross-origin hard dependency — if that domain's font CDN path changes, CB2 emails degrade silently to fallback fonts.

**3c. Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks share the family name `Lineto Brown Sub`:
- Block 1: EOT + WOFF, no `font-weight` declared (implicitly `normal`)
- Block 2: WOFF2 only, `font-weight: 400`
- Block 3: WOFF2 only, `font-weight: 600`

Block 1 and Block 2 both resolve to weight 400 with different source sets. Browser behavior on the conflict is undefined; the EOT/WOFF sources in Block 1 may never be reached.

**3d. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA).

**3e. Universal selector text-size override**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied via `*` selector — disables OS-level text scaling for users who rely on it. This compounds the `maximum-scale=1` issue above.

**3f. Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
414px targets iPhone 6/7/8 viewport exactly. Devices with widths between 418px and ~599px (many Android handsets, iPhone SE 3rd gen in some orientations) receive neither mobile nor desktop layout. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px`.
