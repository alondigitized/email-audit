# $1K COULD BE YOURS TO WIN
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a sweepstakes-as-CTA email — "Download the App, Win $1K" — dressed up with a product grid underneath. CB2 is trying to pull double duty: drive app installs via a contest mechanic, then pad the scroll with new-arrivals product content recycled from yesterday's "SO MUCH NEW" send. The two halves don't actually belong in the same email. The sweepstakes hook is legitimately interesting — a $1K prize tied to an app download is a concrete, low-friction ask — but the email buries the deadline (August 15) and the mechanics in small type, which undercuts urgency on the one signal that could drive action.
- The product grid below the fold feels like an afterthought pulled from yesterday's send rather than a deliberate second act. There's no connective tissue: it just drops from "win $1,000" to "So Much New" with no editorial bridge. That's a missed opportunity to use the sweepstakes hook as a reason to shop immediately, not just to download an app.
- Bottom line: the prize amount is big enough to anchor a whole email, but the execution is diluted. If the goal is app downloads, cut the product grid entirely and give the mechanics, winner selection, and deadline the space they need.

## What's working

- **The $1,000 prize figure is prominently sized** — it's the biggest text in the hero and immediately communicates scale.
- **The app-download mechanic is low-friction** — one step (download), not a form fill or a purchase requirement.
- **App Store badge is visible** — removes ambiguity about where to go.
- **Deadline exists (August 15)** — a real date is present, even if undersized.
- **"Meet the CB2 App" feature grid is clean** — the four icon/benefit tiles (Exclusive Deals, Personalized Shop, Manage Your Items, etc.) give a reason to download beyond just winning.

## What's weak

- **The hero image is decorative dead weight** — a dark, moody room set that has nothing to do with an app or a sweepstakes; it just pads the vertical height before the offer appears.
- **The deadline is set in tiny fine-print type** — "Now Until August 15" is there but visually subordinate; urgency is wasted.
- **The product grid below is a recycled paste from yesterday's "SO MUCH NEW"** — subscribers who opened that email will immediately recognize the duplication; cadence fatigue kicks in.
- **Two competing asks in one email** — download the app vs. shop the new arrivals; neither CTA fully wins attention.
- **"More For You" personalized shelf at the bottom is tiny** — the product tiles are so small they're unreadable at inbox preview size.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make this email do one thing: win app installs.** Cut the product grid entirely. Use the space to show the prize ($1K prize rendered large), the mechanic (download + sign in = entry), and the deadline in a countdown-style visual. One CTA: "Download & Enter."
- 2. **Move the August 15 deadline into the headline** — "Win $1,000 — Download by Aug 15" is a better hero line than the passive "$1K Could Be Yours to Win."
- 3. **Replace the decorative room photo** with a phone mockup showing the app UI — this is an app-install email; the product is the app.
- 4. **If you keep the product grid**, add a line of copy connecting it: "Download the app to shop these first — app members get early access." Give the grid a purpose.
- 5. **Subject rewrite** — the current subject uses a sweepstakes vagueness ("Could Be Yours") that reads like a lottery spam signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win $1,000 — download the CB2 app by Aug 15`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$1K giveaway: one step to enter`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Download the app + sign in = you're entered. No purchase necessary.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `App download = sweepstakes entry. Ends August 15.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Double-slash font URLs (broken asset requests)**
Three `@font-face` `src` declarations use `//` after the domain, producing malformed paths:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers tolerate the double slash, but it is non-canonical and will fail on strict path routing or CDN rules that normalize URLs.

**Cross-domain font loading**
`HNW05` font variants are loaded from `crateandbarrel.com` (parent brand), not `cb2.com`. If `crateandbarrel.com`'s CDN is down or its CORS policy tightens, CB2 emails silently fall back to system fonts.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Production developer placeholder left in `<head>`**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unprocessed template comment (note the typo: "IMPUT"). It should never reach a send. Indicates a template QA gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without assistive technology. This fails that criterion.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied via the `*` universal selector — affects every element. Overrides both OS-level and browser-level font scaling for users with visual impairments. Should be scoped to `body` only and set to `100%`, not `none`.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
The font is declared three times:
- Once with `.eot` + `.woff2` + `.woff` sources (no weight specified)
- Once with `font-weight: 400` pointing to the Light variant
- Once with `font-weight: 600` pointing to the Regular variant

Weight 400 maps to the Light cut and weight 600 to Regular — inverted from convention. Outlook and some WebKit clients may render the wrong weight.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
414px targets the iPhone SE/6/7/8 logical width. Devices with 415–479px viewports (many Android mid-range) receive no responsive overrides. Standard practice is `480px` or `600px`.
