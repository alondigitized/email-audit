# Designed by Andreas Hansen in Copenhagen
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's "Design Legends" archive send, anchored by a mid-century leather-and-chrome lounge chair credited to "Andreas Hansen, Copenhagen" — a real departure from the celebrity-tie-in and clearance loop this brand has been running all week. It's the most editorial, brand-building email in the recent set: a proper design-history feature with five named legends (Hansen, Frattini, McCobb, Ostergaard, Ackerman & Curry), each with a portrait, a product shot, and a one-line credibility blurb. That's a genuinely different creative mode from the sale spam.
- The problem is the top of the email undercuts the whole premise: a hard "ONE DAY LEFT / END OF SUMMER SALE" banner sits directly above the Andreas Hansen hero, so the first thing a reader's eye hits is urgency messaging that has nothing to do with the subject line or the design story below it. The email also buries its own CTA hierarchy — six near-identical "SHOP X" links with no single clear primary action — and the "$5,000 Refresh" sweepstakes banner competes for attention two-thirds of the way down. Compared to yesterday's "Future Heirlooms Under $500" send (5/10, a real price-and-product lookbook), this one has better storytelling but weaker purchase intent — it reads more like a catalog essay than a shopping email.
- Judgment: a solid content idea, poorly sequenced. 5/10.

## What's working

- Named-designer storytelling (Andreas Hansen, Gianfranco Frattini, Paul McCobb, Steen Ostergaard, Evelyn & Jerome Ackerman, Bill Curry) gives the email real editorial credibility, unlike the generic "Goop" celebrity tie-ins.
- Each designer section pairs a portrait/archival photo with a matching product shot — consistent, legible visual pattern repeated 5 times.
- "DIRECT FROM THE ARCHIVES" framing plus city tags (Copenhagen, Milan, Boston, Copenhagen, Los Angeles) adds specificity and a sense of curation.
- Clean footer with app download, loyalty ("15% OFF"), and sister-brand cross-promo (Crate & Barrel, Blinds.com) — no clutter there.

## What's weak

- The "ONE DAY LEFT / END OF SUMMER SALE" banner at the very top has zero connection to the Andreas Hansen/design-legends content that follows — mismatched hero-to-subject.
- No pricing, no product names beyond designer names for most of the top section — pure lookbook, no purchase trigger for 80% of the email.
- Six+ "SHOP [DESIGNER]" CTAs of equal visual weight mean there's no obvious single next action.
- The $5,000 Refresh sweepstakes banner interrupts the design-legends narrative rather than closing it out.
- Subject line ("Designed by Andreas Hansen in Copenhagen") only matches the second section of the email — everything above and below it is unrelated content, so the promise-to-content match is narrow.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the "End of Summer Sale" banner from the top of this send entirely — it's a different campaign competing with the hero and it's currently winning the eye. Lead straight with the Andreas Hansen hero image.
- 2. Add at least one price or "starting at $X" tag to the Hansen chair and 2-3 other hero products — a design-legends story with zero prices reads like a museum wall text, not a retail email.
- 3. Cut the designer count from five to two or three per send and make this a recurring series ("Design Legends Vol. 1") — five legends in one scroll dilutes attention across too many equal-weight CTAs.
- 4. Move the $5,000 Refresh sweepstakes to the very end, after the design content, so it doesn't interrupt the narrative.

# Technical Audit — CB2 "Designed by Andreas Hansen in Copenhagen"

**1. Technical Summary**
Standard MI-platform (Zeta/EPSILON-style) transactional-marketing HTML with multiple font-loading strategies, legacy third-party ad pixels, and incomplete unsubscribe header support at the relay layer.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 67 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP probe (tracked via `mi.cb2.com` and similar redirect domains) — not independently verified as live, but this is expected behavior for click-tracked ESP links and not itself a defect.
- Multiple legacy Dotomi ad-retargeting pixels served over plaintext HTTP: `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?...`, and `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`. Mixed-content HTTP images in an HTML email can be blocked or stripped by mail clients (notably Outlook/Office365 and Gmail proxy in strict environments), silently breaking that pixel's tracking.
- One-pixel tracking beacon `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=...` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/0c05a41e-ba52-4dba-ad8e-36a42b30f634?...` are present and HTTPS — no issue.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 11 content images (`image.mail.cb2.com/lib/...`) are missing `alt` text, including the hero/product shots. This degrades the experience for screen-reader users and for anyone with images-off (the common default in Outlook).
- The 10 Dotomi ad pixels and the UCMController pixel also lack `alt` attributes, though as 1x1 tracking pixels this is standard/inconsequential.
- Extensive `@font-face` fallback chains (Lineto Brown, GT Sectra, HNW05) with `.eot`/`.woff`/`.woff2` sources — appropriately layered for Outlook/legacy support; no issue found.
- MSO conditional comments and `-ms-text-size-adjust` resets are present for Outlook/Windows Mail compatibility — no issue found.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No merge tokens or personalization variables are visible in the truncated source provided. No issues found in the visible markup.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
