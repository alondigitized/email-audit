# How to choose the best rug materials for your space →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's fourth "how to choose X" editorial in about a week and a half, following the coffee table guide (2026-07-24, 6.00/10), the eclectic table vibe guide (2026-07-23, 7.00/10), and the sustainability kitchen listicle (2026-07-24, 7.00/10). The format works — genuinely useful buying-guide content organized by rug material (performance, wool, viscose, sisal & jute), each with two hero products and a specific "Shop [category] Rugs" button — and this time there's an actual sitewide offer ("up to 60% off The Summer Sale") stacked at the top, which the last few editorial sends didn't have.
- The problem is scope creep: after a genuinely strong, tightly-themed rug guide, the email keeps going into a "designers' top tips" listicle, then pivots hard into unrelated territory — coffee makers ("upgrade your morning routine"), a celebrity design partnership (Lupita Nyong'o / TheDesignDesk), a "1500+ new heritage finds" banner, and a random "we love these too" grid. That's five distinct campaigns bolted onto one send.
- For a rug shopper, the first two-thirds of this email is excellent and would drive a click. The back half dilutes it. Judgement: strong content, weak email discipline — trim it to the rug guide and this is an 8; as sent, it's a solid 7.

## What's working

- The material breakdown (performance / wool / viscose / sisal & jute) directly answers the subject line's promise and maps to real shopper decision criteria.
- Every section has its own specific CTA ("Shop Performance Rugs," "Shop Premium Wool Rugs," "Shop Sisal & Jute Rugs") rather than one generic button repeated.
- The "designers' top tips" module (sizing, styling, rug pads) adds genuine educational value and a natural upsell into rug pads.
- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no render bugs.
- "Order Swatches" CTA is a smart, low-friction conversion path for a category (rugs) where texture/color risk is the #1 purchase blocker.

## What's weak

- The "up to 60% off Summer Sale" banner at the very top is disconnected from the rug content below — none of the featured rug tiles show a discounted price, so the sale reads as bait that doesn't pay off in the body.
- The email sprawls well past its own topic: coffee makers, a celebrity home-makeover partnership, and a generic "new arrivals" banner all appear after the rug guide, diluting focus and pushing the "we love these too" grid to a point most readers will never scroll to.
- No urgency or end date on the sale banner — "up to 60% off" with no deadline reads as evergreen, not time-sensitive.
- No social proof (ratings, review counts) on any of the featured rugs, despite material/quality being exactly the kind of decision reviews help with.
- Cadence risk: this is the brand's fourth-plus buying-guide email in under two weeks, and a subscriber who opened the coffee table or eclectic-table guides will recognize the template instantly.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the sisal & jute section (or after the designers' tips module at the latest). Move coffee makers, TheDesignDesk/Lupita Nyong'o, and "new arrivals" into their own separate sends — each is a strong idea getting buried here.
- 2. Either tie the top banner discount directly to rugs ("up to 60% off rugs") or drop it — a sitewide sale banner with no matching pricing on the featured products undercuts trust.
- 3. Add a star rating or "X reviews" line under at least the top 2-3 hero rugs — this category lives and dies on perceived quality/durability, which reviews solve better than copy.
- 4. Give the sale a deadline ("ends Sunday") to convert the vague banner into real urgency.
- 5. Vary the subject template — five straight "How to choose/nail/find X" sends is a pattern power-readers will start skipping.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: the 3 mistakes designers say to avoid`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Swatches ship free — feel the material before you buy`
