# Our 3 tips for a bathroom you’ll love →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Barrel opens strong: "breathe new life into your bathroom" with a genuine 3-tip structure (foundation/function, essentials/organization, natural elements) each paired with real Design Desk renderings and shoppable products. This is the same "Design Desk" service format that scored well on 2026-09-16 ("3 dining rooms, 3 makeovers," 7/10), and the bathroom framing here is just as coherent for its first three screens.
- Then it falls apart exactly the way this brand's history predicts. After the third tip and a "more ways Design Desk can help" module, the email pivots to plush towels, a Dallas Cowboys NFL hosting kit, a "clean kitchen checklist," generic sofa/dresser recommendations with no bathroom connection, a store credit card pitch, and an app download push — none of which touch the bathroom thesis the subject line promised. It's the "Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance" problem again (5/10, 2026-09-17): a focused hook buried under six unrelated modules stacked below it.
- Judgment: strong top-of-email, but this is really two emails glued together. A reader who opens for bathroom tips gets what they came for in the first screen and a half, then has to scroll through unrelated cross-sell to reach the footer. Score it like the mixed-bag sends in this brand's history, not like the disciplined single-thesis "apartment collection" email (7/10).

## What's working

- The "3 tips" structure is genuinely followed through — each numbered tip has its own rendering, curated products with ratings, and a clear subhead.
- "Design Desk rendering" labels on the vanity/shelving images build credibility instead of hiding that these are mockups.
- Two clear, high-contrast CTAs ("Book A Free Appointment," "Shop All Bath") appear right after the tips, well before the reader has to scroll past unrelated content.
- Product cards show star ratings, "Exclusive"/"Bestseller" tags, and clean product photography throughout.

## What's weak

- The email abandons the bathroom subject after the first ~3 screens — towels, NFL tailgating gear, a kitchen checklist, and general furniture recs have nothing to do with "3 tips for a bathroom you'll love."
- The Cowboys NFL hosting module is a jarring tonal break — team-branded aprons/mugs dropped into a bath/home-design email with zero transition.
- "We love these for you" shows six generic furniture items (sofas, dressers, a dining table) with no stated logic connecting them to bathroom shoppers, undermining the personalization signal.
- The email is extremely long for what is, in substance, a single-category (bath) pitch — by the time the credit card and app-download blocks appear, the original hook is long gone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the "more ways Design Desk can help" + Shop All Bath CTA block — end on the bathroom thesis instead of drifting into towels/NFL/kitchen/furniture/credit card/app. If those need to ship, they belong in separate sends (this brand already has send volume for kitchen and NFL content specifically).
- 2. If cross-sell must stay, add a hard section break with a fourth "tip" or clear header ("finish the look with...") rather than silently changing subject mid-scroll.
- 3. Replace the generic "we love these for you" recs with bath-adjacent items (matching hardware, bath rugs, storage) so it reinforces rather than dilutes the hero message.
- 4. Move "Book A Free Appointment" earlier or repeat it once more right before the cutoff point, since it's the strongest, most specific CTA in the email and currently gets buried under six modules.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Foundation, storage, warmth: your bathroom plan`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Design Desk help, starting with your vanity`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See the renderings, then book your free session`
