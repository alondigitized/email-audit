# Enter to win $4,199 in back-to-school goodies!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a giveaway email from Crate & Kids — not a discount send, not a new-collection editorial, but a pure sweepstakes play. The hook is a $4,199 prize bundle tied to back-to-school, and the email enlists seven or eight co-sponsors (Crocodile Creek, Barefoot Books, REEL, and others visible in the logo grid). That co-brand sponsorship is the structural tell: this email exists to build partner traffic as much as to serve the subscriber. The hero does its job — a lifestyle shot of kids' gear (backpack prominent), prize value called out clearly, and a clear CTA button above the fold.
- The problem is what comes after the fold. The email pivots abruptly into what looks like a standard product sell (new fall arrivals at $129, private-label pricing, a "we have the app" banner, TheDesignDesk blog content), none of which connects back to the giveaway. The result feels like two emails stitched together — a giveaway at the top and a mini-catalog below.
- Judgment: the giveaway mechanic is clear and the prize value is compelling, but the dual-purpose structure dilutes both halves. Compared to the July 17 "Summer's heating up! Up to 60% off" send, which was a single focused message from hero to footer, this one asks the subscriber to mentally switch gears mid-scroll — and most won't.

## What's working

- Prize value ($4,199) is stated explicitly in the subject, hero headline, and body — no ambiguity about what's at stake.
- Hero lifestyle image (shark/dino-print backpack scene) is on-brand and relevant to back-to-school.
- Co-sponsor logo grid is visually clean and communicates that multiple brands are contributing — makes the prize feel credible and large.
- Primary CTA ("Enter Now" equivalent) is visible without heavy scrolling on mobile.
- The back-to-school timing is seasonally appropriate and distinct from the recent sale-focused sends.

## What's weak

- Post-giveaway content (fall arrivals, app promo, blog) is completely disconnected from the sweepstakes — splits reader attention and weakens both messages.
- Co-sponsor logos are small and low-contrast; brand names like "Barefoot Books" and "REEL" are hard to read at mobile scale.
- No deadline visible for the giveaway — "enter to win" with no urgency anchor is a missed conversion lever.
- The app download banner buried mid-email is orphaned; no clear reason why giveaway entrants should download the app.
- TheDesignDesk blog teaser at the bottom belongs in an editorial send, not a sweepstakes email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut everything below the giveaway CTA.** Strip the fall arrivals grid, app promo, and blog teaser entirely. One email, one goal. If product needs to be in the email, tie it to the prize ("These are the exact items in the bundle — shop them now, or enter to win them all").
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Enter by [date]" is the single highest-leverage change. Every sweepstakes without a deadline is leaving urgency on the table.
- 3. **Rewrite the co-sponsor section.** Give each partner brand one sentence of context ("Barefoot Books — 200+ award-winning kids' titles") so the prize grid feels premium, not like a logo soup.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Win $4,199 in back-to-school gear — enter by [date]`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `The ultimate back-to-school giveaway (8 brands, $4,199 in prizes)`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, books, desks, and more — enter once, win everything.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `Eight brands. One winner. Deadline [date] — enter free.`

Sender is "Crate & Kids" but the document title reads "Crate & Barrel." Some clients (e.g., Apple Mail tab previews) surface this value.

**Stale dev placeholder comment (confirmed)**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Template directive — including the typo "IMPUT" — was not stripped before send. Visible in raw source.

**Conflicting min-width media query order (confirmed)**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
The 640px rule is declared *after* the 768px rule. At any viewport ≥768px both match, and cascade order means the 640px rule wins — the 768px rule is dead. Images render at 640px max on wide desktop viewports regardless of intent.

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 failure; known accessibility barrier for low-vision users.

**Light-only color scheme declared (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. Forces light rendering in Apple Mail dark mode, risking background inversions on unstyled elements.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags (confirmed)** — redundant but harmless; minor hygiene issue.
