# Vintage vibes are here: Meet Beatrix
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing what "NEW | Our Cotswolds-inspired lookbook" (2026-07-29, 9.00/10) did right — leading with a genuine, single-collection story instead of the kitchen-sink stacking that dragged down "Early bird gets the deal" (2026-07-28, 8.00/10) and "Our new arrivals are crafted to last→" (2026-07-29, 5.00/10). The top of this email is clean: one hero collection (Beatrix bedroom), a matching nursery follow-up, and consistent green/vintage styling that actually earns the subject line "Vintage vibes are here."
- Where it loses points is exactly where past sends have lost points: after the Beatrix story wraps, it piles on fall arrivals, a summer sale banner, a separate fall 2026 lookbook, a $500 sweepstakes, another product grid, a credit card pitch, and an app/design-service footer. That's six distinct asks stacked under one subject line, and "summer sale" sitting directly beneath a "fall 2026 lookbook" section reads as seasonally confused.
- Net: strong top-of-funnel execution, undermined by the same scroll-fatigue problem this brand keeps hitting once you get past the hero. Worth opening and clicking on Beatrix specifically; not worth the scroll for anyone who isn't already dresser-shopping.

## What's working

- The hero commits to one collection — Beatrix bedroom — with a clean mirror/dresser/canopy-bed vignette and a single "Shop Now" CTA, no competing offers in the first screen.
- The Beatrix nursery follow-up mirrors the bedroom section's layout, reinforcing it as a matched collection rather than a grab-bag of SKUs.
- Product photography is consistent and on-brand (warm wood tones, vintage hardware, styled room shots) — no render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.
- The subject's "vintage vibes" promise is visually delivered — scalloped edges, aged brass hardware, and floral wallpaper all read as vintage-inspired on sight.

## What's weak

- The email runs a "summer sale" banner directly under a "fall 2026 lookbook: storybook style" section — two conflicting seasonal signals in the same scroll.
- Six separate CTAs compete after the Beatrix story (fall arrivals grid, summer sale, lookbook, sweepstakes, "we love these for you" grid, credit card) — this is the same stacking problem that dragged "Early bird gets the deal" and "Our new arrivals are crafted to last→" down.
- Every CTA button says "Shop Now" or "Shop the Sale" — none tell you what you're clicking into (no "Shop Beatrix Bedroom" specificity).
- No price or discount appears anywhere near the Beatrix collection itself — the only real offer ("up to 50% off") is buried four sections down and unrelated to the hero product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the Beatrix nursery section (or after "more new fall arrivals" at most) and send the sale, lookbook, sweepstakes, and credit-card pitch as separate sends — this is the single highest-leverage fix, and it's the same fix that made "NEW | Our Cotswolds-inspired lookbook" outperform this brand's other July sends.
- 2. Rename hero CTAs to "Shop Beatrix Bedroom" and "Shop Beatrix Nursery" instead of generic "Shop Now" — gives the click somewhere specific to land.
- 3. Drop or relocate the "summer sale" banner — it directly contradicts the "fall 2026 lookbook" messaging one section later and makes the email feel stale/mistimed.
- 4. Add a price or "starting at $X" anchor near the Beatrix hero — right now the collection has visual appeal but zero purchase-decision info in the top screen.
