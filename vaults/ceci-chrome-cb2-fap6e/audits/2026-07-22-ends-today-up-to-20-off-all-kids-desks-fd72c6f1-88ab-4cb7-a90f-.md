# ENDS TODAY: Up to 20% off ALL kids desks!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids executing a textbook flash sale: one clear deadline, one offer, and a lifestyle hero that shows the product cleanly. "ENDS TODAY: Up to 20% off ALL kids desks" is about as direct as a retail subject line gets — "ALL" (not "select") is the right word choice, and the hero delivers on it with confident 20%-off copy and a visible Shop Now button. For a parent actively shopping for a school-year desk, this is the most actionable Crate & Kids send of the week.
- The problem is everything after the fold. The email piles on a storage sale with the weaker "up to 20% off *select*" qualifier, then carries over the $1,000 sweepstakes from yesterday's July 21 send — making it feel like evergreen padding stapled to a flash sale — then drops a personalized-recs block that visibly surfaces a stroller in a kids desk email. Each section bleeds urgency from the one before it.
- Net judgment: the hero is the best creative unit Crate & Kids has shipped this week, and it's being sabotaged by its own template. Cut everything after the desk product grid and this becomes a clean, high-converting send. As it stands, the urgency is real but the focus is not.

## What's working

- **"ALL" is the right word**: No asterisks, no "select" — every kids desk at 20% off is a confident offer that removes decision hesitation.
- **Hero unit is focused**: Lifestyle desk photography plus a legible 20%-off headline plus Shop Now button — the top third of this email would stand alone as a complete send.
- **"ENDS TODAY" in the subject is credible**: The deadline is both in the subject and reinforced by the hero; no bait-and-switch visible in the render.
- **Clean render throughout**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout drift at any visible breakpoint.

## What's weak

- **Sweepstakes carry-over kills urgency**: The $1,000 sweepstakes banner is a direct lift from the July 21 email — appearing the very next day as evergreen padding directly contradicts the "ENDS TODAY" message above it.
- **"We love these for you" block misfires on category**: The visible personalized-rec products include what appears to be a stroller — wrong category entirely for a desk flash sale, and a visible data-quality failure.
- **Storage secondary offer weakens the primary**: The "up to 20% off *select* storage" banner arrives right after the "20% off ALL" hero; the qualifier words create a contrast that makes the whole email feel less trustworthy.
- **TheDailyDesk editorial is unreadable filler**: Low-contrast, unclear purpose — partnership content? brand content? — it reads like a slot that wasn't turned off before send.
- **App promotion has no business being here**: A "download the app" section inside a deadline-urgency flash sale is wasted real estate that delays the user's path to purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **End the email at the desk product grid** — delete the sweepstakes, storage secondary, stroller recs, app download, and TheDailyDesk sections entirely. The conversion path is hero → product grid → checkout. Every additional section is attrition.
- 2. **Add a deadline timestamp to the CTA**: Changing "Shop Now" to "Shop Before Midnight" or adding "Sale ends tonight at 11:59 PM" directly under the button makes the urgency actionable rather than ambient.
- 3. **Fix the personalized-recs filter**: If recs must appear, constrain the feed to desk-adjacent items (chairs, desk lamps, desk accessories). A stroller in a desk email is a segmentation bug, not a feature.
- 4. **Hold the storage offer for tomorrow**: If storage is genuinely on sale, give it its own email on Thursday — the qualifier words actively dilute confidence in today's primary offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Your kids desk sale ends tonight — 20% off everything"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"20% off ALL kids desks — no exclusions, last day"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Every desk in the collection — no fine print, sale closes at midnight."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"Shop the full kids desk lineup before the 20% off sale is gone."`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional promotional email rendered via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (MI platform). Four webfont assets return 403s and 25 images lack alt attributes; compliance header visibility is degraded by the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken webfonts (403) — 4 failures:**
```
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/7ec25fd5-2752-4d84-b307-af3faabc21bb.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/aaa41944-25b3-4265-80b2-5564efe9b2d7.woff2
https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/fd5f8f50-d539-4a7d-b893-99501d746ef2.woff2
```
The `@font-face` declarations reference these paths but the CDN is refusing them. Email clients that attempt to load web fonts (Apple Mail, newer Outlook on Mac) will silently fall back to system fonts. The fallback stack in the CSS (`Arial`, `Georgia`, etc.) is present in the MSO conditional blocks, so degradation is handled — but the font load attempts generate failed network requests.

**Tracking links (22 skipped):** Click-redirect URLs through the MI tracking domain were not probed; destination URLs and UTM parameter presence are unverified. See Section 6.

**Third-party tracking pixels identified:**
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/` — Salesforce Marketing Cloud open pixel (×6 instances)
- `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/` — MC upload/open pixel
- `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` — LiveRamp/RampID pixel (×5 instances, `n=1` through `n=5`)
- `dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/` — unknown internal pixel with `mi_mid` parameter

The LiveRamp pixel fires 5 sequential requests, which is consistent with their segmentation use case but worth noting for consent audit purposes.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 25 images:**

All product images and all tracking/spacer pixels are missing `alt` attributes entirely (not `alt=""` — the attribute is absent). Examples:
```
bbf6f9d4-86cc-437a-aa80-391067b74306.png  (content image)
2856f177-1a07-40af-b84b-06791ebfd97f.jpg  (content image)
d99d15dc-40b0-441a-a1e0-584687c08eda.gif  (likely spacer)
f3c0d22992a01946.png                       (tracking pixel, ×6)
448796.gif (sr.rlcdn.com)                  (tracking pixel, ×5)
o.gif (mi.crateandbarrel.com)              (open pixel)
```
Content images should carry descriptive alt text. Tracking pixels and spacer GIFs should carry `alt=""` explicitly — absence means some assistive technologies will announce the filename.

**Dark mode locked out:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
The email explicitly opts out of dark mode rendering. This is a deliberate choice but means Apple Mail dark mode users receive the light-mode layout with no adaptation.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
`format-detection` for `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` each appear twice in `<head>` — harmless but indicates copy-paste template debt.

**`<title>` mismatch:** The `<title>` element reads `Crate & Barrel` while the sending brand is `Crate & Kids`. Matters for tab display in webmail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) that surface the title.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND** — QA relay did not capture this header. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5k/day); its absence or non-forwarding by the relay could affect inbox placement and triggers a compliance gap under RFC 2369.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header: NOT FOUND** — One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) is not confirmed. Gmail has required this for bulk senders since February 2024. If the header exists upstream but is being dropped by the AgentMail relay, the relay's header forwarding config needs investigation.

**`Authentication-Results` header: NOT FOUND** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `mail.crateandbarrel.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain, which typically carries DKIM alignment — but this cannot be confirmed through the relay as captured.

These three findings may be relay capture artifacts rather than actual sender omissions, but they cannot be cleared without direct header inspection.

A manual spot-check of 2–3 links by following them through the MI redirect domain would confirm whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` parameters survive to the crateandbarrel.com landing pages.
