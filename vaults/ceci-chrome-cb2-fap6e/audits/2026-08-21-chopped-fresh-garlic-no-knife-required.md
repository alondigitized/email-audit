# Chopped fresh garlic, no knife required 🤯
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a sprawling, category-catalog email wearing a clickbait subject line. "Chopped fresh garlic, no knife required 🤯" promises a specific gadget story, but the hero doesn't even name a garlic tool — it opens on "the kitchen tools we can't live back" with a hands-in-a-bowl image, then just keeps scrolling through utensils, mixing bowls, colanders, cutting boards, storage, dish towels, a bedroom furniture cross-sell, a Levain bakery collab, and a credit card pitch. The garlic chopper only shows up mid-scroll as "Chef'n Veggichop Zipper" under "meal prep, but make it ASMR" — by which point most readers have already bailed.
- The individual product shots are clean and the "starting at $X" pricing anchors are useful, but there's no single hero offer, no discount visible until deep in the bedroom furniture module (25% off), and the email tries to be five emails at once (kitchen tools, dish towels, bedroom furniture, bakery collab, credit card). Compared to the brand's tighter recent sends — the 25% off bedroom email (2026-08-21) or the free-shipping send (2026-08-20) — this one lacks focus. Subject overpromises, body underdelivers, and it buries its own best hook.

## What's working

- The subject line's specific, curiosity-driven hook ("no knife required 🤯") is genuinely scroll-stopping copy, better than the brand's usual "up to X% off" formula.
- Product photography is consistent and high-quality throughout — warm, styled kitchen scenes that reinforce brand aesthetic.
- Clear "starting at $X" pricing under each category module gives a fast anchor without requiring a click.
- The "we love these for you" grid at the bottom is a legible, scannable product roundup with prices visible.

## What's weak

- The garlic chopper teased in the subject doesn't appear until roughly two-thirds of the way down the email, disconnected from the hero.
- No discernible discount or offer in the hero — "the kitchen tools we can't live without" is generic aspirational copy, not an offer.
- The email sprawls across unrelated categories (kitchen tools → dish towels → bedroom furniture → bakery collab → credit card), diluting any single CTA.
- Multiple near-identical "Shop Now" buttons with no differentiation make it unclear which one matters.
- The 25% off bedroom furniture module and Levain bakery collab feel bolted on and compete with the kitchen narrative the subject line set up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with the garlic chopper.** If the subject promises "no knife required," the first image and headline should be the Chef'n Veggichop in action, not a generic tools montage — the payoff needs to be immediate.
- 2. **Cut the email in half.** Split kitchen tools and bedroom furniture/bakery collab/credit card into separate sends — this one is doing the job of three emails and losing all of them.
- 3. **Add a real offer to the hero.** Either surface the 25% off (currently buried near the bottom) higher up, or run this as a pure product-education send without implying a discount at all.
- 4. **Give the garlic chopper its own CTA** ("Shop the Chopper") instead of a generic "Shop Now" so the subject-line promise has a direct click path.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The garlic hack that skips the knife entirely`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meal prep gadgets we can't stop using`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Chef'n's zipper chopper + more tools that make prep faster`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The kitchen edit: chop, mix, store, repeat`
