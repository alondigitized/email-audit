# The Design Desk: Zanna Roberts Rassi’s twin room makeovers →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids leaning fully into its "Design Desk" editorial franchise — fashion stylist Zanna Roberts Rassi's twin daughters get individually designed bedrooms, and the email actually shows the split: "rumi's mystical garden bedroom" and "juna's city by the sea bedroom," each with its own hero photo, shop-the-room product grid, and a genuine quote from the kid ("The Tucker sheets are so soft, like a marshmallow!"). That's a real content hook, not just a product dump, and it's a stronger persona-fit for parents furnishing kids' rooms than the generic clearance or backpack-bundle sends in this brand's recent history.
- Where it falls apart is length and focus. After two full room breakdowns, the email keeps going: a backpack bundle promo, a "$1000 gift card sweepstakes," a generic "we love these for you" product grid, a credit card pitch, and an app download pitch — five more unrelated modules stacked below the actual content. By the time a reader scrolls past two complete room makeovers, the editorial premise has already paid off; everything after is a bolt-on module tax that dilutes rather than extends the story.
- Solid concept, poor edit. This should have been the two rooms plus one clear CTA, not a kitchen-sink send tacked onto a legitimately good idea.

## What's working

- Real editorial hook — a named stylist and her actual kids' rooms gives this more credibility than a generic "shop kids furniture" push.
- Each room section is self-contained: hero photo, room-specific product carousel, a kid's testimonial quote, and both "Shop Room" and "Book a Free Appointment" CTAs.
- The two rooms have visually distinct palettes (garden/botanical vs. coastal blue-and-white), reinforcing that these are two different real spaces, not a template reused twice.
- Product photography and room photography are clean, no render bugs, no broken images.

## What's weak

- Way too many bottom modules — backpack bundles, sweepstakes, generic recommendations, credit card, app download — none of which relate to "twin room makeovers," turning a tight editorial email into a catalog.
- No specific discount or price anywhere in the two hero rooms — this is pure inspiration/lookbook content with no monetary offer, which softens the Business Impact case for anyone shopping on price.
- "Book a Free Appointment" is repeated 2x and competes with "Shop Room" as the primary CTA in each section — unclear which action Crate & Kids actually wants.
- The sweepstakes ("enter for a chance to win $1000") appears with no visible deadline or rules, which reads as filler rather than a credible urgency driver.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the two room sections — end on one clear CTA ("Shop the Twins' Rooms" or "Book Your Free Design Appointment"), and move backpack bundles/sweepstakes/credit card/app promos to separate sends. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Pick one primary CTA per room instead of two competing buttons — if the goal is appointments, make "Book a Free Appointment" the button and demote "Shop the Room" to a text link.
- 3. Add a light incentive tied to the design service itself (e.g., "book a free appointment and get 15% off your room") to give price-motivated openers a reason to click, not just inspiration-motivated ones.
- 4. Give the sweepstakes a deadline if it's going to stay in any version of this send — "no deadline visible" reads as evergreen filler, not urgency.
