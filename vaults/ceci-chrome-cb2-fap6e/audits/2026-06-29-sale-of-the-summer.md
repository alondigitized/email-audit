# SALE OF THE SUMMER
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- "SALE OF THE SUMMER" is the eighth-plus sale email in what the brand history shows as a week-plus drumbeat from CB2 — and the subject is the weakest thing about it. The body actually delivers: a clear "WAREHOUSE SALE / JULY 4TH SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" hero, a scarcity section ("MAKE IT YOURS BEFORE IT'S GONE"), a product grid, and a personalized "MORE FOR YOU" block at the bottom. The email body punches above the subject line that got them here.
- The problem is the entry point. "SALE OF THE SUMMER" is a generic superlative that buries the concrete July 4th / 60% off angle that appears one click further inside. After back-to-back sends including a fake-reply gambit ("Re: your summer savings," June 27) and a teaser ("It's back. And now it's on sale.," June 26), subscribers are numb. The subject does nothing to break through the noise it's contributed to building.
- The sender name "CB2 SALE" — not the usual "CB2" — is a small but real friction point. It registers as slightly unfamiliar in an inbox where brand recognition is the first open signal. The email underneath is competent. The inbox framing undercuts it.

## What's working

- **Hero discount is unambiguous.** "UP TO 60% OFF" is immediately visible without scrolling, combined with "WAREHOUSE SALE" and the July 4th framing.
- **July 4th timing is credible and current.** The send date (June 29) gives enough runway to feel urgent without feeling stale.
- **"MAKE IT YOURS BEFORE IT'S GONE" scarcity block** adds a second urgency layer after the hero — works structurally even if the language has appeared before.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalized product section** at the bottom is a genuine click driver — it signals the brand is trying to surface relevant items, not just shouting the same five categories.
- **Clean visual layout** — no render bugs, no broken images, hierarchy reads hero → scarcity → browse.

## What's weak

- **Subject line wastes the strongest assets.** "SALE OF THE SUMMER" doesn't mention 60% off, July 4th, or Warehouse Sale — all three of which appear in the hero. The best copy stayed inside the email.
- **"CB2 SALE" sender name is off-brand.** The prior seven sends presumably came from "CB2." A subtle name change lowers recognition and can trigger inbox skepticism.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout are non-specific.** Every clickable section says "SHOP NOW" — no category differentiation, no verb-noun specificity. The June 28 "WAREHOUSE SALE" send had the same problem.
- **Scarcity language is recycled.** "Before It's Gone" echoes the June 23 "selling fast" framing that's already appeared in this series. It loses force each repetition.
- **No deadline date visible in the hero.** "July 4th Sale" implies an end date, but no explicit "Ends July 4th" copy appears, so the urgency is implicit rather than concrete.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line to surface the offer.** "SALE OF THE SUMMER" is the most impactful change — swap it for something that contains a number, a deadline, or both. The body already has the goods; the subject needs to match.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off ends July 4th — warehouse sale now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `July 4th warehouse sale: up to 60% off`
- 2. **Add an explicit deadline to the hero block.** "ENDS JULY 4TH" pinned under the 60% OFF headline converts the implied urgency into a hard stop. Converts browsers into buyers.
- 3. **Revert sender name to "CB2."** "CB2 SALE" is unnecessary differentiation that costs recognition. The subject line handles the "sale" signal — the sender field should handle brand trust.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTA copy by section.** "SHOP FURNITURE," "SHOP RUGS," "SHOP LIGHTING" instead of four identical "SHOP NOW" labels. Reduces decision paralysis and improves click attribution.
- 5. **Rotate the scarcity language.** "Before It's Gone" has run in this series. Try "FINAL DAYS" or show a quantity or inventory signal for specific hero items.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends July 4th — final days on furniture, rugs & lighting`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% off the warehouse. Closes out Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** restricts user-initiated zoom — accessibility failure for low-vision users. Line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`

- **Conflicting `<p>` resets across style blocks:** The global block (no media query) sets `p { text-align: center; }`. The screen-scoped block sets `p { padding: 0 !important; margin: 0 !important; }` but does not override `text-align`. Result: all `<p>` elements are center-aligned universally, including any left-aligned body copy.

- **Inconsistent `font-display`:** Three of the six `@font-face` declarations include `font-display: swap`; the other three (including the first `Lineto Brown Sub` and `Lineto Brown Light Sub` declarations) do not. No practical rendering impact in email clients, but indicates copy-paste inconsistency in the template.

- **Alt text:** Cannot audit — image tags not visible in truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` carry descriptive `alt` attributes; this is both an accessibility requirement and a deliverability signal.
