# Your free living room redesign
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's design-services pitch — a clean strategic pivot away from the Midseason Sale drumbeat that's dominated the last week of sends. After six consecutive promotional emails (including the same-day "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" send), a services email that doesn't lead with a discount is the right call for subscriber hygiene. The subject line "Your free living room redesign" is one of the sharper subjects CB2 has sent this month — concrete room, concrete benefit, "your" personalizing it — and it earns the open.
- Inside, the execution is uneven. The dark, moody hero shot with "THE SECRET TO A WELL-CURATED HOME" and "INTERIOR DESIGN SERVICES" sets a premium tone, but the body quickly fragments into a "5 WAYS TO MEET" format matrix (in-person vs. online, with separate CTAs for each), which introduces unnecessary decision load for what should be a single conversion moment: book a consult. The product grid stitched below the services pitch muddies the message further — are you selling design expertise or sofas? Both, but neither fully.
- The bottom-of-email $1,000 credit card offer is a jarring tonal mismatch. This is CB2's most brand-elevated email type, and ending it on a bank card pitch undercuts the premium positioning the hero was building. Net judgment: smart send at the right moment, but the CTA architecture and the credit card appendage need surgery.

## What's working

- Subject is the cleanest CB2 has written all month — specific room ("living room"), specific benefit ("free"), possessive ("your"), under 30 chars.
- Hero image and headline ("THE SECRET TO A WELL-CURATED HOME") are genuinely premium — dark editorial tone stands out after a week of sale banners.
- Sending a services email on the same day as the outdoor sale email (separate from-address) is smart send-list hygiene; this reaches a different intent state.
- In-person and online meeting options acknowledge that CB2's customer base is geographically distributed — the segmentation instinct is right.
- Product grid at the bottom has visible prices, giving subscribers a tangible feel for the price tier they're designing at.

## What's weak

- "5 WAYS TO MEET" is too much decision architecture for a single conversion email — five paths diffuse the click, not concentrate it.
- Two CTAs (IN PERSON / ONLINE) split the conversion action; one primary CTA with a secondary mention of both modes would focus the click.
- The $1,000 credit card offer at the bottom is a tonal collision — a bank product pitch appended to CB2's most premium services send.
- The hero copy ("THE SECRET TO A WELL-CURATED HOME") leans editorial-vague; the actual offer (free design service) is buried one scroll below.
- No urgency signal anywhere — a free service offer with no deadline or appointment-window creates easy deferral.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one CTA.** Replace "5 WAYS TO MEET" with a single "Book a Free Session" button; move format details (in-person / virtual) to a radio-select on the landing page.
- 2. **Lead hero copy with the offer, not the philosophy.** Swap "THE SECRET TO A WELL-CURATED HOME" for "Your free living room consult starts here" — make the value prop visible before any scroll.
- 3. **Add a soft deadline.** "Slots fill fast — book by June 6" would convert deferral to action; CB2's designers genuinely have limited availability, so this is honest urgency.
- 4. **Strip the credit card module.** Move it to its own dedicated send or the footer of a sale email — not here.
- 5. **Tighten the product grid.** Show 2–3 living-room-specific items with a "shop what your designer recommends" framing, not a generic multi-category browse grid.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` via wildcard selector.** The rule `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}` overrides the correct `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100% }` declaration set 12 lines earlier. The `none` value disables automatic font scaling on iOS Safari, making small text unreadable without zoom.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** (`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`). Disables pinch-to-zoom, a documented WCAG 2.1 failure (Success Criterion 1.4.4). Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1`.

- **Mobile breakpoint too narrow.** The sole mobile breakpoint fires at `max-width: 418px`. Common modern viewports (iPhone 16 Pro Max: 430px, Pixel 9: 412px landscape) fall outside this range. The `.mobile`, `.hide`, `.block`, and font-size overrides will not apply for those devices. Recommend raising to `max-width: 430px` or `max-width: 480px`.

- **Conflicting desktop `img` rules.** Two `@media` blocks set conflicting `img { max-width }`:
  - `min-width: 768px` → `max-width: 100% !important`
  - `min-width: 690px` → `max-width: 690px`
  
  At 768px+, both rules match; the `!important` on the wider breakpoint wins, meaning images stretch to 100% of the container regardless of the 690px cap. The ordering should be reversed (690px block declared last) or collapsed into one rule.

- **Double-slash paths in font URLs.** Three `@font-face` `src` values contain `//` after the domain:
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
  
  Most HTTP servers normalize this, but some CDN/WAF configurations return 301 or 404 on double-slash paths, causing font fallback to Helvetica/sans-serif.
