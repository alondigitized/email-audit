# FLOOR SAMPLE SALE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's smart tactical pivot on day seven-plus of the same July 4th sale run. Every prior send in this series has pushed an online discount — "Up to 50% off furniture," "MORE ADDED TO SALE," "SALE'S GOING FAST" — so opening with a **Floor Sample Sale** and a **FIND A STORE** CTA breaks the pattern in a meaningful way. The creative is clean: a warm bedroom hero, a countdown clock in the banner, and a secondary online-sale block to capture shoppers who won't make it to a store. It's a two-track email.
- The problem is reach. Floor sample sales are in-store only, and the majority of an email list isn't within driving distance of a CB2 location. The "FIND A STORE" CTA does nothing for that segment. The secondary "JULY 4TH SALE UP TO 50% OFF / SHOP NOW" block rescues it, but it's visually subordinate to the hero — it reads like a footnote rather than a co-equal offer.
- Verdict: a strategically smart angle that's execution-constrained. The hero CTA is the wrong primary action for most of the list; the real workhorse is the online sale block, and it's been demoted.

## What's working

- **Subject line distinctiveness**: "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" breaks pattern against six-plus prior sends all anchored to "50% OFF" or "SALE." Genuinely new hook.
- **Countdown clock placement**: Banner-level urgency above the fold reinforces the July 4th deadline without requiring copy in the hero.
- **Extended hours callout**: "Open 9am–9pm at Stores" is specific and valuable for in-store traffic. A small detail that does real work.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product grid**: Six products with visible pricing (including sale pricing) gives online shoppers a reason to click even if the floor sample angle doesn't apply to them.
- **Dual-track structure**: The secondary "JULY 4TH SALE UP TO 50% OFF / SHOP NOW" block is a legitimate lifeline for the online segment.

## What's weak

- **Hero CTA mismatch**: "FIND A STORE" as the primary action alienates the majority of the list who aren't near a CB2 store. The floor sample sale has an inherently small addressable audience within a mass email.
- **Online sale is visually buried**: The "JULY 4TH SALE UP TO 50% OFF / SHOP NOW" section — the higher-reach offer — lives below the fold and feels like an afterthought beneath the hero.
- **No floor sample specifics**: No callout of product categories or savings depth on floor samples (e.g., "up to 70% off select sofas"). Without a hook, the curiosity ceiling on this offer is low.
- **Cadence fatigue**: This is the eighth-plus send in the same sale week. Even with the floor sample angle, the overall envelope still screams "another CB2 sale email."
- **Preview text unknown**: Cannot confirm from this render whether preheader is real copy or leaking system junk — a missed amplification point given the subject is only 17 chars.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hero hierarchy**: Lead the email with the online 50% off sale — the higher-reach offer — and make the floor sample sale the secondary section. Right now reach and hierarchy are inverted.
- 2. **Add floor sample depth**: Headline with a steeper discount ("up to 70% off display pieces") and a category or two ("dining chairs, sofas, lighting") so the in-store segment has a reason to drive over.
- 3. **Segment the send**: Floor sample email only goes to subscribers within X miles of a store. The remainder gets a straight July 4th sale email without the in-store detour.
- 4. **Preheader is doing nothing**: With a 17-char subject, the preheader carries almost the full inbox impression load. Use it to name a category or a discount floor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Floor samples: up to 70% off in stores`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rare: floor sample pricing, one day only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, chairs, lighting — displayed once, priced to clear. Extended hours today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In-store only: floor sample deals + 50% off online. Shop both.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Several infrastructure-level issues found: an unfilled template placeholder in production HTML, duplicate/malformed font-face declarations with double-slash paths, and a zoom-blocking viewport directive that violates accessibility standards.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Cannot audit CTA links** — HTML is truncated before any anchor tags appear. UTM param coverage cannot be confirmed; flag for full-source re-audit.
- **Cross-domain font assets**: Four `@font-face` rules in a CB2 email load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (HNW05 family). If Crate & Barrel's CDN blocks hotlinking or has downtime, font fallback silently degrades in CB2 mail. Low risk but worth consolidating to `cb2.com` CDN.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unfilled template placeholder left in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and the comment itself indicate an authoring artifact that was never removed. Harmless to render but exposes internal workflow tooling.

**Double-slash paths in three `@font-face` src URLs:**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Most servers normalize `//` to `/`, so this likely resolves, but it is a malformed URL and will 404 on strict servers.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — declared three separate times with overlapping `font-weight` values (400/600 and an unweighted block). The unweighted block has no `woff2` source, only `eot` and `woff`, meaning Outlook desktop gets a different font stack than modern clients.

**Zoom blocking:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text) violation for low-vision users.

**Global text-size-adjust suppression:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied to `*` rather than scoped to `body`. This overrides system accessibility text scaling for every element.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Targets 414px-wide devices (iPhone SE/older iPhones). Current standard phones (390–430px logical width) may miss this breakpoint, receiving desktop layout on mobile.
