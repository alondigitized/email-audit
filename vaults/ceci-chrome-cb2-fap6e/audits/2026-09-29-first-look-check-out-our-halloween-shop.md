# First look: Check out our Halloween shop →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-organized Halloween kickoff with a split personality. The hero, "now open: the halloween shop", sits under a thin banner for a baby-essentials sale. The Halloween content is charming and easy to scan, in dark, moody, playful photography. It runs treat baskets, costumes, decor, then bedding, each with a three-up product grid and its own "Shop All Halloween" button. The subject promises a "first look", and the body delivers that.
- The email then loses focus. Below the Halloween modules there's a "everything you need for spooky season" C&B tile, a second "ending soon! up to 20% off the best baby essentials" block, a "we love these for you" stroller/nursery grid, a credit-card promo and an app-download pitch. The only price-led offer in the email is the baby sale, which has nothing to do with the subject. The Halloween half has no incentive at all.
- It's a solid seasonal content send, weakened by stacking a second campaign on top and underneath it. Compared with the 9/29 "Meet our break-resistant wine glasses" send, which was a tight single-idea story, this one is broader and less disciplined.

## What's working

- The hero ("now open: the halloween shop") is on-season and on-subject, with a clear "Shop All Halloween" button.
- Each Halloween module follows the same pattern: header, short blurb, three products, one CTA. That makes the scroll easy to follow.
- The photography is strong. The black-cat basket, the ghost-and-pumpkin bedding and the dinnerware flat-lay all feel giftable and on-brand.
- The Halloween products are kid-relevant and seasonally timely: treat bags, costumes, party decor.
- The footer nav and app promo give a clean exit path, and I see no broken images or layout breakage.

## What's weak

- The top banner, "ending soon! up to 20% off the best baby essentials →", is the first thing a reader sees. It competes with the Halloween hero and uses urgency language for an unrelated category.
- The baby sale appears again mid-email, and a baby-product grid follows it. That is a third of the scroll spent off-topic.
- The Halloween modules have no price, discount or deadline hook. Nothing tells a reader to buy this week instead of later.
- The email is very long. The credit-card and app-download blocks at the bottom add clutter after the main story has ended.
- The Halloween product prices and names are small and hard to read, so the grids lean on photos alone.
- This is roughly the sixth Crate & Barrel-family send in three days (9/27 clearance, 9/27 fall, 9/28 SMEG, 9/28 fall decor, 9/28 baby, 9/29 wine glasses). A "first look" message gets lost in that cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove the baby banner and the second baby block from this send.** Give Halloween a clean run and let the baby sale have its own email. This is the single biggest lift, because it restores one message and one hierarchy.
- 2. **Add one Halloween incentive.** Examples: a free-shipping-by-date line, a "order by Oct 20 for delivery before Halloween" deadline, or a bundle price. Put it in the hero.
- 3. **Trim the tail.** Cut the credit-card and app-download blocks, or move them to a slim footer strip.
- 4. **Enlarge product names and prices** in the grids, and link each tile to its own product page.
- 5. **Add social proof.** A "top-rated" or "customer favorite" tag on one item per module would give people a reason to click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Halloween is here: treat bags, costumes & decor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our new Halloween shop just opened 🎃`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Personalized treat bags, cozy costumes and spooky-cute decor, plus order-by dates for Halloween delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Be the first to shop pumpkin baskets, cat-print bedding and party-ready tableware.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard table-based XHTML Transitional layout with responsive media queries. The main confirmed issue is widespread missing alt text (about 50 `<img>` elements flagged). The header-based compliance and authentication checks were inconclusive because of the relay, and 93 tracked links could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **93 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains, and UTM propagation are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Five identical third-party pixels** load from `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `&n=5`. The domain is a LiveRamp/RampID endpoint. The five pixels differ only by the `n` parameter, which adds requests and third-party exposure.
- **Six duplicate pixel requests** load `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png?mi_u=…`. Also present are `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif` and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/ab1e2420-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…`. These are open and tracking pixels with per-recipient identifiers (`mi_u`, `mi_mid`) in the query string. The source shows nothing wrong with them apart from the missing alt text below.
- No broken links were confirmed. None could be tested.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text, about 50 images (QA WARN).**
  - About 33 content or hero images are hosted on `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/1/…` (for example `51d4c5f9-25a0-4018-b814-7eed720076fa.jpg`). One is under `/m/47/` (`bbf6f9d4-…png`), and two are GIFs (`29edf3d7-…gif`, `cdab6b90-…gif`).
  - Several spacer and rule images are on `s7d5.scene7.com`, such as `25%5FMI%5FBottom%5FSpacer%5F40px%5FWhite`, which appears 4 times, and `042025%5FCBK%5FKidsBeds1%5FRule%5FSpacer%5F40px`.
  - The social image `20260916%5FCBK%5FHalloweenLaunch%5FTeriary%5FSocia…` is also flagged.
  - Screen readers may read out filenames or URLs. If the email has image-only content blocks, the content is lost when images are blocked.
  - The truncated source doesn't show whether these images carry `alt=""` (decorative, acceptable for spacers and pixels) or no `alt` attribute at all. Tracking pixels and spacers should use explicit `alt=""`. Content images need descriptive alt text.
- **`<meta name="viewport" content="… maximum-scale=1" />`** disables pinch-zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 failure.
- **Redundant or duplicate head tags:**
  - `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" … charset=utf-8>` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are both present.
  - `format-detection` is declared 4 times: `telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`, then `date=no`, `address=no`, and `telephone=no` separately.
  - A leftover template comment reads `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`.
- **Unescaped ampersand in `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>`.** This is invalid under the XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE the email declares. Clients render it fine, but it is not well-formed.
- **Conflicting `img` max-width rules.** `@media (min-width:768px){img{max-width:600px}}` is followed by `@media (min-width:640px){img{max-width:640px}}`. Both match at ≥768px, and the later rule wins, so the 600px cap never applies.
- **`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; line-height:100%}`** is a global override. It disables text-size adjustment, which can hurt accessibility on iOS.
