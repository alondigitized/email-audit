# Final hours for up to 50% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-executed sale-finale send doing exactly what a sale closer should: loud urgency, concrete discount, loyalty kicker, and a clean CTA above the fold. "ENDS TONIGHT" in the hero, "Final hours" in the subject, and a 4X rewards layer for members — three independent reasons to act now. CB2's minimal aesthetic keeps the noise down so the offer actually registers.
- The one structural problem: the clearance offer ("UP TO 60% OFF") buried below the fold is objectively stronger than the hero offer ("UP TO 50% OFF"), and most subscribers will never see it. Whoever clicks "SHOP THE SALE" is leaving money — and conversion rate — on the table. The strongest offer should lead.
- Compared to the 5/17 "NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE" launch, this closer lands with appropriate escalation. The cadence is getting heavy (5+ CB2 sends in a week), but a legitimate finale email earns the send.

## What's working

- **"ENDS TONIGHT" above the hero image** — urgency is the first thing the eye hits, before the logo, before the discount.
- **Sender display name "CB2 LONG WKND SALE"** — smart use of the from-name field as campaign context; signals this is the finale before the inbox is even opened.
- **Double Rewards block is prominent** — 4X rewards is a loyalty sweetener that adds a second reason to buy without diluting the sale narrative.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — large SALE headline, readable discount, single primary CTA button; nothing is fighting for attention.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **60% clearance offer is buried below the fold** — stronger than the 50% hero; swapping these would immediately increase click depth.
- **Product grid is too small to be persuasive** — thumbnails at the bottom are postage-stamp sized; they tease categories but don't surface a single must-click item.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" CTA is generic** — it moves you to a broad sale landing page rather than a specific category or product, creating a post-click scavenger hunt.
- **No social proof or bestseller callout** — a "most-loved items going fast" signal would complement the scarcity message of "final hours."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 60% off, not 50%** — restructure the hero to say "UP TO 60% OFF — CLEARANCE + SALE" and let the bigger number own the above-fold real estate.
- 2. **Add one product hero above the CTA** — pull the single highest-converting SKU (a sofa, a lighting piece) and link it specifically; keep "SHOP THE SALE" as a secondary CTA.
- 3. **Make "SHOP THE SALE" CTA more specific** — split into two buttons: "SHOP FURNITURE" and "SHOP CLEARANCE" to reduce post-click drop-off.
- 4. **Add a bestseller badge or "selling fast" tag** to at least one grid item — anchors urgency to product scarcity, not just the clock.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight only — up to 60% off, double rewards`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance up to 60% off + 4X rewards dollars — ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our biggest markdowns of the Long Weekend — grab them before tonight`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Users with low vision cannot zoom. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Mobile breakpoint is too narrow** — responsive styles trigger at `max-width: 418px`:
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; } }
```
Most modern Android and iPhone Pro/Max devices have logical widths of 428–430px (iPhone 14 Pro Max = 430px). These devices will receive the desktop layout. Standard email breakpoints are 480px or 600px.

**`<title>` is generic and mismatches the subject**:
```html
<title>CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME</title>
```
Subject line is "Final hours for up to 50% off". Some email clients surface `<title>` as a tooltip or tab label. Not a hard failure, but a missed signal.
