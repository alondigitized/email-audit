# Designed by Andreas Hansen in Copenhagen
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- CB2 pulls out yet another creative mode this week — the sixth, by my count — and this one is pure heritage-editorial: "CB2 DESIGN LEGENDS," a roll call of named designers (Andreas Hansen, Fattorini, Paul McCobb, Steen Østergaard, the Ackermans, Bill Curry) each with a portrait, a one-line bio, and a "SHOP [NAME]" link. There's no discount, no urgency, no single hero product — it's a lookbook dressed as a newsletter, closer to the "90+ fabrics" custom-upholstery send from 8/8 than to the editorial-hero mode that scored well on 8/1, 8/5, and 8/8.
- The execution is clean and the concept is legitimately differentiated — genuine designer credentials instead of generic lifestyle copy — but it buries the CTA six scrolls deep behind a wall of near-identical black-and-white portrait tiles, and by the time you hit "MORE FOR YOU" and the app-download banner, the email has sprawled into a catch-all. This reads like a brand-building piece for design-literate shoppers, not a conversion piece, and the subject line undersells that intent by naming just one of six designers.
- Judgment: a tasteful miss on impact. It'll land fine with committed CB2 design nerds but won't pull a broad open or a fast click — filed alongside the 8/8 fabrics send and the 8/7 design-services pivot as CB2's weaker, most product-agnostic mode this week.

## What's working

- Consistent black-and-white portrait treatment across all six designers gives the email a coherent, gallery-like visual identity.
- Named, specific designer credentials (city, decade, movement — e.g. "Renowned in the design community for his ability to balance precision and warmth") add real authority other CB2 sends this week lacked.
- Each designer section has its own clear "SHOP [NAME]" CTA, so intent-to-click is unambiguous once you reach a name you recognize.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds together top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The subject promises one designer ("Andreas Hansen") but the email delivers six — a mismatch between promise and payoff that risks feeling like bait-and-switch once opened.
- No offer, no price, no urgency anywhere in the primary content — after five discount/restock/editorial sends this week, this is a hard pivot to zero-incentive browsing.
- The hero and hierarchy are weak: "INTRODUCING ANDREAS HANSEN — Copenhagen" sits in small type over a busy studio photo, competing with a chair/table product shot rather than leading with either clearly.
- Six repeating portrait-plus-bio blocks in a row is monotonous — nothing differentiates the *visual* pacing between designer #2 and designer #5, so scroll fatigue sets in before reaching Bill Curry at the bottom.
- The email tries to do three jobs (designer story, app-download sweepstakes, "more for you" product grid) — none gets enough weight to be the clear takeaway.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the designer roster from six to two or three and give the remaining ones more visual room (bigger photography, pull-quote treatment) — this single change would fix both the hierarchy problem and the monotony problem at once.
- 2. Rewrite the subject to reflect the full "design legends" concept rather than spotlighting only Andreas Hansen, so open expectations match what's inside.
- 3. Move one shoppable product image directly into the Andreas Hansen hero block so the top of the email has something concrete to click, not just a portrait.
- 4. Cut or relocate the app-download sweepstakes and "more for you" grid to a separate send — they dilute an otherwise-focused editorial concept.
- 5. If this is meant to run as a recurring "Design Legends" series, add a small dateline or "Vol. 1" marker so repeat sends read as a collectible franchise rather than a one-off grab-bag.
