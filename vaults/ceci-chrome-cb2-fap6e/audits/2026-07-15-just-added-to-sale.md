# JUST ADDED TO SALE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "JUST ADDED TO SALE" is the fourth Summer Edit / sale email CB2 has sent in roughly six days — following the July 9 launch, the July 11 reprint, and the July 13 "GET IT ON SALE." The "just added" framing is actually the smartest creative pivot of the series: it's new news within the same sale, which gives fatigued subscribers a reason to open that a raw reprint wouldn't. The hero execution holds up — Summer Edit + 40% off is visible immediately, and the Outdoor Collection 50%-off section below it creates a genuine secondary pull with a stronger discount than the lead offer.
- The problem is clutter. This email is doing six jobs at once: push the Summer Edit sale, push the Outdoor collection, collect reviews via sweepstakes, drive app downloads, pitch a loyalty offer, and close with a wedding registry module. The "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" block mid-scroll is the worst offender — it breaks the purchase funnel at peak scroll momentum and asks subscribers to pivot to an entirely different action right when they're browsing product cards.
- Workmanlike. The "JUST ADDED" angle saves this from being a pure reprint, and the 50%-off outdoor story is actually the best offer in the email. But the structural scatter and the all-caps subject keep the ceiling low.

## What's working

- The "JUST ADDED" subject frame creates a logical reason to open for subscribers who already know about the Summer Edit — new inventory, not a reprint.
- The Outdoor Collection 50%-off block is a stronger offer than the 40% mainline and is visually distinct; it gives the email a real secondary pull.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product rail is a welcome sign of segmentation in an otherwise broadcast-feel send.
- The hero visual — dense botanical greenery behind the Summer Edit headline — is seasonally on-brand and holds the eye.
- Clean render; no broken images or layout bugs visible.

## What's weak

- The "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" module sits directly below the Outdoor section, snapping subscribers out of shopping mode at peak scroll momentum.
- All-caps subject ("JUST ADDED TO SALE") reads as inbox shouting and undercuts CB2's editorial brand positioning.
- Product cards in the "MORE FOR YOU" grid are very small — prices, names, and details require effort to read at a glance.
- Too many competing objectives crammed into one email: Summer sale, Outdoor sale, review sweep, app download, loyalty pitch, wedding registry — most subscribers will disengage from the noise.
- Fourth sale email in six days on the same Summer Edit frame; cadence fatigue is a real suppressor here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut or relocate the review sweepstakes module.** Move it above the footer where it doesn't interrupt the purchase flow. This is the single change with the highest click-recovery value.
- 2. **Surface the 50%-off outdoor story in the subject** — the best offer in the email is completely hidden behind a generic "JUST ADDED TO SALE" line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just added: outdoor furniture now 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New to the sale — up to 50% off outdoor picks`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to add what the subject withholds** — a deadline, a specific category, or the outdoor call-out. Right now these almost certainly echo each other.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh drops in the Summer Edit — shop before they sell out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The outdoor pieces just landed in sale. Ends soon.`
- 4. **Add a visible deadline to the hero.** Every Summer Edit email in this series has been deadline-free — a simple "Ends July 20" beneath "SHOP THE SALE" would sharpen urgency without restructuring anything.
- 5. **Consolidate the app/loyalty blocks.** "EARN 10% BACK" and "GET 10% OFF" are currently two separate full-width sections competing for the same mental real estate. Collapse into one line in the footer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
The value `none` is non-standard and disables text scaling entirely on mobile. Spec-compliant value is `100%`. This will cause unreadable text on some Android clients.

**Accessibility violation — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Standard responsive email breakpoint is 480px–600px. At 418px this misses several common Android viewport widths (420px–428px logical width). Likely causes layout breakage on those devices.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub":**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` with overlapping weight and source stacks. The third declaration (`woff2`, weight 400) conflicts with the first (which uses `.eot`+`.woff` with no weight scoping). Last-declared wins in most clients, making the `.eot` stack dead weight.

**"Lineto Brown Light Sub" missing woff2:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` declared — no `woff2` fallback. Modern clients will load unnecessarily large files.
