# JULY 4TH SALE STARTS NOW
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's July 4th pivot send — same warehouse-sale skeleton they've been running since June 10, now dressed in patriotic urgency. The hero swap works: the warm coral/terracotta background reads "July 4th" without going full flag-bunting, and "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS" underneath the CTA is the wittiest line CB2 has written in this entire promotional run. That single copy choice earns goodwill the surrounding template doesn't.
- The structural problem is the same one that torpedoed the June 20 "Even more markdowns just added" send: there are two competing offers in one scroll. The hero promises **50% off** with a "SALE STARTS NOW" frame. Scroll down and there's a **Warehouse Sale / 60% off** section. For a subscriber who's been getting CB2 sale emails for two straight weeks, that gap reads as confusion or quiet bait-and-switch — not a reward. The "STARTS NOW" claim in the subject is also factually stale since the warehouse sale has been live since at least June 10.
- The bottom "MORE FOR YOU" product grid is solid execution — individual item cards with actual product photography give the email somewhere to land after the dual-sale confusion. But this email needed one clear number and one clear deadline, not two discount tiers stuffed into one send.

## What's working

- **"BETTER THAN FIREWORKS"** is sharp, on-brand, and seasonal without being kitschy — the best tagline in CB2's recent run.
- **Coral/terracotta hero background** earns the July 4th moment thematically without resorting to flags or stars-and-stripes clichés.
- **"SHOP NOW" button** is high-contrast, unambiguous, and above the fold.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" product grid** at mid-scroll gives the email a concrete landing pad with real item photography.
- **Category navigation tiles** (Furniture, Rugs, Lighting) make the email self-navigating for intent-driven browsers.

## What's weak

- **Dual discount confusion**: hero says 50% off, mid-email Warehouse Sale section says 60% off — the reader doesn't know which to trust.
- **"STARTS NOW" is a lie**: the warehouse sale has been running for two weeks; this framing will alienate any subscriber who opened the June 10-through-24 run.
- **No deadline visible**: "July 4th" implies a date but it's never stated explicitly — "Ends July 4th" in the hero would have sharpened urgency significantly.
- **ALL CAPS subject line**: marginal spam signal and feels blunt relative to the clever "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS" copy buried inside.
- **No loyalty or member callout**: given the promotional pressure, a "members get early access" or "extra 10% for Crate Insider" hook would have differentiated this from the prior six sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one number and commit.** Either the hero is "UP TO 60% OFF — JULY 4TH SALE" or the Warehouse Sale module disappears. Running 50% and 60% side by side reads as disorganized, not generous.
- 2. **Add an explicit end date to the hero.** "ENDS JULY 4TH" or "3 DAYS ONLY" turns "STARTS NOW" urgency into actual deadline pressure.
- 3. **Lowercase (or mixed-case) the subject.** The interior "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS" line proves the copywriting team has range — let the subject show it.
- 4. **Loyalty hook in the hero.** One line — "Crate Insider? Extra 10% in cart." — differentiates this from the public-facing warehouse blasts.
- 5. **Anchor the "MORE FOR YOU" grid with pricing.** Product cards without visible prices are a missed conversion moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Better than fireworks — up to 60% off starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `July 4th sale: ends Friday, up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our biggest discounts of the summer. Ends July 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, rugs, lighting — up to 60% off through the 4th.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line 6)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Many ESPs now flag this automatically.

**Blanket `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```
Applied globally rather than scoped to `body`. Overrides OS-level accessibility text scaling for all elements.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint: `max-width: 418px`**
This targets the exact 414px iPhone width. Gmail's mobile webview clips at 480px; content between 419–479px gets neither desktop nor mobile styles applied. Standard safe breakpoint is `480px` or `600px`.

**Conflicting desktop image rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 100% !important} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img {max-width: 690px} }
```
The `690px` rule fires first as viewport widens, then the `768px` rule overrides it with `!important`. Between 690–767px, `max-width: 690px` applies; above 768px, `max-width: 100%` takes over. The `!important` on the wider breakpoint wins unintentionally — likely intended to cap images at `690px` on all desktop sizes.

**Double-slash URLs in `@font-face` declarations**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Three font `src` URLs contain `//` double slashes after the domain. Most servers normalize this via 301 redirect, adding a round-trip per font load. Confirm these resolve without redirect; fix to single slash.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
The family is declared three times: once for the legacy EOT/WOFF stack, then twice more for woff2-only weight variants (400 and 600). The redundancy is harmless but inflates `<head>` size.

**Template placeholder comment left in production HTML**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unresolved ESP template comment (also contains a typo: "IMPUT" → "INPUT"). It should have been removed or filled before send.
