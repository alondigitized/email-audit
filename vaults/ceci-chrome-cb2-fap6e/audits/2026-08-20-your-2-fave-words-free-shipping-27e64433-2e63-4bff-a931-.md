# Your 2 fave words: FREE SHIPPING!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' cleanest, most single-minded send of the recent stretch: "it. all. ships. free." with a hero grid of eight categories (kids bedding, baby bedding, kids gear, baby gear, crate cuddlies, bath, curtains, throw pillows) all tagged "ships free," and two "Shop All Free Shipping" CTAs bracketing the hero. Compare that to 2026-08-20's "NEW MARKDOWNS ADDED: Up to 60% off clearance" send, which buried a strong hero under scroll — this one leads and closes with the offer and doesn't waver.
- The problem is what's stacked underneath: backpack bundles, a bestseller bedroom rug, a "we love these for you" product grid with $1000+ strollers and car seats, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a Design Desk booking CTA — six distinct asks after the free-shipping hero. It reads like every merchandising team got a slot in the same email. The free-shipping promise itself is also unqualified in the visible creative (no minimum spend or excluded-category disclosure visible above the fold), which risks feeling like bait once a shopper hits checkout.
- Solid top, cluttered bottom. As a parent shopper I'd click the hero and never make it past the backpack bundle before losing interest.

## What's working

- The hero headline "it. all. ships. free." is short, on-brand, and impossible to misread.
- Eight-tile category grid makes the offer feel comprehensive without needing separate emails per category.
- Two "Shop All Free Shipping" buttons (top and bottom of the hero block) reduce friction — no scrolling required to act.
- No render bugs — images, type, and buttons all display cleanly.
- Backpack bundle section is well-timed for back-to-school season and has clear "JB" monogram personalization cue in the product shot.

## What's weak

- Six unrelated CTAs after the free-shipping hero (bundles, bedroom bestsellers, "we love these," credit card, app download, Design Desk) dilute a genuinely strong single-offer premise.
- No stated minimum order or exclusions for the free-shipping claim visible in the render — "no buried fine print" signal fails since there's no visible qualifier at all, positive or negative.
- The credit card and app-download blocks feel bolted on and have nothing to do with the subject line's promise.
- "we love these for you" personalization claim isn't backed by any visible personalization signal (no name, no browsing history reference) — it's generic merchandising copy dressed as a recommendation.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the second "Shop All Free Shipping" button, or move backpack bundles/bestsellers/card/app into a follow-up send — one clear offer converts better than six competing ones.
- 2. Add a one-line fine-print qualifier near the hero button (e.g., "on orders $49+") so the free-shipping claim doesn't feel like bait once shoppers see shipping charges at checkout.
- 3. If back-to-school bundles must stay, make them the *second* email in a two-part series rather than sharing real estate with the free-shipping hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping on everything — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `No minimums, no excuses: it all ships free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, gear, cuddlies & more — free shipping storewide.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up for the nursery or the dorm. Shipping's on us.`
