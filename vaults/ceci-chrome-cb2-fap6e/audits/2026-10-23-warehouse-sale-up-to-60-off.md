# WAREHOUSE SALE | UP TO 60% OFF
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, price-led Warehouse Sale send, and it is almost a repeat of the 10-20 "WAREHOUSE SALE: UP TO 60% OFF" email (7/10). "UP TO 60% OFF" is the biggest thing in the hero, set in oversized white type on a dark burgundy chrysanthemum photo. A shopper gets the offer in about a second. The subject matches the hero word for word.
- The creative runs out of ideas below the hero. Five stacked category buttons (Furniture, Lighting, Decor, Rugs, Shop All) are a plain list with no product shown. A second banner for the Semi-Annual Dining Event (up to 30% off) competes with the main offer, and it is the same event CB2 pushed on 10-21. The footer modules (app, rewards, gift list, text signup) pile on four more asks.
- Verdict: the offer is clear and the design is polished, but the email is repetitive and has no product imagery. It is a solid 6. The fourth warehouse-sale send in four days will read as wallpaper.

## What's working

- "UP TO 60% OFF" is huge and high-contrast, so the offer lands immediately.
- The subject and hero say the same thing, with no mismatch between inbox and body.
- The dark floral hero is moody and on-brand for CB2.
- The CTA buttons are white on dark, clearly visible, and spread out for easy tapping.
- The "last-chance markdowns" line adds some credible scarcity.

## What's weak

- No product appears anywhere. The hero is flowers, so nothing shows what 60% off actually buys.
- Five category buttons with identical styling give no hierarchy, and none says where the best deals are.
- The dining banner (30% off) dilutes the 60% message. It is also a repeat of the 10-21 send, and its white "THE SEMI-ANNUAL" text is hard to read over the busy photo.
- The fine print is long. The deal's end date (Oct 28) appears only in the footer, so the hero has no urgency.
- The footer stacks the app, rewards, gift list and text signup (15% off), which is a lot of competing asks for one send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the flower hero, or add a row below it, with 3–4 real marked-down products showing sale prices. Shoppers click products more than category buttons.
- 2. Put the deadline in the hero ("Ends Oct 28") with a countdown or a "Last 5 days" banner, and cut the footer modules to one.
- 3. Make the first CTA specific and give it more weight, e.g. "Shop furniture markdowns", with the other categories as smaller text links.
- 4. Drop the dining banner, or run it as a separate send, so the warehouse offer has no competition.
- 5. Vary the subject line from the 10-20 send so the cadence doesn't read as identical.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML is a conventional XHTML table layout with responsive media queries. The confirmed problems are missing alt text on content images, 11 cleartext `http://` third-party pixel requests, and viewport settings that block zoom. The header-based compliance checks could not be completed because of how the email was captured. The source was truncated, so findings are limited to what was visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified tracking links:** 57 links were skipped as tracking/click-redirect domains, so none were HTTP-probed. Destination validity, redirect chains and 404s are unverified, not confirmed healthy.
- **Cleartext pixels:** 11 Dotomi (Epsilon) sync pixels load over `http://`:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437…`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
  - Image proxies and clients may block or rewrite non-HTTPS sources, so these cookie-sync calls will likely fail silently. Where they do load, the request leaves the HTTPS context unencrypted.
- **Ten sync pixels:** Ten separate cookie-redirect pixels in one send is a lot of third-party requests. That is a render-weight concern, and it is also a privacy exposure for recipients.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on content images (7):** All are on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`:
  - `99e03f1a-…jpg`
  - `b2c04e8a-…jpg`
  - `2248d9e6-…jpg`
  - `2c006ef6-…jpg`
  - `0042f239-…jpg`
  - `e6d03b59-…jpg`
  - `219caa71-…jpg`
  - With images blocked, these show nothing. Screen readers will read the filenames or skip the images. If any are linked, the link has no accessible name.
- **Missing alt on pixels:** `mi.cb2.com/p/up/…/o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/b7fe354c-…` and the Dotomi pixels have no alt. These should carry `alt=""` and be hidden from assistive tech. Absent `alt` is not equivalent to an empty one.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Text resizing disabled:** `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` appears in both the `*{}` rule and `body`. Together with the global `line-height:100%`, this limits user text scaling.
- **Minor:** `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` is not self-closed in an XHTML 1.0 document. This is harmless in practice.
