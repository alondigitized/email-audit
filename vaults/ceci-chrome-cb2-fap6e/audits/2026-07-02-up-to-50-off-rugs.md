# Up to 50% off rugs
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's third email in two days — following the July 1 "MORE ADDED TO SALE" send (scored 9/10 in this session) and the "Up to 50% off furniture & more" send from earlier that same day. The strategic instinct here is sound: narrowing to a single category (rugs) gives subscribers a reason to open that the prior broad sale emails couldn't claim. The subject line delivers on that promise with 19 characters of clean specificity.
- But the email body immediately breaks the promise. The hero locks in on rugs, then the very next section — "MORE SAVINGS THIS WAY" — fans out to Shop Furniture, Shop Outdoor, Shop Decor, Shop Lighting. By the time you hit the warehouse sale block (60% off, different price point), the rug focus is entirely gone. The category narrowing that made the subject compelling is abandoned in the scroll.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid at the bottom is the email's quiet strength — specific SKUs, visible prices, something to actually click. It just comes too late and too small to save the diffuse middle.

## What's working

- **Subject line does exactly what it should** — 19 characters, a clear number, a clear category, no spam signals. One of the stronger subjects in this CB2 run.
- **Hero composition is clean** — the moody room scene with rug front and center, high-contrast type overlay, single SHOP NOW CTA. No ambiguity about the offer.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalization block** — specific products with individual prices give the email a genuine click hook beyond generic sale CTAs.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds clean, no overlapping type, no broken images visible at this render size.

## What's weak

- **The body betrays the subject** — after a rugs hero, four full category tiles pivot to furniture/outdoor/decor/lighting. The category focus evaporates.
- **Two competing discount levels in one email** — 50% off rugs in the hero, then 60% warehouse sale below. The bigger number undercuts the hero offer rather than amplifying it.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing no work** — three instances of the same generic CTA across a single email, none category-specific or urgency-loaded.
- **No rug depth** — no sub-category tiles (flatweave, shag, outdoor rugs), no sizing callout, no room-scale guidance. The category promise isn't backed by any product content.
- **Cadence is in the red** — three emails in 48 hours, nine-plus in the last week. Even a great subject fights unsubscribe impulse at this volume.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the category throughout.** If the subject says rugs, the body should say rugs: rug sub-categories (flatweave / wool / outdoor / runner), a size-guide moment ("Not sure which size? Shop by room"), specific rug SKUs in the hero, linked rug room scenes. The furniture/lighting/outdoor tiles belong in the broader sale email — not here.
- 2. **Kill one of the sale tiers or subordinate it clearly.** Either lead with 60% warehouse and mention 50% off rugs as a sub-headline, or keep 50% rugs as the sole message and drop the warehouse section entirely. Two price ceilings in one email breed doubt.
- 3. **Write a CTA that matches the category.** "SHOP RUGS ON SALE" or "FIND YOUR RUG" beats three "SHOP NOW" repeats and costs nothing to change.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** This is the July 4th sale — say that. "Ends July 4" in the hero or below the CTA adds credibility the subject can't carry alone.
- 5. **Promote the "MORE FOR YOU" block.** It's doing the most behaviorally useful work and it's buried at the bottom in the smallest real estate. Pull one or two of those product tiles up near the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off rugs — ends July 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your rug moment: up to 50% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Flatweave, wool, outdoor — every style in the sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New rugs added — shop before the July 4th deadline`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based template with responsive CSS breakpoints. Two production-blocking issues are present: an unstripped developer placeholder comment and double-slash font URL paths that will trigger redirects. The HTML is truncated, so link/UTM and compliance sections reflect confirmed findings only.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML source is truncated before body links appear. The following applies to visible font asset URLs:

- **Double-slash paths on font URLs** (confirmed): Multiple `@font-face` declarations use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/...` (double slash). This will produce a `301` redirect on each font fetch, adding latency and potentially failing in strict email client sandboxes that block redirected resources.
  - Affected: `lineto-brown-light.woff2`, `lineto-brown-regular.woff2`, `GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`

- **Cross-brand font origin**: Three `@font-face` rules load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (family `HNW05`). No issue per se, but if the sister brand rotates those assets, CB2 emails break silently.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **`maximum-scale=1` on viewport** (confirmed): `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — disables user-initiated zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or changed to `maximum-scale=5`.

- **Aggressive global `line-height` reset** (confirmed): `*{ line-height: 100% }` overrides inherited line-height on all elements. This collapses readable body text and can cause overlapping descenders on taller glyphs in Outlook.

- **Mobile breakpoint is narrow** (confirmed): `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` with `.mobile { width:414px }`. Devices with viewport width 419–689px (e.g., folded foldables, landscape phones) fall into no responsive bucket — neither the `<418px` mobile styles nor the `>690px` desktop `img` cap applies.

- **`font-display: swap` in email** (confirmed): Declared on three `@font-face` blocks. This property is unsupported in email clients; it is inert but adds dead code. Not a bug, but worth noting for template hygiene.

- **Alt text**: Cannot audit — image tags not present in truncated source.
