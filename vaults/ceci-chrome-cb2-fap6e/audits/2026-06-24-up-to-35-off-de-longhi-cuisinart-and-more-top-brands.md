# Up to 35% off De’Longhi, Cuisinart and more top brands!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's kitchen-brands sale email for June 24, spotlighting De'Longhi, Cuisinart, and other names at up to 35% off. The production is clean — sharp product photography, a recognizable C&B template, and a credible discount anchored to the brand's ongoing summer sale. On its own, it's a competent multi-brand catalog drop.
- The problem is everything around it. Yesterday — June 23 — this same subscriber received *two* kitchen-adjacent emails: a De'Longhi category spotlight (6/10) and a kitchen-brands sale at the exact same 35% off (also 6/10). Today's email is that second one, reskinned with a different subject line and shuffled product order. The cadence has moved past aggressive into self-defeating territory. A subscriber who didn't buy after the June 23 kitchen sale email isn't going to buy today because the subject line now leads with "De'Longhi, Cuisinart."
- The email functions; the program doesn't. Until Crate & Barrel spaces these sends or differentiates the offer, each new kitchen drop reduces trust in the ones that follow.

## What's working

- **Recognizable hero imagery:** The De'Longhi espresso machine leads the email and matches the subject line — zero bait-and-switch.
- **Credible 35% off discount:** The offer is consistent with C&B's broader summer sale cadence, which makes it feel real rather than manufactured urgency.
- **Multi-brand product grid:** Cuisinart, knife sets, bar accessories, and smaller kitchen goods give different buyer profiles something to land on.
- **Clean render:** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum — the template holds together top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is the story, not the email:** A third kitchen-focused email in two days, with the same percentage off as yesterday's kitchen-brands send, makes this feel like a retry rather than a new send.
- **Subject line over mobile threshold:** At 56 characters, "…and more top brands!" likely gets clipped on smaller viewports — the softest part of the line is what survives.
- **No urgency anchor:** No deadline, no "ends Sunday," no countdown — nothing to convert a browser into a buyer on this send versus the one yesterday.
- **No single hero product with a direct link:** The email asks subscribers to browse a category grid rather than acting on a specific highlighted item, which raises click friction.
- **"More top brands" is filler:** The subject promises named brands then trails off with a vague catch-all that adds length without adding specificity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this send 48 hours** — or don't send it at all. The June 23 kitchen-brands email already covered this ground. If the list converts better on a second touch, test a 3-day gap, not a 1-day gap. Sending faster doesn't compound results — it compounds unsubscribes.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline in the subject and hero:** "Ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" turns a passive browse email into a decision email. The 35% off alone isn't doing that work.
- 3. **Pick one hero product and lead with it:** Elevate the De'Longhi La Specialista or the Cuisinart knife block as a named, linked centerpiece. The grid can still exist below, but give subscribers a specific thing to click.
- 4. **Cut "and more top brands!" from the subject:** Tighten to the named brands and let the body reveal the rest.
- **Subject Alt A:** `De'Longhi & Cuisinart, up to 35% off — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your kitchen upgrade: 35% off Cuisinart, De'Longhi & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus knife sets, bar tools, and small appliances — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends soon — shop De'Longhi espresso, Cuisinart knives, and more.`
