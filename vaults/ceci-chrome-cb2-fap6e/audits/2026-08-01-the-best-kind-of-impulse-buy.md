# The best kind of impulse buy
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- CB2 leans fully into "shoppable editorial" here — five named designer pieces (the Fable Sectional, Gwyneth Chair, Ambrose Sofa, Jackson Bed, Macron Console) get individual spotlight treatment before any discount appears. It's the closest sibling to the 07-31 "Rooms for your mood board" send that scored 8/10 for stepping away from the discount cadence, and it's executed with the same clean, warm-toned photography CB2 has been running all week.
- The problem is structural: the subject line "The best kind of impulse buy" promises something punchy and specific, but the email delivers a long, unhurried designer showcase with the actual "SALE UP TO 40% OFF" banner buried roughly two-thirds of the way down, after five product spotlights and a category grid. That's a mismatch between subject-line energy and body pacing — nothing about the top of the email reads as an "impulse buy," it reads as a considered furniture catalog.
- It's a well-produced, on-brand send that will perform fine with existing browsers, but the subject is doing more work than the body backs up, and there's no urgency mechanic anywhere — a step back from the "ENDS TONIGHT" / "24 hours left" sends that scored 9/10 twice last week.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks anywhere in the long scroll.
- Five-star rating stamp under the CB2 logo at the very top adds immediate social proof before any product is shown.
- Each designer spotlight (Fable, Gwyneth, Ambrose, Jackson, Macron) is clearly attributed to a named designer/studio, which reinforces the "ONLY AT CB2 — exclusive designs" hero promise.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom shows real prices, giving browsers a fast, scannable path to buy without more scrolling.
- Footer rewards callout ("EARN 10% BACK") and CB2 app download give loyalty members a reason to stay engaged.

## What's weak

- The subject promises an "impulse buy" but the hero and first five screens are slow, editorial designer profiles — the pacing doesn't match the promise.
- The one concrete offer ("SALE UP TO 40% OFF") is buried far down the email, after the hero, five spotlights, and a best-sellers grid — most scrollers won't reach it.
- No urgency or deadline on the sale banner ("up to 40% off," no "ends today/tomorrow"), a step down from the urgency mechanic that drove last week's 9/10 sends.
- "SHOP NOW" as the sale CTA is generic — every other CTA in this email ("MEET OUR BEST SELLERS") is more specific, making the discount button feel like an afterthought.
- Five nearly-identical designer-spotlight blocks in a row (image + name + one-line blurb) get repetitive well before the sale banner arrives.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 40% off sale banner (or at minimum a "Sale ends [date]" teaser) above or immediately after the hero, so the subject's "impulse buy" energy is paid off in the first screen instead of six scrolls later.
- 2. Trim the designer spotlights from five to two or three — keep the strongest pieces and let the sale/CTA carry more of the middle of the email.
- 3. Add a deadline to the sale banner ("Ends Sunday," "48 hours only") — this brand's best-performing recent sends (07-28, 07-29) both leaned on credible urgency.
- 4. Swap "SHOP NOW" for a more specific CTA on the sale banner, e.g. "Shop up to 40% off," matching the specificity of "MEET OUR BEST SELLERS."
- 5. Tighten the subject to reference the actual offer or a named piece, so open intent and body content line up.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `New designer exclusives, plus 40% off through Sunday.`

## Technical Audit: CB2 — "The best kind of impulse buy"

### 1. Technical Summary
The email is a standard MSO/Outlook-hardened HTML template with heavy vendor tracking (Epsilon/Conversant "dotomi" pixels) and 13 unlabeled images; no unsubscribe or auth headers were captured by the relay, and 10 tracking pixels load over plain HTTP.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 72 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (redirect domains), so live-destination validity is unconfirmed.
- 10 third-party tracking pixels resolve over **http://** (mixed content), all via Conversant/dotomi cookie-redirect endpoints:
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`
  - Most modern mail clients (Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail) block or proxy non-HTTPS image loads, meaning these tracking calls likely fail silently in the majority of inboxes.
- Two additional first-party tracking assets load correctly over HTTPS: `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/...?mi_mid=...&cp_tp=...`.
- The body HTML was truncated before any CTA/product links appeared, so click-through destinations themselves could not be inspected in this pass.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 13 images are missing `alt` text, including all 10 dotomi tracking-pixel images and 3 content images served from `image.mail.cb2.com` (e.g. `3bdf0b35-bd42-4149-8adc-0c41ace80e2d.jpg`, `70336f16-cdc5-4fec-ae54-737f26b5c503.jpg`, `10974092-908e-4eb9-baf1-554c9a2f362a.jpg`, plus 8 more listed in QA output). Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled or skip them entirely.
- Head markup includes correct MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>`), Apple/Gmail reformatting guards (`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`), and mobile breakpoints at 418px/690px/768px — no structural rendering defects found in the head/style block.

No merge tags, personalization placeholders (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIRSTNAME%%`), or dynamic-content blocks were visible in the captured HTML (head + partial body). No issues found in the inspected portion — full body was not available to confirm absence of broken/unresolved tokens.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from the available data — the captured HTML was truncated before any body CTA or product links appeared, and the 72 tracking links were excluded from the HTTP probe. No UTM parameters or landing-page destinations could be inspected in this pass.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Migrate the 10 dotomi.com tracking pixels (`UCMController`, `pub1.php`–`pub10.php`) from `http://` to `https://` — as-is they will be blocked/dropped by most modern mail clients, reducing the reliability of whatever attribution data they're meant to collect.
