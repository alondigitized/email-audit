# FREE SHIPPING ends today!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' urgency-close for the free shipping promotion that launched on 7/14 with "FREE SHIPPING ends tomorrow!" — the same day Crate & Barrel ran its own parallel deadline send. The sequencing is textbook: tease yesterday, kill today. The hero does its job — "ends today! free shipping" is above the fold, the threshold is visible, and the send is clean enough that nothing gets in the way.
- What undercuts it is the same thing that undercut yesterday's C&K send: the email immediately pivots from urgency into a scrolling catalog of fall arrivals (in mid-July), personalized picks, a design service, and an app push. That's four separate pitches underneath a one-thing subject line, and they dilute the close. By the time you're two screens down, you've forgotten the offer that made you open.
- The good news is this is a solid, functional deadline email. The bad news is it's nearly identical in structure to the 7/14 send — same categories, same fall arrivals banner, same app block — with "today" swapped for "tomorrow." Subscribers who opened yesterday will clock that immediately.

## What's working

- **Hero lands the offer immediately** — "ends today! free shipping" is big, above the fold, and supported by a visible shipping threshold.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — no broken images, no overlapping text, the grid navigation below the hero reads clearly.
- **"500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" banner** — the entry price point anchors the browse session and gives a reason to click beyond the deadline alone.
- **"We love these for you" personalized recommendations** — a curated product grid with individual items adds specificity the hero alone doesn't provide.
- **Coordinated brand-family timing** — running the C&K deadline send on the same day as C&B's "ENDS TODAY" email reinforces the promotion without overlap confusion.

## What's weak

- **Subject ALL CAPS "FREE SHIPPING" is a mild spam signal** — the copy is earned urgency, but the capitalization raises the filter risk slightly without adding persuasive value.
- **Four distinct pitches below the hero** — fall arrivals catalog, personalized picks, TheDesignDesk cross-sell, and an app download block fragment a send that should be a single-action close.
- **Fall arrivals in mid-July feels premature** — seasonal content in an urgency deadline email introduces cognitive friction ("wait, is it back-to-school already?").
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic** — every section uses the same verb + noun; a CTA like "Shop Free Shipping" or "Grab it before it ends" on the primary button would lock the click to the offer.
- **Cadence is high** — this is the fifth Crate & Kids / Crate & Barrel email in 24 hours; even subscribers who love the brand will feel the volume at this rate.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten the email to one ask** — cut or move the app block and TheDesignDesk cross-sell to a post-purchase or separate send. On a deadline day, every pixel should serve the urgency close, not introduce new decision points.
- 2. **Rewrite the primary CTA to echo the offer** — "Shop free shipping" or "Order before midnight" beats a generic "Shop Now" for a deadline send.
- 3. **Pull a single hero product** — choose the one new arrival or bestseller most likely to convert and lead with it directly under the offer. A "here's what I'd spend my free shipping on" product moment would make this feel editorial rather than catalog-blasted.
- 4. **Drop the fall arrivals framing** — in July, the copy should read "new arrivals" or "shop now" rather than signaling a season that hasn't started. The date-awareness mismatch is subtle but noticeable.
- 5. **Subject and preheader refinements:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping ends at midnight — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: free shipping on your order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ new arrivals are waiting — order by tonight to ship free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No code needed. Free shipping on orders $29+, today only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch**
`<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` — email is branded "Crate & Kids SALE." Some accessibility clients (e.g., screen readers, iOS preview) surface the `<title>`. Should read "Crate & Kids."

**Viewport blocks user zoom**
`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` — `maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA).

**Universal text-size-adjust override**
`*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` — applied to the wildcard selector. Overrides user browser/OS text-scaling preferences globally; accessibility concern for low-vision users.

**`line-height: 100%` on universal selector**
`*{... line-height: 100%}` — clips descenders in Outlook and older Android clients. Should be set per-element, not globally.

**Conflicting desktop image max-width rules**
Two media queries fire for viewports ≥ 768px:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared second — wins */
```
The 640px rule overrides the 768px rule on large screens due to source order, so desktop images cap at 640px, not 600px. Likely unintentional; one rule should be removed.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`format-detection` is declared four times — once combined, then three individual overrides. Redundant; clean up to a single combined declaration.
