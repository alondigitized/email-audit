# New items added. One week left to shop.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's third email in two days — following two sends on July 1 (the "Up to 50% off furniture & more" and the "MORE ADDED TO SALE" emails audited in this session) — and it leans on the same sale architecture but adds a smarter subject hook: "New items added" is a genuine refresh signal rather than another pure discount blast. That's the right instinct after this long a drumbeat.
- The body, however, undermines the subject's promise. Rather than leading with the new additions, it leads with the same 60% off Warehouse Sale dark-green hero from the "WAREHOUSE SALE" June 28 send, followed by a competing July 4th red block at 50% off. Two separate sale propositions, two different discount levels, two CTAs — the email never resolves which is the better deal or where a subscriber should actually click. The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid at the bottom is the most click-worthy part of the email and it's buried below two competing heroes.
- Net judgment: competently assembled but strategically muddled. The cadence fatigue is real, and leading with a 60%-off hero when the subject promised new arrivals is a bait-and-switch in tone even if the items are technically in the same sale.

## What's working

- **Subject line refresh hook is the strongest thing here** — "New items added" gives an existing subscriber a legitimate reason to open after multiple identical sale emails.
- **60% off warehouse hero is visually dominant** — the dark-green textured block with large type stops the scroll; the discount is legible at a glance.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid** — specific product thumbnails with names and prices make this section more clickable than the generic sale heroes above it.
- **"One week left" urgency is credible** — tied to a real deadline (July 4th), not manufactured scarcity.

## What's weak

- **Two competing sale offers dilute both.** A 60% off Warehouse Sale hero followed immediately by a 50% off July 4th block creates confusion — the subscriber doesn't know which is the better deal or where to click.
- **The body doesn't deliver on the subject's promise.** "New items added" implies you'll see the new items; instead the top half is the same warehouse sale hero that's been running since June 28.
- **CTA copy is generic across both blocks.** "SHOP THE SALE" and "SHOP NOW" give no category direction or differentiation from each other.
- **Third email in two days from this sender.** Cadence fatigue is a real open-rate suppressor; even a good subject can't fully overcome a subscriber's "another CB2 sale" reflex at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the new items, not the generic sale hero.** The subject promises new additions — show them above the fold. A "JUST ADDED" grid or callout hero with 3-4 new pieces would deliver on the subject's hook and differentiate this send from the prior two.
- 2. **Collapse the two sale sections into one.** Pick the stronger discount (60% warehouse) and mention the July 4th context as a deadline driver, not a separate parallel promotion. One offer, one CTA, one click destination.
- 3. **Make the CTA category-specific.** "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" or "SHOP WAREHOUSE DEALS" beats "SHOP THE SALE" — a specific noun increases click intent.
- 4. **Pull the "MORE FOR YOU" grid higher.** It's the most personalized, most action-ready part of the email and it's buried below two competing sale blocks. Swap its position with one of the sale sections.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just added to the sale — 60% off while it lasts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New pieces, still up to 60% off — 7 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals hit the Warehouse Sale before the July 4th deadline.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See what just dropped — up to 60% off, one week to go.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta** — prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` via `*` selector** — disables iOS Safari's automatic text scaling across the entire email, not just reset elements. Compounds the zoom issue above.
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `max-width: 418px` is too narrow. Common logical widths for modern iPhones range 390–430px; Android flagships often sit at 412px. A 418px ceiling means devices at exactly 419–430px get neither mobile nor desktop styles. Standard safe value is `480px` or `600px`.
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { ... }
```

**Double-slash font URLs** — three `@font-face` declarations reference paths with `//` in the pathname. Most servers normalize these, but CDN/cache configs may not:
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Single-slash equivalents are safe replacements.

**Mixed font CDNs** — `Lineto Brown` and `GT Sectra` load from `cb2.com`; `HNW05` loads from `crateandbarrel.com`. An outage or cache-busting on the Crate & Barrel CDN silently breaks CB2 email typography.

**`font-display: swap` in email CSS** — this property is not recognized by any email client renderer. It's dead code (minor, but indicates template wasn't stripped for email context).

**Alt text** — cannot verify from truncated source; all `<img>` tags must have `alt` attributes.
