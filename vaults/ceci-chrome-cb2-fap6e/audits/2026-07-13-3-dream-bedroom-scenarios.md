# 3 dream bedroom scenarios
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 Free Design Services making its pitch inside the bedroom category thread — a logical follow-on to the July 11 "When your bedroom is just for you" send, which already primed the category. The creative is polished: warm hero image, a clear "DREAM SCENARIO" headline, a "PERFECT BEDROOM PAIRINGS" product grid, and a well-structured "3 WAYS TO MEET" explainer (in person / virtually / online) with dual "BOOK NOW" CTAs. It reads clean and on-brand.
- The liability is the frame mismatch. Subscribers who've been in a five-day 40%-off sale loop — CB2's heaviest promotional stretch in recent memory — will open expecting a product deal and instead get a service pitch. That's not a death sentence, but it means the open converts to a click only for the segment who was already thinking about design help. Everyone else bounces.
- The cadence is also genuinely fatiguing at this point. Six emails in five days from a single brand, two of which hit the bedroom category back-to-back, is hard to defend regardless of how good the creative is.

## What's working

- **Hero clarity** — "DREAM SCENARIO" over a well-lit, aspirational bedroom image lands immediately; no ambiguity about the category.
- **"3 WAYS TO MEET" structure** — breaking the service pitch into three distinct booking modes (in person, virtual, online) is smart UX; it removes the main friction point of "how does this even work."
- **"BOOK NOW" CTA repetition** — two visible, identical CTAs in the lower module means the eye always has an action available.
- **Product pairings grid** — "PERFECT BEDROOM PAIRINGS" bridges editorial → commerce naturally; it gives a shopper something to click even if they don't want the design service.
- **Visual hierarchy** — the email reads top-to-bottom without confusion: hero → product → service → footer.

## What's weak

- **No sale mention anywhere** — subscribers who clicked through from the July 11 or July 13 Summer Edit emails are conditioned to expect a discount. This email offers nothing on price, and the gap is jarring.
- **Two bedroom emails in two days** — the July 11 "When your bedroom is just for you" already occupied this slot; this send risks reading as leftover inventory rather than a planned sequence.
- **"CB2 Free Design Services" sender name** — the sub-brand sender feels transactional and slightly unfamiliar; many subscribers won't register it as CB2 instantly, and some may hesitate before opening.
- **No urgency signal** — the design appointment offer has no deadline, no scarcity, no reason to book today vs. next month.
- **Preview text not visible / not confirmed** — at the render resolution I can't confirm a preheader is present; if it defaulted to nav link copy or "view in browser" the inbox first impression loses a full line of selling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-bound hook to the service CTA** — "Book by July 20 and get your consultation matched to this season's new arrivals." The design service has no natural deadline; manufacture one.
- 2. **Acknowledge the Summer Edit sale inside the service pitch** — one line ("All pairings below are on sale now — up to 40% off") ties the two campaigns together and preserves the sale energy for people who opened expecting a deal.
- 3. **Fix the sender name** — "CB2 Interiors" or just "CB2" in the display name; "CB2 Free Design Services" reads like a department, not a brand.
- 4. **Surface one specific room transformation** — "3 dream bedroom scenarios" teases a before/after or three distinct aesthetic directions, but the email doesn't visually deliver three named scenarios. Show three distinct hero tiles with labels (Minimal / Warm / Bold) to honor the subject line's promise.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three bedroom looks. One free appointment. Book before the Summer Edit ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In person, virtual, or online — free design help for your bedroom this month.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line 4)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` (CSS `*` rule)**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element, not just the body. This suppresses OS-level text scaling globally, compounding the zoom issue above.

**Double-slash font URLs — 4 instances**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Extra leading slash. Most servers handle this gracefully via redirect, but it adds a round-trip latency hit and is inconsistent with the single-slash declarations earlier in the same `<style>` block.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks share the family name `"Lineto Brown Sub"` with overlapping `font-weight` values (400 appears twice). The third declaration (woff2, weight 400) overwrites the earlier combined eot/woff2/woff declaration. Likely harmless in practice but fragile.

**Cross-domain font dependency on `crateandbarrel.com`**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05"; src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...") }
```
Four `HNW05` cuts load from the sibling brand's domain. If that domain's CORS headers don't explicitly allow `mail.CB2.com` origins (they won't for webmail clients), this font will silently fail. It also creates a delivery dependency on an uncontrolled third-party host.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px`**
Responsive overrides only apply below 418px. Common Android devices render at 360–412px logical width, but iPhones 14 and later are 390px+. The breakpoint catches most but misses some 420–600px viewport widths where the layout may not adapt correctly.
