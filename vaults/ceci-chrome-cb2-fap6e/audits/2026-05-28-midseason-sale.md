# MIDSEASON SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's post-Memorial Day pivot to a softer "midseason" cadence — a natural calendar beat after the 5/26 Memorial Day double-tap, but executed without the sharpness of those sends. The email stacks five distinct modules into a single scroll: a new-collab teaser banner, the midseason sale hero, an outdoor sale section (the best thing in the email), a $1,000 review giveaway, curated product picks, and dual loyalty/app modules. Each module is competent in isolation; stacked together they fracture the commercial message and leave no single offer owning the send.
- The outdoor sale section — "THE OUTDOOR SALE up to 40% off" with a pool photo and a clear "SHOP THE SALE" button — is a better email than the one it's buried inside. The main midseason hero, by contrast, shows no concrete discount in the headline. Two days after "Final hours for up to 50% off," a generic "MIDSEASON SALE" subject with no percentage is a steep step down in persuasive energy.
- **Verdict:** Over-packed and under-specified. The outdoor 40% offer deserved to lead the subject line. The review contest belongs in its own send.

## What's working

- Outdoor sale sub-section delivers the email's only concrete offer: "up to 40% off" + a clean, category-specific CTA
- "MORE FOR YOU" product grid is visually cohesive — warm neutrals, consistent photography, natural-wood palette that fits CB2's aesthetic
- Seasonal timing is sound: late May, post-Memorial Day, outdoor season at peak
- Loyalty benefit ("Earn 10% back") is visible and reinforces subscriber value
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds

## What's weak

- "MIDSEASON SALE" subject is 14 characters of zero differentiation — no %, no product, no deadline, no reason to open now vs. next week
- The new-collab teaser banner sits *above* the main sale hero, splitting attention right at the opening frame and undercutting sale urgency
- The midseason sale hero has no visible discount percentage — after "50% off" in the 5/26 hero, this reads as a regression in offer strength
- The $1,000 review-contest module is a conversion detour in a sale email; it competes with every CTA around it
- Third CB2 email in three days (5/26 LAST CALL, 5/27 all-green editorial, 5/28 this) — cadence is cresting into fatigue territory

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the strongest concrete offer in the subject.** "MIDSEASON SALE" is a label. The outdoor 40% off, or a sitewide range, or a category hook — any of those convert better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Midseason starts now: up to 40% off outdoor`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off outdoor + new midseason prices inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Memorial Day quiet ends here — outdoor furniture at its best price of the season.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 40% off outdoor, plus new picks across the store. Shop before the weekend.`
- 2. **Remove or defer the collab teaser.** A "coming soon" announcement bleeds urgency from a live sale. Either cut it from this send or push it post-scroll as a loyalty-first reveal.
- 3. **Put a discount range in the midseason hero headline.** "Up to 40% off" or "up to X% off sitewide" in the hero type alone would lift this email's opening beat substantially.
- 4. **Spin the review contest into its own send.** A $1,000 giveaway is a legitimate acquisition and UGC tool — but it deserves a focused send to a post-purchase or lapsed segment, not a mid-email detour inside a sale.
- 5. **Consider a skip day or content-only send before the next promo.** Three sale-adjacent emails in three days trains subscribers to ignore subject lines. The 5/27 green editorial was a soft moment; this email could have absorbed that slot with a lighter touch.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
Industry standard is `480px` or `600px`. The 418px value targets only the smallest iPhones (SE gen 1/2); Android devices at 420–479px wide will receive desktop layout. Likely intentional but should be documented.

**Viewport meta restricts user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from pinching to zoom. WCAG 1.4.4 failure; breaks accessibility for low-vision users.

**Universal selector overrides browser accessibility settings**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` disables OS-level text scaling for users who rely on it. Should be scoped to `body` or removed.

**Global paragraph center-alignment**
```css
p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}
```
Forcing `text-align:center` on all `<p>` elements will misalign any paragraph intended to be left-aligned and may affect screen reader flow.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for the same family**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` with overlapping weight/source combinations. The first block has no `font-weight` descriptor, creating an ambiguous fallback cascade.

**Double-slash in font URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
`//` after the origin occurs in four `@font-face` `src` values. Technically resolves correctly but is a sign of unreviewed template concatenation.

**Cross-domain font loading from crateandbarrel.com**
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-...woff2")
```
`HNW05` is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, a different origin. If that domain's CORS policy changes or the path moves, four weight variants of this font silently fail. Gmail and Outlook strip external `@font-face` regardless, so the practical impact is limited to Apple Mail / Outlook.com — but the external dependency is a fragility.
