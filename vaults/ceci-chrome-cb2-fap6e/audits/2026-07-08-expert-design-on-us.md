# EXPERT DESIGN, ON US
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's design-services pitch — a clean service email promoting free interior design consultations anchored by a "$5,000 REFRESH" value frame. After seven consecutive days of sale urgency (50% off, countdown timers, "HOURS LEFT" subject lines), this send is a deliberate brand-equity play: put the design expertise front and center, let the product catalog breathe, and rebuild premium perception before the next sale cycle.
- The execution is visually strong. The hero lifestyle photo — moody room, rich textures, velvet sofa — does exactly what CB2's creative usually does well: make the reader want to live inside the picture. The "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA is specific and action-oriented, and the "$5,000 REFRESH" anchor gives the free service a concrete perceived value.
- The strategic problem is timing. This arrives the same day as the "BACK IN STOCK" tonal reset, meaning CB2 pushed two departure-from-sale sends on the same date. Subscribers who spent the week in deal-hunting mode are being asked to switch mental gears abruptly — twice. The email also carries zero urgency, no personalization, and no bridge from "you were just getting 50% off" to "now let an expert help you spend." That gap leaves engagement on the table.

## What's working

- **"$5,000 REFRESH" anchor is the right number.** It makes a free service feel like a meaningful gift, not a foot-in-the-door sales call.
- **"BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA is specific.** Verb + noun + "free" = low-friction action; one of the stronger CTA lines CB2 has used this week.
- **Visual hierarchy is correct.** Hero image → value headline → CTA reads in the right order; the eye lands where it should.
- **Lifestyle photography is premium.** Rich textures, warm tones, and aspirational staging — this is CB2's aesthetic at its best and earns trust in the brand.
- **Tonal pivot is strategically correct.** Following six high-intensity sale emails with a service email is the right instinct for subscriber fatigue management.

## What's weak

- **No urgency anywhere.** Unlike every prior send this week, there's no deadline, no timer, no "limited spots." A free design service is finite — that scarcity is real and unused.
- **Two tonal resets on the same day.** "BACK IN STOCK" also landed July 8 — stacking two brand-recovery emails in one day undercuts both.
- **Subject is all-caps and imprecise.** "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US" doesn't tell you what the service is, who it's for, or why today. The $5,000 angle buried in the hero should have been in the subject.
- **No social proof visible.** A service email lives or dies on trust — client testimonials, before/after photos, or a designer bio would have closed that gap. Nothing like that is visible in the render.
- **Product grid at the bottom dilutes the message.** The "MORE FOR YOU" product tiles send mixed intent: are we booking a consultation or browsing furniture? The email can't decide.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the $5,000 in the subject.** "Free $5,000 Design Refresh" or "Your $5,000 Design Consultation Is Free" converts the subject from positioning to offer. The dollar number is the hook — it belongs in the first five words a subscriber reads.
- 2. **Add a scarcity signal.** "Limited appointment slots this month" or "X designers available in your area" would add credible urgency without resorting to the countdown-timer formula CB2 has been running all week.
- 3. **Add one testimonial or social proof block above the product grid.** One real client quote ("We redesigned our living room in an afternoon" / [Name], [City]) would do more work than the lifestyle photography alone.
- 4. **Cut or move the product grid.** The "MORE FOR YOU" section competes with the consultation CTA. Either remove it entirely or move it to a second CTA section below a clear divider.
- 5. **Stagger the two tonal-reset sends.** "BACK IN STOCK" and "EXPERT DESIGN" should not land the same day — one of these should have been held for July 9.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free $5,000 design consultation`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We'll redesign your space. Free.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited appointments available — CB2 designers meet you where you are`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tell us your space. We'll handle the rest, at no cost to you.`

**Double-slash in font asset URLs (low severity):**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
```

Three `@font-face` `src:` values contain a double slash (`//`) in the path. Most servers normalize this silently, but it is technically malformed and will fail on strict CDN/proxy configs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unresolved template placeholder left in production HTML (high severity):**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a developer instruction comment with a typo ("IMPUT") that was never removed. It is inert as a comment but indicates the template was not fully completed before send.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint (medium severity):**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; ... } }
```
`418px` is not a recognized email client viewport breakpoint. The industry standard is `600px` (desktop→mobile fold) and optionally `480px`. This breakpoint will miss the majority of iOS and Android devices rendering at 375–428px viewport width, causing the desktop layout to render unstyled on most phones.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (medium severity — accessibility):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility scanners. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on body (low-medium severity):**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }
```
Disables browser text-size accessibility adjustments on mobile. Most email guidelines recommend `100%`, not `none`.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for `"Lineto Brown Sub"` (low severity):**
The font family is declared three times — once with only `woff`, once with `eot/woff2/woff`, and twice more with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600` variant entries. The first declaration (no `font-weight` specified) will shadow the weight-variant declarations in some clients, potentially preventing bold rendering.

**Cross-brand font asset leakage (low severity):**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05";
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-...woff2") ... }
```
Four `@font-face` declarations reference `crateandbarrel.com` webfont URLs inside a CB2 email. These are sister-brand assets, not CB2 brand assets. This is almost certainly a copy-paste artifact from a shared template. If Crate & Barrel's CDN path changes or those files are removed, font fallback will silently occur — with no error surfacing to CB2's team.
