# That’s so ‘70s
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 breaking from its Labor Day sale cadence entirely — "That's so '70s" is a pure design-editorial send, not a promo. The hero commits fully: "TIME WILL BE KIND TO THIS / A vintage Italian reissue. Something '70s. That cult favorite." backed by a moody, well-lit lifestyle shot, then a clean "SHOP ACCENT CHAIRS" button. What follows is genuinely differentiated — six chairs, each with a named designer and a provenance line (1954 vintage Italian reissue, 1986 vintage Danish reissue, art deco, California midcentury), which is more editorial credibility than any prior CB2 send in this history has shown.
- The tradeoff is conversion mechanics: there's no price on a single chair in the hero scroll, and the only "offer" in the whole email is the Double Rewards 20% back promo, which is gated behind opening a CB2 store credit card — buried two-thirds down the page, disconnected from the '70s chair story above it. Compared to recent sends like "Now and future classics" (6/10, also editorial) and "OUTDOOR ON SALE" (7/10, subject-to-hero match), this one lands in between: best-in-history visual execution and honesty of premise, but weaker on giving a reason to click today.
- Judgement: strong brand-building send, weak direct-response send. If the goal is engagement/consideration, ship it as-is. If the goal is this week's revenue, it needs a price or percentage somewhere above the fold.

## What's working

- Subject line and hero headline match word-for-word ("Something '70s" / "That's so '70s") — no bait-and-switch, unlike the "best-selling lamp" send that promised one product and delivered a category dump.
- Every chair gets a designer credit and provenance tag (Aldine — David Girelli, '70s inspired; Meda — Gianfranco Frattini, 1954 vintage Italian reissue), which reads as authentic design journalism, not sale copy.
- Clean, consistent visual rhythm — one hero shot per chair, consistent framing, no overlapping text or broken images anywhere in the scroll.
- Double Rewards deadline ("NOW THROUGH SEPT 5TH, 2026") gives at least one piece of the email real urgency, even if it's disconnected from the chair story.
- "SHOP ACCENT CHAIRS" CTA repeats top and bottom of the chair scroll, so a scrolling reader always has a clear next action.

## What's weak

- No price is shown anywhere in the six-chair hero scroll — a reader who falls in love with the Fitz or Loop chair has to click through blind on cost, which is real friction for furniture-category purchases.
- Zero product discount in this send — the only monetary offer is credit-card-gated reward dollars, buried after the chair story ends, with no percent-off visible on the actual furniture.
- Subject is pure vibe ("That's so '70s") with no urgency, offer, or specificity — fine for open rate among engaged design fans, weak for anyone triaging a crowded inbox.
- Preview text isn't visible/legible in this render, so the inbox line likely defaults to boilerplate — a wasted opportunity to tease the vintage-reissue angle before the open.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price (or "from $X") under each chair name in the scroll — this is the single biggest lever; right now the CTA asks for a click with zero pricing context, which suppresses click-through for a considered furniture purchase.
- 2. Either drop the Double Rewards block from this send or open with a line tying it to the chairs ("Get 20% back in rewards on your new favorite chair") — right now it reads like a second, unrelated email pasted underneath the first.
- 3. Give the subject one concrete hook alongside the vibe — pair the era cue with a designer name or a number to boost specificity without losing the curiosity angle.
- 4. Add a one-line social proof or "cult favorite" stat under the top 1-2 chairs (e.g., "our #1 reordered chair") since the copy already claims cult status but shows no evidence.
