# Future heirlooms under $500
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 doing an actual product-and-price lookbook — "Future Heirlooms Under $500" — and it's a real departure from the sale/clearance/celebrity-tie-in loop this brand has been stuck in all week (2026-08-20 Floor Sample Sale 4/10, 2026-08-20 clearance 4/10, 2026-08-19 Goop 5/10, 2026-08-18 Paige Desorbo 5/10). Every hero item is named with a designer credit ("Designed by Aurélien Barbry," "Designed by Studio ANANDA"), which reads as genuinely differentiated merchandising copy rather than another markdown banner.
- The problem is the email completely fails to deliver on its own subject line. "Future Heirlooms Under $500" promises a price ceiling, but not a single product in the entire scroll — dining chair, side table, sculpture, candle holder, table lamp, sconce, the "MORE FOR YOU" grid — shows a visible price except the four small cross-sell items at the very bottom. For a value-forward hook like "under $500," that's a glaring miss: the reader has to click through blind to find out if anything is actually in range.
- It's also a long, repetitive scroll: five nearly identical hero-image-plus-CTA blocks stacked back to back (Furniture, Decor, Lighting, then a "$5,000 Refresh" sweepstakes, then a "More for You" grid) with no anchor navigation and no price framing to reward the scroll. Good creative direction, bad execution on the one selling point that got someone to open it.

## What's working

- Clear "Future Heirlooms Under $500" hero headline with a tight one-line subhead ("Time will be kind to these") sets expectation immediately.
- Designer-credit callouts (Aurélien Barbry, Studio ANANDA) on multiple products lend an editorial, higher-end feel distinct from the sale-heavy recent sends.
- Section CTAs are specific to category ("SHOP FURNITURE," "SHOP LIGHTING") rather than generic "Shop Now" everywhere.
- No render bugs — images are crisp, text is legible, no overlap or broken assets anywhere in the scroll.
- Clean top nav bar (New Arrivals / Furniture / Decor / Rugs / Lighting) gives the email a real-site feel.

## What's weak

- Zero visible prices on any hero product despite "Under $500" being the entire subject-line promise — this is the single biggest disconnect between hook and payoff.
- Five back-to-back full-width hero blocks with almost identical layout (image, product name, CTA button) make the email feel padded rather than curated.
- The "$5,000 REFRESH" sweepstakes block is dropped in the middle of a shopping email with no visual separation or explanation of why it's there — it interrupts the "heirloom" narrative.
- "MORE FOR YOU" grid at the bottom is the only place prices actually appear, which buries the value story the subject line promised at the very end of a long scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put prices on every hero product shown, not just the bottom grid — if the subject is "Under $500," show the $ next to each item so the reader doesn't have to click to check.
- 2. Cut the hero sequence from five full blocks to two or three, and lead with the lowest-priced or most compelling "heirloom" piece first.
- 3. Move or clearly badge the sweepstakes block ("bonus: enter to win") so it doesn't read as a random interruption in a product-focused email.
- 4. Add a "shop the collection" price range callout right under the hero headline (e.g., "12 pieces, all under $500") to make the promise concrete before the scroll starts.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Design-forward. Priced right. Built to last.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer pieces from Aurélien Barbry, Studio ANANDA & more — starting at $59.95`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New furniture, decor & lighting that won't break the budget`
