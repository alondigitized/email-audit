# Study & storage faves—now at 20% off!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is a category-focused sale email from Crate & Kids anchoring on a back-to-school-adjacent hook: study desks and storage at 20% off (with a 25% desk headline in the hero, which actually beats the subject line promise). The layout is organized — hero leads with the desk discount, a grid follows for storage, then a "Design Desk pros" service callout, then product recs, then app promo and editorial. That's a lot of sections, but each has a visible CTA and the visual hierarchy doesn't collapse.
- The problem is cadence. This lands alongside two other Crate family emails today — "500+ NEW fall arrivals" from Crate & Kids just hours ago (5/10), and Crate & Barrel's "Heritage-inspired" editorial. A subscriber seeing three emails in one morning is already filtering, and this one has to earn its click against that fatigue. The "SALE" in the sender display name removes any mystery.
- The offer is real, the category is seasonally timely, and the execution is clean. But this is a decent-not-great send doing competent blocking and tackling — not the 8/10 reset like the July 8 "Unique designs + Free Shipping" send that succeeded precisely because it *stopped* pushing the sale angle.

## What's working

- **Offer exceeds the promise**: Subject says 20% off; hero announces 25% off all kids desks — a pleasant upgrade that rewards opening.
- **Category specificity**: Study + storage is a tight, coherent theme — not a catch-all "summer sale" dump.
- **Design Desk callout**: The free interior design service section adds genuine utility and differentiates from a pure discount email.
- **Clean structure**: Each section has a visible CTA button and doesn't bleed into the next — no layout breakage visible.
- **Seasonally credible**: "Study" framing in July maps cleanly to back-to-school preparation, giving the discount a reason to exist.

## What's weak

- **"SALE" in sender name telegraphs the whole email** before the subject line is even read — kills curiosity, invites skip.
- **Subject undersells the hero**: Saying 20% off when the email opens to 25% off desks is backwards — put the better number in the subject.
- **No deadline anywhere visible**: "Now at 20% off" has no expiration signal, which drains urgency from the discount.
- **Personalized recs section is generic**: The "just (not) for you" block uses the winking caveat without delivering visible personalization — product tiles look like broad catalog filler.
- **Third Crate family email today**: Inbox fatigue is real; showing up three times in one morning from related brands degrades the signal on each individual send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 25%, not 20%**: The hero beats the subject — put "25% off kids desks" in the subject line so the email already delivers when it's opened.
- 2. **Add a hard end date**: Even "through Sunday" would transform "now at 20% off" into an actionable deadline rather than ambient sale noise.
- 3. **Rename the sender**: "Crate & Kids" or "Crate & Kids | Study Sale" instead of "Crate & Kids SALE" — the word SALE is a discount-bin signal, not a trust signal.
- 4. **Consolidate to two sections**: Desk hero → storage grid → one CTA. The Design Desk callout, app promo, and TheDesignGeek block each dilute the click path. Either cut them or send them separately.
- 5. **Make the personalization section actually personal**: If Crate & Kids has purchase/browse history, surface it here — "Based on your wishlist" beats a wink.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Line:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector** — overrides the correct `100%` value set on `html` and `body`. This suppresses iOS auto-sizing for all elements, degrading legibility on small screens.
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Should be `100%` or removed from the universal selector.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries** — two desktop breakpoints declare opposing constraints; the later-declared rule wins at all widths ≥640px:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* wins at 768px+ */
```
Net effect: images are capped at 640px on desktop, not 600px. Verify which is correct and remove the dead rule.

**Dark mode excluded** — `color-scheme: light` and `supported-color-schemes: light` suppress OS-level dark mode without providing a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` override. Images with white or transparent backgrounds will invert incorrectly in Apple Mail dark mode.

**`<title>` brand mismatch** — document title reads `Crate & Barrel`, not `Crate & Kids`. Minor but affects screen readers and tab display in webmail clients.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — `telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` are declared both in the combined meta and as separate tags. Redundant; de-duplicate.
