# New chairs. Instant character.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 making a deliberate pivot away from its five-day sale drumbeat — "GET IT ON SALE," "JUST ADDED TO SALE," another "collectors" editorial — with a clean new-product launch centered on new chair arrivals. After four or five promo/sale sends since July 9, this reads as a genuine pattern break, and the editorial execution holds up: styled photography, named product spotlights (HARLOW, LEROY), and a punchy two-beat subject line that earns a look.
- The main tension is familiar for a new-arrivals send with no price hook. There is no "starting at $X," no introductory offer, no member pricing on the chairs themselves — only an ancillary credit card band mid-scroll (10% / zero interest). Subscribers who spent the last week being trained to expect 40% off have no conversion trigger here; this is brand equity spend, not a close.
- That said, the creative is clean, the named chairs feel like characters rather than SKUs, and the visual hierarchy works. This is a legitimately good product launch email that just needs a price anchor to convert the people it attracts.

## What's working

- **Best subject line of CB2's recent run.** "New chairs. Instant character." is 30 characters, two clean beats, immediately specific — a sharp contrast to the generic SALE / COLLECTION / "dream bedroom" headlines of the past week.
- **Named product spotlights (HARLOW, LEROY) give the email personality.** Individual chair names over dedicated product shots is the right call — it makes the new arrivals feel collectible, not commoditized.
- **Visual hierarchy is strong.** The hero chair anchors the email at open; the product grid flows naturally below without noise.
- **Pattern break is welcome.** After the sale fatigue of July 9–15, a pure new-product launch registers as distinct rather than as noise #6.
- **Render is clean throughout.** No broken images, no layout breaks, consistent spacing.

## What's weak

- **No price signal on the chairs.** "New" is awareness, not conversion. Without "starting at $XXX" or an introductory offer, there is nothing to move someone from browsing to buying today.
- **Credit card band mid-scroll breaks the editorial tone.** A financing offer dropped in the middle of a curated furniture story feels transactional and off-register — it belongs in the footer if it belongs anywhere.
- **No hero chair.** Three or four chairs shown at roughly equal weight diffuses the focal energy. One strong lead with supporting cast would land harder.
- **Zero urgency.** "New" implies no deadline. Without "ships in 2 weeks" or "limited initial stock," the easy response is "I'll come back to this," and they won't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor in the hero.** "Starting at $XXX" or "From $X/mo with CB2 Credit" turns a browse email into an action email. Single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Choose one hero chair and lead with it full-bleed.** Make HARLOW or LEROY the unambiguous protagonist; let the rest follow in the grid. One hero is stickier than a panel of four.
- 3. **Relocate or cut the credit card band.** It undermines the editorial flow. Push to footer or remove from this send.
- 4. **Add a soft urgency signal** — "Ships in 3–4 weeks" or "Limited opening quantities" — honest, functional, and costless.
