# Up to 60% off clearance + new markdowns!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' clearance email hitting on June 4 — the same day as a separate Crate & Barrel clearance refresh ("NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →") and the third clearance-ceiling send in three days across the two brands. The headline "up to 60% off + new markdowns" is clean and the hero image is warm, but the email is doing too many jobs at once: sell clearance, promote TheDesignDesk (twice), push the app, and cross-sell Crate & Barrel. Those competing goals dilute what should be a focused clearance conversion.
- The TheDesignDesk editorial block appearing twice — once mid-email and once near the footer — is the single most damaging structural choice. A subscriber in bargain-hunting mode doesn't want to be steered toward a design consultation; they want product links. Running it twice just amplifies the friction.
- Bottom line: a solid sender with a real offer, undercut by an unfocused editorial structure and fatigue from a dense send cadence this week.

## What's working

- Hero image is lifestyle-warm and the "up to 60% off + new markdowns" headline reads immediately — no ambiguity about the offer.
- "Shop Clearance" CTA button is clearly visible above the fold.
- "We love these for you" personalized product grid is a strong mid-email signal; specific SKUs with apparent pricing visible.
- Short sub-navigation bar ("Shop Furniture", "Shop Rugs", "Shop Kids Bedding", "Shop 60% Off Furniture") gives browsers a quick exit to their category.
- Brand typography and photography remain consistent and trusted — no janky renders visible.

## What's weak

- TheDesignDesk appears twice, making the email feel editorially unfocused for what is supposed to be a sale send.
- The Crate & Barrel cross-brand promo ("we want you two to just get better") pulls attention away from the C&K clearance goal and adds a competing CTA.
- The app-download block is a secondary conversion goal that competes with immediate purchase intent — better saved for a standalone send or footer-only placement.
- "New markdowns" is unsubstantiated in the hero — no specific category or price anchoring to prove freshness vs. the June 2 send. A subscriber who got last Tuesday's "UP TO 60% OFF!" email has reason to shrug.
- No urgency mechanism is visible — no deadline, no countdown, no "while supplies last" framing with category callout.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut TheDesignDesk to footer only.** On a clearance email, a design consultation upsell above the product grid is friction. Move it to the footer so it doesn't interrupt the buy signal.
- 2. **Prove the "new" in new markdowns.** Add a "Just marked down" or "Added this week" sub-header above the product grid with at least one category callout (e.g., "New to clearance: nursery furniture"). The brand history shows this exact "new markdowns" hook was used in the June 4 Crate & Barrel send — if a subscriber got both, it reads as templated copy.
- 3. **Add a deadline or quantity signal.** Even "limited styles" or "clearance ends June 8" would lift urgency meaningfully without feeling gimmicky.
- 4. **Move the app promo to footer or remove it.** This email has five distinct CTAs (Shop Clearance, TheDesignDesk ×2, Learn More for C&B, App Store/Play Store). Consolidate to two max.
- 5. **Drop or substantially shrink the Crate & Barrel cross-sell.** It belongs in a loyalty/ecosystem email, not a clearance send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns just dropped — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just got bigger: 60% off nursery & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh cuts on furniture, gear, and more — shop before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Hundreds of new items added to clearance this week`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Blocks user zoom — WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Low-vision users cannot scale content. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; max-height:1000000px; mso-line-height-rule:exactly; line-height: 100%}
```
Applying `line-height: 100%` globally via `*` collapses line spacing on multiline elements. This is a known rendering defect on Gmail Android and Samsung Mail. The `max-height: 1000000px` hack (common Outlook fix) applied globally is unnecessary overhead.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting desktop image `max-width` breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
Because `min-width: 768px` is declared *after* `min-width: 640px`, it takes precedence at ≥768px screens — images cap at 600px, not 640px. At 640–767px screens they cap at 640px. This is likely unintentional and may cause sub-pixel layout gaps on large-screen clients.

**[LOW] Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Hard-locked to light mode. Apple Mail and Outlook on iOS will force-invert unsupported emails in dark mode, potentially rendering white text invisible on white backgrounds.

**[LOW] Duplicate and redundant meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<!-- then individually: -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Four `format-detection` declarations where one suffices. Also `<meta charset="utf-8">` appears twice (once as `http-equiv` Content-Type, once as bare `charset`). Not harmful but indicates template hygiene issues.

**[LOW] Unencoded `&` in `<title>`**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
Invalid XHTML — `&` must be `&amp;`. Some strict-mode parsers will reject this.
