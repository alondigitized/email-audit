# Registry faves: top nursery furniture right this way →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing a real registry-targeted send — "Registry faves: top nursery furniture right this way →" and a hero reading "everything you need for your nursery" sitting on a warm, styled nursery photo. Structurally it's the strongest kind of email this brand sends: a clean category walk (cribs & bassinets → chairs → dressers → on-the-go gear → room finishing touches), each section with real product photography and trust badges like "FSC Certified" and "GREENGUARD Gold Certified" baked right into the tiles.
- The problem is what's stacked on top of it. Before you even get to the nursery hero, there's a "Double Rewards is On! + up to 40% off The Labor Day Event" banner — the same generic sitewide promo this brand has now run across multiple sends this week (per the 09-01 and 09-02 history). It's competing with, not reinforcing, the registry-faves framing the subject line promised.
- Net: good bones, diluted focus. If the goal is to reach a nursery-registry shopper, lead with the registry hook the subject already earned instead of bolting on the same Labor Day banner every other Crate & Barrel/Kids email is running this week.

## What's working

- Nursery-specific hero copy ("everything you need for your nursery") matches the subject line's registry framing.
- Clean, repeatable category grid — cribs & bassinets, nursery chairs, dressers, on-the-go gear, room finishing touches — each with real product shots, not stock lifestyle filler.
- Trust badges (FSC Certified, GREENGUARD Gold Certified, "Best Arrival"/"New Arrival" tags) are visible right on the product tiles, which matters for a nursery-safety-conscious shopper.
- No visible render bugs — images load, no overlapping text, no broken tiles.
- Design Desk and AutoShip sections add credible value-adds (free design consultation, recurring baby-gear delivery) that fit a new-parent persona.

## What's weak

- The top banner leads with "Double Rewards + up to 40% off The Labor Day Event" — a sitewide, category-agnostic promo that has nothing to do with nursery/registry and has appeared near-identically in other recent sends.
- No single hero product or CTA — the top of the email is all category-level ("Shop Now," "Shop Cribs & Bassinets"), so there's no obvious "click here first" moment.
- No visible reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the render, despite this being a big-ticket furniture purchase where trust signals matter most.
- The Double Rewards banner repeats again mid-email, which reads as filler rather than reinforcing the nursery message.
- Subject promises "Registry faves" but nothing in the body visually calls out registry-specific curation (no "add to registry" badges, no registry checklist framing).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the Labor Day/Double Rewards banner from the top slot entirely (or shrink it to a thin strip) and let the nursery hero be the first thing seen — the subject already sold "registry faves," so open with a registry-specific offer or checklist, not a sitewide furniture promo.
- 2. Add a single hero product-of-the-week (e.g., a specific crib or glider) with its own "Shop This Crib" CTA above the category grid, so there's one obvious first click.
- 3. Surface actual registry mechanics — an "add to registry" badge on tiles, or a "most-added this month" callout — to make good on the subject line's specific promise.
- 4. Add star ratings or a short review snippet on at least the crib/chair sections; this is a high-consideration purchase category where trust signals convert.
