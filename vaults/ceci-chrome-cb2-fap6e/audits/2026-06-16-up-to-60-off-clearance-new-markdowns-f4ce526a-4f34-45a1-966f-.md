# Up to 60% off clearance + new markdowns!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids returning to the clearance well for at least the third time in four days — following the June 15 "Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!" send almost beat-for-beat. The hero earns its keep: bold discount claim, botanical imagery, and a clear CTA button, but the email then loses its thread across five distinct content blocks that feel stitched together from different briefs. A "product of year: lléo!" callout competes with a personalized recommendation grid, which competes with a CrateKitchen cross-promo, which competes with an app-download incentive and TheDesignDesk pitch. Any one of those could be a good email. Together, they are mush.
- The "we love these for you" personalization section is the email's most interesting idea and it's buried in the middle. The app "$250 chance" section at the bottom is a complete non-sequitur in a clearance send. By the time a recipient gets past the hero, attention has already scattered across three brand identities and two conversion goals.
- Verdict: a serviceable clearance email that would score higher if it hadn't already been sent effectively last week. The lléo product spotlight is the creative signal worth isolating — it's the only thing here that isn't a repeat.

## What's working

- **Hero lands cleanly** — "up to 60% off + new markdowns" is prominently readable against the botanical backdrop with a visible CTA button; no ambiguity about the offer.
- **lléo product-of-year callout** — elevates one specific product above the clearance pile and gives the email a differentiated hook beyond pure discount.
- **"we love these for you" framing** — signals personalization and product curation, even if the underlying grid looks fairly generic.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout is intact, text readable, images loading across all sections.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue is severe** — this subject is nearly identical to "Selling fast: up to 60% off clearance!" from June 15; loyal subscribers have already seen this pitch at least twice this week.
- **CrateKitchen cross-promo has no business being here** — a kitchen brand block buried inside a Crate & Kids clearance email breaks the category contract and dilutes the kids-focused lens.
- **App "$250 chance" feels bolted on** — a sweepstakes/lottery section at the bottom of a clearance email is a CTA collision with no connective tissue.
- **Five competing destinations, zero hierarchy below the hero** — lléo, personalized grid, kitchen, app, design desk all want the click; the email never commits to one.
- **No deadline or urgency signal** — "clearance + new markdowns" floats without a "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" anchor that would credibly accelerate action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the cross-brand blocks** — remove CrateKitchen, app sweepstakes, and TheDesignDesk entirely. This is a kids clearance email; fight one battle.
- 2. **Lead with lléo as the hero, clearance grid as body** — "product of the year" is a more compelling hook than a generic percentage. Let the clearance offer be the secondary proof point.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even "offer ends Sunday" triples the urgency signal and gives subscribers a reason to act now vs. next week.
- 4. **Compress to two CTAs** — "Shop the sale" for clearance + "Shop lléo" for the featured product. Everything else is a distraction.
- 5. **Differentiate this subject from June 15** — the current line is functionally identical to last week's send. Swap to the lléo angle or anchor on deadline:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Product of the year is on sale — lléo up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New clearance markdowns land today — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new markdowns just dropped on nursery, gear, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The lléo stroller, your most-viewed cribs, and 60% off clearance — today only.`

**Conflicting `max-width` media queries** — two desktop rules are defined in order:
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }  /* defined first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* defined second — overrides */
```
At 768px+ both queries fire; the 640px rule wins by cascade order, making `max-width: 640px` the effective cap. The 768px rule is dead code. If 600px was intentional for large viewports this is a rendering bug.
