# NEW MARKDOWNS! Up to 60% off clearance→
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the second consecutive clearance-push from Crate & Barrel — yesterday's "New markdowns are already selling fast, don't miss out!" (scored 9/10) already did the heavy lifting of announcing the post-Memorial Day clearance wave. Today's follow-up leans on the same message with a more aggressive all-caps subject and an identical discount ceiling. The core email is well-executed: clean hero, specific offer, legible product grid, no render chaos. But a subscriber who didn't act yesterday on "selling fast" needed a different hook today — a harder deadline, a category focus, or a deeper cut — not the same message turned up louder.
- Below the fold, the email stacks a Design Desk pitch, an anniversary mention, personalized picks, an app download promo, and a loyalty club CTA. That's five distinct value propositions fighting for attention after the hero. A clearance send this long risks diluting the only action that matters: buy the discounted stuff.
- Verdict: strong template, weak brief. The creative team executed well on a message that should have evolved from yesterday's send.

## What's working

- Hero reads instantly — "up to 60% off clearance" + "Shop Now" button requires no decoding or scrolling to understand the offer
- Product grid sits directly below the hero, reducing friction between the offer and the browse experience
- Clean white Crate & Barrel aesthetic renders without layout breaks or obvious bugs
- Post-Memorial Day clearance framing is seasonally accurate and subscriber-expected
- The "→" in the subject is a subtle mobile-native cue that implies a destination

## What's weak

- "NEW MARKDOWNS!" all-caps is an unnecessary spam signal — the offer itself ("Up to 60% off clearance") already carries the weight
- No deadline or urgency mechanic visible anywhere in the body — "clearance" implies scarcity but there's no "ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" to force a decision today
- Five distinct content sections below the fold (Design Desk, anniversary, app, loyalty, personalized picks) dilute the clearance conversion goal
- CTA copy "Shop Now" is the most generic option available — missed a chance for "Shop Clearance Deals" or "Browse Up to 60% Off"
- This is the second clearance email in 24 hours with the same 60% ceiling and no escalation angle; no subscriber needed this reminder stated identically

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard expiry in the body** — a single callout line like "Clearance ends Sunday — items won't be restocked" converts fence-sitters. Right now there's no reason to act today vs. next week.
- 2. **Cut the email to hero + grid + one CTA** — Design Desk, app promo, and anniversary content belong in a separate send or a suppressed segment, not here competing with a clearance message.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTA** — "Shop Now" → "Browse Up to 60% Off" or "Shop Clearance" — a six-word swap that lifts click specificity.
- 4. **Drop the ALL CAPS on the subject** — one exclamation point plus a concrete number is enough urgency; the caps risk spam filtering and look dated.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh markdowns on furniture, lighting, and décor — shop before they're gone`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Preventing user zoom on mobile is a Level AA accessibility failure. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Conflicting `img` max-width breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
These are declared in source order with the 768px rule first. Since 768px > 640px, at viewport ≥768px both rules match — the 640px rule wins due to cascade order (later in source), not the 600px container width. This likely causes images to overflow their 600px table container on wide-screen clients. The 768px rule should come *after* the 640px rule, or consolidate to a single breakpoint.

**Issue — Dark mode not declared:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Opting out of dark mode entirely forces light rendering in Apple Mail and some Android clients. Acceptable if intentional, but flag to confirm it is a deliberate decision and that light-background images (white product shots, white CTAs) have been validated against forced-dark rendering in clients that ignore this meta.

**Cannot assess — `alt` text on images:** Body is truncated. Verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, particularly product images and CTA buttons.
