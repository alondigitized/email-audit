# Caraway: Made for cleaner cooking in colors you’ll love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel running a Caraway-brand deep-dive — cookware, bakeware, food storage, and gadgets, stitched together with review-quote headers ("I wish I'd made the switch to Caraway sooner!", "Food slides around easily and browns beautifully"). It's the same single-brand-partner playbook as the 9/21 Jenny Lind send (7.00/10), and it's executed with similar discipline: hero pan shot, then four coherent modules (cookware, bakeware, food storage, gadgets/utensils) each with color-swatch counts and a clear "Shop X" CTA. Where it loses points versus Jenny Lind is that there's no actual offer anywhere in the visible render — no %/$ off, no bundle price, nothing beyond "colors you'll love" — and the email runs long, tacking on an unrelated Venus Williams collab, a "we love these for you" grid, and an app-download pitch before it ends.
- The top two-thirds is genuinely well-composed retail email design: consistent color-swatch UI, real product photography, unambiguous buttons. But it's a browse-and-admire email, not a convert-now email, and the back half dilutes the Caraway focus the subject line promised.

## What's working

- Hero product shot (pan with eggs) is appetizing and immediately legible as cookware.
- Star-rating + customer-quote pattern repeats across modules, giving lightweight social proof without needing full review blocks.
- Color/material swatch counts ("6 Colors Available," "4 Colors Available") signal real product depth and choice.
- Each module has its own clear CTA button ("Shop Cookware," "Shop Bakeware," "Shop Food Storage," "Shop Kitchen Bestsellers").
- Visual hierarchy in the top four modules is clean — quote, product photo, swatches, button, in a repeatable rhythm.

## What's weak

- No visible price, discount, or bundle offer anywhere in the render — this is pure lookbook, not a promo.
- The email badly overstays its welcome: after four Caraway modules it pivots to an unrelated Venus Williams tennis collab, then a generic "we love these for you" product grid, then an app-download pitch — none of which relates to the subject line's Caraway promise.
- Subject says "colors you'll love" but the hero and most product shots show neutral/cream tones, not a color range — the visual doesn't back up the copy.
- By the time a reader scrolls to the Venus Williams and app sections, the "cleaner cooking" narrative from the subject has been abandoned entirely, weakening topical coherence versus the tightly-scoped Jenny Lind or Oceanside sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer to the hero module — even a modest "15% off Caraway" or "free gift with $150 Caraway purchase" would convert this from browse to buy; right now there's zero urgency or incentive.
- 2. Cut the email after the four Caraway modules (cookware/bakeware/storage/gadgets) and spin the Venus Williams collab and "we love these for you" grid into separate sends — bundling dilutes both.
- 3. Swap in an actual color range shot (the blue, sage, or gold-hardware pieces mentioned in copy) for the hero instead of the neutral pan, so the subject line's "colors you'll love" is proven visually in the first frame.
- 4. Move the app-download/$500 chance pitch to its own dedicated send rather than tacking it on after an already-long browse email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New Caraway colors, same easy cleanup`
