# THAT COLLECTED FEEL
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's decor-mosaic format again — "EVERYTHING ELEVATED" leads with a single Italian ceramic vase and "Glass. Vase. Collected, not overplayed" style copy, then cascades into five near-identical category tiles (Table Jewelry, Handblown Glass, Italian Ceramics, Candle Holders, Porcelain Vases), each with its own moody, low-light product shot and a "SHOP X" button. It's the same lookbook engine CB2 ran for the 2026-09-19 "Fall bedroom refresh" and 2026-09-21 "New to covet" sends, just re-skinned for tabletop decor.
- The photography is genuinely strong — warm, styled, consistent — but the email buries its one real offer. "FINAL HOURS / BEST SELLERS UP TO 40% OFF" doesn't show up until roughly two-thirds of the way down, after six scroll-stops of moodboard tiles that all ask for the same attention. By the time a reader hits an actual discount, most will have bailed or already clicked into a random vase.
- Verdict: beautifully art-directed, poorly prioritized. This reads like a catalog page, not a marketing email with a job to do — there's no reason to lead with atmosphere for five screens when the urgency lever (found lower) is what actually drives clicks, per CB2's own history (2026-09-22 "Just one more day" scored a point higher purely for surfacing urgency early).

## What's working

- Hero image (Italian ceramic vase) is sharp, well-lit, and on-brand — immediately signals "design accessories," not generic home goods.
- Visual consistency across all category tiles — same dark, warm palette, same button style — gives the email a cohesive, premium feel.
- "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom shows actual prices ($24.95, $9.95, $4.95, $19.95) with thumbnail images — concrete and scannable.
- No render bugs — every tile, button, and image loads cleanly with no overlap or broken assets.

## What's weak

- The only real offer (Best Sellers up to 40% off, "FINAL HOURS") is buried after five decorative category tiles — most readers won't scroll that far.
- Six sequential "SHOP X" CTAs (Bangle, Senelle, Ariana, Lucid, Luna, plus more-to-covet links) dilute hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to click first.
- Subject "THAT COLLECTED FEEL" is abstract mood-copy, not a product or offer — gives a scanning inbox zero reason to open over the dozen other CB2 sends this week.
- This is the third moodboard-style send in five days (compare 09-19 "Fall bedroom refresh," 09-21 "New to covet") — cadence fatigue risk for anyone opening more than one of these.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "BEST SELLERS UP TO 40% OFF" banner to the top, right under or replacing the hero — lead with the offer, let the category tiles serve as a "shop the collection" continuation below it, not the whole show.
- 2. Cut the five category tiles down to two or three — Handblown Glass and Italian Ceramics carry the visual weight; Table Jewelry, Candle Holders, and Porcelain Vases can be one combined "more finishing touches" strip.
- 3. Rewrite the subject to name the offer, not the mood — this only works as a subject if a hard discount is actually the hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Best sellers, up to 40% off — final hours`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Italian ceramics we can't stop restyling`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Handblown glass, ceramics, and candle holders — sale ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New vases, vessels, and jewelry dishes for the console table.`
- 4. Give the "NEW & BEST SELLING" single-product block (currently just one unlabeled image + "SHOP NOW") an actual product name and price — right now it's the least informative CTA in the email.
