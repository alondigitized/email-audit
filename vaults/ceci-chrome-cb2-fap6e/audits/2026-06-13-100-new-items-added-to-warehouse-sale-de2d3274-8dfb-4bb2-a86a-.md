# 100+ new items added to Warehouse Sale
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's Warehouse Sale re-engagement send — the "100+ new items" angle is designed to pull back anyone who saw the June 10 launch and didn't convert. The subject line is the best thing here: a specific, under-50-char hook that gives a real reason to re-enter a sale you already know exists. But the body doesn't back it up. Inside, the hero is a near-identical recycling of the June 10 "WAREHOUSE SALE / UP TO 60% OFF / [category links] / NOT SOLD OUT. YET." template. There's no dedicated "just added" module, no new-arrivals callout, no visual proof of those 100+ items. The subject made a promise; the body shrugs.
- What saves it is the "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid at the bottom. Priced items, specific product names, and a few showing 10% off — that's doing real conversion work. But a mid-email sweepstakes ("LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000") competes for click attention in a sale email that should be laser-focused on getting someone to shop.
- Judgment: A solid-subject email wearing the same body as every CB2 Warehouse Sale send this week. The differentiation lives in the inbox preview and nowhere else.

## What's working

- Subject line "100+ new items added" is specific, credible, and under 40 chars — the strongest inbox hook CB2 has sent since the June 10 launch
- "UP TO 60% OFF" hero maintains the sale frame without confusion; visual hierarchy puts the discount first
- "NOT SOLD OUT. YET." scarcity module is retained — the best owned line in CB2's toolkit and it still works
- "MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid with real prices and specific item names is genuinely browsable
- "EARN 10% BACK" loyalty nudge in the footer adds incremental value for members

## What's weak

- The body doesn't prove the subject line claim — no "just added" badge, no new-arrivals module, no visual differentiation from the June 10 template whatsoever
- Fourth CB2 email in four days (Jun 10 launch → Jun 11 SO EARTHY → Jun 12 SOFAS/Todd Snyder → Jun 13 this); cadence fatigue is real and there's no "final hours" framing to justify the frequency
- No sale deadline visible — the expiration date/time is absent from the hero and from any urgency module
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" sits mid-email and competes directly with the sale CTA; it belongs in a separate send or deep in footer
- Category CTAs (FURNITURE, LIGHTING, DECOR) are text links without button affordance — lower click pull than the June 10 version appeared to have

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a "JUST ADDED" hero module** — a dedicated row or band below the hero that says "100+ new arrivals — shop what's new" with a dedicated CTA. The subject makes a promise; the body must keep it.
- 2. **Kill the $1,000 review sweepstakes from this email** — it's a conversion killer mid-sale-funnel. Save it for a post-purchase trigger.
- 3. **Show a sale deadline** — "Ends [date]" or "Last day: [date]" in the hero. No deadline = no urgency = no pressure to act now.
- 4. **Elevate the loyalty play above the fold** — "EARN 10% BACK" is buried in the footer; for members this is a second reason to buy today, not a footnote.
- 5. **Rename "MORE FOR YOU" to "NEW IN WAREHOUSE SALE"** — reframes the personalized grid as fresh inventory rather than algorithmic retargeting.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users with low vision from zooming. Change to `initial-scale=1` only, or add `user-scalable=yes`.

**Wildcard `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` overrides OS accessibility settings**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none;...}
```
Applying this via `*` is more aggressive than the `body`-scoped version already present. The body-level rule is correct; the wildcard version should be removed.

**Double-slash typos in three font `src` URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
`//` after the domain works on most servers due to path normalization, but it's non-canonical and can fail on CDN configurations that treat double-slash as invalid. Remove the extra slash.

**Cross-brand font domain in a CB2 email**
Four `@font-face` declarations load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. This is not a blocking issue if both domains share infra, but it introduces a cross-origin dependency that can cause CORS failures or Content-Security-Policy blocks in certain webmail clients. Verify these fonts are intentionally served from the sibling domain.

**Redundant `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` — one without weight/style descriptors (implicit normal/normal) and two with explicit `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The implicit declaration conflicts with the `400` explicit one. Consolidate to the two explicit weight-keyed declarations only.

**"Lineto Brown Light Sub" has no woff2 source**
```css
@font-face{
  font-family: "Lineto Brown Light Sub";
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
}
```
Only `.woff` is declared. All other face declarations include `woff2`. Add a `woff2` source as the first format for modern clients.

**Single mobile breakpoint at 418px is narrow**
The `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` breakpoint targets only very narrow screens (iPhone SE-class). No breakpoint exists for 419px–767px (most modern phones). Consider adding a second breakpoint around 600px for the mid-range phone viewport.
