# The sale you can't miss
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Same beach-at-dusk hero, same "SHOP THE SALE" button, same "up to 50% off" copy CB2 has run repeatedly this stretch — this is the third-plus near-identical Labor Day hero, this time paired with a subject line so generic ("The sale you can't miss") it could belong to any retailer running any sale. Compare to 2026-08-30's "That's so '70s," which broke format entirely and scored an 8 — this one goes the opposite direction, doubling down on the most-repeated creative with the least-specific subject yet.
- The email itself is competently built: clean category grid, credit-card cross-sell up top, clearance banner, app/rewards footer block. Nothing is broken. But there's no hook — no product name, no percentage in the subject, no urgency deadline visible anywhere except the fine-print-adjacent Sept 9th line buried in the credit card banner. It reads as filler in a sequence that's already fatigued the inbox.
- Judgement: functional but weak. This is the kind of send that trains subscribers to stop opening because the sale "sale" itself never changes.

## What's working

- Clear four-section funnel: credit card offer → sale hero → category grid → clearance banner → loyalty/app footer.
- Category grid (Outdoor, Rugs, Lighting, Decor & Mirrors, Entertaining, Shop All) gives multiple entry points beyond the single hero CTA.
- "CATCH IT WHILE YOU CAN" rug callout adds a specific hero product, unlike some prior sends that stayed fully generic.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, buttons are visible and unambiguous.
- Clearance banner ("UP TO 60% OFF") gives a secondary, stronger offer for bargain-focused clickers.

## What's weak

- Subject "The sale you can't miss" names no product, no percentage, no category — pure filler copy with zero information content.
- Hero is the exact same green/dusk beach photo, same layout, same "SHOP THE SALE" button as the two prior Labor Day sends this week.
- No visible deadline in the hero itself — the only date (Sept 9th) is buried in the top credit-card banner, not the sale.
- Cadence problem: this is at least the third "up to 50% off" Labor Day send in four days with no new creative angle.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the generic subject and put the actual number in it — "50% off" beats "you can't miss" every time on open rate. This is the single highest-leverage fix here.
- 2. Retire the beach-hero creative for this cycle — CB2 has three variants of it live already; swap in the rug or a new lifestyle shot to break pattern fatigue.
- 3. Move the Sept 9th deadline into the hero itself, not just the credit-card banner — visible urgency should live where the eye actually lands.
- 4. Lead the preview text with a category or product tease instead of leaving it to repeat/pad the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 50% off, ends Sept 9`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rugs, lighting, decor — all up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on rugs, lighting, and outdoor — through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Labor Day sale ends Sept 9. Here's what to grab first.`

# Technical Audit — CB2 "The sale you can't miss"

**1. Technical Summary**
Email renders via standard MSO/VML-hedged HTML with heavy `@font-face` loading, but ships legacy insecure ad-redirect pixels and lacks verifiable unsubscribe/authentication headers in the captured relay data.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 61 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by automated HTTP probing (QA tool limitation, not necessarily a defect — cannot confirm live status).
- 10 sequential Dotomi ad pixels served over **plain HTTP**: `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php`, plus `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437...`. Mixed-content HTTP resources in an HTTPS mail context are commonly blocked/stripped by mail clients (Gmail, Outlook), silently breaking these tracking calls.
- Legitimate tracking pixels (`mi.cb2.com/p/up/.../o.gif`, `dv.cb2.com/o/...`) are correctly served over HTTPS.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- 9 content images missing `alt` text (all under `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/...`), plus the 12 tracking/ad pixels above also lack `alt`. Screen readers and images-blocked clients will show nothing for the content images.
- Markup includes full Outlook/MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`, `xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`) and standard reset styles (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`) — appropriate hedges for Outlook desktop rendering.
- Responsive breakpoints present at 418px (mobile) and 690px/768px (desktop) via media queries — no structural issues found in the truncated source.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No merge tags or personalization tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) visible in the truncated HTML. No issues found based on available source.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found — one-click unsubscribe via mail client UI cannot be confirmed present.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header not found — even if `List-Unsubscribe` exists, one-click (POST) unsubscribe support is unconfirmed.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status cannot be verified from this capture.
- Note: all three are flagged as relay-capture limitations (AgentMail), not confirmed absent from the original send — cannot conclude a compliance violation from this data alone.
- No in-body unsubscribe link/footer visible in the truncated HTML (truncation limits confidence here).

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Truncated HTML cuts off before CTA/product links are visible, so UTM parameter presence and landing-page alignment cannot be evaluated from the provided source.

**7. Recommendations**
- Migrate the 10 Dotomi ad-pixel URLs (`pub1–10.php`, `UCMController`) from `http://` to `https://` to prevent mixed-content blocking in modern mail clients.
