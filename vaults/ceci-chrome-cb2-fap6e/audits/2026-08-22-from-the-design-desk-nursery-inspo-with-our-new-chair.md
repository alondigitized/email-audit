# From The Design Desk: nursery inspo with our new chair
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' most editorial, design-forward send yet — "1 nursery chair, 4 ways" is a genuinely clever hook, and the execution follows through: a mood board, then four fully styled nursery vignettes (field green stripes, oat tan plain weave, denim blue, linen natural) all built around the same glider reupholstered in different fabrics. It's a real content idea, not just a markdown dump, and it reads more like a design magazine feature than a promo blast.
- The problem is length and repetition. Four near-identical "New Arrival / New Arrival / Bestseller" three-up grids stacked on top of each other is a lot to scroll through for one message, and by fabric #3 the visual novelty has worn off even though the styling is genuinely nice. There's no price, no discount, no urgency anywhere in this email — it's pure inspiration/consideration content, which is a legitimate strategy but caps how hard it can perform on clicks today.
- Compared to the brand's recent clearance and Jenny Lind sends (2026-08-21, "NEW markdowns! Up to 60% off," 7/10; "Our most loved furniture collection," 6/10), this is a nice change of pace and shows real design credibility — but it needs to be tighter to convert, and "Book a Free Appointment" competing with "Shop [Fabric] Furniture" as dual CTAs on every section dilutes the ask.

## What's working

- The "1 nursery chair, 4 ways" concept is a strong, specific hook that's fully paid off in the body — this isn't clickbait.
- Each fabric section is styled as a complete room vignette (rug, art, crib, curtains), which sells the aesthetic, not just the chair.
- "The Design Desk" masthead and consistent typography/color-swatch treatment give it a real editorial identity distinct from Crate & Kids' usual promo template.
- The mood board section near the top ("start with a mood board") is a nice touch that reinforces the design-service angle before diving into products.

## What's weak

- No price, discount, or any concrete offer visible anywhere in the email — for a subscriber base used to markdown emails, this could underperform on clicks despite strong visuals.
- Four repeated three-up product grids (New Arrival / New Arrival / Bestseller) is visually redundant — by the third repetition the eye stops tracking new information.
- Two competing CTAs per section ("Shop [X] Furniture" and "Book a Free Appointment") dilute the primary action — it's unclear which one the brand wants clicked.
- The email is extremely long for mobile — a reader has to scroll through the equivalent of 5+ full-screen sections before reaching anything actionable beyond the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut two of the four fabric sections (keep field green and one neutral, e.g. oat tan) and route the rest to a landing page — this alone would cut scroll length by ~40% without losing the concept.
- 2. Pick one CTA per section instead of two — "Shop the Chair" as primary, with "Book a Free Appointment" only once at email footer level, not repeated four times.
- 3. Add a light incentive tied to the design-service angle (e.g. "free swatches with any appointment" or a small % off custom upholstery) — this is a considered-purchase category, so even a modest nudge could convert browsers.
- 4. Add a sticky/early "Shop the Chair" button right under the hero, before the mood board, so mobile users who don't scroll far still get one clear action.
