# All the things to finish their dream room
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' back-to-school room-refresh send, and it opens well before it sprawls. The hero nails the brief — "finishing touches for their dream room" over a genuinely styled kid's room, a "Starting at $29" price anchor, and one clean CTA ("Shop New Arrivals"). That's a strong first screen.
- Then it does what this brand's recent sends keep doing (see the 2026-07-31 "Dinnerware designed to wow" 7.00/10 and 2026-08-02 "How a top designer picked her forever furniture" 8.00/10): it keeps going. Decor → rugs → lighting → kids bedding → baby bedding → a $5000 sweepstakes → a 50%-off sale banner → a "we love these for you" cross-sell block with a $1300 stroller and a $1499 dresser → a credit card pitch → an app download pitch → a store locator. By the time a reader hits "we love these for you," the "dream room" thread from the subject line is long gone.
- Judgment: solid top-of-funnel creative buried under too many competing offers. The hero alone would have been an 8-9; the full scroll drags it back down to the brand's usual mid-8 range.

## What's working

- Hero copy and subject are aligned ("finishing touches for their dream room" mirrors the subject almost exactly) — no bait-and-switch on open.
- "Starting at $29" is a real, specific price anchor sitting right under the headline — instant answer to "is this affordable."
- Section headers are lowercase and consistent ("fresh & fun decor," "new soft & durable rugs," "modern lighting looks") — cohesive brand voice throughout.
- No render bugs anywhere in the 12,000px scroll — every image loads, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- The Crate Card and app blocks are visually separated with dark backgrounds, so they don't get confused with the product sale sections.

## What's weak

- Ten-plus CTAs compete for attention (Shop New Arrivals, Shop Best Decor, Shop New Rugs, Shop New Lighting, Shop New/Best Bedding, Enter Now, Shop the Sale, Download Now, Book a Free Appointment, Learn More, see nearest store) — no single next action is reinforced.
- "up to 20% off" and "up to 50% off summer sale" both carry an unexplained asterisk with no visible fine print anywhere in the render — reads as exclusions being buried, not disclosed.
- The "we love these for you" block breaks the room theme entirely with a $1300 Nuna stroller system and a $1499 solid-wood dresser — jarring price jump right after a "starting at $29" hero, and no longer about "finishing touches."
- The sweepstakes ("$5000," enter by August 3) and the Crate Card pitch dilute the room-refresh message — three different asks (shop, enter, apply) stacked in one email.
- Subject line is theme-y but not concrete — "all the things" promises nothing specific an inbox scanner can act on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after "modern lighting looks" or "cozy, new kids bedding" — end on one strong CTA instead of extending into baby bedding, sweepstakes, sale, cross-sell, credit card, and app in sequence. Fewer sections will lift click-through more than any copy tweak.
- 2. Move "we love these for you" to its own send, or at minimum swap the $1300/$1499 items for pieces under $100 that match the "starting at $29" promise made in the hero.
- 3. Resolve or drop the asterisks on "up to 20% off*" and "up to 50% off*" — either show the terms inline or drop the star; an unexplained asterisk reads worse than no disclaimer at all.
- 4. Tighten the subject to name a concrete offer instead of "all the things."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Finishing touches for their dream room, starting at $29`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Shelves, rugs, lighting: up to 20% off room refreshes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals for their space — plus a chance to win $5000`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Decor, rugs & lighting starting at $29, back-to-school savings inside`
- 5. Pick one single-product hero image (the scalloped shelf or the dresser shown) and link "Shop New Arrivals" directly to that product rather than a general collection — gives the eye and the click a concrete destination.
