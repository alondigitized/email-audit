# ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Bestsellers & new arrivals from Nuna!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a Nuna-branded spotlight email, but it only holds that focus for about the top third before sliding into the same kitchen-sink format the family of brands has leaned on all week — a back-to-school giveaway, a "summer sale" nursery furniture push, an unrelated product grid, and a new-product teaser for a sleep system. The Nuna section itself is well done: named car seats (RAVA, REVV, EXEC) with star ratings, a strollers/highchairs carousel, and a clean "Shop All Nuna" CTA up top.
- The subject line is the biggest tell that this email is trying to punch above its weight — five star emojis stacked in front of "Bestsellers & new arrivals from Nuna!" reads as an attempt to fake a review score in the inbox, which is a soft trust violation even though the body itself isn't dishonest. Compare to the brand's 2026-07-26 Breville send (9.00/10), which had one clean urgency hook and stuck to it; this one dilutes a genuinely strong hero section with four more asks stacked below it.
- Net: solid product presentation for Nuna specifically, undercut by a gimmicky subject and the now-familiar sprawl once you scroll past the first screen. Middle-of-the-pack send — better than the average promo, worse than the brand's best recent work.

## What's working

- The Nuna hero section is genuinely on-topic: named products (RAVA, REVV, EXEC car seats), star ratings, and a clear "Shop All Nuna" CTA right under the fold.
- Product tiles carry real social proof — star ratings on every SKU plus a pull-quote review for the stroller ("It's lightweight, easy to fold up...").
- No visible render bugs — images load, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.
- Visual consistency is strong throughout — Crate & Kids cream/wood palette, consistent product photography style.

## What's weak

- Subject line uses five star emojis as a fake rating gimmick, which reads as manufactured social proof rather than an actual review score.
- The email promises "Nuna bestsellers & new arrivals" but pivots hard by the midpoint into a back-to-school giveaway, a generic summer sale, and an unrelated "we love these for you" grid — none of it Nuna.
- Two competing headline offers stacked at the very top (a "20% off back-to-school" banner immediately above "new faves & bestsellers from Nuna") split attention before the reader even reaches the hero.
- No visible deadline on either the back-to-school discount or the summer sale, so neither offer creates real urgency.
- Per brand history, this is at least the sixth near-identical mega-promo in about 36 hours (7/25–7/26) — cadence fatigue is a real risk here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the star-emoji gimmick from the subject — it reads as fake social proof, not a rating. Lead with the Nuna angle instead, since that's the one part of the email that's genuinely focused.
- 2. Pick one top banner, not two. Either lead with the back-to-school discount or the Nuna spotlight — stacking both headlines at the top forces the eye to choose.
- 3. Trim the email to what the subject promises. If it's a "Nuna bestsellers" email, cut the unrelated product grid and the generic summer sale, or split them into a separate send.
- 4. Add a real deadline to the back-to-school and summer-sale banners — "ends Sunday" beats an open-ended percentage-off with no clock.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
