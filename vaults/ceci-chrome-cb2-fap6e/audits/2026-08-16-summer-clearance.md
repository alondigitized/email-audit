# SUMMER CLEARANCE
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- After twelve straight days of designer spotlights, product events, and lookbooks, CB2 finally sends the plainest, most direct email of the run: "SUMMER CLEARANCE, UP TO 60% OFF" over a moody teal-water hero shot, with a stacked category-button menu (Shop All, Furniture, Outdoor, Decor, Lighting, Rugs, Bedding & Bath) doing all the navigational work. This is basically the same beat as yesterday's "End of Summer Sale" (5/10, 2026-08-15) — same discount-led, category-stacked format — just with a bigger number (60% vs whatever that one led with) and a different hero image. It's honest, it's clear, and it's not trying to be clever, which after a week of overwrought designer-spotlight copy is almost a relief.
- The problem is the same one that's dogged this brand all week: no single hero product, no urgency deadline, and a real discount buried behind a dagger footnote ("‡") that nobody will read on their commute. The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid below is generic algorithmic merchandising, not curated for the sale. This is a functional, forgettable sale email — better than the designer-spotlight fluff, on par with yesterday's near-identical send.

## What's working

- Clear, unambiguous hero message — "SUMMER CLEARANCE, UP TO 60% OFF" is the first thing the eye hits, no competing copy.
- Category buttons (Shop All, Furniture, Outdoor, Decor, Lighting, Rugs, Bedding & Bath) give multiple concrete entry points instead of one vague CTA.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, six product tiles all load with prices visible.
- The "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" banner and 15% off text-signup are legitimate secondary hooks that add value beyond the sale.

## What's weak

- "Up to 60% off" is footnoted with a dagger and no visible terms in view — feels like a bait number until you dig for fine print.
- No specific end date or countdown — "while supplies last" (buried in the legal footer) is the only urgency cue, and it's not credible as urgency.
- Hero has no single anchor product — it's an abstract water/reflection photo with text over it, disconnected from what's actually on clearance.
- The six "MORE FOR YOU" products (a $2299 marble coffee table, a $1199 bed) don't read as clearance items — no strikethrough pricing or discount tags, so it's unclear if these are even part of the sale.
- Same discount-sale format as 2026-08-15's "End of Summer Sale" — two near-identical clearance sends in two days is thin cadence variety.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add strikethrough/sale pricing directly on the product tiles so "up to 60% off" is provable at a glance instead of an abstract headline.
- 2. Replace the abstract water-reflection hero with an actual clearance product (or a grid of 3-4 marked-down hero items) so the eye lands on something purchasable.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the hero ("Ends Sunday 8/17") — even a soft one beats none.
- 4. Move the 60% off qualifier out of the dagger footnote and into small visible text under the headline so shoppers know if it's storewide or exclusions apply.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% off — Summer Clearance ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance: dining, decor, rugs up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Marked-down furniture, decor, and rugs — while it lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just added across every room.`
