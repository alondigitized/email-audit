# ALMOST OVER: Design Legends Sale
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's urgency-close on the Design Legends Sale — a designer-attribution campaign spotlighting Gianfranco Frattini, Paul McCobb, and Steen Østergaard. The "ALMOST OVER" subject pairs with a hero that reads "DESIGN LEGENDS SALE" and buries the actual discount ("20% off") in small type beneath it. That's the central problem: the discount exists, but it plays second fiddle to editorial storytelling at the exact moment you need to be screaming value.
- The brand history context makes this read even flatter. CB2 opened the Warehouse Sale on June 10th at "UP TO 60% OFF" — a 60% promise — and now four days later they're asking subscribers to get excited about 20% off a curated designer sub-collection. The offer feels like a step backward, and the "ALMOST OVER" urgency doesn't override that math. The "WIN A NEW LIVING ROOM" sweepstakes module mid-email is a smart engagement anchor, but it's grafted onto an already-mixed-signal send.
- The visual execution is clean and on-brand — photography is excellent, designer portraits give it editorial legitimacy — but the creative is working against the subject line's urgency premise. "ALMOST OVER" promises a closing bell; the body delivers a museum wall.

## What's working

- Designer portrait + name pairing (Frattini, McCobb, Østergaard) gives the sale real editorial credibility — feels curated, not clearance.
- Photography quality is excellent; the furniture shots are rich and brand-consistent.
- "WIN A NEW LIVING ROOM" sweepstakes module adds a participation hook that pulls engagement beyond pure purchase intent.
- The hero CTA ("SHOP NOW") is visible and unambiguous, sitting directly under the sale headline.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no broken images, text is legible throughout.

## What's weak

- "20% off" is visually subordinate in the hero — the discount is the reason to click and it's buried under "DESIGN LEGENDS SALE" in small text.
- The "ALMOST OVER" urgency subject clashes with the leisurely designer-editorial body scroll; the promise and the experience don't match.
- Coming four days after a "UP TO 60% OFF" Warehouse Sale launch, 20% off reads as a downgrade — no context explaining why this collection is excluded or why it commands a premium.
- The sweepstakes module mid-email competes with the sale CTA for attention; two separate conversion goals dilute both.
- Designer backstory section feels long for a closing-urgency email — this copy belongs in a launch send, not a last-chance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the discount, not the brand heritage.** Move "20% OFF" to the hero headline in large type; demote "Design Legends Sale" to a subhead. The urgency subject line earns a number, not a name.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the body.** "ALMOST OVER" in the subject but no visible end date or timer anywhere in the email is a wasted urgency signal. "Ends Sunday midnight" in the hero would make it real.
- 3. **Collapse or cut the designer bios.** In a last-chance close, a name + one product + a price is enough. Full portrait + paragraph copy belongs in the launch email (which probably already ran).
- 4. **Resolve the offer hierarchy vs. Warehouse Sale.** A one-liner like "These pieces are not in the Warehouse Sale — 20% off is the only deal you'll see" would reframe the discount as exclusive rather than lesser.
- 5. **Make the sweepstakes its own send** or push it to the footer. Right now it's a mid-email fork in the road.
- **Subject Alt A:** `ALMOST OVER: 20% off Design Legends — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: Frattini, McCobb & Østergaard — 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `These pieces aren't in the Warehouse Sale. This is the only discount.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the curated designer collection before it closes tonight.`

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Strip the template comment** (`<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT...]-->`) from the send template immediately; add a pre-send linter rule to flag `<!--[` patterns.

2. **Deduplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — it is declared three times in `<style>` with different `src` URLs. The last declaration wins in CSS cascade, meaning the first two blocks (including the `.eot` Outlook fallback) are silently overridden. Consolidate into one block with proper format hints.

3. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta or replace with `user-scalable=yes` to restore pinch-zoom for accessibility compliance.

4. **Scope the wildcard rule** — replace `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` with `body{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none}` to avoid unintended suppression of system scaling in nested elements.

5. **Audit cross-domain font loading** — `@font-face` pulls from both `cb2.com` and `crateandbarrel.com`. Confirm both origins have CORS headers permitting email client fetches; some clients block cross-origin web fonts without `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`.

6. **Widen mobile breakpoint** from `418px` to `480px` to cover the full standard mobile-client range.
