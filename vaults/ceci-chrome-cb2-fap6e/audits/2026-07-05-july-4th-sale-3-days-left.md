# July 4th Sale. 3 days left.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- Day-after-the-holiday CB2, and they've made a sensible choice: pivot the hero to **lighting** — a category that hasn't been the lead in this sale sequence yet — while keeping the now-familiar countdown timer anchor. The 50% off lighting message is clean and legible. The bigger deal in the email, however, is buried: "WAREHOUSE SALE 60%" sits below the fold, offered more real estate than most brands would give a secondary section but still clearly subordinate to a weaker headline offer.
- The email is doing a lot at once — lighting hero, three-tile category grid, warehouse sale, personalized product tiles — and each section competes for attention rather than flowing into the next. That's a structural problem: the eye doesn't know where to go after "SHOP NOW" for lighting.
- The core strategic miss here is obvious the day after the holiday: "July 4th Sale" as a sale name already feels past-tense on July 5. "3 days left" is doing a lot of heavy lifting to rescue urgency that the holiday anchor has already spent.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer** (02:17:04:32) is the strongest urgency signal in the email — visceral and credible since the sale genuinely runs past the holiday.
- **Lighting as the hero category** is smart rotation; furniture, rugs, and decor were each headlined in prior sends — lighting is fresh.
- **"WAREHOUSE SALE 60%"** is a legitimately better deal than the hero and gives deal-hunters a secondary hook.
- **"More For You" product tiles** with visible prices give a click target for subscribers who want to skip the category browse and go straight to a product.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- **The stronger deal (60% warehouse) is buried below the weaker deal (50% lighting)** — this is the biggest single structural error.
- **"July 4th Sale" as the brand name on July 5 lands stale** — the holiday has passed and the name of the sale works against urgency rather than for it.
- **"SHOP NOW" is doing three jobs at once** — three separate "SHOP NOW" buttons across three different categories with no differentiation; subscribers have to mentally re-anchor with each one.
- **No social proof or reviews anywhere** in the visible body — in a high-stakes clearance moment, a few review counts or star ratings would push hesitant clickers.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the 8th+ send in this series; a subscriber who has seen seven identical "sale sale sale" emails has already decided.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hero to the 60% Warehouse Sale** — it's the stronger number and differentiates this send from every prior announcement. Light can be the supporting category, not the lead.
- 2. **Rename the CTA buttons to match the section** — "Shop Lighting," "Shop Warehouse," "See Your Picks" — gives each click a distinct destination and reduces decision paralysis.
- 3. **Change the sale framing from "July 4th Sale" to the time remaining** — once the holiday has passed, the anchor is spent; lead with the deadline, not the occasion.
- 4. **Add one proof signal to the warehouse tile** — even "4.8★ · 2,300 reviews" beneath one product image meaningfully increases click trust on a clearance item.
- 5. **Cut the middle category grid** — "More Savings This Way" (chairs, outdoor) is a third concurrent ask; drop it and let the two main offers (lighting + warehouse) breathe.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** (line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`)
Disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Violates WCAG 1.4.4. Remove the `maximum-scale` constraint.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` selector**
The universal rule `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;...}` suppresses iOS text auto-sizing on all elements, including fine-print. Replace with `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` scoped only to body.

**Narrow mobile breakpoint** (`@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)`)
Targets ≤418px logical width. Devices wider than 418px but still mobile (e.g., Android phones at 412–430px logical width) will render the desktop layout. Standard safe breakpoint is `max-width: 480px` or `max-width: 600px`.

**Triple-declared `@font-face` for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
The same font-family name is declared three times — once with eot/woff2/woff sources, then twice more with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The first unweighted declaration will shadow or conflict with the weighted variants in some clients. Consolidate into a single `@font-face` block per weight.

**`"Lineto Brown Light Sub"` declared with `.woff` only** (no woff2)
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com/Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff");
```
All other faces include woff2. Modern clients (Gmail web, Apple Mail) will load woff when woff2 would compress ~30% smaller.
