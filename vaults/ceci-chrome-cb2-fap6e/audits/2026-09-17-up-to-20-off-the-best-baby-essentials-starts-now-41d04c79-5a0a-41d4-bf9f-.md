# Up to 20% off the BEST baby essentials starts now →
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a straight brand-wide baby-gear discount roundup — "up to 20% off the best baby essentials" — that stacks brand after brand (Stokke, BabyBjörn, Nuna, Mushie, WonderFold, BEABA, Maxi-Cosi, Cybex) into one long scroll with no single hero product. It's essentially a category-wide sale email dressed as a curated one, and it reads more like a catalog index than a campaign.
- The top module is clean and the offer is legible immediately — "up to 20% off" with a real baby-lifestyle photo — but after that first fold it turns into an undifferentiated wall of brand-logo-plus-percentage blocks. Compare this to the 2026-09-17 "Furniture sized right for condo & apartment life" send, which scored a 7 by committing to one thesis; this one has no thesis beyond "everything's on sale."
- It'll perform fine as a bottom-funnel nudge for someone already shopping strollers or car seats, but it won't drive discovery clicks — there's too much to scan and no clear "start here."

## What's working

- Hero module leads with a real lifestyle photo (baby in a bouncer) and the offer is stated plainly and above the fold.
- Discount percentages are attached to specific brand/product pairs (e.g., "20% off Degaw Down," "15% off Maxi-Cosi") rather than a vague blanket claim.
- Promo codes are visible next to several offers, which reduces friction for someone ready to buy.
- The car seat/stroller travel-system pairing near the top gives at least one moment of real curation instead of just a brand list.

## What's weak

- No single hero product or "best deal" callout — with 8+ brands competing for attention, the eye has nowhere obvious to land after the top banner.
- The email is extremely long; by the time a reader hits Maxi-Cosi or Cybex they've scrolled past six other brand blocks, which kills momentum.
- Some sub-images (the small four-up product grid under "more ways to save") are dense and low-contrast, making it hard to tell what's actually being sold at a glance.
- The "we love these for you" personalized module and the credit-card pitch are jammed into the bottom third alongside footer content, diluting whatever personalization signal they're meant to carry.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero deal (the travel-system pairing is the strongest candidate) and lead the whole email with it — cut the brand-by-brand catalog down to a "shop by category" grid below the fold instead of ten sequential brand modules.
- 2. Consolidate the discount percentages into a single scannable table or bullet strip near the top ("Stokke 20% · Mushie 20% · Maxi-Cosi 15% · Cybex up to 20%") so people can jump straight to their brand without scrolling the whole thing.
- 3. Move the "we love these for you" personalized recommendations up near the top — that's the one module actually tailored to the recipient, and it's currently buried near the footer.
- 4. Trim the credit-card cross-sell and app-download pitch out of this email entirely — they compete with the core baby-essentials offer for attention.
