# NEW JUST IN
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- CB2 comes in with "NEW JUST IN" after a week-plus drumbeat of sale emails — and promptly buries the lede. The body has real editorial muscle: four named product spotlights (Art Deco Sofa, Bronze Velvet Moderno, French Gold Pendant, Terracotta Vase) leading into a "NEW FOR YOU" personalized grid, all anchored by a "JULY 4TH SALE / OF THE MOMENT / 50% OFF" hero. That's a legitimately strong email package. But the subject sabotages it. Anyone scanning their inbox sees "NEW JUST IN" and files it as a new-arrivals browse; the 50% off hook — the thing that actually drives clicks — is invisible until you open.
- This is the inverse of the June 27 "Re: your summer savings" trick: that one used a misleading subject to inflate opens on a sale email. This one uses a misleading subject that undersells a sale email. Both are bad, but in different directions. The June 27 "best investment pieces are custom" send at least had a subject aligned to its editorial positioning. Here the subject and body are talking past each other entirely.
- The body earns a solid 7 on its own merits. The subject drags the composite score down by squandering the open.

## What's working

- **Editorial product naming** — "THE ART DECO SOFA," "THE FRENCH GOLD PENDANT," "THE TERRACOTTA VASE" give each item authority; this is CB2 at its best, making product spotlights feel like catalog features rather than grid dumps.
- **Hero hierarchy** — "JULY 4TH SALE / 50% OFF" lands immediately in the hero without ambiguity; the sale context is clear within the first scroll stop.
- **"NEW FOR YOU" personalized grid** — the bottom section signals curation, which is a meaningful step up from the generic warehouse-grid sends from June 28.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no overlapping copy, images load correctly throughout.
- **Seasonal relevance** — July 4th framing is timely and credible for a June 30 send.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body mismatch is the core failure** — "NEW JUST IN" promises new arrivals, body delivers a sale event; these are different inbox-level propositions and the subject buries the 50% hook entirely.
- **No prominent hero CTA button** — the product sections rely on small text links rather than a single high-contrast action button anchoring the hero block.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is at minimum the eighth CB2 promotional email in recent memory; "NEW JUST IN" doesn't differentiate or reset the relationship the way a genuine new-chapter subject would need to.
- **Sale urgency is implicit, not explicit** — nothing in the subject or visible preheader tells the subscriber this offer expires; "JULY 4TH SALE" implies a deadline but never names it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to surface the sale + the editorial hook together** — "NEW JUST IN" throws away the 50% off anchor. A subject that marries both angles would double open rate signal.
- 2. **Add a deadline line to the hero** — "Ends July 4" in small caps under the 50% OFF hero converts editorial browsing into urgency-driven clicks.
- 3. **Add one bold CTA button to the hero block** — a high-contrast "SHOP THE SALE" or "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS ON SALE" button immediately below the 50% OFF claim eliminates friction for mobile tappers who don't scroll the editorial.
- 4. **Consider sequencing the editorial section before the sale header** — lead with the four named product spotlights as the "new" hook, then reveal the sale context. That structure would match the subject's promise and still land the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week — 50% off while it lasts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Art Deco Sofa, Gold Pendant + more — on sale now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pieces we're obsessed with right now. July 4th Sale ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop new arrivals during the July 4th Sale — up to 50% off.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom** (WCAG 1.4.4 failure):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Users with low vision cannot zoom. Change to `user-scalable=yes` or remove `maximum-scale`.

**Universal `text-size-adjust:none` is too broad:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```
Applying this to the `*` selector suppresses OS-level font scaling across all elements. Scope it to `body` only.

**Template comment artifact left in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and unfilled placeholder shipped to subscribers. Should be removed in the build process.

**Mobile breakpoint mismatch:** Media query fires at `max-width: 418px` and sets `.mobile { width: 414px }`. iPhone 15/16 Pro Max logical width is 430px — those devices will not trigger the mobile layout. Recommend `max-width: 430px` minimum.

**Malformed double-slash font URLs** (cosmetic, but technically invalid):
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Three `@font-face` declarations contain `//` path segments. HTTP servers normalize these, but CDN caching rules or strict URL matchers may not.

**Triple `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — the same family is declared three times with overlapping `src:` lists and conflicting weight assignments. The first declaration has no `font-weight` scoping, causing the browser to use unpredictable cascade order.

**`<title>` does not match subject line:**
- `<title>`: `CB2, FURNITURE AND DECOR FOR THE MODERN HOME`
- Subject: `NEW JUST IN`

Tab/window title shown in some webmail clients will not reflect the campaign.
