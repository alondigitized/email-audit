# Write a review. Enter for a chance to win a $500 Gift Card. Find out how →
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a review-solicitation email dressed as a giveaway, and it's a strange choice to lead a Crate & Kids send with — the hero asks you to write a product review for a chance at a $500 gift card, which is a low-value, high-effort ask compared to every other Crate & Kids send in the recent history (Sharland England collab launch, Nuna/Doona baby gear, room-by-room catalogs). After the review CTA, the email pivots hard into an unrelated grab-bag: a Venus Williams tennis collab tease, a 20%-off baby-essentials sale, a "we love these for you" product rail, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a design-consultation offer. Nothing on this send ladders back to a single theme the way the 09-19 dining-room or 09-20 Thanksgiving sends did.
- The two-star graphic under the "want to win a $500 gift card?" headline is a genuinely odd visual choice — it reads like a low product rating, which undercuts trust before the recipient even parses the offer. For a persona like mine (parent, engaged with baby/kids gear), the review ask has zero personal relevance unless I've actually purchased the featured Avery Indigo Stripe Upholstered Bed, and the email gives no indication this is targeted to past purchasers.
- Overall this lands as a low-priority, kitchen-sink send with a confusing hero. Score it below the brand's recent average.

## What's working

- Clear, high-contrast primary CTA buttons ("Write a Review," "Shop Bestsellers," "Shop the New Collab") are easy to spot.
- The Venus Williams x Crate&Kids collab module is visually distinct and on-brand for a genuine product launch.
- The 20% off baby essentials banner has a clean, legible layout with a visible CTA and clear deadline framing ("limited time only").
- Product rail ("we love these for you") shows real prices, including a markdown callout on the Nuna stroller, which adds some concrete value.
- No broken images or overlapping text — the render is technically clean throughout.

## What's weak

- The two-star rating graphic at the top is confusing and reads as a negative product review, not a promotional device — it actively undercuts the giveaway pitch.
- The email opens with a low-incentive ask (write a review) instead of a hero product or offer, wasting the most valuable real estate in the email.
- No clear throughline: review contest → tennis collab → baby sale → credit card → app download → design service, with no unifying narrative for the reader.
- The hero product (Avery Indigo Stripe bed) is never explained — is it the item to review, a random room shot, or a subtle upsell? It's ambiguous.
- Heavy stacking of secondary asks (credit card, app, design desk) buries the actual giveaway mechanic and offer end-date deep in the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with an actual offer or product, not the review ask — move "write a review, win $500" to a secondary module further down, and open with the Venus Williams collab or the 20%-off baby sale, both of which are stronger, on-brand hooks.
- 2. Replace the two-star graphic with a five-star full/gift-card icon, or drop the star graphic entirely — it currently reads as a bad review, which is the opposite of the intended message.
- 3. Cut the module count. This send has six distinct asks; trim to three (collab, sale, review contest) so the eye has a clear hierarchy.
- 4. Add the contest deadline ("Contest ends September 30") near the top CTA, not just in body copy — right now urgency is buried.
- 5. Tie the hero image to an explicit action — if it's meant to be "the bed people are reviewing," say so; if it's just an inspiration shot, replace it with the collab or sale hero instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tell us what you think — win a $500 Crate & Kids card`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Venus Williams collab + your shot at $500`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Quick review, big reward: enter before Sept 30`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 20% off baby essentials, ends soon`
