# Your chance to win a $1,000 Gift Card →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' review-giveaway email, and it's the same bait-and-switch shape flagged in "The best collab since 🍪 & 🥛" (2026-08-03, 8.00/10): the subject and hero deliver exactly what was promised — enter to win a $1,000 gift card by writing a review — but by the second screen it pivots into an unrelated back-to-school sale, a Nuna car seat push, a 50% off nursery sale, a six-item product grid, a credit card upsell, an app download pitch, and a design-consultation CTA. None of that connects back to the sweepstakes hook that got the open.
- The execution itself is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent Crate & Kids styling throughout — but the structure asks a reader to make one click among nine competing CTAs, and two details actively undercut the pitch: the hero's star-rating graphic shows only 2 of 5 stars filled next to "want to win a $1,000 gift card?", and the app section later dangles "your chance at $500" with zero explanation of how that relates to the $1,000 headline offer.
- Net: solid production values wrapped around a diffuse, slightly self-contradictory structure. This lands as a middling send that would benefit far more from focus than from more offers.

## What's working

- Hero delivers on the subject immediately — "want to win a $1,000 gift card?" sits above the fold with no scrolling required.
- Concrete numbers throughout: 20% off back-to-school, 50% off nursery sale, fall arrivals "starting at $29," a marked-down backpack at $43.20.
- No render bugs — every image loads cleanly, text doesn't overlap, and the sage/terracotta palette stays consistent from header to footer.
- Individual CTA button copy is specific per section ("Shop the Sale," "Shop Bestsellers," "Book a Free Appointment") rather than generic "Discover" language.

## What's weak

- Nine separate CTAs across eight unrelated offers (review contest, rugs, Nuna car seat, nursery sale, product grid, credit card, app, design service) — no single primary action stands out.
- The hero's rating graphic shows 2 of 5 stars filled — an odd, faintly negative visual to put directly under a "write a review" ask.
- The subject/hero promise $1,000, but the app section later says "your chance at $500" with no framing that separates it as a distinct promotion — reads as a downgrade or inconsistency.
- "Contest ends August 31!!" — double exclamation point is a small spam-adjacent tic in otherwise clean copy.
- Fine print reveals this is a review-to-enter sweepstakes with odds depending on entry volume, a much softer mechanic than the headline implies.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the merchandising sections (rugs, Nuna, nursery sale, product grid, credit card, app, design desk) into a separate send — the current email buries the one thing the subject promised under seven unrelated pitches.
- 2. Replace the 2-star hero graphic with a full 5-star fill or a gift/trophy icon — a half-empty rating undercuts a "want to win" headline.
- 3. Reconcile the $1,000 vs. $500 figures — label the app amount as a distinct "app-exclusive bonus entry" or cut it entirely.
- 4. Pick one hero CTA ("Write a Review →") and demote "Shop Bestsellers" to a smaller secondary text link.
- 5. Drop the second "!!" after "August 31" to keep the copy clean.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Write a review, win $1,000 →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We want your feedback — $1,000 is on the line`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Two minutes, one review, a shot at $1,000.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your honest take could win you $1,000 in Crate & Kids credit.`
