# Quality furniture that lasts and lasts and lasts →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**

- The product grid sections are well-executed — specific furniture pieces, clean imagery, warm earthy tones consistent with the "modern heritage" direction they launched on 7/18. - No render bugs visible — clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- No promotional offer anywhere in the email — a quality-messaging send with zero price incentive is asking a lot of a mid-July subscriber with no purchase intent already.
- The subject's "lasts and lasts and lasts" repetition is distinctive but functionally wastes ~18 characters that could carry a product category, price signal, or even a lifestyle hook.
- Second Crate & Kids email on the same calendar day in the same editorial register — cadence fatigue is real, and the "5-star furniture" send this morning already took this exact positioning slot.
- No social proof in the body, which is a step backward from yesterday's celebrity must-haves send (7/10) — the combination of quality claim + zero reviews is a trust gap.
- No seasonal or time-bound angle to justify the send on this specific day.

- 2. **Collapse to one send today, not two** — the "5-star" email already did the quality-messaging work this morning. Either consolidate or make this one more promotional in exchange for giving back the morning slot.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to lead with a product or category**, not an attribute — "The crib that grows with them" or "Furniture built for the second kid too" is more concrete and curiosity-driving.
- 4. **Add one social proof element** — a star rating module or a "thousands of families chose this" line at the top of the first product grid bridges the claim-to-conversion gap.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules match; the 600px rule wins (later in cascade) overriding the 640px rule. At 640–767px only the 640px rule applies. The 768px breakpoint is likely a copy-paste error — images will render 40px narrower than intended on desktop.

**[LOW] Global `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied via wildcard selector. Disabling text size adjustment globally can cause unreadable small text on mobile when OS accessibility font scaling is active.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` and `charset` meta tags**
`format-detection` appears 4 times (once combined, then three individual); `charset` declared twice (`http-equiv` + `meta charset`). Redundant, not harmful, but indicates template hygiene issues.
