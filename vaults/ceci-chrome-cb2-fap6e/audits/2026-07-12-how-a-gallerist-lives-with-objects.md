# How a gallerist lives with objects
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's second editorial pivot in three days — following the July 10 Todd Snyder collab launch, the brand is now running a "Collectors" profile feature on how a gallerist arranges and lives with objects. The creative execution is strong: the hero image is moody and beautifully lit, the sub-brand "THE COLLECTORS" designation signals intent, and the subject line is the most genuinely curious CB2 has written in this cycle. This is the right direction for a brand trying to rebuild editorial credibility after nine days of countdowns and percentage-off hammering.
- The liability is structural. The email is primarily an inspiration piece — beautiful photography of curated spaces — but it's threaded with the same "$5,000 FREE REFRESH" design-service module that ran as the entire premise of the July 8 "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US" send. Placing a recycled service promo in the middle of an ostensibly pure editorial story breaks the voice and telegraphs that the "Collectors" framing is partly a content wrapper for an evergreen upsell. Subscribers who opened the July 8 email will notice.
- The product grid at the bottom is small, low-contrast, and feels like an afterthought appended to what should have been a pure story experience. The email earns its open — but then doesn't know whether it's editorial or commercial, and ends up being neither fully.

## What's working

- **"THE COLLECTORS" sub-brand badge** immediately signals this is a different register than sale emails — gives permission to read slowly.
- **Hero photography** is the strongest CB2 has shown this week: the burgundy velvet armchair against a dark, dramatically lit room is arresting and on-brand.
- **Subject line is genuinely intriguing** — "How a gallerist lives with objects" is specific enough to be curious without being clickbait; 35 characters keeps it mobile-safe.
- **Editorial cadence break is well-timed** — the brand needed something that wasn't a sale percentage, and the gallerist profile delivers that tonal shift.

## What's weak

- **Recycled "$5,000 FREE REFRESH" module mid-email** — this is verbatim from the July 8 "EXPERT DESIGN, ON US" send; it interrupts the gallerist narrative and signals the "editorial" frame is partly pretextual.
- **No object-level storytelling visible** — a gallerist feature should show specific pieces and why they matter; the email appears to deliver wide lifestyle shots rather than tight object-focused frames.
- **Product grid is too small and undifferentiated** — the bottom shelf of thumbnails doesn't connect to "how a gallerist selects objects" — it's a generic shop module.
- **High weekly cadence undercuts the premium feel** — this is CB2's fifth or sixth send in four days; the editorial ambition fights the inbox noise the brand itself created.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove or relocate the $5,000 refresh module** — it killed the editorial mood in the July 8 email too. If it must appear, put it in the footer or as a footer tile, not a full-bleed mid-email interruption.
- 2. **Give the gallerist a name and a specific object she's curated** — "Marta collects mid-century ceramics. Here's the CB2 piece that holds its own next to them." That's a click. Anonymous aspirational imagery is just mood board.
- 3. **Rebuild the product grid as "Objects from the Collector's Edit"** — tie each item to the story's context (the velvet armchair, the accent lamp). Make the shop module feel like a curation list, not a catch-all.
- 4. **Tighten the cadence** — five emails in four days from a single brand drains the goodwill that an editorial like this is trying to earn back.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A. Double-slash URLs in font `src` declarations (3 occurrences)**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Double slashes may resolve on Apache/Nginx with default config, but some CDN or WAF rules will 404 them. Normalize to single slash.

**B. `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile — WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**C. Global `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;-ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element, not just `body`. Overrides user agent text scaling on iOS across all nodes. Scope to `body` only.

**D. Duplicate `@font-face` blocks for "Lineto Brown Sub"**
Three separate `@font-face` declarations share the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` name — one with no weight/style descriptor, one with `font-weight: 400`, and one with `font-weight: 600`. The bare declaration creates an ambiguous cascade. Consolidate or rename per-weight families.

**E. Alt text on images — unverifiable** from truncated source. Must confirm all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes (decorative images should use `alt=""`).
