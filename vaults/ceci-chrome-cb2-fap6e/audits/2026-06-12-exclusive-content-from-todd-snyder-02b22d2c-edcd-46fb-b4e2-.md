# Exclusive content from Todd Snyder
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's Todd Snyder collaboration editorial drop — an influencer/designer partnership email built around a "step inside the townhouse" home-tour concept. The creative is high-end and the photography is strong, but the email suffers from a fundamental identity crisis: it can't decide if it's selling an experience, a collection, or an app. "DOWNLOAD THE SHOP" as the primary CTA on a furniture collaboration email is a misfire — it sends shoppers toward an app install instead of a product page.
- The subject line "Exclusive content from Todd Snyder" is the worst kind of vague. CB2 has sent at least five emails this week (June 9–13 brand history); by the time this lands on June 12, subscribers are fatigued. Calling it "content" when what you mean is "a designer collaboration collection" is a wasted envelope. It buries the actual hook.
- The good news: the 50% Warehouse Sale banner persists at the top, the product grid for the Todd Snyder collection is clean, and the "NOT SOLD OUT. YET." urgency block — recycled from the June 10 Warehouse Sale launch — still earns its keep. But the email reads like two briefs got merged without resolution: editorial storytelling vs. hard-sell inventory clearance.

## What's working

- The hero photography ("STEP INSIDE THE TOWNHOUSE") is aspirational and on-brand — the townhouse interior is visually compelling and positions the collection well.
- The 50% Warehouse Sale banner at the top anchors an actual offer in what would otherwise be a pure editorial email.
- The Todd Snyder product grid is clean, well-styled, and self-selects for a design-forward buyer.
- "NOT SOLD OUT. YET." still works as an urgency device, even if it's a recycled asset from June 10.
- The CB2 × Todd Snyder co-branding is visually distinct and clear — no confusion about who the collab is with.

## What's weak

- "DOWNLOAD THE SHOP" is the primary CTA on a collaboration collection email — asking someone to install an app when they want to browse furniture kills intent.
- The subject "Exclusive content from Todd Snyder" is content-speak that obscures the actual offer (a designer furniture collection at Warehouse Sale prices).
- Heavy email cadence this week (this is one of two emails sent on June 12 alone, per the SOFAS FOR SUMMER send) makes fatigue a real risk; this one offers the least transactional reason to open.
- No price specificity for the Todd Snyder collection itself — is it in the Warehouse Sale? Unclear. The 50% banner is generic, not tied to this collab.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" algorithmic product grid at the bottom dilutes the collaboration focus and makes the email feel stitched together.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "DOWNLOAD THE SHOP" with a direct collection CTA.** Change to "SHOP THE COLLECTION" or "BROWSE TODD SNYDER × CB2" — something that lands on the collection page, not an app store.
- 2. **Make the collection/price connection explicit.** If Todd Snyder pieces are included in the Warehouse Sale, say so in the hero: "Todd Snyder × CB2 — now in the Warehouse Sale." If not, create a separate urgency hook (limited run, exclusive colorways).
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the collaboration and the hook**, not the word "content."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Todd Snyder designed your next living room`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Inside Todd Snyder's CB2 townhouse`
- 4. **Write a preheader that drives opens**, not app installs.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The collab is live — and it's in the Warehouse Sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Step inside the designer's townhouse. Shop the looks.`
- 5. **Cut the "MORE FOR YOU" grid** or move it to a secondary send. It competes with the editorial story this email is trying to tell.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Global text-size-adjust disabled** — the universal selector rule below silences OS-level font scaling across all elements, compounding the zoom block above:
  ```css
  *{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
  ```

- **Non-standard mobile breakpoint** — `max-width: 418px` targets a narrow window (iPhone SE-sized). Standard practice is 480px or 600px. Layouts may break on Android devices and older iPhones between 419–479px.

- **Cross-domain font load** — `HNW05` typeface loads from `www.crateandbarrel.com`, a sibling brand's domain, not `cb2.com`. If that domain has downtime or changes its CORS policy, font rendering silently degrades in supported clients.
  ```css
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-....woff2")
  ```

- **Double-slash font URLs** — three `@font-face` declarations under `cb2.com` contain a double-slash path segment, which causes an unnecessary redirect before the font is served:
  ```css
  src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
  ```

- **Alt text on images** — cannot confirm; source is truncated before `<img>` tags appear.
