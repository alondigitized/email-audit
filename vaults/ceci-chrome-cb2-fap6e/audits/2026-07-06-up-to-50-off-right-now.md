# UP TO 50% OFF RIGHT NOW
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's final-day urgency push — the countdown timer reads 1 day and 17 hours, which tracks against the July 5 "3 days left" send. After a week of targeted category emails (furniture, outdoor, lighting, decor, floor samples, warehouse), CB2 has reverted to the broadest possible frame: "everything, 50% off, right now." That's a defensible playbook for last-chance coverage, but it's also exactly what yesterday's "3 SALES ON NOW" already executed — and that send scored higher because it was the first time the consolidation happened.
- The structural bones are solid. Countdown timer leads. Warehouse Sale at 60% off adds a value tier above the hero 50%. The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid gives actual reasons to click. But for a subscriber who has received six-plus near-identical CB2 emails since June 28, nothing in this render explains why TODAY is meaningfully different from yesterday — except the ticking clock, which the subject line communicates with all the creativity of a form field.
- The "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS?" tagline under the hero is the only moment of personality. It was clever — the day before July 4th. On July 6 it reads like a party favor someone forgot to sweep up.

## What's working

- Countdown timer at the top immediately grounds the urgency — ~1 day 17 hours is a specific, credible deadline that the subject's "RIGHT NOW" actually matches.
- Warehouse Sale at "UP TO 60% OFF" adds a higher-discount layer; most prior sends in this campaign kept the headline number at 50%.
- "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS?" is the only wit in an otherwise template-driven email — it earns a beat of engagement even if it lands a day late.
- Six category CTAs ("SHOP FURNITURE," "SHOP OUTDOOR," "SHOP LIGHTING," etc.) are verb + noun, unambiguous, and cover the full breadth of the sale without making the subscriber guess where to go.
- "MORE FOR YOU" product grid with specific items and visible prices gives a concrete click path for anyone browsing rather than searching.

## What's weak

- The all-caps subject "UP TO 50% OFF RIGHT NOW" is spam-signal territory and nearly indistinguishable from multiple prior CB2 subjects in this campaign — a subscriber's inbox preview looks like CB2 broke the same message into twelve copies.
- The hero citrus/orange imagery is a visual holdover from the campaign's launch aesthetic — it reads stale next to the product-led photography CB2 used in the July 5 lighting send.
- No single focal point: six category CTAs, a Warehouse CTA, six product cards, an app promo, a credit card offer, and a 10%-off coupon block all live in the same scroll — campaign fatigue plus decision paralysis is a bad combination on a final-day send.
- "BETTER THAN FIREWORKS?" is two days late. July 4th was yesterday; the holiday peg doesn't land on July 6.
- Zero new story: this email adds nothing to what "3 SALES ON NOW" (July 5, 9/10) already said. A subscriber who opened yesterday and didn't buy needs a different angle, not the same one louder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the deadline instead of the offer percentage.** "Ends tomorrow" or "Last day" as a hero headline — the countdown timer already shows the offer amount; the headline should carry the urgency.
- 2. **Pick one undersold category for the hero.** Tabletop and rugs each got a category button in prior sends but have never led a hero section. A final-day narrowing ("Last day: rugs up to 50% off") gives repeat openers a new reason to click.
- 3. **Retire the citrus imagery now.** July 4th visual is stale; swap to product photography that closes the sale with tangibility, not holiday decoration.
- 4. **Strip the bottom promotional blocks for this send.** The app, credit card, and coupon blocks undercut the one mission of a final-day email: shop the sale before it ends. Move them back to a non-sale send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tomorrow: CB2's July 4th Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day. Up to 60% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Warehouse deals at 60% off — the deepest discounts of the sale end tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Furniture, rugs, lighting, decor — final hours to shop.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Leaked template placeholder in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Typo ("IMPUT") and production scaffolding text present in deployed HTML. Should have been removed pre-send.

**[HIGH] Double-slash in multiple `@font-face` src URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Three of six `@font-face` declarations use `//` after the domain. CDN or origin servers may return 404 on strict path matching; causes silent fallback to Helvetica in supporting clients (Apple Mail, Outlook on iOS).

**[MEDIUM] Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub":**
The family is declared three separate times — once with a bare `.woff` (no `format()` hint), then twice with `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600`. The first declaration's lack of a format hint causes browsers/mail clients to attempt to load it even when woff2 is available, adding a redundant network request.

**[MEDIUM] Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
No current production iPhone maps to 418px. iPhone 15 Pro = 393px, iPhone 15 Plus = 430px. This breakpoint will not trigger on 430px-wide devices, causing those users to receive the desktop layout on a phone.

**[MEDIUM] `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Blocks pinch-zoom for low-vision users. No functional email reason to prevent zoom.

**[LOW] Global `text-size-adjust: none`:**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied to all elements, not just the body. Prevents iOS Safari from auto-scaling small text in narrow containers. Compounds the `maximum-scale=1` issue above.

**[LOW] Cross-domain font loading (cb2.com + crateandbarrel.com):**
`HNW05` font family loads from `crateandbarrel.com` webfonts CDN while all other fonts load from `cb2.com`. Email clients that enforce same-origin or CORS restrictions will silently fall back on the Crate & Barrel font variant.
