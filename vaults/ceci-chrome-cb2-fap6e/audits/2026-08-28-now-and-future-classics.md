# Now and future classics
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-28**
## Executive summary

- "Now and future classics" is CB2 leaning fully into design-editorial mode — no discount language until you scroll past six full product features. It's a strong, cohesive visual story: "MOST SOUGHT AFTER" hero, then Deseo Sectional, Gwyneth Chair, Macron Console, Fitz Chair, and Davison Bed each get a full-bleed styled room shot with designer credit ("Designed by grado," "Studio ANANSI," "VUUE"). This is the most premium, catalog-like CB2 send in the recent run — a clear departure from the Labor Day banner-stacking of "SUITE UPGRADES ON SALE" (2026-08-26) and "The perfect sofa does exist" (2026-08-26).
- The trade-off: this is a browse email, not a sale email, and the subject undersells that. "Now and future classics" is vague and doesn't signal there's a Labor Day 50% off sale buried two-thirds down. Someone opening for a deal has to scroll past ~8 hero images to find it. It's beautifully produced but asks a lot of patience before it pays off with an offer.

## What's working

- Consistent full-bleed hero photography across all six product features — no render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- Designer attribution ("Designed by grado / Studio ANANSI / VUUE") adds a premium, editorial credibility signal not seen in prior CB2 sends.
- Clear category grid ("SOFAS & SECTIONALS," "BEDROOM," "DINING ROOM," "ACCENT CHAIRS") gives a low-friction browse path after the hero story.
- "SHOP BEST SELLERS" CTA appears twice (top and mid-email), reinforcing the primary action without being pushy.
- Loyalty/app content (CB2 App download, "Earn 15% Back" card) and social links present at the footer, matching brand-history pattern.

## What's weak

- No discount or offer is visible until "THE LABOR DAY SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" appears far down the scroll — the subject and hero promise curation, not savings, so value-focused shoppers may bounce early.
- Subject "Now and future classics" is abstract and doesn't hint at sale, new arrivals, or any concrete hook — weak differentiator in a crowded CB2 cadence that's sent 6 emails in 4 days.
- Individual product CTAs are missing — each hero (Deseo Sectional, Gwyneth Chair, etc.) has a name/caption but no visible "Shop This" button, so the click path relies on users tapping the image itself.
- "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom has readable prices but is visually generic compared to the styled heroes above it — a big drop-off in production value.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a compressed sale strip ("Labor Day Sale, up to 50% off") directly under the hero, above the first product feature — right now shoppers scroll past six full images before any offer appears, which is a lot of friction for the cadence CB2 has established this week.
- 2. Add a "Shop This Look" button under each named product feature (Deseo Sectional, Gwyneth Chair, etc.) — currently there's no explicit CTA per product, just an image tap.
- 3. Sharpen the subject to signal what's inside — either the curation angle or the sale, not both vaguely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New icons, from the archives to now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the pieces everyone's asking about`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six new classics, plus up to 50% off Labor Day sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Designer-made favorites you'll want in every room`
- 4. Give the "MORE FOR YOU" grid the same visual polish (lifestyle shots, not product-on-white) as the hero section, or cut it — it undercuts the premium tone built earlier.
