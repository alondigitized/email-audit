# Furniture on sale right now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's furniture-specific sale send — "Furniture on sale right now" — arriving five days deep into the July 4th sale drumbeat. The email tries to do too much: a 50% off furniture hero at the top, a secondary outdoor/rugs/lighting category grid in the middle, a warehouse sale block at 60% off below that, and then a "More For You" product carousel below that. Four distinct content zones competing for one click. The message diffuses before it lands.
- The hero is the strongest thing here — large, clean "50% OFF FURNITURE!" with a readable CTA. That's the email. Everything below it is noise that dilutes the primary offer. CB2 ran a much tighter version of this same offer on June 25 ("JULY 4TH SALE STARTS NOW") with better focus. This send is the bloated second iteration — same sale, more clutter.
- Judgment: a decent furniture-specific anchor wasted by over-stacking. The subject line is accurate and specific for once, which is a genuine improvement over the "Re: your summer savings" dark-pattern from June 27, but the body execution doesn't match the subject's focus promise.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is unambiguous** — "50% OFF FURNITURE!" in large type with a clear "SHOP NOW" CTA is readable at a glance.
- **Subject line is honest** — "Furniture on sale right now" accurately describes the email's top offer, no tricks.
- **Category grid is visually clean** — the outdoor/rugs/lighting section uses distinct thumbnail images and tidy "SHOP [CATEGORY]" labels.
- **Product carousel at bottom** — individual SKUs with named products and prices visible add specificity that most prior CB2 sends lacked.
- **No broken images** — all image blocks render cleanly; no gaps or broken-img placeholders visible.

## What's weak

- **Dual-offer conflict undermines the hero** — the "60% OFF WAREHOUSE SALE" block midway through directly competes with the 50% off furniture hero; a shopper scanning fast will be confused which deal to take.
- **"More For You" carousel is generic** — the products shown (a storage cabinet, a bar cart, a chair) have no apparent personalization or seasonal relevance; it reads like a default grid fill.
- **No deadline visible** — neither the 50% furniture offer nor the warehouse sale block shows an end date in the visible render; cadence fatigue is real and urgency is the one thing that could overcome it.
- **Category grid dilutes focus** — outdoor, rugs, and lighting are not furniture; including them after the "furniture on sale" hero breaks the conceptual promise of the subject line.
- **Ninth CB2 promo in roughly two weeks** — at this cadence, even a clean single-offer email suffers open fatigue; this one compounds it by being multi-section.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one offer per email.** Either "50% off furniture" or "60% off warehouse" — not both. The warehouse sale block should be its own send or a footer footnote, not a second hero mid-email.
- 2. **Add a concrete deadline to the hero.** "50% OFF FURNITURE — Ends July 4th" gives the subscriber a reason to act today instead of saving it for later.
- 3. **Cut the category grid or subordinate it.** If the subject says furniture, the body should be furniture. Outdoor and rugs belong in a separate send or a small "also on sale" footnote.
- 4. **Replace the "More For You" carousel with a curated top-3 furniture picks.** Specific hero SKUs with pricing visible above the fold drive more clicks than a generic nine-item grid.
- 5. **Reduce cadence.** This is the ninth consecutive promotional send in roughly 14 days. Rest days or editorial content (the June 27 "custom as investment" attempt was the right instinct) would restore open rates.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` override**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Applied to every element via the `*` selector. This suppresses iOS auto-scaling globally and can make small text unreadable. Should be scoped to `body` only, and set to `100%` not `none`.

**Inconsistent `font-display` across `@font-face` declarations**
Four `@font-face` blocks for `"Lineto Brown Sub"` are declared; only two include `font-display: swap`. The first two (`.eot`-based and `.woff`) omit it, causing potential FOIT (flash of invisible text) in unsupported clients.

**EOT format still present**
```css
src: url("...lineto-brown-regular.eot");
src: url("...lineto-brown-regular.eot?#iefix") format("embedded-opentype"), ...
```
EOT is an IE6–IE8 format with ~0% modern market share. Dead weight that adds parse cost without benefit.

**Double-slash in font URLs**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
URLs contain `//` after the domain. Most servers normalize this, but it is a defect and could break on strict CDN configurations.

**Mobile breakpoint is atypical**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
418px is an unusual breakpoint. Common safe values are `480px` or `414px` (iPhone Plus/Max). At 418px, iPhone 14 Pro (393px logical width) hits this breakpoint but iPhone 14 Plus (430px) does not, potentially leaving the largest-screen iPhones on the desktop layout.
