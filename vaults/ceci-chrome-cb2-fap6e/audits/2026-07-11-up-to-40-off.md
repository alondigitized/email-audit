# UP TO 40% OFF
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is effectively a reprint of the July 9 "Up to 40% off is on now" Summer Edit email, delivered two days later with the same creative frame and the same offer — and it arrives as the second CB2 email of July 11, after the focused bedroom-category send earlier today. The in-email execution is competent: hero is clean, the "SHOP THE SALE" CTA is unambiguous, the "More for You" product grid adds genuine personalization below the fold. But the subject line is CB2's worst in recent memory — 15 all-caps characters with zero hook, no deadline, no product, no personality. A subscriber who opened Thursday's send gets no new reason to act.
- The mid-email "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" sweepstakes banner is the most avoidable mistake here. A four-figure prize is a real hook — burying it mid-sale-email, unsupported, is a waste of a strong asset. The bedroom email from earlier today at least justified its creative with a room focus. This one is just the campaign-level template running again on a two-day timer with nothing new to say.
- Bottom line: the email itself works, the timing and subject kill it.

## What's working

- Hero visual hierarchy is immediate — "THE SUMMER EDIT / UP TO 40% OFF / SHOP THE SALE" is above the fold and unambiguous.
- "More for You" personalized product grid surfaces real prices and "exclusive" badges, giving product-level click reasons the hero never provides.
- Rewards strip ("EARN 10% BACK" + Crate & Barrel cross-brand reinforcement) is present and visible for members.
- No render bugs — clean layout, images loaded, no text overlap.
- "FURNITURE. DECOR. MORE." secondary section with its own CTA gives a second click surface without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- "UP TO 40% OFF" in ALL CAPS is one of the most generic possible sale subjects — no product, no deadline, triggers spam-signal associations.
- Identical offer and near-identical creative to July 9 — a subscriber who opened Thursday gets zero new information.
- Second CB2 email on July 11 (after the bedroom send) compounds already heavy cadence — July 7 through 11 is five sends in five days.
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1000" sweepstakes banner interrupts the sale flow mid-email and is too undersupported to register properly.
- No urgency mechanism visible anywhere — no deadline, no countdown, no "offer ends" language — making "up to 40% off" indistinguishable from the permanent site-wide price impression.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hard-code a deadline into the subject and hero.** "Summer Edit: 40% off ends Sunday" is honest, specific, and answerable — the current subject says nothing a subscriber didn't already hear on July 9.
- 2. **Narrow the creative to a fresh lens.** The July 9 email already did the full-category Summer Edit sweep. A second send should zoom into one room or product family (outdoor, lighting, storage) rather than replinting the whole campaign.
- 3. **Surface the "$1000 review sweepstakes" above the fold, or cut it to its own send.** A four-figure incentive deserves hero placement or its own email, not a mid-scroll banner.
- 4. **Fix the subject case.** All-caps reads as shouted spam in 2026 inboxes. Mixed-case performs better and doesn't trigger spam pattern matching.
- 5. **Promote one "More for You" product to the hero.** The Rove Acrylic Folding Table at $99 with an "exclusive" badge is a genuine hook — that price signal belongs above the fold, not buried in the grid.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS Safari — an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and flagged by Apple's Mail Privacy Protection crawler:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Mobile breakpoint is 418 px** (`@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)`). Most email clients trigger mobile layouts at 600 px or device width. A 418 px breakpoint means devices between 419–599 px (many Android phones) receive the desktop layout. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px`.

**Cross-domain font dependency on a sister brand**: `HNW05` is loaded from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. If that domain's CDN has an outage or the fonts are moved, CB2 emails silently fall back without notice. Fonts should be hosted on CB2's own domain or a dedicated CDN.

**Duplicate `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Sub"**: the family name is declared three times with different `src` URLs. The browser/client resolves via cascade; the last declaration wins, which may not be the intended one. Consolidate into a single block with a `src` list.
