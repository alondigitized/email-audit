# From the archives. On sale now.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is weaving a designer-heritage narrative — "Design Legends Sale" — around Gianfranco Frattini, Paul McCobb, and Steen Østergaard, essentially repackaging the Midseason Sale into an editorial moment about acquiring authentic mid-century pieces. The concept is smart and brand-consistent: instead of another "X% off everything," it gives the discount intellectual scaffolding. For a CB2 subscriber who values design provenance, this framing lands better than a blanket markdown.
- That said, this is at minimum CB2's second email today (the Todd Snyder "Townhouse" launch hit the same inbox earlier on 6/4), and easily the seventh or eighth send in five days. The archival concept deserved more breathing room — sandwiched between a collab drop and an ongoing Midseason Sale drumbeat, it reads more like a campaign add-on than a standalone send. The editorial value is real; the timing dilutes it.
- The email executes cleanly: clear visual hierarchy, no render bugs, three designer profiles with portraiture that distinguishes this from a commodity markdown email. The bottom third recaps the Midseason Sale offer and product grid. Competent, cohesive, overfrequent.

- **"Design Legends Sale" hero framing** elevates this above a standard markdown; attaching famous names (Frattini, McCobb, Østergaard) gives the discount a legitimate editorial reason to exist.
- **Black-and-white portrait photography** for each designer reads instantly as archival, reinforcing the subject line's "from the archives" promise.
- **Clear visual hierarchy** — hero headline → designer triptych → Midseason Sale offer → product grid — guides the eye without confusion.
- **Subject line is clean and short** (32 chars, two punchy fragments) and avoids every spam-flag pattern.
- **No render bugs** visible; layout is tight and images load properly.

## What's weak

- **Cadence collision**: two CB2 emails on the same calendar day (this + Todd Snyder "Townhouse") with a week of near-daily sends before it — inbox fatigue is real and this send pays for yesterday's excess.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic** — three distinct designers each warrant a more specific CTA ("Shop Frattini," "Explore McCobb") that would lift click attribution and feel less like a batch send.
- **No urgency signal**: "On sale now" implies availability without implying scarcity or deadline — the Midseason Sale offer in the lower section doesn't surface a clear end date in the visible render.
- **Designer copy is too thin** — the profile blurbs (from what's visible) don't include a single concrete product name, price, or "why this piece" hook that would make a buyer act.

- 1. **Hold this for a standalone send day** — the archival/designer positioning is the strongest CB2 creative concept this week, and it's being buried by the Todd Snyder collab competing in the same inbox on the same day. This concept earns its own day.
- 2. **Add a deadline to every sale section** — "Sale ends Sunday" or "Last 72 hours" in the Midseason Sale band would double the urgency without requiring a redesign.
- 3. **Make the three designer CTAs specific** — replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop Frattini," "Shop McCobb," "Shop Østergaard" so clicks tell you which designer resonates.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — text scaling disabled globally:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
This suppresses OS-level text resizing across all elements. Combined with `maximum-scale=1` on the viewport meta, users with visual impairments cannot enlarge text. Both rules should be scoped narrowly or removed.

**Zoom blocked at viewport level:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 violation.

**Global paragraph center-alignment:**
```css
p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }
```
Forces center alignment on every `<p>` element. Any left-aligned body copy requires an explicit override on every paragraph — fragile and easily missed in production authoring.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — Can clip descenders on certain font/size combinations in Outlook and older Android clients. Safer to scope to specific elements.

**Single-pixel mobile breakpoint at 418px** — No breakpoint covers the 320–417px range or the 430px+ range (iPhone 14/15 Max native width is 430px). Devices at those sizes receive neither the mobile nor desktop layout.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — Declared three separate times. The first declaration has no `format()` hints and no `font-display` property; the subsequent two add `font-display: swap` but only cover woff2. The first declaration may cause FOIT in clients that parse it before reaching the corrected declarations.

**First `@font-face` for "Lineto Brown Light Sub"** declares only a `.woff` source with no `format()` hint — no woff2 fallback defined, meaning modern clients load the heavier format.
