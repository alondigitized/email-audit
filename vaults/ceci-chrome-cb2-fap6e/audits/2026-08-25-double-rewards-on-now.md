# DOUBLE REWARDS§ ON NOW
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's credit card issuer (not the retailer's own commerce list — sender is "CB2 Credit Card") pushing "DOUBLE REWARDS IS ON: Get 10% ~~20%~~ back in Reward Dollars" on the co-branded Visa card, valid through Sept 9. It's a financial-services offer dressed in CB2's furniture photography, which is a smart trick — a strikethrough "10%→20%" gets attention fast and the hero shot (walnut dining table, velvet chairs, warm lighting) is genuinely on-brand and appealing.
- But the offer itself only applies to people who already have or are willing to open a CB2 Visa Signature card — that's a narrow slice of any list, and the email doesn't say anywhere near the top who qualifies or what "Reward Dollars" actually means in cash terms. After the strong hero it drops into a generic 6-category shop grid, then bolts on an unrelated "LABOR DAY SALE UP TO 50% OFF" banner near the bottom, which muddies whether this is a credit-card promo or a general sale email. Compared to 2026-08-25's "LABOR DAY SALE STARTS NOW" (7/10) which led with one clean sale message, this one tries to do two jobs (credit card upsell + Labor Day sale) and neither lands as the clear single CTA.

## What's working

- The strikethrough "10% → 20%" pricing device is a strong, scannable visual hook for the doubled-rewards concept.
- Hero photography (dining table, velvet chairs, sconces) is warm and on-brand, consistent with recent CB2 sends.
- Clear date-bound header "NOW THROUGH SEPT 9TH, 2026" sets urgency immediately.
- Category grid (Living Room, Lighting, Dining Room, Decor & Mirrors, Bedroom, Rugs) gives multiple entry points if someone scrolls past the card pitch.
- Footer nicely surfaces adjacent value props (10% back at CB2 + Crate & Barrel, app download, 15% off for text signup) for people not interested in the main offer.

## What's weak

- No overlapping text or broken images, but the email conflates two offers (card rewards + Labor Day sale) with no visual separation explaining they're different programs.
- The core CTA is just "LEARN MORE" — vague, doesn't say what happens next (apply for a card? log in?).
- Nothing above the fold clarifies this requires a specific credit card; a reader has to infer "double rewards" = card-only from the small Visa Signature line.
- The dagger/section-symbol footnotes (§, †, ‡) are visible in body copy but there's no immediate context for what they modify without reading dense fine print at the very bottom.
- The Labor Day banner mid-email has its own "SHOP THE SALE" button, competing directly with the card CTA and diluting hierarchy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split intent up top: either lead purely with the card offer and make "who's eligible" explicit in the hero, or move the Labor Day banner to a separate send — right now two offers compete for the one click a subscriber will make.
- 2. Replace "LEARN MORE" with a specific action verb tied to the actual next step, e.g. "SEE MY REWARDS" or "APPLY FOR THE CARD" — vague CTAs kill click-through on financial offers especially.
- 3. Add one line under the headline stating the qualifying detail in plain English ("Cardholders earn 20% back through Sept 9") instead of relying on the reader to parse the strikethrough + footnote.
- 4. Drop or visually separate the mid-funnel Labor Day 50%-off banner from the card pitch — if kept, badge it clearly as "Also happening now" rather than letting it float as a second full CTA.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your CB2 Visa now earns 20% back — through Sept 9`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Double Rewards Dollars, ends Sept 9`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cardholders: get 20% back in Reward Dollars on every CB2 purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No new card needed — just shop and earn double through Sept 9.`

## 1. Technical Summary
CB2's "Double Rewards" HTML email uses legacy DTM/dotomi ad-tracking pixels served over plain HTTP and ships with no unsubscribe or authentication headers captured, alongside widespread missing alt text on product imagery.

Most mail clients (Gmail, Outlook) block or strip non-HTTPS image/pixel loads, meaning this ad-tracking/cookie-sync chain likely fails silently for most recipients.
