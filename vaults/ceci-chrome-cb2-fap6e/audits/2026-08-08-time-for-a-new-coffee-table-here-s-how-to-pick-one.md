# Time for a new coffee table? Here's how to pick one →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel abandoning the sale pitch entirely for a "how to choose a coffee table" buying guide — shape, material, size, each section paired with a room diagram and two hero products with star ratings. It's a smart content play for anyone actually shopping for a coffee table, and the diagrams (room-scale sketches showing clearance) are a genuinely useful touch I haven't seen from this brand's other sends. But there's no discount, no urgency, and no reason to open today versus next week — this reads more like a category landing page emailed out than a promotional trigger.
- The back half is where it falls apart: after three tight "how to" sections, it dumps into an unrelated grab-bag — a new "Cotswolds Collection," a bedding basics banner, a $5,000 Design Desk sweepstakes, a "we love these for you" product grid, a credit card pitch, and an app download banner. None of that is coffee-table-adjacent, and by the time a reader scrolls that far the email has lost its thesis.
- Compared to 2026-08-07's "Buy once, use forever" bestsellers sweep (7/10) — also long and modular — this one has a sharper, more useful hook up top but the same bloat problem in the back half. Net: strong idea, undermined by scope creep and zero offer. I'd call it a 6.

## What's working

- The "find the right shape/material/size" structure with matching room-scale diagrams is a genuinely useful, differentiated content idea for furniture shopping.
- Two products per section, each with a visible star rating, gives real comparison points instead of a single hero shot.
- The subject's question framing ("Time for a new coffee table?") is honestly answered by the body — no bait-and-switch.
- Clean, consistent product photography and neutral styling throughout keeps the "expert guide" tone credible.

## What's weak

- No discount, price-off, or urgency anywhere in the coffee-table content — this is a pure content email with no reason to act today.
- The email sprawls well past its premise: Cotswolds Collection, bedding, a sweepstakes, and a credit card offer have nothing to do with coffee tables and dilute the guide's focus.
- CTA copy is repetitive and generic ("Shop Now" appears many times) — none of it is tied to the specific shape/material/size decision the section just made.
- The $5,000 sweepstakes and credit card promo are buried so deep most readers will never scroll to them, wasting real conversion real estate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the "big on style, easy on effort" cross-sell section — end on the coffee-table thesis, spin Cotswolds/bedding/sweepstakes/credit card into separate sends. A focused guide converts better than a guide that turns into a catalog.
- 2. Add one concrete incentive tied to the guide — e.g. "free swatches" or "10% off your first coffee table" — since right now there's zero reason to buy this week versus browsing later.
- 3. Make CTA copy specific per section: "Shop Round Coffee Tables," "Shop Marble Tops," "Shop Small-Space Tables" instead of repeated "Shop Now."
- 4. Move the sweepstakes higher or promote it via its own subject line — $5,000 is a strong hook that's currently wasted mid-scroll.
