# NEW markdowns! More at up to 60% off →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the second clearance-escalation email from Crate & Kids in two days — the first launched the Summer Clearance Event on May 29, and now new markdowns have been added. The email does its core job: the hero clearly communicates "up to 60% off + new markdowns" with a direct "Shop Clearance" CTA. That message will land for any parent who's been watching for deals on big-ticket kids furniture and gear.
- The problem is structural. Below the hero, the email fractures badly: an editorial "find the perfect bedding fabrics" section, then "new! gear up for summer," then a product grid, then an app promo, then a TheDesignDesk content block. For a clearance email trying to convert urgency, this is too much editorial dead weight. The bedding-fabrics section reads like it was copy-pasted from a different campaign entirely.
- The cadence is also starting to show strain. Crate & Kids has sent full clearance or promotional emails on May 28, May 29, and now May 30. Subscribers are pattern-matching — this email will feel like more of the same, and the open rate will reflect that.

## What's working

- Hero headline "up to 60% off + new markdowns" is the clearest and most punchy the brand has run in this cycle — the word "new" differentiates it from yesterday's send.
- "Shop Clearance" CTA is above the fold, visible without scrolling, and copy is unambiguous.
- Product grid section "we love these for you" shows strollers, cribs, and nursery items with prices — genuinely useful for high-intent parents.
- Summer gear section is seasonally appropriate and aligns with the clearance narrative.
- No visible render bugs; layout is clean and images load.

## What's weak

- The "find the perfect bedding fabrics" editorial block is a full creative mode-shift mid-email — it belongs in an editorial send, not a clearance escalation, and it slows the funnel.
- No visible deadline or countdown anywhere; "new markdowns" urgency evaporates without a date or end-date anchor.
- Sender name "Crate & Kids SALE" reads like a sub-list tag that leaked into the display name — brands typically don't append "SALE" to their identity.
- Three-plus promotional emails in three days means this send is fighting subscriber fatigue, and there's no new hook or segmentation signal to cut through that.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the bedding-fabrics editorial section entirely.** It kills momentum in a clearance email. Swap it for a second product grid focused on the new markdowns specifically — that's what the subject line promises.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** "New markdowns added — ends Sunday" or similar. The urgency is implied but never stated; one line anchors the decision.
- 3. **Fix the sender name.** "Crate & Kids" alone is cleaner and more trustworthy in the inbox than "Crate & Kids SALE."
- 4. **Tighten the product grid to the best 4-6 clearance items.** Show price-was/price-now formatting visibly; at this image size the savings aren't legible enough.
- 5. **Add a loyalty callout** if members get early or exclusive access to new markdowns — this brand has a rewards program and it's nowhere visible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` brand mismatch.**
```html
<title>Crate & Barrel</title>
```
The title reflects the parent brand, not "Crate & Kids." Several clients (Apple Mail, some Android) surface the `<title>` value. Should be `<title>Crate & Kids</title>`.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries — cascade order bug.**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewports ≥768px both rules match. The 640px rule appears *after* in source order, so it wins at equal specificity — the intended 600px desktop cap is never applied. Either remove the 640px rule or place it before the 768px block.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags.** `telephone=no` and `address=no` each appear twice (once inline in the combined tag, once as standalone metas). Redundant but harmless; clean up to reduce parse noise.

**Double charset declaration.** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. The HTTP-equiv form is sufficient for XHTML; the standalone form is the HTML5 pattern. Having both is not an error but is inconsistent with the XHTML doctype.

**Aggressive universal selector reset.**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; line-height: 100%; max-height:1000000px; ...}
```
`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` overrides the `body` declaration and suppresses zoom-based text scaling in VoiceOver/iOS contexts. Scope this to `body` only.

**Unusual mobile breakpoint.** `@media only screen and (max-width: 460px)` — the industry standard is 480px or 600px. At 460–479px viewport widths (e.g., older Androids at 480px with scaling), the mobile layout does not trigger.
