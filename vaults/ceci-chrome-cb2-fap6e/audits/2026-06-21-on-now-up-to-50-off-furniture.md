# ON NOW! Up to 50% off furniture!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's third distinct "warehouse sale" email in roughly 48 hours — the main brand sent "up to 60% off" on June 19, Crate & Kids followed with "up to 50% off" later that same day, and now the main brand is back on June 21 with… also "up to 50% off." That regression in headline discount is the single biggest problem here: a subscriber who saw the June 19 send will clock that the new email is offering *less* than what was already promoted, which quietly undermines trust in the sale's urgency. The email itself is a dense, well-structured scroll — hero sofa image, bestsellers grid, a "don't miss savings on bestsellers" section, a dining table feature, a buffet promo, and a "TheDeepDeal" section at the bottom — but the sheer volume of product blocks dilutes focus.
- The creative is clean and on-brand. The white sofa hero communicates aspirational lifestyle well, and the category grids give the subscriber multiple entry points. But "ON NOW!" as a subject opener reads breathless rather than urgent, and coming after a week of sale drumbeats, cadence fatigue is real. This email would land harder as a curated picks edit or a "final hours" closer — not another broad "up to X% off" blanket.
- The bottom line: competent execution of a format that has already been sent twice this week, with a headline offer that's actually *weaker* than the flagship June 19 send.

## What's working

- Hero image is aspirational and on-brand — white sofa in a calm, well-lit room sells the Crate & Barrel lifestyle immediately.
- "Up to 50% off" is prominently placed in the hero banner; the offer is not buried.
- Multiple product categories (seating, storage, dining) give different entry points for different shopping intents.
- Layout renders cleanly — no visible broken images, overlapping text, or formatting artifacts.
- "TheDeepDeal" section near the bottom adds a value-signal kicker for scrollers who make it that far.

## What's weak

- The stated discount regresses from "up to 60% off" (June 19 email) to "up to 50% off" — any subscriber who saw both will notice the step-down.
- "ON NOW!" opener reads as filler urgency — it doesn't tell me what's new or why today is the moment to act.
- The email is extremely long with multiple product grid sections; by the third block, the editorial signal (what does Crate & Barrel actually recommend?) is completely lost.
- No specific deadline visible — "ON NOW!" without an end date undercuts the urgency it's trying to create.
- Fifth or sixth sale-cadence email this week from the brand family; inbox fatigue is a real risk here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reconcile the headline discount** — the top line must be at least "up to 60% off" to match (or beat) the June 19 send. If inventory at 60% is exhausted, say so: "Last chance on 60% off — hundreds more at 50%" tells a story and reframes it positively.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends June 23" transforms "ON NOW!" from noise into a real reason to act today.
- 3. **Collapse the email to three sections max** — hero + one curated category + one deal section. Long product grids work for browse-mode shoppers; a tighter edit converts the rest.
- 4. **Lead subject line with the stronger hook** — move the offer before the time signal: "Up to 50% off furniture — sale ends Sunday" beats "ON NOW! Up to 50% off furniture."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Top picks from the warehouse sale, curated for your living room.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Viewport `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming in on text. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `text-size-adjust` declarations**
The wildcard rule overrides the body-scoped rule:
```css
/* wildcard — declared first, lower specificity but overrides in some parsers */
*{ -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
/* body — declared later but same effective specificity on <body> */
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; }
```
Result is browser-dependent. iOS Safari may suppress auto-scaling entirely, causing small text to stay small after device rotation.

**[MEDIUM] Conflicting `img max-width` media queries — wrong cascade order**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared after */
```
On screens ≥768px, both queries fire; the later-declared 640px rule wins due to cascade order. Images cap at 640px on desktop, not 600px as likely intended. Swap declaration order or use a ≥768px max-width rule that explicitly supersedes.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`telephone=no`, `address=no`, and `date=no` are each declared twice — once in the combined tag and again as individual tags. Redundant; no functional harm but indicates template hygiene issues.

**[LOW] Dark mode explicitly disabled without fallback**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Email clients that force dark mode (Outlook 2019+, some Android Gmail builds) may invert or desaturate colors unpredictably since no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` CSS block is present to control the outcome.
