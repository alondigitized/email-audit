# A well-collected home office
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is staying in editorial mode after three consecutive hard-sell Midseason Sale emails and the Todd Snyder collab drop. "A well-collected home office" leans into the "collectors" identity CB2 has been building — which threads back directly to the "Tell everyone you studied art history" send on 6/1. The hero uses their signature moody, dark-toned photography and the "THE COLLECTORS" masthead label to frame this as a lifestyle feature, not a sale circular.
- The problem is that the editorial wrapper obscures the commercial payload. There's no obvious discount percentage in the hero, no urgency signal, and the Todd Snyder/pricing section buried mid-scroll looks disconnected from the home-office premise. As a soft pivot from sale cadence this has merit — but the subject line is too gentle and the offer architecture is too soft to drive meaningful conversion.
- Overall judgment: a well-intentioned editorial reset that undersells whatever offer lives inside. The brand voice is consistent, the photography is strong, but the email doesn't give a reader a single compelling reason to click.

## What's working

- **"THE COLLECTORS" masthead** is a crisp brand-building label that rewards loyal subscribers who've been following the art/culture arc since the 6/1 send.
- **Hero photography** is rich and on-brand — moody, editorial, aspirational, consistent with the CB2 aesthetic premium subscribers expect.
- **Cadence relief** is legitimate: following back-to-back sale pushes and the Todd Snyder launch, a content beat here reduces unsubscribe pressure.
- **Product grid at the bottom** provides tangible shop-ability after the editorial setup.

## What's weak

- **No visible discount or offer in the hero** — the most prominent real estate communicates mood, not value. After five days of explicit sale messaging, readers expect a hook.
- **Subject line "A well-collected home office" is entirely atmospheric** — 27 characters, zero offer specificity, zero urgency. It could be a magazine headline, not an inbox CTA.
- **Todd Snyder cross-sell feels forced** in a home-office editorial — the Townhouse collab is lifestyle/menswear, not home office, and stitching it in mid-email fragments the narrative.
- **The $1,000 callout mid-email is ambiguous** — at thumbnail scale it reads as a price point, not a discount or credit, and that's the wrong first impression.
- **Multi-theme construction** (editorial → collab cross-sell → product grid) means the email tries to do three jobs and lands none of them definitively.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lane: editorial OR offer.** If this is a "Collectors Home Office" story, build it — one hero, 4-6 curated products with story context, single CTA to a dedicated landing edit. If it's a sale extension, lead with the discount explicitly.
- 2. **Surface the offer in the hero.** Even "Sale prices on home office favorites" in a small lockup under the hero image would lift click intent without breaking the editorial mood.
- 3. **Drop the Todd Snyder mid-email splice.** The collab had its own email (two of them). Let this send breathe as a standalone home-office moment; the cross-sell dilutes both.
- 4. **Tighten the subject to name a specific benefit** — "Curate your home office — sale styles inside" is one option that keeps the editorial voice while flagging the commercial payload.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Leaked developer placeholder** — The following comment appears verbatim in the shipped `<head>`, with a typo ("IMPUT"):
  ```html
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  This is an unfilled template instruction that should have been removed pre-send.

- **Zoom blocked** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />` prevents user-initiated zoom. This violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by Apple Mail and some ESPs as an accessibility defect.

- **Global text-size-adjust suppression** — `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` is applied universally. The intended target is `body`, not `*`; applying it globally can interfere with nested overrides.

- **Conflicting `@font-face` declarations** — "Lineto Brown Sub" is declared three times:
  1. `eot` + `woff2` + `woff` stack (no weight qualifier)
  2. `woff2` only, `font-weight: 400`
  3. `woff2` only, `font-weight: 600`

  The unweighted declaration shadows the weight-keyed ones in clients that process CSS sequentially, making weight resolution unpredictable.

- **Narrow mobile breakpoint** — `@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)` targets only the narrowest phones. Devices rendering at 420–767px CSS width (common Android flagships, many iPads in portrait) receive no responsive overrides and will likely render the desktop layout unconstrained.

- **`line-height: 100%` globally applied** — `*{line-height: 100%}` collapses line height to match font size on every element. This is often intentional in email resets but will clip descenders on multi-line text unless overridden per-element. Confirm all text blocks have explicit `line-height` declarations.
