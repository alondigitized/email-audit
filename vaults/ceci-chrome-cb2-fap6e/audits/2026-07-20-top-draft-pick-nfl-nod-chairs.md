# Top draft pick: NFL Nod Chairs!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids drops a licensed NFL collab — "Nod Chairs" in team colorways — and the hero executes it well: three chairs styled cleanly against a neutral backdrop, a legible "Introducing the NFL Game Day Collection" headline, and a clear shop button. The subject's "top draft pick" wordplay earns its pun.
- The problem is the second act. Below the chairs, the email pivots to what appears to be a sweepstakes entry section (gold trophy imagery, enter-to-win language), then falls all the way into a generic "we have these for you" product grid — ottomans, storage, rugs — that has nothing to do with NFL. The focus collapses. What started as a tight licensed-product launch becomes another catalog dump, echoing the same structural weakness from the July 20 organizing-products send ("This makes organizing a breeze!").
- The NFL angle is genuinely differentiated from their recent run of editorial and quality-narrative emails. But they undersell it by not anchoring the whole email to it. If you're an NFL fan shopping for a kid's room, the hero grabs you — and then the bottom half lets you down.

## What's working

- Hero is clean and on-theme — three NFL-licensed chair colorways displayed at a readable size with immediate brand context.
- Subject line wordplay ("Top draft pick") is light, appropriate for the brand, and memorable without being corny.
- At 31 characters the subject is very mobile-friendly, and the NFL novelty stands out against their recent editorial cadence.
- Sweepstakes element adds a non-purchase reason to click — broadens appeal beyond active buyers.
- Timing is right — July is prime NFL preseason hype, giving the send seasonal legs.

## What's weak

- Email loses its spine after the hero; the generic product grid at the bottom is brand-diluting and thematically incoherent with an NFL launch.
- No price anchor anywhere visible — the NFL collab is a premium product, but there's no stated price or "starting at $X" to manage expectation or drive urgency.
- The sweepstakes section is visually competing with the hero CTA rather than flowing through it — two incompatible conversion goals in one scroll.
- No team-specific call-outs visible in the hero; showing all teams at once makes it feel like a category launch rather than something personally relevant.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the generic product grid entirely.** This email should be 100% NFL from top to footer. Any non-NFL product is dead weight — it signals "we ran out of content" and undercuts the licensed-collab positioning.
- 2. **Add a price anchor in the hero.** "Starting at $X" or "From $X" gives the NFL premium context and reduces abandonment from sticker shock downstream.
- 3. **Make the sweepstakes a footer moment, not a mid-email detour.** Run the chairs CTA first, let the brand deliver its promise, then close with "P.S. — enter to win" so both CTAs get clean air.
- 4. **Lean into team personalization.** If they have team preference data from past orders or browse history, a team-specific hero would dramatically outperform the generic multi-chair display.
- 5. Rewrite the subject to add urgency or scarcity — the current version is fun but flat.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Flagged by WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove the `maximum-scale` cap.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied globally (desktop CSS block)**
```css
html { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
This overrides the `body`-level `100%` setting and suppresses OS-level text scaling for low-vision users. Change to `100%`.

**Conflicting desktop image max-width breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }  /* declared after */
```
The 640px rule is declared last so it wins the cascade for all viewports ≥640px, including those ≥768px. Result: images are constrained to 640px on all wide screens instead of the intended 600px. Reorder so the wider breakpoint rule appears last, or consolidate into a single rule.

**Dark mode not supported**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Apple Mail and Gmail Android will not apply system dark mode. No issue per se, but a known rendering gap on those clients.
