# This home office is a study in creativity
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- CB2 breaks from its recent discount-cadence run (07-22 through 07-29 were nearly all "% off, ends today" sends) with a pure editorial email: fashion designer Aurora James's Laurel Canyon home office, told through named, sourced pieces — the Felena Vase by Ross Cassidy, the 1962 Klub Chair by Steen Østergaard, the Koa Side Table, the Fiora Rug. It's the most premium-feeling CB2 send in this history, trading urgency for aspirational storytelling.
- The trade-off is real, though: not one of the six featured hero products shows a price. A reader curious about "how much is that chair" gets nothing until they scroll past the entire editorial section into an unrelated "More For You" grid. This is a brand-building email, not a conversion email, and it reads that way — beautifully shot, but light on reasons to act now.
- Judgement: a strong departure that will refresh the inbox after a week of sale blasts, but it leaves money on the table by hiding pricing on every product it actually wants you to fall in love with.

## What's working

- Named designer/provenance copy under each piece (Felena Vase by Ross Cassidy, Klub Chair designed by Steen Østergaard in 1962) adds credibility rarely seen in promo email.
- Consistent "SHOP [PRODUCT NAME]" CTA under every featured item makes each piece individually shoppable rather than funneling to one generic link.
- Hero image is large, moody, on-brand lifestyle photography that sets an aspirational tone immediately.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content anywhere in the send.
- Genuine cadence break from the "% OFF ENDS TODAY" run of the past two weeks, which should stand out in-stream.

## What's weak

- Zero visible pricing on any of the six featured hero products — curiosity has no payoff until deep in the scroll.
- No offer, discount, or urgency anywhere in the body; the only near-offer is a generic loyalty/signup line buried in the footer.
- Subject line is mood copy with no product or designer name — weak pull for someone scanning for a reason to open.
- "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $1,000" sits abruptly between the designer profile and the product grid — a jarring tonal break mid-narrative.
- Preview/preheader text isn't visible in this render, so it's unclear whether it's complementing the subject or wasted on boilerplate.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or "shop the look from $X" caption under the hero products — right now the email builds desire with zero payoff for a price-conscious browser.
- 2. Name the designer or a specific piece in the subject line — CB2's best curiosity hooks (e.g. 07-25's "most important room" reveal) still name a concrete destination.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Aurora James turned her attic into this`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Inside a fashion designer's Laurel Canyon home office`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The pieces behind her creative studio, from $XXX`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the vintage-inspired chair, rug, and vase from her home`
- 3. Move the "$1,000 review" CTA to after the "More For You" grid so it doesn't interrupt the editorial flow.
- 4. Pull one or two prices from "More For You" up into the hero section as a "starting at" anchor, so the email isn't all mood with no numbers until the very bottom.
