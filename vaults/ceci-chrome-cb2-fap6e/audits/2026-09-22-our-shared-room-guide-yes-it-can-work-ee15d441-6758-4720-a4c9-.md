# Our shared room guide (yes, it can work!) →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running an editorial "shared kids room" guide framed around bunk beds, color coordination, and small-space furniture — a genuinely useful angle for anyone with two kids splitting a bedroom. The email is dense and long (a dozen modules deep), stacking room vignettes, product grids, a "go vertical" bunk-bed section, a nursery color module, a lookbook teaser, and finally a financing-card pitch — it reads more like a catalog scroll than a single focused send. Compared to the brand's recent Jenny Lind deep-dive (7/10, tightly single-product) and the Oceanside sofa send (6/10, single-product), this one dilutes its own premise by tacking on unrelated modules (nursery rugs, lighting, the credit card, an app download) that have nothing to do with sharing a room.
- The hero concept ("yes, it can work!") is smart copywriting for a real parental anxiety, but the email doesn't deliver a payoff fast enough — no single hero image sells "this is what a shared room looks like" before you're already three modules deep into generic furniture grids. Judgment: a well-targeted premise buried under bloat and cross-sell; solid but not a standout.

## What's working

- The "our shared room guide" hero headline directly addresses a real, specific parenting problem (siblings sharing space), which is sharper than generic seasonal copy.
- The "go vertical" bunk-bed module ties directly back to the shared-room theme with a real vignette photo and clear specs (Sleeps 2, converts, GREENGUARD Gold Certified).
- Clean, consistent visual language — muted earth tones, consistent typography, GREENGUARD/quality badges are a nice trust signal for a parent buyer.
- Room vignette photography looks real and lived-in (styled shelves, layered bedding), not sterile product-on-white shots.

## What's weak

- The email sprawls well past the shared-room premise into nursery colors, rug-finding, lighting tips, a lookbook teaser, a "we love these for you" product grid, and a credit-card pitch — the core idea gets lost.
- No single dominant hero image/CTA sells the shared-room concept at a glance; it's a wall of similarly-sized modules with no clear visual hierarchy pointing to one primary action.
- Two "Book a Free Appointment" CTAs and a "Shop All Furniture" CTA compete right at the top, diffusing what the one primary click should be.
- The credit-card and "it's all on the app" blocks feel bolted onto an otherwise editorial email — separate agenda intruding on the guide's premise.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email roughly in half — keep hero, "balance it out," and "go vertical" (all on-theme), and move nursery color/rug/lighting/credit-card/app modules to a separate send. Single-theme focus is what made the Jenny Lind send score 7/10.
- 2. Lead with one large hero shot of an actual shared bunk-bed room styled for two kids, with a single CTA ("Shop Shared Room Furniture"), before any secondary "balance it out" grid.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA verb/action for the whole email (either "Shop the Guide" or "Book a Free Appointment") rather than repeating both at the top.
- 4. Add a light social-proof line near the bunk-bed module (e.g., a review quote about durability/safety) since parents buying bunk beds care heavily about safety reassurance.
