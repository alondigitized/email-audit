# JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's collab-launch email for the CB2 x Todd Snyder "Timberframe" collection — a deliberate editorial pivot after nine consecutive days of sale emails (50% off, countdowns, "TWO SALES END TONIGHT"). The brand-story instinct is right: giving subscribers something aspirational after a heavy promo run is smart sequencing. The hero is warm and well-shot — natural wood frames, Americana restraint, CB2's signature neutral palette. Todd Snyder's name carries real design credibility among the target demo.
- The problem is execution discipline. The email splits its attention between the collab launch and what looks like a secondary "COLOREFRESH" promotional block mid-scroll — two different narratives competing for the same open. Launch emails need a single thread. The moment a subscriber's eye hits an unrelated section, the collab story is interrupted and neither message wins.
- There's also no price anchor anywhere visible. This is a deliberate premium launch, but even a "from $X" or "complimentary shipping on the collection" line would give the reader a reason to click today rather than "maybe later." Right now it's aspirational without a hook.

- **Subject line is the strongest in recent CB2 history.** "JUST DROPPED: CB2 x Todd Snyder" is concrete, specific, and short at 31 chars — immediately clear this is a new launch, not another sale.
- **Cadence break is well-timed.** After days of countdown timers and urgency stacking, an editorial collab launch feels earned rather than noisy.
- **Hero imagery nails the aesthetic.** The warm wood tones and restrained palette communicate Todd Snyder's Americana sensibility without over-explaining.
- **"SHOP THE COLLECTION" CTA is unambiguous.** Verb + object, clearly styled as a button — no guesswork on where to click.

## What's weak

- **The secondary COLOREFRESH block breaks the collab story.** One email, one message. The midpoint pivot out of the Timberframe narrative is the email's biggest structural flaw.
- **No price anchor.** A launch email without a "starting at $X" or even "complimentary shipping" line gives no reason to act today — the collab will still be there next week.
- **The email is very long for a single launch.** Multiple lifestyle shots without a tightened scroll path means click energy dissipates before the product grid.
- **No urgency signal.** "JUST DROPPED" implies newness but implies permanence. A limited-quantity note or launch-window offer would sharpen the close.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the COLOREFRESH section entirely.** Reserve it for its own send. One email, one idea — the Todd Snyder collab is strong enough to stand alone.
- 2. **Add a price anchor in the hero.** A simple "Timberframe, from $XXX" line or "Complimentary shipping on the collection this weekend" turns browsing intent into click intent.
- 3. **Tighten the scroll path.** Reduce the lifestyle shots from 4+ to 2 — hero image + one room shot — and push the product grid higher. The subscriber is on mobile; the grid is the action, not the mood board.
- 4. **Give Todd Snyder a line of copy.** A one-sentence quote or design POV ("I wanted furniture that ages the way a good suit does") would justify the collab premium and differentiate from generic CB2 launches.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — source is truncated before body links appear. Issues visible in the `<head>`:

- **Cross-domain font assets from `crateandbarrel.com`**: Four `@font-face` declarations load `HNW05` webfonts from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...` inside a CB2 email. Email clients that block cross-origin requests (Outlook desktop, some corporate environments) will silently fail these fetches. Not a deliverability risk but increases DNS lookup count and can be blocked by strict CSP/firewall rules in enterprise inboxes.
- **Double-slash URLs on CB2 font assets**: Three font `src` declarations have a double slash after the domain:
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/...`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/...`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/...`

  Most web servers 301-redirect `//path` → `/path`, meaning each font load wastes a round-trip. Should be normalized to single slashes.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Zoom prevention (`maximum-scale=1`)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation for low-vision users. Drop `maximum-scale=1`.

**Aggressive global text-size-adjust on `*`**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applying `text-size-adjust:none` to every element can suppress OS-level text scaling for accessibility users. The intent (prevent Gmail auto-scaling) is better achieved scoped to `body` or `table td`, not `*`.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*`**
Same `*{}` rule sets `line-height: 100%` globally. This collapses line height to the font size across all elements, which can cause descenders to clip and multi-line text to overlap in some clients (particularly Samsung Mail, older Android).

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
418px targets the iPhone SE viewport exactly but misses the common 480px range used by many Android devices. The typical safe mobile breakpoint is `max-width: 480px` to catch the full spectrum of small-screen clients.

**`font-display: swap` in email CSS**
```css
font-display: swap;
```
Appears on GT Sectra and Lineto Brown `@font-face` declarations. This CSS property is ignored by all email clients — it only applies in browsers. Harmless but dead code that could mislead template maintainers into thinking FOUT behavior is controlled.
