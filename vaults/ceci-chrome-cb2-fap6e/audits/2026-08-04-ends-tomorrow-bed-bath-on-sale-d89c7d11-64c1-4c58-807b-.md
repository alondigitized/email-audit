# ENDS TOMORROW: BED & BATH ON SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2 running its most reliable playbook — the clean, stacked-discount mechanic that hit 9/10 twice last week and appeared again on 8/1 ("final weekend for 30% off"). "ENDS TOMORROW: BED & BATH ON SALE" pairs a credible one-day deadline with a specific category, and the hero delivers instantly: warm wood-tone bedroom, "UP TO 30% OFF," "Bedding ships free," and a high-contrast "SHOP THE SALE" button before any scroll.
- Where it gets muddled is the second hero. Immediately below the Bedroom + Bath event, a second banner pitches "SUMMER CLEARANCE UP TO 60% OFF" — a bigger, more aggressive number competing for the same eyeball real estate one screen later. A shopper who just registered "30% off ends tomorrow" now has to reconcile that against "60% off," with no clarity on whether it's the same sale, an overlapping sale, or a different category. That's a self-competing offer stack, not a hierarchy.
- Below the fold it's the standard, well-executed CB2 kit: wall art spotlight, a review-for-$1,000 sweepstakes hook, a strong "MORE FOR YOU" grid with visible strikethrough pricing, and the loyalty/app/text-signup block. Solid, on-brand, no render bugs — but the subject's urgency is spent on a page that immediately dilutes it with a second, bigger claim.

## What's working

- Hero delivers the subject's promise immediately: "ENDS TOMORROW," "UP TO 30% OFF," free bedding shipping, and a clear "SHOP THE SALE" CTA, all above the fold.
- Strikethrough pricing in the "MORE FOR YOU" grid (e.g., $999.00 → $849.00, $399.00 → $349.00) gives concrete, credible savings signals.
- Loyalty and retention plays are stacked cleanly at the bottom — 10% Rewards Dollars, 15% off for text signup, gift registry — without cluttering the top of the email.
- Clean render throughout: no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent warm neutral palette matching recent sends.

## What's weak

- Two competing percentage-off claims (30% and 60%) stacked back-to-back within one scroll dilutes the "ends tomorrow" urgency and creates confusion about which sale actually applies to what.
- Both primary CTAs say "SHOP THE SALE" — identical copy on two different offers gives the reader no way to distinguish where each button leads.
- The hero links to a lifestyle bedroom scene, not a specific product — strong on mood, weak as a direct product hook compared to CB2's shoppable-editorial sends (e.g., 8/1 "The best kind of impulse buy").
- Subject line leans on ALL CAPS ("ENDS TOMORROW"), a mild spam-adjacent pattern versus CB2's better-performing recent subjects that skipped caps entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut it to one offer per send, or clearly subordinate the second: if Summer Clearance and the Bedroom+Bath event are genuinely different promotions, separate them into distinct sends, or make the hero explicitly state "30% off bedroom + bath, PLUS up to 60% off select clearance" so the reader isn't left reconciling two numbers.
- 2. Differentiate the two CTA buttons — "Shop Bedroom + Bath" vs. "Shop Clearance" — so each click has an unambiguous destination.
- 3. Swap the lifestyle hero for the shoppable-editorial format that scored well on 8/1 — name one hero bed or bath product with a price, then link the room shot as secondary.
- 4. Drop the ALL CAPS treatment on "ENDS TOMORROW" in the subject to reduce the spam-pattern signal while keeping the urgency word itself.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tomorrow: up to 30% off bed & bath`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day for 30% off bedding + free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, summer clearance is now up to 60% off select pieces.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on bedding, bath, and best-selling decor.`
