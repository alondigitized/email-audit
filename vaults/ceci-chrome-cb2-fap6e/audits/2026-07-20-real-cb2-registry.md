# REAL CB2 REGISTRY
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a registry program email from a CB2 sub-brand sender, not the main CB2 promotional stream. The hero is clean and on-brand — two people in formal wear, "THE REGISTRY" in large type — and the structure is logical: hero intro → "SHOP MOST HEARTED" social-proof grid → inspirational interstitial → "MORE FOR YOU" product grid. It's aesthetically consistent with CB2's house style.
- But the email has a fundamental targeting problem: there's no offer, no urgency, and no personalization signal to indicate this went to anyone who has actually started a registry. Receiving this cold — without any registry context — it reads like a brand awareness push dressed up as a program email. That's a mismatch the design can't rescue.
- The subject line "REAL CB2 REGISTRY" is the most pressing problem. "REAL" is defensive and unexplained — it implies fake alternatives exist without telling you why that matters, and the ALL CAPS construction is a spam-adjacent pattern. After yesterday's editorial "Add these to your art collection," this sender-switch to "CB2 Registry" + a cryptic all-caps subject is a notable downshift in sophistication.

## What's working

- The hero image is visually strong — formal couple + "THE REGISTRY" type is immediately legible and emotionally appropriate for the program.
- "SHOP MOST HEARTED" is smart framing: social proof via implied popularity rather than a review count.
- Prices are visible in the product grids ($39.95, $79.95, $99.95), which grounds the browsing and reduces sticker-shock anxiety.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- No CTA buttons — navigation depends entirely on clicking product images, which is low-conversion for an audience not already in purchase mode.
- The subject "REAL CB2 REGISTRY" is ALL CAPS and carries no offer, urgency, or curiosity hook.
- Zero personalization or registry-state signal — nothing to indicate the recipient has an active registry, no name, no item count, no "you added X" trigger.
- No offer or incentive — no discount, no gift-giver perk, no completion bonus. A registry email without a value hook is pure brand spend.
- The sub-brand sender ("CB2 Registry") breaks the familiar sender recognition built across the prior week of CB2 emails; a subscriber might not immediately connect it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete offer or trigger hook.** Even "complete your registry and get 20% off remaining items" or "your registry milestone" would create a reason to act. Without it, there's no call-to-action beyond casual browsing.
- 2. **Add a primary CTA button above the fold.** "Start Your Registry" or "View Your Registry" should be a visible button in the hero — not just implied by product grids.
- 3. **Fix the subject line.** ALL CAPS + "REAL" is doing the opposite of what a registry email should do (feel considered, personal, celebratory).
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your CB2 registry — the most hearted picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Build the registry they'll actually shop`
- 4. **Re-route to the CB2 main sender or make the sub-brand explicit.** "CB2 Registry" is a cold name. Signing as "CB2 | Registry" or even going back to the main CB2 sender with a registry badge in the hero reduces the trust gap.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the styles couples are adding most right now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces your guests will actually want to buy.`
- 5. **Segment this list.** If the recipient has no active registry, this should either not send or flip to a "Start a registry" acquisition email with a genuine hook (cash-back, discount on self-purchase after registry close, etc.).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` breakpoints:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 100% !important} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img {max-width: 690px} }
```
The `690px` rule appears after the `768px` rule in the stylesheet. At viewport widths ≥768px, both fire — the `690px` cap wins due to cascade order, overriding the intended `100%` behavior. Fix by merging or reordering.

**No dark mode support detected** — no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rule in visible CSS. The hardcoded `background:#ffffff` and `a { color: #333333 }` will not adapt in dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Gmail app on iOS). Risk of invisible light-on-light text.

**`font-display: swap` in email context** — declared in multiple `@font-face` blocks. This property is ignored by all major email clients; harmless but dead code.
