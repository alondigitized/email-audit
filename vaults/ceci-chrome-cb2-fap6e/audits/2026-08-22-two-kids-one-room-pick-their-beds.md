# Two kids, one room? Pick their beds →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This one actually delivers on its subject line. "Two kids, one room? Pick their beds" leads straight into "two kids, one room: find their sleep solution" with a real hero shot of a loft bed with built-in desk and shelving — the exact space-saving use case the subject promises. It's a clear step up from this brand's usual clearance-catalog sprawl (see 2026-08-21's "NEW markdowns!" send): three named bunk/loft SKUs (Grayson Storage Loft Bed, Hampshire Bunk Bed, Finn Bunk Bed) each get their own hero image, name, and "Shop now" button before the email drifts into the familiar shared-spaces grid and sweepstakes filler.
- Where it falls apart is length and focus. After the three bed heroes it sprawls into bedding, decor, storage, backpack bundles, a $1000 sweepstakes, nursery furniture, and a credit card pitch — none of which relate to the "two kids one room" premise. By the time a reader hits "we love these for you" the email has fully abandoned its own subject line.
- Solid top, bloated bottom — the first three screens are this brand's best targeted work in the recent stretch; everything after is undifferentiated filler that dilutes it.

## What's working

- Hero directly answers the subject line: "two kids, one room: find their sleep solution" with a matching loft-bed-with-desk image.
- Each of the three featured beds gets a distinct product shot, a short functional callout (e.g. "the FSC-certified oak loft bed has room below for a reading nook or desk"), and its own CTA.
- "Exclusive!" tagging on the Hampshire and Finn bunk beds signals differentiation/scarcity without overselling.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, buttons are legible, layout is consistent brand navy/tan throughout.

## What's weak

- Email overstays its welcome: sweepstakes, backpack bundles, nursery gliders, and a credit-card offer all appear after the bed content and have nothing to do with sharing a room.
- Visual hierarchy weakens fast after the fold — "more faves for shared spaces" is a generic 4-up grid competing for attention with the actual hero product.
- The credit card block ("get the card you love just got better") and "It's all on the app" footer add clutter that dilutes an otherwise tight premise.
- No pricing or discount visible on any of the three hero beds — a parent comparison-shopping bunk beds has no anchor price to click toward.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email after the three bed heroes plus one CTA to the full bunk/loft bed category — drop the sweepstakes, backpack bundles, and credit card sections entirely, or push them to a follow-up send.
- 2. Add a price or "starting at $X" under each of the three featured beds — right now there's a price tease ("starting at $24") only on the unrelated shared-spaces grid, not on the actual heroes.
- 3. Replace the generic "more faves for shared spaces" grid with a size/space-planner tool CTA ("Find the right bed for your space") — that's what a two-kids-one-room parent actually needs next.
- 4. Tighten preview text to reinforce the specific use case instead of leaving it generic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bunk vs. loft: which bed fits your kids' room?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sharing a room? These beds were made for it`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `3 space-saving bunk & loft beds, starting under $600`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `FSC-certified, built to share — shop bunk & loft beds`
