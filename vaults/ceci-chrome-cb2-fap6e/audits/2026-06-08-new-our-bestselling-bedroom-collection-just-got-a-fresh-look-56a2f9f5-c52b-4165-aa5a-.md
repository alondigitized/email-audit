# NEW! Our bestselling bedroom collection just got a fresh look
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's bedroom-refresh announcement — the brand is using a "new look for a bestselling collection" hook to re-merchandise existing bedroom furniture rather than lead with a clearance angle. The hero image is clean: natural-wood, warm-palette bedroom photography that communicates the collection's aesthetic immediately. That's the strongest card in the hand.
- The email earns its open but loses focus fast. After a solid bedroom opening it pivots mid-scroll into a Father's Day gift deadline callout and then into a personalized product carousel that has nothing to do with bedrooms. That split personality undercuts the initial premise. The top-banner discount (visible but small) is a missed chance — it should be the hero, not a footnote, if the brand is trying to drive a purchase.
- Against the recent Crate & Barrel cadence — kitchen discounts on 6/7, organization editorial, and the Crate & Kids delivery-speed play — this bedroom refresh is at least a category change. But the brand has been sending hard across multiple categories all week, so inbox fatigue is a real risk.

## What's working

- Hero bedroom photography is warm and aspirational; the natural-wood palette immediately communicates the refreshed aesthetic.
- "Complete your bedroom" cross-sell section is logically sequenced and keeps the user in-category for at least two scroll stops.
- The Father's Day deadline callout ("order by 5/11") is a concrete urgency signal — rare in this brand's recent sends, which have leaned editorial.
- Top promo banner is present and sets a discount expectation before the hero image even loads.
- Multiple CTA buttons are consistently styled and unambiguous.

## What's weak

- The email splits between bedroom collection and Father's Day gifting — two different shopping intents in one send, and neither is fully served.
- The top-banner offer is the only price signal but it's the smallest element on screen; the hero copy doesn't reinforce it.
- "NEW!" in the subject is a weak hook — the actual news (a collection redesign) is more interesting than the generic label.
- The "we love these for you" personalization carousel appears below the Father's Day block, which means a bedroom shopper has to wade through gifting content to reach it.
- The Spektrum furniture module and TheDesignOne consultation block at the bottom feel bolted on — this is four emails stitched into one scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one story.** Split this into two sends: bedroom-refresh now, Father's Day gifting separately. The bedroom collection hook is genuinely differentiated from last week's discount wave — don't dilute it.
- 2. **Pull the discount into the hero.** If there's a banner offer, put the percentage in the headline copy so it registers before the CTA.
- 3. **Replace "NEW!" in the subject with the actual news.** The collection name or the aesthetic change is more compelling than a generic flag.
- 4. **Move the personalization carousel above the Father's Day block.** In-category relevance should come before the category pivot.
- 5. **Cut the Spektrum and TheDesignOne modules.** They break the visual rhythm and reduce the urgency of the bedroom offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Conflicting image max-width media queries (dead rule):**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second */
```
On screens ≥768px both rules match; equal specificity means the later-declared `640px` wins, making the `600px` rule a dead letter. Images will render at 640px max on desktop, not 600px as apparently intended.

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue — Dark mode not supported:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Explicitly opts out of dark mode. Background color `#e6e7e8` set on `body` will be forced onto dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook 2019+). No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` override is present.

**Issue — Aggressive `*` selector reset:**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ... }
```
`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on the universal selector can suppress system-level accessibility text-scaling in older WebKit email clients.
