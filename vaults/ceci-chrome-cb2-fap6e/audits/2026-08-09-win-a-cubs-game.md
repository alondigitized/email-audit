# WIN A CUBS GAME
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-09**
## Executive summary

- CB2 pulls out a fifth creative mode this week: the sweepstakes. "WIN CUBS TICKETS AND A DESIGN REFRESH" leads with a co-branded CB2×Cubs lockup, then stacks a genuinely stacked prize — club-level tickets, a $2,000 travel voucher, a game day tour, and a $1,000 CB2 gift card — over a moody sectional-sofa hero. It's a clean, well-executed sweepstakes email, but it's not a shopping email at all: there's no discount, no specific product push beyond one small "New & Best Selling" glassware block below the fold.
- This works fine as a one-off engagement play (email list growth, brand affinity, local-market buzz for Chicago), but it does nothing for someone trying to buy furniture right now. Compared to 8/7's "AVAILABLE AGAIN" restock alert (8/10) or 8/1's shoppable editorial (8/10), this send trades commerce intent for contest intent — a deliberate, reasonable choice, but it will underperform on clicks-to-cart for most of the list.
- Execution-wise, it's clean: no render bugs, a clear single CTA, decent visual hierarchy. The Cubs tie-in is also hyper-regional — great for Chicago-area subscribers, largely irrelevant (maybe even confusing) for anyone outside that market.

## What's working

- Prize stack is concrete and impressive (tickets + $2,000 travel voucher + $1,000 gift card) — not a vague "enter to win" teaser.
- Single, unambiguous CTA ("ENTER NOW") sits right below the offer copy with no competing buttons in the hero.
- CB2×Cubs co-branded lockup at the top immediately signals what the promo is before you even read the headline.
- Hero image (warm sectional, marble coffee table) still reinforces CB2's product aesthetic even though this is a contest, not a sale.
- No layout bugs — text is legible over the image, sections are cleanly divided.

## What's weak

- No discount or shoppable offer in the primary message — a big miss for anyone opening with purchase intent.
- Hyper-local tie-in (Cubs, September 13 game) will read as irrelevant noise to any subscriber outside Chicago.
- "New & Best Selling" section shows a single cocktail-glassware product shot with no product name, price, or second item — weak follow-through as the only shopping moment in the email.
- Subject "WIN A CUBS GAME" doesn't match on-page copy ("WIN CUBS TICKETS AND A DESIGN REFRESH") — you can't literally win a game, and the mismatch feels like a rushed subject line.
- ALL-CAPS subject with no punctuation reads a little like a spam pattern-match, especially stacked against CB2's cadence of frequent, differently-styled sends this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body mismatch first — align subject with actual prize copy so it doesn't sound like a factual error.
- 2. Add a real discount or offer for non-entrants right in the hero (e.g., a secondary CTA "or shop the sale") so the email still converts people who don't care about the sweepstakes.
- 3. Geo-target this send — Chicago-area subscribers get the Cubs framing, everyone else gets a generic "design refresh" sweepstakes without the team tie-in.
- 4. Expand the "New & Best Selling" block to 2-3 products with names/prices instead of one unlabeled glass shot.
