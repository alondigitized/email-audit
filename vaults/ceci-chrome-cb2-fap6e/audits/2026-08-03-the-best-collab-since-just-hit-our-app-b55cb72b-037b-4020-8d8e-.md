# The best collab since 🍪 & 🥛 JUST hit our app
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's app-collab push, and it's a classic bait-and-switch structure: the subject and hero promise something genuinely novel — a Levain Bakery tie-in exclusive to the app — but by the second screen the email has already abandoned that thread for an Aspen collection sale, then a $5000 Design Desk sweepstakes, then a six-item product grid, then a new credit card pitch, then a second app plug, then a second Design Desk CTA. It's the same kitchen-sink sprawl flagged in "How a top designer picked her forever furniture" (2026-08-02, 8/10) and "Dinnerware designed to wow" (2026-07-31, 7/10) — except here the sprawl is worse because the hook (bakery collab) never resolves; you never see what the actual Levain product is, just an illustrated street scene and a phone mockup.
- The hero itself is well executed — a moody NYC illustration, a clean headline, and one unambiguous CTA ("Shop Early on Our App"). But for a furniture/home persona, the lead offer is off-category entirely, and the email never earns back that attention once it pivots into six unrelated modules.
- Judgment: technically clean, creatively curious subject line, but structurally undisciplined — this reads as "everything we have to push this week" rather than one clear pitch.

## What's working

- The hero illustration (phone mockup over an illustrated NYC block) is distinctive and on-brand — nothing else in this brand's recent history looks like it.
- Single clear CTA in the hero ("Shop Early on Our App") with good button contrast against the navy background.
- Product grid is clean and legible — six items, consistent pricing display, strikethrough sale prices visible (Le Creuset items, KitchenAid mixer).
- No visual bugs — every image renders, no overlapping text, no broken layout across the full length.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a bakery collab; the email delivers a sale, a sweepstakes, a credit card ad, and an app download pitch before ever showing an actual Levain product — the hook is abandoned almost immediately.
- Six distinct offers/CTAs compete for attention (bakery collab, Aspen 15% off, $5000 sweepstakes, product grid, Crate Card, app download) with no visual signal for which one matters most.
- Design Desk sweepstakes and app-download CTA both repeat later in the email almost verbatim — redundant real estate that could've supported the bakery story instead.
- "15% off" carries an asterisk with no visible fine print resolution in the body — the exclusion terms are buried below the fold in the tiny disclaimer block.
- For a furniture-focused subscriber, nothing in the first two screens is furniture — it's bakery collab, then tableware — so the category-specific reader has to scroll past irrelevant content to reach anything on-brief.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one story. Either this is the Levain Bakery collab email (show the actual product, a countdown to the Aug 3 app launch, one CTA) or it's the Aspen sale email — trying to be both plus a sweepstakes plus a credit card ad dilutes all of them.
- 2. If keeping the collab as the hero, show the actual collab item (a cookie, a tote, whatever it is) — an illustrated street scene with no product shot doesn't deliver on the "just hit our app" promise.
- 3. Drop one of the two Design Desk CTAs and one of the two app-download CTAs — repeating identical asks trains readers to skip both.
- 4. Move the fine-print/asterisk resolution for "15% off" closer to the offer, not buried in the footer disclaimer block.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Levain Bakery x Crate & Barrel — app-exclusive, live now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Our biggest bakery collab yet drops on the app today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See the Levain collab before anyone else — app only, through Aug 3.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One day only: shop the Crate & Barrel x Levain drop first.`
