# The Warehouse Sale is selling fast
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's sixth-or-seventh warehouse-sale send in roughly two weeks and it carries all the weight of that run. The hero — "WAREHOUSE SALE / UP TO 60% OFF" floated over a warm, four-poster bedroom lifestyle image — is clean, the offer is legible, and the "SHOP THE SALE" button is unambiguous. But the creative template is essentially identical to the June 20 "Even more markdowns just added" email: same structure, same headline hierarchy, same aspirational lifestyle block over a sale tier. "Selling fast" in the subject line is the same manufactured urgency CB2 has been deploying all month, and by now it reads as ambient noise.
- What partially redeems it: the "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom shows personalized (or at least category-filtered) tiles with real discount badges — that's the most actionable content in the email. The "$1,000 review contest" is a novel break from the drum-beat, but it sits incongruously mid-email as though dropped into a sale template without editorial thought.
- Judgement: a capable first send; a wasteful sixth. The offer is real but the creative is exhausted.

## What's working

- Hero offer is immediately legible — "UP TO 60% OFF" in large type, single unambiguous "SHOP THE SALE" CTA, nothing competing for attention.
- Warm bedroom lifestyle image is on-brand — modern, minimalist, aspirational; fits the CB2 subscriber aesthetic precisely.
- "MORE FOR YOU" product tiles carry explicit percentage-off badges, making the discount concrete at the item level.
- No render bugs visible — layout is clean, no overlapping text, no broken image placeholders.
- The $1,000 review contest is a genuinely novel hook that breaks the promotional monotony, at least conceptually.

## What's weak

- "Selling fast" urgency is fully fatigued at this cadence — a subscriber who has seen five prior warehouse sale sends does not believe this is a new crisis.
- Creative structure is carbon-copy of June 20's "Even more markdowns just added" send — different lifestyle photo, identical bones; there is nothing to signal this email is worth opening over the last one.
- The review contest module sits mid-email with no logical bridge to the warehouse sale; it reads like a module-swap, not an editorial decision.
- "Up to 60% off" is aspirational — experienced CB2 subscribers know the deepest cuts are narrow; the hero claim is too vague to drive urgency on its own.
- The personalized product grid is below the fold; most non-scrollers will click on the generic sale CTA or not click at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the sale by category in the hero.** "Up to 60% on rugs. Up to 50% on sofas. Up to 40% on lighting." — link each to a filtered sale page. That removes the "up to" bait problem, adds specificity, and gives a subscriber a reason to click *this* email versus the five before it.
- 2. **Retire "selling fast" — replace with a falsifiable signal.** A hard end date ("Sale ends Sunday") or a specific stock line ("Only 18 items still under $200") is credible because the subscriber can verify it. "Selling fast" is not.
- 3. **Move the product grid up.** The discounted tiles are the most compelling content. They belong above the lifestyle editorial, not below a review contest.
- 4. **Give the review contest its own email on a non-sale day.** It's a good mechanic; it's being killed by context.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rugs, sofas & lighting — up to 60% off this week`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Warehouse Sale ends [date] — what's left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Real prices on the pieces moving fastest right now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Rugs from $X. Sofas from $X. Stock is going.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta tag**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Users with low vision cannot magnify content.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` on `*` selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Overrides OS-level text scaling for all elements. This is acceptable on `body` for layout control but applying it to `*` suppresses user accessibility preferences more aggressively than needed.

**Mobile breakpoint/width mismatch**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) {
  .mobile { width:414px !important; }
```
The media query fires at ≤418px but forces 414px width. On a 415–418px viewport this forces horizontal overflow.

**Cross-domain font dependency on crateandbarrel.com**
```css
@font-face { font-family:"HNW05";
  src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2")
```
Four `@font-face` declarations pull from `crateandbarrel.com` instead of `cb2.com`. If Crate & Barrel rotates or removes these assets, the font silently falls back — undetectable in CB2's own monitoring.

**Double-slash paths in cb2.com font URLs**
```css
url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
```
Four occurrences. Technically resolves via server normalization but is non-canonical and will fail on strict CDN configs or if path normalization is ever removed.
