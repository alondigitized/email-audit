# Write a review. Enter for a chance to win $1,000. Find out how →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids leaning on a UGC / sweepstakes mechanic — write a review, enter to win $1,000 — but then burying that hook under an avalanche of unrelated content: fall arrivals, a finishing-touches product section, a personalized picks grid, a credit card push, an app download pitch, and a DesignDesk promo. The subject line makes a clear promise ("write a review, win $1,000") and the hero delivers it at the top, but from there the email sprawls into a five-topic catalog dump that kills the momentum completely.
- This is the second Crate & Kids send today (the birthday gift guide hit earlier). At that cadence, the bar for each individual email to earn attention goes up — and this one doesn't clear it. The sweepstakes mechanic is compelling in theory but the email never commits to it. A focused one-topic send around the review incentive would almost certainly outperform this kitchen-sink build.
- The email isn't broken — it's just unfocused. The hero concept is solid; the execution loses the thread three sections in.

## What's working

- Hero lifestyle shot (bedroom scene) is clean and well-composed, immediately contextualizing the brand.
- "$1,000 gift card" dollar figure in both subject and hero creates a consistent, memorable hook — the reader isn't confused about the offer.
- "we love these for you" personalized product grid is a strong retention tool and feels appropriately warm for the Crate & Kids voice.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29" section grounds the shop incentive with a concrete entry price.
- No visible render bugs — images loaded, layout is intact, text is legible.

## What's weak

- The email makes four or five distinct asks (write a review, shop fall arrivals, shop finishing touches, get the credit card, download the app) — none of them land with full force.
- The credit card and app download sections have no business being in a sweepstakes email; they signal the team just appended standard modules rather than building a send around the brief.
- Subject line at ~64 characters is too long for mobile and will be truncated at "Enter for a chance to win $1,000. Find…" — the "Find out how →" closer gets clipped.
- No deadline visible anywhere in the email — sweepstakes without an end date remove the single biggest motivator to act now.
- Second C&K send today creates fatigue risk; this send doesn't have enough differentiation from the birthday-party email to justify back-to-back delivery.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the email to a single topic.** Drop the credit card module, app module, and DesignDesk entirely. Keep hero → review CTA → two or three product examples to remind them what to review. Everything else is noise that dilutes the conversion goal.
- 2. **Add a sweepstakes deadline.** "Entries close August 1" or similar creates urgency the subject line promises but the body never delivers.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA button copy.** "Find out how" is passive and vague. Change it to "Write a review, enter to win" so the action and reward are in the same breath.
- 4. **Tighten the subject line under 50 characters.** The three-sentence subject is too long and fragments the hook.
- 5. **Suppress this send from subscribers who already got the birthday-party email today** — or at minimum batch them so only one lands per day.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your review = a shot at $1,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Entries close August 1 — any recent purchase qualifies`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Takes 2 minutes. Could win you a grand.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stale template comment (build artifact):**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
<!---->
```
The misspelled placeholder (`IMPUT` → `INPUT`) and its empty companion comment were never removed. These are harmless but indicate the template was not cleaned before send.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
Both queries cover viewports ≥ 768px. Because the `640px` rule is declared *after* the `768px` rule in the stylesheet, it wins at all sizes ≥ 640px — effectively making the `600px` cap unreachable. Images will render at 640px max on all desktop viewports, not 600px. If the design intention was 600px on wider screens, the ordering is incorrect.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`** (applied globally via `*{}`):
Setting this to `none` rather than `100%` blocks user-agent font scaling on mobile, which is an accessibility concern for low-vision users. The `body` rule correctly uses `100%`; the `*{}` override undoes it.

**Dark mode:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Only light scheme declared. Apple Mail and a growing share of Gmail Android users are in dark mode by default. No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` overrides are present. Images with transparent backgrounds or white-on-white text will likely render poorly in dark clients.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags:**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The combined declaration on line 1 is sufficient. The three individual duplicates below it are redundant and add unnecessary `<head>` weight.
