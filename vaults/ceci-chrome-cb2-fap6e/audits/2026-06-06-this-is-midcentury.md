# *This* is midcentury
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- CB2 pivots hard from the repetitive "MIDSEASON SALE" subject-line drumbeat that dominated their last four sends, and the creative team deserves credit for it. "*This* is midcentury" lands as an editorial statement — warm tones, curated product shots of chairs, consoles, and a credenza that all read unmistakably MCM — with the 40% sale mechanic woven in rather than shouted. The result is an email that feels less like a clearance rack and more like a mood board with a price tag.
- The product selection is cohesive: every piece in the scroll — the barrel-back burgundy chair, the rounded cream armchair, the fluted credenza — reinforces a single aesthetic thesis. That discipline is rare in a sale email and makes the collection feel intentional rather than "here's what didn't sell." There's also what appears to be a $100 credit or threshold offer visible mid-email, which adds a second conversion lever without muddying the hero.
- The main weakness is a structural one: the editorial subject line ("*This* is midcentury") gives up the discoverability you'd get from leading with the 40% off signal. It will win opens from CB2's existing loyalists who trust the brand voice, but it surrenders clicks from subscribers who skim and only bite on an explicit number. Given the three consecutive "MIDSEASON SALE" subjects before this one, the pivot is earned — but the execution leaves open-rate upside on the table.

## What's working

- **Cohesive editorial curation** — every product shot belongs to the same midcentury vocabulary; the scroll never breaks aesthetic continuity.
- **Hero image does real work** — the outdoor/pool lifestyle shot opens with aspirational warmth before the furniture grid begins.
- **Subject line breaks the pattern** — after three near-identical "MIDSEASON SALE" sends, "*This* is midcentury" reads fresh and differentiating.
- **40% offer is visually anchored** — the discount is legible and prominent without cannibalizing the editorial feel.
- **Mid-email conversion offer** — the apparent $100 threshold or credit gives a second reason to click for subscribers who scrolled past the hero.

## What's weak

- **Subject hides the sale** — subscribers who scan subjects for a deal signal will not see "40%" or "$" and may skip the open entirely.
- **No time-bound urgency** — nothing visible in the body copy or hero establishes a deadline; "Midseason Sale" language without a countdown creates no scarcity.
- **CTA specificity is low** — "Shop the Collection" is a category gesture, not a product invitation; a specific hero item with a "Shop Now" link would lift CTR.
- **Preview text not visible** — the webview render doesn't expose it, but based on CB2's pattern of weak preheaders this is a likely missed opportunity.
- **Small product grid at bottom dilutes focus** — the thumbnail grid feels like a category page, not a curated editorial; it fights the MCM thesis the top half built.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the number in the subject** — "40% off" is the single highest-lift addition; the editorial voice can survive it. The asterisk formatting adds almost nothing that a stronger subject wouldn't replace.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — one visible line ("Ends Sunday") anywhere in the hero stack would convert fence-sitters without touching the aesthetic.
- 3. **Hero-link one signature piece** — pick the burgundy barrel chair or the fluted credenza as the editorial anchor and link it specifically; "Shop the Collection" is a soft ask.
- 4. **Kill or trim the thumbnail grid** — the curated-editorial energy built in the scroll collapses once you hit a 6-item product dump; end with a stronger single CTA instead.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}
```
Applied to every element. This overrides the user agent's ability to resize text in mobile browsers — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation.

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 failure; also flagged by Apple Mail accessibility linting.

**[MEDIUM] `line-height: 100%` applied globally**
```css
*{... line-height: 100%}
```
A universal `line-height: 100%` collapses line spacing on inline elements. Combined with custom fonts that have tall ascenders (GT Sectra, Lineto Brown), this will cause text clipping in Outlook and Gmail clipped-message views.

**[MEDIUM] Double-slash URLs in `@font-face` declarations**
Three of the four CB2-hosted font rules contain `//` in the path:
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Most servers normalize `//` to `/`, but this is fragile and should be fixed to prevent edge-case 404s. The "Lineto Brown Light Sub" single-format rule (`lineto-brown-light.woff`) does not have this issue.

**[MEDIUM] Cross-domain font loading without documented CORS headers**
Fonts are loaded from two distinct origins:
- `https://www.cb2.com/` (CB2 fonts)
- `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/` (HNW05 font, four variants)

Cross-origin font requests require `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on the font server. If Crate & Barrel's CDN does not return this header for email clients that enforce CORS (Apple Mail on macOS Ventura+), the HNW05 font will silently fail.

**[LOW] Narrow mobile breakpoint**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px)
```
At 418px, this breakpoint misses the 390px–430px range that covers the iPhone 14/15 Pro default width (390pt). Mobile styles will not apply on those devices. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px` or a two-breakpoint system.

**[LOW] Template placeholder left in production source**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unfilled developer comment — note the typo "IMPUT." It is visible in view-source, harmless to rendering, but indicates an incomplete production sweep.

**[INFO] Version comment exposed**
```html
<!--V2-->
```
Exposes internal template versioning. Not a vulnerability, but unnecessary.
