# UP TO 30% OFF BEDROOM & BATH
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- CB2 announces a new "Semi-Annual Bedroom + Bath Event" at up to 30% off — but the body copy says the sale is "Starting Days Away," meaning it hasn't launched yet. The subject line ("UP TO 30% OFF BEDROOM & BATH") and the CTA ("SHOP THE SALE") both imply the sale is live right now. That contradiction is the defining problem of this email: it will either send clicks to a page where nothing is discounted, or frustrate readers who notice the teaser language. This is a campaign sequencing failure dressed up as a send.
- The second big issue is message saturation. Below the not-yet-live 30% bedroom promo, CB2 immediately stacks the live Furniture Event at 40% off — which is a *better* discount than the event they're ostensibly promoting. Then come two separate "$1,000 WIN" modules (app download, leave a review), then a personalized product grid, then CB2+, then a loyalty celebration offer. That's six distinct calls-to-action competing for the reader's attention. The "MORE FOR YOU" personalized grid is the strongest element in the email and it's buried under all of this.
- This is CB2's third or fourth email in roughly 48 hours, and the fourth time the Furniture Event has appeared in the inbox this week. The bedroom/bath angle is a legitimate category expansion, but running a teaser for an unstarted sale while an active, better-discounted sale is also in the email undermines both events.

## What's working

- **Headline hierarchy is clean at first glance** — "UP TO 30% OFF" reads immediately in the hero, the category is explicit, and the layout doesn't break.
- **Personalized "MORE FOR YOU" grid** is the right instinct — bedroom-adjacent products (nightstand, stools, folding table) align with the stated category focus.
- **CB2+ loyalty module** ("EARN 5% BACK") is present and scannable, which rewards members who are paying attention.
- **Subject line is concise** at 30 characters — tight, mobile-friendly, no truncation risk.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, typography holds up.

## What's weak

- **The sale isn't live, but the CTA says "SHOP THE SALE"** — "Starting Days Away" and "SHOP THE SALE" are mutually contradictory; this creates a trust-eroding experience on click.
- **30% off is undercut by the email directly below it** — the Furniture Event at 40% off sits immediately under the bedroom hero; CB2 is out-discounting its own new event in the same send.
- **Four competing modules destroy focus** — 30% bedroom + 40% furniture + $1,000 app win + $1,000 review win is four separate campaigns fighting for the same click.
- **Third email in ~48 hours** — CB2 is running near-daily sends this week; this one will land during inbox fatigue, and it's weaker than the "THINGS OF QUALITY" editorial or the "UP TO 40% OFF ENDS TODAY" urgency sends from earlier this week.
- **No deadline on the primary offer** — the hero 30% off has no stated end date, which removes purchase pressure; compare to the July 22 "ENDS TODAY" send, which at least gave a reason to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't tease a not-yet-live sale with a live CTA.** Either hold the bedroom/bath send until the event is actually live (so "SHOP THE SALE" lands somewhere real), or reframe the hero as a countdown/preview ("BEDROOM & BATH SALE STARTS FRIDAY — ADD TO WISHLIST") with a wishlist or reminder CTA instead.
- 2. **Strip the Furniture Event module out.** If the goal is to launch a new bedroom category event, don't muddy the email with the live, better-discounted furniture sale. They're competing messages.
- 3. **Cut the two $1,000 win modules to the footer.** The app download sweepstakes and review incentive are secondary housekeeping messages; they're killing the hero's momentum. One small text link in the footer is enough.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the 30% offer** once it's live — a "Starts Friday, runs through August 3" or similar gives the reader a reason to set a mental bookmark.
- 5. **Elevate the personalized grid** — move "MORE FOR YOU" up above the CB2+/app modules. That's the most conversion-relevant unit in the email and it's buried.

## Technical Audit — CB2 "UP TO 30% OFF BEDROOM & BATH"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML Transitional table-based email with VML Outlook conditionals and custom web fonts. The HTML source contains several infrastructure issues including a template artifact comment, malformed font URLs, and an accessibility-hostile viewport configuration.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before CTA and footer links are visible. The following is flagged from what is present:

- **Cross-brand font CDN dependency**: Four `@font-face` declarations load from `www.crateandbarrel.com` (not `cb2.com`). If that domain has a CDN incident or SSL issue, font loading silently fails in CB2-branded email. Not a tracking issue per se, but a resilience dependency across brand domains.
- No tracking pixels or UTM-parameterized links are visible in the truncated source — these must be verified in the full render.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta (line: `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />`)** — Blocks user-initiated zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Most modern iOS/Android browsers override this, but it remains a declared accessibility regression.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` applied to `*` selector** — The `*{}` rule in `<style>` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` globally. This suppresses system-level font scaling on all WebKit clients, including VoiceOver users who rely on Dynamic Type. The `body` rule separately sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:100%`, creating a specificity conflict where the `*` rule wins for all non-`body` elements.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — Cascades to all elements including `<p>`, `<td>`, and `<a>`. Can clip descenders in certain Outlook versions where `mso-line-height-rule: exactly` is also set (which it is, in the same rule).

**Template artifact comment left in production:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
Misspelled ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") and left in the deployed HTML. This is a build-process failure — the template was not cleaned before send.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declaration for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — Three separate `@font-face` blocks declare the same `font-family: "Lineto Brown Sub"` family. The first uses only `.eot`/`.woff`, the second and third split `woff2` by weight. This creates redundant download triggers in non-Outlook clients; the first block's `.eot`/`.woff` sources will be fetched unnecessarily on modern clients alongside the `woff2` blocks.

**Malformed font URLs with double slashes:**
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Four URLs contain `//` in the path segment (not the protocol). HTTP servers typically normalize this, but it is technically a malformed path and will fail on strict servers or proxies. Indicates copy-paste error in the template.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px`** — Targets `414px` (legacy iPhone 6/7/8 width) with a 4px buffer. Devices with 390px viewports (iPhone 14/15) fall between this and the 690px desktop breakpoint and receive neither responsive ruleset.
