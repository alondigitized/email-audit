# Up to 20% off our Back-to-School Sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids running a genuine back-to-school send, and it's the most kitchen-sink email I've seen from this family of brands yet — hero sale banner, six-tile school-supply grid, a "focus zone" desk/chair vignette, a fall-arrivals push, a "we love these for you" product rail, a credit card pitch, an app download pitch, and a Design Desk pitch, all stacked in one scroll. Compare to "Something special is up to 60% off! Shop clearance" (2026-07-26, 8.00/10), which was also a mega-email but at least kept everything under one clearance umbrella — this one is back-to-school in the subject but pivots to unrelated fall-arrivals and baby-gear content halfway down.
- The top of the email has a real, visible bug: the small header ribbon reads "up to 50% off summer sale →" directly above a hero banner that says "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off." Those are two different sales with two different discount amounts stacked on top of each other in the first screen a reader sees. That's the kind of thing that makes a subscriber distrust the offer before they've even scrolled.
- The back-to-school category grid (backpacks, lunch boxes, duffel bags, pencil cases, food storage, water bottles) is clean and well-photographed and is the strongest asset in the email. But the email loses its own thread after that — judgment: solid category merchandising undercut by a messaging collision at the top and scope creep by the bottom.

## What's working

- The 6-tile back-to-school category grid (backpacks, lunch boxes, duffel bags, pencil cases, food storage, water bottles) is well-shot, evenly cropped, and instantly scannable.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $29!" gives a second concrete, specific offer with a real price anchor.
- The "we love these for you" rail shows real prices and strikethrough discount pricing (e.g., $54.60 → $43.20), which builds credibility.
- No broken images, lorem ipsum, or overlapping text anywhere in the render — the layout itself executes cleanly.

## What's weak

- The header ribbon says "up to 50% off summer sale" while the hero directly below says "back-to-school sale: up to 20% off" — two conflicting offers in the first screen.
- The email drifts far from its own subject: after the school-supply grid it pivots to a bedroom/desk vignette, then fall arrivals, then baby gear (strollers, car seats, a nursery chair) that has nothing to do with "back-to-school."
- Two nearly identical "Shop the Sale" buttons stacked back to back under the desk/chair image is redundant and dilutes the CTA.
- The credit card and app-download modules push the email length well past what a back-to-school shopper needs to scroll through to find relevant product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the header/hero mismatch first — pick one offer (20% off back-to-school, since that's the subject and hero) and drop the "50% off summer sale" ribbon entirely; a reader who sees two different percentages in two seconds stops trusting either one.
- 2. Cut the email to back-to-school-relevant content only: category grid, focus-zone desks, fall arrivals (tied to "new for the school year"), and end it there — move credit card, app download, and baby-gear cross-sell to a separate send.
- 3. Collapse the two stacked "Shop the Sale" buttons under the desk image into one.
- 4. Add a deadline to the hero ("Sale ends [date]") — right now there's no urgency signal anywhere in the visible copy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back-to-school sale: up to 20% off backpacks & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Their new backpack is 20% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Backpacks, lunch boxes, and desks for the new school year — all on sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock the whole backpack, from lunch box to desk chair.`
