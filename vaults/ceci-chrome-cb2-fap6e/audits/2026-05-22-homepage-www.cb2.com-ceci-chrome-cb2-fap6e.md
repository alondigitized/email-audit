# Homepage snapshot · www.cb2.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is genuinely my store — this is where I go when I want furniture that has an opinion about itself. Right now they're in full Memorial Day Sale mode, which I literally had on my calendar. The brand is doing a lot of things right — timely, loyalty hooks visible, sale feels real — but the first thing I see when the page loads is an email capture modal that buries the entire hero. Not a great opening move.

## What's working

- **"DOUBLE REWARDS IS ON" promo strip**: I clocked this before the modal even loaded. Immediate signal that my CB2 Insider membership has extra value right now — that's a reason to stay.
- **"FINAL WEEKEND: NEW ITEMS ADDED"**: Smart copy. "New items added" tells me the sale has been refreshed and I shouldn't assume I've already seen everything. Gives me a real reason to dig in.
- **"UP TO 30% OFF"**: Specific percentage, visible, legible. I can math that against whatever I've been eyeing.
- **"THE COOLEST PEOPLE WE KNOW" editorial module**: Partially visible at the bottom — that headline is peak CB2 editorial voice. Irreverent without trying too hard. It's why I chose this brand over West Elm.
- **Nav structure**: Sofas, Seating, Bedroom — my categories are one tap away. No hunting.

## What's weak

- **The modal is a dark pattern**: It fires immediately on page load and blocks everything I came to see. I opened this site from a Memorial Day sale email. The first thing I hit is another email form. That's friction before I've seen a single product.
- **Phone number is required in the modal**: I'm tech-comfortable but giving my mobile number for a 15% off full-price offer — during an active sale — is a hard no. Required field makes it worse.
- **The asterisk on "FULL-PRICE ITEMS\*"**: A 15% discount on full-price items, during a 30% off sale, is almost certainly useless for everything in my cart. The asterisk is doing a lot of heavy exclusion work that I'll only discover at checkout.
- **Hero imagery doesn't match CB2's edge**: An outdoor pool daybed in warm wood tones reads Restoration Hardware Outdoor. I want matte black, velvet, chrome — the dark moody interiors that made me choose CB2 in the first place. This hero could live on any mid-range furniture site.
- **No trust signals visible**: No review counts, no returns callout, no clear free-shipping threshold I can actually read in the screenshot.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the modal to exit-intent or 30+ seconds**: Let me see the sale I came for first. Capture my email when I'm about to leave, not when I just arrived.
- **Swap the hero to a dark-aesthetic interior shot**: Pull a moody living room with CB2's velvet sectional, architectural pendant, matte black side table — the stuff that actually separates this brand from Crate & Barrel. Speak to the customer who picked CB2 on purpose.
- **Make the modal email-only**: Drop the phone number requirement. The conversion lift from a lower-friction ask will outpace whatever SMS list you're building by requiring it.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold legibly**: If there's a number in that top strip, it needs to be readable at mobile scale — not buried in 10px nav text that disappears into the header.
