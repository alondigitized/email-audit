# Everything in stock now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "Everything in stock now" is CB2's availability-reassurance pivot after June 23rd's "selling fast" scarcity send — a logical strategic counter-move in theory. Where that earlier send created urgency through shortage, this one tries to flip the frame: we've got inventory, come shop. The problem is the body never makes good on the subject's implied promise. The hero leads with "SEIZE THE MOMENT / WAREHOUSE SALE" — generic urgency copy that could have run two weeks ago — not with any callout of what's actually been restocked or why "now" is the moment to act. Subject and body aren't having the same conversation.
- Visually, this is one of CB2's cleaner sends in the current sale cycle. The three-category tile structure — Accent Chairs, Bedroom, Storage — gives the eye clear direction and is a significant improvement over the congested three-promotion stack in the June 25th "FLOOR SAMPLE SALE" send. The warm wood credenza lifestyle shot in the hero is CB2 at its most confident aesthetically. The "MORE FOR YOU" product grid at the bottom adds legitimate personalization depth.
- Bottom line: a structurally sound email undermined by a subject that promises a specific restocking angle the body never delivers on. Eight sends into the same sale, "everything in stock" needed to actually mean something. It doesn't, and that's the miss.

## What's working

- Three-category tile structure (Accent Chairs, Bedroom, Storage) gives the email focus without the multi-promotion clutter that hurt the June 25th send.
- Warm wood credenza hero image is the strongest lifestyle creative CB2 has run in this sale cycle.
- The availability frame ("in stock now") is a smart strategic counter to the "selling fast" scarcity play from June 23rd — even if it's underexecuted.
- 50% off discount is visible and concrete deeper in the email.
- "MORE FOR YOU" recommendation grid adds personalized pull at scroll depth.

## What's weak

- The subject's restocking angle ("in stock now") goes completely unreinforced in the body — "SEIZE THE MOMENT" is leftover urgency copy from a different brief.
- No specific product hero or restocked-item callout to justify the subject's implied promise.
- No deadline visible anywhere in the email; "now" in the subject implies immediacy but there's no date anchor to convert that into action.
- Category tiles (Accent Chairs, Bedroom, Storage) feel like week-one sale content — not differentiated enough for the eighth send in this sale cycle.
- Open fatigue is real at this cadence; "Everything in stock now" doesn't do enough to break through against the prior seven sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject promise mean something in the body.** If "in stock now" is the hook, the hero needs to deliver — a grid of 3–4 specific items with "BACK IN STOCK" callouts, not a generic "WAREHOUSE SALE" banner. Shoppers who got burned by sold-out items will click for specific availability; they won't click for a recycled hero.
- 2. **Replace "SEIZE THE MOMENT" with copy that closes the gap to the subject.** Something like "BACK IN STOCK / BEFORE IT'S GONE AGAIN" bridges subject to body and earns the urgency framing.
- 3. **Add a concrete deadline.** "Warehouse Sale ends July 4th" in the hero or above the CTA turns the vague "now" into a real conversion lever.
- 4. **Surface specific products.** After eight sends, category names alone are invisible. A 4-up product grid with actual item names, original prices, and sale prices would make this a genuinely useful email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back in stock: the warehouse sale items you missed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `These sold out — now they're back at 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Accent chairs, bedroom, storage — restocked and still on sale through July 4.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `What sold out in week one is back. Shop before it's gone again.`

## CB2 "Everything in stock now" — Technical Email Audit

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with VML/MSO conditional blocks for Outlook; head section contains a confirmed developer artifact and two accessibility/rendering defects. Body-level link, tracking, and compliance elements cannot be fully audited due to HTML truncation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. The following is confirmed from the `<head>`:

- **Cross-domain font dependency:** `HNW05` font family loads exclusively from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/…` (four `@font-face` declarations). If that domain has CSP restrictions, CDN rules, or goes down independently of cb2.com, CB2 emails silently fall back to system fonts with no CB2 control.
- **Double-slash path bug in font URLs:** Four `@font-face` src attributes use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/…` (double forward slash), e.g.:
  ```
  url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
  ```
  Most servers normalize this, but it is a malformed URL and should be corrected.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Zoom restricted (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility scanners. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or change to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue 2 — Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width:414px !important; … } }
```
The breakpoint is 418px but the forced width is 414px (iPhone SE width). Standard practice uses 480px or 600px. At 419px–479px viewport widths, the mobile layout never fires, potentially causing layout overflow on mid-size Android devices.

**Issue 3 — Duplicate/conflicting `@font-face` declarations:**
`"Lineto Brown Sub"` is declared four times — once with a single `.eot` src, then again for `woff2` at `font-weight: 400`, then for `woff2` at `font-weight: 600`. The first declaration (no weight specified, `.eot`-only) will win in non-MSO clients for weight 400, overriding the `woff2` version intended for that weight.
