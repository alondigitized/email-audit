# MIDSEASON SALE
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's second email on June 5th — sent the same day as "A well-collected home office" (5/10) — and it recycles the exact subject line from the June 3rd "MIDSEASON SALE" send (8/10). That's a meaningful problem before even opening: a subscriber who saw that subject two days ago has no reason to believe this is a new message. The email body itself is competent: a clean editorial hero, a product grid, and a cross-sell into interior design services are all present and rendering without issues. But the creative is doing no work to justify a second look. The "$1,000" offer in the design services section is the most differentiated element in the email and it's buried below the fold.
- The interior design services module is CB2's structural advantage over pure-play retailers and it should be leading sends, not sitting third in the scroll. When you've already sent "MIDSEASON SALE" twice in 72 hours, the only thing that can save the second send is a *new* hook — a specific discount, a deadline, a category angle. None of those appear in the hero.
- Bottom line: this is a re-send that forgot to change its subject line or sharpen its offer. The cadence burn is real and the brand history shows CB2 has been living in this sales-mode tunnel for nearly two weeks straight.

## What's working

- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout blowout, no overlapping text — the editorial photography holds up well at smaller widths.
- **Interior design services cross-sell.** "Book Your Free Appointment" is a genuinely differentiated CTA that no pure-play furniture retailer can match.
- **"More For You" product grid.** Multiple lower-funnel entry points with individual product thumbnails give browsers something to click across different categories.
- **Visual hierarchy in the hero.** Eye lands on the headline, then "SHOP NOW" — no ambiguity about what the email is asking you to do.

## What's weak

- **Exact repeat subject line.** "MIDSEASON SALE" ran June 3rd. Sending it again June 5th signals inbox fatigue and signals that nothing is new.
- **No specific discount percentage anywhere in the hero.** A Midseason Sale with no "up to X% off" visible in the primary panel asks the subscriber to take the deal on faith.
- **Two CB2 emails in one day.** "A well-collected home office" already hit inboxes today; this send competes with itself and accelerates list fatigue.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is the laziest possible verb.** After multiple Midseason Sale sends, it has no motivating power — no urgency, no category, no reason to click *now* vs. later.
- **No deadline visible.** The sale has been running since at least May 30th. Without an end date in the hero, there's no credible reason to act on this send versus waiting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a deadline.** This is the highest-leverage single change. "Sale ends Sunday" in the hero converts passive browsers. Without it, every send in this arc is "shop whenever you feel like it."
- 2. **Show the discount number.** "Up to 40% off" (or whatever the real figure is) belongs in the hero — not just "MIDSEASON SALE." The number is why people click.
- 3. **Rename the subject.** The current subject is a direct duplicate of June 3rd's. Any differentiation — category, deadline, percent — saves the send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your room. On sale right now.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free design session + Midseason Sale picks curated for you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The CB2 Midseason Sale closes Sunday — shop before it's gone.`
- 4. **Elevate the design services module.** Move "Book Your Free Appointment" + the "$1,000" offer above the product grid. It's the most defensible offer in the email and currently one scroll below where most subscribers bail.
- 5. **Suppress or rest the list for one day.** Two emails in one inbox day on the same brand trains subscribers to ignore the second one. This send would perform better sent tomorrow as the only CB2 message.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — CB2 "MIDSEASON SALE"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based email with responsive media queries. Three infrastructure-level issues confirmed from source: double-slash font URLs causing redirect overhead, a live template placeholder comment, and cross-brand font origin dependency.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Double-slash URLs in `@font-face` declarations** — three font requests will hit a 301 redirect before resolving:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Each redirect adds ~50–150ms latency on initial render. Single-slash paths in the three preceding `@font-face` blocks (same stylesheet) are correct — this is inconsistent within the same `<style>` block.

**UTM parameters** — HTML is truncated before CTA links appear; UTM coverage could not be verified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Unremoved template placeholder comment** (production defect):
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is a developer scaffolding comment with a typo ("IMPUT" vs "INPUT") that shipped in production. No functional impact, but exposes internal tooling artifacts and indicates a QA gap.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user-initiated zoom:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Low-vision users who rely on pinch-zoom are affected.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` applied via universal selector**:
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
The `none` value (rather than `100%`) suppresses OS-level font scaling. Combined with `max-height: 1000000px` on every element, this is an unusually aggressive reset that risks overriding accessibility accommodations set by the user's device.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 418px`** — non-standard threshold. Devices with viewport widths of 419–479px (e.g., iPhone SE landscape, some Android mid-range) will not receive mobile-optimized layout styles. Standard breakpoint is 480px.

**Cross-brand font origin dependency** — four `@font-face` declarations load from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`:
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-…woff2")
```
If Crate & Barrel's CDN or the `/webfonts/` path changes, CB2 emails silently degrade to fallback fonts. This is an infrastructure coupling risk, not a rendering issue today.

**Duplicate `@font-face` family name without weight specifiers** — "Lineto Brown Sub" is declared four times. The first declaration has no `font-weight` or `font-style` descriptor, which means it acts as the default catch-all and may shadow the weighted variants in non-Outlook clients.
