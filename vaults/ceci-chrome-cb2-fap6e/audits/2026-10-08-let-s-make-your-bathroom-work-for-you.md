# Let’s make your bathroom work for you →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a Design Desk service email. A strong sofa-sale banner sits on top, and the email beneath it is about bathrooms. The "up to 20% off bestselling sofas & sectionals" strip leads, but the subject promises bathroom help. The hero "breathe new life into your bathroom" never mentions the discount. The subject and the first thing the eye lands on are selling two different things.
- The body is polished: three numbered steps (foundation, essentials, natural touches), each with rated products. It then repeats the sofa sale, adds Affirm financing, an app download and a second Design Desk appointment CTA. That's roughly seven distinct asks in one send. The email works as a browsing lookbook but not as a focused campaign.
- Cadence matters too. This is at least the fourth Crate & Barrel send I've audited on 2026-10-08, after the clearance sends and "Look what Santa just delivered…" (6/10). A subscriber would see a crowded inbox. Verdict: well made, but unfocused and one send too many today.

## What's working

- The numbered three-step structure (balance & function, plush towels, natural elements) gives the bathroom story a clear order.
- Product tiles show star ratings and "Exclusive" tags, which add social proof and scarcity.
- The sofa sale has a specific figure ("up to 20% off") and a clear Shop Now button right at the top.
- "Book a Free Appointment" is a concrete, low-friction service CTA that fits the Design Desk brand.
- The render is clean. I see no broken images or overlapping text, and the brand voice is consistent.

## What's weak

- The top banner (sofas) and the hero (bathroom) pull in different directions, so the subject doesn't match the first thing you see.
- There are too many CTAs and modules: the Design Desk appointment twice, Shop All Bath, a sofa repeat, Affirm, a recommendations grid, the app and a Crate & Barrel Home card. No single action stands out.
- The bathroom content has no price or promotion. The only discount is on sofas, and it carries an asterisk.
- There is no deadline anywhere, so there's no reason to act today.
- The email is very long, and the second half is generic filler (sofa repeat, Affirm, app) that dilutes the theme.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. Either promote a bathroom offer (such as towels or bath hardware) or cut the bathroom editorial and lead with the sofa event. Don't run both.
- 2. Move the sofa banner out of the top slot, or match the subject to it. Put a bathroom-specific offer or appointment CTA in the hero.
- 3. Cut the second half to one reinforcing module (the plush towels grid) plus one footer CTA. Drop the repeated sofa block, the Affirm and app modules, and the second appointment banner.
- 4. Add a real deadline to whichever offer you keep, such as "ends Sunday".
- 5. Hold the send, or space it away from the other same-day promos.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is built on a Marigold/Mapp-style XHTML template with VML/MSO fallbacks. It has 82 tracked links and heavy image-alt gaps (about 50 images flagged). The QA header checks (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) came back empty, but that most likely reflects the AgentMail relay, not the sender.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- 82 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they use click-redirect domains. Destination validity, redirect chains and UTM preservation are **unverified**, not confirmed good or bad.
- Five identical `https://sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif?s=56aaa278…&n=1` through `n=5` pixels are in the body. This is a LiveRamp (rlcdn) third-party sync pixel, fired five times with only `n` varying. It adds third-party requests and a privacy/consent surface. It also adds nothing to rendering.
- Tracking pixels `https://mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif?mi_u=…` (open pixel) and `https://dv.crateandbarrel.com/o/e35bb580-…?mi_cid=…&mi_mid=…` carry per-recipient tokens (`mi_u`, `mi_mid`). Both have no `alt`. These are first-party, so this is informational only.
- The `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png?mi_u=…` images (7 of them) look like per-recipient dynamic or recommendation images. They are server-rendered on open, so they fail if the image host is slow or blocked.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **Missing alt text, confirmed by QA:** about 50 `<img>` elements have no `alt` attribute. Examples:
  - `image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/fe9013727564027a7c/m/47/bbf6f9d4-…png`
  - `s7d5.scene7.com/…20250117_CB_DesignDesk_Bath_Renderings_PI/…`
  - about 30 `…/m/1/*.jpg` images
  - 7 `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/*.png` images
  - the `.gif` tracking pixels
  
  If these images are blocked, the email shows broken-image boxes. Screen readers will read out the filenames or URLs. A missing `alt` is different from `alt=""`. Decorative images and pixels should carry an explicit `alt=""`.
- The `scene7` hero image URL contains `2025/01_Core/20250117…`. This is an old asset path, so the creative may be reused from January 2025. It is a staleness signal, not a defect.
- `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1>` blocks pinch-zoom, an accessibility (WCAG 1.4.4) concern.
- `<title>Crate & Barrel</title>` has an unescaped `&`. It is tolerated by most parsers but is invalid XHTML, which the doctype claims to be.
- The head has duplicate `<meta name="format-detection">` tags (3 variants). It has both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` and `<meta charset>`. It also has the leftover template comment `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. All are harmless but sloppy.
- `p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}` is a global rule that forces centered text for every `<p>` unless it is overridden.
- `*{ line-height:100%; max-height:1000000px }` is a global rule that can collapse line spacing in some clients.
- Only the first part of the HTML was available because the source was truncated. Body-level issues such as table `role="presentation"`, heading structure, link text and tap-target size could not be assessed.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens such as `{{…}}`, `%%…%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]` appear in the visible source or the subject. The per-recipient `mi_u` and `mi_mid` parameters are populated.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** both are flagged missing. The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip or fail to capture them. **Unconfirmed** as a sender defect. Check the raw message headers before treating it as real. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The `From` domain is `mail.crateandbarrel.com`, a sending subdomain. Alignment should be verified from raw headers.
- The truncated source does not show the footer, so the in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address could not be verified. Neither is confirmed present or absent.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
Unable to assess. All 82 links go through click-tracking redirects that were not probed, so final landing URLs and UTM parameters were never observed. A follow-up should resolve a sample of the redirects, especially the primary CTA, to confirm the landing page and UTM tagging.

**7. Recommendations**
1. Re-run header capture from the raw MIME message to settle the List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post and Authentication-Results warnings. These are the only compliance items that could be real blockers.
2. Add `alt` to every `<img>`: descriptive text for content images, and `alt=""` for pixels and decorative images.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Drop the duplicate `rlcdn.com` pixels, or consolidate them to one, unless there is a deliberate need for all five.
5. Escape the `&` in `<title>` and clean up the duplicate head meta tags.
6. Resolve the redirects for the main CTAs to check the destination pages and UTM parameters.
7. Pipeline note: the QA run reported a 57% pass rate, 0 issues and 3 warnings. The alt-text warnings come back as a single list item per image. Counting them as one category would give a more useful score.
