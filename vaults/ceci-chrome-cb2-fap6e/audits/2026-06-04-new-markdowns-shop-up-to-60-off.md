# NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's Summer Clearance refresh — same "up to 60% off" ceiling as the June 2 send, but the "NEW MARKDOWNS!" hook attempts to justify another clearance drop two days later. The hero is clean and lands the offer immediately: a warm lifestyle bedroom shot with the discount prominent above a clear "Shop Now" CTA. That's the strongest part of the email.
- The problem is what comes after. The email sprawls — clearance hero, then a product grid, then a Crate & Kids section, then an outdoor lifestyle block, then individual product listings, then an app download pitch, then TheDesignDesk editorial. By the time you're three scrolls in, the clearance urgency has completely evaporated. The mid-email pivot to Crate & Kids content is particularly jarring when the subject promises markdowns.
- This is a decent clearance send undercut by a kitchen-sink structure and a cadence problem: two "up to 60% off" emails in 48 hours erodes the urgency that "MARKDOWNS" is supposed to convey.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate and unambiguous** — "up to 60% off / The Summer Clearance Event" lands above the fold with a clean Shop Now button; no guessing required.
- **Product grid below the hero** shows breadth of the sale without the subscriber having to click through blind.
- **Seasonal lifestyle imagery** is on-brand and warm — the bedroom shot and the outdoor furniture section both feel summer-appropriate.
- **Sender name "Crate & Barrel SALE"** signals promotional intent before the subject is even read — right subscriber expectation.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue** — the June 2 send already ran "UP TO 60% OFF! Shop our top deals →" with the same ceiling; "NEW MARKDOWNS!" feels like a re-skinned repeat rather than a genuine escalation.
- **No deadline anywhere visible** — "Summer Clearance Event" has no end date or countdown, which removes the one lever that would force action today vs. tomorrow.
- **Email scope is too wide** — clearance, kids' furniture, outdoor seating, app download, and TheDesignDesk editorial all compete for attention; each dilutes the others.
- **Crate & Kids mid-email block is off-target** — a subscriber responding to a markdown email about home furnishings doesn't need a children's gift delivery upsell in the middle of the scroll.
- **"up to 60% off" without any anchor price** — no "was $X, now $Y" example in the hero means the offer stays abstract.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline or urgency signal to the hero** — "Ends Sunday" or a 3-day countdown stamp does more work than "NEW MARKDOWNS!" in the subject. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Kill or quarantine the Crate & Kids block** — move it to its own dedicated send or push it to the footer. It breaks the clearance momentum.
- 3. **Anchor the hero to a specific category or product** — "New markdowns on sofas, dining, and rugs — up to 60% off" is more clickable than a blank ceiling.
- 4. **Trim the app download section** — a clearance email is not the right moment for app acquisition; it's a conversion moment; the app pitch interrupts it.
- 5. **Show struck-through original prices in the product grid** — even at thumbnail size, was/now pricing converts better than a flat discount banner.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Zoom disabled via viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[HIGH] Global text-size-adjust disabled**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to `*`, not `body`. Blocks OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Change to `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }`.

**[MEDIUM] Inverted image max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }  /* declared first */
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }  /* declared second */
```
At ≥768px both rules match; the cascade resolves to `640px` (last-wins), overriding the intended `600px`. The wider breakpoint should be declared last, or consolidate to a single rule.

**[LOW] Dark mode explicitly blocked**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Forces light-mode rendering in Apple Mail, Gmail app, and Outlook. Images and white backgrounds may invert badly without fallback. Consider `content="light dark"` with tested dark-mode color overrides.

**[LOW] Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
`format-detection` appears four times — once combined (`telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no`) and then individually for `date`, `address`, and `telephone`. No functional error, but indicates template is not being linted before send.
