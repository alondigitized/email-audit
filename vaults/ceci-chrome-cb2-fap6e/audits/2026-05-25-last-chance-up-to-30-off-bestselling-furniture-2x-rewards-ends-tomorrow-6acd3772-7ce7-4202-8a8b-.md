# Last chance! Up to 30% off bestselling furniture + 2x Rewards ends TOMORROW!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's late-stage Memorial Day push — same "2x Rewards ends TOMORROW" beat the brand has pounded since at least May 23, now packaged as a furniture-specific send. The layout is clean and well-structured: a clear hero headline, a four-item product grid above the fold, a sofa vignette, and a TheDesignDesk editorial section below. Nothing is broken.
- The critical tension is the offer itself. Yesterday's Crate & Kids send (May 25) was headlining 50% off, and last week's C&B sends were also running 50% on select categories. This email steps back to "up to 30% off bestselling furniture" — a softer ceiling that will register as a downgrade to any subscriber who has been reading the week's sends. Combined with the seventh-or-eighth-consecutive urgency email in the sequence, fatigue is real.
- The bottom line: a competent, mistake-free promo email that is doing everything right mechanically but is undermined by offer regression and send-frequency exhaustion.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is immediate.** "The Memorial Day Event: up to 30% off furniture" lands in the first scroll with a visible Shop CTA — no ambiguity about what this email is for.
- **Product grid is well-merchandised.** Four furniture items with prices visible above the fold give shoppers something to click directly without hunting.
- **2x Rewards is consistently surfaced.** The loyalty hook appears in the subject, and the email body reinforces it — good omnichannel cohesion for members.
- **TheDesignDesk editorial section adds retention value.** The content block below the commerce offers a reason to stay on the email beyond the immediate deal.
- **No render bugs visible.** Layout is clean, images loaded, no broken-image gaps or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Offer regression from the week's own sends.** "Up to 30% off" follows days of "up to 50% off" from the same brand family — that's a step backward that active subscribers will notice.
- **Subject line is 74 characters and cluttered.** "Last chance! Up to 30% off bestselling furniture + 2x Rewards ends TOMORROW!" stacks three competing messages and blows the mobile-truncation window well before "TOMORROW."
- **Two different discount figures appear in the email.** A "25% off furniture" value appears lower in the layout alongside the "up to 30% off" headline — this creates confusion rather than clarity about what's actually on offer.
- **"Last chance" rings hollow at send #7+ in a week.** Every email this week has been "final weekend," "ends tomorrow," or "last chance." The urgency word has been depleted.
- **No personalization visible.** No first name, no "based on your browsing" signal — this reads as a pure blast to the list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the offer story.** Pick one number — 30% or 25% — and use it everywhere in the email. The dual-number confusion in a single send erodes trust faster than a weaker headline would.
- 2. **Shorten and sharpen the subject.** The current subject tries to carry three messages at once. Drop the brand-name clutter, commit to the single strongest hook:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 30% off our bestselling sofas & tables`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Furniture sale ends tonight — your 2x Rewards too`
- 3. **Lead the preheader with the product, not a repeat of urgency.** The subject already has "last chance" and "TOMORROW" — the preheader should give a product signal that makes opening feel worthwhile.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, dining tables, storage — all on sale through midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sectional you saved is 30% off right now.`
- 4. **Compress the send cadence for next campaign.** Seven+ promo emails in five days is the root problem no single send can fix — a tighter 3-touch arc (launch → midpoint → close) would have preserved urgency for this last-chance moment.
- 5. **Add even one social-proof signal to the product grid.** A star rating or "X sold this week" under the hero items would address the absent reviews/social-proof signal.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta blocks user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries — wider breakpoint gets smaller cap**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At ≥768px both rules apply; cascade order means 600px wins. At 640–767px only 640px cap applies. Result: wider screens get a *smaller* max-width. Both blocks are also labeled `/* For desktop: */` which confirms this is likely a copy-paste ordering error. Fix: either remove the 640px rule or reorder so 768px comes first.

**Global `text-size-adjust: none` via wildcard selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Blanket suppression on `*` can prevent legitimate OS-level text scaling for accessibility. Should be scoped to `body` only.

**Dark mode not declared**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
`light` only. Apple Mail and recent Outlook versions will force-invert without a `light dark` declaration. If dark mode is intentionally unsupported, this is correct — document it explicitly.

**Duplicate and redundant `format-detection` meta tags**
The combined tag and three individual tags cover the same attributes:
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
...
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
Not a rendering failure, but bloat. Remove the three individual tags.

**Duplicate charset declaration**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Redundant; keep only `<meta charset="utf-8">` (the HTML5 form).
