# BACK IN STOCK
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a deliberate tonal reset after six consecutive high-intensity sale emails — CB2 has been running urgency-stacked countdowns since at least July 5, and this "BACK IN STOCK" send is the first since the sale cadence that doesn't lead with a percentage off or a ticking timer. The "SEIZE the Moment" hero with that rich burgundy armchair is on-brand and aesthetically confident.
- The problem is execution coherence: the subject says "BACK IN STOCK" but the email body is essentially a category browse — BEDROOM, SECTIONALS, and what appears to be a room-refresh featured section. Subscribers who opened expecting "that sofa I saved finally came back" are going to land in a general catalog and feel mildly misled. The subject is a promise the email doesn't clearly keep.
- The $5,000 featured section mid-scroll is the most interesting creative choice in the email, but it's sandwiched between category headers and hard to parse quickly. The product grid at the bottom is small and unlikely to drive clicks on its own. Competent execution of a slightly misfired concept.

## What's working

- The "SEIZE the Moment" hero is visually the strongest image CB2 has led with in this entire sale run — the deep burgundy chair reads as premium and seasonal without screaming "clearance."
- "BACK IN STOCK" is a clean subject-line reset from the repetitive "SALE ENDS" cadence — it earns the open because subscribers are trained to hunt for wishlist items.
- Category sectioning (BEDROOM, SECTIONALS) gives structure to a multi-offer email and lets different segments self-select quickly.
- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, no junk in the layout.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a restock alert; body delivers a catalog browse — the two are not the same email, and the disconnect erodes trust.
- After a week of 50% off language, no price hook (not even a small one) makes this email feel like a step backward in value for a sale-conditioned subscriber base.
- The $5,000 mid-section is ambiguous at a glance — it's unclear whether this is a product bundle, a room design service, or a spend threshold — and that ambiguity kills clicks.
- No single unambiguous hero CTA button above the fold — the eye gets the mood but nowhere to go immediately.
- Seven sends in four days (including this one) from the same brand is inbox fatigue territory; cadence itself becomes a miss at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the subject match what's actually in the email** — either rename the email to "New Arrivals" / "Just Restocked: Bedroom + Sectionals" or audit the actual back-in-stock items and build a product-specific notification instead of a catalog send. The mismatch is the single biggest lift opportunity.
- 2. **Add one price anchor** — even a "from $299" or "members save 10% today" signal in the hero area would retain the post-sale subscriber without re-running a full sitewide promo.
- 3. **Clarify the $5,000 section** — if it's a room design service or curated bundle, give it a label and a CTA. If it's a featured product, show the product.
- 4. **Add a single hero CTA button** — "Shop What's Back" or "See New Arrivals" below the SEIZE headline would anchor the click before scrolling.
- 5. **Consider a rest day** — six sends in four days is aggressive; the back-in-stock pivot would land harder if subscribers had one day of silence first.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just restocked: bestselling bedroom + sectionals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back by demand — the pieces you kept watching`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our most-saved sofas, beds, and chairs — now available again.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Restock alert: these sold out fast last time.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with MSO conditionals and is structurally sound for broad client compatibility. However, several pre-send QA failures are present: a developer placeholder comment shipped in production, duplicate `@font-face` declarations with malformed URLs, and a viewport `maximum-scale` constraint that blocks user zoom.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML source is truncated before product or CTA links appear. The following is flagged from what is visible:

- No UTM parameters are observable in the truncated portion. If product image links and CTAs do not carry `utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=...`, attribution will be lost (see §6).
- No tracking pixel or open-pixel tag is visible in the `<head>` or early `<body>` in the provided excerpt.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on universal selector**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
Applied to every element rather than just `body`. Overrides user browser accessibility settings globally.

**Duplicate `@font-face` declarations for "Lineto Brown Sub"** — three separate `@font-face` blocks use the same `font-family` name with overlapping `font-weight` ranges, creating unpredictable font resolution across clients:
- Block 1: no weight specified (eot + woff2 + woff sources)
- Block 2: `font-weight: 400`
- Block 3: `font-weight: 600`

**Malformed double-slash font URLs**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
`//` in the path (after domain, not as protocol-relative) is technically valid via HTTP redirect normalization, but can produce 301s in strict CDN configurations and adds unnecessary round-trips. Four affected URLs.

**Conflicting desktop image `max-width` rules**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 100% !important } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 690px) { img { max-width: 690px } }
```
At ≥768px both rules apply; the `!important` on 100% overrides the 690px cap, making the 690px rule a dead rule for viewports ≥768px. Consolidate into a single breakpoint.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint**: `max-width: 418px` is unusual (vs. industry-standard 480px). Will not activate on devices with 420px–480px viewport widths (e.g., iPhone SE in landscape), leaving those users without mobile-optimized layout.

**Cross-brand font CDN dependency**: HNW05 webfonts are loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, not `cb2.com`:
```css
src: url("https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/1e07d600-3787-4517-bd3d-b140f9e54ec2.woff2")
```
CB2 email rendering depends on Crate & Barrel's CDN availability. Any Crate & Barrel CDN incident or CORS policy change breaks CB2 email typography.
