# Up to 60% off even more markdowns!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's clearance escalation play — the subject says "even more markdowns," the hero delivers "up to 60% off clearance," and a Father's Day deadline (order by 6/12) provides the one piece of genuine urgency. The structure is competent: clear hero, category tiles, personalized product row, app cross-sell, footer deal. Nothing breaks.
- The problem is cadence. This is the third Crate & Barrel family email on June 6 alone, following "17+ washable rugs" and "Weekend Forecast: Up to 30% off outdoor." The jump from 30% to 60% in the same day feels less like a real escalation and more like inbox carpet-bombing with bigger numbers. A subscriber who saw the morning send and didn't click has no new reason to click here — the creative is different but the emotional message is identical: "things are on sale."
- The Father's Day hook is the most actionable signal in the email but it's buried below the fold. Lead with that and this email works harder; bury it and it's just another clearance push.

## What's working

- Hero photography is aspirational and on-brand — moody, layered room styling consistent with the 6/6 outdoor send that scored 8/10.
- "Up to 60% off" headline is the highest discount headline in any recent send, immediately visible without scrolling.
- Father's Day order deadline (6/12) adds a credible, specific time-bound reason to act now.
- Product grid below the hero gives multiple entry points across categories (dining, lighting, accent chairs), reducing the risk of a single-SKU miss.
- "We love these for you" personalized row signals segmentation effort.

## What's weak

- "Even more markdowns" in the subject is a soft escalation cue with no new information — it's a sequel that doesn't tell you what changed.
- Father's Day urgency is below the fold, not in the subject or hero, so it does no work at the inbox level.
- "Shop Now" CTA is generic — it could be on any email from any brand.
- Third C&B family email today; open fatigue is a real risk and subscriber trust erodes with high-frequency same-day sends.
- No loyalty/member pricing or "members get X% more" — a missed lever Crate & Barrel has used in other sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move Father's Day deadline into the subject.** That's the one signal competitors don't own today. "Order by June 12 for Father's Day — up to 60% off clearance" gives a reason to open *and* a reason to click.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" with something specific** — "Shop Clearance" or "Shop Father's Day Gifts" puts a noun in the button.
- 3. **Suppress the second same-day send** or gate it to non-openers of the morning washable-rugs send. Two clearance emails in one day from the same brand family is a list-health risk.
- 4. **Surface one hero product at 60% off** instead of the abstract category grid — a specific item creates a "that's actually a good deal" moment that a percentage range never does.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Order by 6/12 — up to 60% off clearance for Dad`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last clearance call before Father's Day: up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, lighting, rugs — hundreds of markdowns, order by June 12`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Gifts he'll actually use, up to 60% off. Order by June 12.`

### 7. Recommendations

Issues 1–5 below are repeats from the June 4 Crate & Kids technical audit. They have not been applied to the shared template.

1. **Fix `maximum-scale=1`** — replace viewport meta with `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`. Restores pinch-to-zoom; required for WCAG 1.4.4 compliance.
2. **Scope `text-size-adjust`** — remove from the wildcard `*` selector; apply only to `body`. The current rule suppresses scaling across every element.
3. **Correct media query ordering** — place `min-width: 640px` before `min-width: 768px` so the narrower breakpoint is overridden by the wider one, not the reverse. The `max-width: 600px` desktop cap is currently unreachable.
4. **Strip the font placeholder comment** — either load the intended web font or delete `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. Do not ship template instructions in production HTML.
5. **Deduplicate `format-detection` meta tags** — keep only the single combined form; remove the three standalone tags.
6. **Audit full source** for UTM coverage on all CTAs, footer CAN-SPAM fields, and `alt` attributes on all product images before the next deployment cycle.
