# THE AUGUST SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- CB2 opens August with its biggest-swing play yet: a sitewide "UP TO 40% OFF" sale, stacked on top of a credit-card pitch, an app-download sweepstakes, a review-solicitation sweepstakes, and a six-product "more for you" grid — all in one scroll. It's the most commercially aggressive send in the recent run, and after five days of daily emails (8/1 editorial, 8/2 sweepstakes, 8/2 designer spotlight, 8/3 Goop editorial, 8/4 "ends tomorrow" bed & bath, 8/5 two-stack discount, 8/5 marble editorial), this is now the sixth-plus email in six days — cadence fatigue is real.
- The hero itself is clean and on-brand: big serif "THE AUGUST SALE," a clear 40%-off number, one unambiguous "SHOP THE SALE" button over a moody floral-and-furniture shot. That part works. But everything below the category grid is a grab-bag of unrelated asks (Reward Dollars card, app sweepstakes, review sweepstakes) competing with the actual sale for attention, which dilutes what should be a single-minded discount email.
- Net: a strong top half undercut by a bloated, unfocused back half and a subject line that doesn't even try to differentiate itself from the five sale-flavored sends already in this subscriber's inbox this week.

## What's working

- Hero is instantly legible: sale name, specific discount (40%), one clear button, all above the fold.
- Category grid (Furniture / Decor / Lighting / Shop the Sale) gives shoppers a fast path into their specific interest instead of dumping everyone on a generic sale page.
- "New & Best Selling — Lighting" section spotlights one specific hero product (the pendant) rather than just another category tile — good for click-through.
- "More for you" grid shows six real products with real prices, no lorem-ipsum, no broken images.
- Consistent CB2 visual identity (typography, neutral palette, photography style) throughout — nothing looks off-brand or broken.

## What's weak

- No urgency anywhere on the actual sale — no end date, no "ends tonight," which is a step back from 8/4's "ENDS TOMORROW" and 8/5's "two sales end tonight," both of which scored higher partly on urgency.
- The email tries to do five jobs at once (sale, credit card, app sweepstakes, review sweepstakes, product grid) — no single-minded focus, which hurts scannability on a long scroll.
- "Leave us a review, win $1,000" and "Download the app, win $1,000" sit back-to-back and read as filler asks rather than a considered content strategy.
- Subject "THE AUGUST SALE" is the least specific subject in this brand's recent run — no %, no category, no urgency, and it's the sixth-ish email in six days, so a generic sale name blends into the noise.
- Zero reviews/ratings/social proof anywhere despite literally asking subscribers to write reviews.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline to the hero.** "Up to 40% off" with no end date is the single biggest miss — CB2's own history shows "ends tomorrow"/"ends tonight" sends outperforming evergreen framing. Put a date or "through 8/X" directly under the hero.
- 2. **Cut the email in half.** Move the credit-card pitch, app sweepstakes, and review sweepstakes into separate, dedicated sends. A sale email competing with three unrelated asks dilutes the one CTA that actually drives revenue this week.
- 3. **Make the subject line earn the open.** "THE AUGUST SALE" tells the reader nothing they don't already expect from a retailer in August.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% Off Everything — Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The August Sale is live: up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on furniture, decor, and lighting — plus this week's lighting pick.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop 40% off sitewide before the weekend deals disappear.`
- 4. **Give "Shop the Sale" a more specific CTA.** Swap for category-anchored copy like "Shop Furniture Deals" on the category tiles instead of a repeated generic "Shop Now."
- 5. **Add a proof point.** One line of review count/star rating on the hero or lighting feature ("4.8★ from 1,200+ reviews") would do more for trust than asking for a review via sweepstakes.
