# Up to 40% off is on now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is pivoting from the concluded July 4th sale into a fresh campaign frame — "The Summer Edit" — with up to 40% off. The timing is the main liability: the brand just ran back-to-back 50% off emails through July 7, sent two deliberate reset emails on July 8 (back in stock, design services), and is now reopening a discount campaign at a *lower* ceiling. That 40%-after-50% optic will register with anyone who's been in this inbox all week.
- The email itself is clean and on-brand. The hero reads clearly, the outdoor collection gets a separate 50% off callout (the stronger hook), and "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" is smart post-sale copy. But the $10,000 review sweepstakes block spliced in mid-email breaks purchase intent at exactly the wrong moment, and there's zero urgency machinery — no end date, no countdown timer, nothing to move a hesitant buyer today versus next week.
- Net verdict: a competent new-campaign launch that suffers from a week of heavy sale context and a discount ceiling that feels like a markdown, not an upgrade.

## What's working

- **"THE SUMMER EDIT" is a clean campaign reset** — it signals a new chapter rather than another drip of the same July 4th promo.
- **Outdoor Collection up to 50% off** is the actual competitive hook, and surfacing it in the second panel is smart — it outperforms the 40% hero.
- **"NOT SOLD OUT, YET"** is benefit-forward, post-sale copy that signals scarcity without leaning on pressure tactics.
- **"MORE FOR YOU" personalized product grid** at the bottom adds relevant entry points and makes the email feel like it knows the recipient's preferences.
- **Visual hierarchy is intact** — plant-forward photography, minimal text clutter, and clear section separation throughout.

## What's weak

- **40% after 50% reads as a demotion** — the brand history makes this ceiling feel like a step back, even if it's technically a new campaign.
- **No urgency mechanics** — "is on now" in the subject with no end date or countdown in the body gives no reason to act today.
- **$10,000 review sweepstakes block mid-email** is a jarring tonal break; it interrupts purchase intent at the critical scroll depth.
- **"SHOP THE SALE" is the only CTA** — too generic for an email covering furniture, outdoor, and back-in-stock across at least three distinct categories.
- **No personalization signals in the hero** — no name, no category hint, no member callout above the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the outdoor 50% off** — flip the hero and the outdoor panel. 50% is the sharper hook, and outdoor is a seasonal-urgency category (summer is finite). The 40% Summer Edit becomes the secondary frame.
- 2. **Add a deadline or countdown timer** — even "offer ends July 13" would sharpen "is on now" into something actionable. The July 4th finale emails (July 7) earned their 8/10s precisely because the timer was doing real work.
- 3. **Break the single CTA into two** — "Shop Furniture" and "Shop Outdoor" instead of "SHOP THE SALE." The email already has two distinct sale events; treat them as two clicks.
- 4. **Move the sweepstakes block to the footer** — or cut it entirely from this send and give it its own dedicated email where it's the hero, not the noise.
- 5. **Surface one specific featured product with a price in the hero** — even one sofa or accent chair with the discounted vs. original price gives the sale immediate credibility.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Outdoor: Up to 50% off — Summer Edit starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer refresh: new sale, up to 50% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, outdoor, lighting — fresh picks, real discounts, ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Summer Edit is live — including the back-in-stock pieces you missed`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional email template with VML/MSO conditional blocks. The truncated source reveals several infrastructure-level issues: a leftover template placeholder comment, double-slash font URL paths, duplicate `@font-face` declarations, and viewport/text-scaling attributes that violate WCAG accessibility requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font CDN double-slash paths** — three `@font-face` declarations contain malformed URLs with double slashes:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers tolerate this via redirect, but it adds a round-trip and can fail on strict CDN configs.

**Cross-brand font CDN dependency** — `HNW05` font is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`, not `cb2.com`. Any CDN change or outage on the parent brand's domain silently breaks CB2 email typography.

Cannot assess click-tracking or UTM coverage — source is truncated before body links appear.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — prevents user zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of functionality. This attribute explicitly blocks that.
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on the `*` selector** — disables OS-level text scaling for visually impaired users. Combined with the viewport restriction above, this is a double block on accessible text scaling.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint at 418px** — the media query fires at `max-width: 418px` but sets `.mobile { width: 414px }`. Devices at 419–480px (a large chunk of Android mid-range) get neither the mobile nor the desktop layout. Standard safe breakpoint is 480px or 600px.

**`Lineto Brown Sub` declared three times** — the first `@font-face` block has no weight/style qualifiers; the second and third add `font-weight: 400` and `font-weight: 600` respectively. The unqualified first declaration creates a specificity conflict — behavior is undefined across email clients that partially support web fonts (e.g., Apple Mail, Samsung Mail).

**Conflicting `p` rules** — global styles set `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` then client styles override with `p { padding: 0 !important; margin: 0 !important; }`. The `text-align: center` from the first rule survives the override, which may center body-copy paragraphs unintentionally depending on where the client style block is parsed.

**`line-height: 100%` on `*` selector** — percentage line-heights do not inherit correctly in all email clients; unitless values (e.g., `1.5`) are safer. This is a known Outlook/MSO rendering trigger.
