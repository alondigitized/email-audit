# NEW MARKDOWNS! Up to 60% off →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth clearance email from Crate & Barrel in three days — following the June 2 "UP TO 60% OFF!" send and yesterday's "NEW MARKDOWNS! Shop up to 60% off →" — and the copy is nearly identical to that June 4 send (same subject line, same ceiling discount, same "NEW MARKDOWNS!" hook). The brand is burning its credibility on the word "new" here: if the June 4 morning send already announced new markdowns, there's no story left to tell by evening. The hero image is clean and the discount is legible, but the clearance grid beneath it is visually cluttered with small thumbnails that fail to telegraph individual offer strength.
- The lower half of the email loses energy fast. A drinkware lifestyle photo, a furniture recommendation block labeled "we love these for you," an app download pitch, and a "TheDesignDesk" editorial module all stack up without a unifying thread. It reads like three separate emails stapled together.
- Verdict: technically competent but strategically exhausted. The send cadence has inverted the urgency — another "NEW MARKDOWNS!" email the same day as an earlier one teaches subscribers that clearance is permanent and the urgency is fake.

## What's working

- The hero "up to 60% off clearance" message is immediately legible — white knockout type over a dark banner, discount claim front and center.
- The "Shop Now" CTA button is prominent and unambiguous in the hero.
- Product grid photography is clean and consistent with the brand's premium aesthetic.
- The colorful drinkware lifestyle photo breaks the visual monotony of the upper clearance grid and creates a nice mid-email pause.

## What's weak

- "NEW MARKDOWNS!" is the same hook used in the earlier June 4 Crate & Barrel send — sending it twice in one day destroys the claim's credibility entirely.
- The product thumbnails in the clearance grid are too small to communicate desirability; no individual item price or discount is visible at this scale.
- The "we love these for you" personalization module shows generic bedroom/dining furniture with no visible price signals or "sale" callouts — it blends into noise.
- The app download pitch and TheDesignDesk editorial block feel like tacked-on filler at the bottom, diluting the clearance message.
- No time-bound deadline visible anywhere — "up to 60% off" with no end date makes the urgency feel hollow, especially on the fourth clearance send this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the duplicate send or differentiate it sharply.** If there are genuinely new markdowns since the morning send, name a specific category that dropped ("Bedroom just hit 60% — new today"). Otherwise skip the send.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "this weekend only" would restore the urgency the word "NEW" can no longer carry.
- 3. **Show item-level prices in the grid.** Even one "Was $199 → $79" callout in the hero grid does more conversion work than the category thumbnail alone.
- 4. **Cut the app pitch and TheDesignDesk module.** They belong in a non-sale editorial send; stacked behind a clearance email they read as filler.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line** — the arrow glyph and caps are carried over from the morning send verbatim.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bedroom clearance just got deeper — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `These pieces drop to 60% off this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on sofas, beds & dining — ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh cuts just added — shop the lowest prices of the season.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: `maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom on iOS. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue: `-webkit-text-size-adjust` declared inconsistently across three style blocks**
- Block 1 (global `*` rule): `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;}`
- Block 2 (body): `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust:100%; }`
- Block 3 (html): `html { -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; }`

`none` suppresses all text scaling; `100%` allows it. The last-declared `none` on `html` wins in most WebKit renderers, effectively overriding the `100%` on `body`. Standardize to `100%` on `html, body`.

**Issue: Competing `img` max-width rules at overlapping breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At viewports 640–767px, both rules match. Cascade order means the 600px rule (declared first) is overridden by 640px — but the intent appears inverted (desktop should cap at 600px, not 640px). Verify which value is authoritative and reorder or consolidate.

**Issue: Hardcoded `color-scheme: light` blocks dark mode**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Clients that respect these tags (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) will never render a dark variant. If no dark-mode stylesheet is implemented this is intentional, but should be documented; otherwise change to `light dark`.

**Issue: Global `p` alignment override**
```css
p{margin:0 auto; text-align:center;}
```
Forces center-alignment on every `<p>` globally. Any body-copy paragraphs that should be left-aligned require explicit overrides. Scope this to specific containers rather than the global selector.
