# End of Summer Sale is over tomorrow
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a legitimate urgency send — "Ends Tomorrow / End of Summer Sale" — and it's the most coherent hero this brand has shown all week: clean serif typography over a moody tablescape shot, black "SHOP THE SALE" button immediately visible. But it commits the exact same sin as the 08/20 "Floor Sample Sale" send: nowhere in the entire body, including the four category tiles (Furniture, Lighting, Rugs, Decor), does an actual discount percentage or dollar figure appear. You scroll a full-length email for a "sale" and never learn what the sale actually is.
- The category-grid structure (Furniture/Lighting/Rugs/Decor stacked vertically, each with a moody lifestyle photo) is well-executed visually but functionally just a sitemap — it doesn't sell urgency, it just repeats "here's a room category" four times before you even hit the second CTA. Combined with zero specificity in subject or body, this reads as competent design wrapped around an empty offer.

## What's working

- Clear urgency framing in both subject ("over tomorrow") and hero eyebrow ("ENDS TOMORROW") — consistent, unambiguous deadline.
- Hero visual (glassware/tablescape in warm gold/blush tones) is the most premium-feeling image CB2 has run this week — no render bugs, crisp typography, high contrast against the photo.
- Primary CTA "SHOP THE SALE" is bolded, high-contrast, and repeated twice (hero + mid-page) for scroll-through capture.
- Category tiles are well-labeled and visually distinct, making category-specific browsing easy for someone who already knows what they want.
- Footer app/rewards/text-signup blocks are clean and modular, no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- No discount percentage or dollar amount anywhere in the visible body — "sale" is asserted, never quantified, which is a real trust gap versus a competitor's "30% off everything."
- Four back-to-back category tiles with no differentiated offer per category makes the middle of the email feel like padding rather than a curated sale edit.
- The "WALL ART & DECOR" secondary module breaks the sale narrative entirely — it's a "New & Best Selling" callout, not a sale item, undercutting the "ends tomorrow" urgency right when the reader might convert.
- No countdown timer, specific end-date/time, or "last chance" reinforcement beyond the one hero line — for a 24-hour urgency play this feels underbuilt.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a number in the hero — "20% OFF FURNITURE, DECOR & MORE" instead of a bare "SALE" — this is the single biggest lever; readers won't click urgency copy with no visible payoff.
- 2. Cut the "WALL ART & DECOR — New & Best Selling" module from this send or move it to a separate email; it dilutes the one-day urgency message.
- 3. Add a real countdown or explicit deadline ("Ends 8/23 at 11:59pm ET") next to "ENDS TOMORROW" so the urgency is concrete, not just declarative.
- 4. Consider dropping to 2-3 category tiles with the discount badge overlaid on each image, rather than 4 generic tiles with no offer visible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: End of Summer Sale ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off ends tomorrow — shop End of Summer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, rugs, lighting & decor — one day left`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your last chance to save before fall arrives`
