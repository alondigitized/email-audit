# Fill your cart: FREE shipping starts now!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids leads with the same free-shipping hook that the parent brand (Crate & Barrel) already fired on the exact same day — the July 10 "STARTS NOW: Free shipping on orders over $59!" email already went out this morning. For subscribers on both lists, this is signal fatigue masquerading as coordinated marketing. The offer itself is clean and clear in the hero ("starts today! free shipping"), but the email then stacks four additional content sections — 500+ fall arrivals, the Design Desk, personalized product recommendations, and an app download pitch — each competing for attention and collectively burying the lead.
- The creative is competent but cluttered. The hero photography (plush seating, cozy room staging) earns its place, and the category navigation tiles give a fast scanner a reason to click through. But the mid-email "Design Desk 100% free" consultation offer is genuinely differentiated and gets completely lost sandwiched between a fall arrivals banner and a personalized product grid.
- This is a 6/10 email that could be a 7 if it respected the one-message rule and a 4 for subscribers who already got the Crate & Barrel version this morning.

## What's working

- Hero leads with the free-shipping offer clearly — "starts today! free shipping" is above the fold and reads instantly.
- Category navigation tiles give a visual shortcut to shop specific rooms without reading body copy.
- "500+ new fall arrivals, starting at $59" anchors a price point, which is better than vague "shop now" banners.
- The "we love these for you" personalization block is the right idea — product-level recommendations pull harder than category banners.
- No visible render bugs; the layout holds cleanly at this display size.

## What's weak

- Same-day free-shipping email as Crate & Barrel — any subscriber on both lists gets a near-identical offer twice in one morning, which trains unsubscribes not clicks.
- Four distinct content blocks after the hero create a scroll-and-fatigue experience with no single second hook to anchor on.
- "Starts now" implies urgency but there is no visible end date — "starts" with no "ends" feels like a permanent deal, which drains urgency.
- The Design Desk free consultation offer is buried mid-email where engagement is lowest; it deserves a dedicated send.
- App download CTA at the bottom competes with the shipping offer for the last impression.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the same-day sister-brand echo.** Coordinate the Crate & Barrel and Crate & Kids send calendars so the free-shipping trigger fires on different days, or roll it into one unified cross-brand email. Sending the same hook twice in one morning is the fastest way to train list fatigue.
- 2. **Add an end date to the offer.** "Ends Sunday" or "through July 13" converts "starts now" from ambient noise into a credible deadline.
- 3. **Promote the Design Desk to a hero or dedicated send.** "Free design help, no strings attached" is a meaningful brand differentiator that gets wasted as a third-fold module.
- 4. **Cut the email to hero + one product grid.** Strip the app CTA and reduce to hero offer → category tiles → curated picks → single CTA. Length is the enemy of the click here.
- 5. **Put the shipping threshold in the subject line.** "Free shipping on $59+" is more honest and more clickable than the generic "FREE shipping" flag.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free shipping on $59+ — starts today, Crate & Kids`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart ships free this weekend →`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `500+ fall arrivals just landed. Grab yours before the sale clock runs out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: free design help from our Design Desk, no appointment needed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting desktop image max-width rules** — two media queries both labeled "For desktop" produce an override conflict:

```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```

For viewports ≥768px, the 600px rule wins because it appears later in the cascade. Images will be narrower than intended on large desktop clients. The 640px breakpoint rule is effectively dead for viewports ≥768px.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** blocks user zoom:

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) recommends against suppressing pinch-to-zoom. This affects users with low vision reading on mobile.

**`line-height: 100%` applied to all elements via `*` selector** can collapse multi-line text blocks. Safer values are unitless (e.g., `1.4`) or explicit `px`.
