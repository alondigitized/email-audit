# Tell us what you think for a $1000 gift card
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's own version of the review-for-$1000-giveaway play that Crate & Kids ran the same day ("Your chance to win a $1,000 Gift Card," 2026-08-04, 7.00/10) — but where the Kids send was flagged for a bait-and-switch structure, this one actually delivers what the subject promises: three stars, "let us know what you think for $1000," a lifestyle hero, and a "Write a Review" button right where your eye lands. The top of the email is genuinely well-built.
- The problem is everything after that first screen. This is a kitchen-sink send — after the giveaway, it pivots to summer produce recipes (completely off-category for a furniture brand), then an Aspen collection sale, then a six-item product grid, then a Crate Card rewards pitch, then an app-download sweepstakes for a *different* $500 prize, then a free interior-design consult offer. Six distinct campaigns stapled into one email dilutes the giveaway that earned the open in the first place.
- Judgment: strong open, weak follow-through. The hero alone would be an 8-9; the unrelated scroll-and-sprawl below it drags the overall effectiveness down.

## What's working

- The eyebrow ("CRATE & BARREL REVIEW GIVEAWAY") + headline + hero image are directly on-message with the subject line — no bait-and-switch, unlike the Crate & Kids sibling send.
- "Write a Review" is a specific, unambiguous verb+noun CTA sitting right under the hero.
- The sweepstakes deadline ("ends August 31") gives real, dated urgency in the body copy.
- Product tiles in "we love these for you" show dimensions and prices up front — useful detail for a furniture shopper.
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Six unrelated campaigns crammed into one send (giveaway → produce recipes → Aspen sale → product grid → credit card → app → design service) — no single dominant CTA once you scroll past screen one.
- The "in season now: summer produce" recipe block is off-category for a furniture/home brand email and reads like a mis-slotted content module.
- A second, different sweepstakes ($500 via the app) competes directly with the $1000 gift card giveaway for attention — confusing which prize is "the" offer.
- No actual customer reviews or star ratings are shown anywhere, which undercuts a review-solicitation email — showing a snippet of past reviews would build credibility for the ask.
- Subject and preview carry no urgency or personalization, even though the body has a real deadline (Aug 31) that never makes it up to the subject line.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: one focused purely on the review giveaway (hero + CTA + maybe a testimonial strip), one for the Aspen sale/product grid. Combining them halves the punch of both.
- 2. Cut or relocate the produce/recipes block — it doesn't belong in a Crate & Barrel furniture email and reads as an accidental cross-post from a different vertical.
- 3. Move the Aug 31 deadline into the subject line to convert the open-worthy urgency that's currently stranded in the body copy.
- 4. Add a one-line social-proof element ("Join 12,000 shoppers who've already left a review") near the CTA to make the ask feel less one-sided.
- 5. Resolve the dual-sweepstakes confusion — either fold the app's $500 prize into the same $1000 giveaway messaging or clearly separate them with different visual weight.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Review one product, win a $1,000 gift card`
- **Subject Alt B:** `2 days left: tell us what you think, win $1,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sweepstakes ends August 31 — no purchase necessary.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Rate your favorite piece for a shot at $1,000 in Crate & Barrel credit.`
