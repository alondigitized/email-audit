# First look: Check out our Halloween shop now →
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids' best send of the recent history — subject, hero, and first three modules are all genuinely Halloween: a bunting banner reading "TRICK OR TREAT," a jack-o'-lantern treat bag, a black cat pillow, a ghost, and a personalize-it row (pumpkin treat bag, cat hooded towel, ghost & cat pillows). It's the first email in the brand-history log that doesn't bait-and-switch the subject line.
- The problem is what comes after: by the fourth scroll it drops the Halloween theme entirely for a crib ad ("500+ new arrivals, starting at $29"), then "we love these for you" — a totally generic product grid mixing a backpack, gold knobs, a wall mirror, and dinner plates — followed by a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a Design Desk booking CTA. Only two of eight grid items are even Halloween-themed.
- Verdict: strong on-theme hero earns real credit, but this is still a kitchen-sink email that buries its own halo product under five unrelated modules and three separate secondary CTAs competing with "Shop Halloween."

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguously on-theme: Halloween bunting, jack-o'-lantern bag, ghost, black cat pillow, orange "Shop Halloween" button.
- Subhead "first look: the halloween shop" matches the subject line's "First look" framing exactly — no bait-and-switch.
- Second module (trick-or-treat bags, costumes, decor) and the "Personalize It" three-up (pumpkin bag, cat towel, ghost & cat pillows) both stay in-category.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or lorem ipsum.
- Pricing and sale price (gold knobs $64.80 struck from $72.00) are visible on the product grid.

## What's weak

- Theme collapses after the third module: crib/nursery ad, then a mixed grid of backpack/knobs/mirror/plates that has nothing to do with Halloween.
- "We love these for you" personalization label is generic and undercut by an obviously non-personalized, catalog-dump grid.
- Three competing secondary CTAs (Crate Card, app download sweepstakes, Design Desk) after the primary offer dilutes the "Shop Halloween" ask.
- No urgency or dates — "first look" implies early access but there's no timeframe (e.g., "shop before it sells out" or a launch date).
- Six of eight bottom-grid products break theme entirely, making the email feel bolted together rather than a true Halloween launch send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the length — end the email after the "Personalize It" module and "Shop All Halloween" CTA; move nursery/crib, generic grid, credit card, app, and Design Desk content to separate sends. A "first look" email should stay a focused reveal.
- 2. If the grid stays, swap in Halloween-relevant items only (the pumpkin treat bag and haunted house countdown calendar already appear — cut the backpack, knobs, mirror, and dinner plates).
- 3. Add urgency to the hero subhead, e.g. "shop before your favorites sell out" or a specific date the full collection drops.
- 4. Drop at least two of the three bottom-funnel CTAs (Crate Card / app / Design Desk) — keep at most one non-Halloween ask.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Treat bags, costumes, and spooky-cute decor for the whole crew.`
