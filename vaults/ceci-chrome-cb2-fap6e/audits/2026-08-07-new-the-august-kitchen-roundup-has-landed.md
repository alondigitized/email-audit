# New! The August kitchen roundup has landed →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel's kitchen category catalog dressed up as an email — "new! the August kitchen roundup" is code for "here are twelve things we want you to look at," not an offer. It opens strong with a genuinely appetizing Le Creuset/braised-dish hero, then just keeps going: Ninja's new color, two JURA machines, Made In cookware, KitchenAid bowls, a GE nugget ice maker, a four-tile "more kitchen newness" grid, a tabletop teaser, a $23K honeymoon sweepstakes, a personalized "we love these for you" grid, a credit card pitch, an app download push, and a Design Desk appointment CTA — in that order, in one email.
- The one real deal in here — Le Creuset 3.5-Qt Dutch Oven at $219.95 (sugg. $355) — is buried six-plus screens down inside a sub-grid, competing with four other full-price "new arrival" callouts. Compare that to today's other Crate & Barrel send, "Up to 60% off the Summer Sale" (2026-08-07, 9.00/10), which leads with the discount in the first screen. This email leads with vibes.
- It's clean, well-photographed, and on-brand, but it's also one of the longest, least-focused sends in this brand's recent history — closer in spirit to the sprawling Design Desk dining email (2026-08-06, 6.00/10) than to the tight sale sweeps. Judgement: a pleasant scroll for an already-engaged Crate & Barrel shopper, a skippable one for anyone opening cold off the subject line.

## What's working

- Hero image (Le Creuset lid + finished dish) is genuinely appetizing and immediately legible as "kitchen."
- "Ships Free" is stamped on nearly every module, giving a consistent low-friction value prop even without a price cut.
- Product copy is specific where it counts — "dual grinders, 46-preset drinks," "27 specialty drinks already saved," "3.5-Quart," "copper-finished handles" — real detail, not filler adjectives.
- The one actual discount (Le Creuset Dutch Oven $219.95 vs. $355, Bakeware Set $179.95 vs. $222.50) is shown with clear strikethrough pricing.
- No render bugs — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything renders cleanly top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No single offer or hero product is prioritized — by module 6 it's unclear what this email actually wants you to buy.
- The only real discount is buried past the fold, after five full-price "exclusive/new" callouts, so a scroller has to work to find the deal.
- This is the second Crate & Barrel email hitting the inbox today (alongside the 60%-off Summer Sale send) — cadence fatigue is real at this volume.
- Nearly every CTA says "Shop Now," which gives zero signal about what you're clicking into once there are ten of them in one email.
- The email tacks on unrelated asks (credit card, app download, Design Desk booking, sweepstakes) that dilute the kitchen-roundup premise the subject line promised.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the discount, not the roundup. Put the Le Creuset Dutch Oven's $219.95/$355 in the hero module — a real number beats "new! the August kitchen roundup" every time.
- 2. Cut the email in half. Fold the JURA/Ninja/Made In/KitchenAid/GE sections into a single scannable grid (like the "more must-see kitchen newness" module already does) instead of five separate full-bleed modules.
- 3. Drop the off-topic modules (Crate Card, honeymoon sweepstakes, app download, Design Desk) from a "kitchen roundup" send — they belong in their own dedicated email and just add scroll fatigue here.
- 4. Vary CTA copy to match the product ("Shop the Dutch Oven," "Shop Espresso Machines") instead of repeating "Shop Now" ten times.
- 5. Space this out from the same-day Summer Sale send — sending two Crate & Barrel emails in one day risks unsubscribes.
