# NOW & FUTURE CLASSICS ON SALE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- CB2 is anchoring a sale event around a genuinely smart concept — "future classics" — furniture worth buying because it will matter decades from now. The hero executes that idea well: a deep-brown velvet sofa commands the frame, "WORTH A DOUBLE TAKE" is better-than-average sale copy, and the SALE lockup dominates the fold. The visual identity is unmistakably CB2. That's the ceiling.
- The floor drops fast. After the hero, the email splinters into eight product category grids (Living Room, Lighting, Bedroom, Rugs, Dining, Décor, Pillows, Outdoor) and the "future classics" story disappears entirely. What should feel like a curated moment turns into a department store circular. The biggest structural miss: no specific discount number anywhere in the hero. "SALE" alone is a soft motivator for a brand where a sofa costs $1,500+. Without a "up to X% off" anchor, the email asks for trust instead of creating urgency.
- The ALL CAPS subject line is the other glaring own-goal. CB2's brand voice is elevated and aspirational; shouting in the inbox contradicts it before the reader even opens.

## What's working

- The velvet sofa hero image is genuinely arresting — rich texture, dark tones, editorial quality.
- "WORTH A DOUBLE TAKE" is distinctive sale copy that nods to the "future classics" positioning without reading like clearance.
- SALE typography + "SHOP NOW" button hierarchy is clean — the eye lands in the right place above the fold.
- "NEW" labels across all eight category sections signal freshness, not excess inventory.
- CB2 logo, nav, and brand aesthetic render crisply — no identity confusion.

## What's weak

- No specific discount percentage in the hero — "SALE" alone is weak motivation at CB2 price points.
- ALL CAPS subject line undercuts the premium brand voice and reads as promotional spam.
- "FUTURE CLASSICS" framing evaporates after the hero; the concept is established then abandoned.
- Eight product category grids dilute urgency into passive browse mode.
- The 15% off callout (app/membership) is buried below multiple sections — buried loyalty benefit is a wasted retention lever.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor a number in the hero** — "Up to 40% off" or "From $X" in the sub-hero gives a reason to click beyond curiosity.
- 2. **Carry "Future Classics" into the grid** — pick 2–3 hero pieces, name them explicitly as future classics, and give each a one-line rationale. Makes the concept sticky.
- 3. **Fix the subject casing** — sentence or title case restores brand premium and removes the spam-signal flag.
- 4. **Collapse 8 categories to 3** — Living Room / Bedroom / Dining with a "More →" link concentrates click intent and respects mobile scroll depth.
- 5. **Surface the 15% membership offer in the sub-hero** — if it's a member benefit, it belongs at the top of the email, not below the fold.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The kind of furniture your grandkids will want someday. Starting today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New season arrivals. Real discounts. Your shortlist starts here.`
