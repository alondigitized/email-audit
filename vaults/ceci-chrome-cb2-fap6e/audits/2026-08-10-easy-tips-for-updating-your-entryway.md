# Easy tips for updating your entryway
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel handing the send off to TheDesignDesk, its in-house design service, to make the case for entryway styling — "create a warm welcome" leads into curated vignettes (console tables, mirrors, art, rugs, moodboards) each paired with an Exclusive/Reseller product pairing and a "Book a Free Appointment" CTA. It's a tasteful, editorial-style layout — moody architectural photography, consistent green accent color, clear section breaks — but it is not a promotional email in any real sense: there's no discount, no price anchor beyond a stray honeymoon-sweepstakes banner, and the primary ask (book a design consultation) is a much colder conversion than "shop this sale."
- Compared to the brand's recent cadence — 60% off clearance (2026-08-09), the wine glass deep-dive (2026-08-08), the dinnerware spotlight (2026-08-09) — this is a category-inspiration play, not a revenue play. That's a legitimate email type, but it's buried under a rug/art/lighting grid that reads more like a catalog than a call to action, and a jarring honeymoon-sweepstakes insert two-thirds down breaks the entryway narrative entirely.
- Net: well-photographed, on-brand, but low urgency and unfocused CTA mix (Book Appointment vs. Shop Category vs. Enter Sweepstakes vs. Download App) will suppress clicks for anyone not already in a "redesign my entryway" mindset.

## What's working

- Strong, consistent hero imagery — architectural doorway shot ties the "warm welcome" theme to the entryway subject cleanly.
- Clear section rhythm: each vignette (statement piece, contrasts, rug, moodboard) follows the same photo → subhead → Exclusive/Reseller pairing → CTA pattern, easy to scan.
- "3 ways to meet a design desk pro" (home/in-store/online) is a genuinely useful, concrete explainer block that reduces friction for booking.
- "we love these for you" product grid at the bottom shows real prices and product names — good for browsers who skipped the design-service pitch.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the body — this is pure inspiration/service content wearing a marketing email's clothes; nothing time-bound or price-driven to click for.
- Two competing CTAs stacked throughout ("Shop [Category] Furniture" vs. "Book a Free Appointment") dilute the single ask instead of reinforcing it.
- The "$23,000+ dream honeymoon getaway" sweepstakes banner is a jarring non-sequitur dropped into an entryway-styling narrative — it has nothing to do with the subject line or surrounding content.
- Subject line ("Easy tips for updating your entryway") promises tips/how-to content, but the email is a shoppable showcase with a service pitch, not tips — a mismatch that will disappoint openers looking for advice.
- Rewards-card teaser ("new! the card you love just got better") and app-download block at the footer add more unrelated asks, further fragmenting an already-crowded CTA set.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one primary CTA and hold it — either this is a "Book a Free Design Consultation" email (drop half the shop buttons) or a shoppable entryway-refresh email (drop the appointment CTAs to one closing block). The current mix asks for four different actions.
- 2. Cut or relocate the honeymoon sweepstakes banner — it has zero connection to entryway/design content and reads like an ad-network insert breaking trust in the editorial flow.
- 3. Rewrite the subject to match the actual content — either commit to "tips" with real how-to copy up top, or align the subject to the design-service angle.
- 4. Add one concrete incentive (e.g., "book a free consult, get $50 off your first order") to give the appointment CTA a reason to click now instead of "someday."
- 5. Move "we love these for you" (the shoppable, priced grid) higher — it's the strongest click-through content in the email and it's buried near the bottom.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Console tables, mirrors, rugs — plus a free session with TheDesignDesk`

Standard MI/Cheetah Digital-templated HTML email (VML/MSO conditional comments, fluid-hybrid layout) with heavy tracking instrumentation; automated QA reports a 57% pass rate driven almost entirely by missing image alt attributes and undetected compliance headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 75 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (expected behavior for redirect-domain links, not itself a defect — no destination-URL validation was possible from this data).
- Multiple tracking pixels detected: `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/rp/f3c0d22992a01946.png` (6 instances, same asset re-fired), `sr.rlcdn.com/448796.gif` (5 sequential instances, `n=1`–`n=5` — LiveRamp/RampID pixel fired multiple times), `mi.crateandbarrel.com/p/up/50c75a732e99b42a/o.gif`, and a `dv.crateandbarrel.com` open-tracking beacon with `mi_mid` param. Volume/redundancy of pixels (6x identical MI pixel, 5x RampID pixel) is worth flagging for review — likely artifacts of modular content blocks each carrying their own tracking pixel rather than a single global one.
- Raw HTML source was truncated before reaching `<body>`, so CTA/link `href` values themselves could not be verified for correct UTM tagging or broken syntax — flagged as a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **31 images missing `alt` text**, spanning hero/content imagery (`image.mail.crateandbarrel.com/lib/.../*.jpg`), a Scene7-hosted hero (`20250226_CB_DesignDesk_Entryway_PI_hero`), and all tracking pixels (which appropriately have no alt but were still flagged by the checker — expected/benign for 1x1 beacons, not an accessibility concern). The content images (hero + 25 product/lifestyle photos) are the real accessibility gap: screen readers will announce filenames or nothing for primary visual content.
- Template includes standard Outlook/MSO conditional VML blocks, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and mobile media queries (`max-width:460px`) — no structural rendering defects observed in the head/style block provided.
- `color-scheme`/`supported-color-schemes` both set to `light` only — no dark-mode-specific styling declared, which can cause inverted/illegible rendering in clients that force dark mode (Outlook.com, some Gmail dark mode). Not confirmed as broken, just unmitigated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found — no unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) or broken personalization syntax present in the available HTML/QA data.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header not found** — either absent or stripped by the AgentMail relay before capture. If genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM/Gmail-Yahoo bulk sender requirement gap; if it's a relay-capture artifact, confidence is low and needs re-verification against raw MIME headers.

- **Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this data; cannot confirm authentication compliance one way or the other.
- These three are QA warnings tied to the AgentMail relay's header capture, not confirmed failures — recommend validating directly against the original SMTP transaction headers before treating as real compliance defects.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed — the truncated HTML source did not include body content or CTA `href` attributes, so no UTM parameters or landing-page URLs were available to check for consistency with the tracked click domains (`mi.crateandbarrel.com`, `dv.crateandbarrel.com`). Flagging as a data gap rather than a pass/fail.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the 25 content/product images and the hero image; leave tracking-pixel `alt=""` as-is (already correct practice, just noisy in this checker).
2. Verify List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post / Authentication-Results directly against raw SMTP headers (bypassing the AgentMail relay) to confirm whether these are real compliance gaps or capture artifacts.
3. Consolidate redundant tracking pixels (6x identical MI pixel, 5x RampID pixel) if they're firing from repeated content-block includes rather than intentional multi-touch tracking.
