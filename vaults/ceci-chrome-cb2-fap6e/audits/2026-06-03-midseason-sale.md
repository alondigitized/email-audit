# MIDSEASON SALE
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's fourth straight promotional send in five days — following the 6/2 "New markdowns are here" email (itself the third in the same Midseason Sale arc) — and it shows. Where "New markdowns are here" at least promised new inventory, "MIDSEASON SALE" is a content-free re-broadcast of the same campaign with no new angle, no urgency trigger, and no specific offer callout in the hero. The rendered body is handsome — warm bedroom imagery, clean grid, nice room-scale sofa shot — but the structure has two competing narratives fighting for attention: the sale and the $1,000 design services pitch. Neither wins decisively.
- The $1,000 design services block is the most interesting thing in the email and it's buried at the fold. The hero, meanwhile, gives you "MIDSEASON SALE" and a "SHOP NOW" button with no percentage, no category, no reason to click today instead of yesterday. It's the promotional equivalent of a billboard that just says "STILL OPEN."
- This email isn't bad — CB2's production quality is consistently solid — but it's doing nothing the prior sends didn't already do. For a subscriber who got the 5/30 outdoor-sale email (scored 9/10 here), the 6/1 art-history editorial, and the 6/2 new-markdowns email, this is inbox wallpaper.

## What's working

- The hero bedroom imagery is genuinely warm and aspirational — the muted linen tones and layered styling match CB2's aesthetic precisely.
- The interior design services block with the $1,000 callout is a smart cross-sell that adds real value beyond the sale, especially mid-campaign when sale fatigue is setting in.
- "BOOK YOUR FREE APPOINTMENT" CTA on the design services block is specific and action-oriented — measurably better than the generic "SHOP NOW" above it.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" personalization row at the bottom adds relevance signal and keeps the email from feeling like a pure broadcast.
- Layout is clean — no render bugs, no overlapping text, no broken images.

## What's weak

- No discount percentage or dollar figure in the hero — "MIDSEASON SALE" with a "SHOP NOW" button asks the reader to trust that there's a deal without showing them one.
- ALL CAPS subject line with zero offer specifics is the weakest subject in this campaign cycle — even "STEALS OF THE WEEK" (5/31) and "OUTDOOR IS ON SALE" (5/30) had more angle.
- Two major CTAs (sale vs. design services) compete without a clear hierarchy — the design services block interrupts the sale browsing flow rather than complementing it.
- Fourth promotional email in five days with no urgency signal — no "ends Sunday," no "X days left," nothing to motivate action today over next week.
- The "MORE FOR YOU" product row has pricing but no visible sale badge or strike-through price, so the "sale" framing isn't reinforced at the product level from this render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific offer callout in the hero** — "Up to 40% off" or the actual markdown depth. This is the single highest-leverage change: the entire email premise is a sale and the hero doesn't prove it.
- 2. **Inject an urgency anchor** — A "Ends Sunday" or "3 days left" line in the hero sub-copy would justify sending email #4 in the arc. Without it, there's no reason to open today.
- 3. **Pick a lane: sale OR design services** — If the design services $1,000 offer is the hook, lead with it. If the sale is the hook, move design services to a footer module. The current 50/50 split dilutes both.
- 4. **Rewrite the primary CTA** — "SHOP THE SALE" or a category-specific label ("SHOP BEDROOM") outperforms the generic "SHOP NOW."
- 5. **Subject line — give it an angle** — "MIDSEASON SALE" is the plainest possible label for an ongoing campaign. Any of the alts below would create more of a reason to open on day 5 of the arc:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off — Midseason Sale picks for you`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sale hits the bedroom (new markdowns inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sofas, rugs, and lighting — all on sale, all ending soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New drops added + free interior design with your purchase`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Developer placeholder left in production HTML:**
```html
<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
```
This is an unfilled template comment with a typo (`IMPUT` → `INPUT`). Benign to rendering but confirms the template shipped without a final QA pass on placeholder cleanup.

**Issue — Zoom/scaling disabled (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom. Paired with:
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
This prevents OS-level font scaling on all elements. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint too narrow:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 418px) { .mobile { width: 414px !important; } }
```
Breakpoint targets ≤418px only. iPhone 14 Pro Max (430px), iPhone 15 Pro Max (430px), and Pixel 8 (412px in some orientations) will not receive mobile layout styles. Standard email mobile breakpoint is 600px.

**Issue — Double slashes in font asset URLs:**
```css
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2")
src: url("https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2")
```
Three of the six `@font-face` declarations have double forward slashes. Most web servers normalize this, but it is a hygiene defect and will 404 on any server with strict path handling.

**Issue — `line-height: 100%` on `*` selector:**
```css
* { line-height: 100%; }
```
Percentage `line-height` on a wildcard selector is a known email rendering risk — some clients (older Outlook, Samsung Mail) interpret it relative to the element's font size rather than as a unitless multiplier, causing text overlap in multi-line nodes.

**Note — Font duplication:** `"Lineto Brown Sub"` has four separate `@font-face` declarations, with the first two lacking `font-weight` and `font-style` descriptors. This can cause incorrect weight/style resolution in clients that do support web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail).
