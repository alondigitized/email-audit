# Paige Desorbo's dreamy NYC apartment
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's "Free Design Services" sub-brand celebrity-tie-in send, and it's a genuinely different creative mode than the twelve straight days of sale/lookbook emails already logged this week. The "COLLECTED, NOT EXPECTED" mood-board hero with a moodboard-collage of textures, a model, and florals is a step up in production value, and "Interior Design Services" is a real, distinct CTA (book a free appointment) rather than another product grid. But nowhere in the visible render does "Paige Desorbo" or "NYC apartment" actually appear — the subject line promises a specific celebrity home tour and the email delivers generic "Upper West Side building" copy with no name-drop, no before/after, no recognizable face tied to the subject. That's a bait-and-switch gap between subject and body.
- The offer itself ($5,000 refresh sweepstakes, free design consult) is a genuine, distinct value prop for anyone who's ever wanted an interior designer without agency fees — but it's buried under a generic "meet with a designer Aug 15–Sept 11" sweepstakes banner up top with no percentage-off or dollar-off product discount, which will underperform against CB2's usual 40-60%-off subject lines for a deal-driven subscriber.

## What's working

- Distinct creative mode from the sale-heavy cadence this week — a services/consultation pitch, not another furniture grid.
- The "COLLECTED, NOT EXPECTED" moodboard hero has real visual texture (fabric swatch, marble, model, florals) instead of a flat product shot.
- Clear, repeated CTA copy — "BOOK YOUR FREE DESIGN APPOINTMENT" appears three times, unambiguous action.
- Room-by-room category grid (Sofas, Dining, Bedroom, Lighting, Rugs, Decor) gives multiple entry points if the reader isn't ready to book a consult.
- Clean layout, no render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, footer/social/rewards blocks all render properly.

## What's weak

- The subject promises "Paige Desorbo's dreamy NYC apartment" but nothing in the visible email — no name, no face, no labeled before/after — delivers on that specific promise; it reads as a stock "Upper West Side building" design tour.
- No discount or dollar amount is the primary offer — the hero pushes a sweepstakes ("WIN A $5,000 REFRESH") which is a weaker, lower-probability incentive than a straightforward % off.
- Three near-identical living-room shots in a row (empty room from a similar angle) add scroll length without adding new information — could be trimmed to one or two.
- Heavy competing CTAs by the bottom (reviews sweepstakes, app download, rewards, referral, sign-up) dilute the single primary "book a design appointment" action established up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject/body mismatch first** — either add a named, photo-credited "Paige Desorbo" section with her actual apartment photos, or don't reference her by name in the subject at all. As sent, the click-through will feel misleading the moment the email opens.
- 2. Lead the hero with something concrete a designer-curious reader can act on — swap "WIN A $5,000 REFRESH" for the actual dollar value of a free consult, or pair the sweepstakes with a real discount (e.g., "20% off with a free design consult") to give both urgency and immediate value.
- 3. Cut one of the three similar empty-living-room photos to shorten the scroll before the room-category grid.
- 4. Add a name/credential ("as seen with @paigedesorbo" or similar) near the hero if this is a legitimate influencer partnership — right now there's no visible attribution, which also raises a disclosure/FTC question if it's paid.
