# WAREHOUSE SALE IS SELLING OUT
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, price-led send with one clear idea: the Warehouse Sale, "UP TO 60% OFF*". The big white "60%" over a moody console-and-lamp photo is the strongest element, and the eye lands on it first. The problem is the stack above it. An "ENDS TODAY / SEMI-ANNUAL DINING EVENT / UP TO 30% OFF*" banner sits over the Warehouse hero, so two sales compete for the top of the email.
- The subject promises scarcity ("SELLING OUT") and the body only half delivers. The "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" travertine pedestal block near the bottom is the one real scarcity cue. The four category tiles (Furniture, Lighting, Rugs, Decor) are pretty but have no prices, and they don't name a single product. It feels like a catalog, not a sale.
- Judgement: a solid, honest, well-designed sale email held back by a split offer and no product-level proof. It repeats the Dining Event creative from the last several days (10/24, 10/25, 10/27), so the hero banner reads as leftover.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 60% OFF*" hero is large, high-contrast and unmistakable, with a "SHOP THE SALE" button.
- "A curated selection of last-chance markdowns." matches the "selling out" subject.
- The four labelled category tiles (Furniture, Lighting, Rugs, Decor) let the reader jump to what they care about.
- "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" with a "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" button is a witty, credible scarcity cue.
- The footer pairs the app, 10% Rewards Dollars, and 15% off for texting sign-up, which gives loyalty and a way to re-engage.

## What's weak

- The Dining Event banner ("ENDS TODAY", up to 30% off) is stacked above the 60% hero, so there are two offers and two "SHOP" buttons in the first screen.
- The category tiles have no prices, no "from $X" and no product names, so nothing is concrete.
- The "SELLING OUT" urgency in the subject has no deadline, no stock counts and no end date in the body.
- The tile labels in small white text sit over light images, so "RUGS" and "DECOR" are hard to read.
- The disclaimer block is dense and tiny, and the asterisks on both offers make the discounts feel heavily qualified.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop the Dining Event banner, or move it below the Warehouse content, so the 60% hero is the only first-screen offer.
- 2. Put real products with sale prices in the grid, such as "Was $X, now $Y", or at least "from $__" on each category tile.
- 3. Add a deadline or stock signal to the hero, such as "Ends Sunday" or "Only 3 left".
- 4. Put the labels on a solid or gradient bar so they stay legible on pale photos.
- 5. Move the "NOT SOLD OUT, YET" block higher, since it is the best scarcity copy in the email.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The QA run reports a 57% pass rate (0 errors, 3 warnings, plus many INFO-level warnings). The confirmed issues are missing `alt` text on 6 content images and 11 insecure `http://` third-party pixels. The header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be completed because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **57 tracking links were not probed.** The QA tool skipped them as click-redirect domains, so link validity, redirect chains and destination status are unverified.
- **Insecure third-party pixels.** 11 images load over plain `http://` from Dotomi (Conversant):
  - `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2&dtm_cid=2437&...`
  - `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2437/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?d98e199bf5027afea04b790655b3dee2=1`
  - Mail clients that block or rewrite non-HTTPS content may drop these. Gmail's image proxy fetches over HTTPS, and Apple Mail may flag mixed content.
  - Ten sequential cookie-redirect pixels in one email is a heavy third-party tracking footprint.
- **Open-tracking pixels without `alt`:** `https://mi.cb2.com/p/up/09aebc5b21344cf7/o.gif?mi_u=...` and `https://dv.cb2.com/o/8d45aab5-963f-41ed-92f1-8914c1677ab9?mi_cid=...`. These are first-party and served over HTTPS, so this is minor.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `alt` on 6 content images.** All are hosted on `image.mail.cb2.com/lib/fe9213727564027a72/m/1/`:
  - `4393627b-2943-4652-978d-65bbbeb4efe4.jpg`
  - `9192bf0d-4ac5-455e-9943-b8830c0a82ed.jpg`
  - `90e47615-6a75-4601-bfdf-ac4bf2a3e4ea.jpg`
  - `d334caa9-ab70-43c7-8c21-c012ba0e1037.jpg`
  - `053b2049-1508-4dd4-8eb7-67c1470bc46e.jpg`
  - `690846ad-562b-45ae-a39f-3ddff75470dd.jpg`
  
  If these carry sale content, the message is lost with images blocked or in a screen reader. If they are decorative, they should have `alt=""`.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** The viewport meta is `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1"`. The CSS also sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on `*` and `body`. Together these block zoom and text scaling, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10 concern.
- **Font declarations are inconsistent.**
  - `@font-face` URLs use a double slash (`https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...`).
  - `Lineto Brown Sub` is declared several times with conflicting `src` and `font-weight`.
  - `HNW05` is loaded from `crateandbarrel.com`, a different domain from the sender.
  - Web fonts fall back in most clients, so this is low severity.
- **Template leftover in the source:** `<!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->`. It is harmless but shows an unfinished template.
- The Outlook conditional blocks, VML/Office namespaces and `lang="en"` are present and look correct in the visible source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens appear in the truncated HTML or the QA output. Tracking URLs carry identifiers (`mi_u=<hash>`, `mi_cid`, `mi_mid`), but they are populated. I can't verify the rest of the body because the source is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found (WARN).** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a proven absence.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (WARN).** Without it, one-click unsubscribe under RFC 8058 can't be confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found (WARN).** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sending domain is `mail.cb2.com`.
- **Body unsubscribe link and physical postal address not verified.** Neither is visible in the truncated source or reported by QA. They are likely in the footer, which was cut off.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
No data available. All 57 tracking links were skipped, so UTM parameters, redirect targets and landing-page alignment can't be assessed from this run.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-run the compliance checks against raw `.eml` headers, bypassing the relay. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
2. Resolve one or two sample tracking links with redirect-following enabled. Record the final landing URLs and their UTMs.
3. Add `alt` text (or `alt=""` if decorative) to the six `image.mail.cb2.com` images.
4. Ask the sender whether the Dotomi pixels can be served over HTTPS, or removed. As written, 11 pixels load over HTTP.
5. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta and `text-size-adjust:none` from the CSS, so zoom and text scaling work.
6. Capture the full HTML, including the footer, to verify the unsubscribe link and postal address.
