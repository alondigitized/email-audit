# Add these to your art collection
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- "Add these to your art collection" is CB2 continuing its editorial gear after last week's heavy promotional drumbeat — and it's the right instinct. The hero leans into art-as-interior-identity, leading with an oversized vintage map print in a styled living room under the headline "ART + EDUCATION." That curatorial positioning is genuinely interesting and differentiates CB2 from Crate or West Elm doing a straight art sale.
- The execution wobbles mid-email. A "$5,000" offer block appears without enough legibility at scroll depth to land — and given that yesterday's send was already a $1K sweepstakes, running a second prize mechanic back-to-back risks training subscribers to see CB2 as a contest newsletter, not an art destination. The bottom product grid — small vessels, glassware, decorative objects — feels grafted on and pulls the editorial thread apart.
- Honest verdict: a genuinely distinctive subject line and a strong hero, undermined by a confusing mid-email offer and a cluttered bottom grid that erases the art mood it built at the top.

## What's working

- The "ART + EDUCATION" hero headline is an unexpected editorial framing — positions CB2 as curator, not just retailer.
- Hero lifestyle image (vintage map print in warm living room) is well-shot and aspirational; the eye lands on it immediately.
- Subject line is short (32 chars), mobile-friendly, and uses second-person possessive ("your art collection") for a soft personalization effect.
- Clean render — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breaks visible.
- Following yesterday's SO MUCH NEW editorial reset with another non-sale editorial send shows cadence discipline.

## What's weak

- The "$5,000" mid-email block is illegible at a glance — unclear if it's a sweepstakes, a design consultation, or a product price, and arriving one day after the $1K app sweepstakes it reads as a contest cadence rather than a considered offer.
- "ART + EDUCATION" as a brand concept is never explained — the pairing of two abstract nouns creates intrigue but delivers no payoff visible in the scroll.
- Bottom product grid (vases, vessels, glassware) breaks the art-collection frame completely; these items aren't "art" to most subscribers and dilute the editorial identity built in the hero.
- No price anchoring on any featured art piece — the hero map print has no visible starting price, making the browse feel unactionable.
- CTA buttons are not distinguishable in the render; there's no clear primary call-to-action that surfaces above the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or explain the $5,000 block** — if it's a sweepstakes, don't bury it mid-scroll the day after a $1K contest; if it's a design service value, say exactly that ("Get a $5K-value design session, on us"). Ambiguity here does negative work.
- 2. **Price-anchor the hero artwork** — even "starting at $X" makes the hero feel shoppable rather than editorial wallpaper.
- 3. **Cut the bottom vessel/glassware grid** — it contradicts the art-collection thesis. If you need scroll depth, use more art prints at varying price points, or pull in a "how to hang" editorial moment.
- 4. **Resolve "ART + EDUCATION"** — one line of copy explaining what "education" means (a guide? a curation service? a style quiz?) would make this a genuinely differentiated send rather than a decorative headline.
- 5. **Add one visible CTA above the fold** — "Shop the collection" or "Browse art prints" as a button under the hero image.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your next statement piece is a print`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Maps, prints & originals — curated by CB2`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New framed art starting at $XX — the collection that changes a room.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Art you'd actually hang. New prints, maps, and originals now in.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Mobile users cannot scale the email. Change to `maximum-scale=5` or remove the cap.

**Universal `text-size-adjust: none` overrides user agent accessibility settings**
```css
* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; -ms-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
Applied twice (also in `body {}`). The `*` selector is overly broad and suppresses OS-level accessibility font scaling.

**Double-slash URLs trigger 301 redirects on font assets**
Three font `src:` values have `//` after the domain:
```
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2
https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2
```
Most servers handle this with a redirect, adding latency. Normalize to single slash.

**Conflicting `@font-face` declarations for `"Lineto Brown Sub"`**
Three blocks declare the same family name with overlapping weight logic:
- Block 1: no `font-weight` (defaults to 400)
- Block 2: explicit `font-weight: 400`
- Block 3: explicit `font-weight: 600`

Block 1 and Block 2 resolve to the same descriptor, pointing to different source files (`lineto-brown-light` vs `lineto-brown-regular`). Browser behavior is undefined; one declaration will silently win.

**Cross-domain font dependency on `crateandbarrel.com`**
`HNW05` (all four weights) loads from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/`. A CDN change or downtime on that sibling domain degrades CB2 email rendering independently.
