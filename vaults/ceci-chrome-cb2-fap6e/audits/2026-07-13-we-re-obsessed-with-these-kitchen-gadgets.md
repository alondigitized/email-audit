# We’re OBSESSED with these kitchen gadgets
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Barrel pivoting from the Summer Sale blasts to an editorial-mode send — curated kitchen gadgets framed through an "editors are obsessed" lens. The tone shift is smart after back-to-back promotional hammers: yesterday's 60% off hero and the "hand-assembled in the USA" brand story piece. Variety keeps subscribers from tuning out.
- The problem is execution: the "OBSESSED" subject is influencer-adjacent copy that doesn't quite fit Crate & Barrel's aspirational-home register, and the email leans heavily on long product grids without surfacing a clear price hook in the hero. It's a catalog scroll, not a converting email. The Traeger section buried deeper in the email is the strangest inclusion — outdoor grills feel like a channel mismatch for a kitchen gadgets curated list.
- Judgement: respectable editorial filler, but it'll convert well below the Summer Sale send from yesterday. No offer tension, no urgency, no anchor price.

## What's working

- **Editorial framing is a real cadence pivot.** After two back-to-back sale/free-shipping blasts from Crate & Kids and C&B proper, an "editors' picks" angle gives subscribers a reason to open that isn't discount fatigue.
- **Brand recognition is instant.** The C&B logo, color system, and photography style are unmistakable and trustworthy — no moment of "who is this from?"
- **Product grid density is high.** Multiple individual products are surfaced with images — this is a legitimate browse-mode email for kitchen enthusiasts.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **No offer in the hero.** The Summer Sale (60% off) was live as of yesterday's email — if these gadgets are on sale, the hero completely hides it. Editorial tone without a price hook is a click killer.
- **"OBSESSED" in all-caps reads as influencer copy, not Crate & Barrel copy.** The brand usually leads with aspiration or craft language — this subject line could have come from a TikTok shop roundup.
- **Traeger inclusion is a misfit.** Outdoor grilling equipment in a kitchen gadgets curated list breaks the tightness of the premise. It reads like a paid placement or category cross-sell that wasn't editorially justified.
- **Email length undermines urgency.** A catalog this long with no deadline, no countdown, and no visible discount is asking a lot of subscriber attention on a summer Sunday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tie the editorial to the live Summer Sale.** If these gadgets are already discounted, say so in the hero — "Our editors are obsessed. So is our sale." One line in the hero that bridges the two would triple click intent.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to be more specific about the payoff.** "We're OBSESSED" tells me nothing I want to click on. What are the gadgets? Give me one.
- 3. **Cut the Traeger section or make it a standalone send.** It dilutes the kitchen-gadgets premise. If grill season is the story, that's a different email.
- 4. **Tighten the email to 4–5 hero products** with more editorial copy per item (why editors love it, what it's for). Fewer items, more conviction.

- **Subject Alt B:** `5 kitchen gadgets worth buying this summer`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (accessibility regression)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4. Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust:none` on universal selector overrides body setting**
```css
*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none; ...}
```
The `body` rule sets `100%`; the `*` rule then resets it to `none` for all descendants. This suppresses font scaling on older WebKit browsers. Change `*` rule to `100%` or remove it.

**Conflicting `img` max-width media queries**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img {max-width: 600px} }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img {max-width: 640px} }
```
At ≥768px, both rules apply — cascade resolves to `600px` (last declared wins). Between 640–767px, `640px` applies as intended. At ≥768px the narrower constraint wins, which may clip images on wide viewports. Reorder or consolidate: put the 640px rule after the 768px rule, or use a single `max-width` breakpoint.

**Dark mode forced off**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
Opts out of dark mode entirely in Apple Mail. No dark-mode `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block present. This is a deliberate choice but worth flagging — ~30% of Apple Mail users have dark mode active.

**Duplicate `format-detection` meta tags**
```html
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no,address=no,email=no,date=no,url=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="date=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="address=no">
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no">
```
The combined declaration on line 1 covers all cases; the three individual declarations are redundant. No functional impact, but indicates template hygiene drift.

**Duplicate charset declarations**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8" />
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
The `http-equiv` form is correct for the declared XHTML 1.0 doctype; `charset=""` is HTML5 syntax. Redundant but not harmful.
