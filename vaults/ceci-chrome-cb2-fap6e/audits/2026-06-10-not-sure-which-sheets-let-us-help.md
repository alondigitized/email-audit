# Not sure which sheets? Let us help.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- Crate & Kids is back in buyer's guide mode — "Not sure which sheets? Let us help." is a direct play on purchase hesitation, the same editorial strategy that powered the coffee table guide (2026-06-09, 8/10). The execution follows: a clean hero image of soft, layered bedding, followed by a multi-section breakdown of sheet collections organized by material or use case. No discount, no urgency — pure education.
- The approach is well-matched to the product category. Sheets are a tactile, confusing purchase (percale vs. sateen, thread counts, toddler vs. twin sizing), and a guide format that names the friction point in the subject line is genuinely useful. The photography is consistent, the layout scrolls cleanly, and no render issues are visible.
- The ceiling here is the email's passivity. The hesitation framing ("not sure?") invites consideration but doesn't resolve it into action. A strong guide needs a conviction moment — a recommendation, a best-seller callout, or a single hero product — and from the render this email appears to present multiple parallel options without ranking them. That dilutes the click.

## What's working

- Subject line directly addresses purchase hesitation, which is a stronger hook than generic "new arrivals" copy.
- Hero photography is soft, lifestyle-appropriate, and immediately communicates the category without text.
- Multi-section layout makes it easy to self-select into the right product — good scannable structure.
- No render bugs; the column layout holds and text/images appear properly aligned throughout.
- Consistent with Crate & Kids' editorial voice — no tonal whiplash from a sudden sale pitch.

## What's weak

- No clear "best pick" or editorial recommendation — the guide presents options side by side without a point of view, which leaves the purchase decision exactly where it started.
- No pricing, discount, or offer of any kind — for a brand that has run frequent promos, a guide with zero price anchor may feel inert by comparison.
- Small, low-prominence CTAs throughout; from this render they read as secondary links rather than action-driving buttons.
- Heavy send cadence across the Crate & Barrel/Kids portfolio (this is at least the 5th email in ~two days) — fatigue risk is real and this send doesn't have urgency to cut through it.
- "Let us help" in the subject resolves the question too vaguely — it promises help without hinting at what the help looks like (a quiz? a ranked list? a material guide?).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single editorial recommendation.** Pick one sheet collection as "the one we'd start with" — a "best for toddlers" or "our most-washed, still-soft pick" callout breaks the analysis paralysis this email tries to address but doesn't resolve.
- 2. **Make the CTA copy do more work.** Replace generic "Shop [collection]" links with something that carries the guide voice: "See why percale works for hot sleepers" or "Find the right size for your kid's bed."
- 3. **Anchor with a price or value signal.** Even one "starting at $X" or "free shipping on orders over $X" gives the reader a reason to act now vs. bookmark.
- 4. **Thin the send cadence.** This send would land better after a gap — the volume from 6/9 to 6/10 risks training subscribers to ignore Crate & Kids emails.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our material guide + the one pick we'd start with`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Soft, breathable, easy to wash — here's what that actually means`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Conflicting image `max-width` media queries:**
```css
@media only screen and (min-width: 768px) { img { max-width: 600px } }
@media only screen and (min-width: 640px) { img { max-width: 640px } }
```
At viewports ≥ 768px, both queries fire; the 768px rule wins (600px), which is correct for a 600px container. At 640–767px, only the 640px rule applies, allowing images to render 40px wider than the container. Low visual impact but technically inconsistent.

**Dark mode opted out globally:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light">
```
No `prefers-color-scheme: dark` stylesheet block present in the excerpt. Apple Mail and Gmail dark-mode will apply automatic color inversion, which can render backgrounds and text unpredictably. Intentional or not, this is a deliberate light-only stance with no fallback styling.

**`max-height: 1000000px` on `*`:**

This is a common iOS auto-zoom workaround, but applying it to the universal selector can interfere with animated GIFs and some CSS-driven interactive elements in supported clients.
