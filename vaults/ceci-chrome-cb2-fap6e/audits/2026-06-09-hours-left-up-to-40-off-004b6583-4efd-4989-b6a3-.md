# HOURS LEFT: Up to 40% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is CB2's panic-close send — a classic "HOURS LEFT" urgency blast at the tail end of the Midseason Sale. The subject lands hard on the sale deadline, the hero visual delivers the brand's usual high-quality photography, but the email quickly loses focus: within two scrolls you're looking at a Design Legends section advertising only 20% off, a $5,000 review sweepstakes, and a "More For You" product grid. Three agendas fighting each other is two too many for an urgency send.
- The most damaging detail is the discount mismatch: the subject promises "Up to 40% off" but the most prominent mid-body sale module reads "Up to 20% off" (Design Legends). A subscriber who opened for 40% off and immediately sees 20% feels deceived before they even reach the real Midseason items. That's a trust leak at the worst possible moment.
- This is also CB2's sixth or seventh email in three days, following the June 8th "Vintage reissues on sale. Only at CB2." send that already featured the Design Legends Sale at the same 20% figure. The creative well is visibly running dry, and a subscriber tracking this cadence has every reason to start ignoring the next one.

## What's working

- **Urgency framing is immediate and unambiguous** — "HOURS LEFT: Up to 40% off" is the right move for a closing-hours send; short, concrete, action-forcing.
- **Hero photography holds the CB2 standard** — the warm, terracotta-toned Midseason Sale image is polished and on-brand.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is visible above the fold** — no hunting required; the primary action is accessible without scrolling.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean across the visible sections; no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **40% in subject vs. 20% in body** — the Design Legends Sale module prominently shows 20% off, which undercuts the subject's top-line claim and feels like bait-and-switch to a fatigued subscriber.
- **Review sweepstakes is a conversion killer** — "LEAVE US A REVIEW, WIN $5,000!" in the middle of an urgency close-out email drains purchase momentum and splits the click intent.
- **Three distinct agendas in one send** — Midseason Sale, Design Legends Sale, and a sweepstakes. An urgency email should have one job.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the sixth+ CB2 send since June 6th; the "HOURS LEFT" hook loses credibility when the recipient has been in a perpetual sale for a week.
- **Subject is all-caps opener** — "HOURS LEFT:" reads as a spam signal in many inbox previewers and erodes the premium brand positioning CB2 usually maintains.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the review sweepstakes entirely** — it is the single biggest click-momentum drain in this email. Move it to a post-purchase trigger or a standalone loyalty send.
- 2. **Reconcile the discount claim** — either lead the body with an actual 40%-off product or change the subject to match the Design Legends 20% figure. The mismatch is the #1 trust problem.
- 3. **Cut the email to hero + one CTA + one product grid** — for a close-out urgency send, fewer elements = more conversions. Remove the Design Legends module or absorb it into the hero.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to break from ALL-CAPS pattern** — CB2's best recent subjects ("*This* is midcentury", "Every room needs something green") use editorial voice. Lean into that.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Some of our most-loved pieces are still on sale — but not for long.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sofas, chairs, rugs — all still 40% off through tonight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before body links appear. The following is flaggable from the `<head>` alone:

- **Double-slash in font URLs (4 instances):** The `src` attributes in `@font-face` blocks use `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/...` (two forward slashes). While most servers normalize this, it is a malformed URL and should be corrected.
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownLight/lineto-brown-light.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/GTSectra/GT-Sectra-Display-Regular.woff2`
  - `https://www.cb2.com//Styles/CB2/fonts/BrownRegular/lineto-brown-regular.woff2`

- **Cross-brand font CDN dependency:** Four `@font-face` `src` values pull from `https://www.crateandbarrel.com/webfonts/...` (sibling brand). If that domain has a CDN outage or CORS policy change, CB2 email font rendering breaks independently of cb2.com uptime.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Template placeholder not removed:** A raw dev comment remains in `<head>`:
  ```
  <!--[IMPUT HERE CLIENT FONT IMPORT SCRIPT if needed]-->
  ```
  Note also the typo: "IMPUT" instead of "INPUT." This is a pre-send QA failure — template scaffolding was shipped to recipients.

- **`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta:** Prevents user-initiated zoom. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is flagged by accessibility validators. Users with low vision cannot zoom the email.
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
  ```

- **Aggressive `text-size-adjust: none` on `*`:** The global reset `*{-webkit-text-size-adjust:none; -ms-text-size-adjust:none;}` disables all OS-level text scaling. Compounds the zoom issue above.

- **Global `line-height: 100%` on `*`:** Applied universally via `*{line-height: 100%}`, which collapses line height on multi-line text blocks to effectively single-spacing with no descender room. This is commonly overridden inline but increases fragility.

- **Mobile breakpoint is 418px (`max-width: 418px`):** The breakpoint triggers only on viewports ≤418px. iPhone 14/15 Pro (393px CSS width) and SE (375px) fall within it, but several mid-range Android devices at 420–430px will receive the desktop layout on a phone screen.
