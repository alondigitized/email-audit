# Nuna, Uppababy, Stokke… all the best baby gear is here →
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Crate & Kids doing what it does best when it isn't running a countdown-timer marathon: a clean, category-led gear guide. The subject promises specific name-brand products — Nuna, Uppababy, Stokke — and the hero and body actually deliver on that with real product shots (infant car seats, convertible car seats, strollers, high chairs) organized into logical shopping categories. Compared to the app-teaser and countdown-timer sends I've logged from this brand all week, this is a legitimate step up in relevance and craft.
- The catch is there's no offer anywhere. Every section is "Shop Now" with no price, no percentage off, no loyalty angle — just curated merchandising. For a category as high-consideration and price-sensitive as baby gear (strollers and car seats run into the hundreds), a "best of" email with zero pricing signal is a missed opportunity to close. The $5,000 sweepstakes buried mid-scroll is the only urgency/incentive mechanic in the whole send, and it's easy to miss.
- Net: strong product-market fit on subject-to-content alignment, weak on conversion mechanics. This beats the recent Crate & Kids sends on relevance but still leaves real money on the table by not pairing brand-name gear with any kind of offer.

## What's working

- Subject line names three specific recognizable baby-gear brands (Nuna, Uppababy, Stokke) and the hero section delivers exactly that — real stroller/car seat photography, not stock lifestyle filler.
- Clear category taxonomy: car seats → strollers → high chairs → new arrivals → bestsellers, each with its own "Shop Now" CTA button, which mirrors how a parent actually shops.
- Editorial framing ("the very best baby gear," "fan-fave strollers," "most-loved high chairs") reads as curated rather than promotional, which fits a considered-purchase category.
- No render bugs — every product tile, button, and image block displays cleanly.
- The bottom "we love these for you" grid adds a personalized-feeling product rail with visible star ratings.

## What's weak

- Zero visible price, discount, or offer anywhere in the body — for big-ticket items like strollers and car seats, this is a real gap versus competitors who lead with price.
- The $5,000 sweepstakes entry is visually buried between the bestsellers module and the app-download block — it's the only urgency mechanic in the email and it's easy to scroll past.
- Star ratings on the "we love these for you" tiles are tiny and low-contrast — social proof is present but not doing much work visually.
- The email ends with an app-download push ("get $500... download the app") that's disconnected from the baby-gear narrative — feels bolted on, same pattern I flagged in the 2026-09-10 Crate & Kids app-teaser send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a visible price anchor or offer somewhere near the hero — even "shop new arrivals from $49" or a loyalty callout ("members save 15% on strollers") would give high-consideration shoppers a reason to click now instead of bookmarking for later.
- 2. Promote the $5,000 sweepstakes into its own section near the top, not sandwiched between merchandising blocks — it's currently the strongest hook in the email and it's underused.
- 3. Cut or relocate the app-download CTA — it dilutes a well-executed gear-focused send; if it must stay, move it to a standalone follow-up rather than tacking it onto a product email.
- 4. Increase contrast/size on the star ratings in the "we love these for you" rail so social proof actually registers at a glance.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: enter for a chance to win $5,000 toward your nursery`
