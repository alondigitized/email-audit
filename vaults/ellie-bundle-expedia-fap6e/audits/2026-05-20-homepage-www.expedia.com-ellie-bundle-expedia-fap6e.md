# Homepage snapshot · www.expedia.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I opened Expedia expecting to see flight-hotel bundle deals and One Key Cash balance, and instead I hit a bot wall. This is not a homepage — it's a CAPTCHA challenge asking me to "show my human side" with a slide-to-verify widget. As a Sunday-morning-coffee trip researcher on my phone, this is the worst possible first experience: I'm blocked before I even see a search bar.

## What's working

- **The Expedia logo and wordmark** are immediately recognizable — at least I know I'm in the right place before the wall hits.
- **"Submit feedback" link** is present — points for giving me a way to flag the problem rather than just leaving me stranded.

## What's weak

- **The CAPTCHA itself** — I'm a real person with coffee in hand, researching a beach trip. Being accused of being a bot on my first tap is humiliating and infuriating.
- **Zero personalization** — no "Welcome back, Ellie" prompt, no One Key Cash hook, nothing that recognizes me as a returning customer.
- **The error explanation copy** — listing "There is a robot on the same network (IP 75.140.9.0) as you" reads like a server log, not customer copy. This is not the voice I want from a brand I trust with my family's travel plans.
- **No fallback path** — there's no "Sign in instead" or "Continue as guest" escape hatch. If the slide widget fails (common on mobile), I'm completely stuck.
- **No offer, no search, no nothing** — I came to bundle flights + hotel for summer. I got a bouncer.

## Recommendations

- **Fix the mobile bot-detection threshold immediately** — real users on shared home/office networks should not trigger this. A logged-in One Key session should bypass the challenge entirely; use session auth as the signal.
- **Add a "Sign in to continue" escape hatch** — if the challenge fires anyway, give me a signed-in path out. My account is proof I'm human.
- **Rewrite the CAPTCHA copy in a customer-friendly voice** — "Just making sure it's you — slide to continue" instead of IP-address forensics. Keep it warm, not accusatory.
- **If the block is unavoidable, surface the loyalty hook anyway** — even on an interstitial, a "Your One Key Cash is waiting" line keeps me motivated to get past the friction instead of bouncing to Booking.com.
