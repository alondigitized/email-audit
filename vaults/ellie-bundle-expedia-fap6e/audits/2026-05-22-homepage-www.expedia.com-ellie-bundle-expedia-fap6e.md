# Homepage snapshot · www.expedia.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened Expedia on my phone and instead of the homepage I got a bot check — "Show us your human side… We can't tell if you're a human or a bot." There's a slider CAPTCHA and an IP-level block notice. As a traveler trying to research a family trip, this is the worst possible first impression: the site literally won't let me in. Whatever is behind this wall is irrelevant because I never get to see it.

## What's working

- The bot-check page itself renders cleanly — no broken layout, text is legible, the slider widget appears functional.
- A "Submit feedback" link is present, which at least gives a stuck user somewhere to go.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage** — I can't see it. A bot wall is not a homepage.
- **The IP block message** is unusually detailed and clinical ("IP 75.140.9.0"), which feels technical and cold for a consumer-facing product. Reading "there is a robot on the same network as you" makes me feel accused, not helped.
- **No recovery path** — there's no "try again," no phone number, no alternate sign-in. If the slider doesn't work, I'm stuck.
- **Zero trust signals** — for a site that handles my credit card and travel itineraries, greeting me with a suspicion screen before I've done anything destroys trust immediately.
- **No Expedia brand warmth** — the Expedia logo sits above pure accusatory text. A brand built on wanderlust and family memories is leading with "we think you're a bot."

## Recommendations

- **Serve the homepage first, verify in the background** — most real users should pass passive bot scoring; only challenge on suspicious signals, not by default for mobile browsers.
- **If a challenge is unavoidable, frame it warmly** — "Just a quick check before we get you to your next adventure" beats "We can't tell if you're a human or a bot."
- **Add a One Key / sign-in escape hatch** — let logged-in loyalty members bypass the check entirely by authenticating first. That's a real return-visitor hook and removes friction for the customer segment most worth protecting.
- **Strip the raw IP address** from the consumer-facing copy — telling me my IP is flagged reads like a security audit, not a travel brand.
