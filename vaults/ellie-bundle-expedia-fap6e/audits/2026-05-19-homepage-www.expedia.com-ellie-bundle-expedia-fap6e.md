# Homepage snapshot · www.expedia.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened Expedia on my phone, coffee in hand, ready to price out a flight-hotel-car bundle for our summer trip — and instead of a homepage, I got a bot wall. The whole screen is a "Show us your human side..." CAPTCHA slider with an IP flag and a reference ID. I never got to see deals, search boxes, or One Key Cash. This isn't a homepage experience; it's a closed door.

## What's working

- The Expedia logo and wordmark are visible and recognizable — at least I know I'm in the right place.
- The explanation text ("You are browsing and clicking at a speed much faster than expected of a human being") is actually more transparent than most CAPTCHA pages; it tells me *why* I'm blocked.
- The "Submit feedback" link is present, offering a human escape valve.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is inaccessible.** A bot gate on Sunday-morning browse mode is the worst possible experience for a high-intent planner like me. I don't have time to fight a slider; I'll just open Booking.com instead.
- **No bypass for logged-in members.** If I had One Key Cash and was already signed in on another tab, there's zero recognition of that — I'm treated as a bot regardless.
- **IP-level flagging (75.140.9.0) with no easy resolution.** The explanation mentions a "robot on the same network," which means I'm collateral damage from a shared connection. There's no "I'm a member, let me in" path.
- **Reference ID is shown but useless.** `ID: 167b9971-a900-5f45-12db-0dfba7111151` means nothing to me as a consumer. It's internal debug noise surfaced at the worst moment.
- **No search box, no deals, no bundle builder** — the entire homepage value prop is zeroed out.

## Recommendations

- **Offer a member sign-in bypass.** If I can authenticate via One Key / Expedia account, skip the CAPTCHA or fast-path me through — logged-in traffic should not hit the same bot filter as anonymous crawlers.
- **Replace the raw IP address with plain language.** "Someone on your Wi-Fi may have triggered our security check" lands better than "IP 75.140.9.0" — reduce the technical jargon to reduce panic/bounce.
- **Add a "Call us instead" or live-chat CTA** on the block page so a high-intent user (me, trying to book a family trip) has somewhere to go that isn't just "try again."
- **Tune the bot-detection threshold for mobile browsing speeds.** Sunday-morning mobile browsing with a latte in hand is human speed; the slider sensitivity appears set for desktop-bot detection and flags real people.
