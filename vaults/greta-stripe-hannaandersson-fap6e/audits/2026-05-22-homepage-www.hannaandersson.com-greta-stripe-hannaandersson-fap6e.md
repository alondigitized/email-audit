# Homepage snapshot · www.hannaandersson.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened Hanna Andersson on my phone on a Sunday morning with my coffee ready to shop — and got a blank white wall with a bot check instead of a homepage. The entire viewport is a Cloudflare-style "Press & Hold to confirm you are a human" challenge. Not a single product, promo, or stripe in sight. For a brand I trust with my kids' clothes, this is a jarring, anxiety-inducing first impression.

## What's working

- The "Press & Hold" button is technically large, centered, and tappable on mobile — if I had to interact with a verification wall, at least the button is finger-friendly
- The reference ID at the bottom (`4964f44e-55d1-11f1-9888-07de23979e84`) gives me something to share with support if I'm stuck — a very thin silver lining

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is blocked — I see zero brand content, zero product, zero Hanna
- The challenge copy ("you are a human and not a bot") is clinical and cold — feels like a security alert, not a brand I trust for my kids' Christmas PJs
- The white void above and below the challenge text wastes the majority of the screen — no reassurance that I'm even on the right site (no logo, no brand color, no stripes)
- No Hanna branding visible at all — I have no confirmation this is hannaandersson.com without already knowing the URL I typed

## Recommendations

- **Show the brand while gating:** overlay the bot check on a blurred or dimmed version of the actual homepage hero — I should see Hanna stripes and smiling kids behind the challenge modal, not pure white
- **Add the Hanna logo above the challenge prompt** — a single wordmark would confirm I'm in the right place and soften the security friction dramatically
- **Replace "confirm you are a human (and not a bot)" with something warm and on-brand** — something like "Just a quick check before we show you the good stuff" fits the Hanna voice far better
- **Tune bot-detection thresholds for mobile Sunday-morning patterns** — loyal customers shopping on phones during weekend coffee time should not be hitting this wall; the false-positive rate here is a real retention problem
