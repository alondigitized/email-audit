# Homepage snapshot · www.hannaandersson.com · 2026-07-05
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- I opened hannaandersson.com on my phone on a Sunday morning with my coffee, and I didn't get the homepage — I got a bot wall. The entire above-the-fold experience is a "Before we continue... Press & Hold to confirm you are a human (and not a bot)" interstitial. There is zero brand content, zero product, zero offer visible. This is not a homepage review — it's a friction-first gate review, and that's the story.

## What's working

- The "Press & Hold" button is large, centered, and readable on mobile — if I have to interact with a bot gate, at least the tap target is easy to hit
- The reference ID (`0cfd a0d6-785a-11f1-8017-c546b76c7d63`) is visible at the bottom, which at least gives me something to reference if I need to contact support about being blocked

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is hidden behind a bot-detection interstitial — I came here to shop kids' organic cotton, and I'm being interrogated before I see a single stripe
- Zero brand presence above the fold: no logo, no Hanna colors, no iconic stripe imagery, no product, nothing that says "you're in the right place"
- No offer, no navigation, no category entry point — a Sunday-morning mom in shopping mode will close this tab before she finishes her first sip of coffee
- The white-screen experience is visually indistinguishable from a broken or phishing site — there's no Hanna Andersson branding to reassure me I'm even on the right domain
- "Press & Hold" is a desktop-derived interaction pattern that feels awkward on a phone — I'm holding a coffee cup in my other hand

## Recommendations

- Add the Hanna Andersson logo and brand-colored background to the bot-gate page itself — even if I have to clear a check, at least let me know I landed on the right site
- Investigate why mobile organic traffic is hitting the bot gate at all — Sunday morning browsing from a residential IP should not look like bot traffic; this is real customer attrition
- A/B test bot-detection methods that don't require active user interaction (passive fingerprinting, cookie checks) so the gate is invisible to legitimate shoppers
- If the gate is unavoidable, add a one-liner below the button: "You're almost at Hanna Andersson — we just need a quick check" with a small stripe graphic, so the brand experience starts even in friction
