# Homepage snapshot · www.hannaandersson.com · 2026-06-07
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- I opened Hanna Andersson on my phone Sunday morning with my coffee and hit a wall — literally. The entire viewport is a bot-verification challenge, not the homepage. There's no product, no campaign, no stripes, nothing. From a shopper's perspective, this brand just turned away one of its most loyal customers at the door.

## What's working

- The "Press & Hold" button is clearly labeled and centered — at least the bot-gate itself is legible and not broken visually.
- The reference ID at the bottom (`80714e3b-6259-11f1-9523-5e49fbf30569`) gives IT something to trace, which is mildly reassuring if I were actually trying to escalate.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is invisible.** A loyal, high-AOV customer like me — someone who opens Hanna emails on Sunday morning and stocks up at the semi-annual sale — just got stopped cold before seeing a single product.
- **"Press & Hold" on mobile is a terrible UX pattern.** On a phone while holding a coffee cup with a 2-year-old nearby, this is genuinely annoying to complete. It's optimized to block bots, not welcome humans.
- **No brand identity whatsoever.** Not a single stripe, not a logo, not even a favicon hint that this is Hanna. This could be any website behind any bot wall.
- **The blank white space is enormous.** The challenge message sits in the middle of a completely empty white screen — roughly 60% of the viewport is dead whitespace. It communicates nothing while I wait.

## Recommendations

- **Move the bot-gate to a lighter fingerprinting approach (e.g., invisible challenge)** so returning shoppers on mobile never see this wall. Press & Hold is a friction point that kills the Sunday-morning browsing session before it starts.
- **If a hard gate is required, wrap it in brand chrome** — at minimum show the Hanna Andersson logo and a single iconic stripe above the challenge, so I know I'm in the right place and feel reassured rather than suspicious.
- **Add a soft fallback message** like "Just a quick check — we'll have you shopping in a second" so the wait feels intentional and friendly, not broken.
- **Test this flow specifically on mobile Safari** — "Press & Hold" behaves inconsistently across iOS versions and is far harder to trigger on a touchscreen than a trackpad.
