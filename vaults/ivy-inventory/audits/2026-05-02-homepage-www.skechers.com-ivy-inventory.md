# Homepage snapshot · www.skechers.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- This reads like a mid-tier athletic brand running a clearance push dressed up as a season launch. Two stacked promotions before I even hit the hero — BOGO 50% at top, 25% off apparel right below — tells me they're moving inventory, not building brand equity. The Slip-ins feature headline is the only product-forward move on the screen, and it lands below the fold.
- **4/10**

## What's working

- **BOGO 50% banner** is impossible to miss — blue strip, top of page, high-contrast yellow text. Promotion lands before anything else. That's disciplined placement.
- **Celebrity split hero** (Brooke Burke left, Tony Hawk right) gives the brand a lifestyle anchor for two distinct age-and-gender segments simultaneously. Efficient use of one hero slot.
- **"Hands Free Slip-ins" product headline** visible at the bottom of viewport — gives me an actual product story, not just a discount.
- **Cart indicator with item count** ("0") and account icon are exactly where I expect them. Navigation overhead is clean and fast to scan.

## What's weak

- **Two promotional banners stacked before the hero** — BOGO 50% plus 25% off apparel back-to-back signals panic pricing, not a curated sale event. One promo strip reinforces urgency; two reads as a clearance rack.
- **No visible CTA button in the hero image** — Brooke Burke and Tony Hawk are smiling but I have no "Shop Men's" or "Shop Women's" button visible at this viewport. I'd have to scroll or guess where to tap.
- **The celebrity names ("Brooke Burke," "Tony Hawk") are tiny overlaid text** — I can barely make them out. If the brand is paying for celebrity equity, it should be readable.
- **Hero communicates lifestyle, not product** — I can see sneakers on both models but no style name, no price, no tech callout. As an auditor, this tells me nothing about what I'm actually looking at.
- **"Spring Apparel Sale" banner is generic** — "Apparel" is doing no work here. A brand with Skechers' SKU depth should be more specific (activewear? hoodies? sneakers?).

## Recommendations

- **Kill the double-banner stack** — run one combined strip: "BOGO 50% + 25% Off Apparel — Shop Now." Frees a full horizontal band and reads as one coordinated sale, not two competing ones.
- **Add tappable category buttons directly on the hero image** — "Shop Women's" over Brooke Burke, "Shop Men's" over Tony Hawk. Obvious nav that also de-risks the dead hero click.
- **Enlarge the celebrity name callouts** or replace them with a style name + price badge so the hero doubles as a product card.
- **Lead the Slip-ins headline above the celebrity image**, not below it — that's the product story the brand owns; bury it below the fold and you've wasted it.
