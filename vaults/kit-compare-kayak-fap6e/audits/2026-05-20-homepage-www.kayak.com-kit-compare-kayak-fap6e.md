# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- First thing Kayak hits me with on mobile is a full-screen cookie wall confessing it uses tracking for "advertising purposes" — which, as someone who uses a VPN and blocks pixels on principle, is not the vibe. Behind it I can barely make out a search form and an orange "Search" button, which is exactly why I'm here. The brand is doing the right thing with the tool; the onboarding is doing everything wrong.

## What's working

- **"Search" button (orange, behind the modal):** Even partially obscured, the CTA is big, colored, and unmistakable — I know the tool is one tap away once I dismiss the wall.
- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill:** That proactive chip at the very bottom is genuinely smart UX. It reads like a machine that already knows what people want to do on a Tuesday in May — that's the Kayak I came for.
- **Pre-filled form defaults ("1 adult, Economy, 0 bags"):** Sensible defaults that save taps. Feels like the product team thought about the repeat user.
- **Honest cookie disclosure:** It says "advertising purposes" right in the body text. I still hate it, but I respect the transparency more than a dark-pattern opt-in.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal is the entire above-the-fold experience:** On mobile, 100% of the viewport is legal boilerplate. The travel brand effectively doesn't exist until I tap a button.
- **"Advertising, analytical, and functional" framing:** For someone like me who uses Kayak *specifically* to avoid OTA retargeting, leading with "we use advertising trackers" is actively off-brand. It re-frames Kayak as part of the ad ecosystem I'm trying to escape.
- **No returning-user recognition:** No "Welcome back, Kit" or "Your alerts (3)" or "Last search: SFO → JFK." I'm a power user and the page treats me like a cold first-timer.
- **Zero above-fold value prop:** There's no headline telling me why Kayak is better than going direct to the airline. The tool's whole job is to prove its search breadth — and I can't see any of it.

## Recommendations

- **Make "Manage preferences" vs. "I understand" more prominent and move it to a slim bottom banner** — preserve content visibility above the fold so the travel search tool is actually the first thing I see.
- **Add a returning-user module that surfaces active Price Alerts** — a "You have 2 active alerts" chip above the search form would be the single most relevant thing for a user like me.
- **Inject a session-aware headline** like "Compare 900+ airlines. Book direct." — speak to the meta-search value prop before I've even touched anything.
- **Swap the "Flights to London" pill for a personalized "Your last search" chip** once I'm logged in — a power user feature that earns loyalty without a points program.
