# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-05-27
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see is a cookie consent wall blocking the entire homepage — not ideal when I'm on mobile and I just want to check flight prices. Behind the modal I can make out the flight search form and an orange Search button, plus a cheeky AI prompt pill at the bottom reading "Flights to London next weekend." This is Kayak doing what Kayak does: search-first, utility-forward, no lifestyle fluff — but the gate that greets me before I see any of that is friction I didn't ask for.

## What's working

- **The orange "Search" CTA** — even half-obscured behind a modal, it reads as the primary action. High contrast, clear copy.
- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill** — this is the one thing that actually speaks to how I use Kayak. Frictionless, AI-assisted prompt that knows the use case. Native-feeling, not marketing-y.
- **Minimalist form design** — "Select dates / 1 adult, Economy, 0 bags" behind the modal tells me the search form is clean and defaults to sensible parameters without bombarding me with options.
- **Cookie language is legible** — "Terms & Conditions" and "Privacy Policy" are hyperlinked and the copy is plain English. I've seen worse.

## What's weak

- **The modal is the entire above-the-fold experience** — literally nothing about Kayak's value prop, current deals, or navigation is visible until I tap "I understand." On mobile, that's the whole screen. Worst possible first impression.
- **No Price Alerts hook** — I'm a Price Alerts subscriber. There's zero acknowledgment that I might be a returning user or have alerts set. No "Your saved searches" teaser, nothing.
- **Zero fare deals visible** — Kayak's whole pitch is "find the best price." I don't see a single price, deal, or promotion surfaced anywhere in what I can see.
- **"I understand" button design** — dark fill, white text, very generic. On mobile it looks more like a modal close button than a consent action, which is borderline deceptive UX even if technically compliant.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the consent modal by 2-3 seconds or trigger it on first interaction** — let me see the homepage for half a second before gating me. Or better, use a sticky bottom bar instead of a full-page takeover.
- **Surface a returning-user state** — if I've visited before or have Price Alerts, show "Welcome back — 3 routes are being tracked" above the search form. One line, massive loyalty signal.
- **Add a fare deal strip above the fold** — one line below the search bar: "Today's best fares from NYC → LAX from $89." That's Kayak's whole reason for existing. Show it immediately.
- **Swap "I understand" button copy to "Continue to search"** — makes the action legible and gets me to the actual product faster without the legal-feel friction.
