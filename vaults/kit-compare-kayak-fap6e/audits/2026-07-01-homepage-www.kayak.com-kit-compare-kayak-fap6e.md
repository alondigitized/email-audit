# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-07-01
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see is a full-screen cookie wall — not the search bar I came here for. The modal admits right in the second line that Kayak uses tracking for "advertising purposes," which is exactly the kind of thing that makes my skin crawl. The tiny sliver of UI visible behind it — a Search button and a "Flights to London next weekend" chip — tells me the actual product is competent, but I have to get past this gate first.

## What's working

- **The search bar / "Search" button** is visible enough below the modal to confirm I'm in the right place — the product intent (flight search) is not hidden.
- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion chip** is a smart contextual nudge; if that persisted from a previous session or is algorithmically inferred, that's actually the kind of feature that keeps me coming back.
- **Modal renders cleanly** — no jank, no layout breakage on mobile.

## What's weak

- **The cookie consent is the entire above-the-fold experience.** I opened Kayak to search flights, not to read a privacy disclosure. This is a wall.
- **"Advertising purposes"** is called out explicitly in the consent copy — for someone who uses Kayak *precisely because* they hate OTA retargeting, this is an immediate trust gut-punch.
- **No "Reject" or "Manage" option visible** — only "I understand," which is a classic consent dark pattern. I have to accept to proceed, full stop.
- **No returning-user recognition** — I'm a Price Alert subscriber. There's no "Welcome back," no alerts badge, no recent search pre-populated. Cold-start treatment for a loyal user.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a "Manage preferences" or "Reject non-essential" button** alongside "I understand" — users like me will respect that Kayak is upfront about ad tracking if we can actually opt out.
- **Pre-populate the search form** with my last search or active price alert destination so dismissing the modal drops me directly into a relevant flow, not a blank form.
- **Move the consent banner to a bottom strip** instead of a full-screen takeover — let the homepage breathe and show me what I came for.
- **Add a returning-user hook** above the search bar ("You have 3 active price alerts") so loyal meta-searchers know their data is being used *for them*, not just sold against them.
