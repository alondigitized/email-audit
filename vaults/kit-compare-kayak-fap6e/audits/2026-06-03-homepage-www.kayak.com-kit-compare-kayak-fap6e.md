# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-06-03
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- I literally cannot see the homepage. KAYAK greeted me with a full-screen cookie consent modal the instant I landed — "Welcome to KAYAK" in bold, followed by a paragraph about how they process my data for "advertising purposes." As someone who is *constitutionally allergic* to retargeting, this is not the vibe. Behind the modal I can make out a search form and a "Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill, which is actually the product I'm here for — but I have to click through a data-collection acknowledgment before I can see anything useful.

## What's working

- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill** — I can see it at the very bottom of the viewport peeking below the modal. Smart contextual nudge that anticipates what users want; if I could actually *see* the page, this would work well for me.
- **The "I understand" button** — renders cleanly, good tap target size, clear contrast on dark background. At least the modal itself is functional.
- **"1 adult, Economy, 0 bags" default** — visible behind the modal; default traveler config is correct for a solo meta-search user like me who knows exactly what they want.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen cookie consent modal on first load** — this is the entire experience above the fold. The homepage doesn't exist until I click through.
- **"Advertising purposes" disclosure** — explicitly mentioned in the modal copy. For a user who hates retargeting, KAYAK just told me it's going to follow me around the internet. Hostile framing.
- **Zero product value visible** — no price alerts, no deal strips, no search results, nothing that communicates why KAYAK is worth the cookie trade. They're asking for consent before they've shown me anything worth consenting for.
- **"Select dates" text visible but non-functional** — I can see the form label but can't interact with it until the modal is dismissed. Friction.

## Recommendations

- **Defer the consent modal by one interaction** — show me the search form first, trigger the modal when I hit "Search." I've already expressed intent; *then* ask for my data consent. This is how many EU-compliant travel sites handle it.
- **Reframe the consent copy for power users** — replace "advertising purposes" with something like "personalized price alerts" to lead with the user benefit, not the data-harvest mechanism. Functionally equivalent, massively different read for a retargeting-averse millennial.
- **Surface the "Flights to London next weekend" pill above the modal or make it the dismiss mechanism** — let me tap a destination suggestion and have that dismiss the modal + fire the search simultaneously. One tap, maximum intent signal.
- **Add a "Manage preferences" secondary link** — a retargeting-averse user who sees "I understand" as the only option will bounce or feel coerced. A visible opt-out path would actually increase trust and consent.
