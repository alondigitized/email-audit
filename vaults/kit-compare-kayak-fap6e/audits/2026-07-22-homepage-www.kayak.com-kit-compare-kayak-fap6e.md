# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-07-22
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- I opened Kayak — my literal go-to tool for sniffing out prices before booking direct — and the first thing I'm looking at is a GDPR-style cookie wall that swallows the entire screen. The brand is trying to be the smart meta-search layer between me and the airlines, but right now it's the dumb modal layer between me and everything else. Behind the fog I can make out a search bar and a "Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill, so the bones are clearly functional — if I could see them.

## What's working

- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill** — the one flash of brand personality I can see. It's conversational, timely-feeling, and pitched at exactly the impulsive planning mode I'm often in.
- **"Search" button** is in Kayak's signature burnt-orange, which I already associate with the tool — brand recognition doing the heavy lifting even through the modal glass.
- **Functional search form underneath**: "Select dates / 1 adult, Economy, 0 bags" — the defaults are sane, not trying to upsell me on business class before I've even consented to cookies.

## What's weak

- **Full-bleed cookie consent modal** that blocks 100% of the homepage — my first milliseconds with Kayak are spent dismissing legalese, not pricing a flight. This is the worst possible first impression for a power user who came here to search fast.
- **Zero personalization or returning-user signal** — I'm a Price Alert subscriber. Kayak knows me. There's no "Welcome back, Kit" or "Your alert for LAX→JFK triggered" — nothing.
- **No offer, no reason to stay** — the entire visible viewport is a consent screen. If I weren't already a convert, I'd have bounced before I saw a single flight.
- **"I understand" as the sole CTA** — that button gets all the visual weight, but it delivers zero brand value. Dismissing a cookie modal is the least satisfying tap in UX.

## Recommendations

- **Auto-dismiss or collapse the cookie notice into a bottom banner** — don't gate the entire homepage. A slim bottom strip keeps legal requirements intact while letting me actually see the search tool I came for.
- **Surface a returning-user module above the fold** — if I'm logged in (or if my Price Alerts are firing), show me that first. "Your alert: JFK → MIA dropped to $189 — book now" is the only hero copy that would make me tap immediately.
- **Make the "Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill plural and swipeable** — one suggestion is cute, a swipeable row of trending routes (priced) turns it into actual signal.
- **Add a subtle "no account needed, book direct" micro-copy line** near the Search button — that's Kayak's whole brand promise and it's invisible right now.
