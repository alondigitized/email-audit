# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I open Kayak — my go-to price radar — and the very first thing I see is a wall of GDPR legalese about "advertising purposes." That's not a welcome mat; that's a confession. Behind the modal I can just barely make out a flight search form and a "Flights to London next weekend" suggestion chip, which tells me the product itself is exactly what I came for — but I can't actually use it until I consent to being retargeted. For someone who opened this app specifically because I hate retargeting, the irony is loud.

## What's working

- **"Search" button** — even obscured, the orange CTA peeks through and I know exactly what to tap after I dismiss the modal. Color choice is strong, readable.
- **"Flights to London next weekend" suggestion chip** — this is smart UX. A quick-launch intent chip saves me typing and shows Kayak knows I came here to search flights, not browse editorial content.
- **"1 adult, Economy, 0 bags" pre-fill** — the form underneath has remembered or defaulted to a sensible search state; that saves friction post-modal.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal itself** — it's the entire above-the-fold experience, and it names "advertising purposes" three times. For someone whose whole deal is avoiding retargeting, this is Kayak telling on itself before I've done anything.
- **"I understand" as the sole CTA** — there's no "Reject non-essential" option visible in the button area. Classic dark pattern: one big dark button, no easy out. That breeds distrust immediately.
- **Invisible homepage** — there is zero brand differentiation happening right now. I could be on any random travel aggregator. No hero, no tagline, no reason to trust Kayak over Google Flights based on what I can actually see.
- **No loyalty or member hook** — Kayak has price alerts, which is literally my favorite feature. Zero mention of it in the visible area.

## Recommendations

- **Show a reject/manage option** at the same visual weight as "I understand" — or better, implement a consent banner that doesn't block the entire screen, just a bottom sheet. Burying the opt-out destroys trust with privacy-conscious users before the funnel even starts.
- **Move the Price Alert CTA above the fold** — "Set a price alert, book direct" is the exact message that lands for users like me. It's Kayak's actual differentiator vs. OTAs; lead with it.
- **Reduce the modal's text weight** — the current copy reads like a legal disclaimer written by lawyers for lawyers. One plain-English sentence ("We use cookies to make search work and, if you allow it, to personalize ads") would cut friction.
- **Inject a returning-user state** — if I've used Kayak before, show my last search, a saved alert, or my price-watch list the moment the modal clears. That instant relevance is what keeps me from bouncing to Google Flights.
