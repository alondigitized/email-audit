# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- The first thing I see when I open Kayak is a full-bleed cookie consent modal eating the entire screen — ironic for a site I use specifically *because* I don't want to be retargeted everywhere. Behind the modal I can barely make out a flight search form and one AI-style suggestion chip. This looks like a utilitarian price-comparison tool doing exactly what it says on the tin, but the modal kills the first impression hard.

## What's working

- **The suggestion chip** — "Flights to London next weekend" at the very bottom is actually clever. That's exactly how my brain works when I'm dreaming up a trip. An AI-powered prompt feels native to how I search.
- **The search form defaults** — "1 adult, Economy, 0 bags" is already set for solo travel, which is my default. Less friction to adjust than starting from scratch.
- **The orange Search CTA** — high-contrast, impossible to miss once the modal is dismissed. Good button hygiene.

## What's weak

- **The cookie modal annihilates the entire above-the-fold experience.** I came to search for flights, not read a GDPR disclosure. Full-screen treatment is overkill — a bottom sheet or slim banner would handle this without nuking the whole page.
- **No personalization hook whatsoever.** I'm a Price Alert subscriber. Kayak has my data. There's zero "your alerts" or "pick up where you left off" visible — not even behind the modal.
- **The modal's "advertising purposes" language is a vibe killer for me specifically.** I use Kayak to escape ad retargeting hell, and the first thing they do is tell me they're doing exactly that.
- **Zero urgency or contextual offer.** No "fares are up 12% for Memorial Day weekend" or "cheapest days to fly this summer" — the hook that would make me actually engage instead of dismiss and search.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the full-screen cookie modal with a bottom-anchored slim bar** — one line, two buttons (Accept / Manage). Don't make me read a legal brief before I can use the product.
- **Surface the Price Alert status for logged-in users immediately** — "Your 3 alerts: JFK→LHR is down $40" would get me to engage before I even touch the search form.
- **Add a contextual urgency rail behind the search form** — "Fares for this weekend are trending up" or a fare calendar preview. That's the signal I actually came here for.
- **Swap "advertising purposes" language in the modal for plain human copy** — "We use cookies to remember your searches and improve results" lands way better and doesn't make me feel like product.
