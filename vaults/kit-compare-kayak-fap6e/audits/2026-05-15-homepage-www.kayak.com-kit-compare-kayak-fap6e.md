# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- The first thing I see on kayak.com is not a flight search — it's a full-screen cookie consent modal that buries everything I actually came here for. Behind the popup I can just barely make out a search form ("Select dates", "1 adult, Economy, 0 bags") and an orange Search button, plus a "Flights to London next weekend" quick-search chip at the very bottom. Kayak knows exactly who I am — a serial price-alerter — but this opening salvo is a wall of GDPR legalese before I've even touched the product.

## What's working

- **The underlying Search button** — orange, high-contrast, visible even behind the modal. Kayak's core value prop (search) is one tap away the moment I dismiss.
- **"Flights to London next weekend" quick-search chip** at the bottom — this is actually smart UX peeking through. A pre-populated suggestion for a popular route reduces friction for people who know what they want.
- **Modal copy is plain English** — "essential, functional, analytical, and advertising purposes" is clearer than most cookie walls I've seen. No fake "personalized experience" spin.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself** — a full-bleed consent gate as the very first interaction is a dark pattern regardless of how clean the copy is. I came to search flights, not consent to ad tracking.
- **Zero returning-user signal** — I'm a Kayak Price Alert subscriber. There's no "Your alerts are live" badge, no recent-search shortcut, nothing that acknowledges I've been here before.
- **No offer or hook above the fold** — even a nudge like "Prices down 12% on Europe routes this week" would give me a reason to care before the modal clears.
- **Visual hierarchy is non-existent** — the modal forces all attention to "I understand," which is the least valuable action on the page for me.
- **Advertising acknowledgment** — the cookie text explicitly calls out "advertising purposes," which immediately flags retargeting. For someone allergic to ad tracking, that phrase is a red flag before I've even searched anything.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the full-bleed modal with a bottom-sheet banner** — let the search form be the hero. Consent can live at the bottom edge without nuking the entire first impression.
- **Add a returning-user Price Alert status chip** ("2 active alerts — check them here") that appears once cookies are accepted; it costs nothing and immediately differentiates Kayak from Expedia.
- **Surface the "Flights to London next weekend"-style chips before the modal clears** — or make them the focus of the hero. Quick-search chips are the fastest path to a search result for a power user.
- **Soften the "advertising purposes" language** or add a one-tap "Essential only" option directly in the modal so tech-native users don't feel cornered into all-or-nothing consent.
