# Homepage snapshot · www.kayak.com · 2026-06-17
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- The very first thing I see on kayak.com is a full-screen cookie consent wall — not a flight deal, not a price alert, not a search widget. Behind the modal I can barely make out "Select dates," "1 adult, Economy, 0 bags," a burnt-orange Search button, and a timely AI pill that reads "✦ Flights to London next weekend." Kayak clearly knows who I am in theory (meta-search, flight-first), but they're leading with legal compliance theater instead of the thing that would actually keep me on the page.

## What's working

- **The search widget behind the modal** — "Select dates / 1 adult, Economy, 0 bags / Search" is the exact task-mode interface I came here for. No clutter, no upsells obscuring the form.
- **"✦ Flights to London next weekend" suggestion pill** — this feels like Kayak knows I want to move fast. An AI prompt that matches how I actually think ("I'm doing a thing this weekend") is genuinely useful, and it's visible without scrolling.
- **The consent copy is straightforward** — "This site uses cookies and other tracking technologies for essential, functional, analytical, and advertising purposes." Honest enumeration. I can make a real choice. Respects my intelligence.

## What's weak

- **Modal blocks the entire above-the-fold experience** — I came to compare flights, not to manage cookies. Every second I spend reading this wall is a second I'm not in the search results. The retargeting mention ("advertising purposes") is also a small red flag for someone like me who's allergic to ad tracking.
- **Zero personalization hooks for returning users** — I am a Price Alert subscriber. My recent searches exist. None of that surfaces before the consent wall, and I can tell nothing is personalized behind it either.
- **No concrete offer or urgency** — no "fares from $89," no "5% off with KAYAK app," nothing to anchor a reason to be here rather than going straight to Google Flights.
- **"Advertising purposes" in the cookie text** — for someone who's specifically chosen Kayak over OTAs to avoid retargeting hell, seeing "advertising purposes" in the first sentence is a mild but real friction point.

## Recommendations

- **Dismiss or shrink the consent modal for returning users** — if I've consented before, surface my last search and my active price alerts the moment I land. The modal should never be the first thing a logged-in returning user sees.
- **Surface the "Flights to London next weekend" pill (or my personal alerts) above the modal** — make me feel recognized before you ask me for data permission. One alert callout ("Your SFO→LHR alert: fares dropped to $542") converts better than a cookie wall.
- **Add a "compare and go direct" framing** — I know Kayak pushes OTA links, but the power users who actually drive repeat traffic know how to use Kayak without booking through it. Lean into "find the fare, then decide how to book" — it's an honest value prop that keeps people coming back.
- **Show at least one real fare number** — even a "fares to London from $511 this weekend" headline copy behind or alongside the modal gives me a reason to hit "I understand" faster and start using the product.
