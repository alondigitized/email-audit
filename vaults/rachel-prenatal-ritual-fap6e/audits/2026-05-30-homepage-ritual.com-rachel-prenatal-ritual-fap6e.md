# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-05-30
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This looks like a clean, science-first supplement brand that's clearly thought about their audience — "traceable science and sourcing" is basically written for me. The Pregnancy tab in the nav is front and center, which immediately tells me they know who comes to this site. But above the fold it's all brand story and zero action — no CTA, no offer, no product detail — which means I'm intrigued but not yet moving.

## What's working

- **"Pregnancy" in the secondary nav** — I don't have to dig. It's the third tab. That's immediate signal that this brand knows their audience includes people like me.
- **"Traceable science and sourcing"** — this is the exact language that makes me slow down. I'm the person who googles "methylfolate vs folic acid at 11pm." This phrasing earns trust.
- **Minimal, clinical-clean visual aesthetic** — the soft blue background, the close-up of the product in hand, the "clear" capsule imagery — this reads premium and science-credible, not wellness-woo. I respond to that.
- **Video/animation cue (the ⏸ button)** — something is moving in that hero, which adds dynamism without being chaotic.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button above the fold** — there's a beautiful headline and then... nothing to tap. I came here comparing brands; I need a nudge, not a pause.
- **Hero image is product-close-up, not life-stage specific** — I can't tell if that person is pregnant, postpartum, or just health-conscious. A clear "planning" or prenatal context would close the loop.
- **No pricing, no offer, no shipping threshold** — Rachel-who-compares-with-Care/of needs to see cost signals early. Right now I have no anchor.
- **Subtext is cut off** — "Traceable science and sourcing—for" drops off mid-sentence. That's the most relevant line on the page and I can't finish reading it.

## Recommendations

- **Add a Pregnancy CTA button directly beneath the hero headline** — something like "Shop Prenatal" so the primary action is unambiguous before any scrolling.
- **Complete the truncated subtext** or tighten it so it doesn't get cut on mobile — if the hook is traceability, let me read the full hook.
- **Show the person's life stage in the hero** — even a subtle belly or nursery-prep visual context would tell me "this ad is for someone exactly where I am," not just "someone who cares about health."
- **Drop a free-shipping threshold or intro-offer badge** near the nav — one line like "Free shipping on subscriptions" takes 10px and converts comparison shoppers like me.
