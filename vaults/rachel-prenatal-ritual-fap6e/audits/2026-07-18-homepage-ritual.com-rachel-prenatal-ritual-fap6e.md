# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-07-18
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Ritual is leading with a summer sale, not a pregnancy story, which tells me this homepage is in promo mode right now. The brand aesthetic is exactly what I expect — clean, science-adjacent, soft palette — and the "traceable essentials" language is the kind of thing that makes me lean in. But the hero is shouting "sale" while I'm here to decide whether to trust this brand with my prenatal nutrition, and those are two very different conversations.

## What's working

- **"Pregnancy" tab in top nav** — it's the third tab, surfaced right there at the top. I see it immediately and it tells me this brand knows I exist.
- **"Traceable essentials" subtext** — that phrase is doing real work for me. I read ingredient-origin stories. "Traceable" is a signal that Ritual speaks my language without having to say "clean label" like everyone else.
- **Model selection** — the woman in the hero is not a supermodel or an athlete. She looks like she could be me, in her bathroom, taking her vitamins. That registers.
- **Dual CTA approach** — "Shop the Sale" and "Shop All" gives me an out if I don't care about the sale and just want to browse. Smart.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no banners stacked on banners. One message, one image, two buttons. Respects my attention.

## What's weak

- **Hero is a sale pitch, not a pregnancy pitch** — I opened ritual.com because I'm researching prenatal vitamins. "Cool Science / Hotter Savings" does not meet me where I am. The sale is useful information, but it shouldn't be the only thing above the fold.
- **No trust signals visible** — no review count, no third-party certifications, no "as seen in" badges. I'm about to put something in my body every day for nine months. I need credentialing before I shop.
- **"Up to 30%" is doing the heaviest lifting** — "up to" language is something I've learned to distrust. The qualifier (bundle) is there in the subtext, but the headline contrast makes it feel like a bait-number.
- **No urgency** — "Hotter Savings" implies a time-limited event but there's no countdown, no "ends Sunday," nothing. If the sale has no deadline, the urgency is fake, and I notice that.
- **No free-shipping threshold visible** — if I'm going to subscribe, I want to know shipping costs before I dig deeper. Not seeing it here.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the hero or add a persistent secondary strip** for pregnancy/prenatal positioning — even a single line like "Essential prenatal nutrition, now up to 30% off" merges the sale with the category I actually care about.
- **Add a trust bar below the hero**: "Third-party tested · No hidden ingredients · Traceable supply chain" with small icons. One row. Costs nothing and speaks directly to ingredient researchers like me.
- **Put a deadline on the sale** — a real one. "Ends July 21" or a countdown timer. "Hotter Savings" with no endpoint reads like evergreen copy dressed as urgency.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold or subscription benefit** in the sub-hero area — I'm a subscriber in the making and I want to know what I'm signing up for before I tap anything.
