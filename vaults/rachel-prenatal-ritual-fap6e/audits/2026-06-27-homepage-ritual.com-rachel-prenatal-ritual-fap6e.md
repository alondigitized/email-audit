# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-06-27
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- I know Ritual — I've had it in my comparison spreadsheet next to Care/of and Persona for months. This homepage is pushing a Summer Sale hard, which is fine, but the hero is doing double duty as a brand moment with "Made Traceable®" and that tagline actually stops my scroll. The yellow tile aesthetic is unmistakably Ritual, the offer is clear, but the page isn't working very hard to pull me toward the prenatal shelf specifically — I have to navigate there myself.

## What's working

- **"Made Traceable®" tagline** — this is the single strongest hook for me. I want to know where the folate comes from, which country the omega-3s are sourced from, whether the iron form is chelated. Ritual branding this as a proprietary standard and putting it right in the hero is exactly the thing that separates them from generic supplement brands.
- **"Pregnancy" tab in the nav strip** — it's the second category after Multivitamins and it's immediately visible without hunting. One tap and I'm in my aisle.
- **35% off new subscription** — I subscribe for everything; this is a real incentive that maps directly to my shopping behavior, and it's above the fold with a specific number.
- **Clean render, distinctive aesthetic** — the yellow tile product photography is distinctive enough that I'd recognize this brand anywhere. No visual noise.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a sale page, not a prenatal page** — the imagery is two generic white bottles on yellow tile. I can't tell from the hero whether those are prenatal vitamins, multivitamins, or gut supplements. Someone arriving cold wouldn't know where they'd landed.
- **"New subscription only" on the 35% off** — if I paused my subscription mid-trimester and came back, this offer doesn't apply to me. That friction is real and they're not addressing it. I'd feel like a second-class returning customer.
- **No human in the hero** — not a single person who looks like me or is in my situation. The product photography is beautiful but cold. A pregnant woman or someone in the planning phase would make this feel personal.
- **No trust signals visible** — no review count, no third-party certifications, no free-shipping threshold. For a supplement brand I'm considering putting in my body during pregnancy, the absence of any "why trust us" signal above the fold is a gap.
- **"Gut H..." nav is cut off** — small, but sloppy. "Gut Health" getting clipped by the viewport with no scroll indicator sends a minor friction signal.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the generic bottle hero for a prenatal-specific campaign image** — show a real person (31–34, visibly pregnant or early-bump), holding the Essential Prenatal bottle. Add "Folate from Italy. Iron you can actually absorb." as supporting copy. The sale offer can stay; just give it a human anchor.
- **Add a returning-subscriber hook to the nav strip or hero sub-copy** — something like "Already a member? Log in to see your offer" respects me as someone who pauses and returns, and stops me from feeling like the 35% off is bait I can't access.
- **Surface one trust signal above the fold** — even a single line: "3rd-party tested. No synthetic fillers." or a count like "Trusted by 2M+ subscribers" gives me a reason to keep reading instead of heading to the Care/of comparison tab I already have open.
- **Fix the nav overflow or shorten "Gut Health" to "Gut"** — don't let the viewport clip a core category label. Either abbreviate or use a horizontal scroll indicator.
