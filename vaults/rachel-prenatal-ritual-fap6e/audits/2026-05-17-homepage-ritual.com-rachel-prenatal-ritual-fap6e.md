# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Ritual is landing exactly where I live right now — that hero image is the "Essential for Women" bottle, and the tagline literally says "for living life or creating it," which is a direct wink at someone like me who is mid-planning-mode. The brand reads as science-forward, minimalist, and premium. But this viewport is doing all vibe and zero conversion work — there is no button, no offer, no clear next step anywhere I can see.

## What's working

- **"for living life or creating it" tagline** — I stopped mid-scroll. That phrase is doing a lot of quiet, smart work for anyone in the conception window. It doesn't shout "PRENATAL" but it's unmistakable to me.
- **"Essential for Women Multivitamin" bottle in the hero** — The product is front and center, not buried behind a lifestyle shot. I can see the actual item I'm evaluating.
- **"Traceable science and sourcing"** — This is exactly the ingredient-origin promise I cross-reference with Care/of and Persona. Ritual earns credibility in five words.
- **Yellow/gold macro photography** — The aesthetic is elevated and clinical at the same time. Feels peer-reviewed, not drugstore.
- **Pause button on the hero video** — Small signal, but I appreciate that they give me control over motion. Accessibility-aware brands tend to be ingredient-aware brands.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button visible** — I can see beautiful copy and a great product image and then... nothing to tap. I have to hunt for the next step.
- **Navigation is a hamburger "Menu"** — One tap to see categories, but I can't tell at a glance whether they even carry Essential Prenatal vs. just the women's multivitamin. That distinction matters to me right now.
- **No offer or pricing signal** — I'm comparison-shopping against Care/of and Persona. If there's a subscribe-and-save discount or a first-order promo, it should be visible here. I shouldn't have to dig.
- **No social proof above the fold** — No star rating, no "trusted by X women," no clinical-study badge. The copy says science-backed; I want a crumb of evidence.
- **No differentiation on prenatal specifically** — The bottle says "Essential for Women" but I need to know if this is the product for me or if there's an Essential Prenatal I should be looking at. This ambiguity makes me less likely to convert.

## Recommendations

- **Add a direct CTA button immediately below the tagline** — "Shop Essential Prenatal" or "See What's Inside" would get me tapping instead of wondering. Even "Start Your Ritual" is better than nothing.
- **Surface the prenatal SKU variant explicitly** — Swap the hero bottle label or add a secondary module beneath the fold that calls out "Planning? Meet Essential Prenatal." I shouldn't have to infer it from "creating it."
- **Show one trust signal above the fold** — A single line like "4.8★ · 50,000+ reviews" or "Clinically-studied ingredients, third-party tested" costs zero layout space and answers my biggest objection.
- **Add a subscribe-and-save teaser** — Even a thin promo strip saying "Free shipping + 15% off on subscriptions" would immediately address the price comparison I'm running in my head against competitors.
