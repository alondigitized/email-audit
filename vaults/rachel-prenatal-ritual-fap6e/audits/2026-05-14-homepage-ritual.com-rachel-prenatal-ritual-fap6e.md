# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Ritual is coming across as a premium, science-forward supplement brand — the kind that would rather show you a gorgeous translucent capsule than hit you with a promo code. The hero is confident and restrained, leaning into ingredient transparency as the brand's main story. Right now they're pushing the idea that clean sourcing is their differentiator, and that sub-line about "living life or creating it" is doing quiet but real work for someone like me.

## What's working

- **The capsule hero image** — a floating, see-through capsule filled with golden beads on a yellow field is genuinely striking. It communicates "premium supplement" in one image without a single word.
- **"Traceable science and sourcing"** — this subline is almost perfectly calibrated for me. I'm the person who Googles chelated magnesium at 11pm; this brand just told me we share the same values.
- **"For living life or creating it"** — subtle enough to not feel pandering, specific enough to land. That's the right tone for someone researching prenatals at 32.
- **The pause button (||) on the hero** — shows the brand is aware of motion/autoplay accessibility. Small signal, but it reads as thoughtful.
- **Overall cleanliness** — no navbar clutter, no competing banners, white-and-yellow palette feels clinical-but-warm. Matches how I think about vitamins.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button above the fold** — I see the headline and I'm nodding, but there's nowhere to tap. All that brand momentum has no outlet.
- **No pricing or offer signal** — Ritual is competing with Care/of and Persona on value. I need at least a hint that the subscription price is justified before I scroll.
- **No human presence** — I'm planning a pregnancy. I want to see someone at my life stage: a woman in her 30s, maybe a couple, a bump. A floating pill is beautiful but doesn't connect emotionally.
- **"The future of health is clear"** — "clear" is doing double-duty as a transparency pun and a design cue, but it's abstract. Future of *what* health? Prenatal? General wellness? For a first-time visitor I don't know where I land in their product line.
- **Zero reviews or trust badges** — first visit, no social proof visible. Persona and Care/of both lead with review counts; if I'm comparing, I notice their absence here.

## Recommendations

- **Add a primary CTA button directly under the sub-headline** — "Shop Prenatal" or "Build My Ritual" with a visible yellow or navy button. The hero is earning attention; don't let it go unaddressed.
- **Add a one-line proof point below the tagline** — something like "4.8 stars · 50,000 subscribers" or "Formulated with OB/GYN input." Costs zero design real estate, immediately builds credibility.
- **Swap or layer in a lifestyle photo alongside the capsule** — a woman in her 30s holding a glass of water, relaxed but intentional. Keep the capsule, but ground it in a human moment.
- **Surface a subscription callout above the fold** — even "Subscribe & save 15%" in a small banner is enough to preempt my Care/of comparison. I need to know I'm not overpaying for the beautiful packaging.
