# Homepage snapshot · ritual.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is exactly what I expected from Ritual — clean, clinical, almost pharmaceutical in a good way. The transparent capsule hero is their whole identity, and it works on me every time. Right now they're pushing a sale (something off 30%) alongside their full product range, and "Pregnancy" is surfaced right in the navigation which immediately tells me this is my place.

## What's working

- **"Pregnancy" in the top nav** — this is huge. I don't have to dig. It's literally in my face at the top of the page. For someone planning a baby, that one word does more than any hero copy could.
- **The transparent capsule hero image** — this is Ritual's whole IP. I've read their ingredient-origin stories before. Seeing those clear capsules signals "we are not hiding anything," which is exactly what I want from a prenatal vitamin brand.
- **"Build your Ritual and save up to 30%"** — the subscription framing resonates with how I actually shop. I'm a subscriber, not a one-timer.
- **"Shop the Sale" in the nav** — I appreciate a direct sale link. No digging, no coupon codes.
- **Clean, minimal mobile render** — no ads, no popups visible, no layout chaos. Ritual knows their customer is a high-info millennial who bounces at clutter.

## What's weak

- **The hero copy is brand manifesto, not shopper-targeted** — "The future of health is clear" is beautiful but it doesn't answer "why should *I*, someone actively planning a pregnancy, shop here today?" No mention of prenatal, no "before, during, after."
- **No CTA button visible in the viewport** — the offer text runs off the bottom edge. There's no tappable button I can see. That's a miss on mobile.
- **No human in the imagery** — the capsule photo is on-brand but it's product-only. Someone like me wants to see a woman who looks like she could be planning a family. No representation above the fold.
- **Offer terms are cut off** — "save up to 30% this" trails off. I don't know if it's a subscription condition, a code, a bundle deal, or what. That ambiguity makes me hesitate.
- **No trust signals above the fold** — no star ratings, no "clinically studied," no OB/midwife endorsements. For a prenatal shopper who reads every ingredient-origin story, that's an opportunity missed.

## Recommendations

- **Surface prenatal copy in or near the hero** — a sub-headline like "Essential Prenatal: start 3 months before" turns a generic health message into a direct call for someone at my life stage. It doesn't require a full creative swap, just a copy layer.
- **Make the offer complete and button it** — "Save up to 30% when you subscribe" with a visible "Shop Prenatal" button below the fold cutoff. The partial copy is worse than no copy because it creates uncertainty.
- **Add a lifestyle element to the hero** — even a subtle lifestyle shot (a woman's hand, a morning routine) alongside the capsules would give me someone to identify with.
- **Pin a trust badge row just below the hero** — "Traceable ingredients · Clinically studied · Cancel anytime" in three icons is three seconds and would immediately speak to the ingredient-obsessive in me.
