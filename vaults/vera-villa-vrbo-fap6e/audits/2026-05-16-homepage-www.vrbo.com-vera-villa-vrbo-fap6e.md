# Homepage snapshot · www.vrbo.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I opened Vrbo and got a bot wall — not a homepage. The page says "Show us your human side... We can't tell if you're a human or a bot" with a slider CAPTCHA in the middle of the screen. There is zero brand content, zero search, zero listings — just a challenge gate with a session ID at the bottom. For a high-intent planner like me who's ready to search for a 5-bedroom lake house for spring break, this is a hard stop before I've seen a single pixel of actual product.

## What's working

- The Vrbo wordmark at the top at least confirms I landed on the right domain — I didn't get phished.
- The gate renders cleanly with readable copy explaining *why* it triggered ("browsing at a speed faster than expected of a human being").
- A "Submit feedback" link is present — small mercy if the gate is a false positive.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** I cannot see search, destinations, pricing, or any value prop. I'm a Gen X planner with high tech comfort and I'm staring at a CAPTCHA.
- **No fallback content or deep link** — if I solve the slider, I have no idea if I'll land on the actual homepage or get looped again.
- **The session ID (ID: 7a5d7a32-f19e-be8e-4a9c-8618f1b9a745) is exposed verbatim** — meaningless to me as a consumer, just adds noise and feels cold.
- **"Something is preventing JavaScript from working on your computer"** — this reads like a tech error message, not consumer-grade copy. Blaming my browser while I'm on my phone is friction I didn't create.
- **No estimated resolution time or alternative path** (like "try the app" or a phone number). Dead end.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a lightweight static fallback page** behind the bot gate — even just a search bar and destination inspiration tiles — so blocked users have something to engage with while the challenge resolves.
- **Replace the IP/session ID exposure** with a clean support reference code. Showing raw IDs is confusing and slightly unsettling to a consumer.
- **Rewrite the explanatory copy** in a warmer, consumer-facing register: "Our security check caught something unexpected — let's get you through quickly" beats a bullet list of technical causes.
- **Add an "Open in app" nudge** on the gate itself — if the web session is triggering bot detection, offer me the app as an immediate escape hatch. I'm on mobile; one tap to the App Store is actionable.
