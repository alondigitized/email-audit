# Homepage snapshot · www.vrbo.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I didn't get a homepage — I got a bot wall. Vrbo's security gate caught whatever browser or bot was simulating my visit, and instead of their vacation rental experience, I'm staring at a CAPTCHA slider telling me to "show my human side." For a brand that prides itself on trust and family-safe experiences, starting with an accusation that I might be a robot is not a great look. There's zero product, zero inspiration, and zero reason to stick around.

## What's working

- The Vrbo logo at the top is at least legible and on-brand — I know I'm in the right place.
- The "Submit feedback" link exists, so there's technically a path to flag this problem.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is a bot-check wall** — no search bar, no destination imagery, no "whole home" value prop, nothing that speaks to why I came here.
- **"Show us your human side"** — aggressive, slightly accusatory tone for a brand selling family togetherness. Not the welcome mat I expected.
- **Slider CAPTCHA on mobile** — these are finicky on a phone screen; this is a frustrating micro-interaction before I've seen a single listing.
- **IP flag explanation is alarming** — telling me there's "a robot on the same network (IP 75.140.9.0) as you" feels like a security warning I don't know what to do with.
- **No fallback content** — not even a static "please wait" message with a search box or a link to app stores. Dead end.

## Recommendations

- **Add a graceful fallback page** — if the bot-check fires, show a simplified search bar and the brand's core "whole home, no strangers" value prop so the page isn't completely blank from a commercial standpoint.
- **Soften the tone** — swap "We can't tell if you're a human or a bot" for something like "Just a quick check before we get you to your next trip" — same function, warmer register.
- **Investigate the network trigger** — a shared IP false-positive is blocking real customers on mobile networks; that's a conversion bleed worth diagnosing at the infrastructure level.
- **Surface a "Continue to App" button** — for mobile users stuck on this screen, give me a one-tap escape to the Vrbo app where I'm likely already authenticated.
