# Homepage snapshot · www.vrbo.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I didn't even get to see the homepage — Vrbo hit me with a bot-detection CAPTCHA the moment I landed. Instead of destination inspiration or a search bar, I'm staring at a slider puzzle and a message telling me my behavior looks inhuman. Not exactly the warm welcome I expect from a platform asking me to trust them with a $4,000 family vacation booking.

## What's working

- The Vrbo logo renders cleanly at the top — at least I know I'm in the right place.
- The CAPTCHA UI itself is functional and not visually broken (slider, arrows, and refresh icon are present and legible).

## What's weak

- **The entire above-the-fold experience is a bot wall**, not a homepage — a real user intent to book a summer cabin rental gets zero product content.
- **"Show us your human side..."** is cutesy copy that does nothing to reassure me as a high-consideration shopper mid-funnel. It feels condescending.
- **No escape hatch or "continue as guest" path** is visible — my only options are to solve the CAPTCHA or submit feedback via a small text link.
- **The error explanation is paranoia-inducing**: listing IP-sharing and JavaScript blockers as reasons makes me wonder if something is wrong with my device, not their detection system.
- **Zero brand value delivered**: no imagery, no search widget, no destination inspiration — every second I spend here is a second I'm not searching.

## Recommendations

- **Tune bot-detection thresholds for mobile traffic**: a Gen X user browsing from a phone on a shared home network (like mine) will routinely trigger IP-based flags — fix the false-positive rate before it bleeds real bookings.
- **If a CAPTCHA is unavoidable, brand it**: replace the clinical "We can't tell if you're a human or a bot" with something warmer — "Quick check before we find your next getaway" keeps the Vrbo voice intact.
- **Add a visible feedback or support link above the fold on the interstitial**, not buried at the bottom — a stuck user who can't find help will just leave.
- **Log and A/B test CAPTCHA abandonment rate by device type**: I'd bet mobile users are bouncing at a significantly higher rate here than desktop, and that data should force prioritization.
