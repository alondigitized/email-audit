# Holiday shopping: The snuggle is real
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a strong Chewy holiday hero. A French bulldog in a green-and-red bow sits under a huge "UP TO 40% OFF" on pink, so the eye lands on the offer first. The strip below ("Save on toys, treats & more.*" with a navy "Shop deals" button) makes the next step obvious.
- The weak spot is the subject. "Holiday shopping: The snuggle is real" is cute and says nothing about the 40% off, and the dog-in-a-bow hero doesn't match the "snuggle" angle either. Below the hero, the email stacks three competing offers: the 40% off with a tiered EARLY10–EARLY40 code, a free $10 eGift card with code APP10 for the app, and four product tiles. The tile row ("Top picks for you") is tiny, the products are hard to make out, and there are no prices.
- Compared with the 11/7 "Save up to 40% on gifts for good pets" send, this is nearly the same offer under a vaguer subject. The 11/7 subject was the stronger one. This is a competent send that gives up opens in the inbox and clicks in the body.

## What's working

- The hero has the offer in huge type and a charming dog, with a clear focal point.
- The "Shop deals" button next to the headline is large and unambiguous.
- The $10 eGift card block is a clean second offer with a visible code and an app mockup.
- Dog, Cat and "Shop all deals" tiles let the recipient segment themselves quickly.
- The trust row (24/7 service, free shipping with Chewy+, 365-day returns, happy pets guaranteed) and the Chat/Call strip reduce purchase friction.
- The brand look is consistent: blue and pink with clear typography.

## What's weak

- The subject gives no offer, and "snuggle" doesn't connect to the creative.
- The "Top picks for you" thumbnails are small, with identical "Shop now" labels, no prices and no names.
- The "up to 40%" promo needs six tiered codes (EARLY10 through EARLY40) in the fine print, with limits of 5 items per order and 3 orders per customer. That is buried and confusing for an offer the hero shouts about.
- The "Shop the app" eGift offer needs a $49+ first app order, and that qualifier is a tiny footnote ("†"). It competes with the main promo for attention.
- The footer is heavy on legal text, and the nav row (Deals/Dog/Cat/Rx) is faint at the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer in the subject. It is the biggest lever, since the creative is already strong.
- 2. Replace the "Top picks" thumbnails with larger product cards showing name, price and a discounted price. Even two or three would beat four unreadable tiles.
- 3. Show the code or auto-apply logic in the hero. Make clear whether shoppers must enter a code, and what tier applies, so "up to 40%" isn't a surprise at checkout.
- 4. Pick one lead offer. Demote the app eGift to a slim banner, or send it to app users only.
- 5. Make the hero image match the subject, for example a dog or cat cuddled up in a blanket if the "snuggle" angle stays.
