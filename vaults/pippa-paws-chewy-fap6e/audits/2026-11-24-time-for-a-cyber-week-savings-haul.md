# Time for a Cyber Week savings haul 🐾 🛍️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a strong, on-brand Cyber Week send. A navy "50% OFF" sits under a magenta "CYBER WEEK" tab, with a Boston terrier in a red holiday sweater chasing a ball below it. The eye lands on the offer first, and the "Shop deals" button is directly under the headline. It's the same hero formula as the two Cyber Week sends yesterday (11/23), minus the loud magenta type.
- The catch is the fine print. "Up to 50% off" is actually a code ladder (CYBER10 through CYBER50) that the recipient has to enter at checkout, limited to 5 items per order and 3 orders per customer. That's all in tiny gray type at the bottom. The headline is honest enough, but it leaves the shopper wondering which items get 50% and which get 10%.
- The send also reads as the third near-identical Cyber Week email in about 24 hours. Pet owners will find the offer familiar and the creative effective, but the cadence is getting heavy. The pharmacy block (40% off, up to $100 on a first order) is a smart second offer. The "Top picks for you" strip is the weakest part, and it does little to personalize the email.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: "Up to 50% off" in huge navy type, with a magenta "CYBER WEEK" tag and a "Shop deals" button right beneath it.
- The dog photo is warm and seasonal (holiday sweater, festive ball) and doesn't compete with the headline.
- The three category tiles (Shop dog / Shop cat / Shop all deals) are big, tappable, and let people self-segment.
- The pharmacy module has a concrete second offer: 40% off, save up to $100 on a first order.
- The footer trust row (24/7 service, free shipping with Chewy+, free returns, happy pets guarantee) is clean and reassuring.

## What's weak

- Promo codes are buried in the fine print. The headline never says "enter code CYBER50," so shoppers can reach checkout and be confused.
- The "Shop deals" button is small and sits at the top of a very tall hero, with a lot of empty space below it.
- "Top picks for you" shows four tiny product thumbnails with identical "Shop now" links and no names, prices, or prices off. It doesn't read as personalized.
- The 50%, 40% and "up to $100" offers, each with its own conditions, add up to a lot of terms for one email.
- This is the 3rd Cyber Week blast since 11/23 ("Cyber Week savings kick off now!" and "Why wait until Friday for up to 50% off?"), so there's no new reason to open it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the code on the hero: "Up to 50% off with code CYBER50" or a visible code ladder. It lifts clarity and trust more than any other change.
- 2. Make the "Shop deals" button larger, and repeat it below the photo so it isn't lost in the hero.
- 3. Replace the generic top picks with named products, prices, and a savings badge, or at least tie them to the recipient's pet type.
- 4. Give each send in the series a distinct hook, such as a countdown or a "last chance Cyber Monday" angle, so the near-identical emails stop blurring together.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email uses a standard table-based, responsive layout with a `lang` attribute, viewport meta and a mobile media query. The QA data confirms no broken links, but several items can't be verified because of the relay and the probing limits. The confirmed issues are missing alt text on images, an empty `<title>`, and a very URL-heavy plain-text part.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All CTAs go through a click-tracking redirector** (`https://clicks-paws.chewy.com/f/a/<token>~/AAQRxRA~/...`). The destination URLs and any UTM parameters can't be seen in the HTML.
- **7 links returned HTTP 429** ("Shop dog", "Shop cat", "Shop all deals", "Chewy", the Cyber Week hero link, the Pharmacy Rx link and the linked image `7ffd0ba076493042.png`). The QA tool flags these as "link may be valid". This looks like rate limiting from the probe, not broken links, so it isn't a confirmed defect.
- **4 links were never probed** because of the 70s time budget (`.../IgWNBvjc3G3FEs3f5GiuHQ~~/...`, `.../nUSpt8DIDGKSh4x9Yyei4w~~/...`, `.../Ht8wqpNmuQIrK_kUlKH3RA~~/...`, `.../mPclpHyJQ8L0QXAjC7NrKA~~/...`). Another 16 links were skipped as low priority. Their status is unknown.
- **Tracking pixels are present**:
  - `https://clicks-paws.chewy.com/q/FGCLq6sN.../RZKMglenzr...` is an open pixel.
  - `https://meow.chewy.com/p/up/04ebeb1c.../o.gif?mi_u=979488259&mi_ecmp=...` is a second pixel.
  - `https://meow.chewy.com/p/rp/7ffd0ba076493042.png?...` is an image that is also wrapped in a click link.
  - All three are expected for ESP tracking. The `mi_u=979488259` value is a per-recipient identifier exposed in plain query-string form.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title></title>`** in the `<head>`. This is a real but minor issue.
- **5 images lack alt text** (QA-confirmed):
  - `7ffd0ba076493042.png` is wrapped in a tracked link (see the 429 entry above). If it has no alt text, that link has no accessible name. This is the most important of the five.
  - `footer-icons_returns.png` and `footer-icons_guaranteed.png` are probably decorative. They should carry `alt=""` explicitly. The QA check can't tell a missing attribute from an empty one.
  - `o.gif` and `RZKMglenzr...` are tracking pixels, which should also have `alt=""`.
- **Mobile font size**: the media query sets `.row-26 .column-N .block-1.paragraph_block td.pad>div{font-size:8px!important}` at max-width 620px. 8px is below a readable minimum on phones. I can't tell what content sits in row-26 because the source is truncated.
- **Positives seen in the source**:
  - `lang="en"`.
  - The viewport meta tag.
  - MSO conditionals, with `a[x-apple-data-detectors]` handled.
  - A hidden preheader div.
  - A web-font `<link>` wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible HTML. There are no unresolved tokens such as `{{...}}`, `%%...%%` or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The source is truncated, so the rest of the body is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA note says the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is **unconfirmed**. Check the raw message at the origin.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found**, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is a relay limitation, not evidence of failure. The From domain `paws.chewy.com` is a branded subdomain, which is consistent with aligned sending.
- **The unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source.** I can't confirm or deny them. The QA tool says an unsubscribe link is always included in probing, but it doesn't say which probe it was.
- **Plain-text part**: 11,064 of 13,475 characters (~82%) are URLs. This comes from long tracking-redirect tokens, each several hundred characters. It is a real deliverability and readability weakness for plain-text clients and spam scoring.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable from the data. Click-wrapped URLs hide the final destination and UTM values, and no probe followed the redirects to a landing page. The only visible campaign label is `mi_ecmp=2026-11-24-Tu-EarlyBlackFriday` (the image URL for `7ffd0ba076493042.png` has `-NotSettled` appended). It differs from the "Cyber Week" subject line. That is probably an internal naming convention, but it's worth confirming that reporting maps correctly.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to `7ffd0ba076493042.png`, since it's a linked image. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixels and the footer icons.
2. Populate `<title>` with the subject or a descriptive string.
3. Re-run link probing with throttling and a longer time budget. That would clear the 7 HTTP 429 results and the 4 unprobed links. It should also follow the redirects so the final landing URLs and UTM parameters can be checked.
4. Check the original message headers (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`.
5. Make sure the full HTML is checked for a working unsubscribe link and a postal address. The truncated source doesn't show them.
6. Review the 8px mobile font rule on row-26 and raise it to a legible size (about 11px or more).
7. Shorten the plain-text tracking URLs, or add more readable plain-text content, so the URL ratio falls.
