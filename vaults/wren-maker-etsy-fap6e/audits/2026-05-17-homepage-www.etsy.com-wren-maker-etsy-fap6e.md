# Homepage snapshot · www.etsy.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so I didn't even get to see the homepage — Etsy's bot-detection wall hit before any content loaded. What I'm staring at is a white screen with the orange Etsy logo, a message that says "Access is temporarily restricted," a short list of reasons why (rapid clicks, JS issues, bot activity, dev tools), and a "Submit feedback" link. There's no product, no vibe, nothing. This is a complete dead end.

## What's working

- The Etsy logo renders correctly in its recognizable orange — at least I know I landed on the right domain and didn't get a phishing page.
- The "Submit feedback" link exists, so there's technically a path forward if I want to report the problem.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is gone.** I see a hard block page instead of any product content, categories, or personalization.
- **The error message is clinical and cold** — "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" reads like a server log, not a brand voice. For a marketplace that prides itself on warmth and handmade community, this is a jarring tonal mismatch.
- **The IP address is exposed in plain text** (`75.140.9.0`) — that's technical noise that means nothing to a shopper and feels vaguely accusatory, like I'm being told I did something wrong.
- **No recovery path for a real user.** A legitimate shopper who hit this by accident (rapid scrolling, a slow JS load) gets zero guidance on how to get back to shopping — just a generic feedback form.
- **No session context preserved.** If I was mid-browse and this triggered, my cart and wishlist are inaccessible. That's a conversion kill.

## Recommendations

- **Show a human-facing recovery screen, not a server error.** Something like "We want to make sure it's you — tap below to continue" with a single large CTA button, in Etsy's warm brand voice. Keep the IP and bot-detection jargon off the user-facing copy entirely.
- **Add a CAPTCHA or quick tap-to-verify** so a real user can self-clear the block without submitting a feedback form and waiting. Friction here costs immediate bounce.
- **Preserve the session.** Even on the block page, a small "Your favorites and cart are saved — just verify it's you" note would reassure me my wishlist isn't gone.
- **Add the brand's warmth to the error copy.** Even something like "Whoops — something looked a little off. Let's get you back to shopping handmade." keeps the Etsy tone alive in a friction moment.
