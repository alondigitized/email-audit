# Homepage snapshot · www.etsy.com · 2026-06-26
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- I opened Etsy expecting to browse handmade gifts and instead hit a wall — literally. What I'm looking at is a bot-detection block page, not a homepage at all. The Etsy wordmark is there in its familiar orange, but below it is nothing but "Access is temporarily restricted" and a list of reasons my device got flagged. This is a failed page load, not a brand experience.

## What's working

- **Etsy logo** is immediately recognizable in brand orange — at least I know I'm in the right place before the bad news lands.
- **"Submit feedback" link** is present and underlined — there is a visible escape hatch if I think this is an error.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is missing** — I got a bot-block interstitial instead of any shopping surface.
- **The large white void** in the center of the screen (roughly half the viewport) looks broken before the explanation text loads at the bottom. No loading spinner, no skeleton UI.
- **Error copy is clinical and accusatory** — "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" followed by a bullet about "Automated (bot) activity" doesn't feel good to read as a real customer. I'm 28 and tech-savvy enough to know what a bot detection page is, but it still feels like I'm being called a cheater.
- **No path back to shopping** — zero links to the actual homepage, category pages, or a "try again" button. The only option is "Submit feedback," which implies I need to file a complaint to get back in.
- **IP address exposed in plain text** (75.140.9.0) — unnecessary for a shopper and feels a bit creepy to surface on a consumer page.

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Return to Etsy" or "Try Again" CTA** — right now there's no way back to shopping. A single orange button labeled "Go to Homepage" would dramatically reduce bounce from this state.
- **Soften the error copy** — swap "We detected unusual activity" for something warmer like "Something went wrong loading your page" so real shoppers don't feel penalized.
- **Fill the white void** with a loading skeleton or at minimum a brand illustration — the blank white space between the logo and the error explanation makes the page look broken before the user even reads the copy.
- **Remove the raw IP address** from the consumer-facing block page — it adds nothing for shoppers and feels surveillance-y.
