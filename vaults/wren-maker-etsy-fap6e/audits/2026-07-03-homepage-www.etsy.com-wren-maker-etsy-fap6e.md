# Homepage snapshot · www.etsy.com · 2026-07-03
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- I opened Etsy — my go-to for handmade finds — and got a wall of text telling me I've been blocked. No hero, no categories, no gift inspo, nothing. It reads like a CAPTCHA-lite interstitial and leaves me with zero reason to stay or try again.

## What's working

- The Etsy wordmark renders correctly in the signature orange — at least I know I'm in the right place and the brand identity didn't break.
- The "Submit feedback" link is functional and clickable — one escape hatch exists.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage content is absent — no products, no categories, no seasonal editorial, nothing that would make a handmade-gifter like me want to stay.
- The error message cites "automated (bot) activity" from my IP (75.140.9.0), which is jarring and accusatory — I'm just browsing on my phone.
- The copy is cold and technical ("JavaScript disabled or not working," "Use of developer or inspection tools") — reads like a server log, not a consumer brand.
- No retry button, no redirect to the real homepage, no "try again in X minutes" — I'm stranded.
- The large white void between the logo and the error block feels broken and unintentional, like the page half-loaded and gave up.

## Recommendations

- Add a warm, on-brand retry CTA below the error ("Something went wrong — tap here to reload") instead of leaving the user stranded with only a feedback form.
- Replace the technical bullet list ("JavaScript disabled," "bot activity") with a single plain-language sentence: "We had trouble loading your page — please try again."
- If the block is IP-based and temporary, show an estimated wait time or auto-refresh, so users don't assume Etsy is down permanently.
- Serve a lightweight cached fallback homepage (logo + search bar + top-3 categories) even when full JS fails, so returning shoppers can still navigate.
