# Homepage snapshot · www.etsy.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- I opened Etsy on my phone and got hit with a wall — literally an error screen, not a homepage. The orange logo is there, but below it is just "Access is temporarily restricted" and a bot-detection message listing things like "automated activity" and "developer tools." There's nothing to browse, no products, no categories, no search bar — just a dead end with a feedback link.

## What's working

- The Etsy wordmark renders correctly in its signature orange — at least I know I'm on the right site and haven't been redirected somewhere sketchy.
- The "Submit feedback" link is clearly underlined and tappable, so there's at least one functional escape hatch if I'm stuck.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is replaced by a bot-block page** — I can't see a single product, category, or promotion. For a handmade-goods discovery platform, this is catastrophically bad.
- **"Automated (bot) activity" copy with an exposed IP address** feels clinical and almost accusatory — not the warm, creative-maker energy I associate with Etsy.
- **Zero content** means zero chance of engaging me. No Star Seller badges, no gifting categories, no handmade aesthetic whatsoever.
- **No retry mechanism or countdown** — the page doesn't tell me when access will be restored or offer a CAPTCHA to prove I'm human. I'm just stuck.
- **"JavaScript disabled or not working"** listed as a possible reason feels like a developer error message, not customer-facing copy. That's jarring on a consumer site.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the generic bot-block with a friendlier human-verification screen** — a simple CAPTCHA or "tap the images of handmade ceramics" challenge keeps the Etsy brand voice and lets real shoppers back in fast.
- **Add a "try again" button with a timer** so I know I'm not permanently locked out — right now there's no path forward except the vague feedback link.
- **Soften the copy** — instead of "We detected unusual activity," something like "Hold on — just making sure it's really you" fits the warm, indie-maker brand far better.
- **Surface the Etsy logo with a small reassuring tagline** ("You're in the right place — we'll have you browsing handmade finds in a moment") so the brand doesn't completely disappear behind the error state.
