# New Linen Arrivals Just Landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- "New Linen Arrivals Just Landed" is Everlane's third linen email in nine days — after "The Linen Standard" (May 28, 9/10) and "Linen Love" (June 2, 8/10). The template is clean, the hero works, and the Masters of Flax Fibre™ sustainability angle is a real differentiator. But the structural problem is unavoidable: this email is doing the same job as the previous two and adding very little new argument for *why you'd buy today rather than later*. No price, no discount, no deadline, no new editorial framing — just more linen, delivered after the subscriber has already been asked to care about linen twice this month.
- The sustainability section (Masters of Flax Fibre™, zero-waste extraction, no GMOs) is a genuine asset buried in the middle of the email behind a vague "READ MORE" button. That copy deserves to be surface-level, not a sub-story. The dual "SHOP NOW" CTAs are generic in a way that undermines an otherwise focused category send.
- This is competent filler from a brand that has been sending at four-per-week pace. A subscriber who loved "The Linen Standard" has already received the full linen argument. This email asks them to care again without giving them a new reason to.

## What's working

- **"New In: Linen" label on the hero** is clear and scannable — the subscriber instantly knows the category before reading a word of body copy.
- **Subject line is short and honest** (30 chars, no spam signals, no ALL CAPS) — mobile-friendly and doesn't oversell.
- **Product grid mixes flat-lay and on-model shots**, giving the subscriber both styling context and detail clarity.
- **Sustainability copy is genuinely differentiating** — Masters of Flax Fibre™ with 100% mechanical processing is a real fact, not boilerplate.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text is legible, layout holds cleanly.

## What's weak

- **Third linen email in nine days** — category fatigue is now a real risk; the brand is repeating its own best work (May 28) without adding a new angle.
- **No price or offer anywhere** — purely editorial with no incentive to click now vs. bookmarking for later; the action rate will suffer.
- **Both "SHOP NOW" buttons are generic** — no specificity about which piece, which collection, or what the subscriber should look at first.
- **"READ MORE" CTA is sandwiched between two Shop Now buttons** — it breaks the purchase flow and competes with conversion for attention.
- **No urgency signal in the body** — "Just Landed" is in the subject, but nothing in the email body reinforces scarcity, sell-through, or a deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the first CTA specific**: "Shop Linen Arrivals" or "See the New Linen" beats "SHOP NOW" — verb + noun, category anchored.
- 2. **Pull one key sustainability stat into the hero or subhead** — "Made from European flax, zero irrigation" above the first CTA lifts the brand differentiation without requiring a click.
- 3. **Drop the second "SHOP NOW" entirely or replace it with a specific product link** — two identical CTAs dilute attention. Pick a hero SKU and link it directly.
- 4. **Move "READ MORE" to the footer navigation** — it's brand content, not a conversion driver; it shouldn't occupy a full-width section between two shop buttons.
- 5. **Add a from-price anchor** — even "Linen from $68" in the hero or product grid gives the subscriber a cognitive hook that "SHOP NOW" doesn't.
- 6. **Evaluate cadence** — the fourth email in four days combined with the third linen email in nine days is the real ceiling on this send's performance; the creative is fine but the frequency isn't.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Zero irrigation, no GMOs — easy pieces for every summer plan.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Title contains only whitespace. Some clients (Apple Mail preview, tab title in webmail) display this as blank. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.

**Duplicate `img` reset rule**
The `img` block is declared twice in `<style>`:
- First instance: `border:0; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic`
- Second instance: `border:0; height:auto; line-height:100%; outline:none; text-decoration:none; max-width:100%`

The second declaration overrides the first for shared properties and adds `height:auto` / `max-width:100%` — a correct reset — but `-ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic` is lost in the cascade for clients that process the second rule. Consolidate into one declaration.

**Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
Two distinct max-width breakpoints are used:
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` — column stacking, font overrides
- `@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)` — `.hide` utility, `.font12`

A 481–600px viewport falls into the 600px breakpoint for `.hide` but not into the 480px rules for column stacking. This can produce a broken intermediate layout on tablets. Align to a single breakpoint (typically 600px for email).

**All anchor link styles are black with no underline, including hover**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Text links (not image/button CTAs) will be visually indistinguishable from body copy with no hover feedback. If any plain-text hyperlinks exist in the body, they will fail WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color alone to distinguish links).

**Custom font fallback gap**
`h1` references `"MaisonNeue-Light"` (hyphenated), while `h2`–`h4` reference `"Maison Neue"` (spaced). If the custom font fails to load from the Klaviyo CDN, `h1` falls back to `Arial` correctly, but the name inconsistency could cause a matching failure in environments that partially support web fonts (e.g., Apple Mail on older macOS). Normalize to a single spelling.

**Alt text on images** — not verifiable from truncated source. Must be confirmed in full HTML.
