# 30% Off Dresses And Sandals
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's category-focused re-send of the same 30% sitewide offer they already announced yesterday in "30% Off Sitewide, Starting Now." The creative pivot — narrowing the subject to dresses and sandals — is a reasonable segmentation move, but the execution undercuts it immediately: the hero overlay reads "30% OFF SITEWIDE," not "30% off dresses and sandals." The subject promises a curated angle; the body delivers a generic sale email.
- The hero photography is clean and on-brand — a woman in a white wrap dress, editorial and summer-appropriate for Memorial Day. But where are the sandals? The subject line co-headlines them, the body copy mentions them ("sandals that go with everything"), and yet no sandal appears anywhere in the visible email. That's a promise left unfulfilled.
- This is email seven in six days from Everlane. The deal is real, the brand is trusted, and the aesthetic is consistent. But a subscriber who opened yesterday already knows about the 30% off — this send adds no new information and the heavy cadence is starting to feel like noise.

## What's working

- **Hero image lands the brand** — clean editorial photography, white summer dress, outdoor setting. Immediately recognizable Everlane.
- **Offer is instantly legible** — "30% OFF SITEWIDE" in large type over the hero; no hunting required.
- **Memorial Day context is clear** — "MEMORIAL DAY EVENT" label ties the discount to a real-world hook without screaming.
- **Subject line is concrete** — 28 characters, specific percentage, named categories. No clickbait, no ALL CAPS abuse.
- **Body copy has editorial voice** — "Dresses you reach for first. Sandals that go with everything." is short and characteristically Everlane.

## What's weak

- **Subject says dresses + sandals; hero says sitewide** — the subject promises category focus but the email delivers a generic sitewide banner. Inconsistency erodes trust on re-read.
- **Sandals are absent from the visuals** — co-headlined in the subject and body copy, but not shown anywhere in the email. Empty promise.
- **"SHOP PANTS" and "SHOP BAGS" CTAs dilute the message** — including off-topic secondary CTAs in a dresses-and-sandals email fragments attention and breaks the editorial through-line.
- **No urgency deadline** — "for a limited time only" with no end date. Memorial Day weekend is an implied deadline but the email never states it, wasting a natural urgency lever.
- **Seventh email in six days** — the cadence is fatiguing; this send adds no new information over yesterday's "30% Off Sitewide, Starting Now" and will feel redundant to engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show sandals.** If sandals are in the subject, they must appear in the email — a split hero, a second image block, anything. An unsupported claim is a dead click.
- 2. **Resolve the subject/body mismatch.** Either align the hero text to match the subject ("30% Off Dresses + Sandals") or rewrite the subject to match the hero ("30% Off Sitewide — Memorial Day"). Pick one story.
- 3. **Add a concrete deadline.** "Sale ends Monday" or "Offer ends May 26" turns "for a limited time only" from filler into a real call to action.
- 4. **Swap SHOP PANTS and SHOP BAGS for category-specific CTAs.** Replace with "SHOP DRESSES" and "SHOP SANDALS" to reinforce the subject line promise and drive higher-intent clicks.
- 5. **Consider skipping this send entirely for subscribers who opened yesterday.** They already know about 30% sitewide; this email doesn't earn its inbox slot.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Memorial Day Dress Is 30% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses made for summer. Sandals that take you there. Sale ends May 26.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The white dress. The perfect sandal. Both 30% off this weekend.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **External font import in `<style>`:** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` is render-blocking in clients that honor `@import` (Gmail Android, some Outlook builds). ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace. Screen readers and some preview panes announce this as the email title. Should match subject line or a meaningful string.
- **All anchor states set to black with no underline:**
  ```css
  a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  ```
  Links are visually indistinguishable from body text in every state, including hover. This violates WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). CTA links that rely solely on button styling may be okay, but any inline text links will fail.
- **`h4` mobile font size is 10px:**
  ```css
  @media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important } }
  ```
  10px is below any reasonable legibility threshold and will fail WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text). Minimum recommended is 14–16px.
- **Duplicate `.hide` media query block:** The `@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px) { .hide { ... } }` block appears twice verbatim — no functional harm, but indicates a template assembly error.
