# Meet Your Summer Denim Lineup
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane doing the same trick as the 7/25 send: bolt a sitewide "Extra 20% Off Sale Styles" banner onto an unrelated editorial story — this time a denim product-lineup grid instead of a linen-set feature. The banner offer and the nine-jean grid below it aren't the same thing, and the email never clarifies whether any of the pictured denim is actually part of the sale.
- The execution itself is clean — consistent Everlane minimalism, a strong hero shot, a well-organized 3x3 grid with real variety (wide-leg, straight, cropped, dark wash, light wash). But after roughly nine promotional sends in the past week and a half (per the brand history), "Your Choice of Denim" reads as more inventory-clearing than a genuinely new campaign moment.
- Verdict: competent design, familiar fatigue. The stacked-offer trick works better than pure editorial (there's an actual number attached) but the mismatch between "extra 20% off" and "here are 9 full-price-looking jeans" will cost trust on click-through.

## What's working

- Hero image is strong — one clean, well-lit shot of the featured wide-leg jean with a simple text overlay ("Your Choice of Denim").
- The 9-item grid shows genuine variety (fits, washes, styling), which suits a "lineup" concept better than a single hero product.
- Two clear "SHOP NOW" CTAs (banner + hero) give the reader an obvious next step without hunting.
- No render bugs — layout, spacing, and images all display cleanly.
- On-brand throughout: wordmark, black CTA buttons, minimalist product photography, sustainability block.

## What's weak

- The top banner sells a sitewide "Extra 20% off (already up to 70% off)" offer, but the grid below is a denim-only editorial with no visible prices or discount tags — the two messages don't connect.
- No indication of which (if any) of the 9 jeans pictured are part of the sale, which risks a bait-and-click if shoppers land on full-price pages.
- Zero product-level detail visible — no prices, no fit/rise callouts, no size range, nothing to help a shopper pick between 9 near-identical options.
- No social proof (reviews, ratings, UGC) anywhere in the grid, despite Everlane leaning hard on customer-testimonial editorials in recent sends (e.g. 7/26's Paige Harper feature).
- This is the same "stack a generic sale banner on top of an unrelated story" structure flagged in 7/25 — the brand is repeating a format, not innovating on it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Reconcile the banner and the grid — either badge which of the 9 jeans qualify for the 20% off, or drop the sale banner and let the denim lineup stand as pure editorial (like the higher-performing 7/26 send).
- 2. Add a one-line price or "from $X" under each grid item — right now there's no reason to click one jean over another.
- 3. Give the CTA copy a noun: "Shop the Lineup" or "Find Your Fit" instead of generic "Shop Now" repeated twice.
- 4. Add a light social-proof element (star rating or one customer quote) near the grid, consistent with Everlane's own testimonial format used days earlier.
- **Subject Alt A:** `9 Jeans, One Perfect Fit`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Denim Lineup, Ranked by Fit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide-leg, straight, cropped — plus an extra 20% off select styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From dark wash to light — find your go-to pair inside.`
