# “I Would Honestly Wear This T-Shirt Anywhere”
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's second consecutive editorial send in as many days, following July 22's "New Hues Have Arrived" (5/10 for creative fatigue and format strain). The structure is unchanged — wordmark, lifestyle hero with quote overlay, numbered product grid, second lifestyle shot, customer testimonial, then a tacked-on Cult Favorites CTA at the bottom. What saves this email is the dual social-proof layer: a creator voice (@JULIANA.WINS) anchoring the hero and a named customer ("Nicole") closing the product story. That's a more persuasive editorial structure than anything Everlane sent in the prior week of promo hammering.
- The core problem is conversion plumbing. The t-shirt gets strong storytelling for two-thirds of the email, then the Cult Favorites section hijacks the bottom third with a different destination. There's no visible price, no offer, and no "Shop This Tee" button directly linked to the hero product. After a week of nearly daily sends — five promo emails followed by two editorials — subscribers are fatigued and need a reason to act, not just a reason to admire.
- Better than the 5/10 July 22 editorial, but still stuck in that Everlane zone of telling a good story without giving the reader a reason to buy today.

## What's working

- **Dual testimonial structure** is the standout: creator UGC quote (@JULIANA.WINS) in the hero, named customer ("Nicole") mid-scroll — more convincing than a single generic endorsement and a meaningful upgrade over recent sends.
- **Numbered product grid** (tee, pants, sandals, sweater with inline copy) shows the t-shirt in a full outfit context rather than in isolation, making the whole email feel more shoppable even without a price.
- **Subject line has a voice**: conversational and specific enough to break pattern after a week of "Extra 20% Off" subject lines.
- **Seasonal fit**: white t-shirt lifestyle photography in late July is timely and aspirational.
- **Clean render**: no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds end-to-end.

## What's weak

- **No CTA for the hero product**: the t-shirt earns two-thirds of the email and zero "Shop This Tee" button. The only visible "Shop Now" belongs to the Cult Favorites section — a completely different destination.
- **Cult Favorites section dilutes the email**: tacking a second editorial block to the bottom of a product-specific email sends readers somewhere else and diffuses what could have been a tight single-product story.
- **No price shown anywhere**: even a bare "$48" next to the product thumbnail removes the friction of clicking just to see if you can afford it.
- **Creator attribution is barely legible**: @JULIANA.WINS appears as a small overlay on the hero — unclear if it's a paid partnership or ambient UGC, which slightly undercuts the authenticity signal.
- **No incentive**: after training subscribers to expect "Extra 20% Off" on nearly every send this month, a zero-offer editorial will land flat for anyone not already sold on the product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a direct "Shop the Tee" CTA button** immediately below the product grid — the single highest-impact change; right now readers are persuaded with nowhere to go.
- 2. **Show the price** next to the product thumbnails; even small-type pricing removes a click barrier without diluting editorial tone.
- 3. **Cut or collapse the Cult Favorites section** to a plain text link in the footer ("Explore Cult Favorites →") so the entire email stays focused on the t-shirt story.
- 4. **Make the creator attribution read as intentional** — a small "In partnership with @JULIANA.WINS" label or a branded frame around the creator content.
- 5. **Write a preheader that extends the subject** rather than defaulting to leaked body copy or silence.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The T-Shirt Nicole Calls Her Go-Anywhere Piece`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The One Tee Worth Actually Talking About`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"It just feels good" — and Nicole isn't the only one saying so`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Not too wide, not too long. This one's genuinely different.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Value is a single space. Some mail clients (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) render the `<title>` in the preview pane or tab bar when the subject line is unavailable. Should be set to match the subject line or be left as a meaningful fallback.

**CSS `@import` for custom fonts — blocked by major clients**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Samsung Mail. `MaisonNeue-Light` and `Maison Neue` will silently fall back to `Arial`/`Helvetica Neue` in those clients. This is functionally acceptable given the fallback stack defined, but it means the custom type renders for roughly 30–40% of opens at best. Klaviyo's recommended pattern is to use `<link>` tags for web fonts, not `@import`.

**Duplicate `img` style block**
The `img` ruleset appears twice verbatim:
```css
/* block 1 */
img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic }
/* block 2 — ~400 chars later */
img { border: 0; height: auto; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; max-width: 100% }
```
The second block adds `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%` but drops `-ms-interpolation-mode`. This is not a conflict (last rule wins for the shared properties) but the duplication is dead weight and indicates a template merge artifact.

**Duplicate `.hide` media query block**
```css
@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px) { .hide { ... } }
/* identical block repeated immediately after */
```
Exact duplicate — no effect on rendering, but adds ~300 bytes of unnecessary payload.

**Deprecated `min-device-width` media feature**
```css
@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)
```
`min-device-width` is deprecated in CSS Media Queries Level 4 and removed from modern browsers. It still works in email clients via legacy paths, but the intent is `min-width: 10px` — safe to clean up to avoid future breakage.

**Global link color forces `#000` across all states with no hover differentiation**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Hover state is neutralized. Any inline CTA button styles must override this explicitly or the hover will show no visual change in webmail clients that support `:hover`. Confirm CTA `<a>` tags carry inline `style` overrides.
