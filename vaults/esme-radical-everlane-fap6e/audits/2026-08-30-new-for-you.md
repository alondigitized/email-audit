# New for You
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's plainest template of the week: a 3x3 grid of nine product cutouts (shorts, tees, blazers, tanks) under a "New Arrivals" header, no hero shot, no pricing, no offer copy anywhere above the fold. It's essentially a visual sitemap — "here's stuff we sell" — with zero reason to click any one item over another since nothing is labeled, priced, or differentiated.
- The sustainability block below the grid (recycling icon, "we have the better receipts to prove it," Impact Report CTA) is well-executed and on-brand, but it's competing for attention with the actual product push, and for a "New Arrivals" subject line it's an odd second act. Compare to 08/27's "New Styles, Highly Recommended," which at least anchored specific products to named customer quotes — this send has more product photography but less actual information per pixel.
- Judgment: this is a filler/maintenance send, not a campaign email. No offer, no urgency, no product names or prices, generic subject — it'll get opened by loyalists on brand recognition alone and largely ignored otherwise.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography — all nine items are cleanly cut out on matching backgrounds with no render bugs.
- Grid layout has clear visual rhythm — three rows of three, easy to scan.
- Single unambiguous "SHOP NOW" button sits right where the eye lands after the grid.
- Sustainability section is a nice brand-trust beat and doesn't feel bolted-on given Everlane's established voice.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing, product names, or category labels on any of the nine items — pure guesswork for the recipient.
- No hero product or lead item — nine equally-weighted thumbnails give the eye nowhere specific to land.
- No offer, discount, or urgency of any kind, despite fine print at the bottom referencing an "offer valid until 09/07/2026" that never appears in the visible body.
- Subject "New for You" implies personalization that the render doesn't deliver — the grid looks like a generic new-arrivals sweep, not curated-for-you picks.
- Coming one day after "Leather Up" and two after the "Boxy Sweaters" launch, this is the third distinct product-push email in three days — cadence fatigue risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add product names and prices under each grid image (or at minimum on hover/click) — right now there's no information to justify a click over just closing the email.
- 2. Cut the grid to 4-6 items and promote one as a hero with a callout, so the eye has a clear entry point.
- 3. Surface the fine-print offer (valid until 9/7) as an actual visible banner if it's real — it's currently invisible to anyone not reading the footer legal text.
- 4. If "New for You" is meant to be personalized, show a signal of that (e.g., "Based on what you've viewed") — otherwise drop the personalization framing and call it what it is.
