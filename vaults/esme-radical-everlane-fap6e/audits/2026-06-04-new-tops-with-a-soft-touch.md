# New Tops With a Soft Touch
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Everlane's June 4 send is a clean, competent editorial email for summer tops — but it's the fourth consecutive editorial-first send in as many days (after Linen Love, Stripes Are Having a Moment, and New Arrivals Right Now). The hook "A Softer Side" is pleasant without being distinctive. The brand is clearly betting on a fabric-and-feel story ("silk georgette to cotton-silk"), which is sound positioning for early June heat, but the email doesn't give a subscriber a strong reason to click *today* versus tomorrow or next week.
- The product grid of five flat-lay tops is practical — it shows range — but the hero model shot and the grid feel like two separate emails stapled together. The secondary "You Need These / New Arrivals" banner below the grid muddies the message further, introducing a third theme into what should be a focused category push.
- Bottom line: this is an inoffensive, brand-safe send that keeps the Everlane feed warm but won't drive meaningful incremental clicks. It's a 6 on execution, not a 3 — the visuals are clean — but there's no offer, no urgency, and the editorial angle has less narrative sharpness than the best sends in this recent run.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** Model in the boxy black top against clean white styling is exactly Everlane's aesthetic — confident minimal, seasonal feel.
- **Fabric specificity in body copy.** "From silk georgette to cotton-silk" is the kind of material detail Everlane's audience actually cares about; it's the one line that earns a click.
- **Subject line is mobile-friendly at 26 chars** — clear and untruncated in any inbox.
- **No render bugs.** Clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text throughout.

## What's weak

- **No offer or urgency anywhere in the email.** Full-price editorial sends need a sharper editorial POV to compensate — "Softer Side" doesn't clear that bar.
- **The "You Need These" banner interrupts the email's story.** A new-arrivals secondary CTA buried below the grid reads as an afterthought and dilutes the tops category focus.
- **Five flat-lay product thumbs with no prices, no links labeled by product** — the grid tells me nothing I can act on; I'd need to click through just to find out if any of these are in my budget.
- **Sustainability boilerplate at the bottom is wasted space.** Everlane subscribers have seen this exact block dozens of times; it adds scroll without adding value at this point in the cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to one specific product with the fabric story.** Pick the silk georgette top, name it, and make the hero CTA "Shop the Georgette Top" — not "Shop Now." Specificity drives clicks more than category breadth.
- 2. **Cut the "You Need These" banner.** It fractures attention. If new arrivals deserve a send, give them their own email (Everlane did exactly this on May 31 to good effect).
- 3. **Add a concrete hook to the subject line** — the fabric angle is the strongest thing in this email; lead with it rather than burying it in the body copy.
- 4. **Remove the sustainability boilerplate from promotional sends.** Reserve it for standalone sustainability storytelling where it lands with intention.
- 5. **Price-anchor at least one product.** Even "starting at $58" in the grid subhead gives subscribers a reason to click now.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `@import` for custom fonts — will silently fail in most clients**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside a `<style>` block is stripped by Gmail (any rendering mode), Apple Mail on iOS, and ignored by Outlook entirely. `MaisonNeue-Light` and `Maison Neue` will never load in those environments. Fallback to `Arial` is present and correct, but if brand font rendering matters, the font must be inlined via `@font-face` with a `<link>` preload or accepted as unsupported.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A whitespace-only title surfaces as a blank tab label in webmail previewers and can trigger spam heuristics on some filters. Populate it with the subject line or brand name.

**Duplicate `img` CSS rule**
Two separate `img { }` blocks exist in the `<style>` tag. The second (correct) block adds `height: auto; max-width: 100%`. CSS specificity in email is unreliable; merge into a single rule to avoid client-specific override order surprises.

**`alt` attributes** — source truncated before `<img>` tags; must be verified in full source. All product and hero images require descriptive `alt` text for accessibility and for text-only rendering.

**`h4` mobile font size collapses to 10px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { h4 { font-size: 10px !important; } }
```
10px is below WCAG 2.1 minimum readable thresholds and likely below the legal minimum for required disclosures if `h4` is used for fine-print text. Verify what content renders as `h4` on mobile.
