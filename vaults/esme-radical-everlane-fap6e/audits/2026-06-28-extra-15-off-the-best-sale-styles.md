# Extra 15% Off: The Best Sale Styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's fifth consecutive sale email in four days — following the June 25 "Quietly the Best Sale Yet," June 26 "Up to 70% Off," and two sends on June 27. The subject promises "Extra 15% Off: The Best Sale Styles," but the hero inside shows the exact same "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" creative from the June 26 send. The extra 15% is nowhere visible in the email body. That's the defining problem: subject and body are selling different things.
- The visual execution is clean — crisp product tiles, recognizable Everlane aesthetic, a "Everyone's Buying These" social proof section — but it's recycled creative with a new subject line stapled to it. A subscriber who has been following this sale arc since June 25 will open this, see the same hero they've seen twice already, and feel cheated out of the discount the subject promised.
- The honest read: this email could have been the best of the run — "extra 15% off" is the highest-value escalation yet — but the body doesn't deliver. It wastes one of the strongest hooks Everlane had left in this sale cycle.

## What's working

- **Brand identity is unmistakable.** The Everlane logo, clean layout, and muted palette are instantly recognizable without a single word.
- **Four specific product tiles.** Red tee, denim shorts, jeans, and black dress give the email editorial weight and likely link directly to product pages.
- **"Everyone's Buying These" social proof section.** The best-sellers lifestyle block is a genuine conversion nudge — momentum framing is more compelling than pure discount messaging.
- **"SHOP NOW" button is clean and prominent.** High contrast, centered, impossible to miss.
- **Subject line is short and concrete at 36 characters.** Mobile-friendly length, and the "Extra 15% Off" lead is the strongest hook Everlane has used in this entire sale run.

## What's weak

- **The extra 15% off is completely absent from the email body.** The subject promises it; the hero ignores it. There's no code, no banner, no explanation — just the same "UP TO 70% OFF" creative from June 26.
- **Hero creative is recycled.** This is visually identical to the June 26 send. A subscriber who opened that email is looking at the same header.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** No deadline, no "ends tonight," no countdown. The extra 15% off has no expiry signal, which deflates the urgency that a limited-time stackable discount should carry.
- **"SHOP NOW" is too generic for a sale this deep.** At an extra 15% off stacked on 70%, the CTA should be earning its keep — "SHOP THE SALE" or "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" would do more work.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Four near-identical sale emails in four days; even the most loyal subscriber is tuning this out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the extra 15% in the hero — or kill the subject line.** This is the only change that matters. Either update the hero to show "AN EXTRA 15% OFF" prominently (with a code if applicable), or rewrite the subject to match what's actually in the body. The mismatch is the biggest conversion leak in the entire email.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "Offer ends midnight Sunday" would double the urgency. The escalating offers (70%, extra 15%) have no stated endpoint — a credible deadline closes the loop.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific.** "SHOP BEST-SELLERS" is already in the email for the social proof section — it's better copy than "SHOP NOW." Consolidate or at least upgrade the hero CTA to match.
- 4. **Show pricing on the product tiles.** Four product images with no price or crossed-out original price is a missed opportunity. Even "Was $128 / Now $45" on one tile would anchor the deal.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The 15% Off Stacks: Grab It Before It's Gone`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your extra discount is live — here are the picks we'd start with.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty (whitespace only)**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Several spam filters and some preview-text rendering paths use `<title>`. An empty value is a minor deliverability risk and a missed preview opportunity.

**h4 mobile font-size drops to 10px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; ... }
}
```
10px is below the WCAG-recommended 12px minimum for legible text. Any product label or price rendered in `h4` becomes borderline unreadable on mobile.

**All link states, including `:hover`, render identically to body text**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links have zero visual affordance in any state. This is a WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure — links are not distinguishable from surrounding text without color differentiation or underline.

**Duplicate CSS blocks**
- `img { border: 0; ... }` appears twice with divergent property sets (second block adds `height: auto; max-width: 100%`). The first block is dead weight and creates a specificity maintenance hazard.
- The `.hide` media query block (`@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)`) is copy-pasted verbatim twice. No functional impact but adds ~300 bytes of unnecessary weight.

**External font dependency**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Email clients that block external CSS (Outlook desktop, some corporate proxies) will silently fall back to the declared system stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`). This is acceptable if the fallback is tested — flag for QA sign-off.
