# Up to 70% Off Sale Styles Dresses, Tees, & More
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Everlane follows up yesterday's "Quietly the Best Sale Yet" (9/10) with a direct percentage-off blast that trades the editorial wit of that run for functional clarity: "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / UP TO 70% OFF." The leaf botanical hero is a fresh visual break from the lifestyle-model streak, and the 2×2 category grid is clean and clickable. This is a competent, serviceable sale email — nothing embarrassing, nothing that wastes the list.
- The problem is sequencing. Yesterday's send was the "best sale yet" — a deliberately peaked note designed to close the promotional window. Running another sale push the very next day, with a more generic headline and the same 70% off framing, leaks urgency from both sends. The body copy ("Trip's booked. Now grab the pieces to match—at better prices.") is the freshest line in the email and it's buried below the fold as a one-liner. It deserved to be the hero.
- Functionally fine. Strategically, Everlane just told subscribers that "the best sale yet" has a sequel — which makes neither send the best sale yet.

## What's working

- **70% off is concrete and credible** — the single clearest signal in the subject line and hero; no ambiguity about the mechanic.
- **Category grid is efficient** — four clean flat lays (Tops, Sweaters, Bottoms, Tees) give a subscriber an immediate sense of what's on sale without needing to hunt.
- **Botanical hero is visually differentiated** — the yellow leaf breaks the lifestyle-model template Everlane has used for 10+ consecutive sends; eye catches it immediately.
- **"Trip's booked" body copy** — brief, voice-consistent, and actually adds a summer-travel framing that the hero headline misses.
- **"Everyone's Buying These" secondary section** — provides a soft social-proof beat and a best-sellers alternative path to conversion.

## What's weak

- **"Dresses" promised in subject, absent from body** — the subject line leads with "Dresses, Tees, & More" but the product grid shows Tops, Sweaters, Bottoms, Tees — no dresses featured.
- **Hero subtext is barely legible** — "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES INCLUDING VACATION-READY STYLES" renders as small white text on a pale yellow-green leaf; contrast is marginal at mobile scale.
- **Sweaters in a June clearout grid** — highlighting SHOP SALE SWEATERS in late June is seasonally jarring even for clearance; it makes the grid feel like unsold inventory, not curated sale picks.
- **No deadline anywhere** — no "ends Sunday," "48 hours," or similar. After "the best sale yet" yesterday, the lack of any close signal deflates momentum.
- **Generic hero CTA** — "SHOP NOW" does no work when the product CTAs below are already more specific; the button just adds friction before the real decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with "Trip's booked" energy** — flip the email: make the travel-context copy the hero headline, and let the 70% off serve as the supporting fact, not the lead. "Your summer trip just got more affordable" + "Up to 70% off clearout styles" would feel editorial *and* promotional.
- 2. **Show a dress** — if the subject promises dresses, put a dress in the grid. Swap out the sweater (odd in June) for a summer dress and a short. Subject–body alignment is table stakes.
- 3. **Add a deadline** — even a soft "sale ends July 4" creates the urgency this email completely lacks after a peaked send yesterday.
- 4. **Improve hero text contrast** — dark overlay or heavier type weight on the leaf background subtext; currently it's nearly unreadable on a phone screen.
- 5. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a specific CTA** — "Shop the Clearout" or "See All Sale Styles" does more work than a placeholder.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Trip's booked — up to 70% off everything you need`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer Clearout: 70% off ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tops, dresses, bottoms — clearout prices on the pieces that travel well.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Don't sleep on it — sale styles going fast.`

Screen readers and some email clients (Samsung Mail) surface the `<title>` as the preview label. Should be set to the subject line or a meaningful string.

```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active {color:#000;text-decoration:none}

```
Zero visual differentiation between link and body text at all interaction states. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color). At minimum, hover should signal interactivity.

**Duplicate `img` rule block**
`img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; ... }` is declared twice — once in the base reset and again further down in the stylesheet. No functional breakage, but it's noise that indicates a template hygiene issue.

**Inconsistent mobile breakpoints**
- Column stack / MJML layout: `max-width: 480px`
- `.hide` utility class: `max-width: 600px`

Content hidden at 600px stays visible through 481–600px where columns have already reflowed. Any element using `.hide` for mobile-only suppression will behave inconsistently on 481–600px viewports (tablets in portrait, large phones).

```
https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css
```

"MaisonNeue-Light" / "Maison Neue" load from Klaviyo's font API.
