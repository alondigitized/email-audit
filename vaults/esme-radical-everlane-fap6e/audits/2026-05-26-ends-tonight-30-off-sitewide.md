# Ends Tonight: 30% Off Sitewide
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the correct closing move for Everlane's seven-day Memorial Day sequence. Abandoning the category-pivot playbook (dresses → linen → denim → social proof → natural styles) and returning to sitewide with a hard deadline is standard urgency-close execution — and after six category sends, "Ends Tonight: 30% Off Sitewide" is the cleanest subject line in the entire campaign at 30 characters. The "Ends Tonight" close will outperform the May 25 "Natural Styles" pivot because the urgency is unambiguous and the offer scope is maximally inclusive.
- The creative problem is unavoidable: this email is essentially a relabeled version of May 20's launch send. Same hero format, same model aesthetic (woman in a black strappy dress), same "SHOP NOW" button, same minimal body copy. The only new element is the "ENDS TONIGHT" overlay. For subscribers who opened Day 1 and didn't buy, nothing has changed except the clock.
- The email earns its opens on subject line alone. The body doesn't need to do much work — but it doesn't try to do any, either, and that's a missed opportunity on the final send.

## What's working

- **Subject is the best of the campaign's sitewide sends**: 30 chars, urgency front-loaded, discount concrete. Does exactly what a closing subject line needs to do.
- **Hero hierarchy is clean and immediate**: "ENDS TONIGHT / 30% OFF SITEWIDE" dominates the render without competing elements; eye path is offer → CTA with no detours.
- **"Last chance to shop before it's back to full price" is honest urgency copy**: It states the consequence of inaction plainly, which is more persuasive than another exclamation point.
- **Single-CTA layout is correct for a deadline email**: One button, one offer, no competing exits.
- **No render issues**: Clean render, no broken images, no layout breakage.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP NOW" is the weakest possible CTA on a deadline send**: The model is wearing a dress. The subject says "Ends Tonight." The button should say "Shop Dresses" or at minimum "Shop the Sale" — something directional.
- **Category navigation links undercut urgency**: "Shop Women / Shop Men / New Arrivals / Best-Sellers" below the hero are browse-mode links that drain conversion focus on what should be a close.
- **No scarcity signal beyond the time deadline**: "Ends Tonight" addresses time; it says nothing about inventory. A single "sizes selling out fast" line adds a second motivator for zero creative cost.
- **Indistinguishable from Day 1 creative**: Seven sends deep and the visual language is identical to the May 20 launch. Any subscriber still on the fence has seen this hero format six times.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the CTA to match the hero**: The model is wearing a dress — "Shop Dresses" (or whichever category is best represented) converts better than "SHOP NOW" on a send where subscribers already know the offer.
- 2. **Remove the secondary navigation links**: Strip everything between the CTA and the footer. This is a conversion email, not a browse session.
- 3. **Add one scarcity line below the CTA**: "Your size is going fast." — no new design, no new copy block. Just a line that adds inventory pressure to the time pressure.
- 4. **Suppress buyers and Day 1 openers**: The segment still on the fence after six emails is probably size/inventory-blocked or price-inelastic. Personalize by previous-purchase category for those remaining.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: 30% off ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — 30% off everything, tonight only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, denim, linen — all 30% off until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Full price comes back tomorrow. This is the last one.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A space-only title renders as blank in Apple Mail's subject preview and is announced as empty by screen readers. Should match the subject line or a descriptive string.

**[CONFIRMED] `@import` font loading will fail in Gmail and Outlook**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail strips `@import` entirely; Outlook ignores it. The custom font `"MaisonNeue-Light"` (used in `h1`) will silently fall back to `Arial` — acceptable in practice given the fallback stack, but if brand rendering fidelity matters, the font-face declarations should be inlined as `<style>` blocks rather than imported.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `img` CSS rule block**
The stylesheet defines `img { ... }` twice. The second block adds `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%` which the first omits. Not a rendering defect (last-write wins), but indicates a template merge artifact.

**[CONFIRMED] Duplicate `.hide` media query block**
The `@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)` block containing `.hide` rules appears verbatim twice in the stylesheet. Dead code; no functional impact but increases payload size.

**[CONFIRMED] `h4` mobile font-size set to `10px`**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
```

10px is below the WCAG 2.1 / iOS minimum recommended tap/read threshold (~13–14px). If `h4` is used for any actionable or informational text on mobile, this is a legibility defect.
