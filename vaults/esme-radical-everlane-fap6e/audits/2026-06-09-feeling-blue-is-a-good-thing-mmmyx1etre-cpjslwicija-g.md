# Feeling Blue Is A Good Thing
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- "Feeling Blue Is A Good Thing" is Everlane's seventh consecutive daily email — and it shows the strain. The pun subject lands fine, but the body never commits to it: the hero says "MADE FOR SUMMER" (generic) rather than leaning into a blue color story with intent. The product grid below the hero includes a black tank that isn't blue, diluting whatever chromatic focus the concept promised. The lifestyle shot of navy top + white shorts + black sandals is clean and well-photographed, but the sustainability section that follows is brand-boilerplate filler that has nothing to do with "feeling blue" and eats real estate that should be reinforcing the color purchase argument.
- The structural bones are intact — single-column, clear CTAs, no render problems. But compared to "Two Pieces, Zero Decisions" (June 5, 8/10) or "New Linen Arrivals Just Landed" (June 6, 8/10), this email never finds the tight creative throughline that makes those sends work. The concept is gestured at, not executed.
- Verdict: a competent 7. The pun is better than the email it introduces.

## What's working

- **Subject line pun is genuinely clever** — subverting "feeling blue" = sadness into a color celebration is the right move for a summer email.
- **Preheader triplet is well-written** — "Something bold. Something blue. Something all for you." extends the concept rhythmically without repeating the subject.
- **Lifestyle shot earns its space** — the model in navy top + white shorts + sandals is a complete, shoppable look with strong aspirational clarity.
- **No render bugs** — clean single-column layout, all images loaded, no overlapping text.
- **"SHOP THE COLLECTION" CTA is unambiguous** — black button, short copy, no ambiguity.

## What's weak

- **Hero copy abandons the concept** — "MADE FOR SUMMER" instead of something like "Made in Blue" wastes the setup from the subject line.
- **Product grid undermines the color story** — the black tank in the trio isn't blue; it creates visual confusion about whether this is a blue edit or just summer product.
- **Sustainability boilerplate interrupts the purchase funnel** — the supply chain paragraph between "SHOP NOW" and the footer is brand filler in the wrong place for a color-story send.
- **No offer, no urgency, no price signal** — the seventh daily email with zero promotional incentive and identical CTA energy to every prior send this week.
- **Daily cadence fatigue** — subscribers who received "The Vacation Dress" yesterday and six near-identical editorial sends before that are going to be numb to this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the color concept in the hero** — replace "MADE FOR SUMMER" with "This Blue. This Summer." or similar so the visual and subject line actually resolve each other.
- 2. **Curate the product grid to blue-only pieces** — if you're selling a color story, every product shown must be navy, cobalt, or denim. The black tank breaks the logic.
- 3. **Cut the sustainability section from this send** — this belongs in a brand/values email, not a color-story promo. Replace with a second lifestyle shot or a tight 2-up featuring the hero product at price.
- 4. **Add a price anchor or time signal** — even "New this week" or showing a price point earns a reason to click today vs. tomorrow.
- 5. **Break the daily cadence** — consider holding this for a Tuesday send after a rest day; the 7th-in-7-days rhythm erodes open rates regardless of creative quality.
