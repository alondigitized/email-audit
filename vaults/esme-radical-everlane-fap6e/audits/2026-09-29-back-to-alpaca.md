# Back to Alpaca
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-product send. It sells one oversized Alpaca crew sweater at $118. The hero is an editorial photo of a model in a tan sweater and brown wide-leg trousers. "Alpaca's Back" sits beside her, and a full-width black SHOP NOW bar sits directly under it. After a week of "30% Off Sweaters" and "Ends Tonight" urgency, this is a real reset. It follows the product-and-proof approach of today's "Best T-Shirt I've Ever Bought" and keeps the discount out of it.
- The body is well built. It gives a short benefit paragraph, three color swatches (grey, tan, oatmeal), a customer quote, and a values block on Responsible Wool and Alpaca Standards. The price is anchored honestly against cashmere. The weak spot is the framing. "Back to Alpaca" is a returning-favorite hook, but nothing in it gives a reason to act today. There is no deadline, no offer, and no mention of restock scarcity.
- Judgement: a good, on-brand product-launch email that will get browsers but few urgent clicks. It reads better than the promo run before it, but it leaves conversion on the table.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The model and sweater are large, and the black SHOP NOW bar sits immediately below, so the path to the click is obvious.
- The email sells one product with one price ($118). There is no offer clutter.
- The copy addresses the usual objections: "resist pilling," "softer-than-ever," and "without the cashmere price."
- The Angela B. review adds social proof in a natural spot, between the swatches and the second CTA.
- The three-color swatch row shows the "new hues" claim instead of just stating it.
- The Responsible Wool/Alpaca Standard block gives value-driven shoppers a reason to buy.

## What's weak

- "Alpaca's Back" is the only hero copy. It is small, thin, and floats beside the model, so it barely registers as a headline.
- Nothing creates urgency. After three "last call" sends this week, a launch with no reason to click today feels flat.
- The swatches show only sweaters with no labels, prices, or color names, so they don't help someone choose a color. They also don't look like separate links.
- CTA copy is generic. "SHOP NOW" appears twice and "READ MORE" once, and none of them name the sweater.
- The values block is a large gray panel with a "READ MORE" button. It competes with the main purchase path and pushes the footer down.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real headline on the hero, such as "The Alpaca Crew Is Back — $118," in larger type. Right now the hero photo carries all the weight and the copy carries none.
- 2. Give the email a reason to act now. Use a low-stock line ("New colors, limited first run") or a member early-access window. Everlane already sent urgency all week, so a credible scarcity line is the better lever here.
- 3. Label the swatches with color names and make each one a link to its color variant. Add "from $118" or a rating under them.
- 4. Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop the Alpaca Crew," and shorten or drop the values block, or move it below the footer nav.
- 5. Change the subject line to state the product and price so the inbox view does the selling.

  - `…_13` ("Icon | Sign Up for Texts") → `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…`
- **Why it may matter.** Browsers resolve a relative `Location` against the tracker's host. That would send clicks to `trk.send.everlane.com/pages/womens` rather than `everlane.com`, which is likely a 404. Whether this is a real defect or a probe limitation is unconfirmed. A manual click test of one link in a real browser would settle it.
- **Sparse coverage.** Only 12 links were probed and 11 were skipped, so the remaining links are unverified.
- **Open pixel with no alt.** The tracking image `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M3QEN1VCYABJY6J2R9QNT7KB` has no `alt` attribute. It should carry `alt=""`. Low severity.
