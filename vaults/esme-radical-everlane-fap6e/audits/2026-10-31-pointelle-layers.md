# Pointelle Layers
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand product send for a $78 pointelle cardigan and a $68 henley, and the best offer is buried at the bottom. The hero is a close-crop of a model in a black pointelle cardigan with "A Finer Point" in small white type over it. A black SHOP NOW bar sits directly under it. The two product tiles below are well laid out and each has a price and a one-line fit note.
- The real hook is the slate-blue "LIMITED TIME ONLY / EXTRA 25% OFF MARKDOWNS" block. It sits below the product tiles and has nothing to do with them. A reader sees full-price knits first and the discount last, and the discount doesn't apply to those knits. That is the third send in four days to carry the 25% markdowns message (10/29 "Extra 25% Off Markdowns", 10/30 "No Tricks, Just an Extra 25% Off"). The subject line "Pointelle Layers" gives no reason to open.
- Verdict: a tidy, editorial send, mid-tier on impact. It is a product-story email with a stale promo bolted on, and neither half supports the other.

## What's working

- The hero crop is tight and tactile, so the pointelle texture reads immediately.
- The black SHOP NOW bar directly under the hero is unambiguous.
- Both product tiles show a price ($78, $68) and a one-line use case ("Wear it buttoned as a top, or open over one").
- The alternating image/text layout makes the two tiles easy to scan.
- The fine print states the deadline: "Offer valid until 11/01/2026 at 11:59 PM PST."

## What's weak

- The 25% off block is at the bottom, below the knits. It is the strongest offer and the least visible.
- "A Finer Point" is small, thin white text over a busy knit and is hard to read. It says nothing about the product or an offer.
- The subject "Pointelle Layers" is jargon. It promises no offer and no urgency.
- The offer block gives no deadline. "Limited time only" is vague, and the real date is only in the gray fine print.
- There are four "SHOP NOW" or "SHOP ALL KNITS" links to the same category. Neither the hero nor the tile CTAs say which product they lead to.
- The brand repeats the markdowns promo every day. The 10/31 "Newly Arrived Accessories" send also gave the offer little space.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 25% off markdowns block up, either as a banner above the hero or directly under it. State the end date, "Ends tonight", because the offer ends 11/01 PST.
- 2. Make the hero headline legible: larger, bolder, with an overlay or a color that contrasts with the knit. Use "The Pointelle Cardigan, $78" in place of "A Finer Point".
- 3. Change the tile CTAs to name the product: "Shop the Cardigan" and "Shop the Henley". Link each to its own product page.
- 4. Skip the 25% promo block if the knits aren't included. If they are, say so. If they are not, say "Markdowns only" so it doesn't read as bait.
- 5. Add one piece of social proof, such as a review snippet or "Back in stock".

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus an extra 25% off markdowns through 11/1. Pointelle cardigan and henley inside.`

## 1. Technical Summary

The link failure and the missing headers are most likely probe or relay artifacts, not confirmed defects. The HTML was truncated, so the body, footer and unsubscribe markup could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect target.** The probe for `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M69VGGPNTZZE8PQECKKW81XK_0` (link text "Everlane") failed with `unknown url type: '/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V&cm_flow_i…'`.
  - The tracker's redirect appears to return a relative `Location` value (`/pages/womens?...`) instead of an absolute URL.
  - Browsers resolve relative `Location` headers against the tracker host, so real users are probably fine.
  - But `trk.send.everlane.com/pages/womens` is not an obvious storefront path, so the tracker may resolve it to the main site. This can't be confirmed from the data.
  - Whether a recipient actually reaches `everlane.com/pages/womens` should be verified with a browser-based follow or `curl -I`. A strict HTTP client or link-preview scanner that doesn't resolve relative redirects will report this link as broken.
- **Probe coverage was partial.** 12 links were probed and 14 skipped (footer, social and utility links were deprioritized). The skipped links are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on the tracking pixel.** The image at `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M69VGGPNTZZE8PQECKKW81XK` has no `alt`. This is the open-tracking pixel, so the impact is minimal. Adding `alt=""` is still correct for a decorative or tracking image.
- **Empty title.** `<title> </title>` is whitespace only. This is minor and doesn't affect most clients.
- **Web font via `@import`.** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` is stripped by Gmail and some other clients. The font stacks fall back to `Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`, so there's no functional break.
- **Link styling.** The global rules `a:link`, `a:visited` and `a:hover { color:#000; text-decoration:none }` remove the underline from every link. Inline text links will have no non-color affordance, which is a WCAG 1.4.1 concern if any text links exist in the body. I couldn't confirm that from the truncated source.
- The viewport meta tag, MSO conditionals and `mso-hide` handling are present. No other rendering issues were found in the visible portion.
