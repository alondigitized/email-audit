# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane doing its minimalism-as-a-personality thing — which I get, I've bought into it literally five times over with my jeans collection. But what I'm looking at right now is a gray textured wall taking up most of the viewport, with two product shots barely peeking in at the bottom: a pair of black denim shorts and a nude kitten-heel thong sandal. No headline. No price. No "why you should care" — just objects floating in a concrete void. For a brand whose entire founding thesis is *radical transparency*, the homepage is aggressively opaque.

## What's working

- **Seasonal product alignment** — black denim shorts and a barely-there sandal in late May is the right merch. At least someone's paying attention to the calendar.
- **Product photography aesthetic** — the product shots themselves (flat lay style, clean white background at the bottom) look on-brand and sharp.
- **No intrusive modal or cookie banner** — I wasn't forced to dismiss anything to see the page, which is genuinely rare.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a gray wall** — literally a textured gray/concrete background occupying the top 60% of my viewport with no copy, no product, no reason to exist. I don't know if this is a hero image that didn't load, or an artistic choice, but either way it communicates nothing.
- **Zero transparency** — this is *Everlane*. Where's the cost breakdown story? The factory video? The "this shirt costs $18 to make and we charge $68" thing that made me trust them in the first place? The homepage shows me two products and a wall. That's not radical transparency, that's radical emptiness.
- **No visible navigation** — I cannot see a nav bar. I don't know how to get to bottoms, denim, or any category I'd actually shop from this screen.
- **No pricing anywhere** — even a $X starting-at price on the product thumbnails would give me something to calibrate against.
- **No human being in frame** — the products are shown without a body. I own Way-High Jeans specifically because of how they fit. Show me someone wearing them.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the gray void with a headline + cost transparency callout** — something like "The Denim Short. $12 to make. $68 to own." That's the Everlane brand in one line and it would make me stop scrolling immediately.
- **Add a sticky nav or at minimum make the top categories visible** — "Denim / Tops / Shoes / Sale" in a single row would let me route myself in under a second.
- **Put a price on those product thumbnails** — even "$68" in small text under the shorts converts browsers into shoppers by anchoring expectations before the tap.
- **Swap static product flat-lays for a lifestyle shot with a real person** — a 30-something in the shorts + sandals combo, shot candidly, would signal who this is for without requiring any copy.
