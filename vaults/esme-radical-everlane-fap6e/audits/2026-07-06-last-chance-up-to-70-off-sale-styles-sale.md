# Last Chance: Up to 70% Off Sale Styles Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Everlane has finally retired the folded-tee-stack photo that ran in six or seven consecutive sends and replaced it with a stark coral-gradient text block. The creative pivot lands well — "LAST CALL" reads immediately and the color-blocked aesthetic is clean. But the offer is verbatim identical to the July 3rd and July 5th sends: extra 20% off, already up to 70% off. If this truly is the last day, subscribers who survived thirteen consecutive promos have no reason to believe it.
- The subject line contains a typo — "Sale Styles Sale" — two instances of "Sale" back-to-back. It's a minor copy error but it signals carelessness at the exact moment the brand is asking for urgency-driven trust.
- The second block, "Under $100 / Few styles left at our deepest discounts," adds a scarcity signal that the hero lacks, but it's buried below the fold and the text links there are weaker than the hero's buttons. Net judgment: a well-executed visual that's undermined by repeat messaging fatigue and a proofreading miss.

## What's working

- **Creative refresh**: The coral-gradient hero replaces the overused folded-tee stack — a welcome change that immediately signals something different in the scroll.
- **Dual-gender CTAs in the hero**: "SHOP WOMEN'S SALE" and "SHOP MEN'S SALE" as distinct buttons reduces ambiguity and serves segmentation.
- **Scarcity copy**: "Final sizes. Final colorways. Don't miss out." is tighter and more credible than another generic "shop the sale" callout.
- **"Under $100" filter**: The second block gives a concrete price anchor, which is more actionable than a percentage-off range alone.

## What's weak

- **Subject-line typo**: "Sale Styles Sale" is a two-second proofread catch that undermines the urgency the subject is trying to project.
- **Repeat offer, day thirteen**: Extra 20% off / up to 70% off is the same structure as the July 3rd send — subscribers have been trained to ignore "last chance" language at this point.
- **No product imagery**: The hero is pure text on a gradient. After multiple identical photo-based sends, the swap is fresh — but there's nothing to lust after or click on. No specific item drives desire.
- **Asterisk with no explanation**: "EXTRA 20% OFF*" has an asterisk but no footnote visible in the render. That's an unresolved fine-print signal that erodes trust.
- **"Under $100" text links are underweight**: The second section has weak underlined text links vs. the bold buttons in the hero — the price filter is buried and the CTA mechanic is inconsistent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject-line typo immediately** — "Last Chance: Up to 70% Off Sale Styles Sale" → "Last Chance: Up to 70% Off Sale Styles." This is the single highest-ROI fix.
- 2. **Add a specific hard deadline** — "Ends tonight at midnight" or "Sale ends July 6 at 11:59 PM" makes the "last chance" credible after 13 days of escalation. Without a timestamp it reads like every prior send.
- 3. **Show at least one product** — A single hero product shot (e.g., one bestseller with "4 left in your size") would convert scarcity copy into tangible fear-of-missing-out. The text-only hero works aesthetically but leaves the click conversion to imagination.
- 4. **Resolve the asterisk** — Either drop it or add a one-line footnote explaining the exclusion. Unresolved asterisks are a quiet trust leak.
- 5. **Promote the "Under $100" block** — Swap the plain text links for a button matching the hero style, or move the block up. A price filter is a powerful conversion tool; it's wasted buried below the fold as a secondary element.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale Ends Today — Up to 70% Off + Extra 20%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final sizes, final colorways — gone after midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Under $100. Few left. This is the actual end.`
