# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-07-16
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- What loaded on my screen is a full-bleed extreme close-up of what I'm pretty sure is a ribbed knit or corduroy fabric — dark olive-brown, no text, no navigation, no brand name, no price, no CTA. Zero. It's a fabric texture filling my entire viewport. Everlane is clearly leaning hard into "the material is the message" right now, which I respect in theory, but the execution here is so stripped-down that there's genuinely nothing to react to except the weave.

## What's working

- **Texture detail:** The close-up of the ribbed fabric is genuinely interesting to someone who cares about materials. I can see the rib weight, the density, the slight variation in shade that tells me this isn't a synthetic flat-dye situation. That's a sliver of the radical-transparency promise I came for.
- **No visual clutter:** Nothing to distract from the material because there is literally nothing else on screen. Unintentionally meditative.

## What's weak

- **Zero information architecture:** No nav, no logo, no headline — I cannot tell I'm on Everlane's site without prior knowledge. If I landed here cold I'd think the page failed to load.
- **No CTA whatsoever:** I own five pairs of The Way-High Jeans and I would have no idea how to add a sixth. There's no "Shop" button, no category link, no scroll prompt.
- **No transparency numerics:** I'm here for the cost breakdown story. A fabric hero with zero fabric story copy (material name, origin, price-per-yard context, markup math) is the exact opposite of what makes Everlane Everlane.
- **No loyalty hook:** I've bought from them repeatedly and the homepage doesn't acknowledge me, my history, or my membership status at all.
- **Impossible to assess season or campaign relevance:** Could be fall, could be spring, could be 2019. No copy anchor.

## Recommendations

- **Add a one-line headline over the fabric macro:** Even something as minimal as "Ribbed Italian Cotton. $90. Here's why." gives me the radical-transparency hook that justifies the abstracted visual.
- **Drop a ghost-button CTA into the lower third:** `Shop the [Style Name]` in white on the dark fabric — one tap to a product page, no separate scroll required.
- **Surface a returning-customer ribbon or persistent nav strip:** A thin bar at the top with "Hi Esme — your Way-High Jeans are back in stock" or even just the Everlane wordmark + hamburger keeps me oriented and acknowledged.
- **Tag the fabric with a price anchor:** A small badge or overlay — "From $68 · Deadstock Ribbed Cotton" — turns this texture shot into a conversion asset instead of screensaver art.
