# Outfit Makers
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- "Outfit Makers" is Everlane's tenth (or more) consecutive daily editorial send, and the formula is unchanged: lifestyle hero, minimal copy, two product categories, generic "SHOP NOW," then a footer. What's different here — and worth noting — is the pivot to accessories. Shoes and bags are a harder sell than clothing in this format because fit and sizing anxiety are real barriers, and the email offers zero mitigation (no sizing guide, no "true to size" copy, no price anchor).
- The creative is clean. The sage-green shoe photography is the best visual in the series in weeks, and "Final Touches" is a smarter headline than most of June's sends. But accessories demand more persuasion work than a wardrobe staple, and this email isn't doing it. No price. No urgency. No detail that moves a browser toward a buyer.
- At ten consecutive sends in ten days, subscriber fatigue is the lead issue. Even the best creative can't overcome inbox blindness. This needed either a rest day or a reason to open that "Outfit Makers" doesn't provide.

## What's working

- **Sage-green shoe photography** is distinctive and visually rested — the best hero variation this series has produced in a week.
- **"Final Touches" headline** is conceptually clear and better than the generic seasonal labels ("Your Summer Uniform," "Tees Or Tanks?") from prior sends.
- **Two-category structure** (shoes / bags) gives the email a logical edit feel rather than a grab-bag.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, hierarchy reads top to bottom without breakage.

## What's weak

- **No pricing anywhere** — for accessories specifically, a price anchor matters more than for a basic tee; the omission leaves no reason to click beyond idle curiosity.
- **"Outfit Makers" subject is the weakest of the month** — 12 characters that tell you neither the category, the offer, nor the urgency. A subscriber scanning their inbox has no signal to act on.
- **"SHOP NOW" x3** — the same generic CTA appears three times with zero variation; "Shop The Shoes" or "Shop The Bags" would convert better by being specific.
- **Work-to-play copy ("For PTO or in-office days") is dated** — most subscribers aren't mentally in office-season mode in late June; the framing reads like a September campaign dropped by accident.
- **Cadence is punishing** — ten consecutive daily sends with no offer and no variation makes this scroll-past territory for anyone paying even slight attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Skip a day.** No single creative fix rescues send ten of ten. A rest day recovers attention; the next send lands with less resistance.
- 2. **Make the subject do work.** "Outfit Makers" signals nothing. Name the product, name the finish, tease a detail.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The bags. The shoes. Done.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Accessories that finish the look`
- 3. **Write CTA copy to match the product.** Replace all three "SHOP NOW" instances with "Shop The Shoes" and "Shop The Bags" respectively — same click, higher signal.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Slingbacks, satchels, and everything that completes the outfit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Better-for-you leather, ready for summer.`
- 4. **Add one price anchor.** Even "Starting at $98" or "From $148" changes the conversion math; it also self-selects the right buyer in and saves tire-kickers a click.
- 5. **Drop the PTO framing.** "PTO or in-office days" is jarring in late June. Replace with something seasonally correct — "beach to dinner," "market to lunch," anything that matches the subscriber's actual calendar.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` element** — `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace. Screen readers and some email preview panes read the `<title>` as the preheader fallback. This should contain a meaningful string or be omitted if the ESP injects one.

- **Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — The opening tag is `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">` with no `lang="en"`. Required for screen reader language detection per WCAG 3.1.1.

- **Duplicate `img` CSS rule block** — `img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic }` appears once early in the stylesheet, then a second `img` rule block appears later with nearly identical properties plus `height: auto; max-width: 100%`. The first declaration is redundant and adds unnecessary byte weight; the second is the authoritative one. Consolidate.

- **Inconsistent mobile breakpoints** — The stylesheet uses two different breakpoint values: `max-width: 480px` (used for column stacking, padding, font sizing) and `max-width: 600px` (used for `.hide` class). This creates a gap window (481–600px) where the `.hide` behavior triggers but column stacking does not. On tablets in portrait orientation this can produce mixed layout states.

- **External font dependency** — `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` loads "MaisonNeue" from Klaviyo's CDN at render time. Gmail (web), Outlook desktop, and Apple Mail all block or ignore `@import` rules. The fallback chain (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) is correctly specified, so this degrades gracefully — but font load failure is expected in the majority of clients.

- **Font-family name inconsistency** — `h1` references `"MaisonNeue-Light"` (no space, hyphenated weight suffix) while `h2`, `h3`, `h4` reference `"Maison Neue"` (spaced, no weight). If the custom font CSS defines only one variant name, one of these declarations will miss and fall through to the system font. Verify against the Klaviyo font manifest at the import URL.
