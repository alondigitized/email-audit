# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-08-13
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- I open Everlane on my phone and the first thing I see isn't the brand — it's a giant "Get 20% off your first order" popup sitting on top of a cookie consent banner. I own five pairs of their jeans; I am not a first order. There's zero cost-breakdown story, zero factory footage, nothing about how the denim is priced — just a generic email-capture wall stacked on a GDPR banner. This reads like every other DTC brand chasing new-customer emails, not the radical-transparency company I keep coming back to.

## What's working

- **"Unlock 20% off" button** — big, dark, unambiguous, easy to tap or dismiss.
- **Top ribbon "BETTER PRICES, STILL EVERLANE → SHOP WOMEN → SHOP MEN"** — at least this hints at a pricing angle, which is the one thing that could actually hook me if it led somewhere with numbers.
- **Nav bar visible behind the modal** — WOMEN / MEN / SUSTAINABILITY / EVERWORLD plus DENIM right there in the sub-nav, so once I clear the popup I'm one tap from my category.

## What's weak

- **The 20% off first-order modal** — I'm a five-pairs-of-Way-High repeat customer. This offer is not for me, and seeing it first makes the site feel like it doesn't know who's shopping.
- **Cookie banner stacked underneath the modal** — two overlays competing for my thumb before I've seen a single product. That's friction, not welcome.
- **No cost breakdown, no factory story, no numbers anywhere on screen** — this is Everlane's entire pitch to me and it's completely absent from the first view.
- **Hero content ("BA..." / "Pieces t...") is cut off and unreadable** — I can't tell what campaign or story they're actually running.

## Recommendations

- Detect returning/logged-in visitors and swap the "first order" popup for something relevant — new drop, restock alert, or a "your last order" callback instead.
- Put the cost-transparency hook (factory cost breakdown, "here's what you paid for") directly in the hero, not buried a scroll down — that's the actual differentiator, lead with it.
- Don't stack a cookie banner and an email modal at the same time; sequence them so the first thing I see isn't two dismiss buttons.
- If the offer must run, add a real deadline ("ends Sunday") so it doesn't read as a permanent, always-on acquisition gimmick.
