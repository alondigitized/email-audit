# Forget Sale. Try Better Prices.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh "Better Prices, Still Everlane" send in a week, and it's the beat run in reverse: rather than leading with price like 8/16's "Best-Sellers Now Under $100," it buries the price footer entirely — no number, no percentage, no dollar figure anywhere in this email — after the subject line explicitly promises "Better Prices." That's a bait-and-switch on the email's own premise.
- What's actually here is a "capsule wardrobe" product story — button-ups pair with pants, styled as "To Be Worn Together" — bookended by a sustainability blurb and the now-rote "Same quality. Same materials. Same favorites. BETTER PRICES, STILL EVERLANE" banner with no digits attached. After 8/16 finally cracked the code with real prices shown on-page, this one regresses to vague reassurance copy, which after a week of the same campaign reads as fatigue setting in on the creative team too.
- Clean, on-brand render with no visual bugs, but the mismatch between subject promise and body content, plus the sheer repetition of this campaign line, drags it down.

## What's working

- Hero image is well-composed and on-brand — clean, minimal, easy to scan.
- Clear visual hierarchy: hero → shop now → product grid → sustainability → price banner → footer.
- Two well-organized product categories ("At The Top," "Down Below") with three SKUs each, all images rendering correctly.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Better Prices" but zero prices, percentages, or dollar amounts appear anywhere in the body — the core promise is unfulfilled.
- This is the seventh consecutive "Better Prices, Still Everlane" send in a week (following 8/16, 8/15 x2, 8/14, 8/13 x2) — a subscriber has now seen this exact banner phrase seven times.
- The sustainability block ("We don't just say we're better") sits awkwardly between product shots and the price banner, diluting focus rather than reinforcing the offer.
- Regression from 8/16's "Best-Sellers Now Under $100," which finally showed real price tiers — this send strips that away and goes back to vague reassurance copy.
- CTA is generic "Shop Now" repeated four times with no differentiation by offer or urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual number in the body — this campaign has run seven sends without most of them showing a price; if the subject says "Better Prices," show at least one anchor price or percentage near the top, not just in the closing banner.
- 2. Retire "Better Prices, Still Everlane" as a recurring footer — it's been run identically for a week; rotate in the specific savings tier instead (as 8/16 did).
- 3. Cut or relocate the sustainability block to its own send — it interrupts the outfit-pairing narrative and has nothing to do with the price message.
- 4. Vary CTA copy per section ("Shop Button-Ups," "Shop Pants") instead of four identical "Shop Now" labels.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Button-Ups + Pants, Now at Better Prices`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Go-To Pairing, Priced Better`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mix-and-match staples, no price catch.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pairing that works every time — see the price.`
