# Meet the Forever Jersey Tees
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's single-product-launch playbook, same formula as the 09-20 "Introducing the Leather Boxy Tote" send: one clean hero, a four-SKU product grid, then the now-standard "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner and organic-cotton sustainability callout. It's well-shot and legible, but there's nothing here beyond "here's our new tee in four cuts" — no fit guidance beyond a one-line blurb, no price anchor against the sale banner, no urgency.
- The hero (car, striped tee, sunglasses, beach light) is genuinely nice editorial photography and sets a late-summer/road-trip mood that's on-brand and current for late September. But the email is essentially a lookbook page, not a sales push — the "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner directly below is a jarring pivot since none of the four featured tees are marked as sale-eligible, so the discount reads as bolted-on rather than connected to what you just looked at.
- Competent, clean, forgettable — consistent with this brand's recent 5-6/10 run. Nothing broken, nothing that will make someone delete it, but nothing that earns an above-average open or click either.

## What's working

- Hero image is strong editorial photography — genuine lifestyle context (convertible, ocean, sunglasses) rather than a flat product shot.
- Four-tee grid is well organized: consistent left-image/right-copy layout, each with a distinct one-line fit description (shrunken, cropped, oversized, boxy long-sleeve).
- Price is visible on every SKU ($38-$48), and each has its own "SHOP NOW" link, so a reader can act on a specific fit without going to a landing page first.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, alt-text isn't showing.

## What's weak

- No badge or note flags any of these four tees as part of "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" — the sale banner directly under new-arrival product cards, with no bridge, feels contradictory (new drop vs. clearance messaging in the same breath).
- Zero urgency or scarcity signal — no launch-week framing, no "just dropped," no deadline.
- No social proof anywhere in the body — no review stars, no "bestseller" tag, despite this being positioned as a hero-worthy new line.
- Preview text is missing or leaking — nothing usefully complements the subject in the inbox view.
- All four models are shown in a narrow demographic range (same styling, similar build) — no size-range or fit-on-different-bodies signal, which matters for a "cut/fit" story tee launch.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner from this send or replace it with a "New: shop the full Forever Jersey line" CTA — mixing new-launch and clearance messaging undercuts both.
- 2. Add a size-range or fit note under the grid ("True to size, shown in S/M") since this whole email is about four different cuts of the same tee — fit is the entire value prop and it's underexplained.
- 3. Add a light urgency hook to the hero or subhead — "New This Week" or a launch date — since as written this could run any week.
- 4. Fix/verify preview text so it complements the subject rather than leaking junk in the inbox.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Forever Jersey Tees: Four Fits, One Favorite Fabric`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: The Forever Jersey Tee, Recut`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shrunken, cropped, oversized, or boxy — pick your fit in organic cotton.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our best tee just got a refit. Starting at $38.`
- 5. Add one social-proof line ("Our #1 rated tee, now in four fits") near the hero or grid header to borrow credibility from the existing tee's track record.
