# Ends Tonight: Up to 30% Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a last-call reminder for the Fall Essentials Event. It's the second "Ends Tonight" send in the recent run. The hero is a tight crop of knotted green, brown and lavender knits with "ENDS TONIGHT / FALL ESSENTIALS EVENT" on the left and "UP TO 30% OFF / SWEATERS, OUTERWEAR & MORE" on the right. It's a better image than the blank gradient hero Everlane used for its earlier "Ends Tonight: Up to 30% Off" send. The texture says cozy and fall, and the subject matches the hero.
- The execution is thin. The deadline and the discount are both white thin-weight type laid over a busy, mid-tone knit. "UP TO 30% OFF" is the only offer and it's the hardest thing to read. The only product shown is a pile of knits, so no single item is linked or priced. The two black SHOP WOMEN / SHOP MEN buttons are clear but generic. The "Cheers to not missing out" line is nice brand voice, but it's the only copy in the body.
- Overall it's a competent, on-brand reminder that will do its job for people already shopping the event. It's undifferentiated from the three Fall Essentials sends before it, and it gives no reason to act beyond the clock.

## What's working

- The "ENDS TONIGHT" line sits right above "FALL ESSENTIALS EVENT" in the hero, so urgency is visible immediately.
- The hero image is rich and seasonal and suits sweaters and outerwear.
- Two full-width black buttons, SHOP WOMEN and SHOP MEN, are unambiguous and easy to tap.
- The copy "last call for best-sellers... Cheers to not missing out" has a light, human tone.
- The fine print gives an exact deadline (10/26/2026, 11:59 PM PST), which makes the urgency credible.

## What's weak

- White thin-weight text over a busy knit texture makes "UP TO 30% OFF" and "SWEATERS, OUTERWEAR & MORE" hard to read. The words sit on mid-tone areas with no overlay or scrim.
- No specific product, price or best-seller is shown, even though the copy promises "best-sellers".
- The bottom third repeats navigation (Shop Women, Shop Men, New Arrivals, Best-Sellers) that duplicates the buttons and dilutes the click.
- The offer is "up to 30%" on "select styles", with no indication of which items hit 30%.
- It's the same Fall Essentials creative family as the 10/24 "Up to 30% Off Our Best Sweaters" and 10/26 "Ends Tonight" sends. Subscribers will read it as the fourth near-repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the offer on a solid color band or a darker overlay, in a heavier weight. "UP TO 30% OFF" should be the first thing read, and it can't currently compete with the knit texture.
- 2. Replace the nav links with a 3–4 product best-seller row with real prices and a review count, such as the "over 2500 reviews" framing from the 10/24 alpaca send. This delivers on the "best-sellers" promise and gives the click a specific target.
- 3. Add a countdown or a clock-time deadline ("Ends 11:59 PM PT") in the hero, not only in the fine print.
- 4. Give the buttons category copy, such as "Shop Sweaters" and "Shop Outerwear", instead of only gender splits.
- 5. Vary the creative from the earlier sends so a fourth reminder doesn't look like a repeat.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Best-sellers, outerwear and knits, all marked down until midnight PT.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Klaviyo-built HTML email whose links all go through the `trk.send.everlane.com` click tracker. The QA run flagged two link-probe failures (relative redirect targets) and could not confirm unsubscribe headers or SPF/DKIM, because the relay didn't capture them. The HTML source was truncated, so footer, address and unsubscribe markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] "Shop Men":** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M5XD9HJNKSHKAX5H6MGQ8GWB_5…` failed with `unknown url type: '/collections/shop-all-mens-clothing?_kx=…&bxid=…'`.
- **[FAIL] "Everlane" (logo/header link):** `…/l/01M5XD9HJNKSHKAX5H6MGQ8GWB_0…` failed with `unknown url type: '/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=…&cm_flow_i…'`.
- **Likely cause:** In both cases the tracker's redirect target is a relative path (`/collections/…`, `/pages/…`) with no scheme or host. Browsers resolve a relative `Location` header against the request URL, so real users probably land correctly. The probe's HTTP client doesn't resolve it, so this may be a probe artifact. It is still non-standard: a relative `Location` is allowed under RFC 7231, but some clients and security scanners won't follow it. **This is unconfirmed as a user-facing break.** A manual click-through or a `curl -I` on the tracker URL would settle it.
- **Coverage gap:** 12 material links were probed and 7 were skipped, so the other 7 are unverified. The 38% pass rate is skewed by the probe failures and the missing headers.
- **Tracking pixel:** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M5XD9HJNKSHKAX5H6MGQ8GWB` is the open-tracking pixel. It is flagged for missing alt text, which is expected for a pixel. See section 3.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty title:** `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace. This is minor, since email clients rarely use it, but it is a real gap for web-view accessibility.
- **Pixel alt text:** The flagged image is the tracking pixel (`/o/01M5XD9H…`). Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip it. This is low severity.
- **Web fonts:** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` in `<style>` is ignored by Gmail and several other clients. The font stacks fall back to Arial/Helvetica, so the impact is cosmetic.
- **Mobile h4 size:** `@media (max-width:480px) { h4 { font-size: 10px !important } }` renders any h4 text at 10px on mobile. I couldn't see what uses h4 in the truncated source. If it holds legal or price copy, this is a legibility risk.
- **Other:** MSO conditionals, a viewport meta tag, and the `mj-column-per-100` and stack rules are present and well-formed in the visible portion. No other issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `*|…|*`, `{%`) appear in the visible source or QA output. The truncated source can't rule out tokens further down.

3. Verify List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post and Authentication-Results from raw headers outside the AgentMail relay, and stop treating them as sender faults until then.
4. Confirm the footer contains the physical address and a working unsubscribe link, using the untruncated HTML.
5. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel and give `<title>` meaningful text.
6. Check what content uses `h4` on mobile, given the 10px override.
