# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-06-11
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- I've been buying Everlane since the cost-transparency blog posts were a thing, and this homepage feels like they forgot everything that made me care. What I'm seeing is a near-black hero I can't actually read, two product thumbnails (shorts and sandals) floating in the middle of the screen, and another dark stone-texture block below that. No price. No cost breakdown. No "here's the markup math" story. It looks like an expensive brand that forgot to ship its homepage.

## What's working

- **Seasonal product pairing** — beige linen shorts + black thong sandals together signals summer intent. That's directionally correct for June.
- **Clean product photography** — the two product thumbnails are well-shot, neutral background, no clutter. If I could tap them to a PDP, that's efficient.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a black void** — no readable text, no visible CTA, no story. For a brand that built its entire identity on radical transparency and the "true cost" breakdown, showing me nothing is the worst possible choice.
- **Zero pricing visible** — I can't see a single dollar amount. This is Everlane. Price transparency is the whole pitch. Its absence feels like a brand identity crisis.
- **No navigation visible** — I can't even confirm there's a "Women / Bottoms / Denim" path from this viewport. If I wanted jeans — my actual reason for being here — I have no fast path.
- **No people in the frame** — both product shots are flat-lays. For a brand that historically used real factory workers and real bodies, this feels cold and disconnected.
- **The dark bottom section** — a stone/concrete texture block communicates nothing. Editorial for the sake of being editorial.
- **Small unreadable text** — there's a micro-label between the product tiles and the dark lower block. I can't read it. Whatever it says, it's not doing work.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the black hero with a live price story** — literally headline it: "The Way-High Jean. $88. Here's why." One image, the cost breakdown underneath. That's the reason I opened this email and came to this site.
- **Surface navigation immediately** — a sticky top nav or a visible category strip (Women / Men / Sale) above the fold. I shouldn't have to hunt for Denim.
- **Add price + "True Cost" badge to the product thumbnails** — every visible product tile should show its retail price and link to the cost breakdown. This is table stakes for Everlane's brand promise.
- **Put a real person in the hero** — a woman in the Way-High Jean, full body, real size range. Not a flat-lay, not a dark editorial void.
