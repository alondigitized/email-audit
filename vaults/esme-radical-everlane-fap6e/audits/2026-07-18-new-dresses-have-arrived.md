# New Dresses Have Arrived
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- "New Dresses Have Arrived" is Everlane's ninth editorial send in 12 days, and the format is now so locked in it reads like a template: wordmark, lifestyle hero with text overlay ("Dresses for Every Destination"), three-beat intro sentence, four-product grid (image left, short tagline right, "SHOP NOW"), sustainability footer. That predictability isn't fatal — the photography is clean and the product selection is coherent — but the recycled olive sale banner plastered across the top for the **third consecutive day** (July 16, 17, 18, word-for-word) is a structural failure. The subject promises new dresses. The banner promises 20% off sale styles. These are two different shopping trips, and the email never reconciles them.
- The four-product grid does real work: boatneck mini, seamed mini, textured halter midi, scarf-tie silk — distinct silhouettes with short lifestyle hooks. But without a single price shown, none of the four "SHOP NOW" links give a shopper a reason to act now rather than later. The hero CTA, the four product links, and the banner CTA all say the same thing in different sizes.
- This is a competent, aesthetically clean send from a brand operating on autopilot. The creative isn't broken — it's exhausted.

## What's working

- **Photography carries the send.** The cream slip dress hero is on-brand minimalism — clean, summery, aspirational without being overwrought.
- **Product grid offers genuine variety.** Four distinct dress shapes covering different occasions hit the "something for everyone" brief without feeling scattered.
- **Short lifestyle taglines do positioning work.** "Day to night," "vacation ready," "best-dressed guest" — these are three words but they're doing the job of a fit card.
- **Subject is honest and tight.** 24 characters, no spam signals, says exactly what's inside.
- **No render issues.** Clean layout, images load, text is legible.

## What's weak

- **Recycled sale banner, day three.** The exact same "IT'S STILL SUMMER. EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES (ALREADY UP TO 70% OFF)" strip ran July 16 and July 17. Subscribers are either ignoring it or actively annoyed.
- **Content and offer are incoherent.** Sale discount applies to sale inventory; the email showcases new arrivals. Someone who clicks expecting 20% off the silk dress won't find it.
- **Zero pricing in the product grid.** No anchor price anywhere in the product section. Without a number, there's no conversion trigger — just browsing.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP NOW."** The hero button, all four product links, and the banner all use the same generic verb. Nothing differentiates one click from another.
- **No social proof.** The July 13 "Best-Selling Bags" email wired real customer quotes to each product card and it worked. This email has nothing — the taglines read like internal copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or integrate the recycled banner.** Three identical days is subscriber fatigue territory. If the sale is still live, fold it into the editorial body with one sentence: "Our summer sale is still on — new arrivals excluded, but shop both." Don't stack a discordant promo strip on top of an editorial send.
- 2. **Show at least one price.** Even a single "from $88" anchor on the silk dress turns browsing into intent and signals the email isn't hiding anything.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** Replace four "SHOP NOW" links with "Pack it in" (halter midi), "Wear it tonight" (seamed mini), "Best-dressed guest" (scarf-tie silk). The taglines are already doing this work — make the buttons match.
- 4. **Pull one customer quote.** One sentence from a real review on the hero product. Everlane did this to good effect on July 13; this send needs it more.
- 5. **Set a real preheader.** The top banner text likely leaks as inbox preview ("IT'S STILL SUMMER. EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES..."), which is incoherent against a "New Dresses Have Arrived" subject.

Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` value in the subject line preview or tab. A whitespace-only value is a missed fallback.

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail's CSS parser removes `@import` entirely. The custom font (`MaisonNeue`) will not load in Gmail webmail (~28% of opens industry-wide). The declared fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) will render instead — acceptable, but the font load is silently failing for a large segment.

**`h4` mobile font-size set to 10px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below the WCAG-recommended 14px minimum for body text and below the 12px floor that iOS auto-scales. This will render illegibly small on mobile unless iOS text-size-adjust overrides it.

If the Klaviyo font CDN serves a single font-face declaration, one of these names will fail to match and fall through to the system stack silently. This is a font registration name mismatch.
