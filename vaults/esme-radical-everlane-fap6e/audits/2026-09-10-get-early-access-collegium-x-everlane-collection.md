# Get Early Access: Collegium x Everlane Collection
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the best Everlane send I've seen all week, and it's not close. After six straight "extra % off" blasts with a recycled peony hero, this is a genuine editorial drop: a named designer collab (Nick Sisombath's Collegium x Everlane), behind-the-scenes factory photography, and real quotes about craftsmanship. No discount, no urgency bar, no "SHOP NOW" black slab — just a waitlist CTA repeated at a sane cadence down the page.
- The problem is that it's a waitlist email, not a shoppable one. There's no price, no concrete product shot beyond the suede shoes in the hero, and every single CTA is "JOIN THE WAITLIST" — meaning there is nothing to actually buy today. That caps both the offer signal and the click urgency no matter how good the story is.
- Strong brand-building creative, weak transactional pull. Worth opening for the story, low likelihood of an immediate click-to-purchase because there's nothing to purchase yet.

## What's working

- Genuinely new creative direction — factory/artisan process shots and a real founder quote, a hard break from the peony/tulip/cloud-gradient rotation seen in every prior send.
- Clear, single hero product visible above the fold (suede lace-up shoe) with no render bugs, overlapping text, or broken images.
- Consistent black-and-white/tan editorial palette that feels premium and on-brand for a "designer collab" positioning.
- CTA is unambiguous and repeated at reasonable intervals (top, mid, bottom) without feeling spammy.
- No discount fatigue language — a real palate cleanser after this week's "extra 20-25% off" cadence.

## What's weak

- Every CTA says "JOIN THE WAITLIST" — there's no purchase path, so click-through has no transactional payoff.
- No price, materials detail, sizing, or release date visible anywhere in the email.
- Only one product (the suede shoe) is shown clearly; the rest is process/lifestyle imagery, so shoppers curious about the actual collection range see almost nothing.
- No urgency or timing cue (when does the waitlist open, when does the collection drop) despite waitlist emails typically living or dying on scarcity/timing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a release date or "drops [date]" line near the hero — waitlist emails convert on scarcity/timing, and this one has zero time anchor.
- 2. Show 2-3 more product angles/colorways of the Collegium collection, not just one shoe and process shots — give people a reason to want in.
- 3. Swap at least one mid-email CTA to something more specific than "JOIN THE WAITLIST," e.g. "Get Early Access" or "Reserve Your Size," to differentiate primary vs. repeated CTAs.
- 4. Add a one-line price/positioning hint (even "Starting at $X") so subscribers can self-select interest before committing to a waitlist.
