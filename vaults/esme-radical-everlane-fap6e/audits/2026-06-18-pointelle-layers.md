# Pointelle Layers
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- "Pointelle Layers" is Everlane's third consecutive editorial send in as many days — following "Your Summer Dress Rotation" (June 16) and "10/10 no notes" (June 17) — and the fatigue is starting to show. That said, this one earns its own send: where the previous two editorials leaned into styling and aspiration, this email introduces a specific product category (pointelle knit) with a legitimate product story. The hero model shot is clean, the body copy is tight, and the six-item product grid shows real assortment depth without overwhelming the layout.
- The problem is there's nothing to act on. No price. No urgency. No CTA copy that tells you what's on the other side of the click. After a five-day promo cycle that trained subscribers to expect 30% off, this send offers only vibes — and at this cadence, a subscriber who isn't actively shopping knitwear will scroll past without a second thought.
- Competent editorial execution, weak conversion setup.

## What's working

- **Hero image is well-shot and legible on mobile** — the model, the fabric texture, and the "Perfectly Pointelle" overlay all read clearly at thumbnail scale.
- **Body copy earns its line count** — "Lightweight, breathable, and just textured enough" gives three functional product benefits in nine words. That's Everlane at its best.
- **Product grid shows range without clutter** — six colorway/silhouette options in the grid communicate assortment depth and give different taste profiles something to land on.
- **Secondary lifestyle image isn't redundant** — the navy square-neck cardigan closeup adds a second texture read and a different silhouette, so the scroll earns something new.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout gaps, text is legible throughout.

## What's weak

- **Zero price signal anywhere** — no starting-from anchor, no member price, nothing. A subscriber who isn't already looking for knit layers has no financial reason to click.
- **"SHOP NOW" does no work** — it's the third editorial in three days with the same generic CTA. At this point it blurs into background noise.
- **"Pointelle" is insider vocabulary** — on a broad list, a meaningful share of subscribers won't know what this fabric is, which reduces both open and click relevance for non-fashion-obsessed readers.
- **Cadence is too aggressive for editorial** — after the five-day promo sprint, sending three editorial emails on June 16, 17, and 18 is asking a lot of subscriber goodwill. Each one competes against the last two for inbox attention.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the hero or subject** — "Starting at $38" or "From $28" in the hero subhead is the single highest-leverage change. Without it, the only people clicking are already in purchase mode.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop Pointelle" or "See the Collection" costs nothing to write and tells subscribers what they're clicking into. "SHOP NOW" has been on every Everlane send for the past ten days.
- 3. **Add a colorway count or "New" badge on the grid items** — "Available in 8 colors" or a small NEW badge gives the product grid a reason to interact with rather than just scan past.
- 4. **Pull back the editorial cadence to every other day** — three in three days dilutes the impact of each send. Let "10/10 no notes" (which scored 8/10) breathe before following up.
