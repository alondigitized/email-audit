# OOO On
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's third consecutive editorial send — following "The Natural Choice" (July 7) and "Summer Brights" (July 8) — and the brand is now fully settled into storytelling mode after its punishing 14-email sale marathon. "OOO On" is a clever pun (Out of Office = summer vacation = reach for your linen set) and the creative backs it up: a rust jumpsuit hero shot from behind, a nine-piece mix-and-match product grid, and the brand's signature "Why It's Better" sustainability block. The visual execution is among Everlane's cleaner recent sends.
- The subject line is the single biggest liability. "OOO On" lands perfectly if you live in calendar-invite culture — and does nothing for everyone else. It is six characters of pure intrigue with zero product signal, which is a high-risk bet on an already-fatigued list. The email also carries no offer whatsoever: no price anchor, no threshold, no urgency. That's defensible for editorial sends, but back-to-back-to-back is starting to feel like the brand has simply forgotten what its revenue button does.
- Clear judgement: good creative, brave subject, but the third editorial in three days with no CTA specificity or price visibility means this will drive brand affinity more than transactions.

## What's working

- Hero image is immediately legible — rust linen jumpsuit against a white background, clean sightline to the "SHOP NOW" button
- "All Set" headline connects the product category to the OOO/vacation theme without spelling it out
- Nine-item product grid demonstrates real mix-and-match range across color and silhouette
- "Why It's Better" linen sourcing block ("Masters of Flax Fibre™, 100% mechanical process, no GMOs") gives the editorial send a reason-to-believe backbone that commodity retailers can't replicate
- No render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text — the email renders cleanly

## What's weak

- "OOO On" is opaque to anyone outside office-culture shorthand; subject carries zero product or offer signal
- No price visibility anywhere — even a "From $X" anchor would reduce the pre-click friction of clicking into full-price product
- Both CTAs ("SHOP NOW" and "LEARN MORE") are maximally generic; neither tells me what I'm clicking toward
- Nine products in the grid dilutes the "one hero story" editorial angle — three or four pieces would tell the set story more forcefully
- No personalization or segmentation — gendered items (a skirt, a tank, men's shorts) are mixed without any targeting signal

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject so the OOO pun lands on contact** — the current version requires a decoding step that costs you opens on mobile
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with "Shop the Linen Set"** or "Build Your Set" — the CTA should close the loop on the editorial story
- 3. **Add a single price anchor** ("Linen sets from $XX") somewhere above the fold — full-price with no anchor creates avoidable friction
- 4. **Cut the product grid to four pieces** (top + bottom + one layering piece + the hero set) to tighten the mix-and-match story
- 5. **Add a light urgency signal** ("New this week" or "Limited colors") — the editorial mode shouldn't mean zero momentum cues
- **Subject Alt A:** `OOO Mode: Your Linen Set Is Ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Linen sets for your best Out of Office`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Crisp, cool, breathable — and made to mix and match all summer.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The set that travels with you. From the desk to out of it.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only a whitespace character. Screen readers and some preview clients (Apple Mail, Gmail web) surface the title; a blank title degrades accessibility and preview rendering.

**`h4` mobile font size at 10px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { h4 { font-size: 10px !important; ... } }
```
10px is below WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4's practical minimum and Apple's HIG recommendation of 11px minimum for readable text. Any product labels or captions using `h4` will be illegible on mobile.

**Duplicate `img` CSS block:**
Two separate `img { border: 0; ... }` declarations appear in the `<style>` block — one at line ~9, one at line ~28. The second one adds `height: auto; max-width: 100%` but the duplication is unnecessary and can cause specificity confusion in some clients.

**External font dependency — single point of failure:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
`MaisonNeue-Light` / `Maison Neue` are loaded exclusively via Klaviyo's CDN. If this endpoint is slow or unavailable, the entire email falls back to Arial — no local fallback font is pre-loaded.

**`.hide` class uses `float: left`:**
```css
.hide { ... float: left !important; display: none !important; }
```
`float` on a `display: none` element is a no-op but signals copy-paste from an older template. This pattern can cause layout breaks in non-compliant clients if `display: none` is overridden without also clearing the float.
