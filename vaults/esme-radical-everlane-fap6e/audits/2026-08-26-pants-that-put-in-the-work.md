- The catch: there's no discount anywhere in this email. ## What's working

- Clear category thesis in the hero copy ("Clocked In" / "office-ready pants that work well beyond nine to five") — gives the send a point of view instead of just listing SKUs.
- Single strong hero image with a full-body, well-lit model shot, immediately followed by a "SHOP NOW" bar — clean visual hierarchy.
- Three-product grid is well organized: image, name, price, one-line fit description, and its own "SHOP NOW" link for each — no guessing what's being sold or at what price.
- "SHOP ALL PANTS" secondary CTA gives a wider net below the specific picks.
- No render bugs — images load, text aligns, spacing is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- No visible discount, promo code, or price cut anywhere in the body — yet the footer fine print says "*Offer valid until 09/07/2026... Offer applies to select styles only" with no offer shown above it. That's a dangling asterisk with nothing to attach to.
- Subject "Pants That Put In The Work" and hero "Clocked In" are cute but not concrete — no product, no price, no urgency signal to earn the open.
- All three products skew similar (utility/twill pants, $88–$138) — little price-point or style spread for a shopper who wants a lower-cost option or a different silhouette.
- No social proof (reviews, ratings, "bestseller" tags) despite this being pitched as a hero category.

## Recommendations

- 1. Either put a real offer in the hero (if the footer's "offer" is real, surface it — "Extra 15% off Utility Pants" or similar) or strip the dangling asterisk/fine print entirely — right now it implies a discount that never appears.
- 2. Add a price or fit-specific detail to the subject line so it's not just wordplay — e.g. reference the $88 entry price or "stretch" fabric.
- 3. Add one bestseller/rating badge to the top product in the grid to borrow the social-proof credibility 08/25's field jacket send lacked.
