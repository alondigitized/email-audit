# Recommended By You
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's first genuinely social-proof-driven send I've seen from them — no peony/tulip macro, no "extra X% off" bar, no discount messaging at all. Instead it's built entirely around real customer quotes for three products: The Column Jean, the Cropped Luxe Fleece Hoodie, and the Utility Seamed Wide-Leg Pant. Compared to the discount-fatigue run of "Ending Soon" / "Few Hours Left" sends earlier this week, this feels like a legitimate change of pace and a smarter play for re-engaging someone who's tuned out the urgency bar spam.
- The execution is clean but thin. There's no price, no offer, and no explicit personalization beyond the generic "As reviewed by you" line — which is a slightly misleading claim since these read as generic best-sellers, not items tied to anything the recipient actually browsed or bought. The review quotes are a genuine strength (specific, named, credible), but the email asks for a click on faith alone: no fit details, no price, no comparison to what's already in a Column Jean or wide-leg pant category the shopper might own.
- Net: a well-differentiated, low-pressure email that will read as trustworthy, but it's missing the one thing that reliably drives a click from a deal-conditioned subscriber — a reason to act now.

## What's working

- Real, attributed customer quotes for all three products ("I absolutely love these jeans... mid-rise and full length" — Krista F.) read as authentic, not filler.
- Clear repeating pattern (lifestyle shot + flat-lay + quote + SHOP NOW) makes the email easy to scan.
- No discount/urgency language for once — a genuine break from the week's "extra 20-25% off" fatigue.
- No render bugs — hero, three product blocks, and footer nav all display cleanly with proper spacing and alt text.
- "Most Wanted" hero framing plus "Discover some of our most-upvoted styles" sets an honest, low-hype tone that suits a considered-purchase persona.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere in the email — for jeans/hoodie/pants this is a real click-suppressor since price is often the first filter.
- "As reviewed by you" / "Recommended By You" oversells personalization the email doesn't deliver — nothing ties these picks to actual browsing or purchase history.
- No urgency or time-bound reason to click today; combined with no price, the CTA has nothing pulling a fence-sitter to act now vs. later.
- Subject "Recommended By You" is generic and could belong to almost any retailer's algorithmic email — it doesn't hint at what's inside (reviews) or which category.
- Three near-identical SHOP NOW blocks in a row with no visual hierarchy differentiation — nothing signals which of the three is the "best" pick.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price (and any member/loyalty price) under each product name — this is the single biggest lever missing from an otherwise clean layout.
- 2. Rewrite the subject and preheader to lead with the reviews hook, which is the actual differentiator here, not the vague "recommended" framing.
- 3. Either substantiate "As reviewed by you" with real personalization (e.g., "because you viewed the Column Jean") or drop the claim and reframe as "Everlane's most-loved styles" to stay honest.
- 4. Add a light social-proof stat (e.g., "4.8★ / 1,200 reviews") next to each quote — a single named quote is good, aggregate rating is what actually builds trust fast.
- 5. Consider a modest, credible urgency lever (e.g., low-stock flag on a top-rated size) since this send otherwise gives no reason to act today.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Column Jean, the Luxe Hoodie, and more — rated 5 stars by shoppers like you.`
