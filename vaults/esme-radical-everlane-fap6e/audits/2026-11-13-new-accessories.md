# New Accessories
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, seasonal accessories send with no price anywhere. The hero is a camel scarf in snow with a red ribbon, a gifting cue that fits mid-November. "The Finishing Touches" is overlaid in thin white type across the scarf, and it is hard to read. A black "Shop Now" bar sits below it.
- Below the hero are three lines of copy, a six-item grid with no prices, labels or individual links I can see, and a maroon "Better Gift Guide" block. The block says "Now at better prices" but never names a price or discount. That is the only offer-like language in the email, and it is vague.
- It looks good and the brand voice is consistent. As a send it is soft. Compared with "Keep the Fleece" on 11/12, which listed $68 to $88 prices, and "The $68 Cashmere Slim Crew" on 11/11, which put the price in the subject, this one gives a subscriber nothing concrete to act on. It is the third "new arrivals, no offer" send in four days (Everpuff Cloud and Corduroy are the others). Judgement: pretty, forgettable, and likely to be skimmed.

## What's working

- The hero image is seasonal and gift-coded, with the ribbon and snow, and it is a single clear focal point.
- The copy under the hero is short and specific: "cleaner leather, certified organic cotton."
- The six-product grid shows a good range (duffel, backpack, three beanies, scarf) on a neutral background, so it is easy to scan.
- The "Better Gift Guide" block adds a second route into the site and splits it into Shop Women and Shop Men.
- The layout is clean and renders without visible bugs.

## What's weak

- No price or discount appears anywhere. "Now at better prices" is a promise with nothing behind it.
- The "The Finishing Touches" overlay is thin white text on a pale scarf and snow background, so it has very low contrast.
- The six grid items carry no names, prices or visible CTAs, so there is no reason to prefer one over another.
- The headline "New Accessories" is generic. It is the same flat product-launch framing as the rest of the week.
- The maroon block has a lot of dead space below its buttons.
- The "Shop Now" bar and the "Shop Women / Shop Men" links are generic and not tied to a product.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real number in the email and the subject. For example, name the price floor for the scarf, hat and bag set, such as "Gifts from $X". This would lift the email most.
- 2. Label each grid item with a name and a price, and link each one to its own product page.
- 3. Replace the thin white overlay with a bolder, darker headline, or put the headline on the white background above the hero.
- 4. Make the hero CTA specific, for example "Shop Scarves & Beanies" instead of "Shop Now".
- 5. Replace "Now at better prices" with a concrete claim, such as free shipping or a gift deadline.
- 6. Add one line of social proof, such as a review quote or a "best-seller" tag on one item.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The cashmere scarf is back ($68)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cleaner leather, organic cotton, and gift-ready picks from $28.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beanies, bags and scarves they'll actually use. Shop the gift guide.`

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Klaviyo-built template sent through the `trk.send.everlane.com` click tracker. Two probed links failed QA, and the header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be confirmed. The HTML source and QA output are both truncated, so some findings are unverifiable.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect targets.** Two tracked links returned `unknown url type`, with a relative path where an absolute URL was expected:
  - `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V…` (link text "Icon | Sign Up for Texts"), via `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M7AWVVZX51N1GQH02N7YZJS4_17`
  - `/pages/womens?_kx=…` (link text "Everlane"), via `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M7AWVVZX51N1GQH02N7YZJS4_0`
- Both failures have the same shape. The tracker's redirect appears to return a relative `Location`, which the probe's HTTP client couldn't resolve. Browsers resolve relative `Location` values per RFC 7231, so this may be a probe limitation rather than a user-facing break. The QA data can't tell which, so it's unconfirmed. It's worth checking with `curl -I` against the two tracking URLs.
- **Coverage gap.** Only 12 links were probed and 15 were skipped (footer, utility and social links are deprioritized). The other 15 are unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **[WARN] Missing alt text.** The flagged image is `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M7AWVVZX51N1GQH02N7YZJS4`. The `/o/` path indicates the open-tracking pixel, so this is low severity. Adding `alt=""` would clear the warning.
- **Small mobile text.** The `<style>` block sets `h4 { font-size: 10px !important }` at `max-width: 480px`, well below readable size if any `h4` carries content.
- **Empty `<title>`.** The document has `<title> </title>`, which is whitespace only.
- **Links look like plain text.** The global rules `a:link, a:visited, a:hover { color:#000; text-decoration:none }` leave links with no underline or color cue. This is a WCAG 1.4.1 concern for inline text links.
- Other markup checks out: viewport meta, MSO conditionals, `-webkit-text-size-adjust` and the `[data-ogsc]` dark-mode rules are all present.
