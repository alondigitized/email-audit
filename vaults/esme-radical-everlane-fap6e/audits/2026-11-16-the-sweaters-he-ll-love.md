# The Sweaters He’ll Love
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand men's sweater catalog with no price and no offer. The hero is a model in a dark brown crewneck with "Your Sweater Shortlist" in thin type and a black SHOP NOW bar. Below it sit four zigzag product rows (Midweight Cashmere Crew, Felted Merino Hoodie, Cashmere Crew Sweater, Waffle Rollneck Crew), then a maroon "Better Gift Guide" block.
- The execution is tidy, but it is the same template as the 2026-11-14 "Holiday Dresses" and "Gifts For Him Starting at $50" sends. The subject "The Sweaters He'll Love" is gift-framed but has no price anchor. Everlane's best recent send, "The New $48 Cashmere Tee" (11-15), worked because it named a number. This one has none, and the only value language is the vague "Now at better prices" at the bottom.
- Judgement: a competent but forgettable send. Every product row ends in an identical "SHOP NOW" link, and nothing tells the reader why to buy today.

## What's working

- The hero is clear. A strong model shot, an uncluttered layout and a full-width black CTA bar put the eye on the product first.
- The zigzag layout is easy to scan. Each row pairs one image, a short blurb and a link.
- The copy is useful. "Made with better-for-you wools and cotton" and "ready to gift or keep for yourself" cover both gifting and self-purchase.
- The maroon gift-guide block gives a seasonal bookend, and the women/men split lets gift shoppers branch out.
- There are no render bugs. Images load, text is legible and nothing overlaps.

## What's weak

- There is no price or percentage anywhere. "Now at better prices" is a promise with no number, so the offer is invisible.
- Four identical "SHOP NOW" underlined links give no product-specific CTA. Small text links are weaker than buttons.
- There are no reviews, ratings, or sizing and fit details.
- There is no urgency. Shipping cutoffs and gift deadlines are missing even though it's mid-November.
- The two cashmere crews are close enough that they compete for the same click, and the product names carry no prices.
- "Shop Women" in a men's send is a distraction. The "The kind of sweater grown men wear" line also reads a bit awkward.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price in the hero and on every product row, for example "Cashmere crews from $98". This is the single biggest lift, as 11-15's $48 tee showed.
- 2. Replace the four generic "SHOP NOW" links with product-specific ones ("Shop the Cashmere Crew") and make them buttons.
- 3. Add a social-proof line (star rating or "Best-seller") to at least one product row.
- 4. Add a gift deadline to the gift-guide block ("Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery") to create credible urgency.
- 5. Drop one of the two crewnecks and make room for a clearer price ladder (merino hoodie at $X, cashmere at $Y).

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Klaviyo-built, MJML-style template with click and open tracking on the `trk.send.everlane.com` domain. The automated QA shows a 38% pass rate. Two tracked links failed on relative redirect targets, and the header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be evaluated because the relay didn't capture the headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect targets (2 links).** Both tracked links resolve to a path with no scheme or host (`unknown url type`):
  - "Icon | Sign Up for Texts" → `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V&cm_fl…`
  - "Everlane" → `/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=…&cm_flow_i…`
  - Source: `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M7JM2DBADFWV6J8HTVW22S54_20` and `…_0`.
  - A relative `Location` header is legal under RFC 7231, so this may be a probe limitation rather than a user-facing break. It is unconfirmed either way. Real clients resolve it against the tracker host, so the result would be `trk.send.everlane.com/pages/...`. That would 404 unless the tracker proxies it.
- **Header link points to the wrong department.** The "Everlane" logo/header link goes to `/pages/womens`, but the sender is "Everlane Men". This is confirmed by the QA output.
- **Probe coverage is partial.** Only 12 of 30 links were probed and 18 were skipped, so the 38% pass rate understates the real figure.
- **Open pixel has no alt attribute.** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M7JM2DBADFWV6J8HTVW22S54` is the tracking pixel. Low severity, but add `alt=""`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
Findings from the visible (truncated) source:
- `<title> </title>` is empty.
- `<html>` has no `lang` attribute. It carries only the `xmlns`, `xmlns:o` and `xmlns:v` attributes.
- Links are forced to `color:#000; text-decoration:none` in every state (`a:link`, `a:visited`, `a:hover`, `a:active`). Inline links therefore have no non-colour affordance.
- On mobile (≤480px), `h4 { font-size: 10px !important }` renders below common legibility minimums.
- The `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` is stripped by Gmail and some clients. The font stacks fall back to Arial/Helvetica, so this is handled.
- MSO conditionals and `[data-ogsc]` dark-mode rules are present and look correct.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tags (`{{ }}`, `*|…|*`, `%%…%%`) appear in the visible source. The HTML was truncated, so the full body wasn't checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** The QA tool itself attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it is unverified, not a confirmed defect. Check the raw message headers. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown for the same reason.
- The QA notes that an unsubscribe link is always probed and reports no failure for it. The footer and physical postal address fall in the truncated portion of the HTML, so they couldn't be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The visible link parameters are Klaviyo's `_kx`, `bxid` and `cm_fl…` (Klaviyo flow tracking). No `utm_source`, `utm_medium` or `utm_campaign` appears in the visible portion, and the full query strings are truncated.
- The tracker redirect hides the final landing URLs, so UTM presence and landing-page match with the "Sweaters" subject can't be confirmed. The "Everlane" header link landing on the women's page is the one confirmed mismatch (see section 2).

## 7. Recommendations
1. Run `curl -I` on `…/l/01M7JM2DBADFWV6J8HTVW22S54_0` and `…_20` to see the actual `Location` header. If it really is relative, have Klaviyo or the ESP emit absolute `https://www.everlane.com/pages/...` redirects. If the final destinations resolve fine, treat the failures as a probe-side bug and fix the checker.
2. Point the header/logo link at the men's landing page or the homepage to match the "Everlane Men" sender.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a meaningful `<title>`. Add `alt=""` to the open pixel.
4. Raise the mobile `h4` size above 10px, and add an underline or other non-colour cue to inline text links.
5. Inspect the raw headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` before marking compliance as failed. Fetch the full HTML to verify the footer address and the UTM parameters.
6. Make the QA tool resolve relative `Location` headers against the redirect host, and count skipped links separately from failures so the pass rate is meaningful.
