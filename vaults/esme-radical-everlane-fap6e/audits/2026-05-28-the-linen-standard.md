# The Linen Standard
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's editorial reset after an eight-day unbroken promotional blitz — seven days of 30% off Memorial Day messaging, then yesterday's post-holiday markdown pivot. "The Linen Standard" attempts to shift register entirely: brand education, materials story, premium positioning. That instinct is correct. After that cadence, subscribers need a breath before the next offer wave.
- The problem is the email can't commit. The top half is a confident brand story — European flax provenance, "Better Linen" hero, a clean "Why It's Better" sustainability module. Then halfway down, a dark-background banner drops in for "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES." Those two things are at war. The materials story says *this is worth paying for*; the sale banner screams *we're still clearing inventory*. A subscriber's mental model gets whiplashed, and neither message lands cleanly.
- The execution is otherwise clean — renders well, strong photography, good hierarchy in the hero. This is a solid editorial foundation that needed a cleaner editorial commitment.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is the strongest in the recent sequence** — the rust/brown linen styling reads current and the overlay copy ("Better Linen," "SHOP NOW") is legible without competing with the image.
- **"Why It's Better" module is genuinely differentiated** — Flas Fibre™ mechanics, zero-waste extraction, no GMOs is the kind of specificity Everlane subscribers subscribe for; no competitor sends this.
- **Subject line cadence break is correct** — after eight offer-forward subjects in a row, a brand-forward subject ("The Linen Standard") doesn't feel like more noise.
- **No render issues** — clean layout, images load, text is legible, no overlapping or broken elements visible.
- **The three-product grid** (dark top, black dress, cream piece) gives quick visual variety without cluttering.

## What's weak

- **The 70% off sale banner breaks the editorial premise entirely** — it's the most visually aggressive element in the lower half and directly contradicts the premium linen story being told above it.
- **"The Linen Standard" is abstract where it should be evocative** — it doesn't telegraph linen *quality*, it telegraphs a vague brand claim. Compared to the May 22 "30% Off Linen, For A Limited Time," this subject generates less urgency *and* less curiosity.
- **No preview text visible** — the first pullable copy after the hero would be "Lightweight, breathable, and naturally better…" which is decent, but the absence of a dedicated preheader is a wasted inbox slot given the weak subject.
- **Multiple undifferentiated "SHOP NOW" CTAs** (at least three visible) — none are linked to a specific product moment; they feel like placeholders, not destinations.
- **The linen category was already the subject of the May 22 send** — hitting linen again six days later as a *different creative angle* requires stronger differentiation in the subject to not feel like a repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the 70% off sale banner** — or give it its own dedicated send. Inserting it into a brand story email deflates both messages. If the sale inventory still needs clearing, send it tomorrow as a standalone.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to activate curiosity about the materials claim** — the strongest asset in this email is the "made with 1% of global flax" fact; that should be the subject hook, not an interior module.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Why 1% of the world's flax ends up here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The linen most brands can't get`
- 3. **Add a dedicated preheader** that bridges the subject and pays off the provenance story before the open.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rare European flax. Zero waste. Made to outlast trends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Only 1% of global flax makes the cut — ours does.`
- 4. **Consolidate to one CTA** tied to the linen collection specifically — "Shop the Linen Edit" instead of three generic "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Lead the product grid with the hero item** — the model is wearing a specific piece; link that exact item first so the editorial story converts directly.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for custom fonts will silently fail in major clients**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all versions), and Samsung Mail strip `@import` rules. `MaisonNeue-Light` and `Maison Neue` will not load in these clients; the declared fallback chain (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) will activate. Low severity if fallback is acceptable, but worth confirming renders as intended.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Apple Mail and some screen readers surface `<title>` in the preview pane or accessibility tree. A whitespace-only title reads as blank. Should match the subject line at minimum.

**All anchor states stripped to `color:#000; text-decoration:none`**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
No visual differentiation between link text and body copy in any state. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) — links must be distinguishable from surrounding text by means other than color alone.

**`h4` at 10px on mobile**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px falls below the practical accessibility floor (~11–12px minimum) and is likely unreadable on high-DPI screens. WCAG SC 1.4.4 requires text to be resizable to 200% without loss of content; starting at 10px makes this effectively unusable.

**Duplicate `img` CSS rule**
Two separate `img { ... }` blocks appear in the stylesheet — one without `height: auto`/`max-width: 100%`, one with. The second block overrides the first, but this is a template artifact that could cause confusion during future edits.

**Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
Most media queries use `max-width: 480px`; the `.hide` utility class uses `max-width: 600px`. Devices at 481–600px width (common tablets and large phones) will have `.hide` applied but column-stacking and padding resets will not. Verify no layout elements depend on these two breakpoints resolving together.

**Duplicate `.hide` media query block**
The `@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)` block containing `.hide` and `.font12` rules appears verbatim twice. This is a template generation error; no functional impact but adds dead weight.
