# Cardigans That Think They’re Tops
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is send nine in what has become Everlane's daily editorial drumbeat, and "Cardigans That Think They're Tops" continues the exact same template: lifestyle hero image, headline overlay, a few lines of body copy, a product grid with individual "SHOP NOW" links, sustainability block, footer nav. There is nothing formally wrong here — the email renders cleanly, the products are well-photographed, and the cardigan-as-top framing is a legitimately smart angle for summer. But the execution undercuts the idea almost immediately. The hero copy reads "Summer Cardigans" — not the playful "think they're tops" line — so the brand's best asset (that cheeky subject) gets dropped the moment you open.
- The offer structure is zero. No pricing, no discount, no urgency. Three cardigans are shown in the product grid with brief descriptors and their own "SHOP NOW" CTAs, but without prices or any signal that these are featured, new, or on promotion, there is no reason to click now rather than later (or never). After eight consecutive no-discount sends, the cadence fatigue is real: a subscriber who got "Your Summer Uniform" (June 23), "Lightweight Jeans You'll Love" (June 22), and "Mini Dresses for Summer Solstice" (June 21) has seen this formula many times this week alone.
- Net verdict: a competent execution of a tired playbook. The subject line is the email's best idea; it never makes it into the body.

## What's working

- **Subject-to-product coherence is tight** — the three products (Pointelle Cardigan, Lace Knit Cardigan, Cardigan in Ultrasoft Merino) all visibly support the "wears like a top" positioning.
- **Hero image is clean and well-lit** — the navy cardigan model reads immediately; no render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the email.
- **Product descriptions earn their space** — "Slim, cropped, with just a touch of texture" and "The perfect topper for any tee or tank" are specific and functional, not vague brand-speak.
- **AC-included callout in body copy** — "office AC included" is a concrete, seasonally relevant use case that justifies opening a cardigan email in June.

## What's weak

- **The subject's wit never enters the body** — "Cardigans That Think They're Tops" is the email's strongest copy; the hero headline reads "Summer Cardigans" and kills the momentum.
- **No pricing anywhere** — not in the hero, not in the product tiles. Three product CTAs with no price signal is asking for clicks on blind faith.
- **Three separate "SHOP NOW" CTAs below the fold** — disperses click intent across three destinations instead of driving to a curated landing page.
- **Sustainability block is boilerplate** — the "READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT" section is copy-pasted from every prior Everlane send reviewed; subscribers have seen this exact paragraph nine consecutive times.
- **No urgency or newness signal** — nothing communicates whether these cardigans are new arrivals, a limited run, or a permanent category; there is no reason to act today.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Carry the subject's voice into the hero** — Swap "Summer Cardigans" for something like "They think they're tops." or "Summer's most convincing layering trick." The subject earns the open; the body needs to pay it off.
- 2. **Add price to every product tile** — Even if there's no discount, showing "$128" anchors the decision and removes the friction of clicking just to find out what something costs.
- 3. **Collapse three CTAs into one** — Route all three cardigans to a single "Shop the Edit" collection page. Three "SHOP NOW" links below three products trained readers to skip to none of them.
- 4. **Give the body copy the AC angle earlier and harder** — "AC-included" is buried in a short intro graf. Make it the subheadline: *"For the meeting room that's 65°F year-round."*
- 5. **Retire or move the sustainability block** — It has appeared in every email this week. Move it to the footer or a quarterly impact digest; it is no longer differentiating per-send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` is empty (whitespace only)**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some screen readers and preview panes surface the `<title>` tag. A blank value is a missed opportunity and may surface as an untitled document in accessibility trees.

**CSS `@import` for custom fonts will silently fail in most clients**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail (web and app), Outlook 2007–2021, and Apple Mail on iOS strip or ignore `@import`. The `MaisonNeue-Light` font declared for `h1` will not render for the majority of recipients — fallback stack `Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif` is what most users see. This is expected Klaviyo behavior but worth confirming visual designs account for it.

**All anchor tags are visually indistinguishable from body text**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links carry no color or underline differentiation from surrounding copy. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) — color alone cannot be the sole distinguishing factor for links. At a minimum, an underline or non-color-dependent indicator is required on non-hover states.

**`h4` drops to 10px on mobile**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below the WCAG-recommended 12px minimum and well below platform guidelines (14px+). Text at this size is effectively unreadable on most mobile displays and will trigger Apple Mail's font-size auto-adjustment, which may break layout.
