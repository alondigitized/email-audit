# New Arrivals at New Better Prices
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely different creative lane for Everlane's week — a 9-product new-arrivals grid instead of the giant color-block price banners that dominated 08/19–08/23. It's clean, on-brand, and product-forward, which is a relief after the denim-macro reuse flagged in the 08/23 "Sale Forecast" audit. But the subject line ("New Arrivals at New Better Prices") promises new arrivals as the headline story, and the email buries a much bigger number — "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" — three scroll-stops down, disconnected from any product shown above it.
- The structural problem: the grid of 9 items has zero labels, prices, or "new" badges — it's just photos of a jacket, pants, a coat, a cardigan, in no visible category order — followed by a generic "SHOP NOW" that doesn't obviously map to any one item. Then a sustainability blurb (nice, but mid-funnel real estate spent on brand values instead of conversion) sits between the new-arrivals grid and the actual best offer of the whole email.
- Net: pleasant to look at, weak to act on. The 70% off callout is the strongest thing in the email and it's positioned last.

## What's working

- Clean 3x3 product grid with consistent cream background and even cropping — no render bugs, no overlap, no broken images.
- Clear visual hierarchy at the top: logo → "New Arrivals" headline → grid → black "SHOP NOW" button.
- Color variety in the grid (olive, cream, black, navy) gives a sense of range without feeling cluttered.
- Footer nav (Shop Women / Shop Men / New Arrivals / Best-Sellers) gives an easy fallback path if the hero doesn't land.
- The 70% off sale block uses a distinct blue background that visually separates it as a second, distinct offer.

## What's weak

- None of the 9 grid products have a name, price, or link context — the visual is pretty but not shoppable at a glance.
- The subject line's "New Better Prices" doesn't appear anywhere as a stat, price cut, or comparison — it's an implied claim with no visible receipt.
- The best offer (70% off sale styles) is the fourth section down, after a sustainability CTA that competes for the same "READ MORE"/"SHOP" attention.
- Two black CTA buttons ("SHOP NOW" and "READ MORE") back-to-back trains the eye to skim past buttons rather than treat each as a distinct action.
- No indication of gender/category split in the grid — mixing womenswear and a look that reads more unisex/menswear without labeling forces guesswork.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the 70% off sale block, or at minimum move it directly under the hero grid — it's the strongest, most concrete offer in the email and currently gets the least prominent placement.
- 2. Add a one-line price or "NEW" tag under at least the top row of products so the grid becomes shoppable rather than purely decorative.
- 3. Cut or relocate the sustainability block to the footer — it's a values message, not a conversion driver, and it currently interrupts the path from browse to buy.
- 4. Swap "SHOP NOW" for a more specific CTA like "Shop New Arrivals" so it doesn't visually clone the "READ MORE" button below it.
- 5. If "New Better Prices" is the campaign line, show it — a strikethrough price or "now $X" badge on at least one hero item would make the subject's promise visible.
