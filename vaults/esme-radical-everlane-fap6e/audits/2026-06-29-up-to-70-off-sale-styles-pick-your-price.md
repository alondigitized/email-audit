# Up to 70% Off Sale Styles: Pick Your Price
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's sixth consecutive sale email in five days — and the creative lead is effectively a rerun of the June 26 "Up to 70% Off" send. The same white-on-hero sale callout, the same black SHOP NOW button, the same product category structure. The actual differentiator is buried in the middle: four pricepoint-segmented shop buttons (Under $50 / $100 / $150 / All Sale), which is the sharpest UX addition Everlane has shipped all week. It turns a generic clearance blast into a budget-based navigator.
- The execution is clean — no broken assets, clear hierarchy, on-brand type treatment. But the email has no urgency signal anywhere. No end date, no stock callout, nothing credible to push a fence-sitter into clicking today versus tomorrow. Six days into a clearance push with no deadline is leaving conversion on the table.
- The "Pick Your Price" framing in the subject was the right idea at the wrong time. Deployed earlier in the run — say, Day 2 or 3 — it would have opened a new chapter in the sale narrative. Here it reads as another lap around the same track.

## What's working

- **Pricepoint grid** — the four "Shop Under $X" buttons are genuinely useful; they reduce cognitive load for a budget-sensitive shopper who doesn't want to hunt.
- **Subject line angle** — "Pick Your Price" is the freshest framing in the entire five-day run; it reframes the same sale as a personalized shopping tool rather than a blanket markdown.
- **Hero clarity** — "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES / BY PRICEPOINT" lands the full offer in one scan, no ambiguity.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout issues, type is legible at mobile scale.

## What's weak

- **No urgency anywhere** — six emails in, still no end date, no "48 hours left," no stock signal. At this cadence, subscribers have no reason to act now vs. tomorrow.
- **Near-identical creative to June 26** — same white long-sleeve model, same layout; a subscriber who opened both will feel like they were re-sent the same email.
- **No product specificity** — no individual item is highlighted, no sizing or fit callout; everything routes to category pages, not product detail pages.
- **No social proof** — zero reviews, bestseller badges, or "X people bought this week" indicators during what should be the highest-conversion window of the run.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard close date to the hero and subject** — "Sale Ends Sunday" would do more lift than any creative change. Without a deadline, the clearance push has no pulse.
- 2. **Rotate the hero image** — a dress or denim shot signals new inventory, not a resend; the gray long-sleeve has now appeared across multiple sends.
- 3. **Pin one under-$50 product** — put a named item with a photo between the hero and the pricepoint grid to pull clicks below the fold.
- 4. **Throttle cadence** — a 48-hour pause before the next push lets urgency rebuild; the open-rate compression from daily sends is real and it's eroding the pricepoint grid's effectiveness.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale Ends Sunday: Up to 70% Off, Pick Your Budget`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$50. $100. $150. The sale closes Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses under $50. Jeans under $100. Tees for less. Clearance closes Sunday night.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your budget, your pick — shop by price before the sale wraps this weekend.`
