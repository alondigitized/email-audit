# 20% Off Denim
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Everlane sends a denim-focused variation of yesterday's welcome-discount email — same 20% offer, same "enter your email at checkout" mechanic, now with a three-fit education structure layered underneath. The fit guide (Barrel, Wide Leg, Straight) is genuinely useful content and is the strongest thing in the email. But framing it as another welcome-offer wrapper 24 hours after the first is the wrong move: it signals that the 20% isn't scarce, it doesn't escalate the story, and it asks the subscriber to act on an offer they've already been handed.
- The production is clean — Everlane's minimal aesthetic holds, hero loads above the fold, no layout breakage. The problem is strategic, not executional. This email was probably built from a template and deployed without asking whether the sequence made sense the morning after a near-identical send.
- The fit-education content buried in the middle is good enough to anchor its own standalone email. Instead, it's playing second fiddle to a welcome offer the subscriber has already processed.

## What's working

- Hero delivers the offer in one read: "20% Off the Best Denim" + SHOP NOW, above the fold.
- Three-fit format gives a real decision framework — Barrel, Wide Leg, Straight — rather than dumping product on the reader.
- Everlane's signature minimal layout is intact; generous white space, confident product photography, no visual noise.
- Subject line is only 13 characters — renders perfectly in every mobile inbox with no truncation.
- "Cleaner Fashion" section reinforces brand positioning without derailing the buying flow.

## What's weak

- Second "20% off" email in 24 hours with the same checkout mechanic collapses the urgency of both sends.
- Product description copy is visibly cut off mid-sentence in all three fit cards — reads as broken regardless of intent.
- "Enter your email at checkout" adds a manual step that a simple promo code would eliminate.
- No deadline anywhere in the email — zero reason to click today rather than "later" (which usually means never).
- SMS signup section sits mid-scroll and competes directly with the primary denim CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the double-send or add a hard deadline.** Either this email shouldn't have gone out (the welcome series already delivered the offer), or it needs to be reframed as a final notice — "Last 24 hours: 20% off denim" — with a timestamp. Right now it's a re-send dressed as a new send.
- 2. **Fix the cut-off fit descriptions.** The Barrel, Wide Leg, and Straight copy all trail off mid-sentence. Rewrite these as two tight, complete sentences each.
- 3. **Swap "enter email at checkout" for a promo code.** DENIM20 is one tap to copy, easier to track, and removes cognitive friction from the checkout path.
- 4. **Move SMS signup to the footer.** It is competing with the main call to action at exactly the wrong point in the scroll.
- 5. **Add a specific fit-linked CTA.** Instead of three generic "SHOP NOW" buttons, try "Shop the Barrel" → barrel PDP. Specificity drives clicks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 20% off denim ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Barrel. The Wide Leg. The Straight. All 20% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three fits, one discount. Find yours before the offer disappears.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use code DENIM20 at checkout — valid through Sunday.`
