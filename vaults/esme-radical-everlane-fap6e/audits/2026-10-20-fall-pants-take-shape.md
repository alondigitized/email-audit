# Fall Pants Take Shape
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a tidy, on-brand Everlane send built around one idea: fall pants in a tight tonal palette of brown, navy and burgundy. The hero is a model in brown corduroy-toned trousers with a navy sweater knotted over the shoulders, and a black SHOP NOW bar sits directly under it. Three product modules follow in a steady rhythm (image pair, name, one-line description, button). It is easy to scan and looks premium.
- The weak spot is the message. There's no offer, no price and no member benefit. The subject says "Fall Pants Take Shape," the hero overlay says "The Director's Cut," and the body copy says "From baggy to curve." Those are three different hooks. The overlay words are also spread edge to edge ("The ... Director's ... Cut"), so they read as three separate words rather than a headline. The impact-report block near the bottom is a sustainability message that has nothing to do with pants and works against the push to click.
- Compared with the 2026-10-19 "Flat Out Better" and 2026-10-20 "Cashmere for Every Forecast" sends, this follows the same template: editorial hero, black bar, three or four products, no offer. It's competent and forgettable, a solid 6. The "new better prices" news hook from the cashmere send would have lifted it.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. It has a clear subject, a cohesive palette and a full-width black SHOP NOW bar.
- Each of the three modules has a named product, a one-line benefit and its own button. "Baggy Barrel-Leg Jean," "Everywhere Pinwale Corduroy Pant" and "Balloon Pant in Pinwale Corduroy" give you something specific to click.
- The styling is consistent. The same browns, navy and burgundy, concrete backdrops and layout repeat in every module, so the email feels like a single story.
- The spacing is generous and the buttons are the same size and position each time. The email is short and easy to scroll.
- The product copy is short and confident: "Oversized. Lightweight. A statement maker for sure."

## What's weak

- There's no offer anywhere. No price, no percentage off, no free shipping and no member perk. The email gives you no reason to click now.
- The hero overlay "The Director's Cut" is spread across the full width with large gaps, so it reads awkwardly. It also doesn't match the subject line or the "From baggy to curve" intro.
- Every button says "SHOP NOW." The three product buttons don't name what they lead to, so they work as generic links.
- The grey impact-report block, with its recycling icon and "READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT" button, adds a second black button that competes with the product CTAs. It sits at the end of the product run, which is the wrong moment for a brand-values message.
- No prices or sizing cues are visible. The "baggy," "barrel" and "balloon" shapes are fit-sensitive, and nothing here helps a shopper decide.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete hook.** Use a price ("Corduroy pants from $98"), a fit or sizing note, or a member perk. Even "Free returns on every pair" would give the click a reason.
- 2. **Make the hero headline match the subject.** Use a tight overlay such as "Fall Pants, Reshaped," set as one phrase. The wide letter spacing should go. Keep the intro line short.
- 3. **Write specific button copy.** Examples are "Shop barrel jeans," "Shop corduroy pants" and "Shop balloon pants."
- 4. **Show price and fit cues under each product.** Add "$98 · Sizes 24–34" or a "Runs roomy, size down" note under the product name.
- 5. **Move the impact-report block below the footer links or cut it.** Don't let it compete with the product CTAs.
- 6. **Test a single-fabric focus.** Two of the three modules are pinwale corduroy, so a corduroy-only send with more colorways would be sharper.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three new shapes in pinwale corduroy and washed denim. Free returns.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Klaviyo/MJML-style email from `support@everlane.com`, sent through the `trk.send.everlane.com` click tracker. QA probing found 2 link failures, and both look like they come from how the tracker redirects. The compliance and authentication warnings can't be confirmed from the data provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Two tracked links resolved to a relative path.** The probe errored with `unknown url type` on the redirect target:
  - "Icon | Sign Up for Texts": `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M5D01BHCSTYP6159FHNWERPN_15` → `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…&bxid=…&cm_fl…`
  - "Everlane" (logo/header link): `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M5D01BHCSTYP6159FHNWERPN_0` → `/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=…&cm_flow_i…`
  - **Likely cause:** the tracker answers with a relative `Location` header (no scheme or host). Browsers resolve that against the tracker host, which would send users to `trk.send.everlane.com/pages/...` instead of everlane.com. The probe can't resolve it either.
  - **Unconfirmed:** the redirect response wasn't captured, so I can't tell a real misconfiguration from a probe limitation. It's worth a manual `curl -I` on both URLs. If the header is relative, the logo link and SMS signup are broken for real users.
- **Header link:** `_0` is the top-of-email logo link, so a failure there affects every recipient.
- **Probe coverage:** 12 of 25 links were probed, and the 13 skipped were footer or social. The reported 38% pass rate can't be reconciled with "2 issues" from the data given.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Tracking pixel has no `alt`:** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M5D01BHCSTYP6159FHNWERPN`. Low severity. Add `alt=""` so screen readers skip it.
- **Empty `<title>`:** the source has `<title> </title>`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** the source has `<html xmlns=… xmlns:o=… xmlns:v=…>` with no `lang` (and no `dir`). Screen readers can't select the right pronunciation.
- **Link styling:** `a:link, a:visited, a:hover { color:#000; text-decoration:none }` removes underlines from all links. Links are then distinguishable only by context, which fails WCAG 1.4.1 unless buttons or other cues carry it. I can't verify that from the truncated HTML.
- **Fonts:** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/…/custom_fonts.css)` is ignored by Gmail and Outlook. The font stacks fall back to Arial, so nothing breaks.
- **Dark mode:** `[data-ogsc]` rules are present, with an `ev-clay-light`/`ev-clay-dark` swap. No issues visible in the available source.
- **Not assessable:** image `alt` coverage and the Outlook/VML fallbacks, because the source is truncated.
