# More Dresses—Now Extra 20% Off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's second email on July 18 — "New Dresses Have Arrived" shipped earlier today — and it's also their second promotional email in three days, recycling the exact same "Extra 20% Off" mechanic from the July 16 "Extra 20% Off New Markdowns" send. The creative is competent: clean wordmark, lifestyle hero in Everlane's signature neutral palette, a six-product dress grid, and a promotional tile in muted olive. But the structural problem is hard to overlook: the subject is essentially "we just told you about dresses, and now they're on sale," which any subscriber opening two Everlane emails in one day will feel as noise, not value.
- The offer itself — extra 20% off styles already up to 70% off — is genuinely compelling if you're in the market. The stacked discount framing ("already up to 70%… now extra 20%") reads as a clearance push with real teeth. The "UNDER $100" promotional tile at the bottom adds a useful anchor for price-sensitive shoppers, but the "SHOP MEN" CTA sitting alongside it is incongruous in a dress-focused email and dilutes the message.
- Clear judgement: the email would be a 9 if it arrived on its own day with a fresh angle. As the second send on the same day, doubling down on the same category and layering a promotion over this morning's editorial, it reads as cadence misfire. Subscribers who opened "New Dresses Have Arrived" at 9am will feel sold to, not served, when this lands in the afternoon.

## What's working

- **Offer is concrete and prominent** — "EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES (ALREADY UP TO 70% OFF)" fills the hero in large type; no ambiguity about what the email is doing.
- **CTA is unambiguous** — "SHOP SALE" is a clean black button on white, immediately below the hero copy.
- **Six-dress product grid** fills the scroll well with variety across color and silhouette (yellow, white, burgundy, blush, camel), giving the subscriber multiple visual hooks.
- **"UNDER $100" tile** is a smart anchor — price point framing in a promotional email reduces friction at the decision stage.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text is legible, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Double-send on the same day** — "New Dresses Have Arrived" already went out July 18; sending a promotional follow-up the same day collapses the editorial/sale separation Everlane carefully built across July 7–15.
- **Promotional mechanic is already 48 hours stale** — "Extra 20% Off" launched on July 16; repeating it without escalation (different SKUs, new category, countdown) makes this feel like a reminder blast, not a new send.
- **Product grid has no prices or names** — six dress thumbnails with no copy beneath them; the images alone can't close a shopper who doesn't already know the product.
- **"SHOP MEN" CTA in a dress-focused email** — the olive tile breaks the gender-specific editorial lane that the rest of the email is committed to.
- **"Now Extra 20% Off" stacked with "already up to 70% off"** — the math is real but the layered phrasing is confusing on a quick skim; subscribers may assume it's the same sale they already saw.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold this until tomorrow morning** — the single highest-leverage change. If this email had arrived July 19 with the July 18 morning slot cleared for "New Dresses Have Arrived," open rate and click rate would both be higher. Two promotional-adjacent Everlane emails in one day is fatigue territory.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the offer** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" transforms "Extra 20% Off" from an indefinite markdown into an action trigger. No deadline = no urgency.
- 3. **Add prices to the product grid** — even a single line like "$68 → $54" per dress closes the loop between browse and buy without requiring a click.
- 4. **Remove the "SHOP MEN" CTA from the olive tile** — or send a segmented version to men's purchasers. The dress editorial and the men's link fight each other.
- 5. **Rewrite the hero subhead** to distinguish this from the July 16 send — e.g., "These are the ones that always sell out first" — so it doesn't read as a copy-paste of an existing promotion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dresses Under $100—Extra 20% Off Ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The dresses go first. Extra 20% off today.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six styles, all sale-priced and now an extra 20% off. These sell out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Under $100 after the extra discount—but not for long.`

```css

```
Gmail (all variants), Outlook, and Yahoo strip `@import` rules. "MaisonNeue-Light" / "Maison Neue" will silently fall back to Arial/Helvetica in the majority of clients — this is expected behavior, but the fallback stack should be confirmed to render acceptably, especially for the hero `h1` at 56px.

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {

}
```
10px is below the 14px floor recommended by WCAG and Apple/Google mobile HIG guidelines. Text at this size fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) in practice and will be illegible on standard DPI screens.
