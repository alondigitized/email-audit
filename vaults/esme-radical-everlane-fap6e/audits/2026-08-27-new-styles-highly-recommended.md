# New Styles, Highly Recommended
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine departure from Everlane's price-block week — no percentage-off headline, no urgency banner, just "New & Approved" and a social-proof format built entirely around customer quotes attached to four named products. It's a smart structural idea (real testimonials, named customers, specific product callouts) but the execution undercuts it: every quote reads suspiciously polished and none carry a star rating, verified-buyer badge, or photo, so it lands closer to marketing copy dressed as UGC than authentic proof.
- Visually it's clean and on-brand — consistent product photography, generous whitespace, a clear repeating pattern of image/name/price/quote/CTA — but there's no price incentive anywhere in this email, which after a week of 30%-off sends is a real gear-shift that risks feeling like a step down in value for anyone opening out of habit.
- Net: a well-built, well-photographed email with an interesting testimonial hook, but it asks a lot of trust in the quotes' authenticity and offers zero urgency or discount to convert a browser into a buyer today.

## What's working

- Four distinct products each get a full quote, name, and price — much more specific than a generic grid.
- Clean, consistent product photography against neutral backgrounds, all full-body shots showing fit.
- Repeating structure (photo → name/price → quote → attribution → Shop Now) is easy to scan and mobile-friendly.
- Sustainability callout mid-email ("we mean it") adds brand-value messaging without derailing the promo flow.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- No visible discount, price break, or urgency anywhere — a stark contrast to the last five sends this week.
- Quotes feel scripted/marketing-voiced ("Obsessed!!!", "so flattering!!") with no star ratings or verified-purchase marks to back credibility.
- Subject/hero copy ("New & Approved") is vague — doesn't name a category or specific product, unlike 08/25's strong single-product jacket send.
- Hero image text ("New & Approved") sits low-contrast over a photo — legible but not a strong headline moment.
- All four attributions are first-name-plus-initial with no photo or location — reads as unverifiable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a small "★★★★★ Verified Buyer" tag under each quote — this is the single biggest lift, since it converts the testimonial format from "trust us" to "trust her."
- 2. Give at least one hero product a limited-time or new-arrival specific hook (e.g., "back in stock," "selling out") since there's no discount driving urgency.
- 3. Sharpen the subject line to name the actual hook (denim jacket, customer reviews) rather than generic "New & Approved."
- 4. Tighten hero copy to reference the review angle directly, e.g., "Everyone's Talking About This Jacket" — ties subject, preview, and hero into one story.
