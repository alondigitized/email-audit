# Don’t Miss Your Exclusive Access
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a stripped-down, plain-text-style send built entirely around a "secret employee sale, 70% off" hook — and it's undercut immediately by a broken personalization token: "Hi There, ," with a dangling comma where a first name should be. There's not a single product image, price, or piece of visual merchandising anywhere in this email; it's copy and links on a white background from top to bottom.
- That's a real departure from the week's pattern — 8/16's "Best-Sellers Now Under $100" scored well specifically because it finally showed real prices and product shots, and 8/14's send worked because it displayed actual price tiers. This one reverses all of that progress: it's the "Better Prices, Still Everlane" beat stripped of every concrete signal (no product, no specific price, no visible dollar amount), replaced with a vaguer and honestly more suspicious "employee sale" framing and a 70% off claim with zero substantiation.
- The subject and "exclusive access" / "employee sale" angle is a strong curiosity hook on paper, but the execution here — broken personalization, no visuals, unverifiable 70% claim, "final sale" buried in body copy — reads more like a phishing-adjacent template than a flagship Everlane send. Judgment: weakest execution of the week, worse than 8/15's fatigued "Price Changed" send, because this one has an actual rendering bug in the customer-facing greeting.

## What's working

- The "employee sale" framing is a genuinely different curiosity hook than the six "Better Prices" sends this week — it doesn't repeat prior subject language.
- 70% off is a big, unambiguous number, and "No code needed" removes a common purchase-flow objection.
- Clean footer navigation (Shop Women/Men, New Arrivals, Best-Sellers) still gives multiple entry points despite the plain layout.
- Urgency line ("before the best finds are gone") plus a footer expiration date ("valid until 09/07/2026") gives a credible deadline.

## What's weak

- "Hi There, ," — visible broken personalization token, a stray comma where the recipient's name should render. This is a real, visible bug, not speculation.
- Zero product imagery or specific items shown — for an "exclusive employee sale," there's nothing to look at, no proof of what's actually on sale.
- No specific product, price, or category called out — just "best-sellers and other favorites," which is vague after a week of trending toward concrete pricing.
- "All items are final sale" sits in plain body text, not as protected fine print — a real friction point for a 70%-off claim shoppers will want to trust before clicking.
- Single CTA ("SHOP THE SALE") is just an underlined text link, not a designed button — weak visual hierarchy, easy to miss scrolling quickly.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the personalization token immediately — "Hi There, ," is a visible, embarrassing bug that will undermine trust in the "exclusive" framing before the reader gets to the offer.
- 2. Add at least one hero product image with a visible before/after price — this week's data shows real prices/products (8/16) outperform vague callouts (8/15).
- 3. Turn "SHOP THE SALE" into a designed button, not an underlined text link, to anchor visual hierarchy on mobile.
- 4. Move "All items are final sale" out of casual body copy into the disclosure footer where it belongs, so it doesn't read as bait-and-switch mid-pitch.
- 5. Replace "70% off and more" with a specific example ("Up to 70% off, including the $XX cotton crew") to make the employee-sale claim concrete and believable.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Email is built on Klaviyo's MJML-based template (static-forms.klaviyo.com fonts, kl- class conventions) with trk.send.everlane.com link/open tracking. Two tracked CTA links resolve to malformed relative-path redirect targets, and standard compliance/authentication headers are not present in the payload provided.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **"Shop Men" CTA** — tracking wrapper `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M0DBV61BJ9Z7AQ77NBQ8B2HY_3` resolves to `/collections/shop-all-mens-clothing?_kx=...&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCK`, a relative path rather than an absolute URL. QA flagged: "unknown url type."
- **"Everlane Logo" link** — tracking wrapper `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M0DBV61BJ9Z7AQ77NBQ8B2HY_0` resolves to `/pages/womens?_kx=...&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V&cm_flow_i` (query string also appears truncated at `cm_flow_i`), again a relative path.
- Both broken links use Klaviyo `_kx`/`bxid` tracking params correctly appended, but the redirect target is missing its scheme/host — this will break the link in any client that doesn't silently resolve relative-to-tracking-domain, and is invalid per RFC 3986 for an email redirect target.
- Open tracking pixel present: `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M0DBV61BJ9Z7AQ77NBQ8B2HY`.
- 12 material links were probed, 5 skipped by design (footer/utility/social deprioritized) — not a defect, informational only.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- MSO conditional comments and Outlook-specific fixes (`mj-outlook-group-fix`, `o:OfficeDocumentSettings`) are present, indicating standard Outlook rendering support.
- Responsive breakpoints defined via `@media (max-width: 480px)` for headings, tables, and column stacking — consistent MJML/Klaviyo output.
- No other alt-text or markup issues visible in the truncated source provided.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the redirect configuration for tracking links `..._3` and `..._0` so they resolve to fully-qualified URLs (e.g., `https://www.everlane.com/collections/shop-all-mens-clothing?...`) instead of scheme-relative/relative paths.
