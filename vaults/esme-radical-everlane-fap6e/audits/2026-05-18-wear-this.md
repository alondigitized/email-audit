# Wear This
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure editorial email — no discount, no promotion, just an introduction to what I'm inferring is the "Open Collar Polo" wrapped in a "Summer Edit" campaign frame. The visual language is classic Everlane: yellow jacket, cream dress, black sandals, clean white space. After two consecutive 20%-off emails on May 16 and May 17, the absence of any offer will feel like a gear-shift for subscribers who opened those expecting the same.
- The email earns its keep on aesthetics and brand trust. What it cannot do is generate urgency. There's no discount, no deadline, no scarcity signal, and no product detail beyond a model shot and three tiny tiles. "Wear This" as a subject line is one of the laziest choices in the catalog — it tells you nothing, creates no curiosity, and names no product.
- If this is a product launch for the Open Collar Polo, the email under-serves it. If it's just a seasonal editorial push, it lacks the specificity to compete for clicks.

## What's working

- Hero image is immediately seasonal — yellow jacket over cream slip dress and black sandals codes as summer editorial without trying too hard.
- "SHOP THE SUMMER EDIT" CTA is a notch more specific than "Shop Now" and clearly directs to a curated destination.
- "Open Collar Polo" is named in body copy, anchoring the email to a real product rather than pure vibe.
- Sustainability block lands naturally mid-email and reinforces the brand without derailing the layout.
- No render issues anywhere — clean, correct render from top to bottom.

## What's weak

- "Wear This" is essentially no subject line — it conveys no product, no offer, no season, no curiosity hook.
- Zero offer after two consecutive 20%-off emails creates a tonal whiplash; subscribers may feel baited.
- The three product tiles (yellow jacket, dress, sandals) are too small to actually evaluate the product — a straight-on product shot of the Polo is absent above the fold.
- "Post it more than once. Wear it even more." is trying to land a brand-voice moment but the second line is a tautology — it doesn't tell you *why* you'd rewear it.
- Third send in three days; cadence fatigue is a real unsubscribe driver here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the product in the subject.** "Wear This" squanders a product launch. The Open Collar Polo is nameable and differentiable — use it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Open Collar Polo Is Here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet the Polo You'll Actually Rewear`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer's most wearable new piece — shop the edit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One polo. Every summer situation.`
- 2. **Add a product-direct CTA.** "Shop the Open Collar Polo" directly below the hero beats "Shop Now." Send people to the product page, not the homepage summer grid.
- 3. **Commit to the hero product.** Drop the three small tiles or replace them with three colorway shots of the Polo itself. Right now the email can't decide if it's selling one thing or three.
- 4. **Insert a newness or scarcity signal.** No discount is fine — but "Just dropped" or "New summer colors, limited run" gives a reason to click *today* instead of *eventually*.
- 5. **Extend the send cadence.** Three emails in three days from the same brand, each with diminishing distinctiveness, is a fast path to unsubscribes. This email would perform better on Wednesday after a rest day.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for custom fonts will silently fail in Gmail and most webmail clients.**

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```

Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and Outlook 365 (web) strip `@import` directives entirely. The declared `"MaisonNeue-Light"` / `"Maison Neue"` fonts will never load in these clients; fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) renders instead. This is expected behavior for email, but should be confirmed intentional — if brand consistency requires the custom font, it needs to be referenced via `<link>` in the `<head>` (supported in Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Samsung Mail) rather than `@import`.

**`<html>` tag is missing a `lang` attribute.**

```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```

No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers default to the OS language rather than the document language. Fix: `<html lang="en" xmlns=...>`.

**`<title>` is effectively empty.**

```html
<title> </title>
```

Some email clients surface the `<title>` in tab labels or accessibility trees. Should be `<title>Wear This — Everlane</title>` or omitted entirely if blank is intentional.

**`h4` mobile font-size drops to 10px.**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```

10px is below the WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 practical minimum (~14px for body-weight text). If `h4` is used for product subcopy or pricing, this will fail legibility on mobile.

**All anchor states are styled black with no underline.**

```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```

Links are visually indistinguishable from surrounding body text in all states. This is a common email pattern for image-based layouts but becomes an accessibility problem if any plain-text links appear in the footer (unsubscribe, legal). Verify footer links have sufficient contrast or explicit underline treatment.
