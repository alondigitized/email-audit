# New Markdowns, Up to 70% Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's post-Memorial Day pivot — the correct beat after closing the seven-day 30% sequence yesterday with a hard "Ends Tonight" deadline. The offer escalation is real: 30% sitewide → up to 70% off markdowns is a meaningful signal, and the subject nails it. In a crowded post-holiday inbox, a subscriber who watched the entire Memorial Day cadence will notice the step-up.
- The execution is nearly empty. The email is functionally a text postcard: five short lines, no product imagery, no hero, no CTA button. The strongest claim in the email — "up to 70% off" — is buried in the third paragraph behind a generic brand tagline, hyperlinked like any other inline text. Nothing stops the eye. A reader skimming on mobile has no visual reason to engage.
- The strategy is sound. The production is a collapse. After a Memorial Day sequence that clearly did the work visually, this reads like a placeholder draft that shipped by accident. Clearance emails with 70% depth deserve product photography and a styled CTA — not a postcard.

## What's working

- Subject is the best in the sequence: "New Markdowns, Up to 70% Off" is under 30 characters, concrete, and signals a meaningful escalation that makes yesterday's send feel like prologue
- Post-sale timing is correct — this is the right cadence beat after a hard close, and subscribers who've been watching will recognize the shift
- Categories are named in body copy (dresses, tees, pants) — even as inline links they give the reader a direction
- Clean render — no bugs, no broken elements, correct logo and footer throughout
- "Find what you love before it's gone" provides soft scarcity without being dishonest

## What's weak

- No product imagery at all — an email announcing up to 70% off markdowns should show markdown product; blank white space sells nothing
- The hero claim ("up to 70% off") is buried in paragraph three, after a generic tagline that delays the offer; it should be the first line the reader sees
- No CTA button — the only clickable paths are four underlined inline words and the footer nav, which is visually indistinguishable from boilerplate
- "Hey there," is unforgivably generic after a seven-day segmented sale sequence — even a gender split would sharpen this
- "A better wardrobe starts here" is evergreen homepage filler that belongs nowhere near a sale announcement; it actively delays the offer

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hero product image with before/after pricing** — two or three items (a dress at $110 → $49, a tee at $55 → $22) make 70% off visceral; body text cannot
- 2. **Delete "A better wardrobe starts here" and lead with the offer** — the subject already primed the reader; the first sentence should be the markdown announcement, not a brand slogan
- 3. **Add a styled CTA button** — "Shop Markdowns" in Everlane's standard black-fill button, immediately below the body copy, before the footer nav
- 4. **Set a harder deadline** — "before they're gone" is too soft; a specific end date or "while sizes last on these styles" would add credible urgency
- 5. **Segment by gender or category** — even a binary split (dresses/sandals flow vs. tees/pants flow) would lift click-through significantly over a generic blast

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `@import` inside `<style>` (font loading failure in Gmail/Outlook Web):**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules entirely. The custom "Maison Neue" brand font will silently fall back to `Arial` for all Gmail recipients (typically the largest segment). This is expected behavior for web fonts in email, but the team should verify the Arial fallback renders acceptably — particularly for headline type which is set at `56px / font-weight: 300`.

**Issue — All link states set to `color:#000; text-decoration:none`:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links are visually indistinguishable from body text in all states, including hover. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) if color alone is the only differentiator. Keyboard and screen-reader users relying on visual focus indicators have no affordance.

**Issue — Inconsistent mobile breakpoints (480px vs. 600px):**
- Column stacking and primary layout logic fires at `max-width: 480px`
- `.hide` visibility class fires at `max-width: 600px`

Devices rendering at 481px–600px (e.g., many Android phones in landscape, small tablets) will receive the `.hide` suppression but not the column restack — producing a hybrid layout state that is untested.

**Minor — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Non-blocking. Some clients (notably Apple Mail) surface the `<title>` as a preview tooltip; leaving it blank is a missed opportunity but not a defect.
