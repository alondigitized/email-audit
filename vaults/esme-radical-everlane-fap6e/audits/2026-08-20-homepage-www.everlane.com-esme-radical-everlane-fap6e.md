# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-08-20
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- I open the app to buy more of the actual product — Everlane's whole pitch is "here's what it costs us, here's what we charge you" — and instead I get a full-screen email trap asking me to unlock 20% off my *first* order. I already have five pairs of the Way-High. This isn't talking to me, it's talking to someone who's never heard of the brand.

## What's working

- The top ribbon at least names the category I care about — "30% OFF* DENIM, PANTS & MORE" — so if I could get past the wall, denim is clearly in play.
- The sub-nav underneath (NEW, CULT FAVORITES, CLOTHING, DENIM, SHOES, SALE) is legible and organized — I know exactly where I'd tap once I'm let in.
- The modal itself is clean, on-brand typography, single clear CTA button ("Unlock 20% off") — no clutter, no five competing buttons.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage — hero, imagery, everything — is blocked by a "Get 20% off your first order" popup. I can't even see what they're pushing this week.
- That offer is for **first orders only**. I've bought five pairs of jeans here. There's zero acknowledgment I'm a repeat customer — no "welcome back," no account recognition, nothing.
- There's a cookie consent banner stacked at the bottom *underneath* the email modal, so I'm looking at two separate popups asking for something before I've seen a single product.
- The 30% off banner has an asterisk with no visible fine print — that's exactly the kind of buried-exclusion move that makes me distrust a "transparency" brand.
- No cost breakdown, no factory story, no "true cost" messaging anywhere on screen — which is Everlane's entire differentiator and it's nowhere to be found.

## Recommendations

- Detect logged-in / returning customers and suppress the first-order modal entirely — show a "welcome back, here's what's new in Denim" module instead.
- Swap the blind "20% off first order" popup for something that surfaces an actual cost-breakdown teaser (factory cost vs. retail) behind the glass, so even people who dismiss it see the brand story for half a second.
- Put the fine-print terms for the "30% OFF*" banner one tap away, not hidden — a transparency brand can't have an unexplained asterisk on its own homepage.
- Stack only one popup at a time — combine the cookie notice into the same modal flow instead of layering two asks.
