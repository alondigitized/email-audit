# The Softest Sweaters Are Here
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand Everlane send, but it's a near repeat of the 2026-10-16 "Air Alpaca Is Back" email. The hero is the same blush sheer sweater with "Back In Air Alpaca" overlaid and a black SHOP AIR ALPACA bar under it. Only the subject line is new. A subscriber who got both will read this as the same message twice in 24 hours.
- The product edit is good: four alpaca pieces (burgundy slim crew, peach cropped sweater tee, taupe wide-V cardigan, blush wrap top), each with a one-line benefit and a SHOP NOW link. The offer is the weak part. "Extra 20% off markdowns" sits in a brown block near the bottom, after the RWS/alpaca sourcing section. That's the only price incentive, and it doesn't apply to the new alpaca styles above it.
- Verdict: tidy and on-season, but a repeat with a buried offer. It's a mid-pack send, around a 5.

## What's working

- The hero is a strong, tactile image of the sweater, with a clear black SHOP AIR ALPACA bar directly under it.
- The tagline "Lightweight. Airy. Better sweaters in our softest alpaca blend." is short and matches the subject.
- The four-product list is easy to scan, with consistent image, one-liner and link per row.
- The "SHOP ALL SWEATERS" button gives a clear next step after the products.
- The sourcing block (RWS and Responsible Alpaca Standard) is a good brand-trust moment.

## What's weak

- It's nearly a duplicate of the 2026-10-16 "Air Alpaca Is Back" send, with the same hero and the same message.
- The 20% off markdowns offer is at the bottom, below the sourcing block. Most readers will never see it.
- The product rows use small, light-grey "SHOP NOW" text links. They're weak as calls to action, and no prices are shown.
- The product images sit in a grey column on the left with a lot of empty white space on the right. That makes the email very long for four items.
- The sourcing block takes up a lot of space for a secondary message, and "READ MORE" competes with the shopping buttons.

## Recommendations

- 1. Don't send a second Air Alpaca email within a day. Use this one as a product-detail follow-up with new creative, such as a close-up of the fabric or a styled look, and different angles from the launch send.
- 2. Move the 20% off markdowns banner up, directly under the hero or product grid. If it doesn't apply to the alpaca styles, say so plainly.
- 3. Add prices, or a "from $X" line, to each product, and make the CTAs buttons rather than small underlined links.
- 4. Tighten the product grid by filling the empty right-hand space, and show colour swatches or a "new colours" tag.
- 5. Move the sourcing block below the offer, or shrink it to a single line.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect target on the "Everlane" link.**

  - The tracker appears to return a path-only `Location` header (`/pages/womens?...`) with no scheme or host.
  - Browsers and mail clients resolve this against the tracking host, so real users likely land fine. The probe's HTTP client can't resolve it.
  - This is unconfirmed as a user-facing break. If the tracking host doesn't serve `/pages/womens`, the redirect would 404, so verify it manually.
- **Probe coverage is partial.** Only 12 of 31 links were probed and 19 were skipped as footer, utility or social links. Those 19 are unverified, so the "43% pass rate" reflects the probed subset.
- The tracking URL carries `_kx`, `bxid` and `cm_flow_i…` parameters (Klaviyo and Bluecore-style identifiers). They are long, but nothing is malformed. The QA output truncates the value, so I can't check the full string.
