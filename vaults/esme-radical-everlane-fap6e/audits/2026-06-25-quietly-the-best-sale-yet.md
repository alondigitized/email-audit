# Quietly the Best Sale Yet
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the email Everlane's ten-day editorial streak has been building toward — and it's genuinely the strongest send in the run. After nine consecutive category stories with zero pricing, "Quietly the Best Sale Yet" finally drops a hard number in the hero: **UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES**. The formula pivot is overdue and earns it a much higher business impact than any of the recent editorial sends.
- That said, the conversion layer is still half-baked. The primary CTA is the same "SHOP NOW" seen in every prior email, there's no sale deadline anywhere in the visible frame, and the body copy ("All you need for the season is here and marked down. It doesn't get better than this") is forgettable. The category-specific sub-CTAs — SHOP SALE DRESSES, SHOP SALE LINEN, SHOP SALE TOPS, SHOP ALL SALE — are a genuine structural improvement over the usual single button, but they're doing the heavy lifting the hero copy should have handled.
- The subject line "Quietly the Best Sale Yet" is charming and distinctive — it's not screaming like a typical sale blast — but it's all style and no substance. No percentage, no category, no deadline. A subscriber who has been receiving daily Everlane emails for ten days in a row and is showing inbox fatigue may scroll right past it. This is a good email that should have been a great one.

## What's working

- **First concrete offer in the streak.** "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" in the hero is more useful signal than anything in the previous eight editorial sends.
- **Category sub-CTAs enable self-selection.** SHOP SALE DRESSES / SHOP SALE LINEN / SHOP SALE TOPS / SHOP ALL SALE let the reader jump to the category they care about — a real usability upgrade.
- **Subject line voice is distinctive.** "Quietly the Best Sale Yet" avoids the ALL-CAPS, exclamation-point spam traps and has a confident, understated tone that reads well in a crowded inbox.
- **Visual hierarchy is clear.** Hero image → bold headline overlay → black CTA button → category grid. Eye lands exactly where it should.
- **Seasonal timing lands.** Summer Clearout in late June is precisely right; this doesn't feel stale.

## What's weak

- **No sale deadline anywhere.** "UP TO 70% OFF" with no end date or countdown removes the only urgency lever that would drive same-session clicks.
- **Primary CTA is still "SHOP NOW."** After ten sends with identical button copy, this is invisible. It promises nothing specific.
- **Body copy is filler.** "It doesn't get better than this" is a subjective claim that a subscriber has no reason to believe after receiving the same template for 10+ days.
- **Cadence is punishing.** Eleven consecutive daily emails from the same sender, with only structural variation this late in the run, has likely gutted open rates for the streak.
- **No featured product or price anchor.** A single "was $148, now $52" callout would make the "70% off" claim feel real instead of aspirational.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a sale end date in the hero.** "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE — ENDS SUNDAY" would convert far more opens into clicks than any copy tweak. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with the actual offer.** "Shop Up to 70% Off" or "See All Sale Styles" is still two words and vastly more specific.
- 3. **Add one featured price anchor.** Pull the best-performing sale item — name it, show original vs. sale price — directly under the hero CTA. "The Relaxed Jean: was $98, now $29" is real.
- 4. **Body copy rewrite.** "Dresses, linen, tops — all marked down. The clearout won't last long" is shorter, more honest, and creates mild urgency.
- 5. **Slow the cadence.** A send frequency of 3-4x/week would make this email feel like an event rather than noise. At 11 consecutive days, even a 70%-off sale risks being ignored.

- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off. Our real best sale yet.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, linen, tops — the ones you've been watching, now marked down.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clearout is live. Ends Sunday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace. Screen readers and some email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook) surface this as the email title. Should be populated with the subject line or a descriptive equivalent.

- **`@import` for custom fonts will silently fail in Gmail and Outlook** — the `<style>` block uses `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)`. Gmail strips all `@import` rules; Outlook ignores them entirely. The fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) is acceptable, but "MaisonNeue-Light" at `font-weight: 300` will render as regular Arial weight in those clients — a noticeable visual shift for the `h1`.

- **`h4` mobile font-size is 10px** — explicit `@media (max-width: 480px)` rule sets `h4 { font-size: 10px !important }`. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 and general email accessibility guidelines treat sub-12px text as a failure for readability; 10px on mobile is below any accepted threshold.

- **All link states have `color:#000; text-decoration:none` including `:hover`** — the CSS block at the top of `<head>` removes all visual affordance from links in every state:
  ```css
  a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:active  {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  ```
  Links are visually indistinguishable from body text and provide no hover feedback. This is a WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color) concern and degrades usability for sighted users not hovering over obvious CTAs.

- **Duplicate `img` CSS rule** — `img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; ... }` is declared twice across the two `<style>` blocks. Not a rendering defect (second declaration wins), but indicates a template assembly issue worth cleaning up.

- **No `lang` attribute on `<html>`** — `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">` is missing `lang="en"`. Screen readers default to OS language; missing `lang` causes incorrect pronunciation for non-default-language clients.
