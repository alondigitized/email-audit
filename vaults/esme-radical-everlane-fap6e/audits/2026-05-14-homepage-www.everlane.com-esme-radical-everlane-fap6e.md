# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I opened Everlane expecting the cost-breakdown story I know them for, and instead got a full-bleed lifestyle shot of terracotta tiles, a cream knit, and someone's sandal-clad feet — zero words, zero numbers, zero navigation in frame. This looks like a brand that has fully pivoted to aesthetic-over-transparency, which is either a bold minimalist move or a sign they've forgotten who built them. Right now they're pushing whatever this earthy, texture-forward summer-sandals moment is — but I genuinely cannot tell from this screen.

## What's working

- **Clean render / no layout bugs** — the image fills the viewport correctly, no artifacts, no broken elements. At least the technical baseline is solid.
- **Earthy aesthetic is on-brand** — terracotta + cream + textured knit is distinctly Everlane's current visual language. I recognize the brand immediately even without a logo in frame.
- **Sandal product hint** — as someone who buys Everlane footwear I can see there's a shoe play happening, which is at minimum in the right product family for me.

## What's weak

- **No copy whatsoever** — I am Esme Radical. I opened this page to see a headline, a cost story, something. There is literally nothing to read. Everlane built its entire identity on "this is what it costs to make this" and the hero has zero words.
- **No CTA visible** — not a single tappable element I can see. If I didn't already know the site I would not know what to do next.
- **No navigation in frame** — the burger menu or top nav, if it exists, is cut off or invisible. I can't reach The Way-High Jeans in one tap from this screen.
- **Product is ambiguous** — a partial sandal and what might be a knit sweater. Is this a sandals launch? A summer knitwear story? I cannot tell, and that ambiguity wastes my first three seconds.
- **No price, no markup, no numbers** — the one thing that makes Everlane Everlane is completely absent from the visible screen. This could be any DTC brand.

## Recommendations

- **Put a headline on the hero.** Even one line: "New Sandals. $XX. Here's why." costs nothing to add and does everything for a returning customer who came for the numbers story.
- **Surface a nav strip or category pill row** below the hero image so women's, denim, shoes are one tap away — right now I have no wayfinding at all.
- **Add a transparency proof-point** — a small badge or sub-head with the cost breakdown ("Made for $38. Sold for $98.") pulled from Everlane's own radical-transparency playbook. That is the reason I am here.
- **Overlay a CTA button** on the hero — verbatim copy like "Shop New Sandals" or "Shop Women's" — white text, pill button, bottom of the image. It's a five-minute change.
