# The Perfect Long-Sleeve Tee
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine break from the price-block week Everlane's been running — a single-product hero (the boxy long-sleeve tee, styled with a red sweater tied at the waist), a clean 3-color swatch grid, and a soft "Better Prices, Still Everlane" reassurance banner. It's the most product-forward, editorial-feeling send in the recent run, closer to 08/23's new-arrivals grid than to the mint-teal denim banners.
- The problem: there's no discount anywhere in this email. "Better Prices, Still Everlane" gestures at value without a number, and the organic-cotton messaging is a values pitch, not an offer. After a week of hard "%OFF" banners, a subscriber opening this expecting the next deal finds soft brand copy instead — that's a real tonal whiplash risk, even if the creative itself is well executed.
- Judgement: strong creative, weak on the thing that actually drives clicks. This reads like a mid-week brand/product email, not a conversion push — fine in isolation, but it undercuts urgency built up by five days of denim discounting.

## What's working

- Single hero product shot is styled and specific — the tied red sweater adds visual interest without cluttering the tee itself.
- Copy under the hero is concrete: "long-sleeve boxy tee," "a little more weight," "organic cotton" — real product detail, not vague lifestyle copy.
- Three-color swatch grid (white, stripe, black) lets the shopper see fit/options before clicking.
- Sustainability block (organic cotton icon + explanation) is on-brand and adds credibility without over-claiming.
- No render bugs — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images.

## What's weak

- No visible discount, price, or dollar/percent-off anywhere in the email — a hard miss on the Business Impact and Click rubrics given this brand's cadence has been discount-led all week.
- "BETTER PRICES, STILL EVERLANE" is vague value messaging with no number attached — reads like it's dodging a real price commitment.
- Two competing CTAs stacked close together ("Shop All Tees" black bar, then "Shop Now" underlined text) dilute the primary action.
- No urgency signal (no deadline, no "final hours," nothing time-bound) — unusual given the brand has leaned hard on urgency copy in prior sends this week.
- Model is styled in all-black bottoms + red sweater tied at waist — visually strong, but zero size/fit callouts (no "true to size," no model height/size worn) despite this being a fit-forward "boxy" claim.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real number to the value banner — "Better Prices, Still Everlane" should become something like "$10 Off Everyday Basics" or state the new lower price directly next to the tee. Vague reassurance without a figure will underperform every discount send this week.
- 2. Collapse to one primary CTA — drop either "Shop All Tees" or the underlined "Shop Now," keep the black bar as the single action.
- 3. Add a fit note near the swatch grid ("Model is 5'9", wearing size S") — this is a boxy-fit tee, and fit uncertainty is exactly what stalls a click on apparel.
- 4. If there truly is no discount this cycle, lean harder into "new/restocked" framing in the subject and hero rather than implying a price story with no number — don't let copy promise a deal it doesn't deliver.
