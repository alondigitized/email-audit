# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Everlane served me a homepage that, in this viewport capture, is essentially just a tightly cropped lifestyle image — warm ochre knitwear against a terracotta backdrop, no visible headline, no visible logo, no CTA, nothing. If the brand is trying to signal quiet luxury and restraint, sure, mission accomplished. But I came here as someone who opens every Everlane email for a cost-breakdown story, and there is nothing here to grab onto — no number, no product name, no transparency hook. From what I can see, this homepage is doing atmosphere, not commerce.

## What's working

- **Texture-forward art direction** — the close crop on what reads as a knit or fleece in oat/ecru against a burnt-orange surface is genuinely beautiful. Everlane's fabric-first identity comes through without a single word.
- **No noise above the fold** — there's no pop-up, no cookie banner overlapping content, no promo-ticker screaming at me. The page loads into a single clean frame.

## What's weak

- **No headline, no copy, no brand ID** — I cannot read a single word in this screenshot. For a radical-transparency brand, that is a catastrophic miss: Everlane's whole equity is the story behind the price, and there's no story here.
- **No CTA of any kind** — not a button, not a text link, not a "shop now" ghost button. If I arrived from a paid ad or a newsletter, I have nowhere to go.
- **No product identification** — the image could be a sweater, a blanket, a tote. I genuinely cannot tell. Everlane's customer buys on specificity (Peru fleece, 100% Mongolian cashmere, "here's the factory"). An anonymous texture shot is the opposite of that.
- **No loyalty / account hook** — I have five pairs of Way-High Jeans. There is zero acknowledgment that I exist as a returning customer.
- **No offer or transparency callout** — the cost-breakdown story that made Everlane famous is entirely invisible here.

## Recommendations

- **Overlay the hero with a price-transparency callout** — even a single line like "The Organic Cotton Knit — $58. Here's the math." turns an atmospheric shot into an Everlane-specific moment.
- **Add a sticky top nav with account/loyalty entry point** — returning customers should see their status in under one tap; a persistent header with "Hi Esme · Member" or even a simple account icon fixes the no-hook problem immediately.
- **Name the product in the frame** — a small product label or text overlay (product name + material + price) anchors the visual and eliminates ambiguity without breaking the editorial aesthetic.
- **Add one visible CTA button** — "Shop New Arrivals" or "See the Cost Breakdown" in a muted, on-brand type treatment. It doesn't have to be a loud button, but it has to exist.
