# Keep the Fleece
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-built fleece product send with real prices: $68 crew, $78 wide-leg, $68 jogger and $88 hoodie. The hero is a model in forest-green fleece with a "Shop Now" bar. A four-item zigzag grid follows. Every item has a price and a single-line benefit, and every row has its own link.
- The weakness is the pitch. The subject "Keep the Fleece" is clever but vague, and nothing in the body says what the price is relative to. "Now $68" implies a markdown, but there's no was-price and no percent off. The closing block, "Better prices, still Everlane," states the idea without any proof. After the prior sends this week ($68 Cashmere Slim Crew on 11-11, "Two Fits. One Better Price." on 11-09), the "better prices" line starts to feel like a template. The hero overlay "A Fleece Offering" is thin, small and sits on the model's torso without much contrast.
- Judgement: a competent, seasonal, shoppable send that's easy to scan. It has no urgency, no social proof and no clear reason to act today. A solid 6.

## What's working

- Every product has a visible price, so a reader can scan and decide without clicking.
- The alternating image/text grid is easy to scan, and the tight color story of forest green and burgundy looks cohesive.
- The copy is short and in-voice. "Joy of missing out" (JOMO) is a timely, fitting hook, and each product gets one benefit line.
- The hero is image-led with a full-width black CTA bar that's hard to miss.
- Each product row has its own "Shop Now" link, so the click path is short.

## What's weak

- "Now $68" has no was-price, percent off or end date, so the discount is implied rather than shown.
- Every CTA says "Shop Now", including the four product links and the closing block. None names the product.
- The hero overlay "A Fleece Offering" is small, thin and low-contrast against the grey backdrop.
- There are no reviews or ratings. Everlane's 11-11 "Styles Everyone Wants" send used customer quotes, and they would help here.
- "Better Prices, Still Everlane" is a generic closer. It doesn't say what changed or why the prices are better.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the price story explicit. Show the was-price struck through ("$68, was $98"), or name the savings in the hero ("Luxe Fleece from $68"). This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Add a deadline or scarcity cue, such as "Through Sunday" or "Selling fast in Forest."
- 3. Replace the repeated "Shop Now" with product-specific CTAs: "Get the Crew", "Shop Joggers".
- 4. Add one customer quote or star rating per product, as in the 11-11 review-driven send.
- 5. Make the hero headline larger and put the price in it: "Luxe Fleece, from $68."
- 6. Swap the closing block for proof, such as a fabric detail, a return policy or a rating count.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect target, "Everlane" logo link.** Tracking URL `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M78AV9BVXMT4QR3M6WATC2WT_0` resolved to `/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V&cm_flow_i…`. The probe errored with `unknown url type`.
- **[FAIL] Relative redirect target, "Icon | Sign Up for Texts".** Tracking URL `…/l/01M78AV9BVXMT4QR3M6WATC2WT_18` resolved to `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…`. It fails the same way.
- **Likely cause (not confirmed).** Either the tracker returns a relative `Location:` header, or the original `href` was a site-relative path that the Klaviyo wrapper rewrote without a host. A relative `Location` is legal under RFC 7231, but some clients and scanners handle it poorly. Verify with `curl -I` on both tracking URLs. If the header really is relative, it is a real defect, not a probe artifact.
