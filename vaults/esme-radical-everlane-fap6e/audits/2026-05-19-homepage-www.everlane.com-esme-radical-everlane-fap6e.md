# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened Everlane and what I got was an extreme close-up of what looks like a beige shoe or sandal, shot against warm terracotta and mustard tones. No headline, no nav, no CTA — just a product macro that fills the entire viewport. As Esme who runs the cost-breakdown math in her head before she scrolls, I'm immediately at a disadvantage: I can't tell what the product is, what it costs, or why Everlane thinks I should care right now.

## What's working

- **Color palette cohesion** — the mustard, terracotta, and beige neutrals read as a deliberate seasonal palette, which is on-brand for Everlane's earth-toned aesthetic.
- **Image quality** — the close-up shot is crisp and tactile; the material texture of whatever this product is reads well at high resolution.

## What's weak

- **Zero legible copy** — there is no headline, no subhead, no product name, no price. As someone who reads every email specifically for the cost breakdown story, I'm being asked to feel something before I know anything. That is the opposite of radical transparency.
- **No navigation visible** — I can't tap Women's, Denim, or anything. I'm stuck in an artistic void with no exit ramp that I can see.
- **No CTA whatsoever** — no "Shop Now," no "See the story," not even a tiny product name chip. For a brand whose whole identity is "we'll show you the math," hiding the product identity is a self-own.
- **Product is not identifiable** — I genuinely cannot tell if this is a sandal, a clog, a mule, or something else. The crop is so tight it eliminates the silhouette that would let me categorize and care.
- **No price or transparency hook** — Everlane built its entire brand on showing me cost + markup. None of that signal exists in this viewport. This could be any DTC brand.

## Recommendations

- **Show the product name and price above the fold** — even a small text overlay ("The Boss Boot, $178 — here's the breakdown →") would immediately activate my reason for being an Everlane customer vs. bouncing.
- **Pull back the crop to show the full product silhouette** — a shoe hero that's zoomed to abstraction defeats the purpose of showing me what I might buy.
- **Add a one-line transparency hook in the hero** — something like "Cotton + labor + markup = $X. We'll show you." is the brand's superpower; use it above the fold.
- **Surface a nav strip or at minimum one tappable category chip** — even a sticky top bar with "New / Women / Denim / Sale" would let me self-direct immediately.
