# Introducing the Organic Cotton Twill Field Jacket
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's best send of the week, and it's not close. After five straight days of the same mint-teal "30% OFF DENIM, PANTS & MORE" price-block creative recycled across five subject lines (08/20–08/24), this email finally breaks the loop with a genuine new-arrival launch: a clean hero shot of the Organic Cotton Twill Field Jacket, a 3-colorway product grid, a lifestyle close-up, and a sustainability callout for the organic cotton sourcing.
- The execution is tight — no render bugs, clear black "SHOP NOW" CTA on the hero, logical scroll order (hero → colorways → detail shot → material story → secondary sale banner → nav footer). The tradeoff is that this is a full-price product-introduction email with no offer on the jacket itself; the "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" banner near the bottom feels bolted onto a message that was otherwise doing a good job of not being another discount blast.
- Net: strong creative variety and brand storytelling, weak on urgency and price incentive. If the goal was "prove Everlane can do product marketing, not just markdown marketing," it succeeds. If the goal was clicks and conversion today, the lack of an upfront offer will cap performance versus this week's denim sends.

## What's working

- Genuinely new creative lane — first non-price-banner hero this week, which resets fatigue built up since 08/20.
- Product storytelling is layered well: hero → colorway options → detail shot → sustainability/material proof point, in that order.
- The "PFC-free water-resistant finish" and "roomy utility pockets" copy gives a real reason to buy, not just a discount.
- Clean, uncluttered layout with strong whitespace — no overlapping text or broken images.
- Three colorways shown up front lets the shopper self-select before clicking through.

## What's weak

- No price incentive anywhere near the hero — a $ or % off, or "new arrival" perk, would meaningfully lift clicks for a cold product intro.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA is generic; it doesn't reference the jacket, so it reads like a stock button rather than a considered CTA.
- The 70% off sale banner near the bottom undercuts the premium/product-story framing of everything above it — it reads like two emails stitched together.
- No social proof (reviews, ratings, "bestseller" tag) on a brand-new item that a shopper hasn't seen before, which is exactly when proof matters most.
- No sizing/fit guidance visible (true to size? runs large?) despite this being an unfamiliar new silhouette.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a launch incentive tied directly to the jacket ("New: 15% off your first Field Jacket" or free shipping badge) in the hero — right now there's zero reason to buy today vs. next week.
- 2. Rename the CTA from "SHOP NOW" to "Shop the Field Jacket" so it's specific and matches rubric-level clarity, not a stock button.
- 3. Cut or visually separate the "Up to 70% off sale styles" banner — it's tonally at odds with the organic-cotton, quality-story framing above it; if it must stay, move it into its own module further down, past a divider.
- 4. Add one line of social proof under the colorway grid ("4.8★ — 'Perfect for fall commutes'") since this is an unseasoned new product.
- 5. Add a quick fit note ("Relaxed fit, runs true to size") near the product grid — high-value info for a first-time silhouette.
