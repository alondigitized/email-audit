# Your Favorites At Better Prices
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the third "we lowered our prices" beat in four days — following 8/12's manifesto letter and 8/13's straight price-drop announcement — and it finally does what those two didn't: shows the actual price tiers with product grids underneath. "Under $35 / $40 / $50 / $60" organized by tee and long-sleeve styles is a genuinely useful structure, more concrete than the prior two sends' abstract "prices went down" messaging.
- But it's still Everlane running the same monochrome-basics photography and stark black CTA it's used all week, and the price-tier framing is really just a repackaging of the identical "We Lowered Our Prices" message from 8/13 — this is beat three of the same story, not a new one. Subject line "Your Favorites At Better Prices" is nearly interchangeable with 8/13's "We Lowered Our Prices," so a subscriber who opened yesterday's email has little reason to open this one.
- Judgement: solid execution of a tired beat. The price-tier grid is the best idea Everlane's had on this topic all week, but the email needed a harder hook (a number, a specific product, an end date) to justify a third consecutive send on the same theme.

## What's working

- Price-tier segmentation (Under $35/$40/$50/$60) gives shoppers an immediate, scannable way to self-select by budget — more actionable than the prior two sends' vague price-drop copy.
- Twelve product thumbnails across four tiers give real browsing density without feeling cluttered.
- Clean, on-brand minimalist styling (grayscale hero, black CTA bars, thin dividers) is consistent and renders with no visible bugs.
- Hero copy "Same Materials, Same Quality, Same Favorites" plus "Better Prices, Still Everlane" preempts the "did they cut corners" objection before the shopper even scrolls.

## What's weak

- Subject and hero message are a near-repeat of 8/13's "We Lowered Our Prices" — this is the third price-cut send this week with no new information (no specific % off, no dollar amount saved).
- No actual discount percentage or dollar comparison shown anywhere — "Better Prices" is asserted, never quantified, even though the tier grid was a perfect place to show a strikethrough price.
- Two large near-identical grayscale hero banners (same model type, same flat lighting) eat the first two screens before any product or price appears — a lot of scroll for basics-country.
- No urgency or time-bound framing at all, which is consistent with this being a permanent pricing change but makes the email feel skippable given the cadence.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real number to the hero — "Up to 20% Off Your Favorite Tees" beats "Better Prices, Still Everlane" and immediately differentiates this send from 8/12 and 8/13.
- 2. Show before/after pricing on at least the first tier row (e.g., "$35 $28") — the tier grid is the strongest asset in the email and it's currently just categorical, not evidentiary.
- 3. Cut one of the two hero banners — collapse to a single hero with the "Same Materials, Same Quality" reassurance copy overlaid, and get to the price grid faster.
- 4. Since this is the third price-story send in four days, differentiate this one explicitly as "final" or "now live" messaging so it doesn't read as a rerun.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Tees & Layers, Repriced`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Same fabric, same fit — just less on the price tag.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop tees and layers now starting under $35.`

- **Broken redirect — "Everlane Logo"**: `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M00F8KBW1MDHPNB03PGE1NPS_0` resolves the same way, to `unknown url type: '/pages/womens?_kx=...&bxid=...&cm_flow_i...'`.

- 12 of 31 material links were probed (19 lower-priority footer/social links skipped by design); no other broken links reported among the probed set.
