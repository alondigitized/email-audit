# Wait What? These Are An Extra 15% Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's third consecutive sale email in three days — stacking on the "Up to 70% Off" send from June 26 and "Quietly the Best Sale Yet" from June 25. The subject teases something new and compelling ("an extra 15% off"), which is genuinely incremental news. But open the email and that promise evaporates: the hero still reads "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES / WEEKEND EDIT," with no visible callout of the additional 15% anywhere in the body. The "UNDER $100" block mentions a "Free Bonus gift on all weekend discounts" — a different offer entirely. The subject and body are not talking about the same thing.
- The creative is also noticeably stripped down compared to the past two sends. No models, no product images — just a dark denim hero texture, text, and four category text-links. After a streak that included strong lifestyle photography, this reads as a last-minute weekend push assembled quickly.
- The email isn't broken, but it fails its own hook. If someone opens because they want the extra 15%, they'll hunt for it, not find it, and bounce.

## What's working

- **Subject creates genuine intrigue.** "Wait What? These Are An Extra 15% Off" is one of the stronger hooks in this brand's recent run — conversational, specific enough to feel real.
- **Category CTAs are well-labeled.** "SHOP SALE LINEN," "SHOP SALE DRESSES," "SHOP SALE SANDALS," "SHOP SALE UNDER $50" — four clean, scannable entry points.
- **Weekend deadline is visible.** "WEEKEND EDIT" in the hero anchors urgency without being obnoxious.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean; the "UNDER $100" block reads as an intentional design choice, not a broken image.

## What's weak

- **The extra 15% offer is nowhere in the body.** The subject's entire hook is unsupported — the hero still says "UP TO 70% OFF," same as yesterday's send.
- **No product imagery.** After two days of sale emails, there's nothing new to look at — just a dark hero texture. Fatigue compounds when you don't show fresh product.
- **"Free Bonus gift on all weekend discounts" buried and vague.** The UNDER $100 block introduces a third offer (gift with purchase?) with no specifics about what the gift is — easy to miss and hard to act on.
- **Body copy ("For the couch or for heading out...") is generic and disconnected.** It doesn't relate to either the 15%-off angle or the under-$100 angle, making the email feel like a template fill rather than a cohesive message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the extra 15% the hero.** The current hero should read something like "EXTRA 15% OFF ALL SALE STYLES — THIS WEEKEND ONLY" with a promo code or "applied at checkout" line. If there's no code, say so explicitly. The subject earned the click; the body has to cash it.
- 2. **Drop the generic body copy** ("For the couch or for heading out...") and replace it with a single line that explains the stack: "Already up to 70% off. Now take an extra 15% — this weekend only."
- 3. **Show one hero product.** Even a single product image (a $49 linen shirt marked from $88) makes the discount feel concrete and scrollable. The all-text treatment is too thin after two days of the same hero.
- 4. **Clarify the bonus gift.** If there's a GWP, name the gift. "Free canvas tote with any weekend order" is actionable. "Free Bonus gift" is not.
- 5. **Retire the "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE" lockup.** It appeared in yesterday's send (June 26, "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles") and reusing it here signals no new news even before the subject's promise is betrayed.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 15% Off Everything in the Sale — This Weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack It: Extra 15% Off Our Already-70%-Off Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Promo applied at checkout. Shop linen, dresses, sandals + more under $50.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The weekend sale just got bigger — here's what to grab first.`
