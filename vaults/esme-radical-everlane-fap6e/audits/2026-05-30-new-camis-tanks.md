# New Camis & Tanks
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's third straight editorial-first email after the Memorial Day promotional blitz — following "The Linen Standard" (May 28) and "Weekend Getaway Approved" (May 29). The category focus on Tanks & Camis is the right seasonal beat: it's cohesive, summer-ready, and gives the hero model room to breathe. The email is clean and on-brand.
- The problem is structural. A nine-product grid with zero pricing, followed by a sustainability block, followed by a "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" footer banner — that's three distinct email personalities stapled together. The sustainability section kills the shopping momentum precisely when it was building. And the sale banner at the bottom actively undermines the editorial confidence of the top half: are you shopping new arrivals or clearing old inventory? Readers shouldn't have to ask.
- The subject line "New Camis & Tanks" does the bare minimum. It describes the email; it doesn't sell it.

## What's working

- **Hero image is confident and uncluttered** — the olive oversized tank on a model with glasses is editorial, aspirational, and season-right.
- **Product grid fills out the category well** — nine SKUs spanning earth tones, black, and neutrals demonstrate range without feeling overwhelming.
- **Brand consistency is intact** — typography, whitespace, and tone are recognizably Everlane throughout the top two-thirds.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is visible and unambiguous** — twice, which is appropriate for a scroll-depth email.

## What's weak

- **Sustainability block is a momentum killer** — inserted between the product grid and the sale banner, it derails the purchase flow at the worst possible moment.
- **Zero pricing in the product grid** — no cost, no compare-at, nothing to convert browse to intent.
- **The 70% off sale banner at the bottom contradicts the editorial tone** — it signals "we're still in clearance mode" right after two careful brand-reset emails.
- **Subject line "New Camis & Tanks" has no hook** — no offer, no urgency, no point of view. "New" is the weakest possible lead word.
- **No size, fit, or fabric detail visible anywhere** — for a category where fit drives return rates, this is a missed conversion signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the sustainability block entirely from this email.** It belongs in a dedicated brand email or the footer, not between a product grid and a CTA. The email is trying to sell tanks — don't interrupt the shopper.
- 2. **Add pricing or at minimum a "Starting at $X" line** to the product grid. Even a single price anchor changes browse behavior.
- 3. **Either move the 70% off banner above the sustainability section or remove it.** If you're running an editorial reset, the sale appendage undercuts it. If you're promoting the sale, lead with it.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject line to include a point of view or offer:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `The summer tank. New styles, now live.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your off-duty uniform, updated`
- 5. **Rewrite the preheader to add context the subject omits:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Camis, tanks, and crop tops — light enough for the whole season.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop new Tanks & Camis — starting at $28.`
