# Up to 70% Off Sale Styles: Our Deepest Discounts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the ninth consecutive promotional email from Everlane in seven days, and the third iteration of the "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" subject — following the June 26 original and the June 29 "Pick Your Price" version. The creative is functionally unchanged: the same lemon hero, the same "Summer Clearout Sale / Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" overlay, the same black SHOP NOW button. The only novelty is the subject appendage "Our Deepest Discounts," which is a hollow superlative that a fatigued subscriber has no reason to believe after receiving eight prior emails.
- The execution is technically clean. Hierarchy works. The CTA is obvious. But those same things were true on June 26 and June 29. Everlane is trying to manufacture urgency through adjective escalation — "Quietly the Best," then "Extra 15% Off," now "Our Deepest Discounts" — without providing any actual proof of difference. There are no struck-through prices in the product grid, no deadline, and nothing that distinguishes this send from the two before it.
- On July 1, a subscriber who skipped the June 26 and June 29 versions of this same email needs one compelling reason to act now. This email doesn't provide it.

## What's working

- **Hero offer reads in under two seconds.** "Summer Clearout Sale / Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" requires zero interpretation.
- **CTA is unmissable.** High-contrast black button, centered, immediately below the hero — no hunting required.
- **Product grid gives multiple entry points.** Six items spanning tops, denim, and bottoms without overwhelming.
- **Clean render throughout.** No broken images, no layout issues, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **Creative is a copy-paste from June 26 and June 29.** Same lemon, same headline, same button — the brand history makes this unmistakable, and subscribers will feel it.
- **"Our Deepest Discounts" is unverifiable marketing language.** Deeper than what? Compared to which prior send? There's no price proof in the email to back it up.
- **No prices shown in the product grid.** A discount email that omits struck-through prices forces a click just to confirm savings exist — friction that should not exist in an email leading with a 70% off claim.
- **No urgency mechanism visible.** No end date, no "48 hours only," no inventory scarcity signal. The lemon sits there implying nothing.
- **"New Arrivals" link in navigation undermines the clearance message.** Full-price new product pulls attention away from the sale and muddies the email's entire purpose.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show prices in the product grid** — struck-through original and sale price on every item. This single change proves "Deepest Discounts," removes the need for a leap of faith, and is the highest-lift edit possible. Every other improvement is secondary.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** "Sale ends July 4" or "Final 48 hours" gives the subscriber an actual reason to act today rather than wait and forget. Without a deadline, "Our Deepest Discounts" is just noise.
- 3. **Retire the lemon hero.** It has appeared on at least three sends. A product-as-hero — a specific sale item with its before/after price — would signal to fatigued subscribers that something genuinely changed.
- 4. **Remove New Arrivals from the navigation.** This is a clearance email. Every link should point to the sale.
- 5. **Rewrite the CTA.** "See All Sale Styles" or "Shop the Lowest Prices" is more honest and specific than the generic "SHOP NOW."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for custom fonts (line 2 of `<style>`):**
```
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and Outlook. `MaisonNeue-Light` will silently fall back to Arial/Helvetica in those clients — which is fine functionally, but confirms the web font never renders for the majority of recipients.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Space-only title. Screen readers that announce the document title (e.g., JAWS in Outlook) will read nothing. Set to a descriptive string matching the subject line.

**All links forced to black with no underline:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links are visually indistinguishable from body text. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure — color alone cannot be the only visual means of indicating a link. At minimum, underline or a distinct color should be present on `a:hover`.

**h4 mobile font size 10px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below the WCAG-recommended 12px minimum and will be nearly illegible on mobile. If h4 is used for product captions or price labels, this is a usability defect.

**Image alt text:** Cannot confirm from truncated source — audit the rendered DOM to ensure every `<img>` has a non-empty `alt` attribute, particularly product and CTA images.
