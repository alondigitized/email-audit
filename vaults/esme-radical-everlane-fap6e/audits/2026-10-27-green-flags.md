- The creative is pretty, but it asks for a click without giving a reason. The only value line is "BETTER PRICES, STILL EVERLANE," with no number, no product and no deadline. ## What's working

- The hero is strong. A single model, saturated green, and a clear SHOP NOW bar make the eye land on the story immediately.
- The theme is cohesive. Every item in the grid is green, so the "Evergreen Shades" idea reads at a glance.
- The grid is clean and uncluttered, with generous spacing and consistent product photography.
- The copy is on-brand and lightly witty: "lush, leafy, and giving verdant vibes."
- The render is clean. I see no broken images or overlapping text, and the footer is tidy.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer. "Better prices, still Everlane" is a claim, not a deal, and it has no %, $ or deadline.
- None of the nine grid items has a name, price or visible link affordance, so there is nothing specific to click.
- Both CTAs just say "Shop Now" and point to a generic destination. The second "Shop Now" is a small underlined text link that is easy to miss.
- The pale beige "Better Prices" block is large and low-contrast, and it spends valuable space on a vague message.
- The grid mixes women's tops and bottoms with shorts, so it is a bit unfocused for a fall color story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one real price or value claim in the hero, for example "Cashmere-blend V-neck, $98," or state what the "better prices" actually are, such as "Prices cut on 40+ core styles."
- 2. Make the grid shoppable. Add names and prices under each piece and link each tile to its own product page.
- 3. Replace both "Shop Now" buttons with specific CTAs, such as "Shop the Green Edit" and "See Lower Prices."
- 4. Cut the grid to six tiles, and lead with the V-neck sweater from the hero, since the hero product isn't clearly in the grid.
- 5. Add social proof to the "Better Prices" block, such as a star rating or review count, to match the review-led send on 10/27.
