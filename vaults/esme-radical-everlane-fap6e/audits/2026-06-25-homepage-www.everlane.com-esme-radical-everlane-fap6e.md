# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-06-25
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- What I'm looking at is barely a homepage — it's a near-total black screen dominated by an extreme macro close-up of what looks like a black knit textile, probably a shoe upper or knitwear, pressed into the left edge of the frame. There is no visible copy, no navigation, no CTA, no banner, no price. For a brand that built its entire identity on radical transparency and showing you the math, this opening shot is aggressively withholding. It reads less like "we have nothing to hide" and more like "we assume you already know who we are."

## What's working

- **Texture detail is striking**: The extreme macro on the knit textile is actually impressive if you're already a fabric nerd. The weave is clearly a premium construction — visible stitching density, structured but soft-looking. For me, who reads material specs before clicking Add to Cart, this is the one frame I'd zoom in on anyway. It's just doing it prematurely.
- **Minimal distraction**: Zero clutter above the fold. If this is intentional editorial restraint, I can respect the instinct even if the execution misfires.

## What's weak

- **No copy whatsoever**: I have zero context for what I'm looking at. Is this a shoe? A sweater? A tote? Everlane's radical transparency promise is that they tell me what it costs to make and why it's priced where it is. None of that exists here.
- **No navigation visible**: I cannot reach Women's, Denim, or Shoes in one tap. The top nav — if it exists — is hidden in this capture. For a returning buyer like me, that's friction before the first scroll.
- **No CTA, no offer, no hook**: There is no "Shop Now," no "New Arrivals," no "Free shipping on $75+." Every rubric criterion for conversion is missing from above the fold.
- **The crop reads as broken**: On mobile, a 90% black screen with a sliver of gray wall is a rendering failure until proven otherwise. My first instinct would be to check my connection, not to appreciate the art direction.
- **No social proof or trust signal**: No review count, no "As seen in," no "B Corp certified" badge — nothing that signals this isn't just a placeholder.

## Recommendations

- **Pull back the hero crop and add a headline**: Even 20% more frame — showing the full shoe or silhouette — plus a single line of copy ("Built to last. Priced to prove it.") would immediately re-anchor this as intentional editorial, not a render failure.
- **Add a persistent top banner with one concrete offer**: Free shipping threshold, current sale, new-arrivals drop date. One line. Everlane's audience skews analytical — we want the number, not the mood.
- **Surface the cost-breakdown story in the hero**: If this is a product launch, put the factory cost and the retail price right on screen. That is the entire value proposition. A hero that shows a number — "$38 to make. $88 retail. Here's why." — would stop a radical-transparency shopper cold.
- **Ensure navigation is visible on first paint**: If the hamburger or top nav is cut off at the top of the screenshot, that is a critical accessibility and UX issue to fix on mobile. One-tap category access is non-negotiable.
