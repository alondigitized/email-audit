# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-07-23
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- I came here expecting Everlane's usual transparent-pricing homepage and instead I'm staring at what looks like an extreme close-up product shot — dark denim texture on the left, some light-colored cylindrical object (bag? bottle?) bleeding in from the right — so blurry I can't read a single word of copy. This tells me one of two things: either the page rendered mid-load and this capture is a cropped crop of almost nothing, or Everlane decided that maximalist abstraction is their new brand language. Neither is good for me.

## What's working

- The dark denim texture in the left portion of the image does give off a premium, muted-palette vibe that feels on-brand for Everlane's aesthetic restraint — if I squint and imagine what the full shot might be, I can see the intention
- The color palette (near-black + warm sand/khaki) is consistent with their Fall/transitional season story

## What's weak

- **The entire above-the-fold zone is unreadable** — no brand name visible, no product name, no price, no copy, no CTA button I can actually read
- **The cylindrical object on the right** is unidentified; I don't know if it's a bag, a water bottle, a prop — I literally can't tell what they're selling
- **No transparency signals whatsoever** — as someone who opens Everlane for the cost breakdown story, the complete absence of any pricing, material callout, or ethical sourcing copy above the fold is a miss
- **No navigation bar is clearly visible** — I can't tap to Women's, to Denim, to anything
- **Blurry capture or blurry hero** — if this is the actual render state, it reads as a performance/load failure, which kills trust immediately

## Recommendations

- Surface at least one line of copy over the hero image: product name + "From $X, here's the cost breakdown" is the Everlane value prop in a single line — use it
- Add a visible, high-contrast CTA button (white on dark or navy text on white) anchored above the fold; the faint bottom strip isn't cutting it on mobile
- Make the subject of the hero image unambiguous — if this is a denim feature, show the full pant, a waistband, or a woman in the jeans; don't go so abstract that the product disappears
- Include a sticky nav bar at the top with "Women / Men / Denim / Bags" so I can self-route in one tap without decoding the hero
