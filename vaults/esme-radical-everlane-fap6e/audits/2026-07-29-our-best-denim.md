# Our Best Denim
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane running the identical playbook from 7/27's "Meet Your Summer Denim Lineup" (9/10) two days later — a best-sellers denim product grid capped with a bolted-on sitewide sale banner, this time bumped from "Extra 20% Off" to "Extra 25% Off Sale Styles (already up to 70% off)." The hero is clean and on-brand (the same moody, cropped denim-detail photography Everlane leans on constantly), the four-product grid is well-organized with real fit/fabric copy, and the render is bug-free top to bottom.
- The problem is repetition, not execution. Subscribers who opened 7/27's denim send get almost the exact same email again 48 hours later: same category, same layout skeleton, same discount-stacking rhetoric ("up to 70% off" plus an "extra" percentage on top). The subject line "Our Best Denim" is vague where 7/27's "Meet Your Summer Denim Lineup" at least framed a season story — this one reads like filler between campaigns.
- Solid, on-brand, zero technical issues — but this is now the second denim-grid-plus-sale-banner send inside a week, and the subject line undersells it. Middle of the pack for this brand, not a standout.

## What's working

- Hero image is crisp and true to Everlane's aesthetic — no render bugs, text overlay ("The Denim You Love") is legible against the photo.
- Product grid gives each of the four styles a real description (rise, fabric, fit detail) rather than just a name and price — useful for a shopper deciding between cuts.
- Two clear conversion paths: per-product "SHOP NOW" links plus a bolded "SHOP ALL JEANS" button for browsers who don't want to commit to one style.
- Sale banner ("Extra 25% Off Sale Styles") stands out visually with a distinct blue background block, breaking up the white grid.
- Footer is complete and functional-looking: nav links, store finder, help center, social icons, legal fine print with a real expiration date.

## What's weak

- Subject "Our Best Denim" is a flat superlative claim with no offer, no product name, no urgency — weaker than 7/27's more evocative "Meet Your Summer Denim Lineup."
- This is structurally the same email as 7/27 (denim grid + bolted-on sale banner), sent just two days later — a subscriber who opened that one will feel the déjà vu instantly.
- No discount or offer is visible in the hero itself — a shopper has to scroll past the entire four-product grid before hitting the actual sale (25% off), which undersells the email's real hook.
- No reviews, ratings, or social proof anywhere in the grid — pure product copy, no "why trust this" signal.
- No loyalty/member pricing shown despite Everlane having a rewards-adjacent customer base.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the sale offer into the hero. "Extra 25% Off" is buried below four full products — pull it above the fold so the strongest lever in the email is the first thing seen, not the last.
- 2. Differentiate from 7/27's send. Since this is the same grid-plus-banner format two days apart, either skip a send or swap in a genuinely different angle (e.g., a fit-guide framing, a single hero product story) to avoid training subscribers to skim.
- 3. Sharpen the subject line to carry the actual offer instead of a generic superlative.
- 4. Add a single customer quote or star-rating strip near the top product (Way-High Jean 2.0) — cheap addition, adds trust signal missing from every recent send in this history.
