# The Best Shorts This Summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's seventh email in five days — and the first that tries to go editorial rather than percentage-off. The body is genuinely good: "On Shorts Notice" is a sharp pun, the hero photography is clean and aspirational, and the product grid shows real range. The problem is the boilerplate sale banner bolted on top: "EXTRA 15% OFF SALE STYLES (ALREADY UP TO 70% OFF) / SHOP NOW." That banner is a direct bleed from the Summer Clearout campaign, and it's working against the editorial intent. The email can't decide if it's a product story or a sale notice, and it ends up being a diluted version of both.
- Making it worse: per brand history, Everlane already sent "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles: Pick Your Price" on this same date, June 29. So this is the second Everlane email of the day — landing in an inbox that's already been hit six times this week. An editorial send could have been a meaningful palette-cleanser between sale beats. Running it simultaneously with a pure-sale blast destroys that contrast.
- The clothes look good. The photography is on-brand. But the strategic decision to blend editorial and promotional in the same send, on the same day as a separate sale email, is a genuine mistake.

## What's working

- **"On Shorts Notice" lands.** Witty, on-brand, and a tonal break Everlane's inbox badly needed after six days of percentage-off messaging.
- **Product grid shows genuine range.** Multiple colorways, gender-inclusive styling, and clean photography give the subscriber a real feel for the assortment.
- **Hero photography is aspirational.** Cream wide-leg shorts styled with a matching top and sandals reads premium and seasonal — Everlane's editorial voice at its clearest.
- **SHOP NOW button is unambiguous.** High-contrast black button, no confusion about what to click.
- **Summer timing is right.** Late June is exactly when a shorts-focused editorial should hit.

## What's weak

- **Sale banner is grafted on, not designed in.** "EXTRA 15% OFF SALE STYLES" looks copy-pasted from the campaign template and creates immediate dissonance with the editorial hero below it.
- **No prices anywhere in the product grid.** If this is a sale, show "was/now" on the products. If it's editorial, cut the sale banner. Sitting between both means neither pays off.
- **Second Everlane email of the same day.** The contrast value of going editorial is completely lost when a pure-sale email lands in the same send window.
- **Sustainability block mid-email breaks shopping flow.** The "READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT" section interrupts the product journey at precisely the wrong scroll depth.
- **Generic single CTA.** After a grid that clearly covers both women's and men's shorts, one SHOP NOW button sends everyone to the same undifferentiated destination.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Force a choice — editorial or sale, not both.** If the banner stays, add "was $X / now $X" on the product grid cards so the sale claim actually pays off. If it's an editorial send, remove the banner entirely and let the product story stand.
- 2. **Don't send two emails on the same calendar day.** This and the June 29 "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" should be at least 24–48 hours apart so each has room to work.
- 3. **Move sustainability to its own send or the footer.** Everlane's impact story deserves its own email — used here it's a mid-funnel interruption at exactly the wrong moment.
- 4. **Split the CTA by gender.** Two buttons — "Shop Women's Shorts" and "Shop Men's Shorts" — same effort, meaningfully higher relevance.
- 5. **Replace the tagline.** "Effortless styles worth your attention" is forgettable marketing-speak. Something like "Worn-all-summer simple." or "The shortcut to summer." is more Everlane.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 70% off the pairs you've been watching.`
