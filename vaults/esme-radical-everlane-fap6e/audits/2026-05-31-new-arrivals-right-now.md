# New Arrivals, Right Now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- "New Arrivals, Right Now" is Everlane's fourth consecutive editorial email in four days — after "The Linen Standard" (May 28), "Weekend Getaway Approved" (May 29), and "New Camis & Tanks" (May 30, scored 5/10). The editorial reset made strategic sense at the start; this fourth iteration shows the well running dry. The product grid — nine tops spanning tanks, tees, and camis — is nearly indistinguishable from yesterday's "New Camis & Tanks" send. A subscriber who opened both will notice.
- The email is technically clean and brand-consistent, but asks nothing of the reader and offers nothing to act on. "Your next outfit? Call it fate." is charm without conversion hook. The sustainability block mid-body then pivots the email away from shopping entirely into brand-values territory, putting a secondary CTA (READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT) in direct competition with the primary.
- With no offer, no price signal, and no hero product, SHOP NOW is doing all the work with nothing to back it up. This is a filler send.

## What's working

- Clean render — no broken images, no overlapping text, product photography is consistent and well-lit across all nine items
- Good color variety in the grid (black, teal, cobalt, white, grey) keeps it visually engaging even without a hero
- SHOP NOW button is high-contrast black on white — impossible to miss regardless of reading context
- Everlane brand voice is consistent throughout: minimalist, confident, no junk language
- Email is tight — no scroll fatigue, full content fits in a short viewport

## What's weak

- Nine products, no hero — the grid is a catalog scan, not a decision moment; nothing pulls the eye toward a single click target
- Yesterday's "New Camis & Tanks" email showed the exact same product category; subscribers who opened both get near-duplicate content two days running
- The sustainability block mid-email breaks shopping intent and introduces a competing CTA (impact report) that steers readers away from conversion
- No offer, no price, no credible urgency — "Right Now" does not compensate for zero promotional reason to act
- "Your next outfit? Call it fate." is pleasant but contributes nothing actionable

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a hero product.** One strong image with a name and price converts better than nine anonymous grid thumbnails. The grid can stay below the fold, but the email needs a pull.
- 2. **Remove or relocate the sustainability block.** The impact report pitch belongs in a dedicated values email, not inserted mid-body where it competes with SHOP NOW.
- 3. **Give the subject a specific category hook.** "New Arrivals" plus one product name or material ("New Arrivals: The Organic Tee Is Back") gives inbox-scanners a reason to open that the current subject doesn't.
- 4. **Surface one concrete reason to click beyond novelty.** "New colorways just landed," "back in stock," or even "free shipping on new arrivals" — anything that makes inaction feel like a miss.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo's MJML-based builder with proper MSO/VML Outlook conditionals and responsive breakpoints. Several technical defects are confirmed in the available source; compliance and full link assessment are partially limited by HTML truncation.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Confirmed issue — `@import` in `<style>` block:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by several email clients (Gmail app on Android, some Outlook versions). Font will silently fail to load and fall back to Arial — acceptable outcome, but the import method is fragile. Prefer a `<link rel="stylesheet">` in `<head>` or inline the `@font-face` declarations directly.

**Partial — UTM parameters not visible in truncated source.** Cannot confirm whether product/CTA links include `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`. Must verify against full source before send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only whitespace. Some email clients surface `<title>` as a tooltip or in accessibility trees. Should be set to the email subject line or a meaningful string.

**Issue — `h4` mobile font size drops to 10px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below WCAG minimum recommended size and will be nearly illegible on standard mobile displays. Minimum recommended is 13–14px.

**Issue — All anchor states force `color:#000; text-decoration:none`, including `:hover`:**
```css
a:hover { color:#000; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover state is visually identical to default state. Not an email-client rendering problem, but eliminates all visual affordance that a link is interactive in webmail clients that render `:hover` (Gmail web, Apple Mail).

**Issue — Duplicate `img` reset block:**
The `img` rule appears twice in the stylesheet with slightly different properties. The second declaration (which adds `height: auto; max-width: 100%`) overrides the first. No functional breakage, but indicates template hygiene issue.

**Cannot assess — No `alt` attributes visible in truncated source.** Full source must be checked for `alt=""` on decorative images and descriptive `alt` on product images.
