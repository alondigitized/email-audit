# Your Summer Uniform
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- "Your Summer Uniform" is Everlane's eighth consecutive editorial send in eight days, and the formula hasn't changed: lifestyle model in a hero with a headline overlay, a few lines of body copy, a product grid, the sustainability paragraph, and a generic "SHOP NOW." What's working here is the seasonal coherence — "Hot Days, Cool Fits" is the right idea for late June, the product grid of shorts and shirts reads as a genuine warm-weather capsule, and the layout renders cleanly. The concept lands better than last week's color-story send ("The Hottest Hue Right Now?") because at least this one has a utility hook: a uniform is a thing you buy.
- What's not working is the same problem it's been for eight sends: no price, no offer, no urgency. Everlane is asking the subscriber to feel something, then click on faith. The June 17 "10/10 no notes" send earned its 8/10 because it backed the editorial voice with specific product CTAs and visible reviews. This one retreats back to pure aspiration. At send #8 in a row with the same structure, the brand is training its list to scroll past.

## What's working

- **"Hot Days, Cool Fits" hero headline** is crisp, seasonally correct, and delivers the brand's minimalist tone in four words.
- **Preview text complements subject** — "Breezy, lightweight, and better for you" adds product texture to "Your Summer Uniform" without repeating it.

## What's weak

- **No price anywhere** — the product grid shows six items with zero price visibility; you're clicking blind.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the only CTA** — generic, destination-unknown, and repeated cadence-of-eight dulls it further.
- **No offer, no urgency** — "summer uniform" is a concept, not a reason to act today; nothing in the email creates pressure or reward for clicking now.
- **Eighth editorial send in eight days** — cadence fatigue is measurable; each send without an offer trains the list that Everlane emails are ambient content, not action items.
- **Sustainability bloc is bottom-of-email filler** — at this scroll depth, the "READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT" CTA competes with the "Shop Women / Shop Men" nav footer and wins nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the hero** — "Shorts + Tops from $68" under "Hot Days, Cool Fits" gives the subscriber something to evaluate without clicking. Single line, zero creative lift.
- 2. **Rename the CTA to "SHOP THE UNIFORM"** — matches the email's own concept, marginally more specific than "SHOP NOW," and differentiates this send from every other one in the archive.
- 3. **Add prices to the product grid** — even at full price, visible cost drives click intent. The grid is decoration without it.
- 4. **Break the editorial streak** — either soft-offer this (free shipping over $100, member-early-access) or pull the cadence back to 4–5/week. The June 17 send proved the editorial voice works when it's earned; at this frequency it evaporates.
- 5. **Move or cut the sustainability bloc** — it's competing with a cluttered footer at the bottom of a transactional email. Either lead with it in a solo send or remove it here.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Most ESPs inject the subject line here automatically; if Klaviyo is not doing so, screen readers and some clients will announce a blank document title. Should be `<title>Your Summer Uniform</title>` or populated via template variable.

**Links stripped of all visual affordance:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
All four pseudo-class states — including `:hover` — are locked to `#000` with no underline. This is an accessibility issue (WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color): links are visually indistinguishable from body text unless the surrounding layout provides context. `:hover` should at minimum change state.

**`img` rule declared twice:**
```css
img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic }
/* ... later ... */
img { border: 0; height: auto; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; max-width: 100% }
```
Duplicate rule block. The second declaration overrides the first. `-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic` is dropped in the cascade — minor, as IE11 is end-of-life, but indicates copy-paste artifact in the template.

**Alt text on images:** Cannot verify — `<img>` tags are in the truncated body. Flag for QA pass: all product images must carry descriptive `alt` text; hero images should not use `alt=""` unless purely decorative.

**`h4` mobile font-size collapses to 10px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below the WCAG 2.1 practical minimum (~14px) and will fail legibility on high-DPI mobile displays. Recommend ≥12px floor.
