# A New Season Comes Into View
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-mood send with no offer. Everlane has spent the week hammering 30% Friends & Family, including "One Last Chance for 30% Off Sitewide" earlier today. After that, a pure fall-collection email is a tonal reset. It looks good, but the creative has to do all the work because nothing in the copy gives a reason to act today.
- The hero is a four-up collage of models in neutral fall layers, with "Scenes From Fall" spread across the middle and a black SHOP THE COLLECTION bar below. A nine-product grid in browns, creams and olive follows, then a second full-bleed model shot and a second identical CTA. The look is cohesive and premium.
- My judgment is that this is a solid brand moment but a weak performance email. It reads as a post-sale breather, and it asks for a click with no price, no product names and no deadline.

## What's working

- The palette is cohesive: espresso, cream and olive, in both the product grid and the model shots.
- The hero collage sets the fall mood immediately, and the black CTA bar anchors it.
- The 3x3 product grid is clean and shows the jackets, knits and layers a fall shopper would browse.
- The second hero image and CTA give a second click chance after the grid.
- Everlane's restrained voice and layout stay consistent with its earlier sends.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or member benefit. After a week of 30% off, a full-price email has no stated reason to act now.
- "Scenes From Fall" is tiny, thin, white text spaced across the collage, and it blends into the bright background. Everlane's earlier closing sends had bold hero headlines.
- Both CTAs say "SHOP THE COLLECTION", which is generic and doesn't name a product.
- None of the nine grid products has a name, a price or a link label, so there are no product details to act on.
- "Every piece is worth noting." is a vague tagline that adds little.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete reason to click. Even a light "Free shipping on fall layers" or a new-arrivals callout would help, since Friends & Family just ended and this has nothing in its place.
- 2. Make the hero headline bold and legible. Use larger, heavier white text on a darker overlay, or move it onto a solid band.
- 3. Name and price the grid items, for example "The Quilted Jacket, $168", and link each tile.
- 4. Vary the CTA copy by section, such as "Shop Outerwear" and "Shop Knits".
- 5. Add a bit of social proof, such as a "Best-seller" tag or review stars on the grid.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Klaviyo-built template that renders with sensible fallbacks. The one confirmed QA failure is the "Sign Up for Texts" link, which returned a relative redirect target. The header checks (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) are inconclusive because of the AgentMail relay, not because the email failed them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **SMS signup link, QA FAIL.** The probe for `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M4XHNGVQX3A7C7ASM125XR34_18` (link text "Icon | Sign Up for Texts") failed with `unknown url type: '/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…&bxid=…'`.
  - The tracker's redirect resolved to a relative path with no scheme or host.
  - Browsers resolve a relative `Location` header correctly, so this may be a limitation of the QA prober and not a user-facing break. I can't confirm either way from the data.
  - Everlane's tracker is still emitting a relative `Location`, and some clients and link scanners handle that poorly.
  - Manually follow the redirect to check it lands on `everlane.com/pages/sms-signup`.
- **Link text.** The anchor text reads "Icon | Sign Up for Texts". This suggests the icon image's alt text or label is leaking into the link text (see section 3).
- **Probe coverage.** Only 12 of 28 links were probed and 16 were skipped (footer, utility and social). The skipped links are unverified, and no other failures were reported in the probed set.
- **Pass rate.** The reported 43% pass rate is driven by the many skipped or informational checks. Only one hard failure exists.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Tracking pixel without alt.** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M4XHNGVQX3A7C7ASM125XR34` has no alt text. This is the open-tracking pixel, so the impact is negligible. Adding `alt=""` would still be cleaner.
- **Missing `lang`.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" …>` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **Empty title.** `<title> </title>` is blank, so there's no accessible document title.
- **Web font import.** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` is stripped by Gmail and some other clients. Font stacks fall back to Arial/Helvetica, so the impact is cosmetic.
- **Small mobile text.** `h4` drops to `font-size: 10px !important` under 480px, which is small for readability.
- **Not flagged.** The viewport meta, MSO conditionals and `#outlook a` handling are present and correct.
