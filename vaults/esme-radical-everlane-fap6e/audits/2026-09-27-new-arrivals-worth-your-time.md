# New Arrivals Worth Your Time
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, generic "New Arrivals" send with no offer whatsoever — a category-refresh email designed to drive browsing, not conversion. The hero photo is strong (a real editorial shot, soft blue backdrop, olive tank layered with clean type), and the 3x3 product grid below does its job showing the new tank/sweater assortment in nine colorways. But compared to every other Everlane send in this run — 09-27's "30% Off Thoughtfully Crafted Styles" at 7.00, the 09-25 "Fan-Favorite Sweaters at 30% Off" at 7-8 — this one has zero discount, zero urgency, and a much weaker subject line.
- The grid itself has a layout hiccup: the third-row-right tile (bald model in black tank) is visibly taller than the other eight, breaking the otherwise tidy 3x3 alignment. It's a minor visual bug, but noticeable on a second look.
- Judgment: solid brand execution, weak commercial send. Everlane already proved this week that 30%-off subject lines pull harder; this one reads as filler content between promo blasts.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuine editorial photography — well-lit, on-brand, not stocky.
- "New Arrivals" text overlay is legible against the tank without fighting the image.
- Black "SHOP NOW" CTA bar is immediate, high-contrast, and unmissable.
- 3x3 grid gives a fast, scannable view of the new colorways/styles.
- Sustainability block ("better receipts") reinforces brand identity without derailing the shopping flow.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the email — no %, no free shipping, nothing time-bound.
- Subject "New Arrivals Worth Your Time" is vague filler copy, not a concrete hook.
- Grid image in row 3, column 3 is a different aspect ratio/size than the other eight tiles, breaking grid symmetry.
- No pricing, no product names, no "shop the look" — just thumbnails with no supporting copy.
- Preview text is not visible in the render at all — can't confirm what shows in-inbox.

- 3. Sharpen the subject line to name the actual product category driving the new-arrivals push (tanks/sweaters), not generic "worth your time" language.
