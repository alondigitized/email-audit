# What Are We Wearing?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- "What Are We Wearing?" is the fifth Everlane editorial send in six days and the weakest of the run. The creative is competent — clean hero image, lifestyle copy, no render issues — but the email never answers its own subject line. The product grid displays six items with zero labels, zero prices, and a single generic "SHOP NOW" that goes... somewhere. Where "OOO On" (July 10) felt like a sharply observed moment, this reads like a slot that needed to be filled.
- The editorial pivot earned real goodwill after Everlane's punishing 14-email sale blitz, and this email doesn't squander it — but it doesn't spend it either. The sustainability module is genuine Everlane equity, but bolted onto a lifestyle send about effortless dressing, it reads like a non-sequitur. The brand is now in a pattern of sending pretty emails that ask nothing and offer nothing.
- The biggest problem is the subject. It's a vague question with no hook. Compare it to "Summer Brights" or "OOO On" — both signaled what you were getting. "What Are We Wearing?" is the kind of subject that gets the benefit-of-the-doubt on the first send and ignored on the fifth.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** The gray ribbed tank, relaxed styling, and natural light read immediately as "Everlane summer" — on-brand and unfussy.
- **"Let's Keep Things Easy" is the right editorial register** — low-key, confident, not overselling.
- **Sustainability module earns brand trust.** The copy is specific ("remove harmful chemicals and plastics") and the "READ OUR LATEST IMPACT REPORT" CTA gives it a credible destination.
- **Layout is clean.** No render bugs, no broken images, no stray lorem ipsum — just a quiet, well-produced email.

## What's weak

- **The product grid is unlabeled.** Six items, no product names, no prices, no copy — a subscriber can't tell what they're supposed to want or why.
- **"SHOP NOW" does no work.** A generic CTA on a grid of mystery products is friction dressed up as a button.
- **The subject is the weakest of the editorial streak.** "What Are We Wearing?" is a question that implies there's an answer inside; the email doesn't deliver a crisp one.
- **Fifth consecutive editorial send with no value mechanism.** The pivot from sale blasting was right, but Everlane has now swung to pure brand content with no offer for five straight sends. Even subscribers who value the editorial voice expect something clickable eventually.
- **Sustainability section feels unearned here.** In a standalone brand-values email it lands; wedged below six mystery products in a "keep things easy" send, it breaks the narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Label the product grid.** Each product should show its name and price — that single change converts a gallery into a shop and would meaningfully lift CTR.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a specific CTA.** "Shop the Easy Edit" or "Shop the Tank" (if the hero is the lead product) tells the subscriber what they're clicking into.
- 3. **Answer the subject line inside the email.** "What Are We Wearing?" sets up a reveal; deliver a numbered or bulleted list — "This week: the [Tank], the [Tote], the [Shirt]." The body copy currently gestures at occasions, not products.
- 4. **Move sustainability to a standalone send or footer module.** It dilutes the editorial narrative when dropped mid-funnel in a lifestyle email.
- 5. **Introduce a soft value mechanism.** Doesn't have to be a sale — free shipping threshold, member early access, or a new arrival callout would give subscribers a reason to act now vs. never.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** (`<title> </title>`): Contains only a space. Some email clients (Gmail web, some screen readers) surface the `<title>` as a preview label. Should be set to match the subject line or left genuinely empty.

- **All links forced to `#000` / `text-decoration: none` across all states:**
  ```css
  a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:active {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
  ```
  Links are visually indistinguishable from body text. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color). Unsubscribe and CTA links are affected.

- **`h4` mobile font-size: `10px !important`:**
  ```css
  @media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
    h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
  }
  ```
  10px is below the WCAG-recommended 14px minimum for body-weight text. Likely affects product caption labels on mobile.

- **TinyMCE internal selector leaking into production CSS:**
  ```css
  body.mce-content-body, /* NOTE: needed for internal tinymce styles */
  .kl-text > div ...
  ```
  The `body.mce-content-body` selector is a Klaviyo editor artifact. It has no effect at send time but adds noise and signals the template was not cleaned before deploy.

- **External font dependency:** Fonts loaded from `https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css`. If this CDN request fails or is blocked (e.g., by corporate firewalls or privacy-strict clients), rendering degrades to Arial. Fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) is acceptable, but the font load is synchronous in `<head>` — a slow response delays render start.
