# New Right Now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- "New Right Now" is Everlane's tenth editorial-flavored send in roughly twelve days, and the format strain is showing. The subject promises freshness — new arrivals, something happening right now — but the email opens with the exact same "Extra 20% Off Sale Styles (Already Up to 70% Off)" banner that ran on July 16, appeared as a sidebar rider on July 17's organic cotton send, and was the headline mechanic on July 18's "More Dresses" email. Before the subscriber's eye even reaches the hero, they're reading copy they've seen verbatim four times this week.
- The hero itself is solid Everlane: clean beach lifestyle, woman in a black tank and white shorts on a striped towel, "New Arrivals" overlay top right. That image earns the subject line. But immediately below it, a 3×3 product grid dumps nine apparel photos with zero names, zero prices, and no per-item CTAs. In an email that's simultaneously running a sale and teasing new items, the anonymous grid is a conversion dead-end — the subscriber has no idea which items are on sale, at what price, or which are genuinely new.
- The sustainability block ("We carefully consider our materials…") is brand-building filler that feels like an email-length padder at this point in the promo cycle. The overall verdict: competent production, coherent visual identity, but the fourth consecutive deployment of an identical sale mechanic against a vague hook subject is burning through subscriber goodwill fast.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy**: Banner → wordmark → hero → SHOP NOW CTA is a clear, uncluttered path for a casual scroll.
- **Hero image is on-brand**: The beach lifestyle shot matches Everlane's aesthetic and makes the "New Arrivals" angle credible.
- **Sale offer is visible above the fold**: The stacked discount ("Extra 20% Off, already up to 70% off") is prominent and readable before any scrolling.
- **No render bugs**: The layout is crisp — no broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder fields.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body dissonance**: "New Right Now" promises new arrivals; the most prominent post-logo element is a clearance sale banner. The subscriber who opened for new items is immediately redirected to clearance, which erodes trust in the subject line.
- **Product grid is anonymous**: Nine product images with no names, prices, or CTAs. In any other send this is editorial elegance; inside a sale email it's a conversion blocker — what's on sale? At what final price?
- **Fourth consecutive "Extra 20% Off" deployment**: The banner text is identical to July 16 and July 18. Subscribers seeing this a fourth time in four days won't feel urgency — they'll feel fatigued or suspicious that this "sale" never ends.
- **No deadline**: The sale offer has no end date visible, which kills the one lever (urgency) that could counteract fatigue.
- **Sustainability block is dead weight**: The "READ MORE" block adds length without serving either the new-arrivals narrative or the sale conversion goal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Ditch the recycled sale banner or give it a deadline.** If the "Extra 20% Off" mechanic is still live, add "Ends Sunday" or "48 Hours Left" — otherwise it reads as permanent markdown theater. If the sale is genuinely ending, make that the subject, not a vague "new right now."
- 2. **Put prices in the product grid.** Even just the sale price beneath each image ("$48 → $32") would unlock this grid as a conversion surface. Without prices, it's a lookbook, not a shop.
- 3. **Resolve the split narrative.** Either this is a new-arrivals email (pull the sale banner, focus on the fresh inventory) or it's a sale email (own it, show what's discounted). Running both simultaneously dilutes both stories.
- 4. **Cut the sustainability block.** It belongs in a brand-story email, not dropped between a sale CTA and the footer links of a promotional send.
- 5. **Give the hero CTA a destination that matches the subject.** "SHOP NOW" under a "New Arrivals" hero should link to the new-arrivals category, not the sale page. Let the banner link handle the sale traffic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The pieces we just added`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New this week — plus 20% off sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh styles just landed. Sale prices too — but not for long.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals are here. Sale ends Sunday.`

**Issue — `<title>` is empty:**
```html

```
Screen readers and some email clients use `<title>` as the document label. A blank title reduces accessibility for AT users.

**Issue — All link states styled identically with no underline:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links are visually indistinguishable from body text in all states, including hover. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color) unless links are differentiated by weight, underline, or contrast. No hover affordance also degrades desktop UX.


10px is below the 12px minimum recommended by WCAG for readable body/label text. If h4 is used for product captions or CTAs, this is an accessibility defect on mobile.
