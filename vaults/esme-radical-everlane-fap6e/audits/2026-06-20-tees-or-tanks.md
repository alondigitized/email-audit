# Tees Or Tanks?
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's fourth editorial send in five days — following the Henna color story (June 19), Pointelle Layers (June 18), and the strong "10/10 no notes" send (June 17). The creative idea is clear: frame tees vs. tanks as a lighthearted wardrobe debate, then show four products as "all winners." It's competent but forgettable. The hero image is clean and the product grid is tidy, but "Tees Or Tanks?" is basically a category page with a soft pun for a subject line — it makes no argument for why you should buy *today*.
- The cadence concern is real. After burning the "30% off" promo cycle through June 15, Everlane has now sent five straight editorial emails with no offer, no price, no member benefit, and no deadline. The individual emails are fine; back-to-back they blur together. A subscriber opening this after "The Hottest Hue Right Now?" and "Pointelle Layers" is going to feel like they're watching the same loop.
- Verdict: a serviceable product-spotlight email that does nothing wrong and nothing memorable. The render is clean, the product copy is decent, but the absence of any pricing, reviews, or urgency means it's doing catalogue work, not conversion work.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** The full-width lifestyle shot of a model in a white tank with a black wrap skirt is confident and on-brand — clean background, clear product.
- **Four-product grid is well-structured.** Each row has a product image, a name, a one-line descriptor, and a "SHOP NOW" CTA — scannable at a glance.
- **Product copy earns its keep.** "100% organic cotton for a soft, lightweight feel" (Boatneck Twisted Top) and "extra-fine ribbed knit" (Soft Stretch U-Neck Tank) are actual product details, not empty adjectives.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact across the full render.
- **Sustainability callout is present.** The impact report block at the bottom reinforces the brand's positioning for subscribers who care about that.

## What's weak

- **Zero pricing.** Not a single dollar or discount figure appears anywhere. Coming off the June 15 "Ends Tonight: 30% Off" closer, subscribers who joined for value are given nothing to act on.
- **"SHOP NOW" is the only CTA copy, used four times.** No specificity — every link reads identically, so the eye stops registering them after the first.
- **Subject is a coin-flip trivia question, not a value proposition.** "Tees Or Tanks?" generates no reason to click; it's the kind of subject line that gets archived on a busy morning.
- **No social proof visible.** No star ratings, no review count, no "bestseller" badge — contrast with the "10/10 no notes" send (June 17) which at least had the subject as an implicit review signal.
- **Hero subheadline adds nothing.** "Whichever side you're on, these styles belong in your wardrobe. All winners here." is brand filler that delays the scroll to actual products.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add prices (and a member price if applicable) to the product grid.** This is the single biggest unlock — it turns four product thumbnails into a shopping decision. Even showing "$58 → $45 for members" on one item changes the email's job from awareness to conversion.
- 2. **Differentiate the four CTA buttons.** Replace every "SHOP NOW" with something product-specific: "Shop the Crew," "Shop the Tank," etc. Four identical CTAs train the eye to ignore them.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero subheadline as a concrete claim.** "Our four most-reached-for summer basics — starting at $38" beats a bland "all winners" reassurance.
- 4. **Add a bestseller or review signal to at least one product.** A "(1,247 reviews)" line under the Soft Stretch U-Neck Tank costs nothing and materially increases click confidence.
- 5. **Sharpen the subject line.** The current subject is fun in concept but soft on specificity.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Organic cotton, ultra-light feel — starting at $38.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is blank:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Screen readers announce the window title; a whitespace-only value produces a meaningless announcement. Should be the email subject line or brand name.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">
```
Missing `lang="en"` prevents assistive technology from selecting the correct language profile.

**`@import` for custom font is stripped by Gmail:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail (webmail and Android app) does not support `@import` in `<style>` blocks. `MaisonNeue-Light` and `Maison Neue` will silently fall back to Arial across Gmail — the largest email client by volume. This is an architectural limitation of the Klaviyo font delivery method, not a bug, but should be confirmed as an accepted trade-off.

**All link states suppress visual affordance:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:link {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:active {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
a:hover {color:#000;text-decoration:none}
```
Links are visually indistinguishable from body text. WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.1 requires non-color differentiation for links. The `a:visited` suppression also removes the standard visited-state cue.

**`h4` mobile font-size drops to 10px:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  h4 { font-size: 10px !important; line-height: 1.3 !important }
}
```
10px is below the 14px minimum recommended for body copy and will likely be illegible on high-DPI mobile screens.
