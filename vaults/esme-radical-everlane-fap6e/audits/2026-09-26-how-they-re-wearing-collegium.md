# How They’re Wearing Collegium
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's brand-story follow-up to "A Bit More About The Limited-Edition Collection" (09-24), and it's a much stronger execution than that one. The UGC/editorial format — two named real people in real locations, styled quotes about how they wear the shoes, plus detail shots of the footwear itself — is genuinely engaging and gives the Collegium collection actual context instead of just another product grid. The hero is a strong, moody image of a model against an off-road vehicle with clean COLLEGIUM X EVERLANE type overlay.
- The problem is this email has zero commercial urgency: no discount, no price, no "limited-edition" reminder beyond the small subhead. It's a strong brand/content play but a weak conversion play, and given how hard this brand has leaned on 30%-off messaging all week (09-25, 09-26 sends), a full-price story email right now reads like a deliberate cooldown beat, not a mistake.
- Judgement: well-crafted content, correctly sequenced after the launch/story emails, but it will underperform on clicks relative to the discount sends in this same run.

## What's working

- Hero image is a real, well-composed editorial shot with legible logo lockup ("COLLEGIUM X EVERLANE") stamped directly on the photo.
- Two distinct personas (DJ/content creator, interior designer) with named captions and personal quotes ground the shoe in real use-cases — strong social-proof signal.
- Detail shots (socks/loafers close-up, outfit repeats) reinforce product texture and fit without needing extra copy.
- Repeated "SHOP THIS STYLE" / "SHOP THE COLLECTION" CTAs are legible black-text-on-white, consistent, and appear right after each persona block — good CTA cadence.
- No render bugs — clean image loads, no overlapping text, no broken layout.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the email — no %/$ off, no urgency, nothing that differentiates this from a blog post.
- CTA copy ("SHOP THIS COLLECTION," "SHOP THIS STYLE") is generic and repeated three times with no variation or product specificity.
- Sustainability blurb + "READ MORE" block near the footer is off-topic filler that dilutes an otherwise tight narrative.
- Subject promises "How They're Wearing Collegium" but nothing above the fold signals price, availability, or why to act now — pure top-of-funnel.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a visible price or offer signal somewhere in the hero or first CTA block — even "Now available" or a specific price point would give this a reason to act today, not just admire.
- 2. Replace generic "SHOP THIS STYLE" CTA copy with the actual product name (e.g., "SHOP THE COLLEGIUM LOAFER") so each persona block drives to a specific, identifiable item.
- 3. Cut or move the sustainability/"READ MORE" block — it breaks the narrative momentum right before the footer and adds no purchase intent.
- 4. Consider trimming to one persona + one CTA if this is meant to be a quick mobile read; two full stories plus a closing CTA makes for a long scroll with no urgency payoff at the end.
