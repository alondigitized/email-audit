# The Price Changed. The Quality Didn’t.
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth "Better Prices, Still Everlane" send in a week, and the fatigue shows: the subject "The Price Changed. The Quality Didn't." is just a rephrase of 8/13's "We Lowered Our Prices" and 8/14's "Your Favorites At Better Prices," with none of the specificity that made 8/14 the week's best performer. Visually it reverts to the plain product-story template — hero shot, six-item flat lay, two lifestyle shots, "Shop All" — with the price message demoted to a small tan footer block instead of leading with actual numbers.
- The product imagery itself is clean and on-brand (minimalist styling, gender-neutral tees/dresses/knits), but there's no offer to anchor a click: no percentage, no dollar figure, nothing beyond "better prices" as a vibe. After four straight days of this beat, a subscriber who opened 8/13 or 8/14 has already seen the pitch and gets no new information here.
- Judgement: a competently produced but redundant send — mid-pack at best, and actively weaker than 8/14 because it strips out the price tiers that email finally added.

## What's working

- Hero image and product grid are clean, well-lit, and consistent with Everlane's minimalist visual identity.
- Clear "Shop Now" and "Shop All" CTAs are visually distinct black buttons, easy to spot.
- No render bugs — images load fully, no overlapping text or broken layout.
- Sustainability blurb ("We don't just say we're better") adds brand-trust texture without cluttering the main flow.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere in the render — just "Better Prices, Still Everlane," with zero dollar amounts or percentages, a step back from 8/14's price-tier breakdown.
- Subject line is the fourth variation on the same "prices are lower" theme this week; nothing distinguishes it from 8/12–8/14.
- Category copy ("Tees, dresses, and knits in a fresh rotation") is generic and doesn't tie to the subject's price claim at all — hero and body feel like two different emails stitched together.
- Price message is buried two-thirds down the email, after two full lifestyle photo blocks, so a scrolling reader hits generic product shots before any pricing context.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put an actual number in the hero: replace the vague "Better Prices, Still Everlane" footer with a real price comparison (e.g., "Now $38, was $48") right under the hero image, not buried below the lifestyle shots — this is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Stop running the same beat five times in a week — rotate in a genuinely different template (new arrivals, size/fit guide, review roundup) for the next send to avoid list fatigue.
- 3. Tie the hero copy to the subject line — if the subject leads with "price," the immediate sub-head should show the price, not "Tees, dresses, and knits in a fresh rotation."
- 4. Move the sustainability/quality block earlier or cut it — it's doing double duty with the price message and dilutes both.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Tees & Dresses, Now From $28`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `See what dropped in price this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh rotation, lower price tags.`
