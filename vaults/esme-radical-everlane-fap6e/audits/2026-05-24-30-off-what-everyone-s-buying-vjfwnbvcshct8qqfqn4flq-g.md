# 30% Off What Everyone’s Buying
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's fifth Memorial Day execution in five days — same 30% sitewide offer, now repositioned as a "best-sellers" / social-proof play. The subject line "30% Off What Everyone's Buying" promises a curated bestseller edit, and the email half-delivers: there's a 6-product grid under "The styles that don't stay in stock for long," which is the right merchandising hook. But the hero image is the exact same yellow-dress shot Everlane has been recycling all week, so the "what everyone's buying" promise feels like a relabel rather than a fresh edit.
- The creative is clean — Everlane's house style is intact, the grid is well-composed, the second lifestyle shot ("You Need These") adds a nice editorial beat. But by send five of the same offer, the diminishing returns are obvious. There's no new news here for anyone who opened any of the previous four.
- Verdict: competent execution of a tired campaign. The bestseller framing is the strongest angle Everlane has tried this week — but they don't commit to it (no product names, no rankings, no "#1 selling" callouts). Solid B email, fifth helping of the same meal.

## What's working

- Subject pivot to social proof ("What Everyone's Buying") is the freshest angle this week.
- Bestseller grid of 6 products gives real merchandising substance below the hero.
- "Don't stay in stock for long" copy adds credible scarcity without screaming.
- Clean visual hierarchy — eye lands on 30% OFF SITEWITE, then SHOP NOW button, then grid.
- Secondary lifestyle shot breaks up the product grid nicely.

## What's weak

- Hero is the same recycled yellow-dress shot from earlier this week — undermines the "bestseller" framing.
- No product names, prices, or "#1 best-seller" labels on the grid — the social proof is asserted, not shown.
- Fifth 30%-off send in five days; offer fatigue is real.
- "You Need These" is a weak, generic header for the secondary block.
- "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" CTA contradicts the "bestsellers" thesis — mixed signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the bestseller framing** — label each grid product ("#1 Bestseller," "Back in stock," sell-through stat). The whole email leans on a promise the creative doesn't fulfill.
- 2. **Swap the hero** — use a real top-selling item, not the dress that's been the hero since Wednesday. Subscribers notice.
- 3. **Add prices on the grid** — bestseller emails live or die on showing the discounted price, not just "30% off sitewide."
- 4. **Replace "You Need These"** with something specific — "New for Memorial Day" or "Just-Dropped Bestsellers."
- 5. **Tighten the subject** for mobile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Bestsellers, Now 30% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `#1 This Week: 30% Off Sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six pieces selling out fast — sale ends Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `What 10,000 customers bought this week. All 30% off.`
