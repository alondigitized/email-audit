# The Jackets Lineup
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-built editorial send with no offer. The hero is a model in a brown waxed jacket with "Jacket Required" overlaid and a full-width black SHOP NOW bar. The four-product zig-zag below it is easy to scan, and each jacket gets a short fabric and fit description.
- It's also the second jacket email from Everlane on 2026-09-30. "New Jackets" went out that day and this one is called "The Jackets Lineup", so the two are nearly the same message. After the 09-27 to 09-29 run of 30%-off sweater sends, a subscriber who isn't shopping for outerwear has little reason to open another jackets email. "Meet The Way-High® Jean" (09-30) at least had a price cut. This one has only product and a cotton-sourcing story.
- Verdict: a polished catalog send that could easily have been folded into "New Jackets". It's fine, but it adds little.

## What's working

- The hero is strong: one editorial image, a short "Jacket Required" line and a high-contrast black CTA bar that is visible immediately.
- The alternating image/text zig-zag gives clear hierarchy and rhythm, and nothing overlaps or is broken.
- Each product block has a useful detail: "PFC-free water resistance", "raglan sleeves", "hidden button front", "contrast canvas collar".
- The intro line, "From rain-ready cotton twill to soft corduroy", sets the season and the range of styles in one sentence.
- The organic-cotton block backs up the brand's sustainability positioning without crowding the products.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, no price and no member benefit anywhere, so nothing creates a reason to click today.
- Every CTA is a generic "SHOP NOW", repeated five times. None names the product or gives a price.
- The product blocks show no prices, colors or sizes, so the shopper has to click to find out if the jackets are in range.
- It overlaps heavily with that morning's "New Jackets" send, and the cadence feels repetitive.
- The footer is a large stack of links, and the legal text is very small and low-contrast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook. Put a price on each product ("$198") or a single member offer, and cut the generic organic-cotton block to make room.
- 2. Make the CTAs specific: "Shop the Mac Coat", "Shop the Cord Jacket". Drop the repeated "Shop Now".
- 3. Don't send two jacket emails on the same day. If you do, make this one the curated "pick by weather" version, such as rain, cold or layering, instead of repeating the new-arrivals list.
- 4. Add a social-proof line to at least one product, like "Best-seller" or a review count.
- 5. Shorten the footer nav to the two or three links that matter.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Which jacket for which weather?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 jackets, 1 for every forecast`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rain-ready twill, soft corduroy and the new mac coat.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Organic cotton, PFC-free water resistance, roomy pockets.`
