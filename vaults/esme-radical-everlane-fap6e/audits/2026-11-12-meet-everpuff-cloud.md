# Meet Everpuff™ Cloud
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand product launch for Everlane's new Everpuff™ Cloud puffers, and it has no price anywhere. The hero is a model in a black cropped puffer over a taupe quarter-zip, with "Cloud Cover" in thin white type on a grey concrete wall. Below it are two stacked modules, one for the Short Puffer and one for the Long Puffer, each with a detail crop beside a full-length shot. The layout is tidy and easy to scan.
- Nothing in the email gives a reader a reason to act today. There is no price, no offer, no deadline and no stock cue. This is a $200-plus outerwear item. Everlane's own recent sends led with numbers: "The $68 Cashmere Slim Crew" on 11/11 and the $68/$78/$88 fleece prices in "Keep the Fleece" on 11/12. This one drops that discipline. The "Better Gift Guide" banner also pulls attention away from the puffer story.
- Verdict: a pretty, competent product announcement that is soft on commerce. It would sell more with a price on the page and one clear hero CTA.

## What's working

- The hero is strong. The model, the styling and the puffer silhouette are clear, and the black "SHOP NOW" bar sits directly under it.
- The two-product structure is easy to follow. The short and long versions are labeled, with one line of benefit copy each.
- The detail-crop and full-body image pairs show the hood and the fit, which helps with a bulky product.
- The copy has specific product facts: recycled Re:Down®, a removable hood, an adjustable hem and inner cuffs.
- The black buttons are consistent and high-contrast, and the whitespace gives the email a calm, premium feel.

## What's weak

- There is no price on either puffer. In a send that follows several price-led emails, that is a missed chance and it adds friction.
- "Cloud Cover" on the hero is thin, white and low-contrast against the light grey wall, so it is hard to read.
- The three "SHOP NOW" buttons all use the same generic label. None says which puffer it leads to or names the product.
- The "Better Gift Guide" banner (maroon, "Now at better prices") introduces a second message and a second set of CTAs right after the product pitch.
- There is no social proof, no reviews, no size or color information, and no urgency or seasonal deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add the price to the hero and to each product block, for example "From $XXX". It is the most useful change you can make, and it matches the price-forward pattern this brand has been using.
- 2. Make the CTAs specific: "Shop the Short Puffer" and "Shop the Long Puffer". Cut the generic label.
- 3. Put the hero headline on a darker overlay or a bolder weight so "Cloud Cover" can be read.
- 4. Add a proof point such as a review count or star rating, plus a warmth or temperature rating if there is one.
- 5. Move the Gift Guide banner below the footer nav, or cut it, so the puffer is the only ask.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
