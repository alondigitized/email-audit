# All New Styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- After twelve consecutive sale emails in nine days, Everlane tries to run an editorial "New Arrivals" send — and then buries it under the exact same "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES (ALREADY UP TO 70% OFF!) / SHOP NOW" banner that has topped every email since June 26th. The move is smart in concept: subscribers are burned out on percentage-off beats, and a product-discovery email would've felt like a genuine reset. But the banner torches that intent before the subscriber even processes the "New Arrivals" headline. They open, see the familiar sale banner, and mentally file this as email thirteen of the same promo.
- The body itself is competent Everlane: a clean 3×3 product grid, a short tagline ("The best styles of the season just arrived"), a clear CTA, and a values section plugging their impact report. The photography is on-brand — soft neutrals, minimalist styling, contemporary silhouettes. Nothing broken. But competent execution of a contradictory brief is still a contradiction.
- The subject line is the worst of the entire run. "All New Styles" is 14 characters of nothing — no urgency, no specificity, no hook. After twelve promotional emails, a vague subject makes the open a coin flip at best.

## What's working

- The 3×3 product grid is clean and well-styled; the minimalist photography is consistent with Everlane's brand.
- "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" is the clearest, most verb-specific CTA in the recent run.
- No render bugs — layout is tight and mobile-proportioned throughout.
- The sustainability section is a smart brand-values moment that other promotional sends have skipped entirely.
- The footer nav (Shop Women / Shop Men / New Arrivals / Best-Sellers) gives secondary navigation without visual noise.

## What's weak

- The sale banner at the top completely undermines the editorial "New Arrivals" premise — this email can't decide what it is.
- "All New Styles" is the weakest subject line in the entire nine-day run: vague, zero urgency, no differentiation from the sale cadence around it.
- No prices or discounts shown on the product grid — if you're going to show new arrivals alongside a sale banner, the reader needs to know whether these items are on sale or full price.
- The 3×3 grid has no product names, no call-outs, no hero item — it reads as a catalog page, not a curated recommendation.
- Thirteenth email in nine days; open fatigue is real, and the weakest subject of the run lands here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the sale banner or commit to the editorial.** If this is a New Arrivals email, remove the clearout banner — it's already in inbox history, subscribers know the sale exists. Let this send breathe as a product moment. Alternatively, drop the "New Arrivals" frame entirely and just run another sale email with a stronger subject.
- 2. **Name a hero product.** Pull one item from the grid, give it a headline, a brief descriptor, and a direct link. Everlane's June 29th "On Shorts Notice" edit worked because it committed to a single category; this email commits to nothing.
- 3. **Show prices or price deltas.** Even "starting at $38" or "new arrivals, not on sale" manages expectations and converts curiosity into a click decision.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject.** The current subject is the biggest lever.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: the pieces worth full price`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh drops while the sale runs`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Neutrals, denim, and sandals — just in. Shop before they go to sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Nine new styles, zero percent off — here's what's worth it anyway.`
- 5. **Add one product callout to the grid.** Bold the name of one item, add a two-word descriptor ("linen trousers," "leather sandal"), and link it specifically. This alone would lift click rate on a product-discovery email.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

After twelve consecutive sale emails in nine days, Everlane tries to run an editorial "New Arrivals" send — and then buries it under the exact same "SUMMER CLEAROUT SALE / EXTRA 20% OFF SALE STYLES (ALREADY UP TO 70% OFF!) / SHOP NOW" banner that has topped every email since June 26th. The move is smart in concept: subscribers are burned out on percentage-off beats, and a product-discovery email would've felt like a genuine reset. But the banner torches that intent before the subscriber even processes the "New Arrivals" headline. They open, see the familiar sale banner, and mentally file this as email thirteen of the same promo.

The body itself is competent Everlane: a clean 3×3 product grid, a short tagline ("The best styles of the season just arrived"), a clear CTA, and a values section plugging their impact report. The photography is on-brand — soft neutrals, minimalist styling, contemporary silhouettes. Nothing broken. But competent execution of a contradictory brief is still a contradiction.

The subject line is the worst of the entire run. "All New Styles" is 14 characters of nothing — no urgency, no specificity, no hook. After twelve promotional emails, a vague subject makes the open a coin flip at best.

## 2. What worked

- The 3×3 product grid is clean and well-styled; the minimalist photography is consistent with Everlane's brand.
- "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" is the clearest, most verb-specific CTA in the recent run.
- No render bugs — layout is tight and mobile-proportioned throughout.
- The sustainability section is a smart brand-values moment that other promotional sends have skipped entirely.
- The footer nav (Shop Women / Shop Men / New Arrivals / Best-Sellers) gives secondary navigation without visual noise.

## 3. What didn't

- The sale banner at the top completely undermines the editorial "New Arrivals" premise — this email can't decide what it is.
- "All New Styles" is the weakest subject line in the entire nine-day run: vague, zero urgency, no differentiation from the sale cadence around it.
- No prices or discounts shown on the product grid — if you're going to show new arrivals alongside a sale banner, the reader needs to know whether these items are on sale or full price.
- The 3×3 grid has no product names, no call-outs, no hero item — it reads as a catalog page, not a curated recommendation.
- Thirteenth email in nine days; open fatigue is real, and the weakest subject of the run lands here.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Kill the sale banner or commit to the editorial.** If this is a New Arrivals email, remove the clearout banner — it's already in inbox history, subscribers know the sale exists. Let this send breathe as a product moment. Alternatively, drop the "New Arrivals" frame entirely and just run another sale email with a stronger subject.
2. **Name a hero product.** Pull one item from the grid, give it a headline, a brief descriptor, and a direct link. Everlane's June 29th "On Shorts Notice" edit worked because it committed to a single category; this email commits to nothing.
3. **Show prices or price deltas.** Even "starting at $38" or "new arrivals, not on sale" manages expectations and converts curiosity into a click decision.
4. **Rewrite the subject.** The current subject is the biggest lever.

   - **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: the pieces worth full price`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh drops while the sale runs`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Neutrals, denim, and sandals — just in. Shop before they go to sale.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Nine new styles, zero percent off — here's what's worth it anyway.`

5. **Add one product callout to the grid.** Bold the name of one item, add a two-word descriptor ("linen trousers," "leather sandal"), and link it specifically. This alone would lift click rate on a product-discovery email.
