# 30% Off All Natural Styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's sixth consecutive Memorial Day email in six days, and the creative strain is showing. Today's pivot to "natural styles" is the thinnest yet: the subject promises a curated category, but the hero banner reads "30% OFF SITEWIDE" in the same layout the brand has used all week. The category pivot is a label, not a real editorial choice.
- The genuinely interesting story in this email is buried: "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" sits in the third content block, below the fold on most mobile devices. That's a materially better offer than the hero 30%, and Everlane appears to have forgotten it's there. A subscriber who opens, scans the hero, and bounces has missed the best deal in the email.
- The wit in the body copy — "the only plastic here is your credit card" — is the cleanest creative line Everlane has written all week. It deserved a bigger stage than third-paragraph body text on a sixth-day re-send.

## What's working

- **Body copy lands**: "Better for you always. The only plastic here is your credit card." is sharp, on-brand, and earns attention. Rare moment of voice in an otherwise template-y send.
- **Hero image coheres with subject**: The knit/mesh natural-fiber product shot actually aligns with "natural styles" — the only send this week where the image and subject hook match.
- **30% offer is unambiguous in the hero**: Large, high-contrast text, single "SHOP NOW" CTA, no clutter in the hero block.
- **Yellow new-arrivals section breaks monotony**: The color shift from the cream hero to the bold yellow creates a visual rhythm that prevents the scroll from going dead.

## What's weak

- **70% off sale is buried below the fold**: The strongest offer in the email — "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" — sits third, where most mobile openers never scroll. If you have 70%, lead with 70%.
- **Subject vs. hero mismatch**: "30% Off All Natural Styles" implies a curated category; "30% OFF SITEWIDE" in the hero immediately defuses that positioning. The creative wrapper is thin.
- **No deadline anywhere visible**: "Memorial Day Event" implies urgency by calendar, but no "ends Monday" or "48 hours left" is anywhere in the render. Compare to the May 23 denim send, which leaned harder on scarcity signals — that one scored a 9.
- **CTA copy is completely generic**: All three CTAs are "SHOP NOW" or "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" — no connection to the natural styles hook or the specific offer tier.
- **No social proof, no product specificity**: No reviews, no bestseller callouts, no featured item. The "You Need These" section names no product, links to no specific thing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the block order — lead with 70% off sale**: The strongest offer should be the hero. Run "Up to 70% Off Sale Styles" at the top; let 30% off new arrivals be the secondary. A subscriber comparison-shopping Memorial Day sales will click on 70%, not 30%.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero**: One line — "Ends Monday" — triples the urgency without changing anything else. This is the easiest one-word-lift in email.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero CTA to echo the category**: "Shop Natural Styles" instead of "SHOP NOW" keeps the subject-to-click arc coherent.
- 4. **Promote the "only plastic here is your credit card" line to the hero subhead**: It's the best copy in the email. It's currently invisible to non-scrollers.
- 5. **Add one featured product with a direct link**: A single named product ("The Relaxed Linen Shirt, now $X") in the hero block would give click-through a specific destination beyond the homepage.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only whitespace. Some email clients (notably Apple Mail and Gmail web) display the `<title>` value as a tooltip or in the tab bar. Should be set to the email subject or left intentionally blank with no whitespace — a bare space can trigger unexpected rendering in some parsers.

**Inconsistent font name casing across heading levels**
```css
h1 { font-family: "MaisonNeue-Light", Arial, ... }    /* no space */
h2 { font-family: "Maison Neue", Arial, ... }          /* space */
h3 { font-family: "Maison Neue", Arial, ... }
h4 { font-family: "Maison Neue", Arial, ... }
```
`"MaisonNeue-Light"` and `"Maison Neue"` are different CSS font-family strings. If the Klaviyo font CDN serves both under separate names, h1 may fall back to Arial while h2–h4 render in the custom typeface, creating a visible inconsistency. Verify the actual font name returned by `https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css`.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { /* most layout rules */ }
@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px) { .hide { ... } }
```
The `.hide` class triggers at 600px while all other responsive rules trigger at 480px. On devices 481px–600px wide (common for small Android devices and foldables), the `.hide` class fires but column stacking and padding overrides do not — potential layout breakage in that viewport range.

**External font CDN dependency**
`@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` — corporate email proxies and aggressive privacy tools (e.g., Apple Mail Privacy Protection) may block external stylesheet imports. Fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) is present and adequate, so this is low severity.
