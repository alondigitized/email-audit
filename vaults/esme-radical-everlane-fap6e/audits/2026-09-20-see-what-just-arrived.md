# See What Just Arrived
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent, on-brand "New Arrivals" send — a hero shot of a model in an olive tank, a nine-SKU new-arrivals grid, and the now-familiar "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner Everlane has run in nearly every recent send (2026-09-20 "Go Red," 2026-09-17 "Corduroy Has Entered the Chat"). Nothing is broken, the layout is clean, and the hero is unambiguous, but the subject line "See What Just Arrived" is generic and does almost no work — it's the same vague new-arrivals framing Everlane could send any week of the year, with no product, color, or fabric hook to anchor it.
- The bigger issue is repetition fatigue: this is at least the fourth email in the recent run to close with the identical 15%-off-sale-styles banner and impact-report CTA. A subscriber who's opened the last few Everlane sends has seen this exact bottom half of the email already. The top (hero + grid) is doing the differentiation work here, and it's decent, but generic subject lines paired with a copy-pasted lower funnel keep this in solid-but-unremarkable territory.

## What's working

- Clean single hero shot with clear "New Arrivals" overlay and a high-contrast black "SHOP NOW" button.
- Nine-SKU grid gives a real sense of the new tank/sweater assortment, including a second on-model shot to break up flat-lays.
- "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner is a concrete, unambiguous offer with two clear CTAs (Shop Women / Shop Men).
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly with no overlap or placeholder content.

## What's weak

- Subject "See What Just Arrived" is generic and interchangeable with any new-arrivals email Everlane has ever sent — no color, fabric, or product hook.
- The 15%-off banner, impact-report block, and footer nav are near-identical to multiple recent sends (2026-09-20 "Go Red," 2026-09-17 "Corduroy"), so repeat openers see the same bottom half again.
- No preview/preheader text visible — a missed chance to complement the subject with something concrete (e.g., naming a specific new piece).
- Grid has zero product names, prices, or "up to 70% off" tie-in near the top — shoppers must click through blind to know what anything costs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the subject for something that names the actual hero product (the olive tank) or a specific category, since "just arrived" is content-free and this brand already leans on vague framing too often.
- 2. Add real preview text that complements rather than restates the subject — currently there's nothing to differentiate the inbox line.
- 3. Vary the bottom-funnel banner/impact-report combo on at least every other send so repeat subscribers aren't seeing byte-identical creative.
- 4. Add a price or "from $X" under 2-3 grid items to reduce click friction for price-sensitive shoppers.
