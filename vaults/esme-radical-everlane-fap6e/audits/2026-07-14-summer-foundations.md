# Summer Foundations
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- "Summer Foundations" is Everlane's seventh consecutive editorial send since July 7 — and the format is now fully codified: wordmark, lifestyle hero, single-sentence subhead, 4-item product grid (image left, copy right, "SHOP NOW"), footer nav. The creative is clean and competent, with the most substantive product copy since the "Best-Selling Bags" send on July 13. Material details are doing real work here — "cut on the bias," "breathable Masters of That Fibre Mark," "soft Butterknit" — and that's a genuine differentiator from the purely aspirational copy in "What Are We Wearing?" (July 12) and "One Dress. Multiple RSVPs." (July 11).
- The email's problem is structural: it's the seventh send in seven days with the same template, no offer, and no urgency. "Summer Foundations" is a perfectly decent email that would score higher if it had arrived after a two-day rest. Instead it arrives as one more entry in a run that is training subscribers to scroll past Everlane without opening.
- Clear judgement: a well-executed product showcase with good copy, worn down by cadence fatigue and an absence of anything to act on.

## What's working

- **Product copy earns its place.** Each product card has a distinct 2-sentence description that names a specific material, cut, or use case — more useful than generic lifestyle language.
- **Visual hierarchy is sound.** Hero → subhead bridge copy → product grid → footer links, all in the right order. The eye lands exactly where it should.
- **No render issues.** All four product images load, text is legible, no overlapping elements, no broken layouts.
- **Subject line is clean and short.** "Summer Foundations" at 18 characters is immediately scannable; no spam signals.
- **Seasonal relevance.** Bottoms-as-foundation for summer outfits is a credible July editorial angle — not evergreen filler.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no reason to click today vs. tomorrow.** Seven editorial sends in a row with zero discounts or promotions makes "SHOP NOW" feel decorative. Compare to July 13's "Best-Selling Bags," which at least had the hook of customer quotes to add social proof.
- **"SHOP NOW" × 5 is indistinguishable from noise.** The hero CTA and all four product CTAs are identical. A reader who wants the Slip Skirt in Silk Charmeuse has no signal that clicking will take them there specifically.
- **Daily cadence is eroding open rates.** This is the seventh Everlane email in seven days. Even strong editorial content loses impact at that frequency.
- **No social proof or ratings.** Yesterday's Bags email anchored each card with a real customer quote — this email drops that element and is weaker for it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Break the daily cadence.** Skip a day. One email skipped recovers more open rate than three additional sends earn in clicks.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs.** Replace "SHOP NOW" under each card with the product name: "Shop the Slip Skirt" / "Shop the Barrel-Leg Pant." Specificity drives clicks.
- 3. **Add one review or rating signal per card.** The July 13 send proved customer quotes work — bring them back here, or at minimum add a star rating + review count.
- 4. **Give the hero copy a point of view.** "Outfit Makers" is inert. Lead with a claim: "Starts at the bottom. Ends with a full outfit." Or name the season problem you're solving.
- 5. **Test a time-gated hook.** Even "new this week" or "limited sizes remaining in the Butterknit" would create soft urgency without a discount.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The bottoms doing the most work this summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 bottoms that go with everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Silk charmeuse, Butterknit, and organic cotton sateen — the fabrics worth dressing around.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Skirts and pants that make the rest of the outfit easy.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace. Some clients (Apple Mail, Gmail web) surface this in thread previews or tab labels. Should be set to match the subject line or a descriptive fallback.

- **Duplicate `img {}` CSS rule block** — The global `img` ruleset is declared twice in `<style>`:
  - First declaration: `border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic`
  - Second declaration: `border: 0; height: auto; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; max-width: 100%`
  
  The second block wins (cascade), dropping `-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic` (Outlook image sharpness) and adding `height: auto; max-width: 100%`. This is likely intentional but the redundancy should be collapsed.

- **`h4` mobile font-size: 10px** — `@media (max-width: 480px) { h4 { font-size: 10px !important } }` — 10px is below the WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 minimum (effectively ~12px is the practical floor for readable body-level text). Klaviyo's own mobile override comment notes `15px !important` as the baseline for `.kl-text`; 10px on headings contradicts that.

- **Duplicate `.hide` media query block** — The `@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)` block for `.hide` is declared twice verbatim. Dead code; second declaration is a no-op and adds byte weight.

- **Custom font loaded from external CDN** — `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css)` for `MaisonNeue-Light`. Font imports are stripped by Gmail, Outlook, and many corporate mail filters. Fallback stack (`Arial, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, sans-serif`) is present and appropriate — confirm visual QA passes under fallback conditions.

- **No `alt` text observable** — No `<img>` elements appear in the provided excerpt. Full-source audit should confirm all images have non-empty `alt` attributes, especially for product/hero shots that carry content meaning.
