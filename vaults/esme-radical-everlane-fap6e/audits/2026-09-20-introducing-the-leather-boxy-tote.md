# Introducing the Leather Boxy Tote
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a single-product launch email done in Everlane's clean editorial style — "Bag It" hero shot, product-color grid, lifestyle detail shot, and a leather-sourcing sustainability callout. It's well-executed as a bag-launch announcement: no personalization bugs, no render breakage, clear single CTA path. But it's a pure new-product push with zero discount, zero urgency, and zero price/sizing detail — this is a discovery email, not a conversion email, and it reads that way.
- Compared to recent sends like "See What Just Arrived" (2026-09-20) or "Corduroy Has Entered the Chat" (2026-09-17), this drops the familiar "Extra 15% Off Sale Styles" banner entirely in favor of a single-SKU focus. That's a deliberate trade — tighter story, but no price incentive to act now, and bags are a lower-frequency purchase category than the tees/cords this list has been trained on lately.
- Solid craft, weak urgency. This will get looked at by anyone who likes the aesthetic, but there's little here to convert a browser into a buyer today.

## What's working

- Clean single-product hero with the bag literally being carried mid-motion — good scale/context cue for size.
- Two-color swatch grid (black on cream, black on white) immediately answers "what colors does this come in."
- Lifestyle ground-shot shows the tote's structure/shape from a different angle than the hero.
- Sustainability callout (LWG-rated leather) adds a credible, non-salesy trust signal.
- No render bugs, no broken images, consistent black-and-white brand voice throughout.

## What's weak

- No price shown anywhere in the email — a real barrier to click-to-buy intent for a considered bag purchase.
- No discount, promo, or urgency of any kind — breaks from the brand's recent "Extra 15% Off" cadence, so loyal subscribers get no incentive.
- "Bag It" overlay text on the hero image is a weak pun with no functional information (no price, no "new").
- Only one product (one bag, two colors) — for a newsletter subject that says "Introducing," there's no styling/pairing content to build desire beyond the object itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add price directly under the hero copy or near the swatches — a $100+ tote without a price is a click killer.
- 2. Give the hero overlay actual information instead of "Bag It" — e.g. "Now Available" or "New: Leather Boxy Tote" ties subject to hero visually.
- 3. Add a third lifestyle/interior shot showing the "slip pocket for the small stuff" mentioned in body copy — that's a called-out feature with no visual proof.
- 4. Consider a launch-week incentive (early-access price, free shipping threshold) to convert new-product curiosity into an actual order.
