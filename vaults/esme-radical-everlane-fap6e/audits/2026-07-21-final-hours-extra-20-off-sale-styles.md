# Final Hours: Extra 20% Off Sale Styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's fifth promotional touch in six days on the same "Extra 20% Off" mechanic — and the email itself is clean, competent, and visually consistent with the brand. The headline, CTAs, and offer are all aligned, which is a notable fix from yesterday's "Ends Tonight: 30% Off" send where the subject promised 30% and the body delivered 20%. Subject-to-body honesty is restored here.
- That said, the creative is indistinguishable from the July 16 and July 18 promo sends. The hero is a washed-out textile close-up with the same offer text stacked on top. "Final Hours" is the only new signal — and it's undercut by the fact that yesterday also said "Ends Tonight." Subscribers who have been watching this sale for five days are either already in or tuned out. The urgency framing feels earned but the well is dry.
- The email does what it needs to do for a last-call send: one offer, two CTAs, no clutter. But it adds nothing — no product, no story, no reason beyond "we're saying it again."

## What's working

- Subject and body now match exactly — "Extra 20% Off" is in both, fixing the July 20 credibility gap.
- Two split CTAs ("Shop Women's Sale" / "Shop Men's Sale") are prominent, above the fold, and unambiguous.
- "Selling fast" in the body copy is the closest this send gets to social proof — a light scarcity signal that works without being pushy.
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breakage, Everlane's minimalist aesthetic holds.
- "Final Hours" in the subject is the sharpest urgency framing this campaign has used; paired with the concrete offer, it's the strongest subject line of the five-day run.

## What's weak

- No specific end time in the email body — "Final Hours" in the subject isn't anchored anywhere in the body copy (no "ends tonight at midnight," no countdown).
- Zero product shown. The hero is an abstract textile shot. There's nothing to click *toward* — only a category page to browse.
- Fifth iteration of the identical promo structure; the email offers no new angle, product, or framing to re-engage fatigued subscribers.
- Fine print in the footer is rendered at an illegible size — exclusions, if any, are invisible in this render.
- "A reminder to shop better styles now at better prices" is filler copy that adds no information and no pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the urgency with a specific time.** "Final Hours" means nothing without "Sale ends tonight at 11:59 PM PT." Add one line in the hero or directly below the CTA. This is the single change that turns soft urgency into a real reason to act now vs. later.
- 2. **Show one product.** Pull the best-seller or a clearance hero item with a before/after price. Even one image of a specific piece gives subscribers something concrete to click toward instead of a category browse.
- 3. **Replace the body copy.** "Selling fast" is the strongest sentence in this email — lead with it. Cut "A reminder to shop better styles now at better prices" entirely; it reads like a placeholder.
- 4. **Lift the offer framing.** "Already up to 70% Off" is burying the most compelling stat — that's the number that signals real value, not "Extra 20%." Test a structure that leads with the total potential discount.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final Hours: Up to 70% Off (Plus Extra 20%)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Call — Sale Ends Tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A few hours left. These won't restock.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final hours. Up to 70% off — no code needed.`


**All link states suppress visual differentiation**
```css

```
Links are rendered identically to body text in all states. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color) — links must be distinguishable without relying on color alone. No underline or other non-color cue is provided.

**h4 mobile font-size drops to 10px**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) {

```
10px is below the 12px minimum for readable text on mobile displays. This likely affects product captions or price labels.

**Inconsistent responsive breakpoints**
Two separate breakpoint thresholds are used: `max-width: 480px` (layout) and `max-width: 600px` (`.hide` class). The 600px rule only affects visibility, but the inconsistency can cause unexpected layout at 481–600px viewport widths.

Gmail strips `@import` rules — these fonts will silently fail in Gmail (web and Android), falling back to Arial.
