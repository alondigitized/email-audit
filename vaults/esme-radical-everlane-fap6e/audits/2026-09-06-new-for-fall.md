# New for Fall
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine reset from the "End of Summer Sale" watercolor template Everlane's run since 08/01 — no olive/cream headline split, no discount-percentage stack, no blue "Under $100" banner. It's a clean 9-tile fall product grid: turtlenecks, cropped hoodie, corduroy, striped knit, dark denim, all in on-brand tan/black/olive tones. Problem is there's no offer anywhere — no %, no price, no "new arrivals starting at." Just a product wall and a "SHOP NOW" button.
- The sustainability block underneath ("We don't just say we're better—we mean it") is a nice brand-values beat but it's competing for the same single CTA slot as the product grid, and it pushes the actual navigation links (Shop Women/Men, New Arrivals, Best-Sellers) below the fold.
- Net: strong visual reset after weeks of sale-fatigue creative, but this reads as a browse email, not a buy email — fine for brand-building, weak for near-term conversion.

## What's working

- Real creative break from the six days of near-identical "End of Summer Sale" sends (08/01–09/06) — no watercolor hero, no repeated olive/cream split headline.
- Clean 3×3 product grid gives immediate visual variety (color, texture, silhouette) without clutter.
- Single unambiguous CTA ("SHOP NOW") directly under the grid, no scroll needed before the primary ask.
- No render bugs — grid images, buttons, and icons all rendered cleanly.
- Sustainability panel reinforces brand identity rather than just hammering discount language again.

## What's weak

- Zero offer or price signal anywhere in the email — after a week of 20%-off sends, dropping straight to no-discount "New Arrivals" is a jarring value-prop cliff for a subscriber trained on daily discounts.
- Subject "New for Fall" and header "New Arrivals" are generic — no product, category, or hook that differentiates this from any other retailer's fall drop email.
- Grid links to nothing specific per item — no labels, prices, or "shop the look," just a static tile wall.
- Sustainability CTA ("READ MORE") competes with "SHOP NOW" for attention and isn't commerce-focused, diluting the primary path to purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook to subject and hero — name the category or a price anchor ("Fall Knits, Just Landed" or "New Arrivals from $58") so it doesn't read as generic seasonal filler.
- 2. Label the grid — even minimal captions ("Cropped Hoodie, $88") under 2-3 hero tiles would turn this from a mood board into a shoppable email.
- 3. Move or shrink the sustainability panel — it's a fine brand beat but shouldn't out-CTA the product grid; consider moving it below the nav links instead of directly after "SHOP NOW."
- 4. Reinforce urgency/deadline if one exists — the fine print references an offer valid until 09/08 but that offer never appears in the visible body, which is a bait-and-switch reader would never see.
