# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- I opened Everlane and what I got was... a macro shot of what looks like beige fabric and a sliver of mustard something at the top corner, against what might be a wood surface. If this is the hero, it's so cropped and dark that I genuinely cannot tell what I'm looking at — is this the Way-High Jeans? A tote? A throw pillow? Everlane built its whole identity on showing me exactly what I'm buying and at what cost, so opening to an unreadable close-up with zero visible copy is a strange way to say "we're still the radical-transparency brand."

## What's working

- The warm, neutral color palette of the fabric shot is at least visually consistent with Everlane's understated aesthetic — it doesn't scream fast-fashion neon at me.
- No garish promotional clutter is fighting for attention above the fold (mostly because there's nothing visible at all, but the restraint is on-brand).

## What's weak

- **Zero readable copy above the fold** — Everlane lives and dies by its transparency narrative; there's not a single word visible to anchor me to that story.
- **The hero crop is so tight I cannot identify the product** — I own five pairs of The Way-High Jeans and I still can't tell if that's denim I'm looking at.
- **No CTA, no navigation, no offer visible** — nothing to click, nothing to act on.
- **No loyalty/account hook** — I'm a repeat buyer; there's no "welcome back" or rewards signal.
- **The image is severely underexposed** — the fabric reads as muddy gray rather than whatever the actual product color is, which defeats the entire point of showing the material.
- **No pricing or cost-breakdown story** — the one thing Everlane promised its original audience (radical transparency on markup) is invisible.

## Recommendations

- **Surface at least one line of hero copy over the image** — even a four-word headline like "The Way-High, reimagined" gives me a reason to stay. Right now I have no idea what this page is selling.
- **Pull the camera back** — show the full garment or at minimum enough of it that I can identify the product category. A close-up of fabric texture is an editorial choice that works in a lookbook, not as a homepage hero on mobile.
- **Add a transparent sticky nav with "Women / Men / Sale" tabs** — if I can't see any copy, at least let me self-route in one tap.
- **Drop a cost-breakdown banner or "our markup: X%" ribbon under the hero** — that's the brand's founding differentiator and it's completely absent from what I can see; putting it back above the fold costs nothing and re-anchors the trust story immediately.
