# 30% Off Linen, For A Limited Time
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Everlane's third execution of their Memorial Day 30%-off wave in three days — following the May 20 sitewide launch and the May 21 dresses-and-sandals re-send. The playbook is visible by now: keep the body the same, rotate the subject line to a new category to re-engage non-openers. The tactic is defensible but the seams are showing. Notably, the subject promises a linen-specific offer ("30% Off Linen") but the hero immediately says "30% OFF SITEWIDE" — technically a pleasant surprise, but a classic bait-and-pivot that erodes the focused editorial promise of the subject.
- The linen angle that earns the open exists only as a two-line subheadline below the hero ("Breathable European linen, designed to keep you cool. Even when it's hot outside."). Below that sits a second module for new arrivals with a "You Need These" hook, and buried at the bottom — the email's most aggressive offer: "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES." That line should be doing more work.
- Everlane's design execution is clean as ever, but this send feels like a targeting layer applied to a recycled asset, not a true linen-edit email.

## What's working

- The salmon/coral linen dress on-model immediately communicates the category — no guessing required.
- "MEMORIAL DAY EVENT" framing in the hero gives the sale legitimate seasonal anchor beyond a vague "limited time."
- "Breathable European linen, designed to keep you cool. Even when it's hot outside." is genuinely good product copy — it earns the open promise.
- The "SHOP NEW ARRIVALS" CTA in the second module broadens click intent for non-sale shoppers.
- The render is clean: no broken images, no layout breakage, no junk text visible.

## What's weak

- The subject promises linen-specific savings; the hero immediately says sitewide — the targeted framing is abandoned in the first scroll.
- "UP TO 70% OFF SALE STYLES" is buried at the bottom and materially outcompetes the 30% offer — inverting the hierarchy of the most compelling deal.
- Both primary CTAs say "SHOP NOW" — generic verb-plus-nothing that doesn't reinforce the linen frame or the sale urgency.
- This is the third email in three days on the same offer; cadence fatigue is real for anyone who opened May 20 or May 21.
- No linen product is named, priced, or featured — no specific item, no anchored "was/now" pricing to motivate the click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the linen frame in the hero.** Either change the hero text from "30% OFF SITEWIDE" to "30% OFF LINEN" to match the subject — or flip the subject to match the sitewide body. The mismatch is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. **Move the 70% off module up** — surface it as a second hero beat, not an afterthought. "Up to 70% Off Sale" under the 30% offer would pull a different buyer and dramatically increase scroll-to-click conversions.
- 3. **Replace generic CTAs with specific copy.** "Shop the Linen Edit" on the hero and "Shop Sale — Up to 70% Off" on the clearance module are both do-able with zero production lift.
- 4. **Name one hero linen piece** — model in The European Linen Dress, $89 → $62. A single anchored "was/now" price in the hero turns browse intent into purchase intent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Linen Dress You've Been Watching — 30% Off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cool, Breathable, 30% Off — Shop Linen Now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Memorial Day sale ends soon — linen styles up to 70% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `European linen from $62 — Memorial Day pricing, while it lasts.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — HTML is truncated before any `<a href>` tags are visible. The following cannot be confirmed without the full source:

- Click-tracking wrapper domains

**Flagged from visible source:**
- Custom font loaded from `https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/USb9mf/custom_fonts.css` — this is an external resource reference embedded in a `<style>` block via `@import`. `@import` in `<style>` is stripped by Gmail; the font will silently fail there regardless, but the `@import` pattern itself is suboptimal vs. a `<link>` tag (which is also stripped, but fails more predictably).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only whitespace. Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) surface the `<title>` in the browser tab or accessibility tree. Should be set to the subject line or a meaningful value.

**Missing `lang` attribute:** The `<html>` tag in the visible source carries no `lang` attribute. Screen readers will default to the OS language, which may be incorrect for international subscribers.

**Font name mismatch between heading levels:**
- `h1` → `"MaisonNeue-Light"` (no space, hyphenated)
- `h2`, `h3`, `h4` → `"Maison Neue"` (space-separated)

These are two different font-family strings. If the Klaviyo custom font CSS defines the family under one name, the other will silently fall back to `Arial`. One of these is likely wrong.

**Duplicate `img` rule block:**
Two separate `img { ... }` declarations in the same `<style>` block:
1. First block includes `-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic` but lacks `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%`
2. Second block adds `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%` but drops `-ms-interpolation-mode`

The cascade merges them, so this isn't breaking, but it indicates copy-paste debt. If a future edit removes the second block, images will lose `max-width: 100%` and overflow on mobile.

**Duplicate media query block:**
`@media screen and (min-device-width: 10px) and (max-width: 600px)` with the `.hide` rule appears twice in the truncated portion. Redundant, no functional impact.

**Inconsistent mobile breakpoints:**
- Most responsive rules trigger at `max-width: 480px`
- `.hide` / `.font12` rules trigger at `max-width: 600px`

This means elements using `.hide` disappear at widths (481px–600px) where the rest of the layout is still in desktop mode. Verify this is intentional.
