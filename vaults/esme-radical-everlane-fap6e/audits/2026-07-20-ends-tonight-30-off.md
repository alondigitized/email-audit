# Ends Tonight: 30% Off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- Everlane returns with another "Extra 20% Off" sale push — their third version of this same mechanic in four days — and the first thing a subscriber will notice is that the subject line promised "30% Off" and the email delivers "Extra 20% Off." That is not the same number, and it is not a rounding difference. It is a factual mismatch that lands somewhere between sloppy and deceptive, and it is the dominant story of this send.
- Strip that away and you have Everlane's locked-in summer template: wordmark, beach hero with text overlay, a single "SHOP SALE" CTA, two sentences of body copy, a four-tile product grid, nav footer. Executed cleanly, no render bugs. But the creative is now so routine that even the copy — "There's still plenty of summer left. These favorites? Not so much." — reads like it was pasted from a previous send because functionally, it was.
- The "Ends Tonight" urgency in the subject is the one thing that differentiates this send from the July 18 version. That deadline is real news and genuinely useful. It is also invisible inside the email body, so the moment a subscriber opens, the pressure evaporates. A strong urgency signal wasted entirely in transit.

## What's working

- **Subject urgency is concrete.** "Ends Tonight" is a credible, specific deadline — one of the stronger openers Everlane has written in this sale cycle.
- **Hero offer reads instantly.** "Extra 20% Off Sale Styles / Already Up to 70% Off" is scannable in under two seconds from the top of the render.
- **CTA is unambiguous.** Dark "SHOP SALE" pill button on a light hero — no guessing what to do next.
- **Product grid is category-diverse.** Four tiles (dresses, tees, tops, deepest discounts) let different buyer intents self-select without requiring a scroll to the nav.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject says "30% Off," body says "Extra 20% Off."** These are different offers. A subscriber who opened expecting 30% off will feel misled the moment the hero loads.
- **Deadline stated in subject, absent in body.** "Ends Tonight" never appears inside the email — no countdown, no reinforcing copy, no deadline line in the footer. The urgency is introduced and immediately abandoned.
- **Third "Extra 20% Off" in four days.** July 16, July 18, and now July 20 all run the same mechanic. Even the stacked parenthetical — "(Already Up to 70% Off)" — has appeared verbatim before. Subscriber fatigue is now a real risk.
- **"SHOP DEEPEST DISCOUNTS" tile shows shoes** — a jarring category mismatch when the top three tiles are all apparel. Nothing wrong with footwear, but the visual break breaks the rhythm.
- **Body copy does no new work.** "There's still plenty of summer left. These favorites? Not so much." is scarcity-flavored but doesn't name any product, category, or reason to act *today* specifically.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the offer mismatch — immediately.** Ship a corrected version where subject and body agree on the same number. "Ends Tonight: Extra 20% Off Sale" aligns with the email body. Sending "30% Off" in the subject and "Extra 20% Off" in the body is a trust erosion event, not just a copy error.
- 2. **Surface the deadline inside the email.** Add one line of hero copy or a footer banner: "Offer ends at midnight tonight." The subscriber paid nothing to open this — give them the one piece of information that drives action.
- 3. **Retire the "Extra 20% Off" mechanic for at least three days.** The July 18 and July 16 sends ran the same headline. Repetition kills urgency credibility — if it "ends" three times, it doesn't end.
- 4. **Replace the deepest discounts tile or reorder.** Shoes as the fourth tile after three apparel tiles reads as an afterthought. Either make it all apparel for coherence, or lead with the highest-discount category first.
- 5. **Add one specific product to the hero.** A hero image of the single best-selling sale item (name + original price + sale price) would anchor the offer and create a reason to click that "browse everything" category tiles cannot.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Hours: Up to 70% Off, Extra 20% Stacked`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale prices drop further at midnight — dresses, tees, tops.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cart items just got cheaper. Extra 20% ends tonight.`
