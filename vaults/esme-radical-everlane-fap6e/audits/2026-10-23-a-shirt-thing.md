# A Shirt Thing
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a calm, well-made editorial send, but it has no offer. A model in a sage oxford shirt fills the hero, "Shirt Tales" sits in thin type on the left, and a black SHOP NOW bar sits directly under the image. Below that are two paired-image rows, one for the heavier boyfriend-fit shirts and one for the lighter, airy cotton. A grey organic-cotton block and a plain nav close it out. It reads like Everlane: restrained and tasteful.
- The weakness is commercial. There's no price, no percentage and no deadline. The only reason to click is liking the shirts. The brand history shows the same pattern. Everlane's "Fall Essentials Event: Up to 30% Off" (10/22) and the early-access send (10/21) at least gave a reason to act. This one, the fourth Everlane send in four days, drops back to pure brand storytelling. The copy also never says which shirt is the heavy one and which is the light one, or where to look for the difference.
- Verdict: a polished, low-pressure send that reinforces the brand but is unlikely to drive urgency. It works as a mid-cycle breather after the sale emails. Used alone, it leaves conversion on the table.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and the SHOP NOW bar sits directly under it, so the path to click is obvious.
- The two-weight story (substantial vs. lighter) gives a simple reason to browse both rows.
- The pairs of full-length and close-up shots show fit and fabric, so you can judge the shirts.
- The organic cotton block adds credible values messaging without crowding the layout.
- The whitespace and type are consistent with the earlier Everlane sends, and nothing is broken.

## What's weak

- There is no price, discount or free-shipping hook anywhere, and no urgency, right after two sale sends.
- "Shirt Tales" is thin, small type on a busy part of the photo. It doesn't say anything about the product.
- All four product images link to generic "SHOP NOW" text. There are no product names, prices or colour or size cues.
- The sage shirt in the hero isn't named or shown again, so the lead product is hard to find on the other side of the click.
- The footer nav and the grey block pull attention away from the product rows and the CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete hook near the hero, such as a price ("Organic cotton shirts from $78"), a fit or return promise, or free shipping. This is the biggest lift.
- 2. Name the two products and label each row ("The Boxy Oxford, heavyweight" / "The Airy Shirt, lightweight"). Give each its own price and link.
- 3. Replace the generic "SHOP NOW" links with specific copy like "Shop the heavyweight shirt" and "Shop the airy shirt."
- 4. Make the hero headline larger and put a short promise next to the CTA.
- 5. Move the organic cotton block lower or shrink it, so the product rows stay the focus.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Two weights of organic cotton shirts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your new fall shirt, in two weights`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Boxy and substantial, or light and airy. Both made from organic cotton.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find your fit: the oversized oxford and the softer, lighter shirt.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Klaviyo-built MJML email with branded click tracking (`trk.send.everlane.com`). The QA run reports a 38% pass rate. Two link probes failed on relative redirect targets, which is probably a probe artifact. The compliance and authentication headers could not be verified because the relay doesn't expose them.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **[FAIL] Logo link** (text "Everlane"): `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M5N87B2V1ZXMA44PB4GCV49W_0` resolves to `/pages/womens?_kx=…&bxid=01KRSAQRJKNNCKD9WS2NMXDB0V&cm_flow_i…`.
- **[FAIL] "Sign Up for Texts" link**: `https://trk.send.everlane.com/l/01M5N87B2V1ZXMA44PB4GCV49W_15` resolves to `/pages/sms-signup?_kx=…`.
- **Cause:** both errors read `unknown url type`. The tracker's redirect target is a host-relative path, and the probe couldn't resolve it. Browsers resolve relative `Location` headers correctly, so these are likely false positives. A scheme-less redirect is still non-standard, so confirm with `curl -sI` against both tracking URLs.
- **Coverage gap:** only 12 of 25 links were probed, and 13 footer, utility and social links were skipped. Those links are unverified.
- **Note:** the logo link going to `/pages/womens` rather than the homepage is a destination choice, not a defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Empty `<title>`:** the source has `<title> </title>`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns=… xmlns:o=… xmlns:v=…>` has none, which hurts screen-reader language detection.
- **Missing alt text:** `https://trk.send.everlane.com/o/01M5N87B2V1ZXMA44PB4GCV49W` is the open-tracking pixel. Add `alt=""`. This is low severity.
- **Mobile heading size:** `h4` drops to `font-size: 10px !important` at `max-width: 480px`, which is a legibility risk if `h4` carries content.
- **Dark mode and Outlook:** `.ev-clay-dark` and `[data-ogsc]` handling and the `mj-outlook-group-fix` conditionals are present. No defects found.
- **Limit:** the HTML is truncated, so body-level checks such as image dimensions and table roles are incomplete.
