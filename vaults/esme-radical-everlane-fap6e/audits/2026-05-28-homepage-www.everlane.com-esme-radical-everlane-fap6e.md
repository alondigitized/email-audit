# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-05-28
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- The viewport I'm looking at is almost entirely a dark void — the hero takes up the majority of the above-the-fold real estate and renders as a near-black rectangle with no legible copy, no visible headline, and no obvious CTA. Below that, I can see what looks like a product module with black denim shorts and a nude kitten-heel sandal floating on a warm gray background. That's essentially everything this screenshot gives me. For a brand whose entire identity is built on transparency, this is a frustrating irony.

## What's working

- **Product-as-hero in the lower module:** The black denim shorts and nude kitten-heel sandal are clean, well-lit, and minimal — that's the Everlane aesthetic I know. Zero clutter, product confidence.
- **Warm gray background on the product section:** It's a better call than stark white for warm-season SKUs. The sandal especially reads well against it.
- **Mobile proportions:** The product objects are sized to be tappable — no tiny thumbnails.

## What's weak

- **The hero is a black hole — literally:** No text, no copy, no CTA, no visible imagery I can parse. A radical-transparency buyer who opens this page on her phone sees nothing meaningful above the fold. I don't know what Everlane is selling, why it matters today, or where to tap.
- **Zero transparency signals:** Everlane's whole brand promise is the cost breakdown, the factory story, the "why it costs what it costs." None of that is surfaced here. That's the one thing that would make me stay.
- **No promo or urgency whatsoever:** Even a free-shipping threshold would give me a reason to load more. The page is silent.
- **No navigation visible:** On mobile, I'd expect a visible hamburger or top nav to get me to Denim, Shoes, or Sale. I can't see one in this viewport.
- **No people:** Everlane imagery traditionally features real, plainly styled models. I see zero humans here. The shorts are just floating. I can't gauge fit, rise, or how they'd look on a real body.

## Recommendations

- **Put the factory-cost callout in the hero:** Even one line — "Jean jacket. $85. Here's the math." — would snap this page into the radical-transparency brand promise and immediately signal to people like me that this is the right tab to have open.
- **Surface a free-shipping threshold in a sticky top strip:** Something like "Free shipping over $X · Free returns" takes one line, costs nothing to render, and answers the #1 checkout anxiety before I even tap a product.
- **Add a lifestyle/fit shot to the product module:** Those shorts need a body. Even a small secondary crop showing how they fall at the thigh would reduce my return-risk anxiety.
- **Make the CTA legible above the fold:** A single button — "Shop New Arrivals" or "Shop Denim" — somewhere on the hero, any contrast color, would have given me somewhere to go before I started squinting.
