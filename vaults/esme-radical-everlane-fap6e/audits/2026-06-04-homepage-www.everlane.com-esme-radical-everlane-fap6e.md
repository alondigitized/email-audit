# Homepage snapshot · www.everlane.com · 2026-06-04
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- I opened Everlane and got a full-bleed extreme close-up of what looks like a black knit shoe — basically a macro shot of the textile, dark against a grey wall. There is zero copy above the fold, no nav, no CTA, no price, nothing. I can see a tiny "x" icon tucked in the bottom-right corner. As a radical-transparency buyer who audits cost-per-wear spreadsheets, this tells me exactly nothing — not the price, not the material, not why I should care. Everlane used to brag about the factory; now they're giving me art photography.

## What's working

- **Render quality:** The image itself is sharp and clean — no broken assets, no layout shift visible in the viewport.
- **Forced visual focus:** With literally nothing else on screen, the eye does land on the textured knit. If this is intentional product-material storytelling, the craft is there.

## What's weak

- **Zero copy above the fold:** No headline, no sub-copy, no brand claim. I have no idea what I'm looking at or why.
- **No CTA whatsoever:** No "Shop Now," "See the breakdown," "New Arrivals" — nothing to tap. Dead end for intent.
- **No nav visible:** Can't see a hamburger, logo, or category strip. If I didn't already know this was Everlane, I'd have no brand confirmation.
- **No price or transparency signal:** The whole Everlane brand promise is radical cost transparency. That promise is invisible here.
- **No returning-shopper hooks:** I have an account. I've bought The Way-High Jeans. Everlane knows this. Showing me a black blob instead of "Welcome back, Esme — new in denim" is a wasted signal.
- **Impossible to discern product or category:** Is this a shoe? A sweater? A tote? I genuinely can't tell without zooming in further.

## Recommendations

- **Put the product name and price in the hero frame:** If this is the ReKnit shoe or some new knit collection, say so. Overlay the textile name, the material story ("100% recycled nylon, $128, here's the factory cost"), and a button.
- **Add a persistent nav bar or logo lock-up at the top:** Even a minimal top-strip with the wordmark and a hamburger icon tells me I'm on Everlane and gives me an escape hatch to denim.
- **Swap abstract macro for a lifestyle-plus-callout format:** Show a model wearing the item, with the cost breakdown inset — that's the brand's core differentiator and it's completely absent here.
- **Surface a returning-member hook immediately:** "Back for more denim? New arrivals in The Way-High →" would convert me faster than any art shot.
