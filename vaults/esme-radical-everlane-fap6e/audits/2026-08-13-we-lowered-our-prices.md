# We Lowered Our Prices
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident price-drop announcement — and it's the second time in two days Everlane has led with "price" rather than product (following 8/12's "From Us At Everlane" manifesto letter). The hero shot is genuinely strong: two identical models mirrored, "Better Prices / Still Everlane" split across them, communicating the message visually without a single dollar sign. Problem is, that's also the whole problem — there is no number anywhere. No "$X off," no percentage, no "starting at $Y." For a brand that's now run two consecutive sends built entirely around "we lowered prices," the total absence of a concrete figure in either send is a real miss.
- Below the fold it reverts to the familiar 9-grid product wall, identical in structure to nearly every Everlane send in this brand history (Totes, Sweaters, Tee sends). No pricing shown on any grid item, no before/after strikethrough, nothing that lets a shopper self-select "is this actually cheaper by an amount I care about."
- Judgement: well-art-directed, poorly substantiated. Worth opening on brand recognition alone, unlikely to convert without a number attached.

## What's working

- The mirrored-model visual concept ("Better Prices" / "Still Everlane") is a smart, on-brand way to make a pricing announcement feel premium rather than discount-y.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, no broken images, no layout bugs.
- Copy block ("Same quality. Same materials. Same favorites. Only the prices have changed") reinforces the message without being salesy.
- Two clear CTAs ("Shop Now") bookend the content, both high-contrast black buttons.
- Category nav (Shop Women/Men/New Arrivals/Best-Sellers) gives a clean fallback path if the hero doesn't land.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete number anywhere — no %, no $, no "starting at." "We lowered our prices" with nothing to quantify it reads as vague, and this is the second straight day Everlane has made this same claim without a figure (8/12's "Prices don't usually go down. Ours just did.").
- The 9-item product grid shows no prices at all, so there's no way to see the actual new price vs. old price — the entire premise of the email is invisible at the point of consideration.
- No urgency or time-boundedness — reads as a permanent repricing, so there's no reason to click today vs. next week.
- Generic grid layout is identical in structure to nearly every other recent Everlane send (totes, tees, pants) — this brand is leaning hard on the "hero + 9-grid" template.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a number in the hero and subject line. "We lowered our prices" → "Now up to 20% off, permanently" (or whatever the real figure is) — this is the single biggest lever; a price claim with no price is a claim shoppers can't act on.
- 2. Add strikethrough old/new pricing under at least the hero product and ideally the grid items, so the "lower prices" claim is provable at a glance.
- 3. Consider a "starting at $X" callout on the grid section header to anchor value before the 9 items.
- 4. Add a light urgency or context cue (e.g., "New markdowns just landed") to differentiate this from a permanent-price-change announcement, which otherwise won't reward fast action.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 20% Off — New Lower Prices`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Same Everlane, Lower Price Tags`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `No sale, no code — just permanently better prices on your favorites.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tees, denim, and outerwear, now for less.`
