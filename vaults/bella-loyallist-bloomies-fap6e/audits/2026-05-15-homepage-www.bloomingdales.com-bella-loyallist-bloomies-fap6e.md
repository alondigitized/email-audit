# Homepage snapshot · www.bloomingdales.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I opened Bloomingdale's and was immediately hit with an email-signup wall that ate the entire viewport — I can barely see the nav and there's zero homepage behind it. For a brand that built its identity on the Loyallist program, serving up a "new subscriber" acquisition modal to someone who's been collecting points since Millennial was a marketing term is a real slap. This page reads like an email-list vendor is in charge, not a luxury retailer who knows its regulars.

## What's working

- **"GET 15% OFF" headline** — big, clean serif type, immediately legible on mobile; if you're a new visitor it's genuinely appealing
- **"SUBSCRIBE NOW" button** — no ambiguity, solid button, tappable target is large enough on phone
- **Clean modal layout** — the hierarchy (offer → value prop → input → CTA) is well-structured; no clutter inside the overlay itself
- **Sign In button in the header** — at least they surfaced it; a returning member can escape the modal via that route

## What's weak

- **Full-viewport modal for existing members** — I'm a Loyallist. I have a card. I've spent more than I'd like to admit at this counter. Hitting me with a new-subscriber popup is tone-deaf and wastes my first five seconds on the site
- **"Valid for new email subscribers only" fine print** — buried at the bottom in tiny type; the 15% promise is hollow for anyone who's already in the system
- **Zero Loyallist surface area above the fold** — no points balance, no tier badge, no "Welcome back, Bella," no member event preview — nothing that acknowledges I exist
- **Entire homepage is invisible** — hero image, category nav, any current promotion or campaign, new arrivals — all of it is hidden behind this wall; I have no idea what Bloomie's wants me to shop right now
- **No urgency or seasonality** — the copy gives me no reason to act now vs. next week; nothing time-bounded, no event hook

## Recommendations

- **Detect login state and suppress the modal for signed-in Loyallist members** — show me a personalized "Welcome back, Bella — here's what's new in your saved designers" strip instead; this is table stakes for a loyalty program with tiers
- **For non-logged-in returning visitors, swap the full-page block for a slim persistent banner** — a bottom sheet that says "15% off your first email" with a dismiss-X respects my time and doesn't hide the merchandise I came to see
- **Put a Loyallist reminder in the hero** — even a subtle "Your next Reward is $X away" or "Friends & Family starts May 20" banner creates immediate value for someone like me
- **Show me the actual homepage** — swap or dismiss the modal within 3 seconds automatically on mobile, or let me tap the logo to pass through; any campaign, any editorial, any designer spotlight is better than a white overlay
