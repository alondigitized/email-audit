# Homepage snapshot · www.bloomingdales.com · 2026-05-29
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- I've been a Loyallist for years, and the first thing Bloomingdale's shows me when I open the site is a full-screen email capture begging me to subscribe for a 15% discount — one that explicitly excludes me because I'm already in the system. The actual homepage, whatever's happening behind this overlay, is completely invisible. Right now Bloomingdales.com reads less like a luxury department store and more like a DTC popup brand running their first Klaviyo flow.

## What's working

- **"GET 15% OFF" headline** — the type is large, clean, and readable on mobile. If I were a brand-new visitor, this would actually be compelling.
- **"SUBSCRIBE NOW" CTA** — no ambiguity, no cute wordplay. Clear button, clear action.
- **Fine-print disclosure** — "Valid for new email subscribers only" is at least present. It's buried, but it's there, which is more honest than some brands manage.
- **Logo and nav chrome** — the hamburger, logo, search bar, and bag icon are all visible above the overlay, so the site isn't completely locked.

## What's weak

- **The entire above-the-fold view is a modal.** I can't see a single product, hero image, or campaign. This is my homepage visit — I came to shop, not to subscribe.
- **The 15% offer is actively hostile to me.** I'm an existing Loyallist member and email subscriber. This offer is invalid for me, and the fine print says so. Opening with something that explicitly excludes your best customers is a miss.
- **Zero Loyallist acknowledgment.** There's a "Sign In" button in the corner but no "Welcome back, Loyallist" hook, no points balance teaser, nothing that says this brand knows who I am.
- **"INTRODUCING" at the bottom** — I can just barely see it, which means there IS a campaign hero below, but it's being smothered. Whatever they're launching, nobody's seeing it on first load.
- **No current season or trend signal** — I can't tell from this screen if it's a Memorial Day sale, a summer arrival push, or anything. The modal strips all editorial context.

## Recommendations

- **Kill the full-viewport interstitial for logged-in users.** If I'm signed in — or even if the site recognizes a returning cookie — suppress this modal entirely. Show me the homepage. Reserve the email capture for anonymous first-time visitors only.
- **Surface a Loyallist hook at the top of the page** — even a slim banner saying "Your Loyallist points: 4,280 | Redeem now" would immediately signal the site knows I'm a top-tier member and wants to reward my visit.
- **Move the email signup to a sticky footer drawer** instead of a viewport takeover. The offer is fine; the placement is what's killing the experience.
- **Swap the overlay background to show the actual hero behind it** (blurred is fine) so I can see there's a campaign worth staying for — a lightbox treatment instead of a full opaque block.
