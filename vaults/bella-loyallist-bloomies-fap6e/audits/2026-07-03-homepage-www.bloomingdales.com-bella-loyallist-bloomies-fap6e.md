# Homepage snapshot · www.bloomingdales.com · 2026-07-03
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- I opened Bloomingdale's expecting to get a read on what they're pushing for the Fourth of July weekend — maybe a patriotic lifestyle moment, a Friends & Family preview tease, or at minimum a peek at the summer designer arrivals. Instead I got a full-screen email acquisition modal before I could see a single product. I'm a Loyallist top-tier member with an existing account and a bag that's never empty — this is not the welcome mat I'm used to.

## What's working

- **The 15% offer copy is legible and prominent.** "GET 15% OFF and insider access to new arrivals" — the value prop is stated cleanly in two lines with no ambiguity. For a first-time visitor this would land.
- **"SUBSCRIBE NOW" is a real button with real copy.** Not "Learn More," not "Explore" — it tells you exactly what tapping does.
- **The modal layout is uncluttered.** Input field, CTA, and legal copy are stacked neatly. No competing visual noise.
- **Sign In button is in the header.** It's small, but it's there — a returning shopper who dismisses the modal isn't completely abandoned.

## What's weak

- **The modal eats the entire viewport.** I cannot see the hero, the nav categories, the seasonal campaign, or any product imagery. I have zero context for what Bloomingdale's is selling right now.
- **The 15% offer is irrelevant to me.** "Valid for new email subscribers only" — I've been on this list for years. This is an acquisition play dressed up as a welcome, and it lands as a slap.
- **No loyalty acknowledgment whatsoever.** No "Welcome back, Loyallist member" hook, no rewards balance tease, no mention of my tier. Saks greets me by name when I land. This treats me like a stranger.
- **Zero product content above the fold.** The only glimpse of actual merchandise is "SUMMER DRESSES" in a thumbnail strip that's half cut off at the bottom. I have no idea what the campaign story is.
- **Fine print with "Exclusions apply" is an immediate trust flag.** For a brand positioned at this price point, vague exclusion language reads as bait-and-switch energy.

## Recommendations

- **Auto-dismiss the modal for signed-in Loyallist members** (or any recognized cookie/session). Surface a loyalty-specific banner instead — rewards balance, upcoming double-points event, or an early preview of a Friends & Family window.
- **Swap the modal's offer to be member-tier relevant.** If you must show a modal to an unrecognized session, make it "Loyallist? Sign in to unlock member pricing" rather than a new-subscriber email grab.
- **Move the Sign In button above the fold with more visual weight.** Right now it's a thin text button in the top corner. At my tier I want to be recognized — give me a visible "Sign In to your Loyallist account" pill with a small icon.
- **Let the actual homepage breathe.** Even a half-screen modal that shows the campaign hero behind it would tell me instantly whether there's something worth staying for. Full-block overlays push bounce rates up for everyone — especially for high-intent returning shoppers like me who came here with a purchase in mind.
