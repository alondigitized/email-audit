# The Scandi edit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-editorial in ASOS's now-familiar format — the same playbook as "Tennis heritage style" (June 12) and "The summer edit by Topshop" (June 11): a strong hero image, a curated brand/product grid, a beauty cross-sell, and brand logos at the bottom. The creative is genuinely good: the hero model nails Scandinavian minimalism (all-white, wide-leg, oversized silhouette, clean wall backdrop), and the white-on-white palette carries the theme consistently through the entire email.
- The miss is structural: there is no discount, no urgency, and no personalization. This is the 7th email ASOS has sent in 9 days — the last four were June 15, 16, 18, and now 19 — and after a Premier member 25% off, a holiday shop drop, a beauty blast, and a tennis editorial, a purely aspirational send with zero offer will land flat for fatigued subscribers.
- The "YOUR MOST HYPED / SHOP BACK IN STOCK" module buried in the lower grid is the most interesting element — it mirrors the June 10 back-in-stock trigger — but it's wasted mid-scroll in an editorial context rather than given its own send with real urgency copy.

## What's working

- **Hero is on-theme and immediately legible.** The white Scandi look reads at a glance — no concept confusion.
- **"SHOP SCANDI BRANDS" CTA is specific.** Verb + noun, not the typical vague "Discover."
- **Multi-brand grid creates discovery.** Arket, Nina Ricci-tier brands, and Charlotte Tilbury as a beauty cross-sell give the email real content depth.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, text is not overlapping — the technical execution is solid.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere in the email.** After a 25% off Premier send four days ago, an editorial with no discount asks subscribers to engage on vibes alone — hard sell at this cadence.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Seven emails in nine days; this one brings nothing new in terms of incentive.
- **Beauty cross-sell (Charlotte Tilbury "GET SET: GLAZE") feels unrelated.** Dropping a lipgloss module into a Scandi fashion editorial breaks the theme and reads like padding.
- **"YOUR MOST HYPED / BACK IN STOCK" is buried and decontextualized.** Great behavioral hook, wrong container — it deserved the June 10 treatment with its own focused send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a light offer to make the editorial actionable.** Even "Free next-day delivery on Scandi brands this weekend" gives a subscriber a reason to click today rather than bookmark and forget.
- 2. **Pull the back-in-stock module out entirely** and give it a standalone send (like the June 10 "Guess who's back?" which scored 8/10) — it's more valuable as a trigger email than as a buried grid block.
- 3. **Cut the beauty cross-sell.** The Charlotte Tilbury module fractures the Scandi theme. If beauty must be included, make it specifically Scandinavian beauty brands (BYBI, Deciem, etc.).
- 4. **Shorten the grid.** Six brand tiles + the back-in-stock module + the beauty section is scroll fatigue. Pick three and go deeper.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — `-webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict:**
- `body` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none`
- The `*` rule sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%`

The `none` value on `body` suppresses iOS auto-scaling globally. Because `*` is lower specificity than a direct element selector in some clients, the `none` on `body` wins and can make small text illegible on iOS Mail without a zoom gesture. Standard practice is `100%` on both.

**Non-standard mobile breakpoint:**
- Media query fires at `max-width: 460px`. Industry standard is 600px. Devices 461–599px wide (many Android phones, small tablets) receive the desktop layout with no responsive adjustments.

**Cannot audit** — `alt` attributes on images, heading hierarchy, color contrast ratios, and VML fallback rendering require the full source.
