# Rina Lipa just spilled our best-kept secret
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- ASOS is threading three playbooks into a single send: influencer collab, style-quiz entry mechanic, and sale callout. The subject's curiosity gap — "Rina Lipa just spilled our best-kept secret" — is the strongest thing in the email. But the body doesn't pay it off cleanly. You land on "Secret's out" and a vague invitation to take a quiz, then scroll past an influencer edit block, then hit a magenta sale panel screaming 70% off. The quiz and the sale are pulling in opposite directions — one is aspirational discovery, the other is discount urgency — and neither wins.
- The influencer mechanic has potential. A quiz that maps your style to a curated edit is stickier than another "shop the sale" push. But ASOS buries the punchline: the quiz is framed as an HQ secret "unlocked" by Rina Lipa, yet the body gives no hint of what completing it gets you. The CTA "ENTER NOW" reads like a sweepstakes entry, not a style recommendation engine.
- Context matters: this is ASOS's seventh email in roughly eight days, including three editorial resets, two sale pushes, and a Premier exclusive. Subscriber fatigue is real. The quiz mechanic is the freshest idea in the recent run — that's exactly why it deserved a cleaner, solo email rather than a cluttered three-section brief.

## What's working

- **Subject line curiosity gap is genuinely strong** — combining a celebrity name with "best-kept secret" creates FOMO without spam signals.
- **Hero model styling earns attention** — the yellow top against a warm neutral background is visually clean and current.
- **Sale panel has sharp visual contrast** — the magenta block with bold white "up to 70% off" reads instantly as a discount anchor.
- **"STEAL RINA'S STYLE" secondary section** adds a clear editorial leg to the influencer angle, even if it's undersized.
- **Subject is 43 chars** — comfortable on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Three sections, zero dominant CTA** — quiz, influencer edit, and sale compete rather than sequence logically.
- **"ENTER NOW" is opaque** — entering what? A quiz? A competition? The copy doesn't say.
- **The quiz payoff is never stated** — there's no "find your summer style in 60 seconds" or "get a personalized edit." The ask is friction with no visible reward.
- **Sale callout in the lower third undermines the influencer premise** — the most urgent offer is the one farthest from the hero.
- **Rina Lipa is name-dropped but not shown to endorse** — no quote, no direct recommendation, no "here's what she picked." She's marketing furniture, not a real collab anchor.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make this a quiz email or a sale email — not both.** Promote the sale as a footer line. Let the quiz and Rina's edit be the full brief. The July 11 and July 8 sends already established the sale mechanic; this one needs to do something different.
- 2. **Rewrite the quiz CTA to state the payoff** — "Take the style quiz → get Rina's picks for your type" beats "ENTER NOW" in every test.
- 3. **Promote Rina's Edit section to the hero, demote the quiz to the mechanic inside it** — the subject promises her edit; lead with the clothes.
- 4. **Add a specific product or price point in the hero** — even one standout item ("the yellow draped top, £38") gives the influencer angle a concrete hook.
- 5. **Give the quiz a time boundary if you want urgency** — "Quiz closes Sunday" is more compelling than the open-ended current framing.

- **Subject Alt B:** `We asked Rina Lipa to solve your style dilemmas`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Take the ASOS style quiz she unlocked — get your personalized summer edit in 60 seconds`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `body`:**
```css
body, #body_style {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; /* ← breaks user zoom on iOS */
}
```
Should be `100%`, not `none`. Using `none` suppresses iOS Mail's automatic text resizing AND overrides the user's system accessibility font-size setting. This violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Fix: `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; -ms-text-size-adjust: 100%; }`.

**Low — SVG font format included (deprecated):**
```css
url("https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
```
SVG fonts have been unsupported since iOS 4.2 (2010) and deprecated in CSS Fonts Level 4. Including them adds unnecessary payload on every open. Remove both SVG entries from `@font-face`; woff2 and woff cover all relevant email clients.

**Low — Media query breakpoint at 460px:**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px) { ... }
```
Standard email mobile breakpoint is 600px. Devices between 460px–600px viewport width (e.g., large Android phones in split-screen, Samsung Galaxy tablets) receive no responsive overrides. This could cause layout breakage in the `*[class=mob*]` grid columns at those widths.

**Low — `font-size: 0em` global table reset without confirmed cell reset:**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; }
```
This is the standard zero-gap fix. Functional only if every content `<td>` resets font-size explicitly. If any content cell is missing the reset, text will render invisibly. Not verifiable from truncated HTML — confirm all `<td>` elements containing text reset to a non-zero `font-size`.

**Note — VML and conditional comment handling is correct:**
```html
<!--[if gt mso 15]> ... .vml-cta { display: block !important; } ... <![endif]-->
```
Outlook VML CTA pattern is properly gated. No issues.
