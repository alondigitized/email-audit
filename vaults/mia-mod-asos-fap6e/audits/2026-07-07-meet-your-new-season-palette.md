# Meet your new-season palette
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- ASOS is doing the same thing on July 7 that it did on July 1: resetting from two weeks of sale noise with a full-price editorial email. Where July 1's "Guess who's back?" leaned into the pivot explicitly, today's send leans purely visual — an earth-tone palette story built around chocolate browns, deep plums, and sage greens in clean editorial photography. The creative is genuinely cohesive; every product image shares the same muted, warm palette and the seasonal framing feels intentional rather than random.
- The problem is the audience. This list just spent 16 days being trained to expect 60–70% off. The July 1 editorial pivot earned an 8/10 because it had the novelty of being *first* — it broke pattern. This send is the second editorial send in six days, and it offers nothing beyond the aesthetic. No price, no urgency, no reason to click today versus next week.
- A good editorial email earns its keep with either a hook (a specific launch, a trend peg, a scarcity signal) or a story tight enough to make the CTA feel inevitable. "A new palette" is a concept, not a reason to shop.

## What's working

- **Color story is airtight.** Every product visible in the email — the chocolate halter, the plum maxi, the sage slip dress, the gold pendant — shares the same earthy warm palette. Nothing looks like a misfit pulled in by the algorithm.
- **Photography quality is high.** The hero model shot is strong, and the six-panel grid below maintains editorial consistency without feeling repetitive.
- **Subject line is clean on mobile.** "Meet your new-season palette" clocks in under 30 characters — no truncation risk, no spam-trigger language.
- **Secondary waterproof jewelry section adds a tactile product detail.** "Waterproof jewellery" is a specific, functional claim — a rare moment of concrete copy in an otherwise mood-board-only email.
- **No render bugs.** The layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no text overlap, no empty containers.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no urgency, no reason to click now.** A subscriber conditioned by 15 days of 60–70% off sale emails is not primed to browse a full-price edit with zero incentive. The cart-to-purchase gap just got much wider.
- **"SHOP THE EDIT" is the weakest possible CTA.** It's the catchall button ASOS uses on every editorial email. After seven sale escalations, it reads as a placeholder, not a call to action.
- **The six-panel product grid is too many items without a point of view.** The scroll depth required to see all the products dilutes focus — there's no single hero product the eye anchors on below the fold.
- **No new-season narrative peg.** There's no copy explaining *why* this palette is relevant *now* — no trend hook, no season anchor, no editorial point of view beyond the color grouping. "A new palette" is decoration, not journalism.
- **Brand logo row ("SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS") disrupts the editorial tone.** Dropping into a brand-marketplace block right after an editorial grid kills the mood and signals to the reader they've left the curated experience.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single hero product link.** Pick the best-performing brown or sage piece from last week's data and make the hero image a direct link to that PDP. "SHOP THE EDIT" should be the secondary CTA, not the only one.
- 2. **Write a genuine editorial hook in the subhead.** Replace "A new palette" + vague body copy with something like: "The season's richest shade? Chocolate brown — and ASOS has 40+ ways to wear it." Give a reason to click today.
- 3. **Drop the brand-logo marketplace block or move it to footer.** It's jarring after the editorial tone and tells the reader the email didn't know who it was for.
- 4. **Lean into the waterproof jewelry section more — or cut it.** If it's worth including, lead with the functional hook: "Rain-safe, pool-safe, ASOS-priced." If not, remove the distraction.
- 5. **Rewrite the CTA** from "SHOP THE EDIT" → "Shop the Palette" or "Explore the Edit" — at minimum use language that echoes the email's own subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your new-season colour: chocolate brown`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The palette drop: earth tones, new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Browns, greens, plums — 40+ new pieces in the season's richest shades.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New-in now: the warm-toned edit ASOS built for this exact moment.`

  ```
  url("https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
  ```
  Remove both SVG entries.

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`** — Intentional ghost-text suppression, correct practice. No issue.
