# Push your summer style further
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS's third editorial reset in roughly two weeks — same pattern as the July 7 "Meet your new-season palette" and the July 1 "Guess who's back?" pivot emails. The formula is familiar: strong hero shot, editorial headline ("Summer forever"), a product grid, then a sale discovery bridge section ("Looking for the Sale?") that acknowledges subscribers are primed for discounts without actually offering one. The creative execution is competent and clean. The problem is strategic: there is no offer here. No code, no percentage, no deadline. After a string of "extra 30% off," "extra 20% off" sale emails through early July, switching cold to pure editorial with zero incentive is a weak pivot. The "Looking for the Sale?" section is the most honest thing in the email — ASOS knows its list is in sale-mode headspace, but redirecting them to "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" without a discount is a half-measure.
- The subject, "Push your summer style further," is 30 characters of nothing. It signals no product, no offer, no urgency. Compared to the July 8 Premier gated offer ("Want an extra 20% off all Sale?") — which earned a 10/10 for exactly the opposite reason — this send has the feel of a content team filling calendar space.
- **Verdict:** Clean execution, hollow strategy. Fine to look at, easy to skip.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong.** Yellow-on-blue tile palette is visually arresting; model styling is current and on-brand.
- **"Looking for the Sale?" is a smart acknowledge.** Bridging editorial and sale in one email prevents full-fatigue abandonment from sale-conditioned subscribers.
- **Layout is clean and render bug-free.** Six-product grid is evenly spaced, no overlapping text or broken images visible.
- **CTA hierarchy is simple.** "SHOP NOW" (editorial) → "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" (sale) → "DOWNLOAD THE APP" reads in a logical order.
- **Brand logos in sale section add credibility.** Showing named top brands is more specific than a generic "Shop Sale" button.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** After back-to-back "extra 20–30% off" sends, an editorial email with no discount will be ignored by the majority of sale-primed subscribers.
- **Subject line is vaporous.** "Push your summer style further" is 30 characters of aspirational blur — no noun, no number, no hook.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA is entirely misplaced here.** It's a cold acquisition ask buried below a sale-discovery section; it pulls focus without earning it.
- **Product grid has no prices or labels.** Six outfits shown, none anchored with a price or product name — the grid functions as decoration, not conversion.
- **"Summer forever" is stale framing.** This is mid-July; that headline ran in June-style sends. "Summer forever" reads as peak-June copy, not peak-July.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete offer.** Even a modest "free next-day delivery" or "Sale prices just dropped further" would give subscribers a reason to click beyond browsing. The editorial creative is good enough to carry a deal; running it naked wastes the hero.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to include a specific pull.** The current subject promises nothing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New summer looks — and yes, Sale too`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer wardrobe just got an upgrade`
- 3. **Write a real preheader.** If the preheader is absent or leaking nav text, the inbox preview is wasted.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals + Sale's still going — see what landed`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New styles in. Your sale picks are waiting too.`
- 4. **Add price or badge to the product grid.** Even "From £18" on two grid items changes this from editorial browsing to shopping intent.
- 5. **Cut or relocate the "DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA.** It belongs in a dedicated app-push email, not sandwiched inside a sale discovery section. Replace it with a sale-link CTA that matches the subscriber's mental state.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Duplicate charset declaration.** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant; harmless in practice but indicates template slop.

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`.** This is a standard ghost-text suppression hack, but it forces every `<td>` to explicitly reset its own `font-size`. Any cell that omits a reset will render invisible text. Risk is low for a mature template, but it's a latent trap.

- **`@keyframes ticker` + `@supports` animation.** CSS animation support in email is narrow: Apple Mail/iOS Mail render it; Gmail (all), Outlook (all), and Yahoo suppress it silently. The `@supports` guard correctly hides `.ticker` and shows `.fallback` for non-supporting clients — implementation is sound. No issue.

- **Web fonts (`FuturaPT-Book`, `FuturaPT-Heavy`) will not load in Gmail, Outlook, or Yahoo.** The fallback stack `Futura, Arial, sans-serif` is appropriate. `mso-font-alt: Arial` is set correctly for Outlook. No rendering failure, but visual parity is not achievable across clients.

- **SVG font format included in `@font-face` src stack.** SVG fonts are deprecated (removed from all modern browsers; never supported in email clients). The entry adds bytes with zero benefit:
  ```
  url("...FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
  ```
  Safe to remove.

- **`img { display: block }` is global** — correct for email to suppress descender gaps. No issue.

- **Alt text on images unverifiable** due to truncation. Must be confirmed in full source.
