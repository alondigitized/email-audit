# Need some inspo?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS running their now-familiar browse-editorial format — dual hero shots, a product grid, a "back in stock" nudge, and brand logos at the bottom. It's structurally identical to "The Scandi edit" sent just three days earlier on June 19, and shares DNA with "Tennis heritage style" (June 12) and the Topshop collab (June 11). At this cadence — the fourth editorial in eleven days — the format has worn through. The subscriber's finger is already drifting toward Archive.
- The body is competent. The dual-model hero is clean, the "YOUR MOST HYPED — back in stock" module is the strongest piece of creative in the email (it creates genuine scarcity signaling without a manufactured deadline), and the product grid is crisply laid out. No render bugs. But the email hangs on a subject line — "Need some inspo?" — that is the weakest in this audit run: zero category signal, zero offer, zero urgency, and 17 characters of pure filler that could belong to any fashion retailer alive.
- There is no offer here. That's a deliberate editorial choice, and ASOS can get away with it when the creative is differentiated or the timing is charged. Neither is true on June 22. The net result is an email that functions as a passive browse prompt, not a revenue event.

## What's working

- **"YOUR MOST HYPED" module** — "Sold-out faves, back in stock" is the one piece of scarcity copy in the email and it earns its space. This is the only hook that rewards opening.
- **Dual-hero layout** — the two full-width model shots create visual polish and hold the eye without feeling cluttered.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. The grid and brand-logo strip are tight.
- **"SHOP NEW IN" CTA** — unambiguous verb + noun, good contrast, above the fold.
- **ASOS Premier footer link** — low-effort reminder for members even without an exclusive offer attached.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "Need some inspo?" is nearly worthless** — no category, no offer, no trend, no urgency. Identical vagueness as the June 19 "Scandi edit" opener before it.
- **No offer anywhere in the email** — three days after "The Scandi edit" (also offer-free), subscribers who skipped that one have no new reason to click this one.
- **Editorial fatigue is real** — this is the fourth editorial in eleven days from ASOS. The format, hero layout, and CTA are nearly pixel-identical to prior sends. The brand is blurring into wallpaper.
- **"SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" strip is filler** — the brand-logo grid (New Balance, Fila, etc.) at the bottom creates scroll weight without giving the reader anything to grab onto; no callout, no deal, no click hook.
- **No trend or story hook** — unlike "Tennis heritage style" (June 12, scored 9/10) which had a Wimbledon-season peg, this editorial has no editorial reason to exist on June 22 specifically.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the email a content reason to exist.** Tie the new-in drop to something culturally timed — a summer solstice, a specific trend word, an event. "Tennis heritage style" earned its 9/10 because the subject and creative had a shared peg. "Need some inspo?" has none.
- 2. **Promote the "back in stock" module to the hero position.** The scarcity signal in "YOUR MOST HYPED" is the email's strongest hook — bury it lower and it loses most of its urgency. Lead with the sold-out-faves story, make new-in secondary.
- 3. **Cut the brand-logo strip or give it a deal.** If the top-brands module doesn't have a promotion attached, it is dead pixels. Either add "20% off New Balance this week" or remove the section entirely.
- 4. **Reduce editorial frequency to let each send breathe.** At 4+ editorials in 11 days, the format is destroying its own open rates. Space these to once a week or differentiate the creative mode between sends.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags inside `<body>/<p>` — confirmed structural defect.**
The document opens as `<html><body>` with no `<head>`, and meta tags are emitted inside a `<p>` element:
```html
<p>
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" ...>
  ...
</p>
```
`<meta>` is a void element belonging in `<head>`; placing it inside `<p>` is invalid per the HTML spec. Most email clients tolerate this (they ignore `<head>` anyway), but strict parsers may drop the viewport directive, breaking mobile scaling on some clients.

**Duplicate charset declarations** — `http-equiv="Content-Type; charset=UTF-8"` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` coexist. Redundant but harmless.

**Empty preheader div** — The hidden preheader `<div>` at the top of `<body>` contains no text:
```html
<div style="display:none !important;..."></div>
```
With no preheader copy, inbox clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) fall back to pulling the first visible rendered text as preview text, which is unpredictable and likely unintended.

**MSO conditional targets `gt mso 15` only** — The VML block uses `<!--[if gt mso 15]>` (strictly greater than MSO 15), excluding Outlook 2013 (MSO 15). If VML CTAs are load-bearing for Outlook rendering, Outlook 2013 users will miss them. Standard practice is `[if gte mso 9]` or `[if mso]`.

**Font loading** — FuturaPT served from `images.asos-media.com` with `font-display: swap` and correct format cascade (woff2 → woff → otf → ttf → svg). `mso-font-alt: Arial` is set. No issues.
