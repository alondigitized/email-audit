# Must-have summer dresses
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS doing what it does competently when it's not running a sale: a clean editorial dress showcase with a seasonal color story ("self-cream to chocolate brown") and a Wanderdoll x Emma Nilton collab feature. The creative is unfussy — six product shots in a tight grid, earth tones and greens that feel deliberately curated rather than clearance-dumped. That's the upside.
- The downside: this is the third ASOS email in roughly two days, arriving the morning after both a "New Arrivals" editorial and a Premier-member sale-stack. There's no offer, no urgency, and no price signal anywhere in the scroll — which is fine for a first-of-week reset but feels thin as a follow-up to yesterday's "extra 20% off" gating. The survey prompt ("We'd love to hear your thoughts") dropped in the middle of a promotional email is a jarring gear-change, and the footer brand carousel — Quiksilver, Levi's, New Balance — is completely off-topic for a summer dress send.
- Net judgment: competent editorial execution that gets buried by cadence fatigue and a cluttered footer third. A committed ASOS shopper saves this for later; most inbox scanners swipe past it.

## What's working

- **Color story is real editorial work.** Cream → chocolate brown → olive/sage across the grid is a cohesive seasonal narrative, not just random SKUs.
- **Hero hierarchy is immediate.** "Summer dresses to know" + a single "SHOP THE EDIT" CTA lands before any scroll — no ambiguity about where to go.
- **Wanderdoll x Emma Nilton collab is a solid mid-email hook.** Having a named collab with its own visual block and dedicated CTA ("SHOP WANDERDOLL") gives a reason to keep scrolling.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, text is legible, buttons are intact — clean execution throughout.
- **Subject line is under 25 characters** — "Must-have summer dresses" reads cleanly on mobile and doesn't truncate.

## What's weak

- **Cadence is unsustainable.** Third email in ~48 hours from ASOS; July 8 already sent both a Premier sale stack and a new-arrivals editorial. This one adds nothing new to that conversation.
- **Survey prompt kills the promotional flow.** "We'd love to hear your thoughts / TAKE THE SURVEY" appears immediately after the product content — it's a cold-water moment in a buying email.
- **Footer brand carousel is irrelevant.** "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" — Quiksilver, Bershka, Guess, Levi's, New Balance — has zero thematic connection to a summer dress editorial. It reads like an automatic template block nobody turned off.
- **No offer anywhere in the scroll.** After yesterday's "extra 20% off Sale" for Premier members, dropping back to full-price editorial with zero price signal or urgency feels like a step backwards for any shopper who's been trained to wait for a code.
- **Massive ASOS logo at the footer** consumes real estate with zero functional value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the survey CTA to the footer or strip it entirely.** A feedback request in the middle of a buying email bleeds click equity from the product CTAs and signals "we're not sure this email is earning its send."
- 2. **Add one price hook — even soft urgency.** "Shop now — these shades go fast each summer" or a "free standard delivery on orders over £X" reminder is enough to convert a browse into a click without running a sale.
- 3. **Cut the "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" carousel or replace it with a dress-specific brand filter** (e.g., "All the labels in this edit") — it belongs in a homepage email, not a dress editorial.
- 4. **Consolidate the two grid sections into one longer one.** Two separate "shop" CTAs (SHOP THE EDIT + SHOP OCCASIONWEAR) fragments the user's intent; pick the stronger destination and commit.
- 5. **Reframe the cadence.** Given yesterday's two sends, this one should have been held 24 hours or made exclusive ("For Premier members: first look at the summer dress edit") to feel earned.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `body`** — disables iOS text-scaling entirely, preventing users from zooming text. The correct value for accessibility compliance is `100%`, not `none`. The later `* { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` rule does not override this on `body` itself (body selector has higher specificity than `*`).

  ```css
  /* Line ~35 */
  body, #body_style { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
  /* Should be: 100% */
  ```

- **Mobile breakpoint at 460px** — industry standard is 600px. Devices with 461px–599px viewport width (many Android phones in landscape, small tablets) receive the desktop layout, causing potential overflow.

- **SVG font format included** — all `@font-face` declarations include an SVG fallback format. SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in all modern email clients. Adds payload weight with zero benefit.

  ```css
  url("https://images.asos-media.com/.../FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg");
  ```

- **`font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th`** — this ghost-text gap fix is common, but the global scope means any inline text accidentally placed directly in a `<td>` without a child element will be invisible with no warning.

- **Dual charset declarations** — both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; minor payload bloat.

- **`alt` attribute coverage** — cannot confirm from truncated source. The global `img { display: block; }` rule does not enforce `alt` text.
