# Up to 60% off Sale starts now
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS firing the starting gun on their summer sale — a clean, red-hero blast announcing "SALE STARTS NOW / UP TO 60% OFF." It follows a week of editorial sends ("Need some inspo?", "The Scandi edit") and a beauty promo, so the sale launch lands as a genuine escalation rather than another filler send. The visual execution is crisp: red hero dominates, dual gender CTAs split the audience cleanly, and a "YOUR SALE EDIT" product strip gives the email substance beyond the headline.
- The core weakness is the one shared by every "up to X% off" email ever sent: the vagueness of "up to." There's no floor, no end date, and no price callout on any of the featured products. The urgency is created by "SALE STARTS NOW" and "Limited time only," but without a deadline the clock never starts ticking for the reader.
- June 22 is also notable because ASOS already sent "Need some inspo?" that same day. Two emails in one day is fatiguing even for a high-engagement subscriber — and it dilutes the impact of this sale launch.

## What's working

- **Red hero is a clean alarm bell** — the color shift from ASOS's usual white editorial aesthetic signals "this is different" immediately.
- **Dual gender CTAs ("SHOP WOMEN'S" / "SHOP MEN'S")** remove the need to browse to a landing page to find your section — a genuine UX shortcut.
- **"YOUR SALE EDIT" editorial strip** shows real product in context (styled outfits, sandals) rather than just screaming a discount, preserving brand tone.
- **Category grid (Tops, Dresses, Footwear, Swimwear, Skirts, Summer)** gives six direct entry points for intent-driven browsers — useful after you've already clicked open.
- **Subject line is 30 characters** — clean, mobile-legible, no truncation risk.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 60% off" without a floor** — a 10% off item is technically "up to 60% off." Erodes trust for repeat subscribers who've learned this phrasing.
- **No end date anywhere** — "Limited time only" is noise at this point. Without a date, urgency doesn't land.
- **No pricing on any product image** — the sale edit shows styled outfits but zero "was/now" callouts. A subscriber has no idea if the items shown are actually discounted.
- **Two emails sent same day** (this + "Need some inspo?") — cadence fatigue undercuts what should be a marquee send.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA mid-email** disrupts the shopping flow at exactly the wrong moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard end date to the hero** — "Sale ends Sunday" or a countdown. This is the single highest-leverage change; without it, "starts now" implies the discount will always be there.
- 2. **Add was/now pricing to at least two product images in the sale edit** — showing "Was £65 → £26" on the jumpsuit makes the 60% real, not theoretical.
- 3. **Kill or defer the "Download the App" CTA** — it competes for the click at the exact moment the subscriber is closest to purchasing.
- 4. **Drop one of the two June 22 sends** — if the cadence is this tight, the launch should be given clear air; the inspo email should have been held for June 23.
- 5. **Subject restructure** — the current subject reads slightly awkward with "Sale" capitalized mid-phrase.
- **Subject Alt A:** `ASOS Sale is live — up to 60% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, tops, footwear — your size is in the sale now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New drops added to the sale daily. Shop before your size goes.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Empty preheader.** The hidden preheader `<div>` (the `display:none` block after `<body>`) contains no text. Inbox clients will pull the first visible text as the preview snippet, which may surface boilerplate or navigation copy instead of intentional preheader messaging.

- **Duplicate charset declaration.** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; parsers treat the first declaration as authoritative.

- **Mobile breakpoint at 460px.** The sole `@media` query fires at `max-width: 460px`. The de-facto safe threshold for modern devices is 480px; emails displayed on 461–479px viewports (some Android devices) will receive the desktop layout. Low risk, but worth aligning to 480px.

- **`font-size: 0em` on all table elements.** The global `table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em }` rule suppresses phantom whitespace gaps in Outlook — standard practice — but will silently hide any text placed directly in `<td>` without an inner `<p>` or `<span>`. No issue if content follows the pattern; flag for QA if any table cells render blank unexpectedly.

No issues with VML Outlook conditional (`<!--[if gt mso 15]>`), web font fallback chain (`mso-font-alt: Arial`), or `-webkit-text-size-adjust` suppression — all correctly implemented.

**Cannot assess:** Image `alt` text coverage and `role`/`aria` attributes are in the truncated body.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**High severity — confirmed failure.**

```html
<title>Up to 60% off Sale starts now, </title>
```

The `<title>` ends with a trailing comma and space: `"...starts now, "`. The intended output was almost certainly `"Up to 60% off Sale starts now, [First Name]"` — the first-name merge token was not replaced at send time and was silently dropped rather than falling back to empty or a default value.

**Impact:** The dangling comma is visible in:
- Browser tab / webmail title bar when the email is opened in a browser
- Some assistive technology screen readers that read the document title on load
- Any downstream system (CRM, analytics) that scrapes the `<title>` for email metadata

**Root cause options:** Token syntax mismatch at render time, missing subscriber attribute with no fallback defined, or a template preview sent without a live data merge. Verify the ESP render log for this send batch.
