# New arrivals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- ASOS sends a clean, well-shot new-arrivals editorial the same day as "Going somewhere?" — their vacation-themed send that already ran May 26. The creative is competent: strong photography, a clear brand look, and a "Your Most Hyped" back-in-stock section that's the only moment of genuine urgency in the scroll. But "New arrivals" is arguably the most forgettable subject line in retail email, and stacking two editorial-only sends on the same day without a price hook makes this one feel disposable.
- The back-in-stock module is the real asset here and it's buried. That section has FOMO, implied social proof, and product scarcity — everything the hero doesn't. Leading with that logic instead of a generic "SHOP NEW IN" header would materially change the conversion case for this send.
- Without an offer or meaningful hook, this is brand maintenance: it keeps ASOS visible in the inbox but won't pull many wallets open. Compare it to the May 25 "Up to 30% off" send — that one actually had a reason to click.

## What's working

- Photography is excellent throughout — varied styling, strong summer palette, confident poses across multiple product categories
- "YOUR MOST HYPED / back in stock" section introduces the only real FOMO hook in the email and has a clear, action-oriented CTA ("SHOP BACK IN STOCK")
- "SHOP NEW IN" CTA appears twice in the scroll — sensible placement for long editorial formats
- Free next-workday delivery callout in the hero sub-copy provides a concrete value anchor for on-the-fence buyers
- "SHOP OUR TOP BRANDS" section diversifies appeal past house-label looks — good for brand shoppers

## What's weak

- "New arrivals" as a subject line is interchangeable with a hundred other brands on any given Tuesday — zero differentiation, zero hook
- No price incentive anywhere in the email; the May 25 "30% off" send set a discount expectation that this one doesn't meet
- The lime-green "DOWNLOAD THE APP" bar mid-scroll is jarring — interrupts the shopping flow and feels grafted from a different campaign
- Cadence fatigue is real: this is the second ASOS send on May 26 alone, after six sends in the preceding eight days
- Back-in-stock section is too far down the scroll to capture subscribers who skim and bounce early

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the back-in-stock hook earlier** — move "YOUR MOST HYPED" to directly below the hero or swap it in as the hero entirely; it's the strongest reason to click today
- 2. **Rewrite the subject** to name a specific trend, category, or "just dropped" product angle — anything more concrete than the two-word placeholder
- 3. **Kill or demote the "DOWNLOAD THE APP" bar** — it breaks the email's shopping logic and belongs in an onboarding sequence, not a conversion send
- 4. **Skip the same-day double-send** — pairing this with "Going somewhere?" on the same calendar day splits attention and trains subscribers to ignore one of the two
- 5. **Add a light deadline to the back-in-stock section** ("Back in stock — usually gone in 24h") to give the urgency signal a time component

## Technical Audit — ASOS "New Arrivals" (hello@official.asos.com)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Well-structured commercial email with solid Outlook VML fallbacks and responsive CSS, but contains a critical structural defect (`<meta>` tags misplaced inside `<body><p>`) and a missing `@font-face` declaration that causes heading fonts to silently fall back to Arial across all clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully audit** — HTML is truncated before any `<a href>` elements appear. Issues to verify in full source:

- Confirm all CTAs carry consistent UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` at minimum).
- Confirm redirect/click-tracking wrapper URLs resolve to HTTPS and don't produce redirect chains longer than 2 hops (degrades deliverability scores).
- Font assets load from `https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/` — confirm CDN has correct `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` headers; some clients block cross-origin font loads.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Missing `@font-face` for `FuturaPT-DemiBold`:**
```css
h1, h2, h3 { font-family: 'FuturaPT-DemiBold', Futura, Arial, sans-serif; }
```
Only `FuturaPT-Book` and `FuturaPT-Heavy` are declared in `@font-face`. `FuturaPT-DemiBold` is never defined, so all `h1`/`h2`/`h3` elements silently render in Arial on every client that doesn't have the system font installed.

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` weight mismatch:**
```css
/* Declared weight */
@font-face { font-family: "FuturaPT-Heavy"; font-weight: 600; }
/* But "Heavy" conventionally maps to 800–900 */
```
Any element using `font-weight: 700+` that matches against this face will get 600. Likely causes bolder elements to render lighter than designed.

**Structural defect — `<meta>` tags inside `<body><p>`:**
```html
<html><body>
  <div>...</div>
  <p>
    <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    ...
```
No `<head>` element is present. The `<meta>` charset declarations appear inside a `<p>` tag in `<body>`. Most email clients are permissive, but this is invalid HTML that can cause character encoding fallback failures in stricter parsers (notably some Android mail clients and Samsung Mail).

**Duplicate charset declarations:**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present — harmless but redundant.

**Responsive breakpoint at 460px only:**
Single breakpoint `@media screen and (max-width:460px)` covers typical phones but leaves tablet portrait (461px–768px) with desktop layout. Not a bug, but flag if tablet rendering matters.

**Image alt text:** Not auditable from truncated source — verify all `<img>` tags have non-empty `alt` attributes.
