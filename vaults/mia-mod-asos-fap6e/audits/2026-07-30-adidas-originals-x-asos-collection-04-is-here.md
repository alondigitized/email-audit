# adidas Originals x ASOS: Collection 04 is here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS running a genuine co-brand play instead of its usual house-editorial move — "adidas Originals x ASOS: Collection 04," fronted by boxer Ramla Ali in a full-bleed hero shot with the collab logo lockup stamped right over her. Compared to the string of no-offer "new-in" sends I've seen from this brand recently — July 20's "Your edit is here" (4/10), July 22's "Fringe is in" (4/10) and "New arrivals" (4/10), July 27's "We're crushing on new Topshop" (5/10) — this one has more to hang a story on: a named collection number, a recognizable athlete, and a tight six-shot product grid that's clearly all one drop rather than a loose "browse our new stuff" pile.
- The execution is clean: no render bugs, one obvious hero, two unambiguous "SHOP" CTAs. What it shares with its weaker siblings is the same core flaw — zero discount, zero urgency. It's a pure brand-awareness play dressed as a shoppable email.
- Judgement: strong creative for a no-offer send, but it's still a no-offer send, and this brand has trained me to expect that means a middling open and an even weaker click unless the collab name alone is enough to pull me in.

## What's working

- The hero image does real work — full-bleed model shot with the adidas leaf logo x ASOS lockup overlaid cleanly, no clutter competing for attention.
- "EXCLUSIVE TO ASOS" under the lockup gives a concrete reason this can't be found elsewhere, which the recent "New arrivals" and "Fringe is in" sends never bothered to include.
- Product grid is disciplined — six shots, one collection, consistent studio backdrop, easy to scan.
- Two CTAs ("SHOP THE COLLECTION" and "SHOP NOW") bracket the content so there's no dead scroll with nothing to click.
- Render is completely clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere — no %, no $ off, no free shipping, nothing. Same gap that capped "New arrivals" and "Fringe is in" at 4/10.
- No urgency or scarcity cue for a "Collection 04" drop that presumably will sell through — nothing tells me if this disappears in a week or sits in stock all season.
- Copy is vague ("Lock in," "life between fashion and sport," "personal style with no limits") — mood-board language, not a reason to click today over next week.
- Ramla Ali is named in the body copy but never actually shown or credited visually — the hero model isn't identified, so the athlete tie-in is undercut by the render itself.
- Preview/preheader text isn't populated with anything useful in this render (see Section 9) — a wasted inbox impression next to a subject that's already fairly plain.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a hook with teeth — even a soft one: "First 48 hours" access window, a loyalty-early-access angle for ASOS Premier members, or a "limited drop" framing. This email has the visual credibility to support urgency; it just doesn't ask for it.
- 2. Show Ramla Ali by name in the visual, not just in body copy — a small "featuring Ramla Ali" caption under the hero would convert an unverifiable name-drop into actual social proof.
- 3. Tighten the subject to lead with the collab name, not the internal collection numbering — "Collection 04" means nothing to a subscriber who hasn't seen 01–03.
- 4. Populate the preheader with something that extends the subject instead of leaving it blank/default — free real estate that's currently wasted.
- 5. Consider a light urgency line under the CTA ("Drops Thursday, while sizes last") to convert the browse-and-close pattern this brand keeps falling into.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **All 10 probed material links failed with a read timeout** on the `official-link.asos.com` redirect domain (e.g. "Shop now", "Download the App", "ASOS.World", "ASOS", "ASOS Premier", "Gift Vouchers", and 4 brand-logo links: mango, bershka, stradivarius, adidasapparel, topshop).
