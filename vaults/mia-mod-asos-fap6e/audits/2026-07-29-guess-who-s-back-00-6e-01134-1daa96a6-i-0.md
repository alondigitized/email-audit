# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS running "restock" as its offer mechanic — "They're back," sold-out favorites returning to inventory. It's a clean, well-executed hero-plus-grid layout with six clearly shot product images (sequin gown, polka-dot halter dress, off-shoulder black top, yellow ruched dress, textured knit, floral maxi), two CTAs ("SHOP NOW" and "SHOP BACK IN STOCK"), and a brand carousel. No render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- The problem is the same one that's dogged every recent ASOS send in this brand history: there's no discount, no price, no urgency beyond FOMO-by-implication. "Missed it the first time around?" is a real hook — scarcity/regret is a legitimate lever — but it's under-leveraged here with no "only X left" or specific item callout. Compared to the July 18 "extra 30% off" send (8/10) or even the July 26 stacked-discount send (7/10), this restock angle is creative but softer on conversion pressure.
- Net: a well-produced, mid-tier editorial/restock send. Better than the flat "New arrivals" or "Your edit is here" sends because restock at least implies urgency, but it doesn't commit hard enough to that urgency to punch above a 5.

## What's working

- Restock framing ("They're back," "Missed it the first time around?") is a genuine differentiator from ASOS's recent run of generic new-in/sale emails.
- Six distinct, well-lit product shots with diverse styling (eveningwear, casual, knit, maxi) give the grid real browsing value.
- Two clear, high-contrast CTA buttons ("SHOP NOW," "SHOP BACK IN STOCK") are unmissable against the white background.
- Clean visual hierarchy — headline, subhead, CTA, then grid — with no clutter or competing messages.
- Brand carousel (Mango, Bershka, Stradivarius, adidas, Topshop) adds cross-sell without crowding the main offer.

## What's weak

- No discount, price, or specific item named — "sold-out faves" is vague; not one SKU is called out by name in copy.
- No urgency mechanic — restocked items *can* sell out again fast, but the email never says so ("back for a limited time," "these go fast").
- Demographic signal is narrow — all six models are styled for evening/going-out looks; nothing here signals everyday/basics for a broader segment.
- Preview text isn't visible/distinct in this render — the subhead essentially restates the subject with no added angle.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add urgency to the restock premise** — "Back — but not for long" or a countdown-style line under the CTA reinforces that these are the same sold-out items that vanished once, which is the whole reason to click now.
- 2. **Name at least one hero product** in the subhead or as a caption under the top-left image (e.g., "The sequin gown everyone wanted is back") — grounds the vague "faves" claim in something concrete and clickable.
- 3. **Layer in a small incentive** — even free shipping or a Premier-member early-access angle would lift this above pure editorial, especially since ASOS has trained this list to expect discounts.
- 4. **Segment by category** — this grid skews evening/occasionwear; a second module with restocked basics/denim would broaden appeal beyond one demographic.
