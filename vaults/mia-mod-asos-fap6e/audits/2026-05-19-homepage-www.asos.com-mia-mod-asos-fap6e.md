# Homepage snapshot · www.asos.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Okay so ASOS is doing the classic "look cool, say nothing" homepage thing — the split editorial hero with the girl in oversized sunnies and the guy in the burgundy polo feels very lookbook-era rather than like, drop culture. It's giving aspirational fashion brand but not specifically *ASOS* in the way that feels personal to me as a Premier member who's been here for years. Right now it's pushing a generic dual-gender launch rather than anything seasonal, sale, or loyalty-driven.

## What's working

- **The split-hero editorial image** — the woman with the chunky oversized sunglasses in warm tones looks genuinely current and on-trend. As someone who plans festival looks, that styling direction is in the right ballpark.
- **"SHOP WOMENS" CTA button** — it's big, white, above the fold, and unmistakable. One tap to my category, no hunting.
- **Clean mobile layout** — the nav bar (hamburger, search, account, wishlist, bag) is well-spaced and thumb-friendly. No cramped tap targets.
- **ASOS logo as hero lockup** — reinforces brand identity mid-hero, doesn't feel lost.

## What's weak

- **"New here? Get your first-timer discount"** — I'm a Premier member who spends here regularly. Leading with a new-customer discount tile makes me feel invisible. No acknowledgement I exist or have history.
- **"Download our app for exclusive discounts"** — I'm *already on my phone*, presumably in a browser. This tile is wasted on me and feels like it's optimising for a different user who wandered in from a search ad.
- **"STYLE EACH MOMENT" tagline** — this says nothing. No seasonal hook, no trend cue, no urgency. Completely interchangeable with any other fashion brand.
- **Zero Premier benefits above the fold** — free next-day delivery is genuinely one of ASOS's best weapons. It's not mentioned anywhere visible. That's a real miss.
- **No festival, denim, or trending category callout** — May is prime festival-season. There's no "Festival fits," "Denim edit," or "New in this week" to pull me in on my actual interests.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the generic tagline for a live campaign hook** — something like "Festival season sorted — new drops weekly" would immediately signal relevance for the May timing and pull me toward browsing.
- **Add a Premier member strip directly below the nav** — a slim dark bar: "Welcome back, Premier member · Free next-day on everything" costs nothing to build and makes returning shoppers feel seen within a second of landing.
- **Replace the "New here?" tile with a "New in Womens" or trending category tile** — the real estate below the hero is wasted on acquisition messaging when I'm already on the site. Show me the denim edit, the festival picks, *anything* I might click.
- **Give the "Download our app" tile context-aware logic** — if I'm in a mobile browser, swap it for a personalised hook ("Back for more? Your wishlist has new stuff in stock") or a live category drop teaser.
