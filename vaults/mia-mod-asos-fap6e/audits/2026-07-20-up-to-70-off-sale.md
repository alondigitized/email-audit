# Up to 70% off Sale
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS running its most generic sale format — "Up to 70% off Sale styles" — with no promo code, no stacked discount, and no urgency mechanic beyond "limited time only." After the July 8 Premier-exclusive send (10/10) and the back-to-back 30%-extra-code sends on July 11 and July 18, this email feels like a deliberate reset to top-of-funnel breadth: hit everyone with a headline number, let the category CTAs do the segmentation work.
- The structure is competent. The hero is clean, the category shortcuts (Tops Under £5, Dresses Under £10, etc) are genuinely useful price-anchors, and the editorial grid of summer looks is cohesive. But nothing here earns attention — there's no code, no exclusivity, no collab hook. For a subscriber who just ignored two nearly identical "extra 30% off" sends, a softer "up to 70% off" banner with no mechanism to redeem is going to land as noise.
- The bottom third is wasted real estate: brand logos, a bright green app-download CTA, and a giant ASOS wordmark that contribute nothing to conversion. This email does the job of reminding you the sale exists, but it doesn't give you a reason to act today.

## What's working

- **Category price-anchor CTAs are strong** — "Tops Under £5 / Dresses Under £10 / Footwear Under £30 / Bestsellers Under £40" is a much more useful segmentation than generic "Shop Women's/Men's" because it frames value immediately.
- **Hero headline is clear and prominent** — "Up to 70% off Sale styles" reads instantly; no ambiguity about what the email is for.
- **Editorial grid is cohesive** — six summer looks in muted, on-trend tones present a consistent aesthetic without visual clutter.
- **"Limited time only" urgency line present** — small but visible below the headline, at least gesturing at a deadline.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap; the email displays correctly.

## What's weak

- **No promo code** — after two consecutive code-driven sends (EXTRA30 / BONUS), there's nothing to redeem here, which makes the offer feel passive and harder to act on.
- **"Limited time only" with no date** — the urgency line is toothless; subscribers know the ASOS sale runs indefinitely.
- **App CTA is the loudest color on the page** — the neon-green "Download the App" banner visually dominates the lower half and competes with the actual sale content for the eye.
- **Brand logo grid adds no conversion value** — Levi's, New Balance, etc. logos with no linked products or offers are decoration.
- **Subheadline copy is illegible at this render size** — the fine-print line below "Up to 70% off" is too small to read in the hero; whatever it says is lost.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific deadline.** "Ends midnight Sunday" turns "limited time only" from wallpaper into genuine urgency. This is the single biggest lift available — the July 11 send got a 9/10 partly because it had a Monday deadline visible in the hero.
- 2. **Reinstate a promo code or stacked offer.** The cadence history shows ASOS subscribers respond to codes. A soft "no mechanism" send after two code sends reads as a downgrade.
- 3. **Move the app CTA to the footer or remove it.** The neon green block mid-email hijacks the eye away from the shop CTAs. App installs are a separate KPI; don't let them cannibalize click-through on the sale.
- 4. **Link the brand logos to filtered sale pages.** "Shop Levi's Under £30" converts; a logo does not.
- 5. **Expand the subheadline into a readable callout.** Whatever the fine-print line says in the hero, either make it readable or cut it — unreadable copy creates distrust.
