# ASOS Premier: 25% off 1000s of styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- ASOS drops a Premier member exclusive — 25% off sitewide with code PREMIER, deadline Sunday — as a direct escalation of the 20% off blast sent six days ago on June 9. The structure is nearly identical to that send: two editorial model shots, a personalized picks grid, and a single "SHOP NOW" CTA. The Premier framing does real work; it positions this as a member reward above the general discount, and the subject line earns that read cleanly. At 38 characters, it's one of the sharper subject lines in this brand's recent run.
- The "Your personalised picks" 2x2 grid is the email's strongest differentiating element and the most compelling reason to click. It's also buried in the lower third. The hero, by contrast, is recycled editorial creative from what feels like the same template as June 9 — same layout skeleton, swapped percentage. For Premier subscribers who got both sends, the visual similarity makes this feel less exclusive than the copy claims.
- A visible rendering artifact ("Ends Sunday free ?") in the body copy is the most damaging issue: it undercuts trust in an email that's asking you to use a code and hit a deadline. Otherwise clean, functional, and logical within ASOS's cadence — but the creative didn't evolve enough from the June 9 send to feel genuinely earned for Premier members.

## What's working

- **Premier exclusivity framing**: "ASOS Premier:" prefix in the subject reads as a member-gated deal, not a mass blast — lifts perceived value above the June 9 general send.
- **Concrete, above-the-fold offer**: Percentage, code, and deadline are all visible in the hero without scrolling.
- **"Your personalised picks" grid**: Browse-history-driven 2x2 product grid is the email's most differentiated element and the single strongest reason to click.
- **Logical offer escalation**: 20% for everyone on June 9 → 25% for Premier on June 15 is a defensible loyalty narrative that rewards program members.
- **Clean visual hierarchy**: Hero headline → offer → CTA → picks flows without competing calls-to-action cluttering the scroll.

## What's weak

- **"Ends Sunday free ?" artifact**: Visible garbled copy after the deadline in the hero body text — looks like a broken special character or a merge field that didn't resolve — undercuts an otherwise polished Premier send.
- **Generic CTA into a broad sale**: "SHOP NOW" → "1000s of styles" diffuses the Premier-member energy; the personalised picks grid already does the targeting work, but the CTA ignores it.
- **Template too similar to June 9**: Same editorial model shots, same layout skeleton, percentage swapped from 20% to 25% — subscribers who got both will pattern-match this as a discount repeat, not an exclusive.
- **No product specificity in the hero**: Two editorial shots with no linked product category; nothing concrete to imagine wearing or buying.
- **Brand logo carousel ("Shop Our Top Brands") dilutes the exclusive feel**: Below a member-exclusive discount, a generic partner logo strip pads the scroll and signals mass marketing rather than curation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the "Ends Sunday free ?" artifact immediately** — this is the highest priority; a broken character in the offer block creates doubt at exactly the moment trust matters most.
- 2. **Tie the hero CTA to the personalised picks landing page, not sitewide** — change "SHOP NOW" to "Shop Your Picks" and link to the Premier member's curated landing; the recommendation grid already earns the click, let the CTA cash it in.
- 3. **Move the personalised picks grid above the editorial shots** — it's the most Premier-specific element in the email and it's buried after generic creative that every subscriber sees.
- 4. **Sharpen the deadline** — "Ends Sunday" is vague; "Ends Sunday 11:59 PM" or the explicit date adds credibility and urgency.
- 5. **Drop the brand logo carousel** — it competes with the exclusivity message and adds unnecessary scroll before the app CTA.

- **Subject Alt B:** `ASOS Premier perk: 5% better than last week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code PREMIER at checkout. Your member deal, ends Sunday night.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We bumped it to 25% for Premier. Better than the 20% everyone else got.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags placed inside `<body>/<p>` — confirmed defect.**

The document opens as `<html><body><div ...></div><p><meta http-equiv="Content-Type" ...><meta charset="utf-8">...`. There is no visible `<head>` block. All four `<meta>` declarations (Content-Type, charset, viewport, format-detection, x-apple-disable-message-reformatting) are nested inside a `<p>` element in `<body>`.

- HTML spec requires `<meta>` in `<head>`. Placement inside `<body>/<p>` is invalid.
- Mozilla/Chrome parsers will silently relocate or discard these; some email clients (notably older Lotus Notes, some Samsung Mail builds) will not, causing charset mis-detection or viewport scaling failures.
- The `<title>` tag is also inside this `<p>` block, which is invalid.

**Preheader text misconfiguration — confirmed.**

Two hidden `<div>` elements serve as preheader:

```html
<!-- First div — body-level, appears before the conditional: EMPTY -->
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;mso-hide:all;font-size:1px;..."></div>

<!-- Second div — inside <!--[if false]><!--> conditional: HAS TEXT -->
<div style="display: none; mso-hide: all;">Sign up to claim your discount</div>
```

The first `<div>` (the one visible to all clients unconditionally) is empty. The second `<div>` containing `"Sign up to claim your discount"` sits inside a `<!--[if false]><!-->`  MSO conditional, which renders it invisible to Outlook. Outlook's preview pane will fall through to the first visible body text rather than the intended preheader string. Non-Outlook clients receive `display:none` on both divs, so the preheader renders as empty in preview — only the spacer zero-width characters (`&nbsp;‌`) are present.

**Net effect:** preheader preview text is effectively blank or shows raw body copy across all major clients.

**`img` tags have no `alt` attributes confirmed missing** from the source structure (the `img { display: block; border: none; }` CSS rule is present but there are no `alt=""` declarations visible for any images). Images-off rendering will show broken image icons with no descriptive text.
