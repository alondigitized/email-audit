# New arrivals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- ASOS sends "New arrivals" again — the same subject line used just 8 days ago on May 26, which itself scored an 8/10. That recycled label is the single biggest miss here, and it drags down an otherwise competent editorial. The photography is clean, the summer aesthetic is well-timed for June, and the layout renders without issue. But there is zero commercial hook: no discount, no urgency, no personalization, nothing to convert a browser into a buyer in what is a peak summer-spend window.
- The one genuinely interesting move is the "YOUR MOST HYPED / SHOP BACK IN STOCK" module mid-scroll — a scarcity and social-proof signal that the new arrivals grid entirely lacks. The problem is that it's buried after the product dump, where engagement has already fallen off. That section deserves to be higher or broken into its own send.
- Verdict: Well-shot, cleanly rendered, editorially inert. ASOS continues the editorial-fatigue pattern visible since May 18 — and this time they didn't even bother with a new subject line.

## What's working

- Summer photography is cohesive and on-season — warm neutral tones, light fabrics, June-appropriate styling across all product shots.
- "YOUR MOST HYPED" back-in-stock module introduces scarcity language that the rest of the email completely avoids.
- "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" is the sharpest CTA copy in the email — specific verb, clear inventory hook.
- Clean render with no broken images, no overlap, no layout failures.
- Brand bar (Topshop, Reclaimed Vintage, Levi's, New Balance) surfaces some sub-brand variety without cluttering the flow.

## What's weak

- Subject line "New arrivals" is a direct repeat of the May 26 send — any engaged subscriber is seeing the same inbox label twice in eight days.
- No offer, no price anchor, no discount — nothing to convert a browse into a purchase in a peak June spend window.
- The product grid has no editorial angle or curation logic visible — it reads as an undifferentiated dump of ~8 items.
- The "MOST HYPED" back-in-stock section, the most compelling hook in the email, is below the fold and gets a fraction of the real estate the generic grid occupies.
- No personalization signal — ASOS has behavioral data; none of it surfaces here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the subject line.** "New arrivals" ran May 26. Sending it again on June 3 trains subscribers to treat it as noise. Angle into what's actually new — a category, a trend, a price tier.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: summer going-out fits`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just dropped: your next holiday look`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Back-in-stock faves + fresh drops for June`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces everyone's been waiting for — they're back`
- 2. **Promote the back-in-stock section above the fold or split it into its own send.** The scarcity signal ("missed it? it's back") is the one hook in this email with real urgency. It belongs at the top, or in a dedicated send that leads with that hook and lets it breathe.
- 3. **Add a light commercial signal.** Free next-day delivery window, ASOS Premier trial prompt, student discount reminder — anything that gives a subscriber a reason to click beyond casual browsing.
- 4. **Give the product grid a filter or editorial frame.** "Best of summer 2026," "going-out edition," "under £30" — even a single line of curation copy turns a grid into a recommendation and lifts click intent.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta>` tags misplaced inside `<body>`:**
```html
<body>
  <div style="display:none..."></div>
  <p>
    <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    ...
```
Meta tags must live in `<head>`. Placing them inside `<p>` inside `<body>` is invalid HTML. Most clients are lenient here, but it can confuse Outlook's Word rendering engine and cause unpredictable layout behavior.

**Duplicate charset declarations:**
Both `http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `charset="utf-8"` are declared simultaneously — redundant, though harmless.

**`FuturaPT-DemiBold` referenced but never declared:**
```css
h1, h2, h3 {
  font-family: 'FuturaPT-DemiBold', Futura, Arial, sans-serif;
}
```
Only `FuturaPT-Book` and `FuturaPT-Heavy` have `@font-face` declarations. `FuturaPT-DemiBold` will silently fall through to `Futura` → `Arial` on all headings.

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` declared at `font-weight: 600`:**
```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "FuturaPT-Heavy";
  font-weight: 600;
}
```
"Heavy" conventionally maps to 800–900. Declaring it at 600 means any `font-weight: 700+` rule will not select this face as expected.

**Preheader structure:**
Two hidden divs exist — the first is empty, the second contains the actual preheader text (`Shop what's just landed at ASOS`). The empty first div is vestigial; no functional break, but indicates a template assembly issue.

**Alt text:** Cannot assess — image tags not present in the truncated source.

**Cannot fully assess** — physical mailing address and unsubscribe link are not present in the truncated source. These are required by CAN-SPAM and must appear in the full email; flag for verification against the complete HTML.

**From domain:** `hello@official.asos.com` sends from a subdomain (`official.asos.com`). SPF and DKIM records must explicitly cover this subdomain — it will not inherit `asos.com`'s top-level authentication. Verify with:
