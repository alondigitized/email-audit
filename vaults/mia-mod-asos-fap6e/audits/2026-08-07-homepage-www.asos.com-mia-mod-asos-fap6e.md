# Homepage snapshot · www.asos.com · 2026-08-07
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is giving me full campaign-mode ASOS — big glossy "STYLE EACH MOMENT" hero with two model-shots and nothing else. It reads like a brand awareness splash, not a "come back and shop" homepage — there's zero acknowledgment I've been here before, no Premier mention, no denim or festival edit pushed at me despite that being literally my whole shopping personality.

## What's working

- The **"SHOP WOMENS" / "SHOP MENS"** split buttons are chunky, thumb-friendly, and impossible to miss on mobile — one tap and I'm in.
- The hero photography itself is genuinely on-brand for me — the model's fringed zebra-print top and denim shorts combo is exactly the kind of statement-piece styling I'd screenshot for a festival lookbook.
- Clean layout, no clutter, no popup shoving itself in my face the second I land. Respect.

## What's weak

- **"STYLE EACH MOMENT"** is filler tagline energy — it says nothing about what's actually new or why I should care today.
- Not a single % off, £ amount, or free next-day/Premier callout anywhere above the fold — for a brand whose whole hook with me is free next-day shipping, that's a miss.
- **"New here? Get your first-timer discount"** banner literally excludes me — I'm a repeat Premier shopper, not new, so this tile is dead space for my account.
- No denim rail, no festival edit, no "recently viewed" — nothing that says the site remembers I buy 4 and return 2 every haul.

## Recommendations

- Swap the generic "STYLE EACH MOMENT" line for something with an actual hook — e.g. "New in: Denim Edit" or a festival-season callout tied to what's trending.
- Add a Premier/loyalty strip right under the hero — even just "Premier: your next-day is free" would remind me why I'm paying for it.
- Replace or duplicate the "first-timer discount" tile with a returning-customer one — "Welcome back, Mia" or a recently-viewed carousel would land way better than a new-customer ad.
- Put one concrete offer above the fold — a %, a threshold, anything with a number on it.
