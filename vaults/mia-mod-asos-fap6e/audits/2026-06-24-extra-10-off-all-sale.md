# Extra 10% off all Sale
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS's sale escalation email — the classic "you didn't bite at 60% off, so here's an extra 10% to close the deal." Two days after the June 22 sale launch, the mechanism is sound: weekend drop, code expires Monday 9am, forcing a decision before the workweek starts. The hero executes this cleanly — red type, promo code front and center, dual CTAs, deadline visible without scrolling.
- The best line in the email is buried in hero subtext: *"We know how hard you've been resisting those prices."* That single sentence does more conversion work than the entire product grid below it — it acknowledges subscriber psychology with genuine brand wit. Everything after the hero, however, progressively loses focus. The product grid carries no sale pricing, the "Shop our Top Brands" logos feel like filler, and the app download CTA is a full non-sequitur that belongs in a retention email, not a sale close.
- The other editorial send that landed the same day (new arrivals, per brand history) is the broader problem ASOS needs to address at the calendar level. Two emails in one day — one sale-urgent, one browsery — train subscribers to treat at least one as noise.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is surgical** — "EXTRA 10% OFF ALL SALE" in red caps, code EXTRA, and "Ends Monday 9am" are all above the fold and readable at a glance.
- **Promo code is explicit and simple** — EXTRA is easy to remember and type; no alphanumeric mess.
- **"We know how hard you've been resisting those prices"** — authentic brand voice that signals audience awareness, rare in template-heavy sale blasts.
- **Dual gender CTAs** — SHOP WOMEN'S / SHOP MEN'S are unambiguous and correctly sized for tap targets.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout breakage, no overlap.

## What's weak

- **Product grid shows zero sale pricing** — crossed-out prices or "was/now" comparisons are absent; the grid reads like a regular editorial drop rather than a sale urgency module.
- **"Shop our Top Brands" and "Download the App" modules dilute the conversion ask** — this email has one job; those modules serve a different one.
- **Deadline not surfaced in subject line** — "Ends Monday 9am" is inside the email; a subscriber scanning their inbox never sees it, which leaves open-rate lift on the table.
- **No personalization** — same six product tiles for every subscriber regardless of category or browse history.
- **Same-day editorial conflict** — the June 24 new arrivals send competed for inbox real estate and mixed the brand's message on the heaviest conversion day of the sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the deadline into the subject line** — "Ends Monday 9am" is the highest-leverage urgency signal in the email and it's invisible from the inbox. Surfacing it would lift opens.
- 2. **Add sale pricing to every product tile** — show the original price struck through and the code-applied final price. Without this, the grid is cosmetic filler that doesn't support the ask.
- 3. **Cut the bottom modules entirely** — brand logos and app download belong in a different send. Replace with a second sale-urgency product block ("Top sale picks — moving fast") or remove entirely and let the page breathe.
- 4. **Elevate the "resisting those prices" line** — it's currently subordinate body copy; it's good enough to be a header or replace the subhead. That voice is the moat; lean into it.
- 5. **Suppress the editorial send on the same day** — a calendar-level fix, not a creative one, but the highest-leverage change available.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty preheader (confirmed issue)**
The hidden preheader `<div>` contains no text:
```html
<div style="display:none !important;...max-height:0px;...overflow:hidden;"></div>
```
With no preheader content, inbox clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will pull preview text from the first visible body content — likely a nav link or hero copy. This is a common mistake that damages open-rate signal.

**SVG font format in `@font-face` (minor)**
Both `FuturaPT-Book` and `FuturaPT-Heavy` declarations include `format("svg")`. SVG fonts are deprecated; no current email client requires them. This adds unnecessary request overhead on old WebKit.

**Duplicate charset declaration (harmless but redundant)**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">
<meta charset="utf-8">
```
Both declare UTF-8. Only the second is needed per HTML5; the first is legacy. No rendering impact.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on body**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }
```
`none` (vs. `100%`) fully disables iOS auto-scaling. On `*` the value is `100%` (correct), but the body override to `none` may suppress scaling in some iOS Mail versions at very small viewport widths.

**Mobile breakpoint at 460px**
The sole responsive breakpoint is `max-width: 460px`, which is narrower than the common 480px standard. Devices rendering at 461–479px (e.g., some Android phones in portrait) will receive the desktop layout. Functional but worth noting.

**`font-size: 0em` on all table elements**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; }
```
Intentional whitespace-gap suppression — standard practice, not an issue.

**VML CTA fallback present**
`.vml-cta { display: block !important; }` inside `[if gt mso 15]` conditional — correct Outlook button rendering pattern. No issue.
