# Sale: further reductions
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS's ninth promotional sale email since June 22 — and the one where the narrative fully collapses. The subject promises "further reductions," yet the hero delivers the identical claim from June 29: "SALE: UP TO 70% OFF." Any subscriber who has been paying attention will feel the contradiction immediately. After a promising editorial reset on July 1 ("Guess who's back?") that looked like ASOS had learned something about list fatigue, the July 4 "extra 30% off" and now this "further reductions" send undo that goodwill in under a week.
- The email itself is technically clean — the render is solid, the six-button navigation grid gives subscribers room to self-select, and the "THESE STYLES SUIT YOU" block is a welcome personalization gesture. But the creative is copy-pasted from prior sale sends: same red-on-black hero treatment, same navigation grid structure, same "limited time only" disclaimer with no actual date.
- The core problem is an empty subject line paired with an unchanged hero. "Further" has to do all the lifting, and it does none of it. A subscriber who ignored 70% off on June 29 is not going to open for a subject that can't even say 75%.

## What's working

- **Hero is unmistakable** — red-on-black "SALE: UP TO 70% OFF" dominates the fold and reads instantly.
- **Six-button navigation grid** lets subscribers self-segment without being funneled into one path — SHOP WOMEN'S / SHOP MEN'S / NEW LINES ADDED / SELLING FAST / SUMMER SALE / BIGGEST DEALS is the right architecture for a broad audience.
- **"THESE STYLES SUIT YOU"** adds a personalization signal that distinguishes this from a pure blast; the styled product images underneath feel editorially curated.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, brand logos in the top-brands rail load correctly across the visible layout.

## What's weak

- **Subject/hero contradiction** — "further reductions" implies a bigger number, but the hero still reads "up to 70% off," word for word what the June 29 email said. A returning subscriber will notice.
- **"Further" is empty** — no percentage, no category, no product — it signals effort without delivering information.
- **Cadence has crossed saturation** — ninth sale email in fifteen days. The July 1 editorial pivot bought goodwill; this send spends it with nothing new to show.
- **Urgency is cosmetic** — "Limited time only. Selected styles marked down as shown." has appeared verbatim in multiple prior sends. It no longer functions as urgency.
- **Top brands rail is filler** — showing Topman, GAP, Levi's, New Balance without any "now X% off" context attached to each logo adds visual length without information.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Quantify "further" in the hero.** If anything has dropped below 70%, that number belongs in the hero. "SALE: NOW UP TO 75% OFF" or "SALE: PRICES JUST HIT A NEW LOW" would immediately justify opening. A hero that still reads 70% after a subject that says "further reductions" is a broken promise visible to any subscriber who opened June 29's email.
- 2. **Lead with one specific category drop.** Swap one of the six generic navigation buttons for a concrete stat: "Dresses: now from £5" or "Footwear just dropped — up to 80% off." Give the subscriber a reason to click that they haven't seen in the prior eight emails.
- 3. **Kill "limited time only," add a real date.** "Offer ends Sunday midnight" outperforms "limited time only" at every stage of a prolonged sale. ASOS has been running this sale for fifteen days; urgency claims without a deadline have no credibility left.
- 4. **Don't send unless the offer changes.** The July 4 + July 6 back-to-back is one email too many. If the inventory hasn't moved meaningfully, sit on the send and let inbox silence do the work.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**SVG font format in `@font-face` stack — no upside, potential extra request:**
```css
url("https://images.asos-media.com/notification/content/fonts/FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
```
SVG fonts are deprecated and unsupported in every current email client. This entry triggers a potentially wasted HTTP request on some older WebKit clients before falling through to TTF. Remove it from both font declarations.

**`font-display: swap` in email CSS:**
```css
font-display: swap;
```
Supported only in Gmail (Chromium-based webmail) and a handful of webmail clients. In native iOS Mail, Outlook, and Samsung Mail it is silently ignored. Not harmful, but it adds noise and implies a rendering behavior that won't occur in most clients.

**Mobile breakpoint at `460px` is too narrow:**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px) { ... }
```
Standard modern email frameworks use `600px` or at minimum `480px`. Viewports between 460–600px (common on mid-range Android devices at 1x zoom) receive the full desktop layout. All mobile-responsive class overrides (`mob100`, `nodesktop`, `nomob`, etc.) will fail to trigger on these devices.

**`-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased !important` on universal selector:**
```css
* { -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased !important; }
```
Applying this globally with `!important` can affect button and image rendering in Apple Mail and iOS in unexpected ways. Should be scoped to text elements only.
