# Your edit is here
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS in full editorial-drift mode — a new-in showcase with no offer, no urgency, and no discount. "Your edit is here" is the kind of vague personalization theater that feels tailored but delivers nothing concrete. Seven product images, two CTAs that both say "SHOP NEW IN," and one restock nudge for "YOUR MOST HYPED" item — that's the entire payload. No sale code, no price reveal, no deadline.
- After the July 18 "Extra 30% off selected Sale styles" send (which stacked a promo code on existing sale pricing and gave you a Monday deadline), this email reads like a deliberate editorial cooldown. That's a reasonable cadence choice — but the execution is lazy. "Your edit" implies personalization; the email doesn't deliver it. The products shown are visually coherent (earthy tones, summer silhouettes) but there's no named capsule, no editorial voice, no "why these?"

- Visual product photography is strong — earth tones and summer silhouettes hang together as a coherent story
- The restock module ("YOUR MOST HYPED / Missed it the first time around?") is the sharpest copy in the email and carries real urgency
- App download CTA is cleanly placed at the footer, not interrupting the main flow
- Top brand logos (New Balance, etc.) add credibility and cross-sell without cluttering the hierarchy
- Render is clean — no broken images, no layout breaks, text sits properly on all blocks

## What's weak

- "Your edit is here" promises personalization it doesn't deliver — no name, no category, no "based on your browsing"
- Both primary CTAs say "SHOP NEW IN" — identical copy on two separate modules kills any sense of distinct destination
- No offer anywhere — after two consecutive sale/discount sends, switching to full-price editorial with zero hook is a cold stop
- The "YOUR MOST HYPED" section is undersized and buried; the most compelling mechanic in the email is treated as a secondary module
- Subject "Your edit is here" at 18 characters reads terse and cryptic in the inbox — no concrete signal of what the "edit" contains

- 1. **Make the personalization real or drop the word "edit."** Either surface an actual behavioral signal ("Your edit based on what you've been saving") or reframe as a curated capsule with a named POV ("Our editors' picks for right now"). "Your edit is here" without personalization is a promise the email breaks on open.
- 2. **Give "MOST HYPED / BACK IN STOCK" the hero slot.** Restocked inventory is the only genuine scarcity in this email — move it above the fold, make it the primary CTA, and give it a count ("Only 3 styles left"). Right now ASOS is burying its best card.
- 3. **Differentiate the two CTAs.** Second module's CTA could say "SEE WHAT'S NEW THIS WEEK" or "JUST DROPPED" — anything that signals a distinct destination from the hero.
- 4. **Add one anchor price or offer.** Even "from £12" or "styles from £20" would give the reader a reason to click beyond curiosity. Full-price editorial needs a hook.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

| Issue | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `<meta>` tags in `<body>` wrapped in `<p>` | `<body> <div>…</div> <p> <meta http-equiv=…> <meta charset=…>` — both metas appear as body children, not in `<head>` | High |
| Empty preheader div | `<div style="display:none !important;…"></div>` — no text content between tags | Medium |
| `-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `body` | `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }` — blocks user font scaling, WCAG 1.4.4 violation | Medium |
| Conflicting text-size-adjust | `body` sets `none`; `*` selector sets `100%` — body-level wins, negating the `*` fix | Low |
| SVG font format included | `url("…FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")` — SVG fonts are deprecated in all modern browsers; adds HTTP request overhead | Low |
| Single breakpoint at 460px | `@media screen and (max-width:460px)` only — no 600px breakpoint for tablet clients | Low |
| `font-size: 0em` on `table, tr, td, th` | Global rule — standard whitespace hack, but can suppress screen reader content if text is placed directly in `<td>` without an inner element | Low |

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Fix `<meta>` tag placement (High)** — Move all `<meta>` tags into a `<head>` block. The current `<p><meta …></p>` in `<body>` is invalid and will be silently dropped or misparsed by strict renderers (notably some versions of Outlook and Gmail's sanitizer).

2. **Populate the preheader div (Medium)** — Add static fallback copy or a merge token with a non-empty default. An empty preheader causes inbox clients to pull the first rendered text (often an image alt or navigation label) as preview text.

3. **Change `-webkit-text-size-adjust` to `100%` on `body` (Medium)** — Replace `none` with `100%` to restore user font scaling without losing the intended rendering fix. This aligns with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

4. **Remove SVG font format (Low)** — Drop the `.svg` `@font-face` source; it saves one HTTP request and eliminates a format no current email client renders.

5. **Add 600px breakpoint (Low)** — Tablet-width clients (iPad Mail, some Android) fall through to desktop layout at widths where the single-column mobile layout would improve readability.
