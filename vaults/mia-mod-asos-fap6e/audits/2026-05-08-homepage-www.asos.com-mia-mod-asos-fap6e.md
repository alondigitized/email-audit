# Homepage snapshot · www.asos.com · 2026-05-08
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-08**
## Executive summary

- OK so ASOS is clearly going for that editorial fashion-brand energy right now — "Style Each Moment" with a moody dual-model hero feels very lookbook, very considered. It reads like a brand that knows it's fashion, not just a shop. But honestly for me as a Premier member who's been shopping here forever, this homepage is giving "new visitor landing page" vibes — there's zero acknowledgement that I exist or that I have status here.

## What's working

- **Dual-model hero with editorial split** — the red-on-red styling between the female and male models is cohesive and genuinely cool. It stopped my scroll reflex.
- **"SHOP WOMENS" / "SHOP MENS" two-button structure** — dead simple, no friction, I know exactly what to tap. Mobile-optimised sizing on those buttons too.
- **"Style Each Moment" tagline** — short, punchy, doesn't try too hard. Feels like something I'd actually say ironically to a mate.
- **Model casting** — the woman with the oversized black sunglasses looks exactly like someone in my friend group. Feels like my world, not a catalogue.
- **Clean dark nav bar** — the heart/wishlist and bag icons are immediately visible. I can jump straight to my saved items without hunting.

## What's weak

- **Zero Premier member recognition** — I pay for Premier, I get free next-day, and this homepage treats me like I just found ASOS for the first time. No badge, no "Welcome back Mia", nothing. That's a miss.
- **"New here? Get your first-timer discount" strip** — visible at the bottom of the viewport but it's literally not for me. As a loyal customer it actually makes me feel slightly deprioritised.
- **No category entry points beyond Womens/Mens** — I want to tap straight into Denim or Festival or New In. Two big binary buttons is fine for a new visitor but for me it adds an extra step.
- **No urgency or offer above the fold** — there's no sale, no countdown, no "just dropped" energy. It's visually beautiful but it gives me no reason to tap *now* rather than close the tab and come back later.
- **No trust signals whatsoever** — no "free returns", no "next-day delivery", no star ratings. For a new visitor this could feel risky. Even for me it's a missed opportunity to remind me why ASOS is worth it.

## Recommendations

- **Surface a Premier member welcome module** — even a small banner ("Hey Premier member — your free next-day is on") above the hero or in the nav would make returning shoppers feel seen and reduce bounce for the most loyal cohort.
- **Swap one of the two CTAs for a category-specific hook** — replace "SHOP MENS" with "New In Womens" or "Shop Festival" to give me a faster path to what I actually want, especially during drop season.
- **Add a slim promotional strip between the hero and the new-visitor modules** — something like "25% off selected denim — today only" gives a reason to act now without cluttering the editorial hero.
- **Inject one trust/delivery signal into the hero overlay** — a subtle "Free next-day on orders over £X" ghost text below the CTAs takes up two lines and immediately handles the "is this worth it?" question.
