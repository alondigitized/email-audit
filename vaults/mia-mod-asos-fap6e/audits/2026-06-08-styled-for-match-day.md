# Styled for match day
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- ASOS dropped this football-fashion editorial three days before the FIFA World Cup 2026 kicks off — the timing is genuinely sharp, and the creative earns it. Three styled models, each in a different take on football-adjacent fashion (yellow jersey as statement piece, green athletic-to-street, full co-ord accessories), give the send a real editorial point of view rather than a spray of products. The photography is clean, the brand palette is confident, and "Football 'fits" as a header is a better hook than the subject line that lands in the inbox.
- The miss is purely structural: this is an editorial with zero commercial tension. There is no offer, no deadline, and no price signal anywhere in the scroll. For a subscriber who has seen ASOS send six editorials in the last two weeks — including two "New arrivals" on the same day — clicking requires real category interest to overcome the lack of a reason-to-act now.
- Verdict: a well-timed, well-shot campaign that leaves conversion points on the table. It looks great and says almost nothing that would move a hesitant buyer.

## What's working

- World Cup timing is near-perfect — three days before the tournament opens, this is the most contextually relevant ASOS send since the June 4 summer editorial.
- Photography is strong: the yellow jersey hero shot has genuine editorial energy, not just a white-background product dump.
- "Football 'fits" headline is punchy and specific; better copy than the subject line.
- Three distinct outfit narratives (statement jersey, street-athletic, accessories co-ord) give different entry points without feeling scattered.
- Layout is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, the eye moves top-to-bottom without confusion.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere: no %, no £/$, no free shipping — pure editorial in a cadence that has been heavily editorial all month.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is generic; it doesn't say what you're shopping for or why today specifically.
- The match-day accessories panel feels rushed — small product thumbnails with no framing copy, easily skipped.
- Subject line "Styled for match day" is safe and forgettable; it doesn't tease the creative angle (three-way editorial, football colour-palette, etc.) that actually makes the email worth opening.
- No personalization or member signal — this reads like a broadcast to the full list with no segmentation nod.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single commercial hook** — even "Jerseys from £18 · Free next-day on orders over £35" under the hero CTA converts the editorial into a reason to act. The content is good enough; it just needs a price anchor.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop the 'fits" or "Explore Match-Day Looks" — specific, on-voice, earns the scroll.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject** to tease the editorial angle, not just the occasion:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your match-day outfit, sorted`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Football jerseys are having a moment`
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader** to complement rather than echo:
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three ways to wear the football trend this summer`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From the terrace to the pub — looks for all of it`
- 5. **Give the accessories section a proper headline** — "Co-ordinate the whole look" with one anchor price point would rescue the bottom third.
