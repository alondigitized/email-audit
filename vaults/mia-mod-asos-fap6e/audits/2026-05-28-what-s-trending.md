# What's trending?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- "What's trending?" is ASOS's third editorial send in three days (May 26: "New arrivals," May 26: "Going somewhere?," now May 28). The format is familiar to the point of fatigue: numbered category tiles at the top, a brand spotlight strip, a couple of editorial read-more blocks, and a "Download the App" footer CTA. There is no offer anywhere in the email — no percentage off, no free shipping, nothing that rewards the click.
- What saves it from being a complete miss is structural competence: the 4-tile numbered grid ("Festival," "Sundresses," "Sequins," "Co-ords") gives the eye somewhere to go, the photography is clean, and the summer/festival framing is seasonally right for late May. The brand partnership strip (Hollister and others) is a rare differentiated element not seen in recent sends.
- But the subject line "What's trending?" is one of the laziest constructions in email marketing — a question so vague it could belong to any brand in any category on any day. After four editorial-only sends in a row, this email needed either an offer or a much more arresting angle to earn the click.

## What's working

- **Numbered 4-tile grid is visually organized** — Festival, Sundresses, Sequins, Co-ords give the reader an immediate, scannable map of the email's purpose.
- **Summer/festival timing is accurate** — content aligns with late-May consumer mindset and ASOS's core category strength.
- **Brand partnership strip is differentiated** — the "Shop Summer by Brand" section with Hollister and other brand logos provides a hook that recent ASOS sends haven't offered.
- **SPF / face + body section cross-sells beauty** — a smart category bridge during a seasonal send; product imagery is clean.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds, images load, no broken gaps or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No offer, again** — the last real promo was May 25's "Up to 30% off"; three editorial sends later, subscriber goodwill is being spent with nothing in return.
- **Subject line is generic to the point of waste** — "What's trending?" is interchangeable with "Need some inspo?" (May 25); both ask a vague question and deliver no signal.
- **"Read More" CTAs are inert** — the blog-content blocks ("15 Tips for Radiant Summer Makeup," "These Knitted Styles Are Warm-Weather Approved") point away from product pages; they add scroll length without adding purchase intent.
- **No prices, no social proof** — nothing in the "We Think You'd Love" product grid surfaces a price, a review count, or a bestseller label.
- **Editorial cadence has collapsed** — four editorial-dominant emails in three days erodes the contrast that makes editorial sends feel special.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface one offer, even a modest one.** "Shop Festival + save 15%" or free-shipping on orders over £X would transform this from pure content into a reason to click. The brand partnership strip is the natural hook — tie a brand deal to a limited offer.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to name a specific trend.** "Festival dresses are here" or "Sundresses under £30" beats any question format.
- 3. **Replace "Read More" blocks with shoppable product links.** The SPF and knits content is useful; send readers to a curated landing page, not a blog post.
- 4. **Cut one editorial block entirely.** The knitted-styles block actively works against the summer/festival message; remove it and sharpen the send.
- 5. **Add a price or review signal to the "We Think You'd Love" grid.** Even "from £12" per product doubles purchase intent signals.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **`-webkit-text-size-adjust: none` on `<body>`** (line: `body, #body_style { … -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }`). The `none` value suppresses all font scaling in older WebKit mobile browsers. The safer value is `100%`, which prevents auto-scaling while preserving user zoom. The `*` rule later sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` but body-level `none` may win due to specificity.

- **`font-size: 0em` on all table elements** (`table, tr, td, th { … font-size: 0em; }`). Blanket zero font-size on `<tr>` and `<th>` kills whitespace gaps but breaks any cell that doesn't explicitly reset font-size — screen reader text and VML fallback copy inside tables will be invisible/zero-sized if not individually overridden.

- **SVG font format declared** in both `@font-face` blocks (`format("svg")`). SVG fonts are deprecated; last relevant client was iOS ≤ 4.1 (2010). Dead weight in the source — remove to reduce parse time and payload.

- **`role="presentation"` not visible** on layout tables in the `<head>` or opening `<body>` markup. Without this, screen readers may attempt to interpret table structure as data. Full source needed to confirm whether it appears on outer wrapper tables.

- **No `alt` text visible** on any `<img>` tags in the available source. Images are set to `display: block` globally but alt attributes are not confirmed. If images are missing alt text, image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate proxies) will render blank spaces with no context.

- **Media query breakpoint at 460px** — ASOS uses 460px rather than the more common 480px. Not a bug, but worth flagging if third-party ESPs or rendering test suites use 480px as the mobile boundary.
