# Go from city to coast with Cotton On
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a Cotton On brand-spotlight send — ASOS giving over the entire canvas to a third-party brand partner rather than curating their own product edit. The summer aesthetic lands perfectly: warm sunset tones, white sundress, bandeau top with denim shorts, all shot with that golden-hour California feel. The photography is genuinely good and the layout is clean.
- The problem is the email doesn't ask you to do anything specific. "Shop Cotton On" dumps you on a brand page rather than a curated summer edit, there's no offer, no price signal, and no product detail. After ASOS's heavy send cadence this week — six emails since May 25, including two on the same day — a brand-collab editorial with zero incentive is thin content. Subscribers who don't already know Cotton On won't be compelled; those who do know it already shop it directly.
- This feels like a commercial placement packaged as an editorial. It's not bad-looking, but it's not working hard either.

## What's working

- **Photography is strong** — the sunset palette is cohesive and seasonally on-point for late May.
- **Clean single-column layout** — no render bugs, clear visual flow from hero to CTA to supporting shots.
- **"SHOP COTTON ON" is unambiguous** — the CTA button does exactly what it says; no confusion about where it goes.
- **Summer timing is right** — city-to-coast framing lands well in the week before June.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no incentive** — this is a pure editorial with zero price or discount signal; most subscribers won't click to browse with no reason to.
- **"City to coast" is Cotton On's tagline, not ASOS copy** — the subject and hero headline feel like someone else's campaign, creating a slight brand-identity mismatch in the inbox.
- **No specific product is featured** — two editorial shots and a brand-page CTA leave no purchase hook; compare to the leopard-print edit (May 22) which at least had a tight product category.
- **Heavy cadence drag** — this is the 7th ASOS email since May 25; a no-offer editorial is the worst content to send when subscriber fatigue is highest.
- **No price or product detail visible** — no sizing, no fit copy, no SKU callout; nothing that moves a shopper closer to a decision.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer or hook.** Even "New in: Cotton On — free delivery on every order" or "Cotton On: styles from $25" gives a browser a reason to click. A pure brand-awareness email belongs in a less fatigued cadence window, not after six sends in a week.
- 2. **Feature one specific product** — take the white sundress or the burgundy bandeau and give it a name, a price, and a "Shop the look" CTA. This is what the leopard-print edit did well; apply it here.
- 3. **Tighten the subject to be ASOS-voiced and offer-anchored:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Cotton On just landed — here's what to wear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in: Cotton On summer picks`
- 4. **Preheader should carry the offer or a teaser, not echo the subject:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh styles from $20 — city dresses to coastal sets`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `White sundresses, bandeau tops, denim shorts — all new in`
- 5. **Pull this send out of the dense cadence.** Running a no-offer brand collab as send #7 in 7 days is the wrong deployment window. Hold it for a day with fewer competing sends, or bundle it into a "new brands" digest.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate charset declaration**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless; one should be removed for cleanliness.

**`-webkit-text-size-adjust` conflict**
```css
body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; }   /* disables scaling */
*    { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }   /* re-enables it */
```
The `*` rule overrides the `body` rule on all child elements. The `none` on `body` is effectively dead. Intent appears to be `none` (suppress iOS font size inflation) — the `*` selector should be audited.

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` weight mismatch**
```css
@font-face { font-family: "FuturaPT-Heavy"; font-weight: 600; }
```
The face is named "Heavy" but declared at weight `600` (semi-bold). True heavy/black weight is `800`–`900`. If any selector requests `font-weight: 800` on this family, the browser may synthesize bold rather than serving the intended face.

**Alt text on images** — not assessable from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes; the base CSS sets `img { display: block; border: none; }` with no alt enforcement.

**Media query breakpoint**: 460px is non-standard (industry norm is 600px). Intentional for ASOS's design system but worth documenting if this template is shared across brands.
