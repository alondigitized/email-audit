# Sale: Bershka, Pull&Bear + more just landed
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is ASOS's "new brands added to the Sale" announcement — a well-timed mid-sale refresh following the June 22 launch and the June 24 extra-10% escalation. The mechanism is smart: after two days of hammering the headline discount, surfacing Bershka, Pull&Bear, and Oysho as fresh entrants gives lapsed openers a reason to re-engage without repeating the same creative. The editorial format (brand hero + product images + shop-now) is ASOS at their most competent.
- The structural problem is a failure of emphasis: the discount percentage is buried in small hero subtext and never reappears at the product or brand-section level. Someone who opens, skims the images, and lands on a "SHOP NOW" button has no visible incentive compared to just browsing the ASOS site. This is a sale email that forgets to sell.
- Three sends in four days is also starting to push the cadence ceiling. The June 25 email has good creative justification, but the brand is spending down subscriber attention fast. This one needed to earn its slot more visibly than it does.

## What's working

- Subject line names the brands explicitly — subscribers with brand affinity to Bershka or Pull&Bear have a concrete reason to open that "Sale update" generics don't provide.
- Hero banner lands immediately and communicates the news: "SALE: NEW BRANDS ADDED" — no ambiguity about what changed.
- Gender-split hero CTAs ("SHOP WOMEN'S" / "SHOP MEN'S") reduce friction at the top of funnel.
- Editorial photography across all three brands is on-brand and consistent — summer styles (dresses, wide-legs, swimwear) are seasonally right for June 25.
- Three distinct brand sections with individual identity (Bershka, Oysho, Pull&Bear) give the email a sense of curation rather than a generic clearance dump.

## What's weak

- The sale discount percentage is visible only in small hero subtext — it never reappears in any brand section, leaving product images without a price hook.
- Every brand-level CTA reads "SHOP NOW" — a generic verb that doesn't reinforce the sale context or the specific brand.
- No deadline or urgency signal anywhere — "just landed" implies freshness but gives no reason to act today over tomorrow.
- The "SAVE ON YOUR EDIT" mid-email banner is visually prominent (bright yellow-green) but isn't clearly tied to any specific action or destination — it floats without purpose.
- Brand logo grid at the bottom (including New Balance and others) muddies the focus; this email is about three specific brands, and the generic brand carousel undercuts the message.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Echo the discount in every brand section.** A single line — "Now in the Sale — up to X% off" — under each brand header would convert browsers who skip the hero body copy. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Make brand CTAs sale-specific.** "SHOP BERSHKA SALE" and "SHOP PULL&BEAR SALE" instead of "SHOP NOW" reinforce the offer and the destination.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "Styles selling fast — sale ends Sunday" gives a reason to act now rather than tab-parking and forgetting.
- 4. **Cut or relink the "SAVE ON YOUR EDIT" banner.** Either make it a CTA to a pre-filtered sale wishlist/edit page, or remove it — it currently wastes the visual weight of the brightest element in the email.
- 5. **Trim the brand logo grid footer.** Linking to New Balance and others dilutes the Bershka/Pull&Bear/Oysho story this email is trying to tell.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bershka & Pull&Bear just hit the Sale — up to X% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New to Sale: Bershka, Pull&Bear & Oysho`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hundreds of new styles now in the Sale — shop before they go`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer's freshest drops just joined the Sale. Shop women's & men's.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Malformed HTML structure**
As delivered, the document opens `<html><body>` with no `<head>` block. The `<meta>` tags (charset, viewport, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, `format-detection`) appear inside a `<body><p>` element:

```html
<!DOCTYPE html> <html><body> <div ...preheader...></div> <p>
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" ...>
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" ...>
```

Meta tags inside `<body>` are invalid per HTML spec. While most email clients are lenient, this can cause viewport scaling to be ignored on iOS (the `width=device-width` meta may not be applied) and triggers validator failures. **Put all `<meta>` tags inside a proper `<head>` block.**

**Empty preheader slot**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;...font-size:1px;...overflow:hidden;"></div>
```
The preheader div has no text content. Inbox clients (Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail) will pull the first visible body text as the preview snippet instead of a crafted message.

**Ticker tape animation — client support gap**
The `@keyframes ticker` animation is gated with `@supports (animation: ticker 5s linear infinite)` which correctly hides the animated version in unsupporting clients via `.fallback`/`.ticker` class swap. However, `@supports` itself is not honored in Outlook (all versions) or older Samsung Mail. Verify the `.fallback` element renders acceptably as a static replacement in those clients.

**Mobile breakpoint at 460px**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px) { ... }
```
Standard email client breakpoints are typically 480px (Gmail app) or 600px. At 460–479px viewport width (common on some Android devices), the desktop layout will render without the responsive overrides. Consider bumping to `max-width:480px`.

**`font-size: 0em` on table elements**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; ... }
```
This is a known whitespace-collapse hack for email. If any `<td>` renders inline text without an explicit `font-size` override on a child element, text will be invisible. Confirm all text-bearing cells have explicit `font-size` set.
