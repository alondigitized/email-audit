# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- "Guess who's back?" is a back-in-stock trigger email that resolves its own subject-line riddle cleanly — the hero lands with "They're back" and "Your waitlist items are back in stock," making the implied personalization (you specifically raised your hand for these) the strongest hook in the send. The concept is sound. Where it stumbles is urgency: items just returned to stock, and there is zero scarcity language, no deadline, no counter. Without any friction toward acting *now*, this is more of a re-introduction than a conversion email.
- The visual execution is confident — five styled shots in a clean grid, diverse cast, two clear CTAs, and a near-perfect descent from hero text to product imagery to action button. The mid-email survey section ("We'd love to hear your thoughts") is a clever dual-purpose mechanic: ASOS doing preference research while the subscriber is already in a clickable mindset. Unusual placement, but defensible.
- Compared to yesterday's "20% off 1000s of styles" (June 9), this send is weaker on conversion incentive but stronger on personalization credibility. A subscriber who waitlisted something has already self-selected — they just need a reason to act today rather than put it off again. This email doesn't give them one.

## What's working

- **Implied personalization lands** — "Your waitlist items" frames this as one-to-one trigger, not a mass blast, even if the grid is generic.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight** — hero text → five product images → "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" in the right reading order, no clutter.
- **Diverse model cast** — five models across different skin tones and hair types, consistent with ASOS's brand positioning.
- **Subject teaser + preview pair well** — "Guess who's back?" answered by the preview's "They're back — Missed it the first time?" creates a micro-reveal that earns the open.
- **Survey engagement mechanic is smart** — polling intent mid-email collects preference signals while the subscriber is warm.

## What's weak

- **Zero scarcity or deadline signal** — back-in-stock without "limited quantities" or "while stock lasts" removes all urgency; why click now vs. next week?
- **No price incentive** — yesterday's email offered 20% off everything; today offers nothing. That contrast actively works against this send.
- **Product grid is anonymous** — five styled images with no item names, prices, or category labels; no way to qualify intent before clicking.
- **Two nearly identical CTAs** — "SHOP NOW" at the top and "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" lower down are redundant; neither is differentiated by destination or product type.
- **Survey section breaks the purchase scent** — mid-funnel pivot to audience research interrupts a subscriber who was moving toward a click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add scarcity copy to the hero** — Swap "Missed it the first time around?" for "Limited stock — they could sell out again." Single highest-leverage change: converts a neutral announcement into an urgency trigger.
- 2. **Label the product grid** — Add item name and price under each of the five images. Back-in-stock emails with labeled products drive higher CTR because subscribers can self-qualify before the click.
- 3. **Collapse to one CTA** — Keep "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" (more specific) and cut or reposition the top "SHOP NOW" button, or give it a distinct purpose ("VIEW YOUR FULL WISHLIST").
- 4. **Move the survey below the fold** — It's valuable data but it's placed where a reinforcing CTA or scarcity callout should live.
- 5. **Add a modest "since you waited" offer** — Even 10% off the specifically waitlisted items would close the gap against yesterday's 20% broad promo and reward the subscriber for their patience.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your waitlist came through — items are back in stock`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back in stock: the pieces you saved are here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `They're selling fast again — grab yours before they're gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited quantities back. Don't miss them twice.`
