# New arrivals
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- After ten days of escalating sale emails and yesterday's "Meet your new-season palette" editorial reset, ASOS sends another full-price new-arrivals email the very next day. The creative is clean and competent — a simple product grid in a consistent aesthetic, two CTAs, a brand-list section — but this send signals the editorial well has run dry fast. Two consecutive editorial emails with no differentiated angle is not a refresh; it's a filler.
- The email does exactly one thing well: it renders cleanly and pushes a coherent women's wardrobe moment (summer silhouettes, neutral-to-bright palette, elevated basics). But without a hero product, a category hook, a price point, or any urgency signal, there is no reason to click today rather than ignore it. "New arrivals" as a subject line is the lowest-information copy available in fashion email — it competes with every other brand's standard-cadence drip.
- Net judgment: a passable but forgettable send that wastes the attention ASOS earned back with the July 1 pivot. The brand is burning its editorial credibility against a blank brief.

## What's working

- **Clean render, no bugs.** The product grid stacks consistently, fonts are legible, no broken images or layout shifts.
- **Double CTA placement works.** "SHOP NEW IN" appears above and below the grid, giving both early-scrollers and readers who browse the products a visible action.
- **Product variety creates range.** Sage tank, floral midi, black skirt, strappy heel — five items that together communicate a summer wardrobe story rather than a single category push.
- **Top-brands row adds legitimacy.** Levi's, New Balance, and other visible logos signal breadth and cross-sell potential without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- **"New arrivals" is the laziest subject line in fashion email marketing.** Zero specificity, zero hook, competes with every other brand's placeholder copy.
- **Back-to-back editorial sends with no differentiation.** Yesterday was "new-season palette"; today is "new arrivals." From the subscriber's view, these are the same email twice.
- **No offer, no anchor.** After two weeks of 60–70% off, full-price with no value prop — not even free returns or an editorial price-point callout — removes every conversion lever.
- **No hero product.** Five items without a standout creates visual scatter; the eye browses and exits without a reason to stop.
- **Sub-headline copy is too small to read** at typical email render scale, so any copy doing work below the headline is wasted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the send a category POV.** Instead of "new arrivals" generically, pick one trend or garment the grid earns — "The new summer dress edit," "Three ways to wear the midi right now" — and make the subject line match. This one change lifts both open rate and click depth.
- 2. **Add one friction-reducing hook.** "Free returns on everything new" or "just landed: restocked sizes" requires no discount but gives a reader a reason to click now vs. bookmark-and-forget.
- 3. **Differentiate from July 7.** Yesterday's palette send and today's arrivals email are visually indistinguishable. Swap one of these for a category-led or brand-led email to break the same-same pattern.
- 4. **Promote one hero product with its name.** A single named item (with a visible overlay or caption) anchors the grid and creates a direct link between browse intent and click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just landed: your summer wardrobe reset`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New in: the dresses everyone's looking at`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals across dresses, tops, and more — just added`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free returns on everything new. Styles added today.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — Missing `<head>` element:**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html> <html><body>
  <div style="display:none !important;..."></div>
  <p>
    <meta http-equiv="Content-Type" ...>   ← inside <body><p>
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    <style>...</style>
    <title>New arrivals</title>
```
All `<meta>` and `<style>` declarations are nested inside `<body>` inside a `<p>` tag. Browsers and most email clients are tolerant of this (they hoist `<meta>`/`<style>` via their error-recovery parser), but Outlook's Word rendering engine and some older mobile clients do not. This can cause charset misdetection, broken responsive CSS, and missing font declarations in affected clients.

**Preheader div is empty:**
```html
<div style="display:none !important;visibility:hidden;..."></div>
```
No preheader text is present. Most email clients will pull the first visible body text as inbox preview copy — which in this case is whatever comes immediately after the empty div. This is a content gap but creates a technical risk of rendering boilerplate (e.g., "View in browser" link text) as the snippet.

**Outdated Outlook conditional comment target:**
```html
<!--[if gt mso 15]>
```
`mso 15` = Outlook 2003. This conditional fires for all Outlook versions above 2003. The correct modern target for all Outlook desktop is `[if mso]` or `[if gte mso 9]`. The current selector works but is semantically stale and could silently mis-target if the logic is ever extended.

**Duplicate charset declarations:**
Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8">` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. Redundant but harmless.

**`font-display: swap` in @font-face:**
This CSS property is web-browser-only; it is silently ignored by every email client. Including it adds noise but causes no harm.

**`font-size: 0em` on all table elements:**
```css
table, tr, td, th { font-size: 0em; ... }
```
Standard gap-suppression technique for email, but requires explicit `font-size` overrides on every content cell. If any `<td>` containing text is missing a font-size reset, text renders invisibly. Cannot verify coverage from truncated source.

**SVG font format in @font-face stack:**
```css
url("...FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")
```
SVG fonts are unsupported in all modern email clients and browsers. Dead weight; minor.

**Responsive breakpoint at 460px:**
```css
@media screen and (max-width:460px)
```
Standard industry breakpoint is 480px–600px. At 460px, narrow-viewport Android devices (commonly 360–414px wide) are covered, but devices in the 461–479px range get desktop layout on a mobile screen. Risk depends on product image grid — cannot verify from truncated source.

**Alt text on images:** Cannot audit — `<img>` tags not present in truncated excerpt. Required for accessibility and clients with images blocked.
