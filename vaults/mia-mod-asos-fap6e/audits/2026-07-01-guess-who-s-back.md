# Guess who's back?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- After ten straight days of sale escalation — 60% off, then extra 10%, then new brands, then extra 25%, then 70% off — ASOS pivots hard to a full-price "back in stock" editorial on July 1. That's either a smart list-fatigue break or a whiplash moment for a subscriber whose mental model of this brand is now "sale machine." The creative itself is clean and editorial: warm summer palette, diverse styling, six product shots across a tight grid. Nothing is broken.
- The core problem is ambiguity at every decision layer. "Guess who's back?" tells you nothing about what to expect — it could be a collaboration, a celebrity, a brand drop, or, as it actually is, a back-in-stock notification. Inside, the subhead clarifies ("your sold-out faves"), but the email then fragments into three separate CTAs (SHOP NOW, SHOP BACK IN STOCK, DOWNLOAD THE APP) without a clear hierarchy. The editorial photography is strong but the mechanism is underexplained — if specific items sold out and returned, name them or show the product.
- The pivot away from the sale is fine strategy, but the execution doesn't earn the click. No urgency (items could sell out again — use that), no price signal, and the app download CTA at the bottom actively competes with the one action ASOS should want: shop the restocked items.

## What's working

- **Pattern interrupt is well-timed.** After seven sale emails in ten days, a clean full-price editorial is a genuine attention reset for fatigued subscribers.
- **Photography is strong and seasonal.** Lavender slip dress, red asymmetric top, orange shorts — the palette reads summer and the styling is contemporary without being niche.
- **Subhead copy does real work.** "Missed a few first-time around? Your sold-out faves are now back in stock." is specific enough to explain the mechanism and creates a mild FOMO hook.
- **No render bugs.** Images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout is solid on this viewport.

## What's weak

- **Subject line "Guess who's back?" is all tease, zero value.** Without the sender context, it reads celebrity collab or brand news — not "items you missed are available." Most subscribers won't connect the dots until they open.
- **Three competing CTAs kill hierarchy.** SHOP NOW → SHOP BACK IN STOCK → DOWNLOAD THE APP is three different asks in one scroll. The app CTA especially pulls in the wrong direction mid-campaign.
- **No urgency lever is pulled.** Back-in-stock items sell out again — that's the entire premise. Zero mention of "limited quantities" or "selling fast" is a wasted conversion trigger.
- **No sale connection.** Whether intentional or not, switching from "up to 70% off" (June 29) to full-price with no acknowledgment will confuse subscribers who are waiting for the sale to continue.
- **Brand logos section ("Shop Our Top Brands") feels padded.** It breaks the narrative flow and adds a fourth destination without supporting the back-in-stock story at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one CTA and make it specific.** Remove SHOP NOW (redundant) and DOWNLOAD THE APP (wrong context). Keep only "SHOP BACK IN STOCK" — and if possible, link to a filtered back-in-stock page, not homepage.
- 2. **Add urgency copy to the subhead.** Change "Your sold-out faves are now back in stock" to "Your sold-out faves are back — and selling fast." One clause, no redesign needed.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to signal the mechanism.** The current subject is pure curiosity gap; pair it with a preheader that closes the gap immediately.
- 4. **Axe the brand logos section** or integrate it only if those brands are specifically the ones restocked. Random brand logos mid-email dilute the narrative.
- 5. **Overlay "Back in Stock" badges on the product images.** Even a subtle text label creates urgency and clarifies why these specific items are featured.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your sold-out faves are back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back in stock — but not for long`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Missed them the first time? Now's your second chance — shop before they're gone again.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The pieces you kept checking on just dropped back in. Grab them before they sell out again.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`prefers-reduced-motion` missing.**
The `@keyframes ticker` animation and `@supports` block have no `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` override. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 2.3.3 (Animation from Interactions). Fix:
```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .ticker { animation: none !important; }
  .fallback { display: block !important; }
}
```

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow.**
`@media screen and (max-width:460px)` leaves viewports 461–479px on the desktop layout. Standard threshold is `480px` (or `600px` for simpler stacks).

**`FuturaPT-Heavy` declared `font-weight: 600`.**
"Heavy" conventionally maps to 800–900. Declaring it at 600 (semi-bold) means weight-based CSS synthesis may render lighter than intended. Should be `font-weight: 800`.

**SVG font format declarations are dead code.**
Both `@font-face` blocks include `url("…FuturaPT-Book.svg") format("svg")`. SVG fonts lost browser support ~2016. No functional impact but should be pruned.

**Image alt text** — cannot audit; HTML truncated before `<body>` content.
