# 20% off Face + Body
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a Beauty/Face+Body category blast — ASOS running a straightforward 20% off discount on their beauty vertical. The hero is clean: a collage of product shots with a recognizable beauty-editorial aesthetic (bright pinks, glitter, skincare close-ups), followed by three brand-spotlight rows (NYX, Bioderma, ANUA, fenty, Gisou) and a "What's trending?" product grid. The structure is functional but predictable.
- The biggest problem is context: ASOS sent "20% off 1000s of styles" on June 9 and a Premier member 25% off on June 15. Sending another 20%-off code six days later — but scoped to beauty only — risks training subscribers to just wait for the next discount rather than creating genuine category urgency. The email does nothing to differentiate itself from the June 9 send in terms of tone, structure, or creative ambition.
- That said, for a subscriber who shops beauty at ASOS, this is a perfectly serviceable trigger. The brand-spotlight rows with names like Gisou and Bioderma will resonate with anyone tracking those labels. Judgement: solid execution, weak strategy.

## What's working

- Hero collage is instantly legible as beauty/skincare — the product variety (lip, skin, eye) communicates category breadth at a glance.
- Brand-name row (NYX / Bioderma / ANUA / fenty / Gisou) adds credibility; Gisou especially skews toward a premium beauty consumer who knows the brand.
- "Selling fast on ASOS" section at the bottom (fashion grid with shoes, denim, tops) is smart cross-sell to keep apparel buyers engaged.
- "What's trending?" module breaks up the brand-block monotony with a lifestyle product shot.
- CTA buttons are consistently styled and repeat at each section — low friction path to click.

## What's weak

- No code, no expiry, no deadline — "20% off" with no mechanic visible means I don't know if it's automatic at checkout or if there's a code to hunt for, which kills purchase intent.
- Creative is almost identical in structure to the June 9 "20% off 1000s of styles" send — two near-identical discount emails within 9 days trains for discount fatigue, not brand affinity.
- The "SHOP NOW" button copy appears on every single module with no variation — feels templated and gives no contextual reason to click any individual section.
- Model / lifestyle imagery is dominated by product-flat-lay rather than human wear — beauty category benefits from skin-on-model shots to signal inclusivity and results.
- The "Selling fast on ASOS" apparel grid at the bottom undercuts the beauty focus — it reads like a remnant template module rather than an intentional cross-sell.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible mechanic and deadline.** Show the code or confirm it's auto-applied, and add a deadline ("ends midnight Sunday"). Without this, "20% off" is just a promise, not an action trigger.
- 2. **Differentiate from the June 9 send.** ASOS sent sitewide 20% off nine days ago. This email needs an editorial angle — "Your summer beauty routine, 20% off" or a seasonal peg — not just a repeated price signal.
- 3. **Vary the CTA copy per module.** "Shop NYX," "Explore Gisou," "See what's trending" — specificity drives clicks; "SHOP NOW" everywhere is invisible.
- 4. **Lead with skin-on-model imagery.** One strong hero of a person (not a product collage) communicates results and aspiration before the deal.
- 5. **Cut or contextualise the apparel cross-sell.** Either make it "complete the look" (beauty + outfit pairing) or remove it — a raw fashion grid at the bottom of a beauty email feels like template noise.
